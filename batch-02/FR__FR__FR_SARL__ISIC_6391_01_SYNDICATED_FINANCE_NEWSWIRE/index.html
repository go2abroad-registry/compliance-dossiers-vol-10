Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum; founders may set share capital from €1. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), including the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) for non-established businesses; principal portals: impots.gouv.fr and the formalities portal operated through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate or register the French SARL through the Guichet unique; the filing is transmitted to the Registre national des entreprises and results in attribution of a SIREN and SIRET. 2. Open and maintain the corporate tax account with the competent Service des impôts des entreprises. 3. Apply to the competent DGFiP office for VAT registration where the company conducts taxable French transactions, makes intra-Community transactions, imports goods, or cannot rely on a reverse-charge mechanism. 4. For a non-established foreign entity, submit the VAT registration application and supporting documents to the SIEE, generally including constitutional documents, commercial activity evidence, identification of directors and beneficial owners, tax residence evidence, power of attorney where applicable, and French or translated banking and invoicing information. 5. DGFiP issues a French VAT number in the format FR plus a two-digit key and the entity's nine-digit SIREN. The SIREN is the principal French business identifier; France does not generally issue a separate income-tax TIN equivalent to the SIREN.

DGFiP issues a French VAT number in the format FR plus a two-digit key and the entity's nine-digit SIREN. The SIREN is the principal French business identifier; France does not generally issue a separate income-tax TIN equivalent to the SIREN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Certain SARLs may elect income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu), including a qualifying family SARL without the ordinary five-year limitation, or an eligible closely held SARL for a maximum period generally limited to five fiscal years, subject to statutory ownership, size, activity, non-listing, and shareholder conditions. There is no United States-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework and do not provide a general elective hybrid classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For French-established businesses eligible for the franchise en base de TVA, the principal turnover thresholds are generally EUR 37,500 for services and EUR 85,000 for sales of goods, with tolerance thresholds generally of EUR 41,250 and EUR 93,500 respectively. These thresholds determine eligibility for the small-business exemption and are subject to statutory adjustments and sector-specific rules. A non-established taxable person generally cannot rely on the French domestic threshold in the same manner and may require registration from the first taxable French transaction. Registration may also be required independently of turnover for imports, intra-Community acquisitions, distance sales, or transactions not subject to an applicable reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT under the French implementation of the EU export exemption where the supplier or an authorized intermediary establishes that the goods left the EU customs territory and the transaction, supplier, and customs records correspond. Evidence normally includes the export customs declaration and electronic exit confirmation, commonly demonstrated through the Movement Reference Number and Export Control System records, together with commercial and transport documentation. Services supplied to a business established outside France are generally governed by place-of-supply rules rather than a French 0% export rate; qualifying B2B services are generally outside the scope of French VAT with customer-side reverse charge in the country of establishment. Specific rules apply to services directly connected with exported goods, intermediated transactions, transport, digital services, and B2C supplies. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, business-purpose evidence, and compliance with the applicable domestic or non-established-business refund procedure.

  • Electronic export customs declaration, including the export MRN and evidence of acceptance and exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and VAT treatment
  • Packing list and transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent
  • Proof of physical exit or delivery outside the EU, including ECS exit results, carrier confirmation, or equivalent customs evidence
  • Purchase orders, contracts, and shipping records linking the exported goods to the invoiced transaction
  • Payment evidence or receivables records where relevant to substantiate the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory export-VAT document
  • Import or destination-country customs evidence where necessary to resolve discrepancies or support an audit

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through accredited private platforms (plateformes de dématérialisation partenaires, or PDPs), with data transmission to the public invoicing portal. The reformed timetable provides mandatory receipt of electronic invoices for all French-established businesses from 1 September 2026; mandatory issuance applies from 1 September 2026 to large enterprises and tax groups, from 1 September 2027 to medium-sized enterprises, SMEs, and micro-enterprises, subject to statutory transition rules. B2B domestic invoices must use prescribed structured or hybrid formats and include required invoice data. E-reporting applies to specified cross-border and B2C transactions. France does not operate a universal pre-clearance requirement for every invoice comparable to jurisdictions requiring real-time invoice approval; PDP transmission, data validation, archiving, and reporting obligations apply according to the applicable timetable.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement for export VAT relief. The decisive requirements are valid export evidence, actual exit from the EU customs territory, correct invoicing, and consistency between customs, accounting, transport, and payment records. If the exemption or refund is unsupported, DGFiP may assess the unpaid VAT retrospectively, together with late-payment interest and applicable penalties. Deliberate non-compliance may attract a 40% penalty; fraudulent maneuvers or abusive conduct may attract an 80% penalty, subject to statutory facts and procedural safeguards. False documentation, fictitious exports, or organized VAT fraud may also create criminal exposure. The statute of limitations generally permits VAT reassessment for the relevant statutory period, commonly three years, subject to extension or special rules for fraud and criminal proceedings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member with full capital and current account convertibility under EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63-66 TFEU). Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate tax withholding (if applicable) and anti-money laundering (AML) verification. No exchange control approval is required for cross-border transfers, but all transactions exceeding €10,000 must be reported to the French customs and tax authorities for statistical and AML purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds exists in France. Exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts, but must report all cross-border flows to the Banque de France via the balance-of-payments reporting system (REP) within 20 business days of the transaction. No minimum conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the prudential supervisory authority; the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) oversees balance-of-payments reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. France recognizes Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under PSD2, and cross-border EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Qonto, or N26) can open accounts remotely. However, for a financial newswire with syndicated finance data, the bank's AML risk assessment may classify the business as 'financial services' requiring enhanced due diligence. EMIs are viable for operational liquidity but not for capital escrow or complex trade finance. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot provide credit or hold client funds beyond e-money limits; they are not suitable for large syndicated finance settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'S010' (Capital contribution) or 'S020' (Shareholder loan). For EU transfers, use SEPA CT with purpose code 'COST' or 'CAPC'. Verification: The bank will require the capital escrow account to be opened before injection; the transfer must reference the SARL's SIREN number and the notarized articles of association. For non-EU transfers, include the LEI (Legal Entity Identifier) and a copy of the board resolution authorizing the capital increase.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers to suppliers or licensors; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) via SWIFT MT700 for high-value syndicated finance data licensing; (3) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for EU-based operational payments; (4) For API-based subscription revenue, use Stripe Connect or similar payment gateways with automatic settlement to the operational account. All outbound transfers must include the correct purpose code (e.g., 'S030' for royalties, 'S040' for service fees) and be reported to the Banque de France if exceeding €10,000.FX Forwards (plain vanilla) – permitted for hedging currency exposure on non-EUR receivables/payables, subject to ACPR authorization if the SARL is not a financial entity.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – permissible for exotic currencies (e.g., CNY, INR) where deliverable forwards are restricted; must be executed via a licensed bank.Currency Swaps – allowed for long-term hedging of syndicated finance cash flows, but require board approval and documented risk management policy.FX Options – permitted for hedging but not for speculative purposes; must be aligned with the underlying commercial exposure.
