Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation procedure or statutory 30-day reservation period. Conduct an INPI/RNE availability search, trademark search through INPI and EUIPO, domain-name search, and conflict review before filing. If the Guichet unique, registry, or competent authority rejects the name, obtain the electronic rejection notice, correct the name or supporting evidence, and resubmit through the Guichet unique within the applicable correction period. A formal administrative challenge may be filed with the competent authority and, where legally available, before the competent administrative court within the deadline stated in the rejection notice. A refusal based on trademark infringement, misleading financial terminology, or regulated-activity implication should normally be resolved by changing the name or obtaining the relevant authorization rather than by appeal.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for a SARL; the share capital is freely fixed in the Statuts. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and at least one-fifth of the nominal value of each share must be paid up on formation. The paid-up cash capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account with a bank or notary and remains blocked until the Kbis is issued.

Funding & unlock path: For the registered office and any hosting premises, obtain a fire safety certificate from the local fire safety commission (attestation de sécurité incendie) for tertiary-use buildings, perform a structural load assessment if dedicated data racks are installed, and register the workplace with the occupational health service of the DREETS. No explosive or environmental risk clearance is required for a pure financial trading office, but the company must maintain a fire alarm and escape plan review before first employees occupy.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — impots.gouv.fr; VAT and customs ancillary competence exercised by Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) — douane.gouv.fr; centralised incorporation and tax registration portal: Guichet Unique / formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL obtains its statutory tax identity ex lege upon corporate formation. The constitutional legal representative files the incorporation dossier (form MO-4) with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce through the online Guichet Unique. The registre then assigns the SIREN (9-digit statutory identifier), the SIRET (establishment identifier), and the code APE/NAF corresponding to ISIC 6499. The DGFiP automatically allocates the TVA intracommunautaire number (FR + 11 digits) upon verification of the incorporation file, normally within four to eleven business days. For a non-resident entity not yet possessing a French establishment, the DGFiP requires an individual application on the Cerfa no. 15768 form or the standard VAT registration application, supported by a certificate of fiscal domicile, the foreign company deed, and proof of the responsible fiscal representative when the non-resident has no permanent representative in France. Registration must be completed before any taxable supply, intra-Community acquisition, or import is made.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A standard SARL is irrebuttably within the scope of French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the entity level under Article 206(1) of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI). Statutory elections available include: (i) the Article 239 bis AA CGI option for newly created SMEs to be taxed as a transparent partnership (régime fiscal des sociétés de personnes) for the first five fiscal years or until the corporation meets the SME thresholds, requiring unanimous shareholder consent and prompt filing; (ii) for an EURL (single-member SARL), the default pass-through taxation under Article 1655 bis CGI unless an irrevocable IS election is made; (iii) the groupe fiscal d'intégration election under Article 223 A CGI allowing a French parent to consolidate wholly- or 95%-owned French subsidiaries for IS purposes; and (iv) no check-the-box regime exists in French law, but hybrid mismatch elections may arise through the design of the foreign parent's entity characterisation; the French entity is nevertheless always treated as corporation-taxable absent the 239 bis AA election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The DGFiP 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption under Article 293 B CGI applies only to small businesses with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 85,000 for sales of goods and EUR 37,500 for services. This threshold is de facto unavailable for the present algorithmic high-frequency trading vehicle because principal trading is not a taxable supply and any fee-based financial services are VAT exempt under Article 261 C(1) CGI, while cross-border B2B financial services fall under the reverse charge irrespective of turnover. No general economic threshold triggers compulsory VAT registration for a SARL engaged in financial or FX trading; registration is mandatory from the first euro of taxable activity, and the franchise exemption cannot be invoked by a deliberately structured HFT entity. Intra-Community acquisition of goods is registrable regardless of value, and the OSS/IOSS regimes are inapplicable to exempt financial services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, exports outside the European Union are zero-rated under Article 262 CGI provided the goods physically leave the EU customs territory within thirty days of the transfer of power of disposal and the exporter retains the harmonised proof of exit. For services, the zero-rating or test figure 'hors champ' is reached where the place of supply is outside France under Articles 259, 259 A and 259 B CGI; a French SARL supplying financial or market-making services to a non-resident taxable person applies the reverse charge and does not charge French VAT, which is equivalent to zero-rating. Because the present ISIC 6499 model operates proprietary ultra-low-latency algorithmic market making, no fee-bearing supply may exist; own-account trading falls outside the scope of VAT and no VAT claim or refund arises. If the SARL provides fee-based algorithmic execution, liquidity-provider services, or brokerage to non-EU banks or investment firms, zero-rating eligibility requires proof that (i) the recipient is a taxable person established in a third state, (ii) the service does not benefit a French or EU fixed establishment, and (iii) the consideration is computed as a fee or spread, not as a capital gain. The DGFiP applies the 'effective use and enjoyment' rule to determine the place of supply for B2B services supplied to third-country recipients.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / EAD) lodged electronically on the DELTA system under the Union Customs Code, reflecting the proper goods nomenclature and the UE exit regime
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), or CMR consignment note proving physical departure from French customs territory to a non-EU destination
  • Proof of exit with the customs office stamp or the electronic Export Accompanying Document (EAD) and the certificate of exit from the border office
  • Commercial invoice and, where applicable, the service contract or order-routing confirmation identifying the non-EU recipient and the VAT exemption or reverse-charge clause
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement vouchers evidencing payment receipt from the foreign counterparty in convertible currency and traceability of the funds within 180 days
  • For algorithm-as-a-service and FX market-making: counterparty entity documentation (LEI code, tax residence certificate, place of effective management evidence) proving the recipient's third-country establishment
  • Trade reconstruction file (time-stamped order audit trail) demonstrating that the supplied service, if any, is distinct from proprietary gains and supports the zero-rated or reverse-charged treatment

France enforces a phased e-invoicing mandate (facturation électronique) from 1 September 2026 for all VAT-registered businesses: acceptance of electronic invoices becomes compulsory for all B2B transactions, and structured electronic invoices must be transmitted through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or an accredited Plateforme de Dématerialisation Partenaire (PDP). From 2024-2025, large enterprises are subject to earlier e-reporting stages. Real-time fiscal clearance is not a French VAT concept; the system relies on continuous transmission of invoice data and e-reporting of transaction summaries. For crypto-asset and algorithmic trading services, the invoice must contain a specific exemption reference (e.g. Article 261 C CGI) and the reverse-charge indication for cross-border B2B supplies. Payment data, order-routing logs, and attestations from the financial services regulator must be retained as part of the pièces justificatives to support e-reporting accuracy. No digital stamping or crypto-graphic fiscal capping applies to French invoices, but the mandatory QR-code Datamatrix was never adopted; instead the PPF/PDP transmission data is the legally probative record.

The DGFiP and DGDDI jointly exercise audit scrutiny over all export zero-rating and reverse-charge claims. Unfounded or fictitious export deductions are penalised under Article 1788 A CGI at 80% of the under-declared tax. Reckless or negligent zero-rating attracts the 40% penalty of Article 1728(b) CGI coupled with late-payment interest of 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI). Fraudulent conduct (fausse facturation, nonexistent exports, sham counterparties, forged bills of lading, circular trading) is prosecuted under Article 1741 CGI with criminal fines up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of five years, doubling for organised fraud, plus confiscation of instruments and proceeds. The VAT refund is retrospectively clawed back with statutory interest, and the unpaid tax remains indefinitely claimable in cases of concealed activity under Article L. 168 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales (10-year limitation for extraterritorial or artificial arrangements). For cross-border financial services, audit also tests whether the SARL maintained a French fixed establishment and whether the reverse-charge or zero-rate treatment artificially avoided VAT on effectively domestic supplies; any mischaracterisation triggers the same penalties, joint and several liability of the legal representative under Article 267 bis CGI, and publication of tax penalties. Persisting non-repatriation of export proceeds beyond the 180-day deadline also engages French exchange-control law and may lead to the transaction being recharacterised as a hidden distribution of profits subject to 30% withholding or corporation tax re-assessment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no general French capital controls, prior approvals, surrender requirements, or restrictions on conversion of lawful commercial receipts, dividends, interest, or capital proceeds. Cross-border payments remain subject to sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, beneficial-owner identification, payment-transparency requirements, tax compliance, and applicable EU financial-market rules. A regulated proprietary high-frequency trading business may require authorization as an investment firm or another applicable regulated status before commencing activities. Profit remittances should be supported by approved financial statements, corporate approvals, tax compliance evidence, and evidence that distributions do not breach French corporate-law capital-maintenance or solvency rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no universal statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros. Export proceeds may normally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be received through correspondent-bank accounts, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, customs, and anti-money-laundering controls. Commercial banks may require evidence of the underlying transaction, customs or invoice records, economic purpose, and source-of-funds information. Cash and certain equivalent payment instruments transported across EU external borders in amounts of EUR 10,000 or more are subject to applicable customs declaration requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, within the Eurosystem; foreign-exchange policy is primarily governed by the European Union and the European Central Bank. The French Treasury Directorate General, the French Customs authority, the Autorité des marchés financiers, and the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution exercise relevant reporting, market, payment, and prudential functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ordinary operating payments and low to medium for a regulated proprietary high-frequency trading treasury. An EU electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro accounts, SEPA payments, cards, and selected foreign-currency services, but it is not a bank unless separately licensed. Client-money safeguarding, payment-purpose restrictions, transaction limits, safeguarding-bank dependencies, delayed compliance reviews, and inability to provide exchange-clearing, prime-brokerage, collateral, securities-settlement, or robust intraday liquidity services can make an EMI unsuitable as the primary treasury bank. Offshore digital EMIs without an EU passport, French branch, or acceptable EU regulatory status should not be used for core regulated activity. The company should maintain a regulated French or EU bank account and use an EMI only as a documented secondary rail after confirming licensing, safeguarding, sanctions, data, and counterparty arrangements.

Defense evidence: Account-ownership schedule; broker and custodian agreements; permitted-account policy; reconciliation procedures; explanation of each intermediary; segregation-of-funds policy; and evidence that all settlement accounts are held in the company's name or with a properly documented regulated intermediary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inject capital from an account held in the investor's verified legal name to the SARL's French bank account, using a bank-to-bank SWIFT transfer or SEPA transfer where available. The payment narrative should identify the legal basis, such as paid-in share capital, share-premium contribution, shareholder loan, or qualifying contribution to equity. The bank should receive the subscription agreement, shareholder or board resolutions, capital-deposit certificate where applicable, updated articles or registry filing evidence, investor ownership documents, source-of-funds evidence, and tax-residence information. The receiving bank should verify the originating account, beneficial owner, purpose, sanctions status, and accounting treatment before release or use. Avoid third-party funding, unexplained pass-through accounts, cash, and crypto-originated funds unless the bank has approved enhanced due diligence. For a regulated trading entity, segregate regulatory capital, operating cash, margin collateral, and client or third-party funds, and reconcile each category daily.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or TARGET services for euro payments, and SWIFT cross-border payments for non-euro currencies or jurisdictions outside the SEPA area. SWIFT MT103 is a bank messaging format rather than an independent settlement system; payment instructions must contain complete originator, beneficiary, address, purpose, invoice or contract reference, and intermediary-bank data. Use commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, or bank guarantees only for genuine goods or service transactions and not to disguise trading, capital, or investment flows. For securities and derivatives, use the regulated broker, exchange, clearing member, central counterparty, custodian, or settlement agent designated in the relevant agreement. Fedwire is a United States dollar domestic settlement rail and is not a French domestic rail; it may be used only through an eligible US correspondent or broker for USD settlement. Maintain payment approval matrices, dual authorization, sanctions screening, invoice or contract support, beneficiary verification, daily bank reconciliation, and exception reporting.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards for documented operating, settlement, or balance-sheet exposures, subject to bank counterparty limits, confirmations, valuation, collateral terms, and applicable EMIR reporting and risk-mitigation requirements.Non-deliverable forwards where the reference currency or market convention requires cash settlement, provided the instrument is executed with an appropriately regulated counterparty and is supported by a documented underlying exposure or approved risk-management mandate.Foreign-exchange swaps for short-term liquidity, funding, and currency transformation, subject to counterparty credit limits, maturity controls, collateral arrangements, valuation, and EMIR obligations.Currency options, collars, and option combinations for hedging identifiable exposures, subject to product-governance, appropriateness or suitability requirements, premium and liquidity controls, accounting treatment, and prohibition of speculative structures outside the approved treasury mandate.Cross-currency swaps for longer-term funding or structural currency exposure, subject to board-approved limits, collateral and close-out provisions, independent valuation, EMIR reporting, and stress testing.Hedging documentation should identify the exposure, hedge ratio, tenor, authorized counterparties, execution venue, valuation source, collateral terms, accounting designation, termination rights, and prohibited speculative uses. MiFID II, EMIR, market-abuse, sanctions, accounting, and prudential requirements may apply depending on the entity's authorization, counterparties, and instrument classification.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for ordinary operating payments and low to medium for a regulated proprietary high-frequency trading treasury. An EU electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro accounts, SEPA payments, cards, and selected foreign-currency services, but it is not a bank unless separately licensed. Client-money safeguarding, payment-purpose restrictions, transaction limits, safeguarding-bank dependencies, delayed compliance reviews, and inability to provide exchange-clearing, prime-brokerage, collateral, securities-settlement, or robust intraday liquidity services can make an EMI unsuitable as the primary treasury bank. Offshore digital EMIs without an EU passport, French branch, or acceptable EU regulatory status should not be used for core regulated activity. The company should maintain a regulated French or EU bank account and use an EMI only as a documented secondary rail after confirming licensing, safeguarding, sanctions, data, and counterparty arrangements.
