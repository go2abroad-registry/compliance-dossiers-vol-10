Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) maintained by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce for the registered office; filings are made electronically through the INPI Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II — Article L. 223-1 through L. 223-43 (constitution, operation, and capital of a SARL); Articles L. 123-1 et seq. (registration in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés); Loi n° 2019-486 (PACTE) and Guichet Unique implementing regulations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) accompanied by the statutory Déclaration de création d'une société (standard form Cerfa n° 13967*02, formerly M0), transmitted via the Guichet Unique to the RCS.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital: L. 223-2 C. com. leaves the capital amount to the articles, and EUR 1 is lawful. At least 20% of in-cash subscriptions must be paid up before filing for registration (L. 223-7 C. com.); funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account or notarial escrow before signature of the statuts, and the balance is payable within 5 years. The blocked funds are released only upon presentation of the Kbis extract.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: if the premises are open to the public, the office must be classified as an ERP under the Code de la construction with a fire-commission visit for 5th-category establishments; employee-only offices must comply with Code du travail fire and evacuation rules (R. 4227-1 et seq.) and obtain an electrical-installation conformity certificate (Consuel). Occupational clearance: declare the first hire through the DUE (URSSAF), display the mandatory labour notices, provide access to workplace medical services, and maintain the premises in compliance with the Code du travail workplace health and safety standards; structural-load inspection applies only if the building dossier so requires, which is standard office use and not physically demanding.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital: L. 223-2 C. com. leaves the capital amount to the articles, and EUR 1 is lawful. At least 20% of in-cash subscriptions must be paid up before filing for registration (L. 223-7 C. com.); funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account or notarial escrow before signature of the statuts, and the balance is payable within 5 years. The blocked funds are released only upon presentation of the Kbis extract.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the foreign entity has a fixed establishment (établissement stable) in France; if yes, register the establishment through the Guichet Unique portal at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, after which INSEE assigns the SIREN/SIRET identifiers and DGFiP automatically issues the French intra-community VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 11 characters). Step 2: If there is no fixed establishment but taxable supplies or reverse-charge acquisitions arise in France, the foreign entity applies directly to the DGFiP/SIEE for a French VAT number without a SIRET, submitting certified statutes, current registration extract or equivalent company register certificate from the State of residence, proof of address and legal representative identity, and a statement of the expected first taxable transaction date and nature. Step 3: The SIEE cross-checks the applicant's establishment via VIES/foreign registries and issues the VAT number. Step 4: Open the professional tax account (espace professionnel) on impots.gouv.fr, register for the CA3/CA12 VAT filing cycle, and comply with Intrastat/e-reporting obligations. All TIN disclosures on invoices and registries must use the single French VAT number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with limited Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope; Tax_Exempt_Zone not applicable. A French SARL is opaque by default and subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the entity level (Art. 206-1 CGI); the standard CIT rate is 25%, with a reduced 15% rate on taxable profits up to EUR 42,500 for eligible SMEs below EUR 10 million turnover (Art. 219-I-b CGI). Only qualifying family SARLs may elect income-tax transparency (Art. 239 bis AA CGI). French CIT applies to profits attributable to enterprises operated in France only (territoriality, Art. 209-I CGI); foreign branch profits of the SARL are generally exempt from French CIT unless profits are artificially channelled back to France. No tax-exempt zone status applies to a digital media buying entity in metropolitan France.

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box regime; entity classification is governed by law and the place of effective management. Available elections: (i) option for IR pass-through treatment for family SARLs (Art. 239 bis AA CGI); (ii) option for fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) for French-resident companies held at least 95% (Art. 223 A CGI); (iii) opt-in to the parent-subsidiary participation exemption at the 5% holding threshold (Art. 145 CGI); (iv) SME election for the reduced 15% CIT rate on the first EUR 42,500 of profit (Art. 219-I-b CGI); (v) election to renounce the small-business VAT exemption (Art. 293 B CGI); (vi) no hybrid-mismatch election exists: ATAD anti-hybrid rules (EU Directive 2016/1164, transposed under Art. 212 bis CGI) are mandatory and override mismatching payee/payor treatments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains a fully convertible Euro currency regime aligned with EU liberalization rules. Capital account transactions require no prior authorization and are subject only to post-transaction reporting to the Banque de France via the central securities depository. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are unrestricted subject to French tax withholding at source where applicable and full AML/KYC compliance enforced by Tracfin. Primary hurdles include verification of beneficial ownership, source of funds documentation, and alignment with anti-tax-evasion standards under the OECD BEPS framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For physical goods exports, proceeds must be surrendered to a French bank within 60 days of receipt and converted at the official rate if required under customs valuation. For digital services and affiliate media buying transactions, no surrender obligation applies; funds may be retained in offshore accounts with supporting invoices, performance contracts, and tax declaration to the Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and Mercury are operationally viable for cross-border affiliate payments but require mandatory linkage to a French licensed bank account for regulatory compliance. Direct issuance of French fintech EMIs is restricted under AMF and EBA licensing; offshore platforms must comply with French data localization and reporting obligations.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard rate, fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2022)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Fiscal year ending 31 December: file by the second business day after 1 May (normally 3 May; extended if that date is a non-business day). Fiscal year ending on another date: file within three months of the fiscal year-end. Quarterly IS installments (acomptes) due 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December; final balance due with the annual return.

Form 2065, Cadre A / lines 01-03 (identification) Legal name of the SARL, SIREN registration number, registered office address, and place of management in France. For a non-resident company's French permanent establishment (PE), enter the PE's French address and the foreign head-office registration number; the PE is taxed at IS rates on its French-attributable profit. Late filing penalty (Article 1728 CGI): 10% of the net tax due (return filed within 30 days of the deadline), 40% where filed after 30 days following the first formal notice, 80% in cases of fraudulent maneuvers or abuse of law. Late payment interest (Article 1727 CGI) accrues at 0.20% per month. Ex officio assessment is possible if no return is filed within 60 days of the deadline; electronic-filing non-compliance may trigger an additional EUR 15 per filing incident. Quarterly installment defaults carry the same scale on the unpaid installments.

Only the profit attributable to the French PE and computed by reference to the relevant DTA provisions should be entered; profits of the foreign head office are excluded. Schedule 2058-A, related-party transactions table Declaration of transactions with related enterprises and transfer prices (loaned funds, commissions, media-buying margins payable to non-resident affiliates). If the documentation threshold is met, complete the supplementary transfer-pricing statement (form 2257-SD); otherwise, keep contemporaneous arm's length documentation for audit defense. Schedule 2054 (tax credits) Declaration of French tax credits, principally the research tax credit (CIR) for qualifying software R&D and other recoverable withholding taxes. Non-resident PEs may claim credits only for expenses attributable to the PE; foreign tax credits on income taxed abroad are claimed by the head office, not by the PE.
