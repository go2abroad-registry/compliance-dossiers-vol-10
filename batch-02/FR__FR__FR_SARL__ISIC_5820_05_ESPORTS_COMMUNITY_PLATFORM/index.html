Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent local Commercial Court Registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce), through the INPI-operated Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L. 223-1 et seq. (SARL formation and governance), including Articles L. 223-2 (capital), L. 223-18 (management), and L. 123-1 et seq. (Commercial and Companies Register).

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic application for registration in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS), submitted through the Guichet unique; the filing includes the executed articles of association and required supporting documents.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies; capital may be set at EUR 1. For cash contributions, at least 20% must be paid up at incorporation and deposited with a bank, notary, or other legally authorized depositary. The balance is payable within five years after registration. Non-cash contributions must generally be fully contributed at formation; an auditor may be required subject to statutory exemptions.

Funding & unlock path: No industrial fire-safety or structural-load clearance is ordinarily required for a digital platform with no public-facing premises. If a venue is operated, assess establishment-receiving-the-public (ERP) classification, applicable fire and accessibility rules, building approvals, and workplace health and safety obligations with the competent municipality, fire and rescue service, and labour authorities.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies; capital may be set at EUR 1. For cash contributions, at least 20% must be paid up at incorporation and deposited with a bank, notary, or other legally authorized depositary. The balance is payable within five years after registration. Non-cash contributions must generally be fully contributed at formation; an auditor may be required subject to statutory exemptions.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The SARL has no board of directors; its shareholders appoint one or more managers (gérants). The founding shareholders resolve that: (1) the subscribed shares and each shareholder’s contribution are as stated in the executed articles of association, and the required initial cash contributions are acknowledged as deposited; (2) the company’s registered office is adopted at [full address] in accordance with the articles; (3) [full name] is appointed manager for the term specified in the articles and accepts the appointment; and (4) the manager is authorized to complete registration formalities, open the company’s operating bank account, and obtain the company’s identification and tax registrations.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing board is ordinarily competent for an online esports matchmaking or tournament-platform business in France. The competent formalities channel is the INPI Guichet unique. Separate rules may apply if the business operates a physical venue, offers gambling, or conducts regulated prize-based gaming.

Category: No general manufacturing or operating permit is required solely to operate an online esports community or tournament platform. Assess gaming and gambling rules with the Autorité Nationale des Jeux (ANJ) if the actual model involves gambling or regulated gaming activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) through the impots.gouv.fr portal; VAT and corporate income tax filings are made via the secured professional portal (Espace Professionnel / Gesti) or via transliterated e-services for foreign taxpayers.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned French SARL, the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is issued after the company is officially constituted via the CFE (Centre de Formalités des Entreprises) or the Guichet Unique; the entity obtains its SIREN/SIRET and French VAT number by filing the M0 form with the relevant Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Foreign entities without French establishment apply for direct VAT registration using form CA12 (Demande d'immatriculation au registre de TVA) filed with the DGFiP unit at the Service des Impôts des Non-Résidents in Paris, attaching the constitutive documents, Kbis analog, CFC certificate, proof of business activity, and professional address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A standard SARL is hypothetically taxable to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at entity level with no check-the-box election under French domestic law. Available elections include: (i) option for IS for qualifying SARLs that are within the IR regime; (ii) formation of an intra-group fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) for 95%+ owned French subsidiaries; (iii) automatic IS treatment for commercial SARLs with actual economic activity; and (iv) application of EU anti-hybrid and DAC6 notification rules to prevent double-dip or mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 for principal supply of goods (elevated threshold EUR 93,500 under the majoration mechanism); EUR 37,500 for provision of services (elevated threshold EUR 41,300); for non-established foreign sellers of digital services, VAT registration is required as soon as the first B2C supply is made, irrespective of threshold, using the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) non-Union scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated (exonération) status applies to physical goods physically exported outside the European Union provided the VAT-registered supplier proves the export through a valid dep în to customs; digital and electronically supplied services sold to a business customer established outside the EU are treated as outside the territorial scope of French VAT if the place of supply is the customer's country, which effectively zero-rates them; for B2B services, invoicing without French VAT is permitted when evidence of non-EU establishment is maintained. The esports community platform must document user residence, IP-based billing address, and contractual terms to support place-of-supply determination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU or electronic declaration via DELTA)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or airway bill / CMR consignment note for freight evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (certificat de remise documentaire or bank receipt) demonstrating actual receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice referencing the export customs declaration number

France has enacted a phased domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate: large and mid-sized taxpayers must issue structured e-invoices through certified plateforms or the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) from 1 September 2026, with all taxpayers (including SMEs) required from 1 September 2027; e-reporting of transaction data to DGFiP via the PPF/registered plateforms is mandatory for exempt transactions and B2C deliveries; alignment is with the EN16931 / Factur-X syntax. There is no real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory digital stamping for standard VAT transactions, but continuous transaction controls require near-real-time API reporting for approved electronic reporting platforms.

Fraudulent or abusive zero-rating claims may result in retrospective recovery of unpaid VAT, a 80% penalty (manquement délibéré / manoeuvres frauduleuses) or 100% penalty for fraud, monthly late-interest of 0.20%, a reassessment period extended to ten years in cases of fraud, possible criminal penalties for fiscal evasion, and denial of the export VAT exemption together with integrity-free assessment for ineligible export documents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no general French foreign-exchange licensing requirement for receiving foreign investment, paying foreign suppliers, repatriating dividends, servicing intercompany debt, or converting euros into other currencies. A French SARL may hold foreign-currency accounts and make cross-border payments, subject to bank AML, sanctions, tax, accounting, payment-services, and documentary controls. Dividend distributions require distributable profits, approval of annual accounts, satisfaction of statutory reserve requirements, and compliance with applicable withholding-tax and treaty procedures. Interest, royalties, service fees, and intercompany payments require arm's-length support, proper invoicing, beneficial-owner analysis, transfer-pricing documentation, withholding-tax review, and possible anti-abuse analysis. Payments involving sanctioned jurisdictions, high-risk countries, opaque ownership, virtual-asset activity, or unexplained third-party settlement may be delayed or rejected. Foreign direct investment screening may apply to activities affecting national interests, but a standard esports community and tournament platform is not ordinarily a controlled strategic activity solely because it operates digitally.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or repatriated through a French bank. Export proceeds may remain in euros or foreign currency and may be held in accounts outside France, subject to tax, accounting, sanctions, customs, payment-services, and financial-reporting obligations. A French resident entity must maintain reliable books and records and support revenue recognition, VAT treatment, customer identity, invoice matching, and bank reconciliation. Cross-border statistical or balance-of-payments reporting may be requested by Banque de France or the relevant payment institution. Cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more entering or leaving the European Union require customs declaration by the carrier or traveler under applicable EU and French rules. Financial institutions may impose commercial settlement deadlines or request evidence of export performance, but those are contractual or compliance requirements rather than foreign-exchange surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank. Relevant supervisory and regulatory authorities also include the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution, the Autorité des Marchés Financiers, the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects, and the French Ministry of the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for operational payments and foreign-currency collection; Low to Medium as the sole treasury bank; generally unsuitable as a substitute for a French capital-deposit relationship unless the provider expressly supports French company formation and escrow mechanics. An offshore digital EMI cannot provide French-regulated banking services merely by offering an app or an IBAN. The provider must be authorized under the applicable EU payment-services or electronic-money regime, passported or otherwise lawfully servicing France, and capable of safeguarding client funds. An EMI account is not a deposit account and normally does not provide deposit-guarantee protection. Providers may restrict regulated collection, merchant acquiring, gambling-related activity, prize flows, crypto exposure, mass payouts, high-volume cross-border transfers, or accounts lacking a French nexus. Stripe and PayPal should generally be treated as payment-collection or merchant-acquiring channels, not as substitutes for a statutory capital escrow account or primary corporate bank account. Account ownership, beneficial ownership, settlement flows, chargebacks, reserves, tax reporting, and source-of-funds evidence must be documented.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

During formation, subscribe capital into the SARL capital-deposit account maintained by an eligible French bank, notary, or other legally permitted depositary, using a transfer from the verified subscriber's own account. The transfer reference should identify the subscriber, the SARL name or formation project, and the purpose as capital contribution. After registration, obtain the deposit certificate and request release into the company's operational account. For post-formation equity, shareholder-current-account advances, or intercompany loans, use a bank account in the funder's verified name, an executed agreement, a board or shareholder approval where required, and a documented purpose. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT cross-border payment messaging where the sender or currency requires it. Provide the receiving bank with the legal entity identifier where available, tax and registration data, beneficial-owner information, purpose-of-payment information, and supporting agreements. Do not route corporate capital through personal accounts, unidentified payment intermediaries, cash, informal netting, or an EMI that cannot evidence safeguarding and account ownership. Stripe and PayPal should generally be treated as payment-collection or merchant-acquiring channels, not as substitutes for a statutory capital escrow account or primary corporate bank account. Account ownership, beneficial ownership, settlement flows, chargebacks, reserves, tax reporting, and source-of-funds evidence must be documented.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) for fiscal years opened from 1 January 2024; 15% reduced rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying small and medium enterprises with annual turnover below EUR 10 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax on dividends paid by a French SARL to corporate shareholders resident outside France (article 119 bis-2 CGI); non-resident individuals generally face a 30% flat levy (12.8% income tax component plus 17.2% social levies); the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive secures 0% withholding for qualifying EU parent companies holding at least 10% of capital for 24 months; double tax treaties commonly reduce the corporate rate to 15%, 5%, or 0% according to the shareholding threshold.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File: French entities belonging to a group with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 400 million, or French entities with annual gross revenue of at least EUR 50 million for simplified documentation; Country-by-Country Report mandatory for groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million (article 223 quinquies C CGI).

Economic substance scope: France does not operate an offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations regime; instead, the abuse-of-law doctrine (article L64 LPF), article 57 CGI transfer pricing rules, and article 155A CGI impose genuine economic substance requirements on intercompany arrangements. An esports SARL must demonstrate real French headcount, actual matchmaking infrastructure and servers, substantive payment flows through Stripe and PayPal, risk-bearing commercial contracts, and financial capacity consistent with its reported profit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion – fraudulent concealment of esports turnover

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI: up to 3 years imprisonment and EUR 250,000 fine; aggravated fraud involving organized activity or money laundering carries up to 7 years and EUR 1,000,000, plus confiscation of assets and exclusion from public procurement.

Director personal liability: Gérants are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts under article L267 LPF when wrongful maneuvers prevent recovery; courts may impose travel bans, asset freezing, management disqualification, faillite personnelle, and imprisonment of the controlling director.
Transfer pricing documentation non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to an arm's length profit, recharacterization of the excess as a hidden distribution subject to 25% withholding, penalty of 5% of the reassessed tax base (minimum EUR 10,000), and 0.20% monthly late interest; Country-by-Country Report failures add penalties up to EUR 100,000.

Director personal liability: The signing gérant faces personal recovery proceedings, civil damages claims by shareholders, and criminal exposure if documentation concealment is qualified as fraud; corporate veil may be pierced to reach family assets.
