Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

A French SARL has no statutory minimum paid-up capital; statutes may fix it freely (e.g. €1,000). Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked opening escrow account (compte bloqué) at a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and released only after RCS registration or on formal refusal. Contributions in services are prohibited; special valuation controls apply to in-kind contributions exceeding €30,000 or representing more than half of share capital.

Funding & unlock path: For retail premises open to the public (ERP 5th type M), a fire-safety inspection by the Commission de sécurité is required before opening; the business must hold a favorable accessibility conformity certificate and maintain worksite structural load certificates. Employees require a General Occupational Health and Safety (DUERP) risk assessment and a medical fitness declaration for the store manager under French Labour Code.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a commercial SARL is automatically liable to corporate income tax (impôt sur les societes, IS) under CGI Art. 206(1) on worldwide profits; standard IS rate 25% with a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for small enterprises meeting turnover and profit conditions (CGI Art. 219). Territorial scope is modified by French territoriality: profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment may be excluded from the French tax base if supported by the relevant treaty and the Art. 209-A mechanism. No pass-through status applies by default; partnership taxation (transparence fiscale) is available only upon statutory election. The VAT small-business franchise is an indirect tax relief only and does not alter the IS classification.

Available election forms

1) Election for partnership/income-tax treatment under CGI Art. 239 bis AA (family SARL, SARL de famille) where all shares are held by relatives; election is made jointly by all shareholders and is irrevocable for 5 years. 2) Election under CGI Art. 239 bis AB allowing a small SARL (<=50 employees, annual turnover <=EUR 10 million, professionally managed and capital substantially held by natural persons) to be taxed as a pass-through entity at income tax rates for 5 years. 3) Reverse election under CGI Art. 206(3): a societe de personnes may elect entity-level corporate income tax, irrevocable unless statutory conditions are met. 4) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under CGI Art. 256 C allowing closely linked French group entities to file as a single taxable person. 5) France has no US-style check-the-box; hybrid mismatch consequences are addressed via ATAD I/II provisions including CGI Art. 212 bis, requiring documentation and self-adjustment for deduction/ non-inclusion mismatches and asymmetric branch structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established SARL there is no VAT registration threshold: registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction upon incorporation. The small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA, CGI Art. 293 B) applies only below annual net turnover of EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for services (effective 1 January 2025 pursuant to Ordonnance n°2024-1312 implementing Directive 2020/285/EU, indexed annually; previously EUR 91,900 / EUR 47,700). For goods sold remotely to consumers in other EU member states, the EU-wide distance-sales threshold is EUR 10,000 (exclusive of VAT); once exceeded, VAT must be accounted for at the destination member state's rate, typically through the One-Stop-Shop (OSS), which is optional below the threshold. Non-resident sellers without French establishment and non-EU sellers have no exemption and must register from the first euro unless using OSS/IOSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods to a destination outside the European Union qualify for 0% TVA under CGI Art. 262-I provided the goods physically leave EU customs territory within 90 days of the supply (CGI Art. 269-3) and the vendor holds the customs export declaration (EX/MRN) together with transport and payment evidence. Intra-Community supplies to VAT-registered EU businesses qualify as exempt under CGI Art. 262-II when the buyer's EU VAT number is obtained, the goods are dispatched to another member state, and the recapitulative statement (DEB/EC Sales List) is filed on time; for EU B2C distance sales, destination taxation through OSS is required above the EUR 10,000 threshold. Supplies of offshore services to non-EU business customers are generally outside the scope of French VAT (CGI Art. 259-0); services physically rendered on IoT hardware located in France at the time of performance (installation, repairs, configuration) remain French-vatable under CGI Art. 259-A(2) unless the service is directly linked to the export of the goods, in which case 0% may be claimed with equivalent documentary substantiation. Unsupported zero-rating carries full liability for 20% French VAT, penalties and interest.

  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

  • Commercial invoice, packing list, transport contract (CMR/AWB/Bill of Lading), buyer's EU VAT ID for intra-Community supplies, DEB/EC Sales List filing acknowledgment, and proof of exchange repatriation or equivalent banking evidence

France does not apply a Latin-American real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping model; it mandates structured e-invoicing and e-reporting through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF/FNFE) network. Under the French e-invoicing reform (Ordonnance 2021-1190, Decret n°2022-1299, as amended): from 1 September 2026, large enterprises and ETIs must issue and receive structured B2B e-invoices, with all VAT-registered entities required to receive e-invoices; from 1 September 2027, SMEs/micro-enterprises must issue e-invoices. Invoices must use Factur-X, UBL or CII formats and be exchanged through DGFiP-immatriculated dematerialization platforms (PDPs) or directly via the PPF using REST/JSON APIs; invoice data must be transmitted within one business day of issuance, and e-reporting data (B2C sales and B2B international transactions) is transmitted monthly with payment data reconciled to acquirer records. Retail smart-home IoT sales via Stripe, Shopify Payments and other PSPs will be matched to B2C e-reporting; no government fiscal stamp is affixed, but the integrity chain requires qualified electronic signatures/seals and a 6-year retention period (Code de Commerce Art. L. 123-22).

DGFiP reconciles export zero-rating claims against customs DELTA-X MRN data, DEB/VIES sales lists, e-reporting files and bank remittance records. A failure to produce customs proof of exit within 90 days, or the absence of inward FX repatriation within the 180-day deadline, triggers recharacterization of the transaction as a French taxable retail or domestic supply, with assessment of 20% TVA plus restitution of any previously credited export VAT. Rectification follows the Art. L. 57 LPF procedure (proposition de rectification with a 30-day response period) within the standard 3-year statute of limitation (Art. L. 169 LPF). Penalties: CGI Art. 1729 imposes a 40% increase for intentional inaccuracy and 80% for abusive manoeuvres or simulated transactions; late-payment interest accrues at 0.20% monthly under CGI Art. 1727; aggravated tax fraud under CGI Art. 1741 carries up to 5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine (up to 7 years/EUR 1,000,000 for aggravated circumstances involving laundering or organized fraud). False customs documentation additionally triggers criminal Customs Code penalties (Art. 414 et seq., up to EUR 450,000 and imprisonment), publication of sanctions, and clawback via the abuse-of-right procedure (Art. L. 64 LPF).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a euro-area member, maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility of the euro for current-account transactions (trade, services, income, dividends, interest, royalties) under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Codes of Liberalisation. Capital-account operations, including equity injections, shareholder loans, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds, are unrestricted for non-resident investors in a French SARL, subject to the mandatory statistical balance-of-payments reporting to Banque de France for transactions at or above EUR 50,000 per counterparty (direct investment declarations under Article L.151-2 CMF). Profit remittance via dividends is permitted without exchange-control authorisation; the only applicable hurdles are French withholding tax under domestic law (25%) potentially reduced to 15% or 0% under double-tax treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, and the standard corporate documentation burden (shareholders' meeting minutes, audited accounts, tax clearance). No material convertibility or transfer risk exists: the euro is freely usable, and the SARL may hold multicurrency accounts without restriction. For the target cross-border IoT hardware model, the practical constraint is not legal convertibility but bank-side compliance documentation for large CNY and USD settlements originating from and paid to non-resident counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio in France. A French resident SARL may freely retain foreign-currency export receipts received from EU and third-country customers on authorised intermediary bank accounts, subject to three cumulative obligations: (i) the underlying receivables must be repatriated to a French or EEA-based banking account within normal commercial terms, with no statutory conversion deadline; (ii) each individual cross-border transaction equal to or exceeding EUR 50,000 must be reported to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes via the bank's eco-system or direct DG Trésor filing; and (iii) offshore accumulation of export proceeds is permissible only where there is a bona fide treasury, investment, or settlement rationale, and the funds remain attributable to the SARL in the French tax net. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively zero percent: the treasury policy may elect to convert USD and CNY export receipts into euro, retain them for future trade disbursements in the invoice currency, or hedge the exposure via permitted forward instruments. For this smart-home IoT retail model, the recommended policy is to convert net euro treasury requirements monthly and hedge the residual USD/CNY exposure.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French Central Bank), acting in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the French Ministry of Economy, Finance, and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; the regime is codified under the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF), EU Regulation No 575/2013 (CRR), Directive 2014/65/EU (MiFID II), and Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions and PSD2 payment institutions holding a French agrément or a valid EU passport (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Penta, Revolut Business EU, Lydia/SumUp), which may open operational accounts fully remotely and offer multicurrency IBANs, virtual card issuance, and API treasury connectivity. Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for offshore non-EEA EMIs (UK FCA-only companies operating post-Brexit, US, Hong Kong, or MENA licences): these cannot provide principal French IBANs and may be treated as unregulated deposit-taking vehicles under French banking monopoly rules, creating legal exposure for the SARL under Article L.511-1 CMF. A compliant structure is a primary operating account at a tier-1 French bank plus an EEA-passported EMI for high-volume Stripe/Shopify settlement sweeps; all balances must remain subject to French accounting and the Banque de France statistical framework. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injections from non-resident shareholders must be effected through compliant SEPA Credit Transfer (for EUR) or SWIFT MT103 (for non-EUR currencies) into the SARL's designated IBAN at a tier-1 French bank. The remittance information field (Field 70) must openly state the purpose as 'Contribution au capital – SARL [name]' and should be accompanied by a bank-level purpose code aligned with the ECB Balance of Payments framework, typically B320 (direct investment capital contribution) or, for shareholder loans, B321. For direct investment statistics, Banque de France requires the non-resident parent to file a direct investment declaration whenever the capital contribution causes the parent's ownership to reach or exceed 10% of the SARL's share capital (Article L.151-2 CMF); this declaration is statistical and does not constitute exchange-control authorisation. The verification bundle to be retained by the SARL includes: the share subscription deed, the notary or gérant's certificate of paid-in capital, the bank statement showing credit of funds, the SWIFT purpose code confirmation, and a copy of the updated KBis extract. Where the injection includes non-EUR currency, the arrangement should specify the conversion benchmark rate and, to minimise future tax and treasury complexity, effect the conversion at the receiving bank at the ECB daily euro reference rate on the value date.Outbound trade disbursements to suppliers of smart-home IoT hardware (primarily Chinese manufacturers) require dual-track clearing rails: (i) for shipments below EUR 50,000 or strategic suppliers with open-account terms, the preferred rail is SWIFT MT103 with a compliant 'remittance information' field referencing the invoice number and customs EORI of the SARL, settled in USD or EUR via the French bank's correspondent banking network or via a Chinese renminbi-clearing bank to avoid bilateral clearing constraints; (ii) for high-value or initial trial containers, a commercial documentary Letter of Credit under UCP600 (ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits) issued by the French bank in favour of the Chinese beneficiary, with Incoterms 2020 rules (FOB or CIF) and customs-aligned invoice values, provides security against shipment risk. The SARL must also use the SEPA instant credit transfer rail for European sub-suppliers and logistics providers. All disbursements require strict dual-signature mandates and a monthly treasury reconciliation against Stripe and Shopify settlement reports. The target clearing currency mix for procurement is approximately 65% EUR, 30% USD, and 5% CNY; hedged as detailed below, with settlement timing aligned to the supplier's 30/60/90-day credit terms.Outright FX Forwards (contrats de change à terme) with a French or EEA-authorised credit institution or AMF-licensed investment firm, hedging EUR/USD and EUR/CNY exposures arising from trade payables and expected export receipts, with tenors of up to 12 months and strict underlying-trade documentation to avoid speculative characterisation under French banking law.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on offshore CNY (CNH) referencing the onshore CNH fix, executed through an international or French AMF-licensed broker, mirroring the hedge of the CNY-denominated procurement cycle where deliverable CNY onshore instruments are not administratively available.Cross-Currency Swaps (change de devises) matching EUR/USD and EUR/CNY procurement and settlement cycles, including periodic exchange of floating or fixed-rate cashflows, provided the underlying liability is evidenced by supplier invoices and the swap is booked under an ISDA or French master agreement with a licensed financial institution.Vanilla FX Options (options de change) permitting the purchase of USD or CNY at a hedged strike while retaining the benefit of favourable market movements, executed solely for hedge accounting alignment with the commercial invoice exposure, never for speculative proprietary trading.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High for EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions and PSD2 payment institutions holding a French agrément or a valid EU passport (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Penta, Revolut Business EU, Lydia/SumUp), which may open operational accounts fully remotely and offer multicurrency IBANs, virtual card issuance, and API treasury connectivity. Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for offshore non-EEA EMIs (UK FCA-only companies operating post-Brexit, US, Hong Kong, or MENA licences): these cannot provide principal French IBANs and may be treated as unregulated deposit-taking vehicles under French banking monopoly rules, creating legal exposure for the SARL under Article L.511-1 CMF. A compliant structure is a primary operating account at a tier-1 French bank plus an EEA-passported EMI for high-volume Stripe/Shopify settlement sweeps; all balances must remain subject to French accounting and the Banque de France statistical framework. The SARL must also ensure the EMI is in the AMF blacklist-free environment and satisfies the 'Droit au compte' (right to account) fallback mechanism established by Article L.312-1 CMF.
