Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SARL must have a genuine registered office in France under Articles L.123-11 and following of the Commercial Code. Commercial domiciliation through an approved domiciliation company is generally permissible if the provider holds the required authorization and the written domiciliation agreement satisfies statutory requirements. A manager's personal residence may be used subject to applicable residential, lease, condominium, planning, and notification rules. A virtual office that supplies only mail forwarding without lawful domiciliation, access to corporate records, and reliable contact capability is insufficient. For trade-finance factoring, reverse factoring, escrow, and cross-border payment activity, a virtual office is commercially and prudentially high risk because the activity requires demonstrable management, compliance, accounting, transaction-monitoring, records, and operational capacity in France. The SARL form alone does not authorize factoring or payment services. Factoring generally requires operation through an authorized credit institution or an appropriately authorized société de financement under the Monetary and Financial Code; payment services and escrow structures may separately require payment-institution or credit-institution authorization. Prior ACPR and legal-perimeter analysis is mandatory.

Name reservation / lock period

France does not generally provide a statutory company-name reservation period of 30 days through the company registry. A name may be used only after incorporation and registration, and preliminary clearance is not a binding reservation. Conduct INPI, RNE, RCS, trademark, domain, and adverse-name searches before filing. If the filing is rejected or a deficiency notice is issued, obtain the written reason, correct the denomination or supporting evidence, and resubmit through the Guichet unique within the stated cure period. For a formal decision of the competent registry or administrative authority, use the available administrative or judicial remedy identified in the decision, generally including a prior administrative complaint where appropriate and an appeal before the competent commercial court or administrative court. A rejection should not be treated as final legal confirmation of third-party rights; separate trademark or unfair-competition exposure must be assessed.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum share capital for an SARL under the Commercial Code; the amount is fixed in the Statuts. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and at least one-fifth paid on incorporation (balance within five years); in-kind contributions are fully paid-up at subscription and verified by a statutory auditor. Given the regulated factoring activity, the ACPR/CRR minimum initial capital threshold (EUR 5,000,000 for credit institutions) must be met; all cash subscriptions must be held on a blocked escrow deposit account in a regulated French bank or with the Caisse des Dépôts until registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the departmental fire and rescue service (SDIS) or the commission de sécurité for ERP-classified premises under Articles R.123-1 et seq. of the Construction and Housing Code; structural load inspection certificate for the registered office; electrical installation conformity certificate (Consuel) for new or modified electrical circuits; periodic workplace health and safety clearance by DREETS/CARSAT covering workstation ergonomics, emergency lighting and exits, first-aid equipment, fire drills, and the mandatory risk-assessment register (DUERP).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), operating through the enterprise tax portal impots.gouv.fr; VAT registration for non-established businesses is handled by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and the Direction des Non-Résidents for non-EU entities.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first register economically in France: for an établissement stable or permanent physical presence, file at the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce to obtain a SIREN and SIRET; then complete the enterprise tax registration process through the DGFiP to obtain a French VAT number. A non-EU entity without a fixed establishment must appoint an individual or legal entity authorized to act as its VAT representative (représentant fiscal) with DGFiP approval. EU-established entities may obtain direct VAT registration by filing Form 3551 (Cerfa 15717) or equivalent electronic filing. The VAT number is issued in the format FR + 11-digit SIREN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is by default subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). It may elect, for a maximum period of five fiscal years, to be taxed in the name of its partners under individual income tax (option for impôt sur le revenu) provided it meets the small-business conditions of Article 239 bis AA of the Code Général des Impôts. For VAT, entities can form a single taxable person (assujetti unique) under Article 256 C CGI, and corporate groups may elect fiscal integration (régime d'intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI where the parent holds at least 95% of the subsidiary.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general registration threshold for non-French suppliers making taxable supplies in France; any foreign supplier without EU establishment becomes liable to French VAT on the first taxable transaction. For EU distance sales of goods (B2C), the annual pan-EU distance threshold of EUR 10,000 applies (with the option to voluntarily register). For B2B supplies, the reverse-charge mechanism generally applies where the French customer is VAT-registered, but non-established sellers must register for French VAT when reverse charge is unavailable or for certain defined services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 262 CGI, zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to supplies of goods dispatched or transported by or on behalf of the supplier to a destination outside the European Union, provided the exporter retains proof of physical exportation. Services supplied to non-EU customers, including factoring and receivables-financing transactions, are generally outside the scope of French VAT or exempt, not zero-rated under the export provisions. Financial and factoring transactions are standardly VAT-exempt under Article 261 C of the CGI and Article 135 of the EU VAT Directive, and therefore no French VAT is chargeable; such supplies are often subject to reverse charge or no VAT, but this is not technically an export zero-rating relief.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU - Déclaration Administrative Unique or Single Administrative Document)
  • Proof of export / dispatch from French customs territory (exit confirmation from the Customs Automated Clearance System)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill (transport documentation confirming the goods have left the EU)
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence confirming payment was received from the foreign buyer

France has implemented a phased mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting regime. All taxable persons established in France must be able to receive structured electronic invoices by 1 September 2026, and must issue e-invoices via a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the public portal Chorus Pro by the applicable phase (large enterprises by 1 September 2026; SMEs and micro-enterprises by 1 September 2027). Real-time clearance is achieved through XML invoice data transmission (Factur-X / UBL / CII), with mandatory digital reporting data fields, but no crypto-stamping or continuous real-time government pre-clearance is currently implemented in France.

Where a French exporter zero-rates supplies without valid documentary proof of export, the DGFiP may retroactively reclassify the supplies as taxable in France, reassess 20% VAT, and apply late-payment interest (intérêts de retard) of 0.2% per month. If a false declaration is made with bad faith or fraud, a fine of 40% of the tax at stake (or 80% for deliberate fraud) may be applied under Articles 1728, 1729 and 1731 of the CGI. Criminal penalties under Article 1741 CGI may also be assessed in severe cases. For factoring and supply-chain finance transactions, the DGFiP additionally scrutinizes whether the operation is a true assignment of receivables versus a disguised financing arrangement; if recharacterized as a loan, the VAT exemption remains but corporate tax and documentary withholding consequences may arise. Exporters must secure and retain all substantiating customs and banking documentation for at least six years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully liberalised and convertible foreign-exchange regime. As a Eurosystem member, the euro is freely convertible on both current and capital accounts under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Capital Liberalisation Code; no French exchange-control authorisation, quantitative limitation, lock-up or prior licensing of outward transfers applies to transactions between residents and non-residents. The operative distinction is not convertibility but reporting and purpose-coding discipline: (i) current account transactions, including cross-border factoring settlements, trade disbursements, service fees and profit remittances, are unrestricted and require only correct classification for the mandatory cross-border transfer declaration; (ii) capital account operations, such as equity injection, shareholder loans and acquisition or disposal of participations, are equally free but require the bank to maintain documentation evidencing the economic substance of the flow; and (iii) any transfer of funds exceeding EUR 12,500 per calendar month to or from a non-EEA country must be declared to the Banque de France by the credit institution under Article R. 152-3 of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier, CMF), using the DG Trésor purpose codes (motif de l'opération). Profit remittance hurdles for a French SARL are procedural rather than regulatory: distribution requires (a) audited or accountant-certified financial statements demonstrating distributable profits, (b) a shareholders' meeting distribution resolution, (c) satisfaction of the French 25% withholding tax for third-country parents subject to treaty reduction (e.g., 10% under the France-China treaty, 0% or 15% under other DTTs), with the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive eliminating withholding for EEA corporate parents, and (d) bank-level verification of the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) chain before release. The only material constraint for a factoring treasury is the statutory prohibition of financing a receivable in a manner equivalent to loan-back structuring and the requirement to maintain factoring collection accounts (comptes de recouvrement) separately from the operational account, as governed by the Dailly Law (articles L. 313-23 et seq. CMF).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender regime exists in France. The former export domiciliation and mandatory repatriation system was abolished by the Loi de modernisation de l'économie (LME) of August 2008; exporters, factoring companies and trade financiers may therefore retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds in foreign-currency accounts (comptes en devises) at authorised banks, or convert to euro at freely negotiated market rates without any statutory ratio, and no time limit for repatriation applies. The only residual obligations are statistical: (i) the SARL's French credit institution must report each reportable cross-border settlement above EUR 12,500 to the Banque de France with the appropriate DG Trésor purpose code and, where available, the underlying commercial transaction reference; (ii) the SARL itself is subject to a direct balance-of-payments reporting duty if its aggregate cross-border transfers exceed EUR 750,000 per annum or if the Banque de France selects it for the quarterly trade-in-factoring survey; and (iii) for non-EEA export receivables purchased by the SARL, no proportion of proceeds must be converted to euro, but the entity must preserve the original invoice, the transport and delivery evidence, the assignment bordereau (bordereau de cession de créances professionnelles) and the notice to the account debtor so that the reporting bank can file a complete and compliant statistical record. These rules are consistent with EU Regulation No 2021/379 on balance-of-payments statistics, which leaves the eurozone without any capital-control restriction on export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), the central bank of the French Republic and a member of the Eurosystem, acting in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty as the competent exchange-control and balance-of-payments statistics authority, and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the integrated licensing, AML/CFT and banking supervision authority for the French financial sector.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility is LOW for primary treasury, escrow and regulated collection rails, and MEDIUM for ancillary operating-treasury functions. Legal constraints are defined by the PSD2 regime transposed into the CMF (articles L. 521-1 to L. 525-15): a payment institution or electronic money institution (EMI) may not carry out factoring or any lending activity, because the purchase of trade receivables with financing features constitutes a credit operation reserved to ACPR-licensed credit institutions or sociétés de financement under article L. 511-1 and L. 313-1 CMF. Post-Brexit, UK-licensed EMIs such as Revolut and Wise no longer enjoy EU passporting and cannot open a core banking account for a French ACPR-supervised factoring entity unless they establish a French branch through the ACPR; French-regulated EMIs and payment institutions such as Qonto, Shine, Lemonway and Mangopay may lawfully host operating expenses, VAT flows and collections on behalf of the SARL, but they cannot serve as (i) the statutory capital escrow account, (ii) the factoring clients collection account where segregated funds of the SARL's clients are held, or (iii) the SWIFT-linked cash concentration account through which bank refinancing and client disbursements flow. The treasury blueprint should therefore use fintech EMIs only as a sub-ledger and payments-clearing aid for low-risk operating expenditure, while all regulated liquidity, factoring settlements and cross-border clearing must remain with full-service ACPR-regulated credit institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is LOW for primary treasury, escrow and regulated collection rails, and MEDIUM for ancillary operating-treasury functions. Legal constraints are defined by the PSD2 regime transposed into the CMF (articles L. 521-1 to L. 525-15): a payment institution or electronic money institution (EMI) may not carry out factoring or any lending activity, because the purchase of trade receivables with financing features constitutes a credit operation reserved to ACPR-licensed credit institutions or sociétés de financement under article L. 511-1 and L. 313-1 CMF. Post-Brexit, UK-licensed EMIs such as Revolut and Wise no longer enjoy EU passporting and cannot open a core banking account for a French ACPR-supervised factoring entity unless they establish a French branch through the ACPR; French-regulated EMIs and payment institutions such as Qonto, Shine, Lemonway and Mangopay may lawfully host operating expenses, VAT flows and collections on behalf of the SARL, but they cannot serve as (i) the statutory capital escrow account, (ii) the factoring clients collection account where segregated funds of the SARL's clients are held, or (iii) the SWIFT-linked cash concentration account through which bank refinancing and client disbursements flow. The treasury blueprint should therefore use fintech EMIs only as a sub-ledger and payments-clearing aid for low-risk operating expenditure, while all regulated liquidity, factoring settlements and cross-border clearing must remain with full-service ACPR-regulated credit institutions.

25.00% standard rate; 15.00% reduced rate on the first EUR 42,500 of annual taxable profit for qualifying SMEs (turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, ownership satisfying Article 219 CGI requirements).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File obligations apply when the French entity or its consolidated group has gross annual turnover or total balance-sheet assets exceeding EUR 400,000,000; below this threshold, smaller entities must nevertheless file Form 2257 (annual intercompany transactions declaration) where related-party flows exceed EUR 1,000,000 or where the entity owns 5% or more of another taxable entity.

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute of the type found in offshore jurisdictions, but economic substance is enforced through Article 57 CGI (arm's length and actual performance), OST (Office of Transfer Pricing) guidelines, ATAD anti-abuse rules as transposed into French law (e.g., Article 2034 CGI), and the principal purpose test in French tax treaties. A factoring SARL must maintain real operational substance in France, including staff, premises, management decisions, accounting records, and a commercial purpose for its factoring activities; an artificial letter-box factoring vehicle will be recharacterized, denied treaty benefits, and subjected to 80% fraud penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Additional tax penalty of 80% (40% in bad faith cases) plus late-payment interest; criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years; for organized tax fraud, fine increased to EUR 3,000,000 and imprisonment up to seven years; confiscation of assets and publication of the judgment.

Director personal liability: The gérant (manager) may be criminally prosecuted as the principal actor; the corporate veil is pierced where the entity was used as a mere instrument to commit fraud (abus de biens sociaux); personal assets may be seized, travel bans imposed, and a 15-year disqualification from managing a business may apply.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of 5% of the amount of undocumented transactions, with a minimum of EUR 10,000 under Article 1735, 4° CGI; reassessment of the profits with default interest; an additional 40% bad-faith penalty may be imposed on the reassessed tax if the omission is deliberate.

Director personal liability: No direct automatic imprisonment, but civil liability for faute de gestion may be invoked when the manager’s failure to maintain documentation causes recovery of the group’s tax liabilities to fail; administrative injunctions and restrictions on the manager’s business activities may be issued.
Profit Repatriation / Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full withheld amount plus late-payment interest and a 40% bad-faith penalty (80% if deliberate concealment is established); the unpaid withholding tax becomes a personal legal obligation of the responsible director where the enterprise lacks sufficient assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid withholding taxes under the regime of personal responsibility for failure to perform statutory collection duties; criminal characterization as tax fraud can lead to five years imprisonment, restitution orders, travel bans, and confiscation of personal property.
AML/CFT and Unlicensed Factoring

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties for exercising banking or finance activities without the required license: imprisonment up to five years and fine up to EUR 7,500,000; ACPR sanctioning committee may impose administrative fines up to EUR 100 million or 10% of global turnover; withdrawal of any license in force.

Director personal liability: Directors face automatic disqualification from managing a regulated financial institution; personal criminal conviction for money laundering (up to 10 years imprisonment); seizure of personal assets; and in cross-border cases, travel bans and extradition requests may be used.
