Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL has no statutory minimum capital and may be formed with as little as EUR 1; under Article L.223-7 of the Code de commerce, cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% at incorporation, with the balance due within five years. Funds are wired into a blocked company-in-formation account and released only after RCS registration upon presentation of the Kbis.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: if the registered office is open to clients, it qualifies as an Établissement Recevant du Public (ERP) of the 5th category, requiring a building safety assessment and periodic commission de sécurité visits with an attestation of fire compliance; purely administrative staff offices must satisfy the Code du travail fire and evacuation rules. Electrical: a Consuel conformity certificate is required for any new electrical installation. Occupational health and safety: a document unique d'évaluation des risques (DUERP) must be prepared and updated; the workplace must pass the preventive inspection requirements practised by Carsat and the Inspection du Travail where staff are employed, including ergonomic, emergency-exit, and shared-office checks.

Paid-in capital rule: The SARL has no statutory minimum capital and may be formed with as little as EUR 1; under Article L.223-7 of the Code de commerce, cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% at incorporation, with the balance due within five years. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), operating through the Impôts.gouv.fr portal and the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

Foreign-established entities must file an economic activity registration through the Guichet Unique; INSEE issues the SIREN/SIRET identifier. For VAT purposes, DGFiP issues a French intra-Community VAT number (FR+11 digits) based on the registration dossier. Non-EU entities without a French permanent establishment must appoint an approved individual tax representative for VAT registration and compliance; EU entities may register directly.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign-established entities must file an economic activity registration through the Guichet Unique; INSEE issues the SIREN/SIRET identifier. For VAT purposes, DGFiP issues a French intra-Community VAT number (FR+11 digits) based on the registration dossier. Non-EU entities without a French permanent establishment must appoint an approved individual tax representative for VAT registration and compliance; EU entities may register directly.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity classification election exists in France. A SARL may elect pass-through income tax treatment under CGI Article 239 if it has fewer than 50 individual members and is a family-type or closely held SARL; otherwise it is subject to corporate income tax (IS). French groups may elect tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI to offset taxable results within a 95%-owned subgroup. France applies anti-hybrid rules implementing ATAD; no hybrid mismatch election is available for outward payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 36,800 annual taxable turnover for service providers (or EUR 91,900 for goods traders) under the VAT franchise thresholds; however, a French SARL engaged in outsourcing services becomes a taxable person from its first taxable transaction if it supplies services to foreign recipients or exercises an opt-in. For non-established suppliers, no registration threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported physically outside the EU may be zero-rated (0% VAT with input VAT deduction) provided the exporter proves removal from EU territory within 90 days and holds transport/export documentation per CGI Article 74 of Annex III. Services supplied by a French SARL to a business customer established outside the EU for 'offshore' or B2B services are outside the scope of French VAT under Article 259-1 2° CGI, and are not subject to French VAT; this outcome requires proof of the customer's foreign taxable status. B2C e-services to non-EU consumers are also outside French VAT when the consumer's residence is outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/EX1) verified by French customs
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill) proving physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and contract of sale
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing foreign currency settlement

Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing is phased in under Ordinance 2021-1190: large enterprises from 1 September 2026, VAT taxable persons including SMEs/VSEs from 1 September 2027. Invoices must circulate in structured electronic format through platforms ('plateformes de dématérialisation partenaires') connected to the Public Billing Portal (PPF) API, with transaction data reported to DGFiP for clearance. B2C cross-border supplies require e-reporting. Mandatory 'digital stamping' is not used; B2G invoices continue via Chorus Pro with API clearance.

If export zero-rating is abused or substantiation fails, DGFiP recovers the undeclared VAT, applies late interest, and imposes fines: 40% of understatements for deliberate non-disclosure, 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres, and up to 10% in the case of use of legal avoidance schemes ('abus de droit'), together with possible criminal penalties for tax evasion (Article 1741 CGI) and joint liability of the tax representative.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current and capital-account transactions within the EU/EEA without prior exchange-control authorization; France has abolished the dual FX market, exchange quotas, and general authorization requirements. Residual convertibility constraints are limited to (i) EU sanctions regimes, (ii) counter-measures against FATF/CTRI non-cooperative jurisdictions under CMF Article L.561-2, and (iii) statistical surveillance: any transfer from or to a non-resident exceeding EUR 15,000 must be declared to the DG Trésor via the credit institution on the Déclaration de Change (DR/FC) form. Profit remittance by a French SARL is unrestricted at FX level; however, dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to withholding tax at 12.8% under the Prélèvement Forfaitaire Unique, reduced to 0% for EU corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for more than 24 months pursuant to the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU, and further reduced by applicable double-tax treaties for most non-EU jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no statutory conversion ratio for export receipts since the 1990 liberalisation and the abolition of the obligation de cession. Two residual obligations apply to a French SARL: (i) repatriation — export receivables must be collected within the contractual payment maturity and credited either to a euro account or to a certificated foreign-currency account (compte de devises) with a French or EU-authorised credit institution; (ii) reporting — any receipt from a non-resident exceeding EUR 15,000 triggers a balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical declaration by the intermediary under CMF Articles R.152-1 and R.152-4. Non-collection beyond 30 days following the agreed settlement date is typically treated by tax and customs auditors as a presumptive indicator of export-invoicing or transfer-pricing irregularities.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Recovery, under the Code monétaire et financier (CMF) Articles L.151-1 et seq. and Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High viability as a settlement overlay, but not as the primary treasury anchor. Qonto and Shine are ACPR-authorised payment institutions in France; Wise Business, Revolut Business, and Payoneer operate as EU/UK EMIs passporting into France; Stripe provides card acquiring and funds-settlement infrastructure. Legal constraints: EMIs are not credit institutions, they cannot take deposits in the prudential sense, they cannot grant credit without a separate credit-institution licence, and e-money balances are not guaranteed by the French deposit guarantee fund (FGDR). Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders may fund the SARL through a capital increase (augmentation de capital) or a shareholder current account (apport en compte courant d'associés). The compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the investing entity to the SARL's IBAN in France with ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPC' (Capital Increase) or 'FINP' (Financing of an Undertaking), quoting the SARL's SIREN and the legal title of the operation; the credit institution attaches the Banque de France BOP motive code 'Investissement direct - apport de fonds' (IV01) to the DR/FC declaration. For any direct investment in France by a non-EU resident exceeding EUR 15,000, the beneficiary must file a Déclaration d'Investissement Direct to the Banque de France (via Telerec) within 20 business days. The corporate-law dossier supporting the credit must include the duly signed minutes (PV d'assemblée générale) registering the capital increase, the subscription certificate, and the bank attestation of deposit or blockage of funds; if the inbound wire lacks a matching legal event, the funds will be re-characterised by French tax authorities as a deemed shareholder loan with potential interest-computation consequences.For a virtual CFO entity, outbound clearing is predominantly services-based: (1) EU/EEA suppliers and subcontractors are settled via SEPA Credit Transfer (instant or one-day) using IBAN-to-IBAN with purpose codes 'SERI' or 'SUPP'; (2) non-EUR obligations (USD, GBP, CHF software subscriptions, foreign contractors) are executed via SWIFT MT103 with an MT202 cover, quoting the invoice numbers and ISO 20022 purpose code 'SURN' (Services), accompanied by a compliant economic-substance narrative to prevent false-positive sanctions hits; (3) if the scope later includes physical goods, high-value orders should use Documentary Collections (D/P at sight) or an irrevocable confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP 600 issued by the tier-1 French bank; (4) FX conversion between euro and foreign currency is transacted at the bank's dealing desk at spot or a negotiated margin, with confirmation on SWIFT MT300; the multicurrency ledger must record both the original currency and the euro-equivalent value in accordance with the accounting standards applicable to an ISIC 6920 activities.FX Forward (change à terme ferme) on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CHF — committed OTC contracts with the tier-1 bank up to 12-month maturity, documented against the SARL's sales invoices and order backlog.Vanilla FX Options (options de change) on EUR/USD — purchased or collared, booked under the ISDA 2002 or the French FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) Master Agreement, with premiums disclosed in the corporate tax file.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) — permitted solely for covered exposures in non-deliverable currencies (e.g., HKD/EUR) with an investment-grade bank, contract settlement referencing a published ECB or WM/Reuters spot rate, no physical delivery, and statistical reporting to the DG Trésor.Cross-Currency Swap (swap de change) — EUR/USD swap converting a USD capital injection or USD revenue stream into fixed-term euro cashflows over a 1-to-3-year horizon, used for financing software capex or acquiring a European subcontractor; subject to EMIR reporting and the FBF master- agreement framework.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High viability as a settlement overlay, but not as the primary treasury anchor. Qonto and Shine are ACPR-authorised payment institutions in France; Wise Business, Revolut Business, and Payoneer operate as EU/UK EMIs passporting into France; Stripe provides card acquiring and funds-settlement infrastructure. Legal constraints: EMIs are not credit institutions, they cannot take deposits in the prudential sense, they cannot grant credit without a separate credit-institution licence, and e-money balances are not guaranteed by the French deposit guarantee fund (FGDR). For an ISIC 6920 virtual CFO business, use the fintech rail for client-fee collection and inexpensive subcontractor disbursements, while maintaining a tier-1 French bank account as the substance-holding and escrow anchor; high-risk non-EU corridors (US LLCs, HKD entities) should be processed through the tier-1 bank because inbound transfers from EMI aggregators trigger enhanced due diligence in subsequent transactions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Entity threshold: annual gross receipts exceeding EUR 400 million OR total balance sheet assets exceeding EUR 400 million; CbC filing requires group consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million. Below threshold, the SARL must still maintain contemporaneous arm's length evidence upon French tax administration request.

Economic substance scope: No French offshore ESR; however, substance-over-form (prééminence de la réalité sur l'apparence) and the abuse-of-law doctrine (Art. L.64 LPF) apply. A SARL performing fractional CFO and accounting services must demonstrate real staff, premises, contracts, and decision-making in France. Absent substance, the administration will disregard the entity, disallow cross-border fees and apply 80% penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraud

Statutory sanction: Up to €500,000 fine and 5 years' imprisonment (€3,000,000 and 7 years if aggravated); civil penalty of 40% (Art. 1729) or 80% (Art. 1729-0 A) on evaded CIT/VAT; loss of tax credits and late-payment interest

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for tax debt (Art. 1745 CGI); personal bankruptcy (faillite personnelle) where the company was a sham; court may impose up to 15 years ban on managing French companies and a travel ban.
Fictitious SARL / shell entity

Statutory sanction: Dissolution of the SARL under Art. 1844-7 French Civil Code; tax recharacterisation and 80% penalty; penal exposure for false declarations of accounts (up to €15,000 for natural persons / €75,000 for legal persons)

Director personal liability: Judicial piercing of the corporate veil due to asset commingling (confusion des patrimoines) or bad management (faute de gestion); directors liable for company debts without limitation (action en comblement du passif); travel bans and imprisonment possible for misuse of corporate assets (abus de biens sociaux: up to 5 years and €375,000).
AML and beneficial ownership deficiency

Statutory sanction: ACPR administrative fines up to €5,000,000 (or 5% of turnover) for AML compliance failures; criminal money laundering offense under Art. 324-1 French Criminal Code: up to 5 years' imprisonment and €375,000 fine (10 years/€750,000 if habitual or organised)

Director personal liability: Directors may be charged as accomplices if they knew or should have known of laundering; civil confiscation of bank account balances; travel prohibition; personal criminal liability; professional revocation of the accounting firm and disqualification of the directors from carrying on regulated accounting activities.
