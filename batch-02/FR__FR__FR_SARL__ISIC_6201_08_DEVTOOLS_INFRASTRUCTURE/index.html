Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), with registration data recorded in the Registre national des entreprises and, where applicable, the Registre du commerce et des sociétés

Name reservation / lock period

France does not provide a general statutory company-name reservation procedure with a universal 30-day reservation period. Name clearance is an availability and prior-rights exercise rather than a binding reservation. Before filing, conduct searches through INPI, the Registre national des entreprises, trademark databases, domain-name databases, and relevant commercial databases. If the Guichet unique or registry rejects the filing, identify whether the objection concerns legal form, misleading wording, regulated terminology, prior rights, public order, incomplete documents, or technical filing defects. Correct and resubmit through the Guichet unique where permitted. Request written reasons and preserve the filing record. A registry or formalities decision may be challenged through the competent commercial court or other legally competent court, depending on the decision and procedural basis; trademark disputes may require proceedings before the competent judicial court or an opposition before INPI. There is no general administrative appeal that guarantees acceptance of a rejected company name. A revised name is ordinarily the fastest risk-control solution.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS), maintained by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce; electronic filing intake is through the INPI Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 through L.223-53 and R.223-1 through R.223-26, governing the formation, management, capital, and dissolution of a Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL).

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for a SARL; the articles may set capital at €1. For cash contributions, at least 20% of each subscriber's contribution must be paid up at incorporation, and the balance must be settled within five years. The paid-up amount must be deposited in a blocked account with a bank or notary, or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and released to the company only after RCS registration.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) or Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) for non-resident businesses. Principal online portal: impots.gouv.fr. Company formation and business registration data are filed through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI); the INPI process allocates the SIREN and SIRET identifiers, while DGFiP administers tax registration and VAT identification.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered through the INPI Guichet unique. Following registration, the company receives a SIREN identifier for the legal entity and a SIRET identifier for each establishment. The DGFiP assigns or confirms the corporate tax account and the French VAT identification number, generally formatted as FR followed by a two-digit VAT key and the nine-digit SIREN. The company must open and maintain its professional account on impots.gouv.fr, select the applicable corporate income tax and VAT obligations, and provide constitutional documents, registered-office evidence, identity and authority documents for the legal representative, bank details, and business-activity information when requested. A foreign entity without a French establishment that is required to register for French VAT applies to the SIEE and submits evidence of foreign establishment, commercial activity, French taxable transactions, representative authority, and VAT status in its home jurisdiction. An EU-established business generally does not need a French fiscal representative; a non-EU business normally requires a French fiscal representative for VAT compliance unless an applicable statutory exemption applies. VAT registration is not automatically required where a business validly uses the French VAT franchise regime or where the reverse charge applies, but cross-border transactions may create registration or reporting obligations independently of the domestic turnover threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is normally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Eligible SARLs may elect for income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu), generally subject to statutory conditions, including qualifying ownership and activity requirements and a maximum election period of five financial years for the ordinary temporary regime. A family SARL may elect for income-tax treatment without the ordinary five-year limitation if the statutory family-company conditions are satisfied. France has no general US-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implementing ATAD 2 can deny deductions, require inclusion, or otherwise neutralize mismatches involving hybrid entities, instruments, or permanent establishments; these rules are not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the French domestic VAT franchise regime applicable to services and non-commercial professional activities, the principal turnover threshold is EUR 37,500 for the preceding calendar year, with a current-year tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250, subject to statutory exclusions, sector-specific rules, and legislative changes. The thresholds do not eliminate VAT registration or reporting obligations for transactions for which VAT is due under a special place-of-supply rule, including certain cross-border B2B services. The principal goods threshold is EUR 85,000 for the preceding year, with a current-year tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500. A business may voluntarily opt for VAT liability and may be required to register despite turnover below a franchise threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption at the French seller level, functionally equivalent to zero-rating, where the exporter can prove that the goods left the EU customs territory and satisfy the applicable export and customs conditions. Required evidence normally includes the electronic export declaration, Movement Reference Number (MRN), customs exit confirmation, transport documentation, commercial invoice, and proof linking the goods to the export transaction. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for exemption where the customer has a valid VAT identification number in another EU Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier retains the required transport and customer-status evidence. Services supplied to a business established outside France are generally governed by the B2B place-of-supply rule and may be outside the scope of French VAT rather than technically zero-rated; the supplier must retain customer-location, contractual, invoicing, and payment evidence. Digital and cloud services require analysis of customer status, establishment, place of supply, and any specific electronically supplied-services rule.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent proof that the goods exited the European Union
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, VAT treatment, and transaction reference
  • Transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, courier record, or carrier delivery evidence
  • Packing list and, where relevant, export authorization, preference, or origin documentation
  • Evidence linking the customs export record and transport evidence to the relevant sales invoice
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification validation and transport evidence to the destination Member State
  • For offshore B2B services: customer establishment and business-status evidence, contract, service records, invoice, and evidence supporting the applicable place-of-supply treatment

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through approved Partner Dematerialization Platforms (Plateformes de dématérialisation partenaires, or PDPs). From 1 September 2026, all French-established businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; large businesses and entities in the intermediate-size category generally become subject to mandatory issuance from that date. Small and medium-sized businesses and micro-enterprises generally become subject to mandatory issuance from 1 September 2027. The regime covers domestic B2B transactions subject to French VAT and requires e-reporting for specified cross-border and business-to-consumer transactions. Invoices must use required structured or structured-plus-image formats and contain mandatory tax data. France does not generally operate a universal pre-issuance real-time clearance system requiring each invoice to receive a tax-authority approval stamp; transmission occurs through the approved platform architecture, with data forwarded to DGFiP. Public-sector invoicing continues to use the Chorus Pro framework where applicable.

France has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. French VAT exemption or zero-rating must be supported by contemporaneous evidence of the applicable export, intra-Community, or place-of-supply conditions. DGFiP may assess unpaid VAT retrospectively, together with late-payment interest and tax penalties, where the exemption is unsupported, incorrectly applied, or connected with fraudulent conduct. The standard penalty framework may include a 5% penalty for certain late or deficient VAT payment situations, a 40% penalty for deliberate failure, an 80% penalty for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, and potentially a 100% penalty in cases involving opposition to a tax audit, subject to statutory conditions and procedural rights. Input VAT refunds may be denied or clawed back, and previously claimed deductions may be reassessed. Customs infringements can additionally result in recovery of duties and taxes, customs penalties, seizure, and criminal or administrative sanctions. Intentional false invoicing, fabricated export evidence, or VAT fraud may trigger criminal proceedings and enhanced sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; France maintains no residual exchange-control authorization for current or capital transactions. Current account convertibility is unrestricted, and capital account transactions (forming an SARL, capital contributions, dividend distributions, loan repayments, intercompany treasury flows) are liberalized. Profit remittance constraints are tax-driven, not FX-driven: dividends paid by a French SARL to non-resident shareholders are subject to French withholding tax of 25% (or reduced tax-treaty rate upon presentation of a French tax residence certificate), but the remittance itself is free. Cross-border flows above de minimis thresholds are subject to statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque de France, executed by the resident payment institution on behalf of the company; no prior approval is needed for a non-resident to own shares in the SARL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or compulsory conversion of export receipts exists for French residents. Export proceeds invoiced in foreign currency may be retained in foreign-currency current accounts at French authorized intermediaries. Under Articles L151-1 to L151-4 and R151-1 et seq. of the Code monétaire et financier, resident exporters must channel export receivables through an authorized French bank (rapatriement) within the payment terms agreed in the commercial contract (subject to French/EU statutory payment deadlines applicable to B2B trade), and provide the intermediary with sufficient information (invoice, nature of transaction, counterparty) to enable the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistics. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio or time limit other than contractual terms. Penalties apply only for deliberate non-reporting or use of unauthorized foreign accounts.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) — Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier), in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor; EU Treaty freedom of capital movement under TFEU Articles 63-65; ACPR (Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority) supervises banks.
