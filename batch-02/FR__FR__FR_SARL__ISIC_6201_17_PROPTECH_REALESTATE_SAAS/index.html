Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), acting through the competent local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); official operator portals: impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel), ProGres for VAT formalities and filings, plus guichet-entreprises.fr / INPI as the central company registration gateway (RCS / Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce). Intra-Community VAT number validation is performed via the EU VIES system.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL obtains its INSEE SIREN and SIRET identifiers automatically upon completion of the corporate formation filing with the RCS through the guichet-entreprises.fr single window. The DGFiP subsequently issues the intra-Community VAT number (n° de TVA intracommunautaire) in the format FRxxYYYYYYYYY, where xx is the two-digit control key and YYYYYYYYY is the nine-digit SIREN. A non-resident entity with a French taxable nexus (e.g., supply of e-services to French final consumers, a French permanent establishment, or French real-estate assets) must register directly with the competent SIE using CERFA form No. 1390-CA, or electronically through ProGres; non-EU suppliers without an EU establishment must appoint an accredited French tax representative (représentant fiscal). For pure intra-EU distance sales of SaaS to final consumers, the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) portal may be used from the member state of establishment, avoiding separate French VAT registration while the annual EU-wide €10,000 distance-sales threshold is not exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no US-style check-the-box regime. Relevant elections and statutory mechanisms: (1) CGI Art. 239 bis AB — a qualifying SARL (all members being natural persons, fewer than 50 employees, annual consolidated turnover below €10 million) may elect, for a maximum of five fiscal years, to be fiscally transparent and taxed at member level under the personal income tax (Impôt sur le Revenu) regime instead of corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés); (2) CGI Art. 223 A — election for the French tax-integration group regime (intégration fiscale), available where a French parent holds at least 95% of the SARL, allowing consolidation of results and neutralization of intragroup transactions; (3) default entity-level taxation at the 25% standard IS rate, with the reduced 15% rate applicable to the first €42,500 of taxable profit of small enterprises (turnover below €10 million). Hybrid mismatch outcomes under the ATAD transposition are neutralized mandatorily in France; no elective hybrid or check-the-box deficiency mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic franchise en base de TVA thresholds applicable to a French-established SARL: €93,500 annual turnover for supplies of goods and €37,500 for supplies of services (including SaaS and electronically supplied services) as of 1 January 2025; below the applicable threshold the franchise-in-base exemption may apply. Non-established foreign suppliers, whether EU or non-EU, have no registration threshold: French VAT registration is due from the first taxable transaction performed in France. For intra-EU distance sales and e-services to final consumers, the EU-wide €10,000 annual threshold determines recourse to the OSS scheme instead of domestic VAT registration in each destination state.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Pure SaaS and electronically supplied services: (1) B2B supplies to customers established outside France fall outside the scope of French VAT under the place-of-supply rule (CGI Art. 259-1; EU VAT Directive Art. 44), resulting in a de facto 0% French VAT charge with reverse-charge accounting at destination; (2) B2C supplies to non-EU consumers are not subject to French VAT; (3) B2C supplies to consumers in other EU member states are taxed at the destination member state's VAT rate via the OSS portal once the annual EU-wide €10,000 distance-sales threshold is exceeded, while below that threshold domestic French VAT at 20% applies; (4) where tangible goods are exported together with the SaaS (e.g., hardware kiosks, IoT edge devices), zero-rating under CGI Art. 262-I applies provided the goods are physically shipped or dispatched outside the EU within 90 days and the export is documented per the Union Customs Code implementing rules. Input VAT incurred in France (cloud hosting, marketing, professional fees, real-estate-related costs) remains recoverable through the French CA3 VAT return or the cross-border EU VAT refund procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/ENS lodged via the DELTA customs system) — required only where tangible goods are exported
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Air Waybill proving physical dispatch outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT bank credit confirmation evidencing collection and receipt of export consideration
  • Signed SaaS master agreement, statement of work, and export invoice annotated with reverse-charge or 0% VAT treatment
  • Customer VAT identification number and VIES/AC validation certificate for foreign B2B customers
  • Service-performance evidence (application logs, hosting usage reports, delivery and go-live certificates) substantiating provision of the electronic service
  • Proof of customer establishment abroad (certificate of incorporation or tax residence certificate) demonstrating place of supply outside France

Mandatory structured e-invoicing and e-reporting under CGI Art. 289 bis, phased as follows: from 1 September 2026 all taxable persons established in France must be capable of receiving e-invoices, and large and mid-size undertakings must issue e-invoices; from 1 February 2027 small and micro-enterprises, including the typical SARL, must issue e-invoices. Invoices must be issued in an approved structured format (Factur-X, UBL, CII) and transmitted through a certified dematerialization platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire — PDP) or the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation — PPF), which relays invoice data and e-reporting information (B2B transactions, B2C flows, and payment data) to DGFiP. France does not operate a tax-authority pre-clearance or digital-stamping system; the obligation is a near-real-time data transmission and fiscal-perimeter control regime administered through the PPF.

DGFiP verifies export support files, customer identity, and actual repatriation of proceeds; failure to substantiate the export, the customer's foreign establishment, or the collection of funds gives DGFiP the right to claw back all previously recovered input VAT and to re-impose French VAT on the supply, plus late-payment interest (intérêt de retard of 0.20% per month) and penalties: 40% for intentional inaccuracy (manquement délibéré) or 80% for fraud, fictitious exports, or undeclared activity (CGI Arts. 1729, 1730 and 1731). The ordinary reassessment period is three years (LPF Art. L.169), extended to ten years for undisclosed or occult activities and coordinated international fraud. French customs may additionally impose fines for false export declarations, and criminal prosecution for fiscal fraud (escroquerie fiscale) is possible in aggravated invoicing schemes. The French foreign-exchange benchmark requires repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days of the export or contractual payment due date (a contractual term of up to one year may be agreed), and non-repatriation may be treated as a customs and currency offense precipitating immediate VAT re-characterization.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a founding Eurozone member and maintains full convertibility for the euro pursuant to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (Article 63). Both current-account transactions (trade in services, SaaS subscriptions, property-management fees) and capital-account operations (direct equity injection, dividend distributions, loan repayments) are free of quantitative exchange control. Profit remittance from a French SARL to non-resident shareholders is permissible without prior authorisation, subject to ordinary corporate formalities (annual accounts, AGM approval of dividend distribution), the withholding tax provisions of Article 119 bis of the French General Tax Code, and anti-money-laundering verification. The only practical hurdles arise from documentary compliance—such as proof of corporate authorisation, BO registration, and, where the beneficial owner is located in a FATF-gray-listed jurisdiction, enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. French exchange-control regulations abolished mandatory surrender or repatriation of export proceeds for both euro and foreign currency transactions. A French exporter may retain balances in foreign currency accounts, whether held with a French resident bank or an non-resident financial institution, subject to the general obligation for all residents to report cross-border transactions to the Banque de France for monetary-statistical purposes. Specifically, when individual cross-border payments exceed EUR 750,000, a declaration to the Banque de France via the dedicated OFA platform is required for the balance-of-payments compilation; this does not constitute permission or exchange-control approval. For VAT and corporate income tax purposes, the business must maintain a clear audit trail linking export invoices to bank receipts, typically provided by a SWIFT MT103 received report and monthly treasury reconciliation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating as the national central bank and exercising delegated exchange-control and external financial relations authority on behalf of the French Ministry of Economy, Finance, and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty (Direction Générale du Trésor). The legal framework is codified under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier), together with EU-level capital-movement rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High as a complementary collection and payout layer, Medium as the sole clearing rail. French-licensed and EEA passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Stripe Financial Services (Ireland/Netherlands branch), Checkout.com (France), and Lemonway (France) are viable for primary subscription collection, cross-border payout, and embedded PropTech payments. The main legal constraint is the PSD2 safeguarding rule: client funds held by an EMI must be segregated in a protected account at a regulated credit institution or invested in secure liquid assets. The EMI itself cannot operate as a full treasury account, cannot provide lending or cash-pooling, and must be subject to the SARL's contractual terms for merchant-of-record vs. payment-facilitator model. For a low-risk, non-licensed PropTech platform, using an EMI for daily receivables and a domestic commercial bank for capital operations and settlement balance is the optimal tiered banking structure. The French ACPR applies conduct-of-business supervision; the SARL must complete the EMI's risk on-boarding and, when operating a trading platform (real-estate sales), may also need to comply with the Order of 2019 on crowdfunding if payment flows are related to real-estate transactions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership opacity - ultimate natural-person shareholders are not fully exposed in the Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs (RBE) or the RBE filing is older than 30 days.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity - ultimate natural-person shareholders are not fully exposed in the Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs (RBE) or the RBE filing is older than 30 days.

Defense evidence: Provide the original RBE extract issued by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce within the last 30 days, the current K-bis extract, the notarised shareholder register (registre de titres), a signed organisational chart with ultimate beneficial ownership percentages, plus all identification documents of each natural-person BO. If the BO is resident in a high-risk jurisdiction, add a FATF-compliant source-of-wealth affidavit and audited financial statements.
Source-of-funds uncertainty for initial capital injection - equity financing from a non-resident investor or parent routed through a third-country bank.

Root cause: Source-of-funds uncertainty for initial capital injection - equity financing from a non-resident investor or parent routed through a third-country bank.

Defense evidence: Supply a legally executed capital injection resolution, constitution of the block funds, investor profile form with passport/passport-copy, bank statements from the remitting account (last 3 months), opening bank evidence of the parent's legitimate business, and an explanation of the economic rationale for the transaction. In case of a loan along with equity, add a signed loan agreement (mandatory for remittance), interest-rate calculation, and evidence of overall Solvency/equity-limits compliance.
Correspondent-banking red flag due to the 'unusual' combination of a PropTech SaaS (ISIC 6201) with the NAICS 531390 real-estate characteristics - banks see potential facilitation of undisclosed real-estate transactions.

Root cause: Correspondent-banking red flag due to the 'unusual' combination of a PropTech SaaS (ISIC 6201) with the NAICS 531390 real-estate characteristics - banks see potential facilitation of undisclosed real-estate transactions.

Defense evidence: Present a business-model description, client-facing terms of service, SaaS-scope statement (ISIC 6201 primary), platform AML surveillance assessments, annual revenue split between pure SaaS and real-estate property-management support, evidence that the company does not hold client monies for sale proceeds, and a detailed policy on acceptance of real-estate partners. A supporting legal opinion from a French avocat may accelerate onboarding.
Use of third-party payment processors (Stripe, ACH/Direct) for revenue concentration, triggering a bank's sub-merchant due-diligence review.

Root cause: Use of third-party payment processors (Stripe, ACH/Direct) for revenue concentration, triggering a bank's sub-merchant due-diligence review.

Defense evidence: Provide executed agreements with processors, processing-statements showing monthly volumes, client-acquisition records, merchant IDs, and a compliance certificate establishing processor-based KYC/AML. Banks generally accept this if the aggregate flow through the processor does not exceed 40% of total annual revenue and a reconciliation ledger is in place.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders shall route investments via SWIFT MT103 using an official EU purpose code identifying the underlying transaction. For capital increases, the recommended code is 'P011 - Domestic transfer from/to abroad (capital contribution)' or, as used by French banks, 'FCAP' in the transaction narrative. The wire must reference the Quick ERN (European Registration Number) of the SARL and the K-bis identification number to enable the bank's automatic reconciliation. The account for inward remittances is the dedicated Capital Escrow Account. If the aggregate cross-border investment (including loans) reaches the French statistical threshold (EUR 15 million annual investment for external trade) or if the SARL is in a sensitive sector (defence etc. - not applicable here), a post-closing declaration to the Economy and Finance Ministry may apply. For any transfer equal to or exceeding EUR 10,000, a documentary anti-money-laundering declaration is embedded in the bank's payment process. A compliance file (purpose, identity, BO) must be pre-submitted to the receiving bank before the wire is awaited.Clearing rails for outbound trade settlements are as follows: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (instant or standard) for euro payments to EU beneficiaries, with beneficiary identification by IBAN and reason codes associated with invoiced SaaS subscription fees; (ii) SWIFT MT103/103+ for non-EEA transfers in foreign currency, including USD and GBP, using highly structured purpose codes; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credit, UCP600) for any real-property-linked cross-border transaction that requires title-vs-payment nexus, using a French issuing bank and a conforming presentation set; and (iv) ACH Direct for US-dollar disbursements, but only where the bank has a US partner and the SARL maintains a US account, considered an ancillary rail. For all outbound payments, the French Tax Administration requires a voucher trail: supplier contract, invoice, verification of economic reality, and, where applicable, an annual report of payments to non-resident beneficiaries exceeding EUR 1,200 (Article 242 ter of the CGI) - the 'Fichier des comptes étrangers.' The SARL must also verify through the EU sanctions lists that the beneficiary is not designated in OFAC/EU annexations.Outright foreign-exchange forward contracts (Match Principles: EUR/USD or EUR/GBP) transacted with a French licensed credit institution for trade receivables/settlement up to 12 months maturity.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for exotic or restricted currencies, but strictly as hedging of actual underlying commercial flows and with a licensed counterparty in a jurisdiction where NDF markets are permitted (not for EUR-domestic).Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) only for material debt in a currency other than EUR, transacted via the bank's derivatives desk and documented under ISDA Master Agreement with French governing-law protocol.Currency options (European-style) executed on regulated markets or via an authorised investment firm, limited to 12-month tenors and aligned to forecast paid invoices.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: High as a complementary collection and payout layer, Medium as the sole clearing rail. French-licensed and EEA passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Stripe Financial Services (Ireland/Netherlands branch), Checkout.com (France), and Lemonway (France) are viable for primary subscription collection, cross-border payout, and embedded PropTech payments. The main legal constraint is the PSD2 safeguarding rule: client funds held by an EMI must be segregated in a protected account at a regulated credit institution or invested in secure liquid assets. The EMI itself cannot operate as a full treasury account, cannot provide lending or cash-pooling, and must be subject to the SARL's contractual terms for merchant-of-record vs. payment-facilitator model. For a low-risk, non-licensed PropTech platform, using an EMI for daily receivables and a domestic commercial bank for capital operations and settlement balance is the optimal tiered banking structure. The French ACPR applies conduct-of-business supervision; the SARL must complete the EMI's risk on-boarding and, when operating a trading platform (real-estate sales), may also need to comply with the Order of 2019 on crowdfunding if payment flows are related to real-estate transactions.
