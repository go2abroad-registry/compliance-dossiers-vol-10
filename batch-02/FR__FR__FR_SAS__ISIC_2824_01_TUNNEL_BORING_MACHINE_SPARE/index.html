Name reservation / lock period

France does not generally provide a separate statutory company-name reservation period comparable to a 30-day reservation regime. A proposed name is tested during the incorporation filing and is not protected merely by an informal search. A rejection does not itself create a general statutory appeal period; the deadline in the notice and applicable procedural law control.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique; official taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; business formalities single window: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (guichet unique); customs clearance and export declarations: douane.gouv.fr (DELTA/GUNI electronic filing). Large international groups are assigned to the Direction des Grandes Entreprises (DGE) in Paris.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is constituted by executing statutory articles of association, appointing a President (and, where the President is a non-resident alien, appointing a French tax representative), and filing the incorporation dossier online at the guichet unique before commencement of activity. The registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS) entry causes INSEE to allocate the 9-digit SIREN and 14-digit SIRET; the DGFiP then attributes the 13-character TVA intracommunautaire number (FR + 11 digits built from the SIREN), verifiable on the EU VIES system. The single registration simultaneously opens the corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) file, designates the local centre de contrôle fiscal, and satisfies social and statistical filings. A foreign non-resident entity establishing a French branch or subsidiary without a SIREN must first register through a designated representative; a French SAS requires no separate VAT permit.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification is determined by commercial law and the CGI. Available statutory elections include: (i) intégration fiscale (CGI arts. 223 A to 223 U) permitting a French parent holding at least 95% of the SAS to elect a five-year tax consolidation; (ii) VAT grouping as an assujetti unique (CGI art. 256 C bis) for entities with close financial, economic and organisational links; (iii) waiver of the small-business VAT exemption franchise (CGI art. 293 B) to enter the normal VAT deduction and charging mechanism; (iv) election of the régime simplifié d'imposition (simplified actual basis) for VAT and IS instalments; (v) election to account for VAT on cash receipts (encaissements) rather than on invoice issuance (CGI art. 269); (vi) for a French head office, renunciation of the territoriality exemption to elect worldwide taxation; and (vii) unilateral advance pricing arrangements for transfer pricing. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD as transposed in CGI art. 212 bis apply automatically without elective check-the-box relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Any supplier established in France making taxable supplies of goods must register for VAT from its first taxable transaction; the small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA, CGI art. 293 B) applies only where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 91,900 for sales of goods or EUR 36,800 for services (2025 limits), with tolerance ceilings of EUR 101,000 / EUR 42,400 and reference ceilings of EUR 79,000 / EUR 33,400 for the preceding year. A manufacturing SAS engaged in TBM spare part production is expected to register ab initio, given projected turnover; non-established foreign suppliers have no threshold and must register from the first French taxable transaction, subject to the OSS rules for intra-Community distance sales of goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the European Union are exempt from TVA at 0% under CGI art. 262-I-1° (b) where the goods are physically removed from the EU by or on behalf of the seller, evidenced by a customs export declaration and transport documentation; the goods must normally leave EU customs territory within three months of the supply (extension available on duly justified grounds). Services directly connected with exported goods, including international transport, are exempt under CGI art. 262-II-1° and IV. Intra-Community supplies to a purchaser holding a valid EU VAT registration number are exempt under CGI art. 262-I-1° (a) with a compliant EC sales list (DEB). For TBM spare parts sold under project-financing or letter-of-credit payment rails to non-EU contractors, zero-rating is available where the goods are physically exported, the customs declaration is discharged, and the commercial documentation (contract, invoice, Incoterms, payment instrument) is consistent. Input VAT directly and proportionally attributable to the zero-rated exports is fully recoverable via the CA3 VAT return; excess credits are refunded under the ordinary periodic or annual refund procedure without separate export-refund filing.

  • Customs export declaration (DAU/DEX) lodged in the DELTA/GUNI electronic customs system and discharged by the office of exit from EU customs territory.
  • Bill of Lading (sea), CMR consignment note (road/rail) or Air Waybill (air), together with destination or arrival evidence located outside the EU.
  • Commercial invoice (facture) and sale contract/work order identifying the TBM spare parts, value, Incoterms and purchasing entity.
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit advice issued by a credit institution established in France evidencing the collection of proceeds under the letter of credit or project-financing drawdown.
  • For intra-EU alternative supplies: EC sales list (état récapitulatif) and VIES validation of the customer's VAT number.

Mandatory French B2B e-invoicing applies under the 2024 Finance Act timetable: from 1 September 2026 all VAT-registered suppliers, including the SAS, must be able to receive structured electronic invoices, and large and mid-size enterprises (ETI) must issue them; from 1 September 2027 SMEs and micro-enterprises must also issue them. Invoices must be exchanged through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified dematerialisation partner (PDP) that transmits structured data to DGFiP. Accepted formats are Factur-X, UBL 2.1 and CII (Factur-X is mandatory for public-sector transactions). The model is 'proactive transmission': the invoice is delivered directly to the purchaser without prior fiscal authorisation, stamping or real-time clearance, and the invoice data and e-reporting payment data reach DGFiP within statutory deadlines (generally no later than the monthly e-reporting cycle and without delay for the invoice flow). Export sales outside the EU are evidenced and transmitted through the customs export declaration rather than the domestic e-invoicing platform; the underlying commercial invoice must still satisfy CGI arts. 242 nonies and annexe III.

The export zero-rating is provisional. The exporter must retain all supporting documentation for at least six years (CGI art. L. 102 B, Livre des Procédures Fiscales); DGFiP may reassess VAT at 20% if the physical export or documentary evidence is insufficient, with late-payment interest accruing at 0.20% per month. Material penalties on the assessed VAT are: 40% for deliberate inaccuracy (manquement délibéré, CGI art. 1729 a), 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres such as fictitious exports, round-tripping, carousel schemes or abuse of law (CGI art. 1729 b), and 100% for opposition to tax control (CGI art. 1730). Erroneous credits recovered may be clawed back together with a denial of the right to deduct where the claim files are not substantiated. Criminal exposure under CGI art. 1741 reaches up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, or seven years and EUR 3,000,000 for organised-group fraud, in addition to professional disqualification orders. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days also breaches the French Monetary and Financial Code (arts. L. 524-1 and R. 522-1), exposing the exporter to administrative fines and customs-currency penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member enjoying full capital-account convertibility under the ECB's free movement of capital regime. There are no surrender or conversion requirements for trade proceeds. Profit remittance abroad by non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, provided the funds are backed by lawful audited earnings and the payment is reported for statistical and tax-streaming purposes. A foreign-owned entity may credit dividends or repay intercompany debt to non-residents without administrative licence, but a prior filing with the Direction Générale du Trésor is mandatory for any direct investment or liquidation of investment regardless of amount. For inbound financial flows tied to share capital or shareholder loans exceeding €15,000, a Form CERFA No. 14325 or the Banque de France's C3 online declaration must be filed within 20 business days to satisfy balance-of-payments statistical reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory timeline for surrendering export proceeds into euros, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. Exporters may retain receivables in foreign currency on a standard current-payment account. The only obligation is the monthly or quarterly reporting of all exports and import payments to the Banque de France via the C3/DTS software, with a reporting threshold of €30,000 per transaction for cross-border flows. Late filing or mis-reporting subjects the company to fines under Article L.165-3 of the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (in close coordination with the European Central Bank and the Direction Générale du Trésor, which retains sovereign authority over foreign-exchange policy and capital-flow surveillance under France's balance-of-payments framework).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legal eligibility for a French SAS to hold treasury funds with an authorised European Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or Payment Institution (PI) is generally Medium, subject to strict limits. Such entities may be used for ancillary payment flows and currency conversion but are not permissible as the sole liquidity clearing rail because they lack the banking licence required for overdraft, standby LC issuance, and capital-escrow instruments. Moreover, a foreign EMI operating in France must hold an ACPR licence or an EU passport with a dedicated French branch registration; otherwise, corporate funds parked with an unlicensed EMI face serious AML and sanctions compliance exposure.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying small or medium-sized company may apply 15% to its first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for a 12-month period, with 25% applying above that amount. The reduced rate is generally subject to turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up share capital, and at least 75% individual ownership or qualifying individual ownership through companies meeting the statutory conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic withholding rate on dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident is generally 25%, subject to exemptions and applicable tax treaties. The domestic rate for a non-resident individual may differ under the applicable statutory provisions. A qualifying EU/EEA parent company may obtain an exemption under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally subject to a qualifying direct holding of at least 10%, a minimum holding period or undertaking, qualifying legal form and taxation, and anti-abuse conditions. Treaty rates vary by recipient, ownership, and treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: French full transfer-pricing documentation is generally required where annual turnover or gross assets are at least EUR 400 million, or where the entity is directly or indirectly majority-owned or controls an entity meeting that threshold, subject to statutory conditions. The documentation includes a Master File and Local File, generally made available to the tax authorities on request. A separate annual simplified transfer-pricing return, Form 2257-SD, generally applies to entities meeting the EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold or the relevant group-relationship conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Thresholds and scope must be checked for the relevant year.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a general regime styled as Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for all companies. French tax law nevertheless requires genuine business purpose and substance in relevant contexts and includes anti-abuse, beneficial-ownership, permanent-establishment, transfer-pricing, controlled-foreign-company, anti-hybrid, and anti-treaty-shopping rules. A French SAS should maintain appropriate personnel, premises, decision-making, books, contracts, and operational evidence consistent with its functions, assets, risks, and reported profits. Substance and documentation are particularly important for intragroup financing, intellectual property, procurement, and management services.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax returns

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, and penalties that may reach 80% in fraud cases; criminal tax-fraud penalties may include imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with higher penalties possible in specified aggravated cases, including fines linked to proceeds or evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors or officers who personally participate in or authorize fraud may face criminal prosecution and personal fines or imprisonment. A court may impose management prohibitions. Personal recovery of tax debts is not automatic; it may arise under statutory conditions, including fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated non-compliance, and requires the applicable legal procedure.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and concealed profit transfer

Statutory sanction: Taxable-profit adjustments, withholding or other tax reassessments where applicable, late-payment interest, and penalties for deliberate or fraudulent conduct. Failure to produce required documentation can result in a specific penalty generally equal to the greater of EUR 10,000 or 0.5% of the transactions concerned, subject to a statutory cap and applicable rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal criminal or civil exposure if they knowingly organize fraud or make false statements. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments do not by themselves pierce the corporate veil or create automatic personal liability.
Failure to withhold or remit tax on outbound payments

Statutory sanction: The French payer may be assessed for unwithheld tax, interest, and applicable penalties. Liability and penalty levels depend on the payment type, the failure, and whether it was deliberate; treaty relief does not apply without satisfying its substantive and procedural conditions.

Director personal liability: The company is ordinarily the withholding debtor. A director may face personal exposure where statutory conditions for personal liability, criminal participation, or fraudulent conduct are met; no automatic veil piercing follows solely from a withholding error.
Shell entity, sham transactions, or abuse of law

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may disregard or recharacterize the arrangement, deny benefits, assess tax and interest, and apply the statutory abuse-of-law or anti-abuse penalty, which may be 80% or 40% depending on the taxpayer's role and the applicable provision.

Director personal liability: Personal civil or criminal liability requires the director's own legally relevant conduct or satisfaction of a specific statutory recovery rule. A shell-company finding alone does not automatically impose personal liability or imprisonment.
Money laundering and terrorist-financing violations

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is a criminal offense punishable, in general, by up to five years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 375,000; aggravated circumstances can increase penalties. Separate sanctions may apply for breaches of anti-money-laundering obligations by regulated professionals.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate may face individual prosecution, imprisonment, fines, and confiscation. A SAS manufacturing business is not automatically subject to all regulated-sector AML customer-due-diligence duties merely because it uses project financing or letters of credit.
Failure to file or pay corporate tax

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharges, late-payment surcharge and interest, reassessment, and potentially increased penalties after formal notice or where deliberate conduct is established.

Director personal liability: The SAS remains primarily liable. Personal liability may arise under applicable tax-collection rules for fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated non-compliance, or under separate criminal or insolvency-law provisions; travel bans and imprisonment are not automatic consequences of a late corporate tax filing.
