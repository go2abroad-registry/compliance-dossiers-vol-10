Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered office's department, operating the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); filings are submitted exclusively through the INPI Guichet Unique portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce): Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée regime), Article L.223-2 (free determination of share capital), Article L.223-7 (liberation and deposit of cash contributions), Articles L.210-2 to L.210-10 and R.123-230 et seq. (formation formalities and RCS registration); PACTE Law n°2019-486 and Decree n°2021-300 (Guichet Unique electronic filing).

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'entreprise (official form M0 / CERFA n°210113), accompanied by the signed Statuts, certificate of deposit of funds, legal-announcement certificate, and gérant's appointment documents; registration results in issuance of the Kbis extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for a SARL; founders may fix capital at €1. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation (balance within 5 years), and the corresponding funds must be blocked in a dedicated deposit account (compte bloqué) with a French bank or notaire, released only upon presentation of the RCS registration certificate; capital contributions in kind must be fully transferred at formation.

Funding & unlock path: For office premises leased in an existing building, the landlord's fire safety conformity (5th-category ERP) is verified before signature; any premises open to the public above 100 persons require Commission de Sécurité approval (SDIS/managed by the landlord). As an employer, the SARL must obtain an electrical installation conformity certificate (Code du travail Art. R.4214-1 et seq.), undergo workplace health clearance with the occupational health service (médecine du travail/SST), and sign a plan de prévention with co-tenants for shared-site co-activity risks.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for a SARL; founders may fix capital at €1. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation (balance within 5 years), and the corresponding funds must be blocked in a dedicated deposit account (compte bloqué) with a French bank or notaire, released only upon presentation of the RCS registration certificate; capital contributions in kind must be fully transferred at formation.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

DREALS/DRIEAT (regional environmental directorate, Ministry of Ecological Transition) for environmental oversight; DREETS/DRIEETS for occupational health and safety; ADEME as voluntary supervisory authority for carbon accounting methodologies.

Category: No specific industrial or operational license is mandatory for ISIC 7020 (office-based ESG/carbon consulting and offset brokerage); the RCS registration (Kbis extract) is the sole authorization. Voluntary recognitions include ISO 14064-3 / ISO 14065 validation for audit engagements and ADEME Label Bas-Carbone accreditation for offset project brokerage.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — official portal impots.gouv.fr; business registration via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; non-resident VAT registration through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and the Direction des Non-Résidents.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent must register the French SARL through the Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises (GFE) to obtain SIREN/SIRET and RCS registration. The GFE transmits the file to DGFiP, which automatically issues the French tax identification number (TIN) and, where applicable, the intra-Community VAT number (numéro TVA intracommunautaire). Non-established foreign suppliers without a French entity must file form SI (TVA) for direct VAT registration with DGFiP and may be required to appoint a tax representative; a TIN for income tax and a VAT number are then issued.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (French SARL is treated as a company with legal personality; domestic default is partner-level income tax (IR) under CGI art. 8 unless the SARL elects corporate income tax (IS), in which case entity-level taxation applies. For VAT and international treaty purposes the SARL is an entity-level taxpayer.) — A foreign parent must register the French SARL through the Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises (GFE) to obtain SIREN/SIRET and RCS registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (French SARL is treated as a company with legal personality; domestic default is partner-level income tax (IR) under CGI art. 8 unless the SARL elects corporate income tax (IS), in which case entity-level taxation applies. For VAT and international treaty purposes the SARL is an entity-level taxpayer.)

Available election forms

A SARL may irrevocably elect for corporate income tax (IS) under CGI articles 206(3) and 239; the election is made by the partners, must be notified within the first tax year, and remains binding for five financial years with possible renewal. Family SARLs may continue to be taxed at member level under IR. No US-style 'check-the-box' exists in France; hybrid mismatch alignment is handled through CGI article 205 A and EU ATAD rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established suppliers: no general threshold; any first taxable transaction in France triggers VAT registration. Distance sales of goods/e-services within the EU using OSS: €10,000 EU-wide threshold. Domestic small businesses: annual turnover exemption threshold of €91,900 for supplies of goods and €36,800 for services, with optional taxation; this is an exemption threshold, not a registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from France to destinations outside the EU are taxable at 0% VAT (exemption with credit) under CGI article 262-I-1, provided the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the EU and supported by commercial documentation. Offshore services, including ESG carbon footprint auditing and carbon offset brokerage, are outside the French VAT territorial scope when supplied under the B2B rule to a taxable person established outside France (CGI art. 259-1); such services are not invoiced with French VAT and input VAT may be recovered under normal deduction rules. If the recipient is established in France or the service is connected to French immovable property, French VAT at 20% applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation) with validated Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR transport document proving physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial Invoice and sales contract
  • Bank transfer / inbound remittance certificate or other irrevocable bank proof of foreign payment
  • Export Customs Receipt or proof of the goods' arrival in the third country

France mandates electronic invoicing (facturation électronique) and e-reporting of transaction data to DGFiP. From 1 September 2026, all VAT-registered businesses must receive structured e-invoices (EN 16931 / Peppol format) and transmit e-reporting data, either through the public invoicing portal (Portail Public de Facturation - PPF) or a certified private platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire - PDP). APIS are used for machine-to-machine exchange with the PPF; transmission is periodic, not real-time, and there is no digital stamping or clearance requirement akin to Italy, Spain, or Latin America.

DGFiP scrutinises export zero-rating and offshore service claims through documentary checks and audit procedures. If the export condition or the non-French establishment of the B2B customer is not substantiated, VAT is retroactively re-assessed, input VAT credits are clawed back, and the taxpayer is liable for statutory interest (5% per month) and penalties up to 80% in cases of bad faith (mauvaise foi) and up to 100% for fraud (manœuvres frauduleuses). Criminal fines may also apply for serious evasion, and the six-year ordinary reassessment period applies to missing documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is an EU member state; capital movements and payments are fully liberalised within the EU/EEA under Article 63 TFEU and Articles L.151-1 to L.151-4 of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, CMF). The euro is freely convertible; France maintains no dual-exchange-rate regime, no approval-based FX allocation, and no statutory distinction between current-account and capital-account transactions for ordinary commercial flows. No exchange licence, economic justification, or prior government approval is required for: (a) subscription and payment of share capital of a French SARL by EU or non-EU shareholders; (b) payment of cross-border advisory invoices or trade disbursements; (c) repatriation of profits, dividends, or liquidation proceeds. The only residual restrictions are: (i) EU/UN sanctions and the French embargo rules applicable to sanctioned jurisdictions and designated persons; (ii) screening of foreign direct investment into France in the 15 strategic sectors extended by Decree No. 2019-1590, which does not capture carbon audit/advisory (ISIC 7020) unless the brokerage arm deals in emissions allowances, which would raise financial-regulation rather than FDI-sensitivity issues; (iii) statistical declaration of cross-border flows to the Banque de France under the balance-of-payments reporting framework and CMF Book V AML obligations imposed on banks. Dividend remittance from a French SARL to a non-resident shareholder is tax-filtered only: a 25% statutory withholding tax applies unless reduced by an applicable double-tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; no central-bank or FX-control approval attaches to such remittance. Conclusion: convertibility is unrestricted for the target entity; the only material compliance hurdle is tax withholding and documentary substance, not exchange control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. France imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory holding or repatriation period for foreign-currency export receipts. The SARL may freely retain USD or other currency proceeds in a foreign-currency account at a French or EU bank, may sell them at market rates, or may use them for settlement, without central-bank authorisation. The only obligations are statistical and AML-related: the enterprise must complete the Banque de France balance-of-payments returns when transaction thresholds are reached (particularly for direct investment flows), and each SWIFT/SEPA wire must carry the appropriate 'nature de transaction' purpose code as advised by the receiving bank. For the carbon-offset brokerage element, the binding constraint is not FX surrender but the AMF/MiFID classification risk: acting as a pure administrative broker for physical carbon credits (no own-account dealing in EUAs, no custody of offset proceeds) is a non-financial commercial activity, whereas dealing in EU Allowances as principal would require AMF/MiFID authorisation and would trigger the securities-reporting framework.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (central bank, balance-of-payments and monetary authority), acting with the Direction Générale du Trésor (Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique) as the competent authority for foreign investment screening, and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as banking and KYC supervisory authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for operational cash management, LOW as sole treasury counterparty. ACPR-licensed or EEA-passported electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business EU licence, Wise Business) may lawfully hold EUR and execute SEPA CT/SCT Inst and some SWIFT traffic under PSD2, and are efficient for fee collection, expense disbursement, and low-cost FX. However, they are not acceptable as the depositary institution for the statutory share-capital block under Article L.223-7 of the French Commercial Code (the attestation de dépôt des fonds must come from a bank or notary), may lack a full foreign-correspondent banking network for non-EEA inbound wires, and may not provide the documented Banque de France statistical reporting expected of regulated banks. A non-EEA, offshore-licensed EMI account is generally not viable for the French SARL because it cannot support the French capital-deposit certificate and may raise CRS/FATCA and French tax-residency-evidence complications. Recommended combination: a tier-1 French bank as the anchoring/capital relationship plus a fintech EMI for daily treasury efficiency, with the bank retained for SWIFT-direct global settlement.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Articles of association (objet social) containing 'brokerage', 'offset marketplace', or 'trading in carbon credits' language, which triggers suspicion of unlicensed financial intermediation or MiFID activity in emission allowances.

Root cause: Articles of association (objet social) containing 'brokerage', 'offset marketplace', or 'trading in carbon credits' language, which triggers suspicion of unlicensed financial intermediation or MiFID activity in emission allowances.

Defense evidence: Rectified objet social limited to 'auditing, consulting, engineering advisory and administrative representation in the voluntary carbon-offset market'; a written legal opinion from a French avocat confirming the company never holds client funds, never takes title to carbon instruments, does not operate a payment account, and acts solely under service contracts with registry-verified offset projects; contractual samples explicitly excluding any dealing on own account.
Opaque beneficial ownership: non-resident intermediate holding companies or a gérant whose source of wealth and tax residence cannot be verified.

Sanctioned or high-risk jurisdiction exposure in carbon-offset sourcing, particularly where offset registries or counterparties are located in restricted territories.

Root cause: Advisory-fee receipts structured as 'commissions' on offset trades without commercial substance, which can be misread as layering or as avoidance of MiFID/AMF regulation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for a non-resident shareholder funding the French SARL: (1) Formation-stage escrow: the gérant selects a French tier-1 depositary bank (or notaire) and requests a 'dossier de dépôt de capital'; the bank opens a blocked fiduciary sub-account in the name of the future SARL under Article L.223-7 of the French Commercial Code and R.223-3 ff. CMF, representing the capital to be immobilised prior to registration; (2) The shareholder remits EUR via SWIFT MT103 from its own home/corporate bank, quoting in field 50K the remitting legal entity, in field 57A the French depositary bank and its BIC, in field 59 the blocked sub-account IBAN, and in field 70 the advised purpose text: 'Dépôt du capital social – [SARL name] – Article L.223-7 du Code de commerce – [date]'; the receiving bank instructs the correct Banque de France 'nature de transaction' statistical purpose code for a non-resident direct-equity capital inflow (formation de capital / investissement direct entrant) which must be shown on the MT103 and in the bank's BOP return; (3) The depositary bank issues the 'attestation de dépôt des fonds' evidencing the immobilised amount; (4) Following signature of the articles and legal publicity, the RCS registration at INPI (Kbis) is obtained and the bank releases the funds into the SARL's newly opened operational EUR account; (5) The gérant files the UBO declaration, the bank runs the CMF Book V AML checks, and the SARL records the share capital in its opening balance sheet with supporting bank certificate and SWIFT advice; (6) Where the funding is structured as a shareholder loan, the parties execute a written current-account loan agreement with arm's-length interest, and the flow is booked with a corresponding purpose code for intercompany financing; related-party loan agreements with the gérant require compliance with the 'conventions réglementées' procedure of the SARL governance rules to avoid voidability at registration stage.Clearing rails for the SARL's trade and advisory settlements: (a) EU/EEA supplier payments and refunds: SEPA Credit Transfer (optionally SEPA Instant) using IBAN/BIC under the EPC rulebook, with the VAT reverse-charge accounting file retained for French tax purposes (B2B services); (b) Non-EEA supplier payments, offset-registry settlement, and any non-SEPA disbursement: SWIFT MT103 with documentary support file (invoice, signed contract, delivery/performance proof) and the Banque de France purpose code for commercial service payments; the bank screens each beneficiary against sanctions lists and the SARL should maintain a standing dual-approval treasury matrix; (c) For import of hardware, software, or energy-measurement equipment with meaningful cross-border logistics: commercial letter of credit (UCP600, evidenced by SWIFT MT700) issued/confirmed by a French tier-1 bank, or documentary collection (SWIFT MT400/422) where supplier risk is moderate; (d) Dividends and profit remittances to the non-resident parent: executed via SWIFT MT103 with withholding-tax payment to the French Treasury (Form 2561) and treaty/PDD justification attached to the treasury file; (e) Tax-only disbursements to the French Treasury: SEPA CT referencing the SIRET and tax nature codes; (f) Treasury discipline: all wires matched to approved purchase orders, timely reconciliation against SWIFT FIN acknowledgements, and retention of documentary evidence for the statutory accounting file.Outright EUR/USD (or EUR/XXX) FX forward (couverture de change à terme) with a French ACPR-authorized bank or EEA investment firm — the standard permitted instrument for locking the EUR equivalent of non-EUR advisory receivables; executed under an ISDA 2002/EMIR-templated confirmation with mandatory trade reporting to an EU trade repository (a French registered TR under EMIR Article 9).Plain-vanilla FX swap (swap de change) for rolling management of short-term USD/GBP receivables and payables without creating material counterparty risk; subject to the same EMIR reporting, margining and ACPR/ESMA clearing obligations where the counterparty is in the clearing scope; alternatively executed as two matched outright forwards for simplicity.Cross-currency swap (CCS) for longer-dated multi-year USD-denominated client contracts, fixed-rate to floating-rate or fixed-fixed, matching the SARL's forecast future cash flows; highly restricted to non-financial counterparty threshold and must be cleared through an EU CCP or posted variation margin to a systemic French clearing member; used only where a genuine commercial exposure of substantial duration is documented in the board risk policy.Non-deliverable forward (NDF): generally NOT available or commercially justified for EUR/USD or EUR/CHF because the euro is freely convertible and an outright forward or FX swap in the liquid interbank market fully covers the exposure; French banks typically refuse NDFs on free-currency pairs for non-financial corporate end-users, and any NDF would be an OTC derivative subject to EMIR clearing/margining where in scope, with trade-reporting obligations — the recommended alternative is an outright deliverable forward.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for operational cash management, LOW as sole treasury counterparty. ACPR-licensed or EEA-passported electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business EU licence, Wise Business) may lawfully hold EUR and execute SEPA CT/SCT Inst and some SWIFT traffic under PSD2, and are efficient for fee collection, expense disbursement, and low-cost FX. However, they are not acceptable as the depositary institution for the statutory share-capital block under Article L.223-7 of the French Commercial Code (the attestation de dépôt des fonds must come from a bank or notary), may lack a full foreign-correspondent banking network for non-EEA inbound wires, and may not provide the documented Banque de France statistical reporting expected of regulated banks. A non-EEA, offshore-licensed EMI account is generally not viable for the French SARL because it cannot support the French capital-deposit certificate and may raise CRS/FATCA and French tax-residency-evidence complications. Recommended combination: a tier-1 French bank as the anchoring/capital relationship plus a fintech EMI for daily treasury efficiency, with the bank retained for SWIFT-direct global settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 1 million in related-party transactions (simplified documentation); full Local File and Master File required if group turnover exceeds EUR 400 million or assets exceed EUR 400 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but requires genuine economic substance for tax residency and treaty benefits. Anti-abuse rules (e.g., Article 155 A of the French Tax Code) target artificial conduit arrangements. A company must have real presence, employees, and decision-making in France to avoid being challenged as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (increased to 7 years for aggravated cases); administrative penalty of 80% of understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they participated in fraudulent acts; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty on understated tax (or 80% if deemed abusive), plus interest; potential criminal charges for tax evasion if intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or implement non-arm's length pricing may face personal liability for penalties and interest; in severe cases, criminal prosecution for tax fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, 40% penalty for abuse of law (abus de droit), and potential criminal charges for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly used a shell structure; travel bans and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1 million for individuals and €5 million for legal entities; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable for AML breaches if they knowingly facilitated money laundering; personal fines and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Failure to File Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: 10% penalty if filed within 30 days of deadline; 40% if later; 80% if fraudulent; daily interest of 0.20% per month on unpaid tax; additional fixed penalties for electronic filing non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they intentionally failed to file; in severe cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion.
