Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office service in France is acceptable only in the sense of a shared administrative office with a physical French seat, leased as the registered office (siège social). A pure mail-forwarding arrangement or a mere virtual address with no physical presence is not permitted for a SARL engaged in regulated GDP and pharmaceutical freight activity. The registered office must be a real physical premises in France, and for GDP pharmaceutical freight, a separate operational facility licensed as a wholesale distribution / Good Distribution Practice (GDP) warehouse under applicable pharmacovigilance rules may be required by the French Agence Nationale de Sécurité du Médicament et des Produits de Santé (ANSM). The registered office may be established at the legal representative's residence on a temporary and justified basis for a maximum period of one year, extendable by court order.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

There is no single general industrial licensing board for ordinary freight logistics in France. For medicinal-product wholesale distribution, the competent authority is the French National Agency for Medicines and Health Products Safety (Agence nationale de sécurité du médicament et des produits de santé, ANSM). For road haulage, the competent regional authority is the DREAL (Direction régionale de l'environnement, de l'aménagement et du logement), or the relevant DRIEAT in Île-de-France.

Category: Wholesale distribution of medicinal products requires ANSM authorization to open and operate a pharmaceutical establishment (autorisation d'ouverture d'un établissement pharmaceutique) where the company performs activities falling within the statutory wholesale-distribution regime. A logistics or freight operator that does not buy, sell, or otherwise conduct regulated wholesale distribution is not automatically subject to that authorization, but must meet applicable contractual and GDP requirements. Road carriage for hire or reward may require registration in the road-transport register and a community licence, subject to the applicable vehicle and activity rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible and no exchange-control approval is required for current-account or capital-account transactions. EU free movement of capital under TFEU Articles 63–66 applies, subject only to public-policy safeguarding measures. Profit remittances by an SARL to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted from an FX-convertibility standpoint; the sole practical hurdles are documentary (proof of source of funds and of the corporate resolution authorising distribution) and fiscal (outbound dividend payments are subject to a 30% withholding base comprising 12.8% income tax withholding and 17.2% social levies, reducible under an applicable double-tax treaty). No distinction is made in France between current-account and capital-account convertibility: both are fully liberalised.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign-currency receipts in resident or non-resident bank accounts. The only binding statutory requirement is balance-of-payments statistical reporting: all cross-border payments of EUR 15,000 or more must be reported by the French intermediary bank to the Banque de France on the monthly monetary-flow declaration. The exporter must also maintain auditable commercial evidence supporting each receivable and, under anti-money-laundering rules, be able to demonstrate the origin of any EUR 15,000+ inbound wire.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as the central bank and monetary authority; Direction Générale du Trésor (Treasury Directorate) within the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty as the competent foreign-exchange administration; Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the banking and financial-licensing supervisor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as the primary treasury infrastructure and Medium feasibility as a supplementary payment rail. ACPR-licensed electronic-money institutions (EMIs) may open passported EU/EEA IBANs but cannot take deposits, cannot grant credit, cannot issue cheques, and are statutorily barred from operating segregated escrow or custody accounts. An EMI is therefore suitable only for routine euro disbursements and not for inbound shareholder capital injections, trade-finance facilities, or pharmaceutical freight settlements where the banking partner must evidence a full credit-institution licence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer in EUR through the TARGET2 settlement system, with full field identification (50F ORDERING CUSTOMER, 57A ACCOUNT WITH INSTITUTION, 59 BENEFICIARY CUSTOMER) and a remittance narrative quoting the shareholders' resolution date and the wording 'AUGMENTATION DE CAPITAL – SOCIÉTÉ À RESPONSABILITÉ LIMITÉE'. The flow is classified for French statistical purposes as Direct Investment – Equity Capital and must appear on the Banque de France monthly monetary-flow declaration where the amount equals or exceeds EUR 15,000. Upon credit, funds remain subject to the bank's account-blocking instruction until the SARL supplies the certified resolution and the validated identity pack; after release they are held either in the operating account or in the segregated capital-escrow account consistent with the bank's covenant.Euro-denominated trade settlements use SEPA Credit Transfer (Direct ACH equivalent), which provides fast clearing among EEA institutions and requires only IBAN and BIC. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File documentation arises when the French entity's turnover excluding tax or total gross assets reaches EUR 50 million, or when the parent group's consolidated turnover or assets reach EUR 400 million (Article L.13 AA CGI); failure to file transfers burden of proof and may trigger procedural fines

Economic substance scope: France has no formal Economic Substance Regulations akin to UAE or BVI, but applies a substantive anti-abuse doctrine through abuse of rights (abus de droit), artificial construction, and BEPS Action 5 minimum standards. A letterbox SARL without actual premises, pharma freight operations, licensed GDP warehouse capacity, or operational control over transport flows can be re-characterized as a sham, resulting in full income recasting and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanction under Article 1741 CGI: imprisonment up to five years and fine up to EUR 75,000; civil penalty: 40% for deliberate intent, 80% for concealment, plus 0.20% monthly late interest

Director personal liability: Gérant or directors who authorised the fraudulent filing are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; travel restrictions, bank seizure, and personal restitution orders may be imposed; in insolvency, judicial officers can pursue the management personally for the whole passif.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: DGFIP re-characterization as an artificial entity; disallowance of deductions, full profit reallocation to foreign parent, VAT recovery refusal, DTA benefit denial, and levy of penalties for undisclosed tax relief

Director personal liability: Veil piercing for fictitious single-purpose companies is admitted; the managing director can be ordered to pay all unpaid corporate taxes and debts arising from unlawful invoicing and GDP licence non-compliance.
AML / GDP License Infraction

Statutory sanction: Administrative: revocation of GDP authorisation, seizure of pharma stock, product recall; criminal sanction under the Public Health Code: up to two years imprisonment and fine up to EUR 300,000 for distribution of falsified medicinal products

Director personal liability: Executive imprisonment, extension of disqualification orders to other regulated activities, forced closure of premises, and personal liability for compensation in supply-chain damage actions
Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment by DGFIP with tax and social debt recalculated, 0.20% monthly interest, 40% deliberate mispricing penalty, and reversal of burden of proof; refusal to provide TP documentation may trigger procedural fines and additional assessments

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false TP documentation are exposed to criminal fraud penalties, civil liability to co-shareholders and creditors, and professional disqualification from serving as a SARL manager in any French entity.
