Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Conditioned acceptance in France: a virtual or commercial domiciliation address is permitted for the registered office only if it is physically established in France and staffed for receipt of official communications during normal business hours. For the PCBA contract-manufacturing industry, a virtual office is insufficient to establish commercial substance where the company performs operational activities such as procurement, quality control, or logistics. Its use as the sole French presence would be a material tax-substance risk and a red flag for banking and AML scrutiny.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises – official single portal: https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; preliminary name search conducted via Infogreffe (https://www.infogreffe.fr) and Data INPI (https://data.inpi.fr).

Name reservation / lock period

Since French corporate law does not provide for advance name reservation, the name is only checked when the incorporation dossier is filed via the Guichet Unique. If the RCS registrar (greffier) issues a refusal due to conflict with an existing name, public-order incompatibility, or absence of the required ministerial authorization, the proposed name is deemed unavailable. The company must revise the name or obtain the relevant authorization and resubmit the dossier. Where the refusal is wrongful or arbitrary, counsel may file an application for review before the Tribunal de Commerce or the President of the civil court for non-commercial matters under Articles L.123-3, R.123-13 and R.123-145 of the French Commercial Code within 10 days from notification of the refusal. There is no ex-parte extension of the 10-day deadline; a legal representative admitted before the bar must sign the appeal, and a copy of the refusal notice and any supporting registrations should be appended.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current and capital account operations. France imposes no approval regime for cross-border payments; freedom of capital movements is guaranteed by TFEU Art. 63 and the EU Capital Movement Directive (88/361/EEC). Practical hurdles: mandatory balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical declaration, filed via the intermediary bank, for each cross-border flow exceeding EUR 50,000, and EU/French sanctions screening by DG Trésor/BdF for restricted counterparties. Profit remittance obstacles: outbound dividend payment incurs 25% French withholding tax (WHT), with 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a qualifying EU parent holding at least 5% for two years, or treaty-reduced rates; no FX licence or prior capital-repatriation authorization applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France does not impose export-proceeds surrender, conversion, or repatriation timelines. PCBA export receivables may remain in foreign currencies in free accounts or be converted at the operator's discretion; banks retain only the BOP statistical reporting duty for transactions above the EUR 50,000 threshold.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), operating within the Eurosystem and subject to ESCB rules; prudential supervision by the ACPR; monetary authority delegated to the ECB. France has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; no exchange controls are maintained.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Medium for daily operational payments; Low for trade and escrow rails. Licensed EMIs (Qonto, Revolut Business, Shine, Memo Bank as a full bank) provide remote onboarding, EUR IBANs, and SEPA/instant payments. Legal constraints: EMIs are not credit institutions under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Art. L522-1) and cannot open the statutory blocked formation account, issue or confirm letters of credit under UCP600, or provide import/export trade loans; deposit protection is only through passported deposit-guarantee schemes. Hence EMIs should be limited to second-liquidity accounts, never the primary settlement rail for PCBA cross-border trade.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent wires EUR (or convertible currency converted to EUR) to the French bank's blocked account in the name of the SAS under formation, with the instruction purpose stated as APPORT EN CAPITAL – CONSTITUTION SAS and the notary's reference. The bank issues the attestation de dépôt des fonds enabling the greffe to enter the SAS in the RCS (commercial register). For compliance, include the UBO declaration, the bank's BOP tagging of the flow as direct investment (equity capital), and the ISO 20022 payment purpose code for capital contribution (e.g., COST or EQUI). Where a notary holds the funds, the notary transfers to the corporate operational account after RCS registration. The flow remains fully free of FX licensing; only BOP and sanctions screening apply.Primary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103/TT for high-volume component payments to suppliers in EUR or USD, using UETR references and trade-related payment purposes; (ii) commercial letters of credit under UCP600 (MT700 issuance, MT720 transfer for back-to-back purchases of sub-assemblies) for large PCB/component purchase orders; (iii) documentary collections (MT400/MT420) for lower-risk relationships. French banks price LC issuance at 0.15–0.5% per quarter plus confirmation costs; the SAS should maintain committed import limits and request EUR/USD multi-currency accounts. For payments in CNY, an MT103 in EUR to a Chinese correspondent converting via onshore deliverable forwards is the standard compliant rail, supported by customs declarations (TARIC 8534 for bare PCBs, 8473 for assembled modules) and supplier contracts.Outright deliverable forward contracts (swap de change à terme) on EUR/USD and EUR/CNY with French credit institutions – fully permitted without prior licence, subject to EMIR margin rules.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on CNY/CNH with offshore counterparties – lawful in France and economically available where the underlying settlement currency is restricted onshore.Cross-currency swaps (CCIRS / XCCY) to hedge the currency mismatch of intercompany loans in USD/CNH against the EUR-functional treasury.FX swaps (swap de change) and forward-forward contracts for short-term liquidity and roll-over purposes.
