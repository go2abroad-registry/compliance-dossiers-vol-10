Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the Impots.gouv.fr professional portal; VAT registration is managed through the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE) and the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned French SARL must be incorporated with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the CFE/Guichet Unique. Upon incorporation, the entity is automatically assigned a French SIREN establishment number and an intra-community VAT identification number (TVA intracommunautaire) by the DGFiP. For non-established foreign entities conducting taxable operations in France, direct VAT registration is obtained by filing Form 1544-SD with the local tax office (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) and appointing a tax representative if the entity has no permanent establishment in the European Union.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned French SARL must be incorporated with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the CFE/Guichet Unique. Upon incorporation, the entity is automatically assigned a French SIREN establishment number and an intra-community VAT identification number (TVA intracommunautaire) by the DGFiP. For non-established foreign entities conducting taxable operations in France, direct VAT registration is obtained by filing Form 1544-SD with the local tax office (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) and appointing a tax representative if the entity has no permanent establishment in the European Union.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France generally does not permit U.S.-style check-the-box pass-through elections for SARLs. A French SARL is a taxable corporation and is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the entity level. The only significant statutory election is the option for small and medium-sized enterprises to be taxed in accordance with the reduced corporate income tax rate and the optional regime for related-party interest limitation adjustments under the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive; no general hybrid entity classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic French suppliers, the main registration threshold is €85,000 for goods supplied by wholesale/retail traders and €37,500 for service providers, except where the reverse charge or distance selling rules apply. Non-established foreign suppliers are generally required to register for French VAT from the first euro of taxable supplies unless the reverse charge procedure shifts liability to the French customer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies to tangible goods physically exported outside the European Union, evidenced by a customs export declaration, and to certain direct services related to goods (e.g., transport and ancillary services) supplied to a customer established outside the EU. Commercial 3D animation and CGI visual-effects services are generally zero-rated when supplied to a non-EU business customer under the B2B place-of-supply rule, provided the recipient is identified by a valid VAT number or equivalent business registration and the service is for the recipient's business use; services rendered for EU customers remain subject to French VAT at 20% unless another EU reverse charge rule applies.

  • EAU (Export Accompanying Document) / Customs Export Declaration (DAU - Document Administratif Unique)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of transport / CMR (for road transport)
  • Bank transfer confirmation or SEPA/SWIFT evidence of payment received from the non-resident customer

France has mandated mandatory electronic invoicing (facturation électronique) for all established taxable persons, phased from 2026. Invoices must be transmitted through the public PPF portal (Portail Public de Facturation) or accredited private dematerialization platforms, with structured data formats (EN 16931 / Factur-X). The system provides for e-invoice clearance, digital stamping of VAT-relevant data, real-time transmission to the DGFiP, and a mandatory electronic reporting obligation for non-resident suppliers registered under the EU One-Stop-Shop or non-Union OSS scheme.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict documentary substantiation. Failure to maintain the required customs and transport documentation within 180 days of the supply may trigger reversal of the zero-rating, assessment of VAT at 20%, late payment interest, and a 40% to 80% administrative penalty for deliberate deficiencies. In cases of fraudulent export schemes, joint and several tax liability may be asserted against the exporter and its directors, and criminal tax fraud sanctions may include fines of up to €500,000 and imprisonment of up to seven years, together with closure of the business and disqualification of managers.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a liberalized FX regime aligned with EU and OECD standards. The euro is fully convertible for current account transactions with no restrictions on profit remittances. Capital account transactions require prior notification to the Banque de France for amounts exceeding €100,000, with mandatory tax reporting under the French Tax Code for foreign assets and income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated and declared to customs authorities (Douane) within one month of shipment. No mandatory surrender to the central bank is required. Banks must verify commercial documentation including invoices, packing lists, and certificates of origin. Large transactions above reporting thresholds trigger automatic declaration to the Banque de France for statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Qualifying small and medium-sized companies may apply 15% to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, with 25% applying above that amount, subject to statutory conditions including turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up capital, and at least 75% individual ownership or qualifying ownership.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For dividends paid to non-residents, domestic rates and recipient classification matter: generally 12.8% for individuals and 25% for legal entities, subject to exemptions, treaty relief, and special rules. A qualifying EU parent may obtain an exemption under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, typically subject to a minimum 5% holding and a 12-month holding-period requirement or commitment, anti-abuse conditions, and other statutory requirements. Payments to entities in non-cooperative jurisdictions may attract a 75% rate unless an exception is established.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

File within three months after the accounting period ends. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the statutory rule is generally the second working day following 1 May. The balance of corporate tax is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a calendar-year company, generally 15 May. Electronic filing may receive an administrative tolerance that does not alter the statutory deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: French full transfer-pricing documentation is generally required where the French entity's annual turnover, or gross assets, is at least EUR 400 million, or where it is associated with an entity meeting specified EUR 400 million criteria, subject to statutory group and ownership tests. A simplified transfer-pricing declaration (form 2257-SD) generally applies at a EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, including specified related-party tests. Documentation and declaration rules are distinct; confirm current thresholds and applicability for the relevant year.

Economic substance scope: France has no general regime named Economic Substance Regulations comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. French tax authorities may nevertheless challenge artificial or non-genuine arrangements under domestic abuse-of-law rules, the principal-purpose test where applicable, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, and substance-over-form principles. The SARL should maintain personnel, decision-making, premises, contracts, and business records consistent with its functions, assets, and risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion, fraudulent returns, or concealed activity

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest, surcharges that may reach 80% in specified cases, and criminal prosecution. Aggravated tax fraud may carry imprisonment and substantial criminal fines under the French Tax Code and Penal Code; applicable maximums depend on the offense and aggravating circumstances.

Director personal liability: Managers may face personal criminal liability where they knowingly participate in or direct the offense. Corporate status does not immunize an individual perpetrator. Civil tax debts are not automatically transferred to directors; personal recovery may be available under statutory conditions, including fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated tax-law violations. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and inadequate documentation

Statutory sanction: Income adjustments, tax, interest, and applicable penalties. Failure to provide required full documentation can trigger a statutory penalty generally equal to the greater of EUR 10,000 or 0.5% of the relevant transactions, with a higher percentage potentially applying where the documentation is incomplete or inaccurate, subject to statutory limits and the case facts. The separate form 2257-SD regime carries its own penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal criminal exposure for intentional fraud or knowingly false records. Ordinary corporate tax adjustments do not, by themselves, pierce the SARL's limited-liability protection.
Improper dividend or royalty withholding and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax, late-payment interest, applicable surcharges, and potential penalties for inaccurate or fraudulent declarations. Treaty benefits may be denied under domestic anti-abuse rules, the applicable treaty, or the Multilateral Instrument.

Director personal liability: The SARL and withholding agent remain responsible for correct withholding and remittance. A manager may be personally exposed for intentional fraud or other conduct meeting statutory conditions; personal liability is not automatic.
Shell arrangements, sham contracts, or abuse of law

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization or disregard of the arrangement, tax reassessment, interest, and applicable surcharges. Abuse-of-law penalties may reach 80% in specified cases, with reduced rates potentially applying where statutory conditions are met.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for their own intentional participation in fraud or sham transactions. Limited liability is not generally displaced solely by a tax reassessment.
Money laundering, corruption, or proceeds-of-crime transactions

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties may include imprisonment, substantial fines, confiscation, and restrictions on business activity, depending on the offense. Regulated professionals may also face supervisory sanctions. The precise penalty depends on the offense, proceeds, and aggravating factors.

Director personal liability: Individuals who commit, direct, or knowingly facilitate the offense may face personal criminal liability. Confiscation or civil recovery may reach assets shown to be criminal proceeds. A travel ban or veil-piercing outcome is not automatic.
Failure to file, pay, or preserve required records

Statutory sanction: Late-filing and late-payment surcharges, interest, estimated assessments, and additional penalties for non-cooperation or missing records, as applicable under French law.

Director personal liability: Company-level penalties ordinarily remain company liabilities. A director may face personal consequences where statutory recovery conditions are met or where the director personally commits a separate offense; imprisonment and travel restrictions are not automatic consequences of an ordinary late filing.
