Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) for non-established businesses and the applicable local Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) for French-established entities. Principal digital portal: impots.gouv.fr; business tax services are accessed through the professional account at espace professionnel.

E-government filing portal

A French-established SARL obtains its SIREN and SIRET identifiers through registration with the Registre national des entreprises via the competent formalities portal, after which DGFiP allocates the French tax account identifiers. The French VAT identification number is issued by the competent SIE after the business establishes a VAT liability or requests VAT registration. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the SIEE using the prescribed VAT registration documentation, including constitutional documents, certificate of tax residence, evidence of economic activity, identification of the legal representative, bank and contact details, and supporting contracts or invoices where requested. The entity must identify its French VAT obligations, including domestic taxable supplies, acquisitions, imports, and applicable reverse-charge transactions. A French VAT number generally follows the format FR plus a two-character key and the nine-digit SIREN. Registration does not by itself create a permanent establishment or corporate income tax residence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). An SARL meeting statutory conditions may elect for income tax treatment, including the family SARL regime where all shareholders are qualifying family members and the company conducts an eligible commercial, industrial, artisanal, or professional activity. Certain newly formed small companies may elect temporarily for income tax treatment for a limited period if the statutory ownership, size, age, and activity conditions are satisfied. The election is subject to formal notification deadlines and cannot be treated as an unrestricted United States-style check-the-box election. France has no general check-the-box regime for SARLs. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive and French implementation provisions may deny deductions or trigger inclusion; they do not constitute a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For businesses eligible for the French VAT franchise regime, the ordinary turnover threshold for services is generally EUR 37,500 for the preceding calendar year, with a current-year tolerance threshold generally of EUR 41,250. Separate thresholds apply to goods and mixed activities. These thresholds are date-sensitive and subject to legislative change. The franchise does not apply where VAT is due under special rules, including certain imports, intra-Community transactions, or reverse-charge obligations. A French-established SaaS or helpdesk business may be required to register despite low turnover where a taxable transaction is not covered by the franchise, where it opts for taxation, or where cross-border VAT reporting rules apply. For qualifying intra-Community B2C digital services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may permit taxation in the supplier's establishment state; above that threshold, destination-state VAT applies, commonly through the Union OSS regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT where the supplier, an authorized intermediary, or the customer arranges the export and the supplier can prove that the goods left the EU customs territory within the statutory evidentiary period. The claim requires a consistent link between the invoice, the goods, the exporter, the customs declaration, and the evidence of exit. Intra-Community supplies of goods are generally exempt with the right to deduct where the customer has a valid EU VAT number, the goods are dispatched to another Member State, and the supplier holds transport and reporting evidence. B2B electronically supplied services are generally located where the business customer is established and may be outside the scope of French VAT when supplied to a non-French business customer, subject to customer-status and evidence requirements; this is not technically a French zero-rating mechanism. B2C digital services are generally taxed in the customer's Member State under EU destination rules, subject to applicable thresholds and OSS procedures.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, VAT treatment, and delivery terms
  • Export customs declaration containing the correct exporter, consignee, commodity classification, customs value, and applicable procedure codes
  • Electronic customs exit confirmation or equivalent evidence that the goods exited the European Union customs territory
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, airway bill, consignment note, courier record, or carrier delivery confirmation
  • Packing list and shipping documentation sufficient to reconcile the goods with the invoice and customs declaration
  • Proof of payment or accounting evidence where relevant to substantiate the commercial transaction
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required EC Sales List reporting
  • For offshore services, contract, customer business-status evidence, customer location evidence, service-delivery records, invoice, and payment trail

France does not operate a universal real-time invoice clearance system equivalent to Italy's SDI. Domestic B2B electronic invoicing is being introduced in phases. From 1 September 2026, all businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices, and large and mid-sized businesses must issue electronic invoices for in-scope domestic B2B transactions. From 1 September 2027, mandatory issuance extends to small and micro-enterprises. In-scope invoices must be transmitted through an approved private platform or the public platform framework designated by the French administration, with structured invoice data and required transaction and payment-status reporting. E-reporting obligations apply to specified transactions outside domestic B2B electronic invoicing, including certain B2C and cross-border transactions. Public-sector invoicing is already handled through Chorus Pro. Digital invoicing requirements do not replace VAT invoice-content, retention, audit-trail, or French-language and currency rules where applicable.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline of 180 days for ordinary export proceeds. Export proceeds must nevertheless be recorded accurately, supported by banking and accounting records, and reported consistently with customs, VAT, and corporate tax filings. If export exemption or an intra-Community exemption is unsupported or incorrectly claimed, DGFiP may assess the unpaid VAT retrospectively, together with late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month and tax penalties. A 40% penalty may apply for deliberate non-compliance and an 80% penalty may apply for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law, subject to statutory conditions. Customs violations may generate additional administrative penalties, duty and tax recovery, and seizure or forfeiture consequences. Serious tax fraud may also create criminal exposure, including imprisonment and substantial fines. Input VAT refunds may be denied or clawed back where invoices, transport evidence, customer status, customs exit data, or transaction substance are inadequate. Retention of invoices, customs records, transport evidence, contracts, payment records, and VAT ledgers is essential for the applicable statutory audit period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France implements full current-account and capital-account convertibility for EU and OECD transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (capital-freedoms provisions) and the OECD Liberalisation Codes. Current-account remittances, including dividends, royalties, subcontracting fees and service payments for the helpdesk SaaS activity, are freely transferable in euros without prior exchange-control authorization. Capital-account inflows, such as share capital deposits and shareholder advances, require only post-transaction reporting to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics when the counterparty is outside the EU, except where the investment falls within the strategic-sector regime of Article L151-3 CMF, which is not applicable to ISIC 6201/NAICS 511210. Profit remittance from a French SARL to foreign shareholders is lawful but must comply with corporate distribution formalities (ordinary shareholders meeting, approved annual accounts) and the 25% withholding tax (or lower treaty withholding for residents of treaty countries) triggered by the paying bank's tax-control system; failure to evidence beneficial ownership or the economic basis of the distribution can cause settlement delays and tax audits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no general statutory obligation to surrender export receipts into euros in France. French foreign-exchange regulations nevertheless require all export proceeds with a counterparty resident outside the European Union to be repatriated to France and credited to an account held at an authorized credit institution or recognized intermediary within 30 days after the contractual maturity date and, in any event, no later than 150 days after the date of shipment or the performance of the service. Export proceeds may be left in foreign currency in an approved foreign-currency account. The collecting bank must retain the commercial invoice, freight and customs documents, works delivery certificates, payment confirmations and, where the transaction exceeds EUR 50,000, file the applicable balance-of-payments declaration with the Banque de France.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting as the central monetary authority and the national foreign-exchange administration under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier), including balance-of-payments and financial relations with foreign countries delegated by the Direction Générale du Trésor

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech e-money institutions (EMIs) with a valid ACPR licence or an EU passporting notice are generally viable as secondary treasury rails in France. Accounts with Qonto, Shine, or Manager.one offer fast SEPA onboarding, EUR IBANs and API access; however, they cannot act as substitute for a full-service banking relationship because they are not licensed to execute commercial documentary credits, provide FX hedging lines, or clear high-value SWIFT MT103 payments with tax-compliance attachments. Non-EU-based digital EMIs without a French/EU branch are legally unsuitable as primary treasury and can only support incidental collection flows because they do not satisfy the authorised-intermediary requirement under French financial-relations-with-foreign-countries rules and lack the ability to file Banque de France declarations. Overall viability is rated Medium, limited to working-capital and low-value subscription collections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the French SARL must be executed by means of an international SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EU-origin funds) through the receiving tier-1 bank from which the capital account will be released after legal incorporation. The payment instruction should include the exact purpose code under the Banque de France balance-of-payments classification: 'P001' for equity capital and share subscriptions, or 'P006' for shareholder current accounts and non-trade advances. The receiving bank must receive, before final crediting, a certified copy of the share subscription resolutions, a capital-deposit certificate in cases of cash contributions, source-of-funds evidence for all non-EU envoys, and, for single transactions above the EUR 50,000 reporting threshold, the attached DNE (Déclaration de Notice d'Échanges) or the bank's balance-of-payments electronic declaration. Capital proceeds may be blocked during legal registration and released only upon production of the RCS certificate evidencing final registration.Outbound trade and treasury disbursements from the French SARL should be settled over SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR payments inside the EU, and over SWIFT MT103 messages for non-EU supplier and licensee payments. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends paid to non-resident entities under domestic law, subject to treaty relief and statutory exemptions. The rate is generally 12.8% for non-resident individuals under domestic income tax rules, subject to treaty and applicable social-levy treatment. A qualifying EU or EEA parent company may obtain exemption under the parent-subsidiary regime, generally requiring at least 10% ownership for at least two years and satisfaction of anti-abuse and other conditions. The applicable France–recipient-country tax treaty may reduce the rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

File within three months after the end of the financial year. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the electronic filing deadline is generally the second working day following 1 May of the following year, subject to the annual administration-granted e-filing extension. Pay the final balance by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a 31 December year-end, generally 15 May. Instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, subject to applicable exceptions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer-pricing documentation is generally required for a French entity or French permanent establishment belonging to a qualifying group where annual turnover excluding tax or gross assets reach EUR 400 million, or where a qualifying related entity meets the corresponding threshold, or where the entity is controlled by or controls an entity meeting the threshold. The annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration (Form 2065-SD-SD) generally applies at the EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, subject to statutory group and control rules. Documentation must be produced to the tax authorities upon request; the statutory production period is generally 30 days, subject to the request.

Economic substance scope: France has no general standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. French tax authorities may nevertheless challenge arrangements under transfer-pricing rules, the general and specific anti-abuse doctrines, beneficial-ownership rules, permanent-establishment principles, and the principal-purpose test or other treaty anti-abuse provisions. A SARL should maintain evidence that its personnel, decision-making, assets, functions, and risks support its claimed tax position and intercompany remuneration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent filing

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest and surcharges; aggravated tax fraud may constitute a criminal offence under the French Tax Code, punishable for individuals by up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, with higher penalties in aggravated cases, including up to seven years and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions. Legal entities may face fines and ancillary penalties under the Penal Code.

Director personal liability: Managers may face personal criminal prosecution and penalties where they personally participate in or direct fraud. Civil recovery from a manager may be ordered in statutory circumstances, including fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated tax-law violations. Imprisonment is possible following conviction; travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Taxable profits may be adjusted under Article 57 of the French Tax Code, with corresponding tax, interest, and applicable surcharges. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation may trigger a penalty generally equal to the greater of 0.5% of the relevant undocumented transactions or 5% of the reassessed transfer-pricing profits, subject to a statutory minimum and cap. Failure to file a required simplified declaration may attract a separate fine.

Director personal liability: The SARL is primarily liable for reassessed tax and penalties. Managers may incur personal criminal or civil liability where they knowingly participate in fraud, organize concealment, or satisfy statutory conditions for personal recovery; ordinary documentation failures do not automatically pierce limited liability.
Undeclared permanent establishment or concealed French activity

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment, interest, surcharges, and possible penalties for concealed activity; criminal consequences may apply where deliberate fraud is established. VAT, payroll, and social-security liabilities may arise separately.

Director personal liability: Responsible managers may be liable for deliberate fraud or statutory failures, but shareholder or director status alone does not automatically remove the SARL's limited liability.
Improper dividend, royalty, or interest withholding

Statutory sanction: The French payer may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, late-payment interest, and applicable surcharges or penalties. The recipient may also face recovery or treaty-relief denial. Recharacterization may generate additional corporate tax and withholding liabilities.

Director personal liability: The SARL is generally liable as withholding agent. Managers may face personal consequences where intentional non-compliance or criminal conduct is established; there is no automatic personal liability solely from office.
Shell-company, abuse-of-law, or treaty-shopping arrangement

Statutory sanction: Benefits may be denied and transactions recharacterized under French anti-abuse rules, with tax reassessment, interest, and surcharges. Enhanced penalties may apply where the statutory conditions for abuse or fraud are met.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur liability if they knowingly designed or implemented fraudulent arrangements. Civil restitution or criminal punishment requires the relevant statutory basis and factual findings; veil-piercing is not automatic.
Accounting-record, invoice, and beneficial-owner failures

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessments and penalties may apply; fictitious invoicing and fraudulent accounting may constitute criminal offences. Failure to file required beneficial-owner information may result in fines and other statutory measures.

Director personal liability: Managers who knowingly authorize false accounts or invoices may face criminal and civil liability. Personal liability depends on participation and statutory grounds, not merely their position.
Money laundering and proceeds of crime

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine for individuals, with higher penalties for aggravating circumstances; legal entities may face substantial fines and ancillary sanctions. Separate reporting, asset-seizure, and confiscation provisions may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors or managers who knowingly participate may be prosecuted personally. Courts may impose imprisonment, fines, confiscation, and other statutory measures. Travel bans are not automatic and require a competent authority's legal basis.
