Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital is prescribed for a SARL; the nominal capital may be set at EUR 1 or a higher amount. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Funds are deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts before registration, and the deposit certificate is filed. The deposit account is normally blocked until registration and release of the funds.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain premises-specific fire-safety compliance under the French building and workplace rules. An establishment open to the public may require an ERP safety and accessibility review by the municipal authority and, where applicable, the departmental fire and rescue service. Industrial or warehouse premises must maintain fire detection or suppression systems, emergency exits, electrical conformity, evacuation procedures, and periodic inspections. Employers must prepare a risk assessment document, the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels, and complete occupational-health arrangements before employing staff.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) or the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères for non-established businesses. The principal digital portal is impots.gouv.fr, including the professional tax account and VAT registration procedures. Company formation and business registration data are generally submitted through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), which issues the SIREN and SIRET identifiers.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered through the Guichet unique before commencing business. The filing generally includes the articles of association, registered-office evidence, beneficial-owner information, identification of managers and shareholders, activity description, and registration declarations. The competent registry transmits the information to the DGFiP, which establishes the entity's tax account. The SARL receives or uses its SIREN identifier for tax administration and, where liable to VAT, obtains a French VAT identification number in the format FR plus a two-digit control key and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity without a French establishment that must register for French VAT applies to the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères and submits corporate, identity, activity, French-taxable-transaction, bank-account, and representative documentation as requested. A non-EU business may require a French tax representative unless an applicable legal or treaty exemption applies. VAT registration is required when the entity makes transactions taxable in France, subject to applicable exemption, reverse-charge, import, distance-selling, and One Stop Shop rules; the domestic small-business franchise threshold is not generally available to non-established businesses for French transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Eligible SARLs formed for a limited period and satisfying statutory ownership, size, activity, and age conditions may elect taxation under the partnership regime, under which profits are attributed to the partners and taxed at partner level; the election is generally limited to five fiscal years. A family SARL meeting the statutory family-ownership and activity conditions may elect the family partnership regime without the ordinary five-year limitation, subject to continuing eligibility. The election is made with the relevant tax service and is not a general United States-style check-the-box election. France also applies anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implementing EU requirements, which can deny deductions, require inclusion, or restrict exemption where arrangements produce deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For French-established businesses eligible for the domestic VAT franchise, the principal turnover thresholds are generally EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods, sales for consumption on premises, and accommodation, with an increased threshold of EUR 93,500, and EUR 37,500 for services and non-commercial activities, with an increased threshold of EUR 41,250. Thresholds are subject to statutory adjustments and activity-specific rules. A non-established business generally cannot rely on the French domestic franchise threshold for French-taxable transactions and may require registration from the first relevant transaction. Mandatory registration can also arise independently of turnover, including for imports, certain intra-Community transactions, distance sales, transfers of own goods, and transactions subject to reverse charge or special schemes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT where the supplier establishes that the goods left the EU customs territory and satisfies the documentary and timing requirements. Evidence normally includes the export customs declaration, exit confirmation, transport documentation, commercial invoice, and a consistent audit trail linking the sale to the shipment. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for exemption rather than export zero-rating when the customer is VAT-registered in another EU Member State, the goods are dispatched to that Member State, and the supplier holds the required customer VAT number and transport evidence. Services supplied to a business established outside France may be outside the scope of French VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule rather than technically zero-rated; the supplier must retain evidence of the customer's business status and establishment. Separate rules apply to B2C services, intermediary services, digital services, immovable-property services, passenger transport, and services effectively used and enjoyed in France. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, import documents, and evidence that the input VAT relates to taxable or qualifying exempt activities. Refunds may be denied where export evidence, customer status, customs exit, or transaction substance is inadequate.

  • Export customs declaration containing the applicable export procedure and accurate exporter, consignee, commodity, value, and origin information
  • Electronic customs exit confirmation or equivalent customs evidence, such as an exit results message or certified alternative proof accepted by French customs
  • Commercial invoice and order or contract documentation identifying the goods, customer, price, VAT treatment, and shipment
  • Transport evidence, including bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, courier record, or carrier delivery and exit confirmation
  • Packing list and, where relevant, export licences, certificates of origin, sanctions documentation, and product-specific regulatory documents
  • Proof of payment or other commercial settlement evidence where relevant to establish transaction reality
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number, dispatch evidence, and required recapitulative statement data
  • An inward remittance certificate is not a standard mandatory French export-VAT document; payment evidence may nevertheless be requested during an audit, particularly for non-routine or high-risk transactions

France does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system for ordinary domestic transactions. The statutory electronic-invoicing reform requires all French-established businesses to be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Issuance obligations are scheduled from 1 September 2026 for large and intermediate-sized enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises, subject to legislative or regulatory amendments. Invoices must generally pass through an approved private platform or the public invoicing portal framework. Electronic reporting of certain B2C and international transactions is also being phased in. Invoices must contain mandatory French VAT particulars, including supplier and customer identification, VAT numbers where required, sequential numbering, date, taxable amount, VAT rate and amount, exemptions or reverse-charge wording, and applicable transport or customs references. Electronic signatures, structured formats, data retention, and audit-trail requirements may apply; there is no general French requirement for a digital tax stamp on every invoice.

France does not impose a general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline of 180 days for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption or refund may be retrospectively denied where the taxpayer cannot prove export, EU exit, customer status, transaction substance, or correct reporting. The unpaid VAT may be assessed with late-payment interest, generally calculated at 0.20% per month subject to applicable statutory rules, and tax penalties. Deliberate or abusive conduct may attract a 40% penalty for deliberate failure, an 80% penalty for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, or other applicable surcharges; failure to produce records or serious accounting deficiencies can trigger additional penalties. Incorrect invoices, false customs evidence, fictitious exports, or fraudulent VAT refund claims may create criminal tax-fraud exposure, customs penalties, repayment of improperly refunded VAT, joint liability in certain circumstances, and restriction or withdrawal of VAT refund claims. Interest and penalties are assessed under the French Tax Procedure Code and may be challenged through the applicable administrative and judicial remedies.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement and the EU Treaty freedoms. France maintains no exchange-control restrictions on inward or outward capital flows, trade settlements, or dividend remittances for registered commercial entities. A French SARL may remit distributable profits abroad after annual accounts approval; however, dividends paid to non-EU resident shareholders are subject to a 30% statutory withholding tax unless a lower treaty rate applies, and the paying bank and French tax authority require documentary evidence of tax residence and beneficial ownership. Current-account payments such as supplier invoices are unrestricted. Statistical reporting under the French Monetary and Financial Code applies to cross-border transactions above the Banque de France filing threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Eurozone and French exchange-control rules impose no mandatory surrender period, forced conversion ratio, repatriation deadline, or conversion benchmark for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or in a foreign currency, provided the transaction is reflected in the books and, when relevant, reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics. No statutory conversion ratio applies to cash-on-delivery collections from emerging markets, but all collections must be routed through licensed banking or payment-institution channels and reconciled against underlying trade documentation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with monetary policy competence exercised through the European Central Bank and foreign-exchange policy coordination through the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty (DG Trésor).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High viability with strict legal conditions. A French SARL may use an EU-authorised Electronic Money Institution or Payment Institution under PSD2 as an operational account provider, provided the EMI holds a French ACPR licence or a valid passport from another EU/EEA regulator. Offshore, non-EU digital EMIs are not lawful substitutes for a French or EU-licensed account, and their collection services may expose the SARL to unlicensed-payment-activity risk and banking de-risking. Airwallex, through its authorised EU entity, is a workable cross-border treasury rail for FX conversion and disbursements, but the SARL should maintain at least one French domestic bank account for statutory, tax, and court payment needs.

Offshore, non-EU digital EMIs are not lawful substitutes for a French or EU-licensed account, and their collection services may expose the SARL to unlicensed-payment-activity risk and banking de-risking. Airwallex, through its authorised EU entity, is a workable cross-border treasury rail for FX conversion and disbursements, but the SARL should maintain at least one French domestic bank account for statutory, tax, and court payment needs.
