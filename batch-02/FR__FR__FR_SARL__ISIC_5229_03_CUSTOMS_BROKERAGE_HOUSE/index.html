Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a SARL (Code de commerce, Article L.223-2); capital may be EUR 1 but must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be deposited in a French bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and held under a blocking certificate until RCS registration. For customs brokerage activity, adequate financial standing and professional civil liability cover are required by the customs authority.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the impots.gouv.fr business portal; customs and EORI registration are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI) through the French customs online services.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). The filing normally includes the articles of association, registered office evidence, director identification, beneficial-owner information, business activity description, and supporting documents for any regulated activity. The formalities authority transmits the data to the Registre national des entreprises, DGFiP, and other competent bodies. The entity receives a SIREN and establishment-level SIRET identifiers. DGFiP assigns or confirms the tax account and French VAT identification number, generally composed of the prefix FR, a two-digit control key, and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in France must register with the appropriate SIE; an EU-established entity may generally register directly, while a non-EU entity may need a French fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement or statutory exception applies. Customs activities require an EORI number obtained from DGDDI and, where the entity acts as a customs representative, the relevant customs authorizations, representation mandates, and electronic customs-access credentials.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A qualifying SARL, including certain newly formed companies and qualifying family SARLs, may elect for partnership-style income taxation under the statutory conditions and within the applicable election period. The family SARL election can remain available while the statutory family-ownership conditions continue to be met; the temporary new-company election is generally limited in duration. There is no general United States-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework and can deny deductions, impose inclusion, or apply other consequences to hybrid arrangements; they do not provide a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic French establishments eligible for the VAT franchise regime, the principal annual turnover threshold is generally EUR 25,000, with a tolerance threshold generally up to EUR 27,500 under the applicable current rules. The thresholds vary by activity and are subject to legislative change. A foreign business making taxable supplies in France may have a VAT registration obligation without relying on the domestic small-business threshold, including where it is not eligible for the franchise regime. Import VAT, intra-Community acquisitions, and certain reverse-charge transactions may create reporting obligations independently of the turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union are generally exempt from French VAT with the right to deduct input VAT, provided the supplier or its customs representative can substantiate exportation and exit from the EU customs territory through an accepted customs export record and supporting commercial and transport evidence. Intra-Community supplies of goods may be exempt where the customer is identified for VAT in another EU Member State, the goods are dispatched to that Member State, and the supplier satisfies invoicing, recapitulative-statement, and transport-proof requirements. B2B services supplied to a business established outside France are generally governed by the place-of-supply rule and may be outside the scope of French VAT rather than technically zero-rated; the customer VAT status, place of establishment, contractual evidence, and reverse-charge treatment must be documented. Services directly connected with qualifying exports may qualify for specific exemption rules where the statutory conditions are met. A customs broker must distinguish its own taxable brokerage service from the underlying export of goods and retain evidence supporting the applicable treatment.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and its Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs exit or export-results message, such as the electronic confirmation that the goods exited the European Union customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and packing or shipment records identifying the exporter, consignee, goods, value, and destination
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent transport document
  • EORI number and customs-representation mandate, including evidence of direct or indirect representation where applicable
  • Import or destination-country customs evidence where relevant to the transaction
  • Proof of delivery or other independent transport evidence when required or when the customs exit record is incomplete
  • VAT invoice showing the legal basis for exemption or reverse charge and the required customer VAT details where applicable
  • Accounting records, job files, broker instructions, customs correspondence, and payment records sufficient to establish the commercial substance of the transaction
  • Inward remittance evidence is not a general statutory prerequisite for French export VAT exemption, but payment records may be retained as corroborating evidence and may be relevant to foreign-exchange, anti-money-laundering, or audit procedures

France does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system for all private-sector transactions. Businesses must issue VAT-compliant invoices and retain reliable electronic records. The French B2B e-invoicing reform requires electronic invoice receipt capability for all businesses from 1 September 2026; issuance obligations apply from 1 September 2026 to large and mid-sized businesses and from 1 September 2027 to small and micro-enterprises, subject to the statutory implementation timetable. Transactions within scope will generally be transmitted through an approved private electronic invoicing platform or the public invoicing portal framework, with e-reporting for certain cross-border and consumer transactions. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. Customs declarations and customs data are filed electronically through DGDDI systems, with MRN and electronic release or exit records serving as principal customs evidence.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds applicable to an ordinary French SARL. Export VAT exemption or deduction may be reassessed where the taxpayer cannot prove export, EU exit, customer status, transport, or the actual nature of the service. The reassessment may include unpaid VAT, late-payment interest generally calculated under the applicable French tax rules, and tax penalties. A 10% surcharge may apply to certain late or deficient filings; deliberate breaches may attract a 40% penalty, and fraudulent or abusive conduct may attract an 80% penalty, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards. Incorrect customs declarations can trigger recovery of customs duties and import VAT, customs fines, loss or suspension of customs simplifications, and potentially confiscation or criminal liability. Deliberate VAT fraud may also create criminal exposure. Recovered VAT, interest, and penalties can be assessed retrospectively for the applicable statutory limitation period, commonly three years for ordinary VAT assessments, subject to longer periods or special rules for fraud, concealed activity, or criminal conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates as a euro-area member with fully liberalised current-account and capital-account convertibility under EU law. The euro is freely convertible and the eurozone applies no exchange-control authorisation for profit remittances, dividends, repayment of shareholder loans or liquidation proceeds to non-residents. Regulatory friction arises principally from AML/KYC obligations, financial sanctions screening, French balance-of-payments statistical reporting thresholds, and for customs brokers the statutory segregation of client funds held for customs duties and taxes. Profit remittance from an SARL is legally unrestricted, but the outgoing payment must be supported by approved minutes, audited/tax-filed accounts, proof of distributable reserves, and banking due diligence evidencing underlying commercial rationale.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in France or the eurozone. Exporters and customs brokers may freely retain euro or foreign-currency proceeds. However, all cross-border fund movements must pass through the banking system and certain flows are reportable to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics, typically when the transaction value exceeds applicable thresholds. For customs brokerage, export proceeds received on behalf of principals must be separately accounted for, must not be mixed with the broker's own funds, and must be remitted to the principal within the agreed settlement timeline without any forced conversion into euro.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) together with the Direction Générale du Trésor under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty, applying the EU Treaty capital-movement liberalisation framework and French Monetary and Financial Code reporting rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for principal treasury and statutory customs client-fund segregation; MEDIUM as a supplementary operational account. Fully digital banking is permissible for payment initiation, but French licensed customs brokers should maintain a Tier-1 bank relationship because DGDDI and AML supervisors expect robust safeguarding of third-party funds. EMIs in France or the EEA holding an electronic-money institution licence under the Second Payment Services Directive may be used for low-value, non-regulated payments, but they are not licensed credit institutions and cannot provide documentary credits, surety bonds, or customs guarantee products. Crossing the statutory thresholds for client-fund custody and treasury concentration mandates movement to a full credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection into the French SARL must be executed by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer, depending on the remitting jurisdiction. EEA/Eurozone shareholders may use SEPA CT with EUR no-corss-border-fee principles. Non-EEA shareholders must route through SWIFT wire with clear purpose codes, e.g. code 'CAPITAL INJECTION' or the bank-specific equity/loan purpose code. Equity subscriptions must be deposited before registration with Banque des Dépôts et Consignations or a notary where applicable, or evidenced by a certificate of the managing director if the articles so permit. The SARL must report the receipt to Banque de France under the balance-of-payments direct-investment reporting rules where the foreign investment exceeds applicable thresholds. Non-EU direct investments are not screened for a customs brokerage house unless the activity falls within a regulated sensitive sector. Funds must arrive from a banking account in the name of the shareholder; third-party payments require a subordination agreement and source-of-funds evidence.Outbound clearing for a customs brokerage house concentrates on settlement of customs duties, taxes, freight forwarders, overseas agents, and trade principals. The primary rails are SWIFT MT103 through the Tier-1 bank for actual remittance, Commercial Letters of Credit (URC 522 or UCP 600) for high-value documentary transactions, and SEPA Credit Transfer for intra-EU payments. Customs duties and import VAT must be debited from the broker's dedicated Customs Duty Account (Compte de Douane) or paid through the fully digital 'Déclaration en Douane' national cashier interface, using the automated Debit at Customs (NAP – Nouvelle Application de Paiement) or the harmonised DGDDI direct-debit system. For every outbound MT103 the broker must state the correct payment purpose, include supporting customs declaration numbers, and ensure that the beneficiary in the end-customer country corresponds exactly to the invoice and shipping documents.
