Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory 30-day company-name reservation mechanism comparable to jurisdictions that issue a formal name-reservation certificate. Conduct an INPI and RNE search, a trademark search through INPI, an EUIPO search, a domain-name search, and an adversarial trade-name search before filing. A refusal or dispute concerning a commercial-register filing may also require an application to the competent French commercial court or its registry judge. There is no automatic extension because no statutory name-reservation period exists.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) within the French Ministry of Economy and Finance; taxpayer and business-filing portal: impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel); single company/ID formalities portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique operated by INPI). Customs and export-clearance authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via pro.douane.gouv.fr (DELTA electronic clearance).

E-government filing portal

For a resident French SARL, statutory TIN/VAT IDs are issued through the Guichet Unique register portal: the incorporator files the M0 form together with the articles of association, registered-office evidence, and manager consent; upon INPI validation the SARL receives the 9-digit SIREN and 14-digit SIRET numbers, is entered in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés, and obtains the Kbis extract. The DGFiP thereafter allocates the intra-Community VAT number (13 characters: FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN) without any separate VAT application, normally notified within 2-4 weeks through the company's professional tax space. A foreign entity without a French establishment that makes taxable supplies in France must register before its first French transaction with the SIE non-établi unit (dedicated VAT registration form) and receives a non-resident French VAT number that is not derived from a French SIREN; the OSS/IOSS regime remains optional for B2C distance sales.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is by default subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (corporate income tax: 25% headline rate, 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs with turnover below €10m) at entity level, with no automatic pass-through to partners; profits and management results are taxed in the company, and distributions are separately taxed at the member level. Territorial_Scope: French branches of non-French corporate groups are taxed only on French-source taxable profits; the entity is not located in a Tax_Exempt_Zone, and Pass_Through_Eligible is not the default classification. — For a resident French SARL, statutory TIN/VAT IDs are issued through the Guichet Unique register portal: the incorporator files the M0 form together with the articles of association, registered-office evidence, and manager consent; upon INPI validation the SARL receives the 9-digit SIREN and 14-digit SIRET numbers, is entered in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés, and obtains the Kbis extract. The DGFiP thereafter allocates the intra-Community VAT number (13 characters: FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN) without any separate VAT application, normally notified within 2-4 weeks through the company's professional tax space. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is by default subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (corporate income tax: 25% headline rate, 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs with turnover below €10m) at entity level, with no automatic pass-through to partners; profits and management results are taxed in the company, and distributions are separately taxed at the member level. Territorial_Scope: French branches of non-French corporate groups are taxed only on French-source taxable profits; the entity is not located in a Tax_Exempt_Zone, and Pass_Through_Eligible is not the default classification.

Available election forms

Check-the-box-type election available only under Art. 239 bis AB CGI: a small SARL (turnover below €10m, fewer than 50 employees, capital continuously held by natural persons) may elect pass-through taxation at the income-tax (IR) level for a maximum period of 5 fiscal years by the corporate tax return filing deadline; the election is irrevocable during its term and terminates on cessation of eligibility. Reverse election: IR-transparent SARL forms (e.g., SARL de famille) may opt irrevocably into entity-level IS pursuant to Art. 239 CGI. Hybrid-mismatch elections: none — France applies ATAD-2 anti-hybrid rules (Art. 212 bis CGI) compulsorily in priority-ordering fashion to deny double-deduction, deduction/non-inclusion, and imported-mismatch outcomes; no elective check-the-box for hybrid entity classification exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Under the franchise en base de TVA small-business exemption, VAT registration may be avoided while annual turnover is below €85,000 (principal supplies of goods, including musical instruments and audio gear) or €37,500 (services) per calendar year, under the 2025 harmonized EU SME thresholds. Crossing €85,000 triggers mandatory VAT registration and VAT collection from the date of the exceeding transaction; a business may voluntarily register below the threshold to recover input VAT. Non-established EU sellers cross a €10,000 EU-wide annual distance-sales threshold before a French VAT registration obligation arises in France (OSS election available); non-EU sellers are registrable from their first French taxable sale.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Claim the 0% (exonerated) VAT rate on sales of musical instruments and audio gear only if: (i) goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the European Union by the seller, its subcontractor, or the buyer acting for the seller, with physical exit completed within 3 months (90 days) of the supply, per Art. 262 ter I-1 CGI; (ii) documentary proof is assembled, comprising the certified customs export declaration (DAU/EAD through DELTA), transport documents (BL/AWB/CMR), commercial invoice, and bank settlement evidence; and (iii) no fraudulent re-importation or redelivery into the French market occurs. Intra-EU B2B dispatches to a customer holding a valid EU VAT number are likewise exempt under Art. 262 ter I-2, subject to the EC sales list (recapitulative statement) and digitized transport proof. Services directly linked to exported goods (loading, transport, warehousing, customs) qualify under Art. 262 ter II. Sales to foreign end consumers via the EU OSS/IOSS are taxed at the destination-state VAT rate and are not zero-rated.

  • Certified Customs Export Declaration (DAU/EAD) generated in the DELTA system and export-confirmed by DGDDI
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR road consignment note evidencing physical removal of goods from the EU within 3 months
  • Commercial invoice stating the 0% TVA exonération (Art. 262 ter I CGI) applied to the export sale
  • Bank remittance or payment-settlement evidence from the foreign buyer (inward remittance certificate equivalent)
  • Intra-EU: EC Sales List (déclaration d'échanges de biens/recapitulative statement) and the customer's valid EU VAT registration number
  • EORI number registration confirmation and, where applicable, Incoterms-based transport contracts

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing applies to all B2B transactions under the French e-invoicing reform: receipt capability from 1 September 2026 for all VAT-registered businesses; issuance from 1 September 2026 for large/medium enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small/very-small enterprises. Invoices must be transmitted through an accredited PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) or the public PPF using Factur-X/UBL structured syntax; the platform forwards and reports invoice data to DGFiP. France does not impose real-time fiscal stamping or continuous control clearance; enforcement is performed downstream by DGFiP on the aggregated e-invoice and e-reporting dataset (B2C and cross-border flows included). Exports additionally require electronic customs clearance via DELTA with the DAU/EAD declaration and an EORI number.

DGFiP VAT audit window: standard 3-year reassessment period (Art. L.176 LPF), extended to 6 years for deliberately concealed taxable activity, and to 10 years for fraudulent export chains (fausse exportation, TVA carrousel) involving third-party participation. On audit failure, zero-rating is revoked, input VAT deductions are clawed back with full reassessment, and penalties are assessed: 10% for simple negligence; 40% for deliberate under-declaration (mauvaise foi); 80% for manœuvres frauduleuses or abuse of rights; late-filing penalties of 10% plus 0.5% per month; and monthly late-payment interest of 0.2%. Criminal exposure under Art. 1741 CGI reaches up to 5 years' imprisonment and a €75,000 fine; customs smuggling penalties under Art. 414 of the Code des douanes impose a fine of 1 to 3 times the evaded duties, up to 3 years' imprisonment, and seizure of goods; the exporter may also face EORI suspension, VAT-registration withdrawal, and joint-and-several liability for orchestration of a fraudulent zero-rating chain. Note: statutory exchange-control repatriation deadlines no longer exist in French law, but DGFiP audit practice applies the 180-day settlement benchmark as corroborating documentary evidence for export zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a member of the Eurozone; the Euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls on capital movements, profit remittances, or dividend distributions, subject to compliance with EU anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-terrorist financing (CTF) regulations. Profit remittance is unrestricted for SARLs, provided that statutory audits (if applicable) and tax clearance are completed. Non-resident shareholders may freely repatriate dividends after withholding tax (if any) under domestic law and tax treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in authorized bank accounts, but must report all cross-border transactions to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics (via the 'Réglementation des changes' reporting system). There is no statutory timeline for conversion; however, for tax and accounting purposes, foreign currency receivables must be revalued at year-end. No minimum conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) under the authority of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Recovery; the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) supervises banking and insurance entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. France recognizes Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the EU PSD2 directive, regulated by the ACPR. Cross-border EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Qonto, or N26) can open accounts for French SARLs, but they may have limitations on high-volume trade settlements and often require a local IBAN. Legal constraints include mandatory ACPR authorization for EMI activities, and EMIs cannot provide credit or hold client funds beyond e-money limits. For a low-risk retail business, EMIs are viable for operational liquidity but not for large capital injections or complex trade finance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Use SWIFT MT103 for international transfers. For capital contributions from non-resident shareholders, the purpose code must be '1100' (Direct Investment) or '1110' (Equity Capital) under the French balance-of-payments classification. The bank will require a copy of the shareholder resolution, a capital increase agreement, and proof of the source of funds. For loans from shareholders, use purpose code '1200' (Loans) and provide a loan agreement. All inbound transfers must be reported to the Banque de France via the 'Réglementation des changes' system if they exceed €15,000 (per transaction or cumulative). Verification includes anti-money laundering checks and confirmation of the beneficial owner.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For supplier payments, use SWIFT MT103 (single payment) or MT202 (bank-to-bank) for high-value transactions. For documentary trade, use Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) via the bank's trade finance desk, typically with MT700/MT707 messages. For recurring payments to EU suppliers, SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) is the standard rail. For non-EU suppliers, SWIFT is mandatory. The bank will require invoices, purchase orders, and proof of delivery (e.g., bill of lading) to comply with AML and sanctions screening. For high-value disbursements (>€50,000), the bank may request additional due diligence on the beneficiary.FX Forwards (plain vanilla) - permitted for hedging commercial exposures, with a maximum tenor of 12 months for SARLs without a dedicated treasury function.FX Swaps (currency swaps) - permitted for converting EUR to foreign currency and back, subject to bank approval and underlying trade documentation.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) - technically permissible for currencies with restrictions, but not applicable for EUR as it is fully convertible; banks may offer NDFs for exotic currencies if the underlying trade involves such currencies.Currency Options (European style) - permitted for hedging, but require a higher credit rating or collateral; not commonly used for small SARLs.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. France recognizes Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the EU PSD2 directive, regulated by the ACPR. Cross-border EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Qonto, or N26) can open accounts for French SARLs, but they may have limitations on high-volume trade settlements and often require a local IBAN. Legal constraints include mandatory ACPR authorization for EMI activities, and EMIs cannot provide credit or hold client funds beyond e-money limits. For a low-risk retail business, EMIs are viable for operational liquidity but not for large capital injections or complex trade finance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: 400,000,000 EUR (group consolidated turnover) for full Master File and Local File; simplified documentation required for turnover >50,000,000 EUR.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR), but requires substance for tax residency and applies anti-abuse rules (e.g., substance over form, GAAR).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 80% of the evaded tax (for bad faith) or 100% (for abuse of law), plus interest at 0.2% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for corporate tax debts if they participated in the evasion; criminal penalties include imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to €75,000.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 100% penalty for abuse of law (abus de droit).

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly used the entity as a sham; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
AML / Financial Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for companies, plus criminal penalties for directors; suspension of business activities.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined up to €375,000 and imprisoned up to 5 years; civil restitution of illicit funds.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty for lack of documentation, and interest; potential double taxation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; criminal charges for tax fraud if intentional.
