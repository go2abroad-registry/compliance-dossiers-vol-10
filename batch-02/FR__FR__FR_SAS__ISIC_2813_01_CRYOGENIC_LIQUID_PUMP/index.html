Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A SAS must have a registered office in France under Articles L.123-11 et seq. of the Commercial Code. A domiciliation company or serviced-office address may be used only if the provider is legally authorized and the domiciliation agreement, identification, mail handling, and beneficial-owner information satisfy applicable requirements. A director's personal residence may be used subject to statutory conditions and contractual or lease restrictions. A purely virtual address with no compliant domiciliation provider, no accessible records, and no operational capability is unacceptable. For cryogenic LNG pump manufacturing, a virtual office is unsuitable as the sole commercial-substance arrangement because manufacturing, warehousing, testing, pressure-equipment compliance, customs, occupational safety, environmental, and product-traceability functions require demonstrable operational premises. The registered office should be supplemented by a declared factory, workshop, warehouse, or qualified contract-manufacturing site before commercial operations begin.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies (Art. L. 224-2, flexible for SAS); the Statuts fix the capital level. Cash contributions must be at least 50 percent paid up at subscription and deposited on a blocked French bank subscription account (attestation de blocage de fonds); the balance may be called within five years; no escrow agent is mandated by law.

Funding & unlock path: Prior approval of the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) for fire safety, including escape routes, fire loads, and cryogenic testing bays; structural load inspections by an approved verification body (APAVE, Socotec, or Bureau Veritas) covering crane rails and reinforced cryogenic enclosures; workplace clearance through CARSAT requiring a written risk assessment, medical fitness certificates for staff exposed to cryogenic fluids, and periodic equipment inspections under Articles R. 4323-1 et seq. of the French Labour Code.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), principally the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE); non-established businesses are generally handled by the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). Tax administration services and professional account access are provided through impots.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the competent business formalities channel, with information transmitted to the tax administration and allocation of a French SIREN and SIRET by INSEE. It must obtain or confirm its tax account access through impots.gouv.fr and request VAT registration from the competent SIE before carrying out taxable transactions for which registration is required. A non-established foreign entity making taxable supplies in France generally applies to the SIEE for a French VAT number before its first taxable transaction; it submits business registration evidence, constitutional documents, proof of identity and authority of its representative, a description of activities, and any required fiscal representative appointment. EU-established businesses generally do not require a French fiscal representative; businesses established outside the EU may be required to appoint an accredited French fiscal representative, subject to applicable exemptions and mutual-assistance arrangements. VAT registration is also required where the entity cannot use an applicable domestic small-business exemption or special scheme. Registration does not replace any required customs EORI registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A qualifying newly formed SAS may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime for up to five fiscal years if statutory conditions are met, including limits concerning age, size, ownership, and business activity; the election is not a general check-the-box election. French law has no US-style entity-classification check-the-box election. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing EU requirements may deny deductions or require inclusion in defined cases; they are statutory anti-avoidance rules, not elective classification regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration turnover threshold applies to a non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in France. French-established small businesses may qualify for the VAT exemption based on statutory turnover limits, which vary by activity and are subject to legislative changes; the exemption does not generally remove a non-established supplier's registration obligations. The amount and eligibility should be confirmed for the relevant tax year and activity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with deduction rights under the French VAT Code, principally where the statutory exporter or an authorized intermediary establishes that the goods left the EU customs territory. The supplier must retain evidence linking the sale to the physical export and satisfy applicable invoicing and customs requirements. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: place-of-supply rules determine whether a service is outside the scope of French VAT, while specific services may qualify for exemption only if statutory conditions are met. Intra-EU supplies are governed by separate rules and are not third-country exports.

  • French or EU customs export declaration and export accompanying/reference data evidencing exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the parties, goods, consideration, and export transaction
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR, or other transport evidence appropriate to the shipment
  • Carrier delivery records, proof of receipt, and other evidence linking the goods to the customs export declaration
  • Customer purchase order, sales contract, and payment evidence where relevant to substantiate the transaction
  • No general French inward remittance certificate or statutory foreign-exchange repatriation certificate is required

France is implementing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction/payment e-reporting through approved platforms, with phased obligations beginning in 2026 and 2027 under the applicable implementation timetable. The regime is not a general real-time invoice-clearance or digital-stamping system. Required formats, platform routing, and reporting scope depend on the transaction and the entity's implementation phase. B2C and cross-border transactions may be subject to e-reporting rather than domestic B2B e-invoicing. Taxpayers should verify current DGFiP specifications and effective dates.

The taxpayer bears the burden of substantiating export exemption and its right to deduct input VAT. Insufficient or inconsistent customs, transport, invoice, or customer records may result in denial of the exemption or input-tax deduction and reassessment of VAT, together with late-payment interest generally charged at 0.20% per month and applicable penalties. Depending on the facts, penalties may include 40% for deliberate failure and 80% for fraudulent activity or specified concealment, in addition to recovery of unpaid tax. Criminal sanctions may apply to serious tax fraud. France imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to European Union sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, counter-terrorist-financing requirements, tax rules, foreign-investment screening, and bank transaction-monitoring procedures. A French SAS may generally receive foreign capital, pay suppliers and lenders abroad, acquire foreign assets, distribute dividends to non-resident shareholders, and repatriate sale proceeds without a general exchange-control approval or mandatory conversion into another currency. Dividend and interest payments remain subject to corporate-law solvency and distributable-profit rules, withholding-tax analysis, treaty relief procedures, beneficial-owner verification, transfer-pricing requirements, and possible interest-limitation or anti-hybrid rules. Payments involving sanctioned countries, sanctioned persons, high-risk jurisdictions, dual-use goods, defense-related equipment, or controlled technology may require authorization or may be prohibited. Direct investment, securities, or cross-border loan transactions may be subject to Banque de France statistical reporting, bank questionnaires, or sector-specific notification requirements. Foreign-investment authorization may be relevant if the activity, technology, customer base, or assets fall within protected strategic sectors, including certain energy, defense, security, critical infrastructure, or advanced-technology activities; the ordinary manufacture of cryogenic pumps is not automatically subject to screening solely by industrial classification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no universal statutory deadline requiring a French SAS to convert or repatriate export receipts. Export proceeds may generally remain in euro or foreign-currency accounts and may be used for permitted foreign payments, subject to the bank's account terms, AML and sanctions screening, accounting records, customs evidence, tax documentation, and any applicable statistical reporting. The company should reconcile export invoices, customs declarations, transport documents, payment receipts, and VAT evidence. Any reporting deadline is transaction-specific and may arise from Banque de France balance-of-payments surveys, trade-statistics obligations, tax filings, customs procedures, financing covenants, or bank compliance requests rather than from a general FX surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. Foreign-investment screening is administered by the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty, and payment-services supervision is principally performed by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution and the European Central Bank, as applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment accounts and treasury operations; Low as a substitute for a French deposit-taking relationship or a capital-deposit account. An EU-licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide safeguarded payment accounts, SEPA transfers, foreign-exchange services, virtual IBANs, and, where authorized, international payment services through passporting or an appropriately authorized French branch. It may not accept repayable deposits or represent safeguarded e-money as a bank deposit. Capital for incorporation of a French SAS must be deposited with an eligible depositary, which may include a qualifying bank or other legally permitted institution; an offshore digital EMI cannot be assumed to satisfy the French incorporation-deposit requirement. The EMI may impose limits or decline activity involving industrial equipment, sanctioned destinations, complex ownership, high-value cross-border payments, dual-use goods, or insufficient commercial substance. The company should retain a primary French or EU credit-institution account for payroll, taxes, customs, trade finance, and contingency liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route paid-in capital to the designated capital-deposit account of an eligible French or EU credit institution before SAS registration, using a regulated bank-to-bank transfer from an account held in the name of the subscriber or documented funding entity. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable tax treaties. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption may eliminate withholding where the conditions are satisfied, including qualifying EU or EEA residence, minimum participation, minimum holding-period requirements, subject-to-tax conditions, and anti-abuse requirements. A French resident corporate shareholder generally receives dividends under the participation regime, subject to the statutory add-back and qualifying conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the annual return is generally due on the second business day following 1 May of the following year. For a fiscal year ending on another date, the return is generally due within three months after the fiscal year-end. A separate extension may apply to electronic filing under administrative rules. Corporate income tax instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, with the final balance generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, or by the 15th day of the fifth month for a 31 December year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: France generally requires comprehensive Master File and Local File documentation where the French entity or the relevant French enterprise belongs to a group meeting the EUR 400 million threshold based on annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets, or where the entity is controlled by or controls an entity meeting that threshold. A simplified transfer-pricing statement, Form 2257-SD, generally applies where the French entity or group meets the EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, or where it is controlled by or controls an entity meeting that threshold, subject to statutory conditions. The statement also covers controlled transactions and categories subject to the applicable reporting thresholds, including the EUR 100,000 per-category threshold in relevant cases. Amounts should be monitored in EUR and reconciled to statutory accounts.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a standalone regime formally titled Economic Substance Regulations comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless assessed through French transfer-pricing rules, the permanent-establishment doctrine, beneficial-ownership requirements, general anti-abuse principles, anti-hybrid rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, withholding-tax anti-abuse provisions, and EU ATAD implementation. The French SAS should maintain real decision-making, qualified personnel, appropriate premises, operational assets, commercial risk control, records, board approvals, and bank and accounting functions proportionate to the cryogenic-pump manufacturing activity. Conduit arrangements, circular financing, artificial royalty structures, and entities lacking control over the risks attributed to them are high-risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, recovery of unpaid tax, late-payment interest, tax penalties generally ranging from 40% for deliberate failure to 80% for fraudulent maneuvers or abusive conduct, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that may include imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000. Aggravated cases may carry up to 7 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 3 million, subject to statutory aggravating conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face criminal prosecution, personal tax-liability proceedings in cases of fraudulent or repeated obstruction, management bans, asset seizure, civil restitution, and potential imprisonment. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be imposed by judicial authorities where legally justified.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment under French transfer-pricing rules, corresponding corporate income tax, withholding tax, interest, and penalties. Failure to provide required documentation after notice may result in a penalty generally determined by the higher of the statutory minimum and a percentage of adjusted profits, subject to applicable caps and procedural rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil and tax liability for intentional concealment, obstructing an audit, or approving sham transactions. Criminal exposure may arise if the conduct constitutes tax fraud, false accounting, corruption, or money laundering.
Undeclared dividend, royalty, or interest withholding

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, interest, late-filing penalties, and potential penalties for deliberate or fraudulent conduct. Treaty relief may be denied and the recipient may be unable to obtain a refund.

Director personal liability: The legal representative may be pursued where the failure results from deliberate misconduct, fraudulent organization, or serious and repeated non-compliance. Criminal liability depends on the underlying conduct and may include tax-fraud or money-laundering charges.
Shell company and artificial permanent-establishment arrangements

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of taxable presence, profit allocation adjustment, denial of deductions or treaty benefits, withholding-tax assessment, interest, penalties, and possible application of the general anti-abuse rule.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims, tax-liability proceedings, management bans, asset seizure, and criminal prosecution where the structure is used to conceal beneficial ownership, evade tax, launder proceeds, or falsify records.
Value-added tax and customs fraud

Statutory sanction: Customs duties, import VAT, VAT reassessment, interest, administrative penalties, confiscation of goods, suspension or withdrawal of customs simplifications, and criminal sanctions for customs fraud. Serious customs offenses may carry imprisonment and substantial fines calculated by reference to the value of goods or avoided duties.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally exposed to customs and criminal proceedings for intentional fraud, false declarations, or organized evasion. Goods, transport equipment, and proceeds may be seized or confiscated.
Anti-money laundering, terrorist financing, and beneficial ownership

Statutory sanction: Regulatory, civil, and criminal consequences may include account restrictions, transaction blocking, confiscation, fines, withdrawal of professional approvals, and imprisonment depending on the offense. Money laundering can attract imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 375,000, with aggravated penalties in organized or repeated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be prosecuted for knowingly facilitating laundering or terrorist financing, face asset seizure and confiscation, management prohibitions, and imprisonment. Travel bans may be ordered by judicial authorities where necessary.
Economic sanctions and export-control breach

Statutory sanction: Transaction nullification, asset freezes, customs seizure, administrative or criminal fines, export-license suspension, and imprisonment under applicable French, European Union, and international sanctions legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for intentional or negligent sanctions breaches, with potential fines, imprisonment, management bans, asset seizure, and reputational or procurement exclusion consequences.
Foreign account, asset, and related-party reporting failure

Statutory sanction: Additional assessments, fixed fines, increased penalties for accounts in non-cooperative jurisdictions, loss of limitation-period protection, and possible tax-fraud investigation. Foreign-account penalties may reach EUR 1,500 per account, or EUR 10,000 in specified non-cooperative jurisdictions, subject to applicable rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally investigated where non-disclosure is intentional or connected with concealment of taxable income, money laundering, or fraudulent accounting.
Bribery, kickbacks, and procurement corruption

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution for active or passive bribery, confiscation, exclusion from public procurement, corporate fines, compliance-monitoring obligations, and imprisonment under the French Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Individuals may face imprisonment, personal fines, management bans, confiscation, and civil restitution. The SAS may also be criminally liable where the offense was committed on its behalf by an organ or representative.
Late statutory accounts, tax return, or corporate records

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, tax surcharges, deemed or estimated assessment, rejection of deductions, audit extension, director liability proceedings, and court-ordered compliance measures.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for insufficient assets caused by management faults, be prohibited from managing companies, and face personal tax or insolvency liability where non-compliance materially contributes to the loss.
