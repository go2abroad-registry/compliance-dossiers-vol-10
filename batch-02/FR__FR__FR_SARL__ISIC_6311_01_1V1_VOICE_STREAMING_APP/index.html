Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce de Paris (Registry Clerk of the Commercial Court), acting through the single electronic window 'Guichet Unique' operated by INPI (Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title III, Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL regime); Article L.210-2 (lawful purpose); Decree n° 2021-1088 on the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); Ordonnance n° 2020-158 liberalising capital formation; Code civil Articles 1832 to 1872.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de Création d'Entreprise (company creation statement, historical M0 form) together with the signed Statuts (Articles of Association) and all ancillary dossiers enumerated below; registration is evidenced by the Extrait Kbis issued by the Greffe.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies to a SARL (the capital is freely fixed, commonly EUR 1 or EUR 10,000). Since 1 January 2021 (Ordonnance n° 2020-158), cash contributions need not be paid at subscription unless the Statuts so provide; any cash contribution actually paid must be deposited with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and remain blocked until RCS registration, as evidenced by a certificat de dépôt de fonds.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises: if the French workspace qualifies as a 5th-category ERP and exceeds 100 persons, a fire clearance from the Commission de Sécurité is required; below that threshold, compliance with the Code du travail rules on aeration, emergency exits and fire-fighting equipment suffices. Electrical installations must meet NF C 15-100 with a Consuel conformity certificate for new works. Occupational clearance: registration with URSSAF and the médecine du travail, first-aid provision, and display of workplace safety notices under Articles L.4621-1 et seq. of the Code du travail.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies to a SARL (the capital is freely fixed, commonly EUR 1 or EUR 10,000). Since 1 January 2021 (Ordonnance n° 2020-158), cash contributions need not be paid at subscription unless the Statuts so provide; any cash contribution actually paid must be deposited with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and remain blocked until RCS registration, as evidenced by a certificat de dépôt de fonds.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), French General Directorate of Public Finances, via the official portal impots.gouv.fr; corporate registrations and VAT formalities are processed through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity forming a French SARL must register through the Guichet Unique, obtaining a SIREN and SIRET number and, when subject to VAT, a French VAT identification number assigned by DGFiP. Non-established foreign suppliers that make taxable supplies in France without a French establishment may apply directly to the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for a French VAT number, and must appoint an individual tax representative approved by the DGFiP if they are established outside the European Union and have no fixed establishment in France.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity forming a French SARL must register through the Guichet Unique, obtaining a SIREN and SIRET number and, when subject to VAT, a French VAT identification number assigned by DGFiP. Non-established foreign suppliers that make taxable supplies in France without a French establishment may apply directly to the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for a French VAT number, and must appoint an individual tax representative approved by the DGFiP if they are established outside the European Union and have no fixed establishment in France.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in France. An SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level. A closely held SARL meeting certain qualitative conditions may irrevocably or temporarily opt for income tax treatment as a transparent partnership under Article 239 bis AA of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI). Hybrid mismatch elections under ATAD transposition are not elective in the ordinary sense; French law applies binding anti-hybrid adjustments, although optional transparency may apply to certain eligible entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For non-established foreign suppliers of electronic services to non-taxable French consumers, no registration threshold applies; VAT registration is mandatory from the first euro of taxable turnover. Domestic small-business thresholds (EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for supplies of services under Article 293 B CGI) are not available to non-resident electronic-services suppliers. Cross-border B2C e-services may instead be declared under the non-Union OSS scheme without a French VAT number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 262 CGI provided the supplier proves actual physical export, files an export customs declaration, and retains the mandated transport and delivery documentation. Services directly related to the export of goods, and services supplied to a business customer established outside the EU, fall outside the scope of French VAT (Article 259-1 CGI) rather than being zero-rated; the supplier may nevertheless claim input VAT deduction or refund if strictly connected to taxable or foreign indeterminable transactions. For the voice streaming application, digital services rendered to non-EU business customers are considered outside the French territorial scope and may support input VAT recovery subject to Articles 259, 271 and 272 CGI.

  • Single Administrative Document (SAD / DAU) or electronic export declaration lodged in the DELTA system
  • Bill of Lading (BoL) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing physical departure from the European Union
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or corresponding bank transfer/FX settlement record demonstrating receipt of the export consideration
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Transport contract and delivery receipt signed by the foreign customer

Under the French e-invoicing reform, domestic B2B e-invoicing becomes mandatory progressively: from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small enterprises. Invoices must be transmitted through Chorus Pro or a registered dematerialisation platform (PDP) using Factur-X, UBL or CII formats. E-reporting of B2C and cross-border transactions to the DGFiP is required via a PDP within one business day of issuance. There is no blockchain-based digital stamping, but real-time fiscal clearance and structured digital reporting are mandated for all affected taxable persons.

Fraudulent or unfounded zero-rating triggers full retrospective VAT recovery, disallowance of input VAT deductions, and late-interest accrual at 0.20% per month. Civil penalties under Article 1729 CGI impose 40% for deliberate omissions or inaccuracies and 80% for abusive fraud or use of forged documents. Criminal sanctions under Article 1741 CGI include imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000. Managers may be held civilly liable for corporate tax debts under Article L.267 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales, and the DGFiP may apply comprehensive cross-border information exchange and anti-abuse audit procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in France. Current-account operations, including royalty payments, software licensing, platform service fees and profit remittances, are free of foreign-exchange licensing and capital-authorisation requirements. France applies complete capital-account openness under Article 63 TFEU: inbound equity subscription, SARL capital increases, dividend distribution and disposal of shares by non-resident investors are lawful through ordinary bank transfers. Residual frictions are reporting and screening based: each cross-border remittance equal to or exceeding 50,000 EUR equivalent is reported by the French credit institution to Banque de France under the TDF (Déclaration de Transaction Financière) framework; non-EU direct investment in a non-sensitive digital-content entity does not require prior Ministerial authorisation under Articles L.151-3 and R.151-4 CMF, except where the purchaser is from a high-risk third country, in which case an FDI notification may be prudent; and SARL dividends paid to a non-resident shareholder are freely transferable abroad but remain subject to French withholding tax at 25% unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% rate, subject to a 10% shareholding held for at least 24 months), or Article 119 ter CGI for EU treaty residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory repatriation deadline and no forced euro-conversion ratio. A French SARL may lawfully retain export and cross-border service receipts in foreign currency, provided receivables are recorded in French GAAP and any onshore EUR disbursement follows standard banking rules. The only compulsory obligation is statistical: payments to or from foreign countries equal to or exceeding 50,000 EUR equivalent are subject to monthly balance-of-payments reporting by the collecting French intermediary to Banque de France, and the SARL must preserve underlying invoices, contracts and account statements for a statutory limitation period of five years under Article L.102 B of the French Tax Procedure Book.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), the National Central Bank, exercising delegated authority for France's foreign-exchange and financial-relations regulation (réglementation des relations financières avec l'étranger) under the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) of the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique, with prudential supervision of credit institutions by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

High for EU/EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) passported into France under PSD2, such as Qonto, Shine, and Revolut Business, which may serve as operational expense and disbursement rails. Low for offshore, non-EEA digital EMIs, which cannot lawfully hold SARL client monies or execute SEPA and French domestic transfers without a French branch or an EU e-money or payment-institution passport. Critical legal constraint: an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot issue the 'attestation de dépôt de capital' certificate required by the French notary and the commercial registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce) for SARL capital subscription evidencing the bank holding of subscribed capital; therefore the capital-escrow function must be placed with a Tier 1 credit institution, while the EMI can be used as a low-value disbursement account with daily sweeping back to the Tier 1 operational EUR account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-EU UBO chain and undisclosed beneficial ownership, compounded by directors residing outside France.

Root cause: Non-EU UBO chain and undisclosed beneficial ownership, compounded by directors residing outside France.

Defense evidence: Certified passport copies of all gérants and UBOs; notarised and apostilled board or shareholder resolution authorising account opening; SARL Kbis extract; Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs (RBE) filing; six-month source-of-funds bank statements for each contributing shareholder; source-of-wealth declaration; proof of residential address; and, if the shareholder is a corporate vehicle, complete ownership-chain evidence down to the individual level.
High-volume, low-value micro-payment flows and virtual-gift or tipping monetisation flagged as potential layering via user wallets.

Root cause: High-volume, low-value micro-payment flows and virtual-gift or tipping monetisation flagged as potential layering via user wallets.

Defense evidence: Documentary evidence that all user payments ingress exclusively through Apple App Store In-App Purchase and Google Play Billing, comprising platform payout statements, PSP merchant settlement reports, transaction-monitoring procedures, an approved AML/CTF programme under Articles L.561-5 CMF and L.561-2 French transposition, a refund policy, and a business-rules architecture that prohibits user-to-user wallet cash-outs.
Potential exposure to sanctioned persons or high-risk jurisdictions through unvetted streaming users.

Root cause: Potential exposure to sanctioned persons or high-risk jurisdictions through unvetted streaming users.

Defense evidence: IP-geoblocking evidence, device-fingerprint records, an IP-country blocking list aligned with OFAC, EU and French sanctions registers, automated sanctions-sanctions and-PEP screening outputs covering platform users and counterparties, and an external legal memorandum from a French avocat confirming that no sanctioned-jurisdiction payments are cleared or settled in the SARL treasury accounts.
Use of an EMI or fintech as the primary operating account may contravene the commercial registry's capital-deposit attestation requirement.

Root cause: Use of an EMI or fintech as the primary operating account may contravene the commercial registry's capital-deposit attestation requirement.

Defense evidence: A signed treasury policy letter confirming that capital escrow remains with a Tier 1 credit institution, that the EMI is used only as a disbursement agent, that daily EUR sweeps are executed back to the operational EUR account, and that the SARL maintains a single financial-account mapping for French tax and statutory audit purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route is a SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's non-French bank into the SARL's French euro capital-escrow account. The instruction must include in field 50 the transferring shareholder, in field 33B the exact EUR amount, and in fields 70/77 the remittance purpose: 'Apport en numéraire lors de la souscription d'actions SARL (capital increase)'. The ECB balance-of-payments purpose code 'DIN - Direct Investment' should be quoted to enable Banque de France statistical classification; the receiving French bank will complete the monthly TDF report for amounts at or above 50,000 EUR. The SARL must preserve the shareholder resolution, the capital-subscription slip, the bank's attestation de dépôt de capital issued after irrevocable blocking, and the fingerprint registration document. For a capital injection of 100,000 EUR or more, or where the shareholder is from a high-risk third country, the bank will additionally require an updated source-of-funds package, a sanctions/PEP screening confirmation or other UBO evidence before clearing the wire.For non-SEPA third-party suppliers (infrastructure, AI hosting, content licensing, affiliate networks), the primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 tracked via SWIFT gpi with a Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR) in field 121; the payment message references the commercial invoice and transaction purpose code. For EU suppliers, use SEPA Credit Transfer (CT) in ISO 20022 XML. For high-value purchases, an irrevocable confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit drawn on a French confirming bank (Crédit Agricole CIB, BNP Paribas Corporate & Institutional Banking, or Société Générale Global Banking & Advisory) may be issued, although documentary LC is not typically required for a digital streaming app with no physical inventory; the customised practice is advance payment or 30/60-day credit terms routed by MT103 with automatic reconciliation. All outbound disbursements above 50,000 EUR to non-EU beneficiaries are reported by the instructing French bank to Banque de France under the TDF monthly declaration, and the SARL must retain each invoice-backed payment order for five years.Plain vanilla EUR/USD and EUR/GBP outright foreign-exchange forwards (Livraison du contrat de change à terme) executed with ACPR-licensed credit institutions under an ISDA Master Agreement, with net amounts settled T+2.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only where the underlying economic exposure is real, illiquid and non-EUR (e.g., user-credit settlements or platform distributor payouts), executed onshore with a registered credit institution; prohibited for speculative or leverage purposes.Cross-currency interest rate swaps between EUR and a funding currency, permitted only if the SARL holds financial debt or lease liabilities, with EMIR clearing and transaction reporting obligations satisfied.European-style FX options and zero-cost collars purchased for hedging revenue-side currency exposure, limited to the amount of forecast cross-border inflows under the SARL's board-approved treasury policy; no unhedged speculation beyond documented transactional exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High for EU/EEA-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) passported into France under PSD2, such as Qonto, Shine, and Revolut Business, which may serve as operational expense and disbursement rails. Low for offshore, non-EEA digital EMIs, which cannot lawfully hold SARL client monies or execute SEPA and French domestic transfers without a French branch or an EU e-money or payment-institution passport. Critical legal constraint: an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot issue the 'attestation de dépôt de capital' certificate required by the French notary and the commercial registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce) for SARL capital subscription evidencing the bank holding of subscribed capital; therefore the capital-escrow function must be placed with a Tier 1 credit institution, while the EMI can be used as a low-value disbursement account with daily sweeping back to the Tier 1 operational EUR account.
