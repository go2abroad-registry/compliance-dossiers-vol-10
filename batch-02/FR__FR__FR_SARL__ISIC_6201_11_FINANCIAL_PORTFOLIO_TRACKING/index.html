Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum statutory capital for a SARL; founders may set capital freely. Cash contributions must be fully paid up at subscription and deposited with a French bank or notary escrow. If in-kind contributions exceed EUR 30,000 or if no in-kind valuation exists, a statutory auditor (Commissaire aux Apports) must assess their value. Escrow release occurs upon successful RCS registration or upon official refusal/abandonment.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is not required for a class 5 ERP unless the leased premises exceed applicable thresholds; however, the company must conduct a fire safety inspection of all office facilities, obtain structural load clearance for any IT server racks or heavy equipment, and comply with French Labour Code workplace health clearance requirements (Articles R.4212 et seq.).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP); businesses use the professional area of impots.gouv.fr and deal with the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Company formation and initial registration are filed through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, operated by INPI; INSEE assigns the SIREN and SIRET identifiers.

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, file incorporation and registration formalities through the Guichet unique, including the registered office, activity, managers, beneficial owners, and required corporate documents. On registration in the Registre national des entreprises and, where applicable, the Registre du commerce et des sociétés, INSEE assigns a SIREN and establishment-level SIRET; the DGFiP communicates the tax account and applicable corporate tax arrangements. A French VAT number is generally issued by the competent SIE after VAT registration; it uses the FR prefix and the prescribed key followed by the SIREN. A foreign-established taxable person without a French establishment must register with the competent French non-resident tax service before carrying out transactions requiring French VAT compliance; a non-EU-established person generally appoints a French fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement provides an exception. EU-established persons generally do not require a fiscal representative. OSS may be used for eligible cross-border EU B2C supplies but does not replace domestic registration obligations where those independently arise.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS). A qualifying SARL may elect temporary income-tax transparency under the small-business option, generally for up to five financial years and subject to statutory ownership, age, activity, and size conditions. A qualifying family-owned SARL conducting an eligible commercial, industrial, craft, or agricultural activity may elect the family-SARL partnership regime without that five-year limit, subject to continuing eligibility. These are statutory French tax elections, not US-style check-the-box elections; France has no general entity-classification election for a SARL. Hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusions in relevant cross-border arrangements but do not constitute an entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to a non-established taxable person making French-taxable supplies: registration is generally required from the first such transaction. A France-established small business may qualify for the domestic VAT franchise under applicable turnover limits; the general services threshold is EUR 37,500, with a higher tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250 under the rules applicable from 2025, subject to statutory conditions and legislative changes. The franchise is not a general exemption from registration for non-established suppliers. For electronically supplied B2C services to consumers in other EU Member States, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may affect place-of-supply treatment for eligible suppliers; OSS can be used to report destination-country VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the EU may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT, provided statutory export conditions are met and the supplier retains evidence that the goods left the EU. Intra-EU supplies are subject to separate exemption conditions, including customer VAT identification and transport evidence. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: B2B services are generally taxed where the business customer is established, often with customer-side reverse charge, while B2C electronically supplied services are generally taxed where the consumer resides under applicable place-of-supply rules. A French portfolio-tracking software subscription therefore requires transaction-specific place-of-supply and customer-status analysis; an export label alone does not establish a 0% rate.

  • For goods exported outside the EU: export customs declaration and its Movement Reference Number (MRN), together with customs exit confirmation or equivalent official evidence of exit.
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the shipment, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR, carrier confirmation, or other reliable proof connecting the goods to the export.
  • Commercial invoice, order, and delivery records supporting the identity of the supplier, customer, goods, destination, and transaction.
  • For services: contract, invoice, customer business-status and establishment evidence, and records supporting the applicable place-of-supply treatment. A customs export declaration and inward remittance certificate are not generally applicable requirements for services.
  • A foreign-exchange repatriation certificate is not a general French VAT zero-rating condition.

France is introducing mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing and transaction/payment e-reporting through approved platforms. From 1 September 2026, all in-scope businesses must be able to receive e-invoices; large and medium-sized businesses must issue them from that date, while SMEs and microbusinesses are generally required to issue them from 1 September 2027. Applicable B2C and cross-border transactions are generally subject to e-reporting rather than domestic B2B e-invoicing. The system is not a universal real-time pre-clearance or fiscal-stamping regime. Businesses must use the prescribed formats and transmission channels and comply with applicable platform and reporting requirements.

The supplier must substantiate the conditions for exemption and retain supporting records for the applicable statutory retention period. If export or other exemption conditions are not proven, the DGFiP may assess French VAT, late-payment interest, and penalties. Intentional omissions or inaccurate returns may attract increased penalties; fraud may trigger the most severe tax penalties and, where the statutory elements are met, criminal prosecution. Input VAT refunds may be denied or recovered where the underlying transactions do not support deduction. France imposes no general 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for claiming export VAT treatment.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a euro-area member state, maintains full and unrestricted convertibility for both capital-account and current-account transactions under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the French Monetary and Financial Code. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, royalties, repayments of shareholder loans, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred to non-residents without prior authorization or quantitative restriction. Settlement may be executed in EUR or any freely convertible currency. The only frictions are statistical declaration obligations for cross-border transactions and anti-money-laundering documentation requirements imposed by the credit institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds in foreign currency may be retained, held on account, or converted freely. For balance-of-payments statistics, resident entities must report cross-border payments to the Banque de France regardless of currency, generally through their bank's periodic reporting framework; however, the reporting obligation does not restrict conversion or liquidity management.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) and the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for exchange-rate policy, foreign-exchange reporting, and balance-of-payments compilation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with legal constraints. Authorized or passported Electronic Money Institutions / Payment Institutions under PSD2 may provide payment accounts with French or European IBANs, cross-currency settlement, and SEPA/SWIFT routing, but they are not licensed credit institutions under French law. They cannot offer deposit-taking, interest-bearing current accounts, or capital escrow services in a manner equivalent to a full bank. For a France SARL requiring capital injection escrow and operational disbursements, a bank-licensed account is preferred; an EU-licensed EMI is viable only as a supplementary operating rail and must disclose that funds are not covered by the French deposit guarantee scheme (FGDR).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders should route equity or shareholder-loan proceeds via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) from the remitting bank to the French SARL's EUR account. The underlying wire must reference a clear corporate action (e.g., 'Contribution en compte-courant d'associé' or 'Incorporation capital contribution'), and the French bank will require the executed shareholder resolution, articles of association, Crowe or notarial deed if applicable, beneficial-owner declarations, and source-of-funds evidence. For non-EEA inbound wires in foreign currency, settlement conversion to EUR is executed at market rate plus bank margin; the French bank reports the transaction for balance-of-payments statistics. All inbound remittances must emanate from the bank account of the qualified shareholder or lender; third-party financing through unlicensed money transmitters is not accepted.Outbound trade-related disbursements for software licensing, SaaS subscriptions, intercompany royalties, IT procurement, and consulting services are executed through SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA beneficiaries, SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA beneficiaries, and Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) where payment is conditional on documentary compliance. The French SARL treasury should maintain separate clearing rails: (i) SEPA direct debits and SEPA credit transfers for EU distribution partners and payroll; (ii) SWIFT MT103 with structured purpose codes (often referencing the EU BOP component type) for Asian and North American counterparties; and (iii) documentary collection or commercial LC for large-scale licensing or reseller transactions. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on gross dividends paid by a French-resident SARL to non-resident corporate shareholders (CGI Art. 119 bis, 2°); 12.8% for non-resident individuals plus applicable social levies; treaty rates may reduce the rate to 0%, 5%, or 15% depending on the jurisdiction and shareholding level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: France requires transfer-pricing documentation when the French entity's annual turnover or total assets exceed EUR 400,000,000 (LPF Art. L.13 AA). Group-wide Country-by-Country Reporting and Master File obligations arise when consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Entities below the thresholds must still maintain reliable functional, risk, and controlled-transaction records for tax audits.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation. Instead, anti-abuse doctrine operates through Article L.64 LPF (abus de droit), Article 155-A CGI, Article 119 bis CGI targeting interposed companies, and the Principal Purpose Test under double tax treaties. A French SARL without employees, premises, decision-making substance, or real risk-bearing activity is vulnerable to reclassification as a mere conduit, denial of treaty benefits, and reassessment of profit to the ultimate beneficiary.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to 80% of evaded tax for fraudulent or abusive conduct, plus 0.20% monthly late-payment interest. Criminal conviction under CGI Art. 1741 carries up to €75,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment for simple tax fraud, and up to €1,000,000 fine and 10 years imprisonment for aggravated fraud; legal persons may be subject to multiplied fines, dissolution, and a business-activity ban.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable through veil-piercing and the action en comblement du passif; criminal personal fines and imprisonment may be imposed; courts may issue management bans, asset seizure, and travel restrictions during criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Abusive Interposition

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under Article L.64 LPF with an 80% penalty where abuse is committed in bad faith (40% otherwise); denial of treaty benefits; additional penalties for false declarations. The tax administration may also apply the general anti-abuse rules of EU ATAD and impose fines for non-declaration of beneficial ownership.

Director personal liability: The court may disregard separate legal personality, hold directors jointly liable for recomputed tax and penalties, order personal restitution, and impose management bans of up to five years.
Money Laundering / AML-KYC Failures in Fintech Portfolio Tracking

Statutory sanction: ACPR administrative fines up to €5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for AML violations; criminal money-laundering sanctions up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine for individuals, with corporate fines multiplied to €1,875,000; confiscation of illicit assets and publication of sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability for complicity or intentional AML violations, personal asset confiscation, a ban on managing financial or commercial businesses, and travel bans pending criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Ex officio reassessment of French taxable income under Article 57 CGI; application of a 40% deliberate misreporting penalty and late-payment interest; non-production of transfer-pricing documentation or forms 2257-SD may trigger mandatory administrative fines and evidentiary burdens against the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors may be sued for faute de gestion and ordered to restore shortfalls in corporate assets; if manipulation is linked to intentional tax evasion, personal criminal liability follows, including imprisonment and disqualification from corporate management.
