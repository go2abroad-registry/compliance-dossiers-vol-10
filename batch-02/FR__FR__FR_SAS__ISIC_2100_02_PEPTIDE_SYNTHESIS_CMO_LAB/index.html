Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office under the Commercial Code, including Articles L123-11 to L123-11-5. A domiciliation company or qualifying director residence may be used where statutory conditions and a written domiciliation arrangement are satisfied. A registered office separate from the laboratory is legally possible, but the company must maintain genuine operational substance and accurate contact details.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI): formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; the resulting registration is recorded in the Registre national des entreprises and, where applicable, the Registre du commerce et des sociétés

Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation period equivalent to a 30-day reservation system. A name search or preliminary availability check is not a legally binding reservation. If the INPI or registry rejects a filing, obtain the electronic rejection notice and its legal grounds, correct the name or supporting documents, and resubmit through the Guichet unique. If the decision is an appealable administrative decision, file a prior administrative appeal with the competent authority within the applicable deadline stated in the notice, normally two months for an administrative challenge, and preserve evidence of electronic submission. A judicial challenge may be brought before the competent administrative or commercial court depending on the decision and legal issue. Trademark objections require separate proceedings before the INPI or the competent court. The filing should be reviewed for Code de commerce Articles L210-2 and L123-1 et seq., intellectual-property conflicts, regulated-sector terminology, and public-order concerns.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr. Customs duties and indirect rights (Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects, douane.gouv.fr) separately administer import VAT, export declarations, and the DELTA-DES electronic customs clearance system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SAS through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); INSEE allocates the 9-digit SIREN and the 14-digit SIRET and registers the entity in the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE). Step 2: Upon registration, the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) allocates the intra-Community VAT identification number in the format FR + two-digit control key + nine-digit SIREN; activation is generally confirmed within four to six weeks, after which the SAS may lawfully issue invoices bearing its French VAT ID. Step 3: A foreign-incorporated supplier not established in France has no SIREN and must register directly with the SIE for the place of its first taxable operation in France, obtain an EORI number for customs clearances, and, if it is a non-EU entity not covered by an OSS election, appoint a fiscal representative under CGI Article 289 A. Step 4: The SAS must activate its professional space on impots.gouv.fr, enroll in EDI/EFI electronic filing, and open an Intrastat (DEB) declaration account if annual intra-EU dispatches exceed the statutory declaration threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Election 1: CGI Article 239 bis AB allows a qualifying closed-held SAS with fewer than 50 employees to elect, for a limited maximum period of five fiscal years, taxation of its results at the level of its members under the Impôt sur le Revenu instead of the standard corporate income tax; the election requires waiver of the small-business VAT franchise. Election 2: CGI Article 223 A allows a French parent holding at least 95% of the SAS to elect fiscal integration (régime de groupe) for consolidated corporate income tax purposes, binding for five renewable fiscal years. Election 3: CGI Article 293 B permits election into the small-business VAT exemption franchise (franchise en base); this is generally adverse for a B2B CMO laboratory because input VAT deduction is forfeited. Election 4: The Crédit d'Impôt Recherche (CIR) is a statutory election/credit for qualifying peptide-synthesis research and development expenditures, claimed by form 2069-A and reported in the corporate income tax return. Election 5: Hybrid-mismatch elections — French law contains no check-the-box mechanism; entity classification is strictly determined by domestic law, and ATAD-2 aligned anti-hybrid rules under CGI Article 212 bis et seq. disallow deductions arising from hybrid instruments, hybrid entities, or dual-inclusion mismatches with the U.S. or other treaty counterparties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR — no registration threshold exists for French-established businesses; VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable economic activity and must be completed before issuing any taxable or export invoice. A non-established EU supplier effecting distance sales of goods to French consumers must register under the OSS once Union-wide distance sales exceed EUR 10,000 in a calendar year (CGI Article 258 A). A discretionary small-business exemption (franchise en base, CGI Article 293 B) applies up to EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services, with adjusted ceilings of EUR 91,900 and EUR 41,600 for well-established businesses; claiming this exemption forfeits input VAT recovery and is unsuitable for a GMP contract-manufacturing entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: export sales of tangible goods dispatched or transported by the French supplier or on its behalf outside the EU are definitively zero-rated under CGI Article 262-I-1°, provided the physical EU exit is substantiated by customs export declaration and transport evidence; goods must effectively leave the EU within a 90-day post-dispatch window. Services: (i) services directly connected to goods exported outside the EU, including tolling, synthesis, analysis and formulation services, may be exempt under CGI Article 262-I-2°(b) where the recipient is established outside the EU and the processed goods are physically exported; (ii) B2B services supplied to a taxable person established outside France are deemed supplied in the customer's country under CGI Article 259 A-1° and are outside the scope of French VAT, provided the customer's non-French taxable status is documented; (iii) supplies to established EU customers remain taxable in France but are reverse-charged (autoliquidation) to the customer under CGI Article 283-2 where the customer supplies a valid intra-Community VAT ID. For this peptide CMO SAS, physically exported GMP peptide batches qualify as zero-rated exports; contract development and synthesis services to a non-EU sponsor qualify as outside-scope services under Article 259 A, while services to French or EU pharma counterparties are subject to French VAT or reverse charge respectively.

  • Commercial invoice expressly citing 'TVA exonérée, art. 262-I CGI' for exported goods or 'non assujettie à la TVA française' for services outside scope

Mandatory e-invoicing in France: from 1 September 2026, large enterprises and mid-caps must issue and receive structured electronic invoices; from 1 September 2027, SMEs and micro-enterprises must comply. All domestic B2B invoices must be transmitted as structured XML data (FACTUR-X, UBL, or CII) through a DGFiP-approved Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the public invoicing portal (PPF / Chorus Pro). The regime also mandates transaction-level e-reporting of B2C sales and foreign/export supplies to the DGFiP. France does not apply real-time digital stamping or pre-clearance; the PPF operates as the central fiscal data-clearing hub, and export zero-rated transactions must be included in e-reporting with customs declaration references.

DGFiP cross-checks export zero-rating claims through transaction-level e-reporting, customs clearance databases, and dedicated tax audits. Missing or defective export evidence reverses the exemption and reassesses VAT at 20% plus monthly late-payment interest of 0.20% per month (CGI Article 1727). Misstatement penalties under CGI Article 1729 include 40% for bad faith (mauvaise foi) and 80% for deliberate concealment or fraudulent maneuvers. Criminal tax evasion under CGI Article 1741 exposes the responsible officers to a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and five years' imprisonment; customs fraud under Code des douanes Article 414 adds confiscation of the goods and fines of one to two times their value. Export proceeds not repatriated within the statutory window — the last day of the month following the contractual due date, benchmarked at 30 days (Article R.712-1-1 Code monétaire et financier) — attract a EUR 1,500 fine under Article 465-1 of the same code. The ordinary three-year audit window may be extended up to six or ten years where export evidence is concealed or fabricated, permitting retroactive clawback of refunded amounts.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to applicable European Union sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, customs, prudential, and reporting requirements. A French SAS may generally receive foreign capital, hold foreign-currency accounts, purchase foreign currency, pay foreign suppliers, make intercompany loans, and remit dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, and liquidation proceeds to non-residents without a general exchange-control approval. Profit remittances remain subject to distributable-profit rules, approved financial statements, corporate-law solvency and reserve requirements, withholding-tax analysis, treaty entitlement, beneficial-ownership and substance requirements, transfer-pricing support, sanctions screening, and bank verification of the economic purpose. Interest, royalties, service fees, and intercompany financing may require arm's-length pricing, contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation, withholding-tax review, and reporting under French tax and international information-exchange rules. Payments involving sanctioned persons, embargoed jurisdictions, dual-use goods, controlled chemicals, or regulated pharmaceutical materials may be blocked or require specific authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be surrendered to a central bank, converted into euros, or repatriated within a prescribed period. No mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export proceeds may normally remain in euros or foreign currency and may be retained in French or overseas bank accounts, subject to contractual banking terms, sanctions controls, tax accounting, customs evidence, VAT treatment, and statistical or balance-of-payments reporting where applicable. The SAS should retain export contracts, invoices, transport and customs records, proof of delivery, bank advices, and reconciliation schedules. Cash and payment instruments physically entering or leaving the European Union at a value of EUR 10,000 or more are subject to EU and French cash-control declarations; this does not generally apply to ordinary bank transfers.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. Foreign-exchange and payment controls are primarily governed by European Union law, French monetary and financial legislation, the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution), the Financial Markets Authority (Autorité des marchés financiers), and customs and statistical authorities where relevant.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine payments and treasury administration, but low as the sole banking platform for a regulated, cross-border peptide-synthesis CDMO. An EU-authorized electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, foreign-exchange services, virtual IBANs, and international transfers within its authorization and correspondent-bank arrangements; it does not provide a French bank deposit account, may not accept statutory share capital deposits unless legally and operationally supported for that purpose, and may impose narrower limits on high-value, high-risk, chemical, pharmaceutical, or non-EU transactions. Offshore digital EMIs outside the European Economic Area require passporting or French authorization, local-agent analysis, and confirmation that services are lawfully available to a French corporate customer. The SAS should maintain a regulated French or EU credit-institution account as primary account, use an EMI only as a documented secondary rail, and confirm safeguarding, insolvency treatment, SEPA access, SWIFT capability, sanctions controls, transaction limits, and account-closure procedures.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for routine payments and treasury administration, but low as the sole banking platform for a regulated, cross-border peptide-synthesis CDMO. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (Impôt sur les Sociétés); reduced 15% bracket on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs (turnover < EUR 10,000,000 and paid-up capital fully released, subject to ownership tests)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax (Art. 119bis-2 CGI) on distributions of French earnings to non-resident shareholders; 0% for parent companies qualifying under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (10% shareholding held for at least 24 months); 75% for shareholders resident in non-cooperative states (ETNC)

Economic substance scope: France applies the OECD substance-over-form doctrine through Art. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Art. 1741 CGI: up to 5 years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine (aggravated: 7 years and EUR 2,000,000 if executed via an intermediary jurisdiction or if coupled with money-laundering); tax administration adds 40% penalty for bad faith or 80% for manoeuvres frauduleuses and 0.20% per month interest; disclosure to the public prosecutor is mandatory for assessed fraud above EUR 10,000

Director personal liability: The SAS president (Président) who subscribes the fraudulent return incurs personal criminal liability; courts impose confiscation of instruments, travel bans (interdiction de quitter le territoire) and imprisonment orders; directors may be jointly ordered to pay the tax debt plus penalties where abuse is proven
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: EUR 14,000 annual fine for France; reversal of burden of proof authorizes the tax inspector to recharacterize cross-border profits; upward adjustment with 40% penalty if deemed non-arm's-length; simultaneous VAT and social security follow-on audits

Director personal liability: Directors ignoring formal requests risk personal assessment guarantees (art. L.267 LPF) for penalties; falsifying TP documents amounts to an obstructive act punishable under Art. 1746 CGI (EUR 15,000 fine and imprisonment terms for wilful obstruction)
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: Application of L.64 LPF: the imposted transaction is ignored and profits are re-attributed to France; 80% penalty on additional assessments; refusal of DTA benefits (FTC v. Above The Belt rule) and 75% ETNC withholding surcharge; publication on official tax blacklists

Director personal liability: The abuse of law procedure may be addressed personally against the managing president; decisions of the Comité de l'abus de droit fiscal bind the company; travel bans and seizure of personal assets are court-ordered where the arrangement evidences bad faith
Pharmaceutical Manufacturing License / ANSM Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal offense of unlawful manufacture of medicinal products (Art. L.5413-1 CSP): up to 5 years' imprisonment, EUR 750,000 fine, doubling for recidivism; administrative closure of the laboratory, withdrawal of good manufacturing practice (GMP) certificate; tax deductibility of related fines is denied

Director personal liability: The head of establishment, scientific directors and partners can be forced to close (fermeture définitive), personal interdiction to direct a pharmaceutical enterprise for up to 10 years, and confiscation of equipment; civil liability toward harmed CMO clients (contractual penalties, consequential damages)
AML / Tracfin Suspicious Transaction Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Art. 324-1 Penal Code: laundering proceeds of tax fraud — up to 5 years' imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine; administrative fine of EUR 75,000 (individual) or 5% of turnover (institution) for SAR omission; seizure of accounts and freezing of assets for anti-money laundering actions

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and president are individually indictable for failing SARs; travel bans under judicial control and confiscation of shares; directors can be barred from exercising commercial functions (interdiction de gérer) for up to 15 years and ordered to personally restitute suspicious funds traceable through corporate accounts
