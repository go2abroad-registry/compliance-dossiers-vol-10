Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation period equivalent to a fixed 30-day reservation. A proposed name is assessed during the registration filing and may be changed or rejected by the Guichet unique, registry, or relevant authority. Upon rejection, obtain the electronic refusal notice and stated deficiency, preserve the filing reference and evidence of prior clearance, correct the name or supporting documents, and resubmit within the applicable procedural deadline. Where the decision is an appealable formal administrative decision, file an administrative reconsideration or hierarchical appeal with the issuing authority and, where appropriate, challenge the decision before the competent administrative or commercial court within the stated deadline. Name rejection does not determine trademark validity; trademark and unfair-competition disputes require separate INPI or judicial analysis.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS; share capital is freely fixed in the articles of association. All cash subscriptions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account opened with a French credit institution or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; at least 50% of cash capital must be paid up upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years, and an attestation of deposit is required for registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required through the submission of a fire resistance report and, when applicable, a favourable opinion of the departmental fire and rescue service (SDIS); structural load inspections must be carried out by an approved technical inspection office (bureau de contrôle) with a conformity report; occupational clearance includes a workplace inspection by the Inspection du Travail, compliance with the French Labour Code, supply of the general internal regulation document, and a health and safety medical clearance for all key personnel operating the plant.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for an SAS; share capital is freely fixed in the articles of association. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), the French national tax administration under the Ministry of Economy and Finance; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; business formalities portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign owner forms an SAS through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); the resulting file is transmitted to DGFiP, which delivers the SIREN/SIRET, French tax identification number (TIN) and, on VAT activation, the French intra-Community VAT number. For a non-established foreign entity making taxable supplies of goods or services in France, a direct VAT registration application under CGI Article 286 quater must be filed with the DGFiP non-resident business service; registration is mandatory before the first taxable supply and no de minimis turnover threshold applies to such foreign applicants.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (French SAS is automatically subject to French corporate income tax under CGI Article 206; it is not a pass-through entity; French territoriality rules apply to the scope of profits taxable in France). — A foreign owner forms an SAS through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); the resulting file is transmitted to DGFiP, which delivers the SIREN/SIRET, French tax identification number (TIN) and, on VAT activation, the French intra-Community VAT number. For a non-established foreign entity making taxable supplies of goods or services in France, a direct VAT registration application under CGI Article 286 quater must be filed with the DGFiP non-resident business service; registration is mandatory before the first taxable supply and no de minimis turnover threshold applies to such foreign applicants.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (French SAS is automatically subject to French corporate income tax under CGI Article 206; it is not a pass-through entity; French territoriality rules apply to the scope of profits taxable in France).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Relevant elections include: the option for actual net profits under the normal real regime; the election by eligible group members for French tax consolidation (régime d'intégration fiscale) under CGI Article 223 A; the option for VAT group registration (assujettis unis) among qualifying French entities; and, with ministerial approval, the election for the worldwide exemption (bénéfice mondial) or worldwide consolidation (bénéfice consolidé) regimes. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are determined by CGI Article 212 bis and OECD practices; there is no elective recharacterization or check-the-box election for the SAS itself.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for a French-established SAS; every taxable supply falls within the VAT net. For non-established foreign VAT-registrable persons, there is also no statutory turnover threshold. Domestic small-business franchise thresholds (€85,800 for sale of goods and €37,500 for services) are not available to foreign entities or export-only taxpayers; the EU cross-border intra-Community distance-selling threshold is €10,000 per calendar year for EU-established suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under CGI Article 262-I, the French SAS may zero-rate machinery exported outside the European Union only if the goods are physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU and the export is established by proper customs export documentation, transport evidence, and invoice. Directly linked freight, transport and insurance services qualify for the same exemption under Article 262-II CGI. Offshore business services and intangible services supplied to a non-EU business recipient may be outside French VAT under Articles 259, 259 A and 259 C CGI if the place-of-supply test is met. The invoice must cite the applicable exemption and the SAS must possess the documentary proof before declaring the transaction; copies must be retained for the statutory period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/ECS) with valid Export MRN and customs exit confirmation showing the goods left French/EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice (export format) quoting the VAT zero-rating basis and stating the destination and terms of sale (Incoterms)
  • Packing list with exact itemised weights, packaging details, marks, and container/seal numbers
  • Bill of Lading (sea), CMR (road), or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical export movement and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/telex advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the offshore buyer within the FX repatriation period

France's facturation électronique reform (Ordonnance 2023-259) will make structured electronic invoicing (Factur-X/UBL) and e-reporting compulsory for B2B transactions, transmitted through a certified dematerialisation platform (PDP) connected to DGFiP by API. The mandated timetable begins on 1 September 2026 for large companies and reaches SMEs during 2027. No general per-invoice real-time tax clearance or state fiscal stamping is applied to ordinary exports; export movements are instead validated by the Customs ECS/ICS system issuing an electronic Export MRN and exit confirmation.

DGFiP and French Customs jointly target export zero-rating claims through document-intensive audits, comparing customs records, transport documents, invoices, and FX receipts. If the export proof is absent or the goods are found not to have left the EU, the 0% VAT exemption is retrospectively clawed back, and 20% TVA plus monthly late-payment interest (CGI Article 1727) is assessed from the date of supply. Bad-faith under-declaration triggers a 40% penalty (CGI Article 1729); deliberate or organised fraud triggers an 80% penalty. False customs declarations may be prosecuted under the Code des douanes with fines of one to two times the value of the goods, confiscation, and imprisonment for individuals. Tax-fraud criminal sanctions can be imposed under CGI Article 1741, directors may be held jointly liable under LPF Article L.266, and the ordinary reassessment period is three years after the filing year (LPF Article L.176), extended in cases of fraud or undisclosed foreign operations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member state; the EUR is fully convertible in both current and capital accounts. No exchange-control approval, quota, license, or prior declaration is required for inbound equity injections into a resident SAS, for external borrowing, or for outbound dividend and loan repayments. Liberalisation is anchored in Article 63 TFEU (free movement of capital), and the French régime de la réglementation des changes has been fully deregulated since 1989–1990. The only residual hard obligations are: (i) statistical — cross-border flows of EUR 50,000 or more must be coded with a Banque de France purpose code (code motif) and reported by the collecting bank; and (ii) tax — dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders trigger French withholding (standard statutory rate around 25–30% depending on recipient status, with 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the EU parent holds at least 5% of the SAS, and further treaty relief available for third-country parents). The SAS may invoice freely in EUR, USD, CNH, or any convertible currency; profit remittance is not blocked by the central bank and requires only approved French GAAP statutory accounts and, for payments above the threshold, correct BOP coding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation applies in France. There is no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to sell foreign-currency export receipts. The SAS may keep USD/CNY proceeds in designated foreign-currency accounts and decide freely on conversion timing. The only binding obligations are (i) reporting of each export collection equal to or exceeding EUR 50,000 to the Banque de France through the bank, using the merchandise-trade purpose code under the BOP nomenclature; (ii) annual recognition of FX gains/losses in the French corporate income tax return; and (iii) standard EU cross-border cash declaration rules, which are irrelevant for bank-cleared trade payments.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), the central bank of the French Republic, administers the balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical reporting regime under Articles L141-4 et seq. and R152-1 et seq. of the Code monétaire et financier (CMF); prudential supervision and AML compliance of credit institutions and e-money institutions are delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

High feasibility for daily operational EUR inflows/outflows, payroll, and SEPA Instant clearing via Qonto, Shine, or Revolut Business (EEA-licensed e-money or credit institutions supervised by the ACPR/ECB). Low feasibility for trade finance: EMIs legally cannot grant credit, issue commercial Letters of Credit, or act as principal in FX derivatives. Recommended hybrid architecture: use Qonto as the French operational clearing and collection account for EU buyers, while BNP Paribas or CA-CIB acts as the sole primary bank for commercial LC transactions (MT700/MT760), SWIFT MT103/MT202 wires, and ISDA/FBF-based FX hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SAS receives initial and subsequent equity injections from the non-resident parent via SWIFT MT103 using the Banque de France purpose code mapped to direct investment in a resident company (capital participation). EUR-denominated injections from an EEA parent may instead travel via SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) to the SAS IBAN; both rails are unrestricted. Non-EUR injections (USD/CNH) are credited to a foreign-currency account at the receiving French bank and converted to EUR at market rate when required. The mandatory verification package comprises the capital-increase minutes, attestation de dépôt des fonds, UBO declaration, and source-of-funds documentation. Shareholder loans (comptes courants d'associés) use a separate BOP purpose code for external loans and are documented for thin-capitalisation purposes at tax level; no FX authorisation is required. All flows of EUR 50,000 or more are automatically reported by the bank to the Banque de France.The SAS uses: (1) SWIFT MT103 or MT103/COVER for T/T settlement of import sub-assemblies and supplier invoices outside the SEPA zone; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EUR payments to EU component suppliers; (3) Commercial Documentary Credits — where the SAS, as seller, receives LCs (MT700) issued by overseas buyer banks and negotiates or discounts compliant presentations through its Tier-1 bank against invoice, bill of lading, packing, insurance, and certificates; (4) opening of commercial LCs by the SAS for its own purchases of high-end components from Asia and the United States; and (5) bank guarantees and standby LCs (MT760) supporting performance and advance-payment obligations under instalment contracts. Every outbound conversion requires the corresponding BOP purpose code (trade in goods or payments for imports) and settles at the bank's daily market rate.Outright FX Forwards (change à terme) under a French FBF Master Agreement or ISDA — EUR/USD and EUR/CNY tenors aligned to bottling-line milestone payments.Plain Vanilla FX Options (calls/puts) with upfront premium for contingency hedging of competitive international tenders.Cross-Currency Swaps (swaps de change) — EUR/USD for multi-year instalment receivables matched against import payables.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) — permitted for illiquid or restricted currencies (e.g., EGP, NGN, XOF/XAF, DZD) settled in EUR/USD through the bank's offshore NDF desk.Zero-Cost FX Collars (collars de change) protecting minimum margins on large multi-year aseptic packaging line contracts without upfront premium.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for daily operational EUR inflows/outflows, payroll, and SEPA Instant clearing via Qonto, Shine, or Revolut Business (EEA-licensed e-money or credit institutions supervised by the ACPR/ECB). Low feasibility for trade finance: EMIs legally cannot grant credit, issue commercial Letters of Credit, or act as principal in FX derivatives. Recommended hybrid architecture: use Qonto as the French operational clearing and collection account for EU buyers, while BNP Paribas or CA-CIB acts as the sole primary bank for commercial LC transactions (MT700/MT760), SWIFT MT103/MT202 wires, and ISDA/FBF-based FX hedging.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 annual net turnover (exclusive of tax) and/or gross balance-sheet total; the same threshold applies for related foreign enterprises controlling or controlled by the French entity. A French entity with cross-border related-party transactions below EUR 400 million must still be able to produce transfer pricing documentation upon 30-day formal request under Article L13 AA CGI; documentation obligation arises where the turnover threshold is met and covers the French Local File, group Master File when applicable, and benchmarking of arm's length results.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations; instead it imposes an economic-substance requirement through the abus de droit doctrine (Article L64 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales), judicial substance-over-form analysis, EU ATAD anti-hybrid and principal-purpose-test rules. A French SAS that cannot substantiate employees, physical premises, banking substance, real business purpose, and genuine decision-making will be disregarded for tax purposes, with distributions/royalties reclassified and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of all omitted taxes, 80% fraud penalty, 0.2% per-month interest, criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and up to 5 years' imprisonment for individuals; legal entities may be fined up to EUR 500,000 or five times the illegal gain, and publication of the sanction may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for company tax debts under abuse-of-law and mismanagement doctrines, subject to criminal conviction for involvement in fraud, management bans, travel controls, seizure of personal assets, and civil restitution for amounts not withheld or wilfully concealed.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Application of abus de droit (L64 LPF) permits the tax administration to set aside the artificial arrangement, recharacterize the underlying flows, disallow treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test, and impose an 80% abusive penalty with interest; the company may also be judicially dissolved as a fictitious entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers risk joint and several liability for resulting tax, penalties, and interest, personal payment of corporate debts if the lack of substance amounts to mismanagement causing insolvency, and travel bans in the context of criminal abuse proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Violations

Statutory sanction: France may re-integrate the transferred profits under Article 57 CGI; additional corporate income tax is assessed together with a 40% bad-faith penalty (or 80% for fraudulent manipulation) and monthly delay interest. Failure to provide TP documentation within 30 days of request triggers a EUR 10,000-per-document sanction capped at EUR 50,000 and a presumption that the advantage is unjustified.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held civilly liable for the full additional assessment if they knowingly approved non-arm's-length pricing without economic justification, and may face criminal proceedings under the wider tax fraud umbrella, including de facto directorship liability and asset freezing.
AML / Money Laundering and Payment Integrity

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is punishable under the French Monetary and Financial Code by up to 10 years' imprisonment and a fine up to EUR 750,000, plus confiscation of the laundered assets; corporate entities face fines up to EUR 750,000 and administrative penalties by the ACPR, including prohibition from commercial activity.

Director personal liability: Directors are individually liable for knowing participation in or wilful blindness to laundering; sanctions include personal imprisonment, seizure of personal and corporate property, exclusion from corporate management, and a travel ban during judicial investigation.
Withholding Tax and Dividend Distribution Noncompliance

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid withholding tax directly from the distributing company plus late-payment interest and a 10% non-payment penalty; the French entity may lose the corresponding tax credit and deduction, and can be subject to administrative fines up to EUR 150,000 for multiple violations.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize non-compliant distributions become personally liable for the recovered WHT and penalties, may be subject to to seizure of company funds under anti-fraud orders, and could face criminal sanctions for fraud if the non-withholding was intentional and material.
