Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du commerce et des societes (RCS) administered by the Greffe du Tribunal de commerce of the registered office (Paris); all formation filings are submitted exclusively through the INPI-operated Guichet unique electronique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title III — Articles L. 223-1 to L. 223-43 on the Societe a Responsabilite Limitee (SARL); implementing decree Articles R. 223-1 et seq.; formation and registration formalities under Articles L. 123-1, L. 123-5 and R. 123-304 et seq.; beneficial ownership declaration under Article L. 561-46 of the Monetary and Financial Code; Loi PACTE n° 2019-486 and Decret n° 2021-300 establishing the Guichet unique.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts constitutifs (Articles of Association) executed either as a private deed (acte sous seing prive) or as a notarial deed, accompanied by the single incorporation declaration transmitted via the Guichet unique.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies (Article L. 223-2 alinea 2 of the Commercial Code); the articles must freely fix the capital amount. If the fixed capital is below EUR 10,000, cash contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation; if capital is EUR 10,000 or more, at least one-fifth of each cash contribution must be liberated at subscription and the balance within five years (Article L. 223-7). The subscribed funds must be deposited into a blocked escrow account with a credit institution or the Caisse des Depots et Consignations and evidenced by a blocking certificate; the funds may be released only upon presentation of the K-bis registration certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification under the ERP regime (Type W workplaces): premises accommodating 301 or more persons or exceeding 7,000 m² require a commission de securite approval; below those thresholds, a technical fire-safety inspection by an approved bureau de controle is recommended. Electrical installations in new or renovated offices require the CONSUEL conformity attestation. Structural load clearance by a technical controller is required where office floor loads exceed 250 kg/m². Occupational clearance: registration with the regional occupational health service (SST), establishment of the Document unique d'evaluation des risques professionnels (DUERP) under Articles R. 4121-1 et seq., and completion of information and prevention visits for each employee, including ergonomic assessment of video-display-unit workstations under Articles R. 4541-1 et seq. of the Labour Code.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies (Article L. 223-2 alinea 2 of the Commercial Code); the articles must freely fix the capital amount. If the fixed capital is below EUR 10,000, cash contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation; if capital is EUR 10,000 or more, at least one-fifth of each cash contribution must be liberated at subscription and the balance within five years (Article L. 223-7). The subscribed funds must be deposited into a blocked escrow account with a credit institution or the Caisse des Depots et Consignations and evidenced by a blocking certificate; the funds may be released only upon presentation of the K-bis registration certificate.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); digital portal: impots.gouv.fr (professional/enterprise area); corporate registry interface: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique, operated with INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered at formation via the Guichet Unique de l'INPI, which issues the SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) numbers; the DGFiP assigns the VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) ex officio in the format 'FR' + 2-key + SIREN without dots. Foreign entities not established in France apply for a French VAT number directly to the SIE using Cerfa form no. 15495*03 (and no. 15767*01 where relevant), submitting proof of foreign incorporation, statutory documents, evidence of the first taxable transaction, and — for third-country suppliers — a local tax representative appointment. The VAT number must appear on all B2B invoices and intra-community recapitulative statements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the entity level: 25% standard rate, with a 15% reduced rate on the first EUR 42,500 of annual taxable profit for qualifying SMEs. Small or family-controlled SARLs may elect, under CGI Article 239 bis AA, to be taxed transparently at the member level (impôt sur le revenu), subject to eligibility conditions and the consequences of the option. France transposes ATAD/ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch rules: foreign entity classification (opaque vs transparent) is assessed under Article 238 bis CGI (qualified 'check-the-box'-type analysis), with corresponding anti-hybrid disallowances; there is no literal US-style check-the-box election, but the CGI options function as statutory classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for a French-established SARL: VAT registration is compulsory for the video game publishing/digital software activity (ISIC 5820) irrespective of turnover; the franchise de TVA thresholds (EUR 91,900 for goods and EUR 36,800 for services for 2025) do not apply to digital publishing or electronically supplied services to EU consumers. For intra-community distance sales of digital services to consumers, the annual EUR 10,000 OSS threshold applies: once exceeded, VAT must be declared in each EU member state of the consumer through the OSS (Non-Union or Union procedure).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération with credit) applies under CGI Article 262-I-1° for tangible goods physically exported outside the EU. For intangible/digital services — including MMORPG licensing, online access, in-app virtual goods, hosting, and downloadable content — 0% VAT applies when the place of supply is outside France: B2B services to a recipient established outside the EU (Article 259-1 CGI), B2C digital services to consumers outside the EU (Article 259 D CGI), and intra-EU B2B supplies to a VAT-registered customer with a valid EU VAT number (Article 262 ter CGI). The supplier must evidence the customer's non-French establishment, the commercial substance of the export, and the arm's-length nature of platform payouts; pure domestic French B2C digital sales attract 20% VAT, and intra-EU B2C digital sales attract the customer's member-state rate via OSS.

  • Customs export declaration (DAU) and exit summary declaration (ICS2 / e-FR) for any physical media or hardware
  • Export invoice identifying the buyer, the nature of the digital goods/services (MMORPG licence, virtual items, server access), and a clear 0% TVA mention with legal basis
  • Contractual documentation: online terms of service, software license agreement, order confirmations, and app-store payout reports (Apple App Store Connect / Google Play Console)
  • Proof of non-French establishment of the customer: valid EU VAT number for intra-EU B2B supplies, or government-issued address/identity evidence, IP-address/location logs for B2C consumers outside the EU
  • Payment / foreign-exchange evidence: SWIFT confirmations, PSP settlement statements (Apple/Google payouts, Stripe, Braintree), banker's attestation de change / inward remittance certificates, evidencing repatriation of proceeds within the statutory deadline

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is phased in under the 2026–2027 French tax reform: from 1 September 2026, all French taxable persons must issue structured e-invoices through certified partner dematerialization platforms (PDPs) interconnected with the DGFiP (Chorus Pro/PPF); B2C and cross-border transaction data must be transmitted via periodic e-reporting. The DGFiP does not perform real-time fiscal stamping of invoices, but complete invoice records must be retained for six (6) years and produced electronically during tax audits (CGI Article L 102 B). Every intra-EU B2B invoice must include the French and recipient VAT numbers; OSS quarterly returns and payment are filed directly on the DGFiP OSS portal.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict DGFiP tax audit (vérification de comptabilité) and DGDDI customs scrutiny. Unsubstantiated or fraudulent zero-rating triggers: retroactive reversal of the exemption and output VAT recovery at 20%; late-payment interest (intérêts de retard, approximately 0.20% per month); and penalties under CGI Article 1729 — 40% for deliberate bad-faith omissions (manquement délibéré), 80% for fraudulent activity (manœuvres frauduleuses / abus de droit), and up to 100% for organized tax fraud. Criminal sanctions under CGI Article 1741 (tax evasion / escroquerie fiscale) include up to five (5) years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine (EUR 1,000,000 for organized fraud). Clawback and reassessment periods extend to six (6) years generally, and up to ten (10) years for concealed activity; input VAT wrongly deducted on export supplies is disgorged with interest, and repeated offenders may face suspension of customs/export authorizations and debarment from the OSS.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full euro convertibility for both capital and current account transactions; France has maintained unrestricted capital movement since the 1988 liberalisation and transposes Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no central-bank exchange controls, no prior approvals for current-account remittances, and no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, royalty outflow, or intercompany repayment by a French SARL to non-resident shareholders. Remittance constraints are limited to: (i) EU/UN/OFAC financial-sanctions screening under Article L.561-1 of the Code monétaire et financier; (ii) the administrative prior-authorisation regime for foreign investments in sensitive sectors under the 2019 Pacte Law, which does not apply to digital MMORPG publishing (ISIC 5820); and (iii) French withholding tax of 25% on dividend distributions to non-EU parent corporations, reducible to 0-15% under applicable double-tax treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds at least 5% (10% under the Directive) of the SARL capital for 24 months.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender period and no mandatory conversion ratio on any export receipts. The SARL may lawfully retain revenues from Apple Distribution International Ltd and Google Commerce Ltd (both Irish-resident settlement entities) in EUR or any foreign currency, in France or abroad. The only binding reporting obligations are: (i) French credit institutions must include cross-border settlements exceeding EUR 300,000 per calendar month per counterpart in the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical return; (ii) the SARL must file the annual Déclaration d'Échanges de Services (DES/SDES) for cross-border service exports and imports where the annual threshold is exceeded, which will be the case given global MMORPG license, marketing, and server-hosting flows; and (iii) transfers exceeding EUR 10,000 must be supported by documented underlying commercial or financing evidence at the request of the bank under France's AML/CTF framework. No export documentation (e.g., a certificate of repatriation or exchange-control form) is required for digital service exports.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB), the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) for foreign-investment screening and trade statistics, and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the competent prudential and AML/CFT supervisor for French credit institutions and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for French-authorised EMIs (Qonto, Shine, Manager.one, Blank) and EU-passported EMIs (Revolut, Wise Europe, N26) that operate under PSD2 licences, provided the SARL maintains a parallel anchor operating account with a French tier-1 bank for trade finance and treasury credit lines. Medium for a standalone EMI-only structure, as ACPR supervisory practice and French corporate creditors expect a substantive banking history. Legal constraints: (i) non-EU offshore digital EMIs without a French or EU PSD2 licence cannot lawfully hold operating funds for a French SARL; (ii) UK-headquartered EMIs lost EU passporting after Brexit and must operate through EU-licensed partner entities; (iii) SEPA anti-IBAN-discrimination provisions in Regulation (EU) No 260/2012 entitle the SARL to reject refusal of instant SEPA credit transfers solely because the IBAN is not French; and (iv) Apple and Google payout settlement requires a fully compliant SEPA IBAN and clean inbound credit history, which French EMIs provide.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial share capital of the SARL is deposited via a domestic virement into a blocked formation account (compte de dépôt de fonds) at the onboarding bank; upon RCS registration and issuance of the extrait Kbis, the blocked account is automatically released to the operating current account. For inbound capital contributions from a non-resident sole shareholder, the compliant protocol is a SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer to the SARL's IBAN with ISO External Purpose Code INVS (Investment), free-text 'Apport en capital - constitution SARL', and supporting documentation including the signed statuts, the shareholders' capital-subscription resolution, the RBE extract, and a completed bank source-of-funds questionnaire. No prior French Treasury authorisation is required because digital game publishing is not a sensitive sector under Article L.151-3 of the Code monétaire et financier; however, the receiving bank must report cross-border financial flows in its monthly Banque de France balance-of-payments return when thresholds are met, and any single credit over EUR 10,000 must be fully evidenced under Article L.561-15 CMF.Outbound treasury clearing uses the following rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer / SEPA Instant (XML pacs.008) for all euro-denominated EU supplier payments, salaries, and French social contributions; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High for French-authorised EMIs (Qonto, Shine, Manager.one, Blank) and EU-passported EMIs (Revolut, Wise Europe, N26) that operate under PSD2 licences, provided the SARL maintains a parallel anchor operating account with a French tier-1 bank for trade finance and treasury credit lines.
