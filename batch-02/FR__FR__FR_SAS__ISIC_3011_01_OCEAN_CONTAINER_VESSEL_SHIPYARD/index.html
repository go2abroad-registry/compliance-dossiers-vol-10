Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual offices are not permitted for a SAS engaged in commercial shipbuilding of deep-sea dry cargo and container vessels (ISIC 3011). Pursuant to Article L. 123-11-1 of the Code de commerce and Article 2 of Décret n° 2017-1094, the registered office must be a physical address where the company can be reached for service of process and where its effective management is located. For shipbuilding activities requiring statutory licenses (Autorisation d'Exercice de la Profession de Constructeur Naval), the registered office must be a verifiable industrial site with adequate infrastructure (dry docks, slipways, fabrication halls). The use of a virtual office or domiciliation service is incompatible with the industrial licensing requirements of the Direction Générale de l'Armement (DGA) and the Ministry of the Sea for shipbuilding, and may trigger administrative penalties, including rejection of the RCS filing and fines under Article L. 123-11-2 of the Code de commerce.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS) kept by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce, with electronic filing via the INPI-operated Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title II, Chapter VII, Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-4 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); Articles L. 210-1 to L. 210-10 (formation and capacity); Articles R. 123-101 et seq. and R. 123-160 (registration and RCS formalities).

Formation / constitutional filings

Demande d'immatriculation au RCS (formulaire M0/M des sociétés) transmitted through the Guichet unique, accompanied by the executed statuts, attestation de dépôt de fonds, déclaration de bénéficiaire effectif, and attestation de parution dans un support d'annonces légales.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS; the articles of association freely fix the capital. Cash contributions must be fully paid into a blocked account held by a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and funds may only be released after RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a statutory appraiser's report (commissaire aux apports) unless legal relief applies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the local fire safety commission after inspection, structural load inspection and clearance for slipways, quays, dry docks, gantry cranes and hull assembly halls by an approved technical control bureau, and workplace health and safety authorization from the Labour Inspectorate (Inspection du travail), including compliance with electrical, machinery, confined-space and vessel-in-build access rules.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS; the articles of association freely fix the capital. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Non-established foreign businesses generally deal with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). The principal online portals are impots.gouv.fr and the professional account (espace professionnel); business-formation formalities are filed through the Guichet unique operated by INPI.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the Guichet unique. Upon registration, it receives a SIREN and establishment-specific SIRET from INSEE; the SIREN is its principal French business identifier. It must obtain or confirm its French VAT number (FR followed by a two-character key and the nine-digit SIREN) with the competent SIE before making transactions requiring VAT registration. A foreign company without a French establishment applies to the SIEE for VAT identification before commencing taxable French transactions; the application normally includes proof of legal existence, constitutional documents, evidence of business activity, identity and authority of representatives, and transaction details. A business established in another EU Member State generally does not appoint a French fiscal representative. A business established outside the EU generally must appoint a French fiscal representative, subject to applicable legal exceptions or mutual-assistance arrangements. Foreign taxable persons making domestic taxable supplies generally have no small-business registration threshold and must register when required by the nature of their transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) by default. Certain qualifying young companies may elect temporarily for income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, IR), generally for up to five fiscal years, if statutory conditions are met, including age, ownership, workforce, turnover or balance-sheet, and non-listing requirements. The election is not an unrestricted check-the-box election and does not make a SAS generally pass-through eligible. No general French check-the-box or elective hybrid-mismatch classification regime applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for a foreign taxable person making transactions for which French VAT registration is required; France generally does not provide a registration threshold for such non-established businesses. The domestic small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base) has separate turnover limits, generally EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for services, subject to applicable statutory rules, prior-year tests, and EU small-enterprise scheme rules. These exemption limits are not a general threshold for foreign businesses or for transactions outside the exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the EU may be exempt with a right to deduct input VAT, subject to the statutory conditions and evidence that the goods actually left the EU. The supplier must retain the export customs declaration and reliable evidence of exit. Intra-Community supplies may qualify for exemption with a right to deduct where the buyer is identified for VAT in another Member State, the goods are dispatched to that Member State, and the supplier satisfies applicable invoicing, reporting, and evidence requirements. Services are not zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore: their VAT treatment depends on place-of-supply rules, customer status, service category, and any applicable exemption. For a B2B service supplied to a taxable customer established outside France, the general place-of-supply rule commonly places taxation where the customer is established, subject to exceptions.

  • Export customs declaration showing the export procedure and the relevant MRN
  • Customs confirmation of exit from the EU or other reliable evidence of actual export
  • Commercial invoice identifying the parties, goods, values, and applicable VAT treatment
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route, such as a bill of lading, sea waybill, CMR, or air waybill
  • Purchase order, contract, delivery records, and proof of payment where relevant to substantiate the transaction
  • Inward remittance or bank records where available and relevant; French law does not impose a universal inward-remittance certificate requirement for VAT zero-rating

France does not generally require real-time tax-authority clearance or digital stamping of each VAT invoice. For domestic B2B transactions within scope, mandatory electronic invoicing is being phased in: all businesses must be able to receive e-invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance begins on that date for large and intermediate-sized businesses and is scheduled from 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises. In-scope invoices must use a compliant structured format and be transmitted through an approved platform (plateforme agréée, formerly PDP) or the public portal as permitted by the applicable rules. Electronic transaction and payment data reporting (e-reporting) applies to specified transactions outside the e-invoicing mandate, including relevant B2C and cross-border transactions. Applicable rollout dates and technical requirements should be verified against current legislation and DGFiP guidance.

No general French statutory deadline requires export proceeds to be repatriated to France; EU capital-movement rules generally permit cross-border transfers, subject to reporting, sanctions, and other applicable controls. If export or exemption conditions are not substantiated, the DGFiP may assess output VAT, deny or recover input-VAT deductions or refunds, and charge late-payment interest, generally 0.20% per month under the applicable rules. Tax penalties may include a 40% surcharge for deliberate failure and an 80% surcharge in specified cases of fraudulent conduct or abuse, subject to statutory qualifications and procedural rights. Serious conduct may also entail criminal exposure.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a eurozone and EU member, applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the French Monetary and Financial Code. There are no systemic exchange controls, no mandatory conversion for trade proceeds and no investment authorisation gates for ordinary inbound equity or shareholders loans. Regulatory formalities are limited to balance-of-payments declarations to the Banque de France for significant flows, EU/UN sanctions compliance, and standard fiscal/dividend procedures. Profit remittance via dividends, interest, royalties or intercompany repayment is lawful and not subject to FX authorisation, provided the underlying transactions are economically substantiated and timely reported at the applicable threshold.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender regime exists in France. Exporters are free to retain, use, and convert export receipts without any mandatory conversion ratio, statutory timeline, or designated bank account requirement. However, the Banque de France requires balance-of-payments reporting for cross-border transactions above the prevailing declaration threshold (currently €750,000 per payment for non-bank filers through intermediary banks), and proceeds arising from qualifying export contracts must be traceable within the enterprise accounting records.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French central bank) with the Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR) and the General Directorate of the Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Recovery administering monetary, banking and external financial regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate and conditional. A non-EEA electronic money institution cannot operate in France without either an ACPR licence or an EU/EEA home-state authorization passporting through the Single Euro Payments Area. Off-balance-sheet digital EMIs lack the full prudential standing and documentary infrastructure for maritime instalment LCs and escrow accounts, and many are unwilling to support the medium-risk enhanced due diligence profile required for a large vessel shipyard. They are viable for onboarding an ancillary collection account or for facilitating initial low-value deposits, but the primary banking relationship should rest with a tier-1 French credit institution to support capital escrow and commercial LC workflows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate and conditional. A non-EEA electronic money institution cannot operate in France without either an ACPR licence or an EU/EEA home-state authorization passporting through the Single Euro Payments Area. Off-balance-sheet digital EMIs lack the full prudential standing and documentary infrastructure for maritime instalment LCs and escrow accounts, and many are unwilling to support the medium-risk enhanced due diligence profile required for a large vessel shipyard. They are viable for onboarding an ancillary collection account or for facilitating initial low-value deposits, but the primary banking relationship should rest with a tier-1 French credit institution to support capital escrow and commercial LC workflows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 in French group consolidated annual revenue or total consolidated assets triggers mandatory Master File and Local File preparation and filing under Articles L.13 AA and L.13 AB CGI; below this threshold, the SAS must retain a proportionate arm's-length documentation file for audit defense.

Economic substance scope: France applies no offshore ESR regime; instead, the General Anti-Abuse Rule (Art. L64 LPF), EU ATAD/DAC6, CFC legislation, and OECD BEPS principles require real economic presence. A functioning shipyard with physical yards, employed engineers and tradesmen, procurement authority, vessel design activity, classification-society oversight, and French regulatory licenses constitutes strong economic substance and should be preserved in all governance and intercompany contracts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 40% for bad faith and 80% for organized fraud under Art. 1729 CGI; default interest of 0.20% per month; criminal fine under Art. 1741 of up to EUR 500,000 or six times the evaded tax plus five years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable under Art. L.267 LPF for the corporate tax debt where they intentionally conceal assets; personal bank accounts and real estate are seizable, and courts may impose a travel ban, a five-year management disqualification, and imprisonment for a tax-evasion conviction.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the entire scheme under the GAAR with full tax reversal; 80% penalty where the arrangement has no purpose other than tax avoidance; cancellation of refundable tax credits; possible dissolution of the company for sham management.

Director personal liability: Judges can pierce the corporate veil, rendering directors personally liable for taxes, interest and penalties; executives face civil restitution, freezing orders, travel bans, and a professional ban preventing them from managing any French company.
AML / Financial Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 1 million or four times the sums laundered; asset freezing orders; withdrawal of the shipyard's export or maritime license; criminal penalties under the Customs Code of up to five years' imprisonment and unlimited asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors face individual criminal liability, personal asset seizure, travel bans, and prohibition from international trade; the compliance officer and signatories are equally exposed where they fail to block sanctioned payments or file the required suspicious transaction report.
Transfer Pricing and Thin-Capitalization Violation

Statutory sanction: Tax recharacterization of the controlled transaction; adjustment of taxable income; 40% penalty under Art. 57 CGI, increased to 80% if the profit is transferred to a privileged tax jurisdiction; where required documentation is not produced within 30 days, a penalty equal to 5% of the undocumented transaction value.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign knowingly false intercompany agreements or issue fictitious pricing certificates risk personal civil liability for the resulting tax shortfall; in aggravated cases, the French criminal court can issue travel bans, impose custodial sentences, and order restitution from personal estates.
