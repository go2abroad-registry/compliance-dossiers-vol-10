Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

French law sets no statutory minimum capital for a SARL. The articles may provide that only one-fifth of cash quotas is paid up at subscription, with the remaining balance callable within five years.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), operating through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and the Direction Régionale des Douanes et Droits Indirects for VAT operational matters; online portals: impots.gouv.fr, professionnels.impots.gouv.fr, and the EU-required national VAT registration platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign SARL or branch obtains a French tax identity (SIREN, SIRET and Numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) by completing the national online registration procedure via le-guichet.fr or, for non-established taxable persons, the specific VAT registration form 'MISE-ANA' filed with the SIE compétent. For a resident SARL, the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce issues the SIREN on incorporation, after which the SIE assigns the VAT number upon fulfilment of the tax regime election. Non-resident sellers performing distance sales into France must register directly for French VAT before the first taxable supply; no minimum turnover exemption applies to non-resident operators, and for intra-EU distance sales the €10,000 EU-wide Union threshold determines whether French VAT registration is triggered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) under Article 206(1) CGI on its worldwide profits, and does not enjoy pass-through treatment under national law.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in France. Available statutory mechanics include: (i) election for a 'SARL de famille' to be taxed under the personal income tax regime (Impôt sur le Revenu, IR) if all shares are held by relatives (CGI art. 239 and 239 bis AA); (ii) election for French consolidated tax grouping under CGI art. 223 A et seq.; (iii) optional territoriality election for qualifying French permanent establishments of non-resident corporations; (iv) the hybrid mismatch election framework under the ATAD/BEPS transposition rules requiring corresponding foreign-tax analysis; and (v) no separate check-the-box regime is available for US-style entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 91,900 annual taxable turnover for sales of goods and goods-related services; EUR 36,800 for other services (franchise en base thresholds under current Finance Law). The franchise is not available to non-resident taxable persons, and intra-EU distance sales of goods become subject to French VAT once the aggregate annual distance sales into France exceed EUR 10,000 or upon voluntary registration; no threshold applies for B2C imports of fast-fashion goods where the fiscal representation rules for e-commerce platforms apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of fast-fashion apparel and footwear physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union qualify for the 0% export VAT rate under CGI art. 262-I-1°. The SARL must prove both the physical removal of goods from EU territory (within a maximum period of 90 days from delivery, unless justified) and the transport at the purchaser's risk and responsibility. Directly linked services, such as freight, handling or export-related processing supplied after export commission, may also be zero-rated under CGI art. 262-II-2°, provided the services are immediately connected to the export transaction and performed for the exporter or the non-resident customer. No further value added in France before export is permitted; extra-Community export through a customs warehouse or via an e-commerce logistics platform requires customs status 'export' and the legitimate export frontier evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation issued via DELTA / Z.008 or the simplified export procedure known as Z.009 or C&P process)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / Sea Waybill evidencing physical removal from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation proving that export proceeds are received from a non-French source and are reconciliated with bank outflows
  • Commercial Invoice relevant to the exported goods, including EAN/SKU, quantities, and CN8 codes
  • Packing List
  • Proof of transport buyer's risk subsidence (e.g., Incoterms clause) where applicable
  • Intrastat declaration for intra-EU dispatch (if the goods are supplied to a foreign ERP customer within the EU, though that is exempt not zero-rated)

France is implementing mandatory B2B e-invoicing and B2C e-reporting under a phased timeline: large and mid-sized enterprises are required to comply from 1 September 2026, and all other businesses from 1 September 2027. E-invoices must be transmitted in a structured format (EN 16931) through a certified Partenaire de Dématérialisation (PDP) or the public 'Chorus Pro' portal. No real-time fiscal stamp is affixed in France; instead, transaction data is transmitted periodically to DGFiP via PDP-to-administration APIs. The regime will include invoice exchange at C3PS/PPAD levels, with data provided to the AIF (Archivage, Instruction et Flux) system. Non-compliant e-invoicing attracts penalties under CGI art. 289 and administrative fines, including rejection of deduction or refund claims.

DGFiP and Customs exercise heightened scrutiny of e-commerce exporters to verify the correlation between customs files, logistic events and bank receipts. Any misrepresentation of export evidence or abusive use of zero-rating, including forged bills of lading, fictitious inward remittances, or failure to repatriate foreign-currency proceeds within the 180-day statutory deadline, triggers immediate assessment of French VAT at the standard rate of 20.0%. Legal mechanisms include the 40% bad-faith penalty under CGI art. 1729(a), the 80% penalty for manipulation or dissimulation under CGI art. 1729(b), plus monthly interest of 0.5% under CGI art. 1734. Criminal liability under CGI art. 1741 and Code des Douanes art. 414 may subject directors to imprisonment of up to five years and fines of up to EUR 500,000, with the company exposed to the enhanced corporate fine of EUR 2,000,000 in serious customs fraud. The general tax audit reprise period is three years under Livre des Procédures Fiscales art. L169, extended to six years where fraudulent conduct is established, allowing DGFiP to claw back past zero-rating benefits for the full extension period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for the euro as regards the current account (receipts for fast-fashion e-commerce sales, refunds, logistics payments, platform fees) and the capital account (equity injections, profit distribution), subject to the general free movement of capital rule under Article 63 TFEU and CMF Article L151-2. No prior administrative licence is required for capital inflows into a non-regulated retail distribution business. Inward foreign direct investment in ISIC 4791 (e-commerce of apparel, NAICS 458110) is not a 'dual-use' or sensitive sector under Decree No. 2023-1294, so the acquisition or constitution of the SARL by a non-resident shareholder is only subject to the ex-post statistical declaration regime. Dividends to an EU/EEA parent are exempt from withholding under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU) plus the French domestic participation threshold of 5% and the two-year holding condition (Articles 145 and 216 CGI); dividends to non-EU residents are subject to withholding under Article 119-2 CGI (generally 30% for non-cooperative states, reduced by treaty to 15% or less, or by domestic rules) unless an applicable tax treaty grants a lower rate. Residual hurdles are documentary, not prohibitive: (i) annual balance-of-payments (BoP) reporting obligations where cross-border flows exceed the thresholds set by the Banque de France decree of 12 February 1987, as updated, (ii) attestations of distributable profits per Article L232-11 Code de commerce, and (iii) traceability of merchant-of-record settlement flows from Klarna BNPL or Shopify Payments to satisfy Article 150-0 B ter CGI on deferred payment arrangements (no material impact here as the BNPL counterparty is the deferred-payment provider, not the French vendor).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, retiming, or domiciliation requirement applies to export receipts in France. Since the liberalisation of 1988-1990 and the consolidation in CMF Article L151-2, all export proceeds in EUR and foreign currency are freely retainable in demand deposit or forex accounts. The SARL may keep USD/CNY settlement receivables offshore or convert them freely without Banque de France approval. The only residual obligation is statistical: reporting of cross-border payments related to exports, imports, and financial flows to the Banque de France (Balance of Payments statistics), by individual monthly declaration where the company's total international payment flows exceed the thresholds of the decree of 12 February 1987 (as revised), or under the 20-business-day cumulative declaration regime for amounts above the threshold; payments below these thresholds require only the company's own accounting records. For fast-fashion e-commerce, Shopify Payments and Klarna settlement flows are normally received under a single acquiring/BNPL contract and may be netted for BoP purposes, as expressly permitted by the Banque de France methodological note on payment institutions as reporting intermediaries.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) as the national central bank and monetary authority; ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) as the integrated prudential and AML/CFT supervisor of credit institutions and payment services providers; Direction Générale du Trésor within the French Ministry of Economy and Finance as overseer of the external financial relations regime. Operational regulation is embedded in the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF) Books I through V and the PSD2 transposition (ordonnance n° 2017-1252).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech viability rating: HIGH for the operational payments layer. Qonto, Shine (Soshape), Revolut Business, and Wise Business are French/European PSD2 payment institutions or EMI credit institutions passported into France; they offer automated onboarding, French IBANs, MT103 reception, SEPA Instant issuance, and efficient receipt of Shopify Payments and Klarna BNPL settlements in EUR. LEGAL CONSTRAINT: they are not credit institutions undertaking banking transactions per CMF Article L311-1; they may not take retail deposits, most cannot issue commercial letters of credit, cannot grant ISDA/FBF derivative master access, and cannot act as a capital-escrow agent for a formal share-capital deposit under Article L223-7 Code de commerce (which requires funds to be held by a regulated credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations). An offshore EMI without either a French or a European passport has NO legal viability: the SARL may not lawfully rely on a non-EU EMI as its sole settlement rail without breaching the CMF L521-2 monopoly on payment services and the ACPR/Tracfin registration duty. Blueprint recommendation: engage a Tier-1 French bank for statutory capital escrow, commercial LC issuance, and FX hedging; retain a Fintech EMI as a secondary settlement account to accelerate Klarna/Shopify receipts and fast SEPA CTInst supplier payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: the non-resident shareholder transfers cash to the SARL's Tier-1 escrow account in France. Euro-zone injection: SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001.001.09, Category Purpose TREA, or EBA intra-community code 'ACCA' for asset-to-liability transfer or cash contribution), credited via TARGET2 within T+1; SEPA Instant (SCTInst) may be used for amounts up to EUR 100,000 if the receiving bank subscribes to the SEPA Instant scheme. Non-EUR injection (USD or CNH) is remitted by SWIFT MT103 (field 72 containing the purpose code) into the SARL's multi-currency operational account and converted on trade date at best-execution. Verification duties: the receiving bank obtains an IBAN, a legalised K-bis extract, the SARL's articles of association, and the capital-deposit documentation; statutory compliance per Article L223-7 Code de commerce requires the funds to remain blocked with the bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts until the SARL is registered, with the release executed against the K-bis. FX-control reporting: the Banque de France requires an ex-post BoP statement within 20 business days for certain direct-investment flows or within the thresholds set by the decree of 12 February 1987, as updated; a standard French direct-investment declaration (CERFA form) is filed under the Banque de France's 12-series when the injection exceeds the relevant threshold. For injections below the threshold, the accounting entry under ledger code 101 'Capital' suffices. On the bank side, AML straight-through processing verifies compliance with CMF Article L561-2(2°) on the origin of funds; a pre-approved KYC dossier prevents holds on the MT103 or pain.001 payment.Clearing rails selected: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR for all European suppliers (fabric mills in Italy, Portugal, Poland) via EBICS batch submission T+1; (ii) SWIFT MT103 plus SWIFT gpi tracking for non-EUR trade settlements (USD to Bangladesh for garment letters of credit, CNH to Hong Kong accounts of Chinese sourcing agents, GBP to UK logistics hubs); (iii) documentary credit: SWIFT MT700 issued by the SARL's Tier-1 bank under UCP600 for fast-fashion supplier financing above EUR 50,000 per shipment, especially covering purchase-order financing from Bangladesh and Vietnam; (iv) open-account with supply-chain finance: a revolving confirmation or recommended-distribution facility via the bank's issuing counter, where the importer-finance repayments are matched to the SARL's disbursement schedule. The bank intermediates through SWIFT gpi, ISO 20022 pain.001 in EUR, and MT103 straight-through processing, ensuring compliance with the EU Cross-Border Payments Regulation (EU) 2021/1230 on fee transparency. For foreign-currency disbursements above EUR 100,000, outgoing MT103s are paired against executed FX forwards in a treasury pairing sheet to eliminate settlement risk.EUR/USD outright FX forwards (vanilla bullet, flexible, up to 24 months) documented under the FBF Master Agreement 2018 (Fédération Bancaire Française) with an ISDA credit support annex where derivatives execution occurs via the Tier-1 bank's treasury deskEUR/CNH forwards for Chinese sourcing payments, available as deliverable offshore renminbi (CNH) contracts with a 10% margin pre-deposit or a rollover lineFX swaps (Tom/Next, spot-forward, and double-swap-point structures) for temporary bridging of USD and CNH payment runs against USD and GBP collection fluctuationsEUR/USD and EUR/CNH vanilla FX options (call/put) and zero-premium cost-reversal structures for hedging purchase-order commitments beyond six months, documented under the ISDA/FBF 2018 Master Agreement approved by the bank's risk directorCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD, EUR/CNH) for capital-structure hedging of any medium-term shareholder-loan or bond-type refinancing, permitted as a class-2 derivative under CMF Article L211-1 and settled via TARGET2 or CLS where both leg currencies are CLS-eligibleNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are legally permissible under French law for exotic or inconvertible pairs such as EUR/BDT for Bangladesh coverage where onshore deliverable hedging is unavailable; however, given the free convertibility of the euro and the existence of deliverable CNH/USD markets, NDFs are recommended only as a residual instrument aligned with the final LC settlement rate

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech viability rating: HIGH for the operational payments layer. Qonto, Shine (Soshape), Revolut Business, and Wise Business are French/European PSD2 payment institutions or EMI credit institutions passported into France; they offer automated onboarding, French IBANs, MT103 reception, SEPA Instant issuance, and efficient receipt of Shopify Payments and Klarna BNPL settlements in EUR. LEGAL CONSTRAINT: they are not credit institutions undertaking banking transactions per CMF Article L311-1; they may not take retail deposits, most cannot issue commercial letters of credit, cannot grant ISDA/FBF derivative master access, and cannot act as a capital-escrow agent for a formal share-capital deposit under Article L223-7 Code de commerce (which requires funds to be held by a regulated credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations). An offshore EMI without either a French or a European passport has NO legal viability: the SARL may not lawfully rely on a non-EU EMI as its sole settlement rail without breaching the CMF L521-2 monopoly on payment services and the ACPR/Tracfin registration duty. Blueprint recommendation: engage a Tier-1 French bank for statutory capital escrow, commercial LC issuance, and FX hedging; retain a Fintech EMI as a secondary settlement account to accelerate Klarna/Shopify receipts and fast SEPA CTInst supplier payments.
