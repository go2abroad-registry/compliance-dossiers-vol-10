Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital (Article L.223-2). The capital is fixed freely in the Articles of Association. Cash contributions must be deposited on a blocked account opened with a bank or with the Caisse des Dépôts and released only after RCS registration. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate registry: Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) operated via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned SARL: (1) file statutory incorporation documents via the Guichet Unique (single electronic portal), including Articles of Association, proof of registered office (or attestation de domiciliation), and identity documents of the manager(s) and shareholders; (2) obtain SIREN and SIRET numbers automatically from INSEE upon validation of the RCS registration; (3) VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) is allocated automatically by DGFiP to the registered enterprise, with format FR followed by a two-digit key and the 9-digit SIREN; (4) non-EU foreign shareholders must provide a completed Form FR-FTA (Foreign Tax Authority attestation) and may need to appoint a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) subject to DGFiP approval if no permanent establishment exists; (5) the SARL must file an adverse declaration of existence (Formulaire M0 / CERFA 13453) within 30 days of commencement; (6) the entity may request an impôt sur les sociétés (IS) status confirmation from the SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) with territorial competence over the registered office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is by default subject to French corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). An irrevocable option for income-tax transparency (régime des sociétés de personnes) is available under Article 239 bis AB of the CGI for SARLs performing commercial, industrial, or artisanal activities with no more than 50 units and meeting specific ownership turnover/profit thresholds. A listed list of eligible enterprises may also elect under the family SARL (SARL de famille) regime. Hybrid mismatch elections under EU ATAD/DAC6 are limited, but taxpayers can file an annual notification and make a choice regarding certain recharacterization rules via the standard tax return. In addition, a SARL can elect to remain subject to IR for the first taxable period provided it meets definitional criteria of a 'small enterprise' (PME under EU Recommendation 2003/361/EC).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 for sales of goods (Article 293 B CGI; franchise en base de TVA) and EUR 36,800 for service providers and liberal activities; e-commerce distance sales exceeding EU-wide EUR 10,000 trigger the OSS (One Stop Shop) registration thresholds, subject to local French registration if intra-EU distance sales exceed the mandatory threshold; an established entity with a fixed establishment in France must register for VAT regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (1) sales of goods physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union by the vendor or a third party on its behalf, provided that the goods leave France within 90 days of the invoice date (Customs Code Article 292, CGI Article 291-I); (2) exports placed under an external community transit or export customs procedure; (3) certain services directly linked to exported goods (e.g., freight, inspection, loading) supplied to a non-EU customer are eligible for 0% when ancillary to an effective export. The supplier must obtain and preserve within 1 month (extended to 6 months for retroactive zero-rating claims) a tangible set of transport or customs documents demonstrating physical exit from EU territory; there is no statutory de minimis threshold for letters of weight or postal dispatches, but the French tax administration applies a EUR 850 value cap for light consignments qualifying by declaration only.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Export / DAU - Document Administratif Unique) via the SYDONIX or DELTA/XML clearance system containing the goods description and exit certificate (Exemplaire n°3) stamped by French customs
  • Bill of Lading (or other transport document: CMR, airway waybill, or postal acceptance receipt) evidencing dispatch from France and arrival in the non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / IRC issued by the bank demonstrating the transfer and receipt of foreign exchange proceeds from the non-EU customer within 180 days of shipment

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing from phase-in periods starting 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for SMEs, requiring issuance of structured electronic invoices (Factur-X, UBL, or CII) transmitted via a certified Plateforme de Démonétisation (PDP) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF). Real-time fiscal clearance via the 'Y' scheme is not yet implemented; however, B2C distance sales transactions (including automobile parts) must be reported via the e-reporting module to DGFiP, with the OSS return providing an electronic breakdown. Businesses are required to use API interfaces of the DGFiP 'Partner of Dematerialization' ecosystem; each invoice must receive a unique identification number and a digital fingerprint (empreinte numérique) and be transmitted in near-real-time to the PDP. Failure to implement compliant e-invoicing by statutory deadlines results in penalties up to EUR 15,000 per infraction (later increased to EUR 60,000 for recurring breaches).

The DGFiP applies an automatic and reinforced anti-abuse scrutiny to export zero-rating claims; the tax inspector may require immediate proof of the physical exit of goods and may retroactively assess VAT (20%) on all operations not justified within 6 months. Penalties apply: (i) standard late payment interest (intérêts de retard) at 0.20% per month from the due date; (ii) a 40% penalty for wilful inaccuracy (manquement délibéré) under Article 1729 CGI, increased to 80% for fraudulent behavior (abus de droit or manoeuvres frauduleuses) and up to 80% for trafficking in false customs declarations under Customs Code Article 414; (iii) criminal prosecution (Customs offenses) may lead to a fine equal to the amount evaded and confiscation of goods and vehicles. The refund claim can be clawed back with a minimum 10% shall-miss vigilance rule triggering a fiscal prosecutor referral. Intentional falsification of an export document (including backdating of signed proof of arrival) constitutes tax fraud and may be sanctioned with 3 years' imprisonment, EUR 45,000 fine, and disqualification from management functions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. Under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the French Monetary and Financial Code, no prior exchange-control authorization is required for current-account payments, capital transfers, dividends, royalties, or ordinary profit remittances between France and EU/EEA jurisdictions. Transactions with non-EEA counterparties are also freely executable through licensed banks, subject to AML/KYC, sanctions screening, and balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the Banque de France. Profit remittance is not blocked by exchange controls. A French SARL may distribute dividends to non-resident shareholders through normal banking channels, provided the company complies with French tax withholding rules, applies any applicable tax-treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary protection, and the paying bank receives the usual corporate and tax documentation. The main practical hurdles are therefore tax documentation, VAT/accounting traceability, and AML source-of-funds evidence, not currency convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no general mandatory surrender or forced-conversion requirement on export receipts. There is no statutory conversion ratio and no fixed statutory deadline within which export proceeds must be repatriated or converted into euro. Export invoices may be denominated and collected in freely convertible foreign currency, and proceeds may be retained in euro or foreign-currency accounts domestically or abroad, subject to annual French foreign-account reporting and Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting. The SARL must preserve the underlying documentary chain for each export settlement, including commercial invoices, packing lists, transport documents, customs declarations, and bank MT103 confirmations. Intra-EU dispatches must be reported through the Intrastat/Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens scheme when thresholds are met; non-EU exports require export customs declarations. Cash or cash-equivalent transfers of EUR 10,000 or more across the French border are subject to customs declaration, and all wire transfers are monitored by the licensed banking intermediary under French AML rules.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), in conjunction with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury), which administers exchange-control policy under the French Code monétaire et financier, in particular Articles L151-1 et seq. and R151-1 et seq.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High as a supplementary collection and payout rail, but not as the primary treasury bank. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) may passport into France under PSD2 and may hold client funds as safeguarded e-money; however, such funds are not bank deposits and are not protected by the French deposit guarantee fund (FGDR). Offshore/non-EEA digital EMIs without an EU license or a licensed French/EU agent cannot lawfully provide payment services to French residents. For an auto-parts e-commerce treasury, an EEA EMI such as Qonto, Wise Business, or Payoneer may be used for low-value global payouts, multi-currency virtual accounts, and fast settlement from card platform providers, but all statutory capital, tax clearing, LC, and major FX transactions should remain with a Tier-1 licensed bank.

Inbound equity or shareholder current-account funds must arrive by bank wire via SEPA or SWIFT MT103, with the remittance narrative stating the legal basis, for example 'SOUSCRIPTION ET LIBÉRATION DU CAPITAL SOCIAL' (subscription and payment of share capital) or 'APPORT EN COMPTE COURANT D'ASSOCIÉ' (shareholder current-account contribution). For non-EU originators, the MT103 should include full field 50F sender details, field 59A beneficiary details, and field 70 purpose narrative containing 'PURP/INTC EQUITY CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or a similarly explicit purpose reference. The receiving French bank will verify the Kbis extract, shareholder register, capital contribution resolution, and may apply source-of-wealth checks. Funds are free from exchange-control approval, but the bank reports the inward transfer to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics where thresholds apply, and the SARL must evidence the contribution in its statutory accounts and tax filings.For EU suppliers, settlement is routed via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) using ISO 20022 pain.001. For non-EU suppliers, the standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for commercial payment, or MT202 COV for bank-to-bank settlement when the underlying payment has already been covered by a separate instruction. For high-value purchases and documentary trade, the SARL may use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued through its Tier-1 bank by SWIFT MT700, with confirmation through a correspondent bank in the supplier's jurisdiction; documentary collections under ICC URC 522 are also permissible. E-commerce card receipts from Stripe and PayPal are settled via card network clearing into the SARL's collection account, with periodic sweeps into the main euro operational account. All payouts must be reconciled to customs entries, import VAT/fiscal records, commercial invoices, and the ERP treasury ledger.Deliverable FX forward contract with a licensed French or EU credit institution for EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNY, and other freely convertible commercial currenciesNon-deliverable forward (NDF) for currencies without full deliverable settlement in France, provided the underlying cross-border trade exposure is documented and the transaction is executed under a bank ISDA/CSA arrangementPlain-vanilla FX swap (swap de change) for temporary cross-currency liquidity conversionCross-currency swap or cross-currency interest-rate swap aligned with the SARL's actual import receivables and export payablesPurchased long-dated FX options for downside protection, where writing uncovered options is avoided and additional bank suitability documentation is required

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High as a supplementary collection and payout rail, but not as the primary treasury bank. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) may passport into France under PSD2 and may hold client funds as safeguarded e-money; however, such funds are not bank deposits and are not protected by the French deposit guarantee fund (FGDR). Offshore/non-EEA digital EMIs without an EU license or a licensed French/EU agent cannot lawfully provide payment services to French residents. For an auto-parts e-commerce treasury, an EEA EMI such as Qonto, Wise Business, or Payoneer may be used for low-value global payouts, multi-currency virtual accounts, and fast settlement from card platform providers, but all statutory capital, tax clearing, LC, and major FX transactions should remain with a Tier-1 licensed bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 of French entity annual net turnover where the SARL belongs to a group whose consolidated turnover or balance-sheet assets exceed EUR 400,000,000 (Art. L.13 AA LPF). Below these thresholds, contemporaneous arm's-length evidence is still required for any cross-border related-party payment; a Master File/Local File becomes compulsory only above those thresholds.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but genuine substance is enforced through the general anti-abuse rules: abuse of law (Art. L64 LPF), anti-hybrid and CFC rules (Art. 205 A and 209 B CGI), thin-capitalisation limits (interest deduction restricted to 30% of EBITDA), and the beneficial-ownership test of DTAs. For the e-commerce SARL, substance is evidenced by local employees, physical warehouse or fulfilment premises in France, actual order-processing and after-sales functions, and commercial decisions taken on French territory; failure to demonstrate such substance will trigger disallowance of intercompany deductions, recharacterisation of flows and 80% penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Fine of EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years (Art. 1741 CGI); additional 40% or 80% tax penalty on assessments, monthly 0.2% late-payment interest, publication of the conviction, judicial confiscation of assets and bank account freezes.

Director personal liability: The SARL manager is jointly and severally liable for the tax debts and penalties when personally involved in the fraud (Art. 1742 CGI); bankruptcy disqualification, professional ban, travel ban and criminal confiscation may be imposed.
Artificial Conduit / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Abuse-of-law procedure (Art. L64 LPF) leading to a 80% penalty on the under-declared tax, full recharacterisation of the scheme, disallowance of deducted payments, and nullification of claimed treaty benefits; anti-conduit measures under EU GAAR can also deny treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally sued for restitution and contribution to corporate tax deficiencies under 'faute de gestion' actions, and can be held jointly and severally liable when the sham structure was knowingly implemented; commercial courts may impose personal bankruptcy orders and travel restrictions.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 30,000 for natural persons and EUR 150,000 for legal entities under the Code monétaire et financier; aggravated money-laundering penalties up to 7 years' imprisonment and EUR 1,000,000 fine where e-commerce flows are concealed.

Director personal liability: The SARL manager and directors are personally liable for AML control failures; legal entity fines are converted into personal criminal liability when the manager personally directed the omission, with asset confiscation and travel bans on progress.
Foreign Account / Payment Platform Non-disclosure

Statutory sanction: EUR 1,500 penalty per undeclared foreign account, raised to EUR 10,000 where the account's total balance exceeds EUR 10,000; where concealment is used to evade CIT or VAT, criminal sanctions apply under Art. 1741 CGI with up to 3 years' imprisonment and EUR 150,000 fine.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally control and fail to declare such accounts face personal criminal liability; the corporate veil is pierced by reason of the intentional non-disclosure, allowing attachment of personal assets and a travel ban until the fiscal debt is settled.
