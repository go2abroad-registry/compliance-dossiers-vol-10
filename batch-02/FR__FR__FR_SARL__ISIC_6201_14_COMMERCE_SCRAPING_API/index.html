Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SARL must have a registered office in France. Domiciliation with a compliant commercial domiciliation provider is generally permissible under Articles L123-11-1 et seq. and R123-166-1 et seq. of the Code de commerce, subject to a written domiciliation agreement, provider authorization, document availability and effective receipt of official correspondence. Name reservation / lock period

France does not generally provide a separate statutory company-name reservation certificate with a uniform 30-day reservation period. Obtain a written name-availability and conflict review, then file the incorporation formalities promptly through the INPI Guichet unique. If the filing is rejected or a deficiency notice is issued, obtain the electronic notification and stated legal grounds, correct the filing or submit supporting evidence within the prescribed deadline, and request reconsideration through the INPI formalities platform. Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registry of the Commercial Court) and the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés), filing made exclusively through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) operated by the INPI and INSEE.

Governing statute

Code de commerce (French Commercial Code), Book II, Title III, Articles L. 223-1 to L. 223-43 (formation, capital, and management of the SARL) and Articles R. 223-1 et seq.; Décret n° 2021-300 implementing the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises; Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (Digital Services Act) Article 24 for online intermediary registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Formulaire M0 — Déclaration de création d'entreprise commerciale (single electronic company creation dossier filed via the Guichet Unique), accompanied by the Statuts (notarial or under private signature), the certificate of capital deposit, and the certificate of publication of the legal announcement.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for a SARL; the minimum legal value of shares is €1 and the drafters may set a nominal capital of €1, although practitioners should set a realistic amount. Full subscription and payment of the capital are compulsory prior to registration; the funds must be blocked in a French bank escrow account (compte de blocage) or with a notary until the K-bis extract is delivered by the RCS.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office is a tertiary commercial lease not open to the public, so the ERP (établissement recevant du public) fire-safety commissioning procedure is not triggered; nevertheless, the workplace must comply with Book II of the French Labour Code (Code du travail) regarding electrical installations conformity (NF C 15-100), emergency exits, sanitation and first-aid provisions. If in-house server racks, uninterruptible power supplies or other electrical infrastructure are installed, a pre-occupancy verification of the civil-liability clearance by the fire and rescue service may be required by the insurer, and the occupational physician must issue a workplace-attitudinal clearance where staff exceed 50 or for VDU workstations.

No industrial licensing board exercises authority over an ISIC 6201 software and data-pipeline service company in France. The only corresponding administrative registration is the DGE (Direction générale des entreprises) user-account for the digital platform operators register (Registre des opérateurs de plateformes en ligne) under the DSA.

Category: Exempt — no operating or manufacturing licence is required for the commercial scraping API and proxy gateway business; the activity is a purely tertiary, non-manufacturing service and therefore does not trigger the installation-classification (ICPE) licensing scheme of the French Environmental Code.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); online portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; professional and VAT filing via the DGFiP e-procedures portal; EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) registration through the same DGFiP tax portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without a fixed establishment in France must first determine whether French VAT registration is triggered. If registration is required, file Form 1601-SD (company application) with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) or, for non-resident entities, the DGFiP foreign-company desk, attaching the entity's constitutional documents, proof of registered address, identity of legal representatives, and description of French taxable supplies. DGFiP will issue the SIREN/SIRET number and, where required, the intra-Community VAT number. For digital services to EU consumers, registration via the EU OSS may replace conventional French VAT registration. A non-EU entity may need to appoint a tax representative in France under Article 302 quater A of the French General Tax Code (CGI).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a check-the-box system. A SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Certain small/new SARLs may elect, under restricted conditions, to be taxed as partnerships (régime des sociétés de personnes) pursuant to CGI Articles 239 bis AA and 239 bis AB. SARLs owned at least 95% by a French-resident corporate parent may elect into French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale). Hybrid mismatch election rules are implemented under domestic law following ATAD; no additional elective check-the-box is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Registration is required from the first taxable transaction for non-resident suppliers without French establishment unless the EU OSS applies. For domestic established small businesses, the small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base de TVA) applies under CGI Article 293 B up to approximately EUR 91,900 annual turnover for goods and approximately EUR 36,800 for services (2024/2025 adjustment levels; confirm current DGFiP thresholds). Above these thresholds, VAT registration is compulsory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to a destination outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate if the goods demonstrably leave EU territory and the exporter holds sufficient transport and customs evidence. Offshore B2B services are generally outside French VAT when the customer is a taxable person established outside France and the place-of-supply rules under Article 44 of Directive 2006/112/EC apply. Services directly tied to exported goods may also qualify for zero-rating. Pure digital scraping API services supplied to non-established non-taxable recipients will be considered outside French VAT when the customer is resident outside the EU; services received by EU consumers remain taxable in the EU member state of consumption unless exempted or handled through OSS.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial invoice issued by the exporter
  • Air waybill, CMR transport document, or equivalent proof of physical departure from EU territory
  • Contract or service delivery report for offshore services evidencing place of supply outside France

France does not apply real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. Mandatory electronic invoicing for French domestic B2B transactions is phased: large enterprises from 1 September 2026 and small/micro enterprises from 1 September 2027. E-reporting will be transmitted to DGFiP via certified partner platforms (PDP) or the Public Invoicing Portal (Portail Public de Facturation). Accepted structured formats include Factur-X and UBL XML. No API pre-clearance is currently required, but periodic electronic VAT returns must be filed with DGFiP.

DGFiP may refuse or claw back export zero-rating where evidence of physical export or non-EU place of supply is insufficient or fraudulent. In such cases, VAT is reassessed at 20%, plus statutory interest, and penalties can reach 80% of the unpaid tax (case of bad faith) or 100% in cases of fraudulent concealment. Criminal tax fraud penalties of up to EUR 375,000 and three years imprisonment can also be imposed. The standard tax reassessment period is generally three years, extending to ten years in cases of hidden activity or fraud. VAT refunds already granted may be recovered together with penalties and interest.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full convertibility for both current and capital accounts under EU law and national regulations. Profit remittances to shareholders are unrestricted for resident and non-resident entities, subject solely to standard tax filing and beneficial ownership disclosure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies in France. Export receipts must be declared to customs and tax authorities (DGFiP) within statutory timelines. Conversion occurs at prevailing market rates with no forced ratios.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France and Direction Générale du Trésor (DGT)

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% standard French corporate income tax rate (Article 219 CGI) applicable to the SARL's global taxable profits, subject to the 15% reduced SME rate on the first EUR 38,120 of taxable income where the statutory eligibility conditions are met.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% standard domestic withholding tax under Article 119 bis CGI on gross dividends paid by a French SARL to a non-French-resident corporate shareholder. For an individual shareholder domiciled outside France, the domestic withholding rate is 12.8% plus applicable social levies where the shareholder is within their scope, with an all-in 30% rate in the standard PFU mechanism; tax treaties may reduce the rate to 15%, 10%, or 5%, and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive entirely removes withholding where the parent holds at least 10% of the capital for an uninterrupted 24-month period and meets the anti-abuse conditions set out in the 2015/121/EU Directive.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the annual return must be transmitted no later than the second business day after 1 May; for any other fiscal year close, the return is due within three months of the fiscal year-end. The balance of corporate income tax is payable at the same time as the annual return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50 million: a French SARL with annual net turnover exceeding EUR 50 million is ordinarily required to prepare and retain a Local File under Articles L.13 B and L.13 AA of the French Book of Tax Procedures; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are mandatory where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750 million. Even below the mandatory threshold, the DGFiP may demand adequate transfer-pricing documentation for related-party transactions in the API/data-commerce sector and will disregard arm's-length evidence absent local documentation.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, French tax policy and the OECD anti-BEPS framework require genuine economic substance as a precondition for tax residence, treaty access, and the application of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. A French SARL operating a commercial web-scraping and proxy API gateway must maintain real corporate presence and decision-making in France: offices, operational employees, technical infrastructure, Russian or foreign subcontracting arrangements under written contracts, bank accounts, accounting records, board minutes, and pricing policies reflecting the functions, assets, and risks assumed by the French entity. A structure that artificially diverts API revenue to a related foreign entity while claiming French treaty benefits will be recharacterized under Article 155 A CGI, the abuse-of-rights doctrine, and the French General Anti-Abuse Rule.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 40% of the additional tax assessed for bad faith, 80% for fraudulent maneuvers or abuse of law, plus monthly late-payment interest of 0.20% per month. Criminal sanctions under Articles 1741 and 1743 CGI: standard tax fraud carries up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 500,000; aggravated fraud involving organized schemes or cross-border conduits carries up to 7 years' imprisonment and a EUR 3,000,000 fine, together with confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: The managing director of a French SARL (gérant) may be declared personally and jointly liable for the company's tax debt under Article L.267 of the Book of Tax Procedures where the tax deficiency results from deliberate, repeated, or serious tax misstatements. The criminal court may add a travel ban, prohibition on managing a business, asset freezing, confiscation, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Abuse and Non-Documented Intercompany Transactions

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment under Article 57 CGI to an arm's-length outcome, plus the 40% penalty for bad faith or 80% penalty for abuse of law, and 0.20% monthly interest; where documentation is entirely absent, the DGFiP may impose a fixed EUR 10,000 documentation penalty under Article 1735 A CGI in addition to the adjustment.

Director personal liability: The gérant who signed or directed transfer-pricing documentation that is knowingly false may be held personally liable to the tax debt, and may be subject to criminal sanctions for issuing false documents or misuse of corporate assets (abus de biens sociaux), including imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of EUR 375,000.
Shell Company and Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The DGFiP may disregard the entity under the abuse-of-rights doctrine and reattribute its entire API/software profit to the actual beneficial owner under Article 155 A CGI; treaty relief is denied and the distributed profits are recharacterized, triggering the 30% or 25% dividend withholding tax regime. The administrative penalty for abuse of law is 80% of the reassessed tax, with interest. If the entity issued false residence certificates, criminal sanctions for forgery apply under Articles 441-1 and 441-2 of the French Criminal Code, up to 3 years' imprisonment and EUR 45,000 in fines.

Director personal liability: The gérant may face a civil restitution order for the tax deficiency, disqualification from corporate management for up to 15 years, travel restrictions, criminal prosecution for forgery and tax evasion, and confiscation of personal assets where the shell structure was used for fraud.
AML Non-Compliance and Crypto Payment Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Unregistered crypto-asset service activity is punishable by criminal fines and imprisonment; money laundering under Article 324-1 of the French Criminal Code carries up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 375,000, rising to 10 years and EUR 750,000 when committed through a professional or systematic operation; the ACPR/AMF may also impose administrative sanctions up to EUR 100 million or 10% of annual turnover, plus asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may be personally sanctioned, prohibited from directional activity, placed under travel bans, and criminally convicted for money laundering; corporate veil piercing is available where crypto assets are intermingled with personal wealth or where the company is used as a conduit for the director's cryptocurrency transactions.
