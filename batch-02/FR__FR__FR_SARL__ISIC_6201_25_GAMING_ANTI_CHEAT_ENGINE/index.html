Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) kept by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce in the jurisdiction of the registered office; filings are transmitted through the single electronic window of the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

Code de commerce (French Commercial Code): Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (formation, capital, management and operations of a Société à Responsabilité Limitée); Articles L.210-1 et seq. (common incorporation rules); Articles L.123-1 and R.123-1 to R.123-14 (registration with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statutory declaration of incorporation (déclaration légale de constitution) submitted via the INPI single window together with the signed articles of association (statuts); no separate central-state duty is levied, but mandatory court-registry fees and chamber-of-commerce taxes are collected electronically at filing, and legal-announcement publication costs are paid to the authorized gazette.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 3 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for SARL; the articles may fix any amount, but all cash contributions must be fully paid upon subscription and deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) at a French bank or with a notaire, released only upon issuance of the K-bis certificate (Articles L.223-7 and R.223-4 of the Code de commerce).

Funding & unlock path:

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for SARL; the articles may fix any amount, but all cash contributions must be fully paid upon subscription and deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) at a French bank or with a notaire, released only upon issuance of the K-bis certificate (Articles L.223-7 and R.223-4 of the Code de commerce).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), impots.gouv.fr; customs and indirect duties: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), douane.gouv.fr; unified company registration portal: guichet-entreprises.fr (Guichet Unique).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities with a French fixed establishment apply via the Guichet Unique (or historically via CFE), obtaining SIREN/SIRET numbers; VAT identification (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN) is issued by DGFiP. Non-established suppliers of B2C digital/electronic services may register under the OSS Non-Union scheme or through the DGFiP Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) in Paris. A local registered tax representative may be required for certain foreign suppliers.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities with a French fixed establishment apply via the Guichet Unique (or historically via CFE), obtaining SIREN/SIRET numbers; VAT identification (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN) is issued by DGFiP. Non-established suppliers of B2C digital/electronic services may register under the OSS Non-Union scheme or through the DGFiP Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) in Paris. A local registered tax representative may be required for certain foreign suppliers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box. SARL may elect income-tax treatment (IR) under art. 239 bis AA CGI for a 5-year period; VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under art. 256 C CGI; fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under art. 223 A CGI; parent-subsidiary regime (régime mère-fille) under art. 145 CGI; hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD as transposed in art. 212 bis CGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold for established businesses; any taxable economic activity triggers registration. Domestic small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA) applies only below approximately EUR 93,600 annual turnover for goods and EUR 37,500 for services (2024 thresholds, with lower limits EUR 85,000 and EUR 34,100); exceeding these triggers mandatory registration. Non-established foreign suppliers of B2C electronic/digital services must register from the first euro (no threshold) under OSS Non-Union, or under the direct SIEE registration route.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods to destinations outside the EU are zero-rated under art. 262-I CGI; intra-EU dispatches to a VAT-registered customer are exempt under art. 262-ter CGI. B2B services (including gaming anti-cheat middleware/SaaS and integrity monitoring) are outside French VAT under the place-of-supply rule of art. 259-1 CGI where the customer is established outside France; B2C supplies of electronic services to EU consumers are declared via OSS at destination-state rates. Zero-rating requires proof of physical exportation (movement outside the EU within one year), supported by transport and commercial documentation, and arm's-length pricing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE via DELTA/ECS electronic system)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR or equivalent transport document
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank settlement evidence (FX repatriation proof)
  • Transport insurance and proof-of-delivery documentation

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing phased from 1 September 2026 (large and mid-size taxpayers) and 1 September 2027 (SMEs/micro), using certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public portal in a Y-shaped double-flow transmission model; structured e-reporting of B2C transactions and cross-border supplies is transmitted to DGFiP; formats include Factur-X/UBL with PEPPOL/API-based interoperability; Chorus Pro remains the portal for public procurement. France does not apply per-invoice real-time fiscal clearance stamping; periodic VAT returns (CA3) and Intrastat/DEB (EC Sales List) filings apply.

False or erroneous zero-rating claims trigger a 40% penalty for bad faith and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres (art. 1729-1 CGI), plus late-payment interest (intérêt de retard) of 0.20% per month. Retrospective reassessment is generally limited to 3 years (art. L. 169 LPF), extended to 6 years for active concealment and 10 years for unfiled returns. Criminal sanctions for VAT fraud (art. 1741 CGI) include fines up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; DGFiP and DGDDI jointly enforce export documentary compliance, with clawback of input VAT and reversal of output VAT exemption upon documentary failure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a member of the euro area; the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the French Code Monétaire et Financier. Current account convertibility is unrestricted, including trade payments, service fees, and profit remittances. Capital account convertibility is equally free under the EU capital movement directive, with residual administrative reporting requirements for balance-of-payments purposes. Profit remittance by an SARL is permitted through shareholder distributions, provided corporate tax returns are filed, financial statements are audited when applicable, and any required withholding tax certificate is obtained under the applicable tax treaty. No pre-approval is required for converting EUR to foreign currency; however, banks may apply tightened compliance screening for certain foreign counterparties or sanctioned jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in France to forcibly surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency export receipts into euro within a prescribed timeline. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts at a licensed credit institution. Nonetheless, French residents must file monthly balance-of-payments reporting with Banque de France when aggregated cross-border transactions exceed EUR 50,000 per calendar month, and they must be able to evidence the underlying trade transaction. Mandatory conversion ratios are not applied by the French authorities.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French central banking authority) under the framework of the Eurosystem and the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR), with cross-border capital account administration coordinated by the Direction Générale du Trésor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for an EU-passported or French-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) is Medium. A digital EMI such as a licensed fintech e-money institution may be used for receivables collection and payouts, but it is not a substitute for a full credit institution when the SARL needs SWIFT-based correspondent clearing, commercial letters of credit, or a committed FX line. Legal constraints include authorisation requirements under ACPR, safeguarding requirements on client funds, and the limitation that EMIs cannot extend credit. The cross-border EMIs established outside France may operate under EU passporting, but they must comply with French e-money regulations and cannot act as the primary treasury bank for all liquidity clearing, especially for capital injections from non-EU shareholders.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent company or non-resident shareholder injects equity contributions via SWIFT MT103 with the creditor beneficiary being the SARL's French bank account and the debtor institution confirmed by a financial institution registry. The wire must reference the purpose code 'Capital Injection / Share Capital Contribution' with the exact wording 'Apport en compte courant d'associé' for shareholder loans or 'Augmentation de capital' for registered equity. Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting must be triggered if the monthly cross-border threshold of EUR 50,000 is exceeded. The bank will require the corporate resolution, the updated statuts, the notarised declaration of subscription and payment, and the French cashier certificate from the bank confirming funds deposit. A compliant injection should use the French account of the SARL only and should never mix trade receivables with capital in the same wire.Outbound trade settlements for gaming anti-cheat licensing fees, middleware revenue share, and advance payments to foreign technology partners are executed through the standard SWIFT MT103 for occasional cross-border corporate payments and through MT202 COV for interbank and subsidiary funding flows. For high-value procurement or recurring service agreements, French banks provide commercial standby letters of credit and documentary collection platforms; however, the licensing-royalty model usually relies on clean payments, invoice matching, and transfer pricing documentation. SEPA credit transfer is the mandatory rail for domestic euro disbursements within France and the EEA, while SWIFT EUR transfers are used only when the receiving account is outside the SEPA zone or when MT103 format is required by the beneficiary bank. All outbound payments must carry a valid purpose code, the underlying contract reference, and a copy of the invoice or royalty statement, and they must be reflected in the SARL's balance-of-payments reporting if applicable.

Legal constraints include authorisation requirements under ACPR, safeguarding requirements on client funds, and the limitation that EMIs cannot extend credit.
