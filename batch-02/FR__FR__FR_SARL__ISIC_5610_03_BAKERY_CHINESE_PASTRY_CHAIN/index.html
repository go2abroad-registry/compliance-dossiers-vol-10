Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital; freely fixed by the associates (commonly €1,000 or more for commercial viability). Per Article L.223-7 Code de commerce, at least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation and the balance within 5 years; in-kind contributions are fully paid up at signature. Cash subscription must be deposited and blocked in a dedicated account with a bank, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary, with release only upon presentation of the Kbis registration extract.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: compliance inspection by the Commission de Sécurité and arrêté d'autorisation if the bakery/shop qualifies as ERP (Établissement Recevant du Public); technical review of extraction hoods and ducts by the SDIS (fire brigade); structural load inspection by an approved bureau de contrôle for heavy ovens, cold rooms, and central-kitchen slabs; occupational clearance: registration with the occupational health service (médecine du travail), the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques (DUERP) established, and evacuation/first-aid plans validated prior to opening.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - French General Directorate of Public Finances, Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty. Digital portals: impots.gouv.fr for tax obligations, formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique led by INPI) for company formation and tax identification.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File formation documents (statuts, M0 form) via the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; the INPI issues a 9-digit SIREN and 14-digit SIRET and notifies the DGFiP. Step 2: DGFiP automatically assigns the French TVA intra-communautaire number (format 'FR' + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN) under Article 286 CGI; the number appears on the INPI extract and the DGFiP confirmatory notice. Step 3: Non-resident entities without a French establishment file a direct VAT registration application with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), 10 rue du Centre, 93000 Bobigny, submitting a certified copy of the statutes, proof of the representative's identity, a business description, and a power of attorney where applicable; EU-established remote sellers may instead register via the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) portal for distance sales. Step 4: Confirm the SIRET with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) of the registered office, which assigns the company's tax file number. Contractual and payroll returns require staff registration with URSSAF through the same Guichet Unique.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope. The SARL is by default subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at the entity level under Article 206-1 CGI; IS applies exclusively to profits from activities carried out in France (territoriality rule, Article 209-I CGI). Pass-through eligibility exists only for the first 5 fiscal years under Article 239 bis AA CGI or as a 'SARL de famille' under Article 239 bis AB CGI. No tax-exempt zone applies; the standard regime governs the bakery chain and central kitchen operation.

Available election forms

Article 239 bis AA CGI: a newly formed SARL may elect pass-through income taxation (IR) for its first 5 fiscal years by a certified declaration filed with the competent tax office before the end of the first fiscal year, or by insertion in the articles of association. Article 239 bis AB CGI: an existing SARL with fewer than 5 shareholders, all natural persons within the family group, may elect SARL de famille IR status. Article 206-4 CGI: a company subject to IR may elect definitive IS taxation (irrevocable). Hybrid mismatch: French law has no general check-the-box classification; entity classification follows the civil-law form, but Articles 212 bis, 212 ter and 212 quater CGI (transposing ATAD 1/2 and BEPS Action 2) neutralise hybrid mismatches, including those triggered through an overseas disregarded entity. For IS groups, intra-group fiscal consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI is available at a 95% ownership threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold: every taxable person making a taxable supply in France must register from its first euro of turnover (Articles 256 and 286 CGI); the SARL registers on the date of commencement. Optional 'franchise en base' VAT exemption for established small businesses under Article 293 B CGI: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for sales of goods and on-premises food/beverage services, and EUR 37,500 for services; the exemption is lost as of the first day of the month in which the tolerance ceiling of EUR 94,300 (goods) or EUR 41,200 (services) is exceeded. The franchise does not cover intra-Community acquisitions and gives no right to input VAT deduction. A baker-pastry business with sit-down service is subject to 10% for on-premises consumption, 5.5% for most takeaway bakery products, and 20% for standard supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Definitive exports of goods out of the EU are exempt with deduction (0% VAT) under Article 262-I-1 CGI, provided the goods are transported physically outside the EU within 3 months of the export declaration and the seller holds documentary proof of export. Intra-EU dispatches to a VAT-registered recipient are exempt under Article 262 ter-1 CGI subject to the customer's EU TIN, Intrastat/DEB declarations and EC sales list filing. For the bakery master-franchise chain: (i) frozen and chilled pastries supplied from the central kitchen to overseas franchisees qualify for 0% when transported out of the EU, evidenced by the French customs export declaration (DAU) and transport documents; (ii) franchising, know-how and central-kitchen processing services rendered to non-resident franchisees fall outside French VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rules (Articles 259-1 and 259-A CGI); (iii) royalty and management-fee income from foreign franchisees is reportable in the export annex of the VAT return as 'exports outside the EU'. Refund of input VAT attributable to export activity is available under the ordinary deduction mechanism or through the 13th Directive refund procedure for VAT incurred in France by non-EU established businesses.

  • Déclaration Administrative Unique (DAU) / export customs declaration filed electronically on the DELTA customs platform
  • Commercial invoice (facture commerciale) in French or English with Incoterms 2020
  • Transport document evidencing movement out of the EU: Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), or CMR consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank SWIFT MT103 / transfer advice showing collection of foreign-currency export proceeds
  • Intrastat / Déclaration d'Echanges de Biens (DEB) declaration for intra-EU dispatches
  • Preferential certificate of origin (EUR.1, ATR, or Certificate of Origin) where a reduced FTA duty rate is claimed

Mandatory e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions under Article 289 CGI as amended by the Loi de Finances pour 2024: from 1 September 2026, all French-established taxable persons must be able to receive structured e-invoices; large and mid-size enterprises must also issue them; from 1 September 2027, SMEs and micro-enterprises must issue e-invoices. Invoices are transmitted through the government-operated Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or via DGFiP-certified Dematerialization Partner Platforms (PDP) using certified APIs and interchange formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII) conforming to EN 16931. The PPF operates as a transmission and central exchange hub comparable to a clearance mechanism; there is no pre-clearance physical 'digital stamp' requirement. Transactional data (e-reporting) is transmitted automatically to the DGFiP, covering B2B sales data and intra-Community B2B/B2C flows. Overseas suppliers selling remotely to French consumers use the OSS portal.

The DGFiP and French Customs (DGDDI) may verify export zero-rating within the ordinary 3-year limitation period (Article L. 176 LPF), extended to 10 years for concealed or fraudulent schemes. Substantiation failure triggers recharacterisation as a domestic taxable supply: VAT is reassessed with 0.20% monthly late-payment interest (Article 1727 CGI), a 40% bad-faith penalty (Article 1729-a CGI), or an 80% penalty for fraudulent manoeuvres (Article 1729-b CGI), plus full recovery of input VAT previously deducted. Criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI for tax evasion and Article 1741-IV CGI for organised fraud carries fines up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years, with publication of the judgment, judicial freezes and clawback of refunds. France does not impose a statutory 180-day repatriation deadline in the CGI; the local audit practice and documentary expectations align with the 180-day benchmark, and inward-remittance evidence must show collection within a commercially reasonable period. Directors face joint and several liability under Article L. 267 LPF for negligent management causing a company's tax default.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. France implements EU free movement of capital under TFEU Articles 63-66 and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier, Arts. L152-1 et seq.). There is no administrative authorization requirement for inbound equity, shareholder loans, profit repatriation, or dividend distribution by a non-resident (including Chinese-resident) shareholder of a French SARL. Practical hurdles are procedural only: (i) all foreign-exchange and cross-border financial operations must be routed through an authorized banking intermediary (intermédiaire agréé); (ii) statistical declarations to the Banque de France are mandatory for capital and financial flows with non-EEA countries; and (iii) the corresponding outbound remittance from China remains subject to SAFE (State Administration of Foreign Exchange) approvals and timing, which must be coordinated with the French payment schedule.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender requirements apply. France abolished exchange controls and all related surrender obligations. Export proceeds from the bakery and franchise operations (e.g., POS settlements, wholesale billing to franchisees, central-kitchen deliveries) may be retained in EUR or foreign currency, repatriated freely, or used directly for payments. The only compliance duties are statistical: the collecting bank records the trade settlement for the Banque de France/DGDDI balance-of-payments system, and the exporter must retain invoices, customs declarations (e-DPE), and the underlying contracts for the statutory five-year documentary retention period.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting within the Eurosystem and under the monetary policy framework of the European Central Bank (ECB). Prudential and AML supervision is exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution), while exchange-control statistics and balance-of-payments reporting duties are administered by the Banque de France Directorate of Statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM, and only as a supplementary liquidity rail, never as the sole banking relationship. ACPR-licensed French EMIs (Qonto, Shine, Anytime) onboard SARLs rapidly and support daily SEPA operations, card issuing, and POS clearing, but they do not provide commercial letters of credit, foreign-currency accounts, or CNY hedging. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Revolut Business, Payoneer) offer multi-currency wallets and competitive FX, yet they operate in France under the PSD2 passport (freedom to provide services) and are not qualified as Banque de France authorized intermediaries; consequently, capital injections and formal outward direct investment declarations must pass through a Tier-1 credit institution. Treasury policy for the SARL must therefore cap EMI balances, sweep funds daily to the Tier-1 bank, and restrict EMIs to non-core receipts and payables.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity (share capital increase) or shareholder current-account (compte courant d'associés) funding from the Chinese parent must be received in EUR at the SARL's account held with a Banque de France authorized intermediary. (2) Commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707): required for high-value bakery equipment and packaging imports from China; the French issuing bank sends the LC to the Chinese advising bank, with documents examined against UCP 600. (3) SEPA instant credit transfer (SCT Inst): appropriate for intra-EU supplier payments (flour, dairy, logistics) only; SWIFT is mandatory for payments to China as SEPA has no Chinese reach. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM, and only as a supplementary liquidity rail, never as the sole banking relationship. ACPR-licensed French EMIs (Qonto, Shine, Anytime) onboard SARLs rapidly and support daily SEPA operations, card issuing, and POS clearing, but they do not provide commercial letters of credit, foreign-currency accounts, or CNY hedging. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Revolut Business, Payoneer) offer multi-currency wallets and competitive FX, yet they operate in France under the PSD2 passport (freedom to provide services) and are not qualified as Banque de France authorized intermediaries; consequently, capital injections and formal outward direct investment declarations must pass through a Tier-1 credit institution. Treasury policy for the SARL must therefore cap EMI balances, sweep funds daily to the Tier-1 bank, and restrict EMIs to non-core receipts and payables.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. A 3.3% social contribution on corporate income may apply to companies with annual turnover of at least EUR 7.63 million and corporate income tax liability exceeding EUR 763,000, producing a maximum effective rate of approximately 25.83%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate recipient under Article 119 bis of the French Tax Code. The 12.8% rate generally concerns dividends paid to French-resident individual taxpayers and is not the standard non-resident corporate rate. A qualifying EU or EEA parent company may obtain a domestic exemption, commonly where the parent holds at least 10% of the distributing company for the required period and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. Treaty rates may reduce the 25% rate, often to 0%, 5%, 10%, or 15%, depending on the treaty and ownership conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and accounts are generally due within three months after the financial year-end. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the normal deadline is the second business day following 1 May of the following year. Tax payment dates may differ from the return-filing date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year taxpayers, the annual filing is generally due on the second business day following 1 May. For other year-ends, filing is generally due within three months after the closing date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 threshold for full transfer-pricing documentation obligations, generally based on the French entity's annual turnover or gross assets, or the corresponding threshold being met by a related French or foreign enterprise or qualifying group circumstances. A simplified transfer-pricing declaration may apply from EUR 50,000,000 turnover or gross assets, subject to statutory conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone economic substance regulation equivalent to the UAE ESR regime. Substance is nevertheless assessed through transfer-pricing arm's-length requirements, the principal-purpose and anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, controlled foreign company rules, the abuse-of-law doctrine, interest limitation rules, and general anti-avoidance provisions. The French SARL should maintain French premises, personnel or operational capacity appropriate to its bakery and franchise functions, decision-making records, inventory and production evidence, customer and supplier contracts, and contemporaneous support for royalty, management-fee, financing, and procurement arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, late-payment interest, 40% or 80% tax penalties, and criminal tax-fraud sanctions. Ordinary aggravated tax fraud can carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000; aggravated circumstances can increase imprisonment to seven years and the fine to EUR 3 million. Corporate criminal fines may be multiplied under French law.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur criminal liability for their own participation and may be declared jointly and severally liable for tax debt where fraudulent maneuvers or serious and repeated non-compliance made recovery impossible under Article L267 of the French Tax Procedure Code. Civil restitution, asset enforcement, management bans, and confiscation may apply. Travel bans are not an automatic tax sanction but may arise from judicial proceedings or enforcement circumstances.
VAT Fraud and Cash-Register Manipulation

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, recovery of wrongly claimed input VAT, late-payment interest, penalties of up to 40% or 80% depending on intent, and possible criminal prosecution for organized or aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may be personally prosecuted where they directed or knowingly tolerated fraudulent reporting. Personal financial liability may follow under tax-director liability rules or criminal restitution orders.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Income tax reassessment, denial of deductions, withholding tax exposure, late-payment interest, transfer-pricing penalties, documentation penalties, and possible 40% or 80% conduct-based penalties. A transfer-pricing adjustment may also create double-taxation and customs or VAT consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal exposure for knowingly approving sham transactions, false documentation, or fraudulent tax positions. Ordinary pricing disagreement alone does not automatically create criminal personal liability.
Undocumented Cross-Border Royalties, Interest, and Dividends

Statutory sanction: Assessment of withholding tax at the domestic rate, interest, penalties, payer liability, and possible denial of treaty or parent-subsidiary relief. The French company may remain liable as withholding agent even if the amount was paid to the recipient.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for deliberate withholding failures, false certificates, or knowingly abusive arrangements. Personal criminal exposure depends on participation, intent, and the facts established by the authorities.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions or treaty benefits, recharacterization of payments, withholding tax, abuse-of-law adjustments, penalties, and potential controlled foreign company consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil and criminal exposure if they knowingly implement fictitious arrangements or conceal the beneficial owner. Mere use of a foreign group company is not unlawful where the arrangement has genuine commercial substance and is properly documented.
Beneficial Ownership and AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative and criminal AML sanctions, account restrictions, transaction blocking, regulatory reporting, fines, and possible prosecution for money laundering or concealment of proceeds. Corporate sanctions may include significant fines and dissolution in severe cases.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, beneficial owners, and compliance personnel may be personally prosecuted. Imprisonment, confiscation, management bans, and judicial restrictions may apply depending on the underlying offense.
False Accounting and Corporate Records

Statutory sanction: Accounting penalties, tax reassessment, rejection of deductions, criminal sanctions for false or misleading accounts, and potential insolvency or bankruptcy-related offenses.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur personal criminal liability for false accounts, misuse of corporate assets, or fraudulent insolvency conduct. Civil liability for losses caused to the company, creditors, or public authorities may also apply.
Payroll, Social Security, and Undeclared Work

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid contributions, surcharges, labor penalties, tax adjustments, closure orders in serious cases, and criminal sanctions for concealed work.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and de facto employer may be personally prosecuted and ordered to pay restitution or social-security liabilities. Directors may also face management bans or imprisonment in aggravated cases.
Customs and Import Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Recovery of customs duties and import VAT, customs fines, seizure or forfeiture of goods, suspension of customs simplifications, and possible criminal proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors and customs representatives may be liable for deliberate misdeclarations, fraudulent origin claims, or prohibited imports. Personal sanctions depend on knowledge, authorization, and participation.
Franchise and Royalty Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Deduction denial, withholding tax reassessment, VAT reverse-charge adjustments, transfer-pricing penalties, and possible anti-abuse recharacterization.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability where agreements are sham documents, payments benefit related persons improperly, or records are knowingly falsified. Commercially genuine royalties with adequate evidence do not by themselves create personal liability.
