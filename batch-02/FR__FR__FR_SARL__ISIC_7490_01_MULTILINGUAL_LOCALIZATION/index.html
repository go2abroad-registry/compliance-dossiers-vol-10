Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de commerce of the registered-office jurisdiction, acting through the Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises operated by INPI (Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle).

Governing statute

Code de commerce (French Commercial Code), Book II, Title III — Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 et seq. (SARL); Décret n°67-236 of 23 March 1967 on commercial companies, as amended.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form M0 (déclaration de création d'une société à responsabilité limitée) filed electronically through the Guichet unique; registration is evidenced by the extract Kbis.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital is imposed; the shareholders freely determine the capital in the Articles of Association. At incorporation, cash contributions must be fully paid or at least one-fifth of their amount must be paid in, with the balance payable within five years. All cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked bank account, with a notary, or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until RCS registration, and the depositary must issue an attestation de dépôt des fonds.

Funding & unlock path: For office premises, no civil-defense pre-clearance is required. The employer must prepare the DUERP (Document unique d'évaluation des risques professionnels), install fire extinguishers and first-aid equipment, maintain clear evacuation routes, and ensure electrical installations are verified. If the premises are open to the public, they must comply with Code du travail and, where applicable, with the ERP classification safety requirements of the Commission de Sécurité; a health and safety visit is triggered upon hiring the first employee.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); portal: impots.gouv.fr and pro.impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Non-resident entities must register through the online Business Formalités portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) by filing an entity creation/extempore form (M06-E) with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for the registered office or French branch; the SIE allocates a French SIRET number, intra-community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire), and NC code. A non-EU-established taxable person must designate an individual or entity accredited as VAT fiscal representative in France before filing, and may be subject to bank guarantee requirements.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Non-resident entities must register through the online Business Formalités portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) by filing an entity creation/extempore form (M06-E) with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for the registered office or French branch; the SIE allocates a French SIRET number, intra-community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire), and NC code. A non-EU-established taxable person must designate an individual or entity accredited as VAT fiscal representative in France before filing, and may be subject to bank guarantee requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not employ US-style check-the-box elections; a SARL is compulsorily subject to corporate income tax (IS) under Article 206(I) of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI). Available elections: (i) family SARLs under Article 239 CA can elect partnership-style pass-through taxation for up to five family members; (ii) French parent-subsidiary tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A allows 95%-owned French subsidiaries to form an AI35 group; (iii) small businesses may elect to waive the VAT small business exemption and apply normal rules; (iv) entities qualifying as sociétés de personnes may elect to be taxed as corporations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No domestic turnover threshold applies to non-resident suppliers; any taxable supply deemed to occur in French territory obligates VAT registration in France, regardless of revenue. For domestic micro-enterprises, the franchise en base exemption threshold under Article 293 B CGI is €91,900 for goods and services sold to customers not exercising taxable activity, and €36,800 for professional services; exceeding these thresholds for two consecutive years requires mandatory VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Articles 262(I) and 262(II) of the CGI, exportation of tangible goods to destinations outside the EU and to the overseas territories of EU member states is zero-rated provided the physical removal from France is evidenced by a confirmed export declaration. For services (including multilingual translation and dubbing), zero-rating applies where the service falls under the place-of-supply rules: B2B services are taxable in the customer's country rather than France if the customer is established outside France; B2C services supplied to non-EU customers are generally not French VAT if the effective use and exploitation occurs outside the EU. Substantiation requires retaining export invoices, transport documents, VAT registration details of foreign customers, and contracts establishing the cross-border nature and destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (single administrative document DAU / DELTA data record with EXIT status)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or multimodal transport document
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the export declaration
  • Evidence of payment and foreign exchange repatriation (bank SWIFT MT103 or comparable remittance document)

France mandates B2B e-invoicing through dematerialisation platforms registered with the State (Plugins de Dématérialisation Partenaires – PDP) or the public portal Chorus Pro. Under the Finance Act 2023 schedule, phased mandation takes effect 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for all VAT payers. Each platform must transmit structured invoices (FACTUR-X / XML) to the tax authority in near real-time, using the AI35 state directory, and apply tidstream digital stamping and clearance reporting. Foreign suppliers remain fully subject to the mandate after the gateway provisions are implemented, but until the operational dates they may issue paper invoices and are require to register their PDP access accordingly.

French customs and the DGFiP conduct post-export verification; if zero-rating is rejected for insufficient proof, incorrect legal basis, or fraudulent intent, the tax authorities reassess VAT at 20% plus early payment interest at 0.2% per month and apply a 40% late-payment penalty under CGI Article 1784. In cases of bad faith or abusive scheme, the penalty increases to 80%, with possible confiscation of goods and criminal prosecution for attempted fraudulent export tax credit claim. Refund claim clawback under Article 271(n) permits the State to demand repayment of deducted VAT within the normal statute of limitation (generally six years).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member state with full EUR convertibility on both current and capital account. Exchange controls were abolished pursuant to Directive 88/361/EEC and Articles 63 to 66 of the TFEU, codified in Articles L151-1 et seq., L152-1 et seq. and L153-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code. Cross-border service fees, royalties, profit remittances, shareholder loans and equity injections do not require prior administrative authorization or conversion licensing. Legal hurdles are principally documentary: mandatory balance-of-payments reporting to Banque de France where aggregate annual cross-border flows equal or exceed EUR 750,000 per reporting entity, and direct reporting of external financial contracts equal to or exceeding EUR 3,000,000. Dividend distributions by a SARL to non-resident shareholders remain freely remittable but trigger the 25% withholding levy (prélèvement forfaitaire) unless reduced or exempted under an applicable double taxation treaty; no separate FX clearing or profit repatriation authorization applies beyond the standard corporate tax and distribution formalities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. France has eliminated all statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios and prescribed repatriation deadlines. The SARL may invoice localization services in EUR or any contractually agreed foreign currency, may retain export receipts in multicurrency treasury accounts, and may decide its own conversion timing subject only to prudent FX risk management. The only binding regime is the statistical reporting obligation to Banque de France on aggregate balances, plus the EU cash declaration threshold of EUR 10,000 for cross-border cash movements; no percentage of export proceeds must be converted into EUR within any statutory window.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as the national central bank, exercising delegated foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments statistical functions in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty), the designated French foreign-exchange administration under the Code monétaire et financier.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility with meaningful structural limitations. Wise B2B and Payoneer, operating in France under the PSD2 e-money passport and ACPR supervision, are viable supplementary collection and disbursement rails for low-value cross-border localization fees. They cannot, however, replace a licensed credit institution for core treasury functions: they cannot hold a statutory capital-blocking deposit for SARL incorporation, cannot issue irrevocable payment instruments, do not provide direct access to the French clearing system or TARGET2 in a capacity sufficient for corporate-grade settlement, and are subject to e-money safeguarding rules rather than bank deposit guarantees. Optimal design is a hybrid: a tier-1 French commercial bank account for statutory capital, primary collections and treasury, with Wise B2B and Payoneer as downstream rails for freelancer payouts and satellite currency corridors.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SARL's statutorily contributed capital is initially deposited into a blocked account held with a licensed French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations. A non-resident subscriber delivers funds by SWIFT MT103 from an account held in the subscriber's exact legal name, with remittance field 70 clearly stating 'APPORT EN ESPÈCES - CONSTITUTION SARL [ENTITY NAME] - [SHAREHOLDER NAME]'. Upon issuance of the Kbis registration extract, the blocked deposit is released into the operating account. Concurrently, the transaction must be captured for French balance-of-payments reporting if the aggregate annual cross-border flow threshold of EUR 750,000 is met or exceeded; Banque de France requires the operation to be classified under the direct-investment/equity capital category (Balance of Payments entity item F.1 equity capital) using the reporting taxonomy applicable to EUR-denominated and foreign-currency-only flows. For service-revenue engineering previously, the SARL should instruct its counterparties to use the BoP purpose code for business services (code 213 – business and management consulting services or code 203 – other business services) in their incoming MT103s; where the revenue is routed through an EMI, the EMI is obligated under PSD2 to render transactional data enabling accurate BoP classification.Disbursements to EU-based subcontractors, freelance linguists and dubbing studios are executed as EUR SEPA Credit Transfers (SEPA CT) within the Single Euro Payments Area, settling same-day through TARGET2 without requiring MT103 messages; they retain the SEPA end-to-end reference structure for audit. Disbursements to third-country service providers, including non-EU technicians and distributors, are executed by SWIFT MT103 single payment instructions or MT202 COV for interbank cover, using correspondent clearing rails and including purpose coding for service transactions. Commercial letters of credit under UCP600 remain legally permissible but are commercially suboptimal for localization services because such services are intangible and milestone-based; LC use is reserved, if ever required at a client's insistence, for hybrid media-delivery transactions with documentary evidence. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid to non-resident corporate shareholders, subject to domestic exemptions, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, applicable tax treaties and anti-abuse rules. Dividends paid to qualifying EU corporate parents may be exempt where the minimum participation, holding-period, subject-to-tax and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the fiscal year. For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the normal electronic filing deadline is the second business day after 1 May of the following year, subject to the year's officially published calendar. The corporate income tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following the fiscal year-end; for a 31 December year-end, it is generally due by 15 May.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory full transfer-pricing documentation generally applies where the French entity or French tax group has annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, or where it is controlled by or controls an entity meeting that threshold. A French entity below the EUR 400 million threshold may still need to demonstrate arm's-length pricing and may be required to file the annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration, generally where turnover or gross assets reach EUR 50 million or where the relevant group conditions apply. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a separate standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless required under general anti-abuse principles, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, permanent-establishment rules, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules and the principal-purpose test in applicable treaties. A French SARL should maintain genuine French decision-making, appropriate personnel, premises or operating resources, commercial risk control, accounting records, banking activity and evidence that localization services are actually performed. A shell or conduit structure may be challenged, with denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization, transfer-pricing adjustments, withholding tax assessments and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, late-payment interest and penalties generally ranging from 10% to 80%, depending on the conduct and procedural circumstances. Criminal tax fraud may result in imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated cases potentially carrying up to seven years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 3 million, including specified organized or offshore arrangements.

Director personal liability: Managers and directors may incur criminal liability as perpetrators or accomplices, civil liability for restitution and tax-related losses, management disqualification and asset-based recovery. Travel restrictions or passport measures may be ordered in criminal proceedings where legally justified; imprisonment is possible in aggravated cases. Limited liability does not protect intentional fraud or personal misconduct.
Fictitious, Dormant or Shell Company Conduct

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, denial of deductions and treaty relief, permanent-establishment or beneficial-ownership recharacterization, withholding tax assessments, 40% or 80% penalties where deliberate or fraudulent abuse is established, and potential criminal prosecution for tax fraud, false accounting or misuse of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fraudulent or grossly negligent management, unpaid taxes in cases of deliberate obstruction or serious repeated non-compliance, unlawful distributions, misuse of corporate assets and criminal participation. Civil recovery, director disqualification and imprisonment may apply depending on the offense.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Taxable income may be increased under the French abnormal act of management and transfer-pricing rules. The resulting tax, interest and penalties may be imposed, including penalties for incomplete or inaccurate transfer-pricing declarations and documentation failures.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for intentional or grossly negligent management and criminal exposure where fabricated documentation, false accounting or tax fraud is involved. Routine transfer-pricing disagreement alone does not automatically create personal criminal liability.
Undeclared Cross-Border Payments and Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: The French payer may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, late-payment interest and applicable penalties. Treaty relief may be denied, and a 75% withholding rate can apply in specified non-cooperative-state situations. False or fabricated residence certificates may create separate criminal exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may be personally exposed where they deliberately organized withholding tax evasion, falsified documents or obstructed recovery. Ordinary administrative withholding errors generally remain corporate liabilities unless aggravated by personal misconduct.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial-Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative, regulatory and criminal consequences may include fines, account restrictions, confiscation, reporting obligations, and prosecution for money laundering. Money laundering may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with aggravated forms carrying higher penalties, including where proceeds derive from serious offenses or are conducted habitually or professionally.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be prosecuted personally, face confiscation, civil recovery, professional or management bans and imprisonment. Corporate limited liability does not shield knowing participation, concealment, facilitation or failure to prevent personally attributable criminal conduct.
False Accounting, Misuse of Corporate Assets and Illegal Distributions

Statutory sanction: Accounts may be rejected or restated, distributions may be recovered, tax deductions denied, and corporate income tax and withholding tax reassessed. Misuse of corporate assets by SARL managers may constitute a criminal offense with imprisonment and fines under French commercial law, in addition to tax sanctions.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally liable for restitution, creditor losses, tax losses, prohibited distributions and criminal misuse of corporate assets. Disqualification from management and imprisonment may apply where the statutory criminal elements are satisfied.
Failure to Maintain Books, Invoices and Tax Evidence

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may reject accounts, assess taxable income by estimation, deny deductions and credits, impose late-filing or insufficiency penalties, and apply aggravated penalties where the evidentiary failure supports fraudulent conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil liability for deficient management and criminal liability for intentional false accounting, destruction or concealment of records. Imprisonment is not automatic for ordinary recordkeeping failures but may arise from associated fraud or obstruction.
