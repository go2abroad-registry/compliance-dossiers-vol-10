Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The articles must state the capital amount and subscribed shares. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation; the balance may be paid within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must generally be fully paid upon issuance. A French bank account may be opened for the capital deposit, and the depositary issues a certificate of deposit. The capital may be released after registration. The stated fee is an indicative USD equivalent of the principal commercial-registration charge; registry, beneficial-owner, legal-notice, banking, translation, and publication charges may apply separately and vary by tariff and exchange rate.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain the applicable building permit and, where relevant, approval for an établissement recevant du public or industrial premises from the mairie and fire-safety authorities, including consultation with the Service départemental d'incendie et de secours (SDIS). Complete fire-compartmentation, emergency access, alarm, extinguishing, evacuation, and hazardous-material controls. Commission structural-load, machinery, electrical, pressure-equipment, refrigeration, and lifting-equipment inspections where applicable. Before operations and hiring, implement the occupational-risk assessment document (DUERP), machine safeguarding, chemical and cleaning-agent controls, food-safety procedures, occupational health arrangements, workplace training, and required labour and social-security registrations.

Current-account convertibility

Full capital account and current account convertibility under EU free movement of capital rules. Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the central bank. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted freely to EUR. Statutory requirements include customs declarations and potential VAT reporting; no conversion ratios or surrender timelines apply.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate in France, plus applicable social contribution of 3.3% for companies whose corporate income tax exceeds EUR 763,000 and whose annual revenue is at least EUR 7.63 million, producing a combined marginal rate of approximately 25.83%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable tax treaties. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive regime may provide a 0% exemption for a qualifying EU or European Economic Area corporate parent meeting ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse requirements. French domestic participation exemption generally applies to qualifying dividends received by a French corporate shareholder, with a taxable add-back of 1% or 5% depending on the regime and ownership relationship.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is due within three months after the fiscal year-end. For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the return is generally due by the second business day following 1 May, subject to the tax administration's annually published calendar and electronic-filing extensions. A newly incorporated SAS must file for its first accounting period by the deadline applicable to the period-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets generally triggers the full French transfer-pricing documentation obligation, including Local File and Master File requirements, subject to statutory extension rules for certain controlled groups. A country-by-country report generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. French entities below the full-documentation threshold must still apply the arm's-length principle and maintain documentation adequate to substantiate material controlled transactions. A transfer-pricing summary declaration, generally Form 2257-SD, may apply independently of the full-documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone economic-substance regulation equivalent to the United Arab Emirates ESR regime. Nevertheless, French anti-abuse rules, the general abuse-of-law doctrine, treaty principal-purpose tests, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled foreign company rules, hybrid-mismatch rules, interest-limitation rules, anti-diversion rules, and transfer-pricing provisions require genuine commercial substance. The SAS should maintain French decision-making, manufacturing capacity, personnel, premises, production records, inventory control, commercial risks, financing evidence, and contemporaneous board and management records consistent with its claimed functions. A low-substance French entity receiving substantial royalties, management fees, financing income, or entrepreneurial margins is a high-risk audit profile.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent filing

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, recovery of unpaid tax, late-payment interest, and penalties generally up to 80% for fraudulent conduct. Criminal tax fraud may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years and an individual fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated sanctions potentially reaching 7 years and EUR 3 million in specified serious cases, including organized fraud or use of accounts or entities abroad.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, director-disqualification measures, personal tax liability in cases of fraudulent or seriously negligent conduct under the French Tax Code, and civil restitution. Corporate criminal fines can be multiplied under French criminal law. A general automatic travel ban does not arise solely from a tax assessment, but judicial control, passport restrictions, asset-freezing, or detention may apply in criminal proceedings.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit diversion

Statutory sanction: Taxable income may be increased under the French abnormal act of management and transfer-pricing rules. The reassessment may include withholding tax, interest, a 10% or higher penalty, up to 40% for deliberate non-compliance, and up to 80% for fraudulent conduct. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation may trigger a separate penalty generally calculated by reference to the higher of a statutory minimum or 0.5% of the transactions covered by missing documentation, subject to statutory limits.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for knowingly approving non-arm's-length arrangements, criminal liability if the conduct forms part of tax fraud, and personal tax-liability proceedings for fraudulent or seriously negligent management. Repeated failures may support management disqualification and asset-preservation measures.
Artificial shell company, conduit, or treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Treaty benefits, deductions, exemptions, and tax credits may be denied under French anti-abuse rules, the abuse-of-law doctrine, beneficial-ownership principles, and treaty principal-purpose tests. Reassessed tax may be increased by penalties of 40% for deliberate abuse and 80% for fraudulent abuse, in addition to interest and potential withholding-tax liability.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil restitution, tax-liability proceedings, criminal liability where fraud is established, and management bans. A shell company is not unlawful solely because it has foreign owners or limited staffing, but artificiality combined with tax evasion, false invoicing, or money laundering materially increases personal exposure.
Undeclared cross-border payments and withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The SAS may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, interest, filing penalties, and penalties for deliberate or fraudulent non-compliance. The payer generally cannot rely on an informal residence certificate where the statutory treaty-relief documentation and beneficial-ownership conditions are not met.

Director personal liability: Directors may be pursued for management fault, fraudulent withholding-tax avoidance, or participation in tax fraud. Civil and criminal consequences may include restitution, fines, management disqualification, asset freezing, and imprisonment where the conduct satisfies criminal tax or money-laundering offenses.
Money laundering, illicit funds, and AML failure

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is generally punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 375,000, with aggravated cases generally punishable by up to 10 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 750,000. Corporate entities may face substantially higher criminal fines, confiscation, dissolution in severe cases, and regulatory sanctions for AML-control failures.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face imprisonment, personal fines, confiscation, civil restitution, management bans, judicial restrictions, and asset freezing. Travel restrictions may be imposed by a court in criminal proceedings but are not an automatic consequence of an AML compliance deficiency.
False accounting and corporate-record manipulation

Statutory sanction: The company may face tax reassessment, accounting and commercial-law penalties, criminal fines, confiscation, and possible dissolution for serious offenses. False accounting may support separate fraud, forgery, use-of-false-document, or tax-evasion charges.

Director personal liability: The president, directors, finance officers, and de facto managers may face criminal prosecution, civil damages, creditor claims, management disqualification, personal liability for insufficient assets in insolvency proceedings, and imprisonment where the facts satisfy the applicable offense.
Deduction of non-business or insufficiently documented expenses

Statutory sanction: The expense may be denied, taxable income increased, and related withholding or social taxes assessed. Deliberate or fraudulent conduct may attract penalties of up to 40% or 80%, together with interest and possible criminal tax-fraud proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for restitution or tax debts where the expense reflects fraudulent or seriously negligent management. Improper distributions may also create repayment claims against recipients and liability under French corporate and insolvency rules.
VAT and excise-related fraud connected with food manufacturing

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, interest, penalties generally ranging from 10% to 80% depending on conduct, denial of input-credit claims, and potential criminal prosecution for organized or fraudulent conduct. Product classification and applicable VAT treatment must be verified against the exact product composition, packaging, and sales channel.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal tax liability for fraudulent or seriously negligent conduct, criminal liability for intentional fraud, civil restitution, management bans, and asset-preservation measures.
