Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); online portal impots.gouv.fr, professional space 'Gérer mon espace professionnel'

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in France but making taxable supplies in France must register for French VAT by filing Form 1606-SD (Cerfa 15433*04) with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) territorialement compétent or, for non-established entities, the Direction des Non-Résidents (DNR) / Service des Impôts des Entreprises Non-Résidents. The filing must be accompanied by certified constitutional documents, proof of foreign registration, evidence of economic activity, and, where required for third-country entities, a fiscal representative appointment. A valid French VAT ID in format FRAB123456789 is issued within approximately 8–15 working days. For entities with a French fixed establishment, the standard SIREN/SIRET registration through INPI and DGFiP is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a 'check-the-box' regime. An SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS). Certain close-held or qualifying SARLs may elect to be taxed as a partnership (Impôt sur le Revenu, IR) under Article 239 bis AA CGI for a maximum of five fiscal years. New SARLs may elect taxation under IS by filing Form 200-2. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; instead, anti-hybrid rules in Articles 205, 209, 212 bis CGI apply automatically to align deductions with French taxable outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 (non-established taxable persons must register from first taxable supply; the domestic small-business franchise exemption of EUR 36,600 for services / EUR 85,000 for goods is not a registration threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% French VAT) applies to tangible goods exported outside the EU upon physical export evidenced by customs declaration. For services, including programmatic advertising DSP/SSP services, French VAT is not chargeable where the recipient is a taxable person established in another EU Member State (reverse charge, Article 259-1 CGI) or established outside the EU (place of supply outside France). For B2C digital services supplied to non-EU consumers, no French VAT applies; B2C domestic supplies remain taxable. All export-service claims require documentary proof of recipient establishment, service description, and, for intra-EU B2B, a valid EU VAT identification number.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/ECS) cleared by French customs for tangible goods
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation evidencing actual receipt of payment
  • Commercial invoice with full description, Incoterms, and customs value
  • For offshore services: service contract, proof of recipient establishment, and, if intra-EU, valid EU VAT ID and reverse-charge notification

France has implemented mandatory electronic invoicing for all B2B supplies, applicable in phases from September 2026 and September 2027. Invoices are transmitted to DGFiP through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a registered Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) via API in structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII). No real-time fiscal stamping or continuous clearance requirement exists in the Italian or Spanish model; however, transaction data must be transmitted within one working day. Businesses may use only certified, compliant software for cash register and billing operations.

DGFiP and French Customs may conduct documentary and physical audits of zero-rated transactions, including retrospective clawback of falsely claimed export exemptions. Reassessment restores taxable French VAT at 20%, plus statutory interest of 0.20% per month. Administrative penalties under Article 1729 CGI reach 40% in cases of bad faith and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Articles 1741 and 1743 CGI exposes corporate directors to fines up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. The routine reassessment window is six years, extendable to ten in fraud or undisclosed foreign positions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro-area regime applies: capital and current account transactions are fully liberalised under Articles L.151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier). No exchange-control approval is required for inbound equity injection, dividend repatriation, or trade settlement. Profit remittance by a French SARL to non-resident shareholders is subject solely to French dividend withholding tax (25% domestic rate, reduced to 0-15% under applicable tax treaty) and to statistical reporting; no administrative FX conversion or prior authorisation is imposed. Residual obligations concern anti-money-laundering reporting, balance-of-payments statistical declarations filed by the credit institution, and the €10,000 customs declaration threshold for physical cash crossing the border.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France imposes no surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio on export or services receipts, including programmatic advertising revenue. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or in any foreign currency, may be credited to domiciled or foreign accounts, and may be used freely. The receiving bank must report each transaction to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics using the relevant payment purpose code, but no prior declaration, repatriation period, or conversion mandate applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) — national competent authority for monetary policy, balance-of-payments statistics, and foreign-exchange regulatory reporting; prudential supervision over credit institutions and payment services is exercised jointly through the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Viability rating: Medium-High as complementary treasury rail, Low as exclusive banking solution. A non-EU offshore EMI without a full EU payment-institution licence cannot lawfully provide payment accounts to a French SARL; the French regime, implementing the Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366 under Article L.521-1 of the Code monétaire et financier, requires an ACPR licence or a valid passport of an EEA licence. European digital EMIs such as Qonto, Revolut Business, and Wise Business are viable for high-volume operational receipts and disbursements, supporting EUR and multi-currency accounts with French or EEA IBANs and SWIFT/SEPA rails. They are not acceptable for the statutory capital-escrow deposit required before RCS registration of the SARL, nor as the principal treasury account for long-term financing, and they may impose lower payment limits and stricter freezing policies during AML review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SARL statutory capital and any shareholder contribution must first be credited to a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) held with a licensed French credit institution or a notary. The contributing foreign shareholder must wire via SWIFT MT103, or via SEPA Credit Transfer if the shareholder is in the eurozone, quoting the payment purpose word 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION / APPORTS EN CAPITAL — SARL CONSTITUTION' and referencing in field 70 the exact wording 'COMPTE BLOQUÉ PRÉALABLE [SARL NAME] — [NOTARY OR BANK NAME]'. The bank's IBAN of the blocked account must be stated precisely, and mis-tagging inbound equity as trade invoice, remuneration, or loan is strictly avoided as it triggers a Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose-code mismatch. After the manager's declaration of subscription and payment (déclaration de souscription et de versement) and RCS registration, the bank releases the funds to the SARL's current operational account. The credit institution files the statistical liaison financière declaration with the Banque de France; no prior administrative authority approval is required and no cross-border restrictions apply to the EUR.EUR disbursements within the eurozone use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and, for urgent low-value payments, SEPA Instant (SCT Inst); euro payments outside the SEPA zone clear via Target2 and SWIFT MT103. Non-EUR settlements, chiefly USD invoices from ad exchanges, data licensing, and fulfilment providers, must be made by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer, or MT202 COV where cover is separate, inserting the supplier invoice number and contract reference in field 70. A Commercial Letter of Credit is confined to high-value hardware or licence procurement and is unsuitable for DSP/SSP programmatic campaign payments, which are settled via commercial credit cards, virtual cards, or direct invoice agents. The beneficiary IBAN and BIC must match exactly the supplier's banking details; any third-party-payment instruction triggers enhanced AML review.
