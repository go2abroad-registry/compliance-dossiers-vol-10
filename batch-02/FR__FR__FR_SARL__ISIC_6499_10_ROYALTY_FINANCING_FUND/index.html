Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Name reservation / lock period

France does not operate a statutory name-reservation system; the name is secured only upon final RCS registration or upon earlier INPI trademark filing. Rejection may occur during the Greffe's formal review of the constitutional dossier. SOP upon rejection: (1) request the written statement of reasons from the Greffier within 8 calendar days; (2) file an administrative appeal (recours gracieux) before the juge commis à la surveillance du registre within 15 days of notification pursuant to décret n°84-406 of 30 May 1984, Article 24; (3) alternatively, amend the name and re-file the complete incorporation dossier through the INPI Guichet unique as a de novo filing, with priority treatment requested; (4) if the refusal is based on a prior identical trademark, initiate opposition or coexistence negotiation before INPI under Article L.712-4 CPI. A rejected name cannot be reserved or extended.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies (Code de commerce, Article L.223-2); the capital is freely determined in the articles. At least 20% of cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow bank account in the name of the company in formation before the registration filing, and the balance must be paid up within five years. The deposited funds are released to the gérant only after RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: No fire-safety certification is required if the premises are not open to the public and are not classed as an ICPE. The employer must still maintain a workplace risk assessment, keep a single safety register, inspect electrical installations, install appropriate fire-detection and emergency measures, and comply with labor-code structural and accessibility standards for employees.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies (Code de commerce, Article L.223-2); the capital is freely determined in the articles. At least 20% of cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow bank account in the name of the company in formation before the registration filing, and the balance must be paid up within five years. The deposited funds are released to the gérant only after RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) of the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Recovery; online portals: impots.gouv.fr for corporate tax/VAT filings and formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique) for entity and tax registration; VAT number validation via VIES (europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies).

E-government filing portal

For a domestically incorporated SARL, the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) completes the single registration dossier within 24-48 hours, which triggers simultaneous issuance of: (i) SIREN and SIRET codes by INSEE; (ii) APE/NAF classification code; (iii) intra-Community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) by the competent Centre de Finances Publiques (CFE); and (iv) EORI number for customs if export/import activity is declared. Foreign entities with a permanent establishment or a direct registration obligation in France must appoint an accredited fiscal representative in the case of non-EU established suppliers, and file Form No. 13700 (France’s non-resident VAT registration form or the EU One-Stop-Shop route for distance sales). The VAT number is generally issued within 1-2 weeks of the dossier being complete.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (SARL is by default taxable to French corporation tax — Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS — on its worldwide profit at the ordinary 25% rate; the territorial scope rule limits taxation to profits attributable to a French permanent establishment; a SARL under specific conditions of small size and management may elect partnership-like pass-through treatment under Impôt sur le Revenu (IR), but the debt-fund activity described under ISIC 6499 in practice remains subject to IS, and revenue-based financing income would be taxed as banking/commercial income, not as capital gains relief assets). — For a domestically incorporated SARL, the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) completes the single registration dossier within 24-48 hours, which triggers simultaneous issuance of: (i) SIREN and SIRET codes by INSEE; (ii) APE/NAF classification code; (iii) intra-Community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) by the competent Centre de Finances Publiques (CFE); and (iv) EORI number for customs if export/import activity is declared. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SARL is by default taxable to French corporation tax — Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS — on its worldwide profit at the ordinary 25% rate; the territorial scope rule limits taxation to profits attributable to a French permanent establishment; a SARL under specific conditions of small size and management may elect partnership-like pass-through treatment under Impôt sur le Revenu (IR), but the debt-fund activity described under ISIC 6499 in practice remains subject to IS, and revenue-based financing income would be taxed as banking/commercial income, not as capital gains relief assets).

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box classification. The available elections are: (i) option for IR under Article 239 bis AA of the French General Tax Code (CGI) for small SARLs (less than 5 employees, turnover under the thresholds set by Article 50-0 CGI) — rarely suitable for an active financing fund; (ii) election for group fiscal consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI if the SARL is a 95% or more French-DGFiP resident subsidiary of a French consolidating parent (allows offsetting losses group-wide and restriction of foreign credit); (iii) approval-based election to obtain the status of ’jeune entreprise innovante’ (JEI) or ’société de financement’ for refinancing vehicles, which can affect the taxation of certain financial instruments; (iv) for hybrid instruments (e.g., revenue-based financing modelled as a redeemable preference interest), the documentation must respect the attribution rule of Article 212 bis CGI (interest deduction) and the hybrid mismatch rules transposing ATAD that disallow a double non-taxation result; no conversion from IS to IR is possible after 5 years of existence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for a French-resident SARL: VAT registration and chargeability commence with the first taxable transaction (no de minimis threshold for corporations). For non-EU-established suppliers, the distance-sales and electronic-services union threshold is EUR 10,000; above this, VAT must be charged in France or declared via the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) / Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) schemes. As a pure financial intermediaire, the SARL typically falls under the VAT exemption of Articles 261 C 1° CGI (lending, credit intermediation, debt factoring), thus no output VAT is chargeable on its revenue-based financing income; input tax recovery is proportionally restricted under the feeder-of-exempt-services prorata rule.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under French VAT law, exports of goods to a non-EU destination are taxable at 0% (Article 262-I-1 CGI) where the exporter proves that the goods actually left the EU within the legal deadline and are held by the buyer outside the EU. Intra-Community dispatches to an EU taxable person are zero-rated under Article 262-I-2 CGI if the buyer communicates a valid EU VAT ID, the buyer’s supplier files an EC sales list, and the goods are transported to the other Member State. For ’offshore’ services (services subcontracted for the needs of export sales, transport, handling and brokerage related to export goods), Article 262-I-3 CGI extends zero-rating to certain ancillary services. For this SARL’s core ISIC 6499 activity — revenue-based financing of digital brands — the service is a financial credit operation that is exempt from VAT (with credit deduction) rather than zero-rated; zero-rating does not apply to pure financing. The only path to zero-rating for this entity would be if the financing service is demonstrably tied to, and invoiced as part of, an export-merchandising service package (fees for arranging the export of goods). Claiming 0% on a financial service not eligible for zero-rating can constitute an abusive VAT practice under Article 266 CGI and the ’Ashworth’ type case law (CJEU).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d’Exportation via the DELTA/T import-export software, accepted by Customs and printed as the EX1 or EX7 control document)
  • Bill of Lading (for sea freight) or Air Waybill (for air freight) and the corresponding Transport Contract with pickup language confirming delivery to the final non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank statement or SWIFT credit confirmation proving that the export proceeds are received in France or on the exporter’s account, in conformity with the currency restrictions of Article L. 161-2 of the French Monetary and Financial Code
  • Proof of commercial character — supplier’s order, commercial invoice, packing list, and insurance certificate aligning consignee, value, and INCOTERM

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing in France is being deployed through the national e-invoicing reform (Loi de Finances 2020, implemented by the DGFiP, with a three-phase timetable: 1 September 2026 for large taxpayers, 1 September 2027 for SMEs, 1 September 2027 for all domestic B2B transactions; foreign suppliers operate via the central platform) — mandatory E-reporting to DGFiP for all cross-border B2C and B2B inter-Community transactions, with digital transmission via an accredited Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public free portal Chorus Pro. There is no ’real-time clearance’ in the Latin American sense; the French mandate requires near-real-time E-reporting (within 30 minutes for B2B invoices once the reform is operational) and the issuance of invoices in a structured e-invoicing format (Factur-X/UBL/CII) with a mandatory electronic signature for fiscal integrity, but no physical digital stamp. For export VAT refund claims, the DGFiP may require digital certification of the exporter’s shipping and customs declarations via the DELTA-T/SIGL (Système d’Information de la Douane).

The DGFiP Audit National des Opérations Internationales (AONI) and the French Customs Anti-Fraud Office (DNRED) target export zero-rating refunds, applying the following penalties: (i) a 40% ’manquement délibéré’ (deliberate negligence) penalty plus monthly 0.4% late-payment interest (Article 1727 CGI) where the taxpayer cannot substantiate the export; (ii) a 80% ’mauvaise foi’ or ’abus de droit’ penalty, increased to 80% + 40% in cases of fraud (escroquerie à la TVA) with potential criminal prosecution for tax fraud (Article 1741 CGI) carrying up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 250,000 fine; (iii) retrospective VAT clawback of the incorrectly zero-rated VAT and reversal of all net VAT credits for the period; (iv) an asset freeze and seizure, plus personal joint liability of the SARL manager under Article 266 bis CGI for deliberate non-payment; (v) the obligation to produce a bank guarantee for further refunds under Article 287 CGI where a fraud risk is detected. In addition, France strictly enforces the FX repatriation rule under Articles L. 211-36 and L. 231-1 of the Monetary and Financial Code: export proceeds must be repatriated and converted by the due date of the contract (subject to the default 180 days in cross-border practice, but the applicable statutory deadline is the maturity date of the invoice; any extension beyond statutory limits requires the exporter to produce an invoice maturity report). Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the legal deadline attracts a fine of up to 5% of the non-repatriated amount under Article 459 of the Customs Code.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible and free transferability of capital is guaranteed under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and Articles L151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier, CMF). There are no residual exchange controls on current or capital account. Profit and dividend remittances are legally unrestricted, but each payment flow must satisfy (i) corporate documentation and any applicable French withholding tax reporting (Form 2777 for sums paid to non-residents), (ii) bank due-diligence and source-of-funds verification, and (iii) EU and French sanctions, embargo, and asset-freeze compliance. Statistical reporting to the Banque de France and customs disclosure to the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) apply only at identified thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender regime or mandatory conversion ratio applies in France. A French exporter may retain foreign-currency receipts or convert them freely in the interbank market; however, all export proceeds must be routed through a financial institution established in the European Economic Area for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, and no remittance may materially benefit a sanctioned person or entity under EU Regulation 269/2014 and CMF Articles L151-1 to L165-1.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as national central bank of the French Republic under the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); banking and financial supervision for prudential and anti-money-laundering purposes is exercised concurrently by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); foreign-exchange policy, balance-of-payments statistics, and financial sanctions administration sit with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) within the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Medium - legal feasibility is confirmed. PSD2-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine (Groupe Société Générale), and comparable EU EMIs holding licences from the ACPR, BaFin, or the FCA with EU passporting rights may open EUR IBAN escrow and collection accounts and support SEPA Instant and SWIFT credit transfers. They are, however, not a complete cross-border treasury clearing rail: they generally do not offer commercial letters of credit, FX forwards or NDFs, nostro multi-currency settlement, or sophisticated trade-payment interfaces, and enhanced due-diligence review of a non-dilutive royalty-financing fund model may trigger sub-limits, lower velocity tolerances, and longer onboarding timelines.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections, being share capital increases, shareholder loans, or tranches of the non-dilutive financing fund, must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or TARGET2 euro-denominated credit transfer, quoting the relevant ISO 20022 purpose code (LOAN for shareholder financing, INVS for investment capital) and attaching or referencing the executed financing instrument. Any aggregate foreign-currency borrowing contracted by the French resident SARL exceeding EUR 50,000 must be declared to the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) on Form 2561 within 20 calendar days of contract execution or drawing, and material direct investment inflows from non-EU controlling shareholders are subject to French Treasury statistical-reporting instructions. Funds are received into a dedicated euro or foreign-currency account, held for one to three business days for verification and negative-news screening, and released for deployment only after full sanctions, fraud, and beneficial-ownership checks have been validated.Outbound disbursements from the Royalty Financing Fund are executed over three complementary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 international wire transfers for royalty advances, profit-share returns, and vendor payments, quoting the ISO 20022 purpose code and the underlying licence or invoice reference; (ii) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 for trade-backed vendor settlements or brand-inventory financing disbursements requiring documentary compliance; and (iii) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for euro-denominated disbursements to EEA beneficiaries below the EUR 100,000 processing cap. Every outward payment must satisfy the EU Regulation 2015/847 data rule requiring full originator and beneficiary identification, pass the bank's sanctions and fraud filters, and obtain same-day FX pricing only after the funds are released from the escrow ledger.Outright deliverable EUR/USD and EUR/GBP FX forwards with maturity up to 12 months, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with an ACPR-authorised credit institutionNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in EUR/emerging-market currency pairs where the counterparty currency is non-deliverable in France, subject to internal credit approval and CMF foreign-exchange reporting where materialCross-currency interest rate swaps hedging the SARL's non-euro financing exposure, documented under ISDA 2002 with a French FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) credit support annexEuropean-style vanilla FX options for royalty cash-flow smoothing, with premium accounting and hedge documentation prepared in accordance with IFRS 9 hedge-accounting requirements

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium - legal feasibility is confirmed. PSD2-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine (Groupe Société Générale), and comparable EU EMIs holding licences from the ACPR, BaFin, or the FCA with EU passporting rights may open EUR IBAN escrow and collection accounts and support SEPA Instant and SWIFT credit transfers. They are, however, not a complete cross-border treasury clearing rail: they generally do not offer commercial letters of credit, FX forwards or NDFs, nostro multi-currency settlement, or sophisticated trade-payment interfaces, and enhanced due-diligence review of a non-dilutive royalty-financing fund model may trigger sub-limits, lower velocity tolerances, and longer onboarding timelines.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400 million (turnover or assets) for full Local File and Master File; smaller entities must maintain basic documentation (transfer pricing policy, functional analysis) if transactions exceed €1 million with related parties

Economic substance scope: France applies anti-abuse rules (e.g., substance-over-form) and requires economic substance for tax residency and treaty benefits. No specific ESR regime, but entities must demonstrate real activity, staff, and decision-making in France to avoid challenges under the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: up to €500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment (Article 1741 CGI); civil penalties: 40% to 80% of tax due plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they participated in fraudulent acts; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, 40% penalty for abuse of law (abus de droit), and potential criminal charges for fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties and taxes if they knowingly used the entity as a sham; possible ban from managing companies
AML / Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for entities; criminal penalties for directors up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine (Monetary and Financial Code)

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned; professional disqualification and travel bans may apply
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty for serious mispricing, and interest; potential double taxation

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; criminal liability for tax evasion if intentional
