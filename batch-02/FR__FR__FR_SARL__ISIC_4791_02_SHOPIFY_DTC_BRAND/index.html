Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies; capital may be set at EUR 1 or more. For cash contributions, at least 20% must be paid upon incorporation and the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Contributions are deposited into a blocked account where applicable, and the deposit certificate is used for registration. In-kind contributions must be transferred and described in the articles; an independent contributions auditor is generally required unless all statutory exemption thresholds are satisfied.

Funding & unlock path: No general civil-defense or occupational clearance is required for a remote online retail business. Any warehouse or public-access premises must comply with building, fire-safety, emergency-exit, electrical, accessibility, and occupational-health requirements. Premises open to the public may be subject to ERP fire-safety approval and periodic inspections; employers must complete the occupational-risk assessment, maintain the required DUERP, provide safe equipment and training, and arrange applicable inspections.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), operating under the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Recovery; taxpayer access via the professional portal impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel) with SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) local management; business formalities through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the SARL through the Guichet Unique single-window portal, the DGFIP automatically registers the entity and issues the 9-digit SIREN (national corporate identifier), the 14-digit SIRET establishment identifier, and the French VAT intra-Community number (FR + two-digit key + 9-digit SIREN) once VAT liability attaches. A new business commencing taxable activity without prior incorporation formalities must file the 'déclaration d'existence' (form M0) with the competent tax office within 15 days of commencement. For cross-border EU e-commerce, the company must separately register through the French OSS portal (One-Stop-Shop) available on impots.gouv.fr to declare and pay VAT due in other EU member states, and must hold a French business bank account for the inward remittance traceability of export sales.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no US-style check-the-box election. A standard commercial SARL is by default within the scope of corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) under Article 206(1) of the CGI. Election mechanisms provided by the CGI include: (i) the pass-through election under Article 239 bis AA, allowing qualifying family-owned SARLs (all shareholders being natural persons within the same family and meeting statutory conditions) to be taxed as sociétés de personnes (shareholder-level income tax) instead of IS; and (ii) the reverse election under Article 206-3 CGI permitting partnerships to opt for IS. France implements the EU ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules (CGI Articles 205 A and following), which deny deduction benefits arising from hybrid entities or hybrid instruments; no unilateral entity-classification check-the-box override exists for French domestic-law entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT registration from the first taxable euro for a French-established SARL selling goods (no general exemption threshold for goods); the small-business 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption (CGI Article 293 B) applies while annual ex-VAT turnover from goods sales remains at or below EUR 91,900 (2024/2025 main threshold; tolerance EUR 101,000), subject to filing the annual turnover declaration and, upon breaching the tolerance, immediate registration. Cross-border EU distance sales to consumers remain French-taxable until the EUR 10,000 Union-wide distance-sales threshold is exceeded, beyond which OSS registration and destination-country VAT rates apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods sold by the French SARL to consumers or businesses outside the European Union qualify for 0% French VAT (exonération avec droit à déduction) under CGI Articles 262-I-1° and 258, provided legal title to the goods passes and the goods physically leave the EU within the statutory export period (normally within 90 days / 3 months of supply). Intra-Community supplies (B2B) to VAT-registered EU customers are 0% rated only where the customer holds a valid VIES-confirmed intra-Community VAT number and the supplier files periodic EC Sales Lists (état récupératif de clients). B2C DTC distance sales to consumers in other EU member states are zero-rated only when the OSS threshold (EUR 10,000) is exceeded via OSS registration; until then French VAT at the French rate applies. Sales to non-EU consumers via the Shopify storefront are treated as export distance sales and are eligible for zero-rating upon proper documentation. The input VAT attributable to exported goods (COGS, Shopify platform fees, logistics) is immediately creditable and refundable as a VAT credit pursuant to standard French refund procedures (monthly or quarterly SIE refund, or the annual 'remboursement de crédit de TVA').

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU – Déclaration Administrative Unique) submitted via DELTA-X with the Movement Reference Number (MRN) evidencing electronical release for export
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier proving physical transport of the goods out of the EU territory
  • Preuve de sortie du territoire de l'Union (validated exit proof, customs stamp or electronic exit confirmation from the customs systems)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract issued by the SARL in the customer's name
  • VIES validation record of the EU customer's intra-Community VAT number and the corresponding EC Sales List (for zero-rated intra-EU B2B supplies only)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 / proof of receipt of payment denominated in foreign currency

France has enacted a mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting reform (Ordonnance 2022-1087 and 2024-2025 implementing decrees). From 1 September 2026 (large enterprises) and 1 September 2027 (all other taxable persons including this SARL), B2B domestic invoices must be issued and transmitted electronically in a structured data format (Factur-X, UBL or CII) through a certified Dematerialisation Platform (PDP) or the State-operated Chorus Pro gateway. For B2C cross-border and domestic online sales, mandatory 'e-reporting' of transaction data to the DGFIP must occur periodically (in practice on a near-real-time continuous feed), including Shopify settlement data. France does not impose Italian-style real-time fiscal stamping; compliance is instead enforced through structured data flows, the FEC (Fichier des Écritures Comptables) digital audit file, NF 525 cash-register certification, and automated VAT gap/reconciliation analytics. Failure to comply with e-invoicing/e-reporting obligations engages a EUR 15,000 per offence fine (CGI Article 1737) and up to EUR 250,000 criminal penalties.

The DGFIP treats export zero-rating as a high-risk exposure area (fictitious exports, carousel fraud, and sham EU distance sales). The audit window for the administration is 3 years under LPF Articles L12-L16, extended to 10 years in cases of concealed or undeclared activity. Penalties for fraudulent export zero-rating include: (i) 100% major-omission penalty on the VAT amount concealed (CGI Article 1728); (ii) 80% penalty under the abuse-of-law procedure (LPF Article L64; 100% where the scheme involves non-cooperative jurisdictions or total opacity); (iii) retrospective clawback of all zero-rated input VAT credits with late-payment interest accruing at 0.80% per month, immediately enforceable via the State tax lien (hypothèque légale); (iv) criminal tax-fraud charges under CGI Article 1741 carrying up to EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment for the SARL's legal representative; and (v) personal joint-and-several civil liability of the manager (gérant) for penalties and restitution under CGI Article 1750 and general corporate law rules. The burden of proving physical export rests exclusively on the seller; contemporaneous customs and carrier records, FX repatriation within the 180-day rule, and complete e-reporting feeds are decisive mitigating evidence.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and operates within the European Union single market. The euro is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions, tax, customs, foreign direct investment screening, and bank due-diligence controls. A French SARL may receive foreign investment, hold foreign-currency accounts where accepted by its bank, pay foreign suppliers, and remit dividends or other distributions to non-resident shareholders without a general exchange-control approval. Distributions remain subject to corporate-law requirements, distributable-profit limitations, annual accounts approval, solvency considerations, withholding-tax analysis, beneficial-owner verification, treaty eligibility, and evidence of the underlying transaction. Transfers involving sanctioned jurisdictions, sanctioned persons, unusual payment chains, high-risk jurisdictions, or insufficient economic substance may be delayed, rejected, frozen, or reported. Interest, royalties, management fees, and related-party payments require transfer-pricing support, withholding-tax review, and documentation of services or intellectual-property rights.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or universal deadline requiring a French SARL to convert export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may generally remain in euros or another permitted currency, subject to the bank account agreement, accounting requirements, tax reporting, sanctions controls, and payment-service-provider policies. The company should maintain commercial invoices, customs records, transport evidence, customer contracts, payment reconciliations, and foreign-currency accounting records. Cash movements into or out of the European Union of EUR 10,000 or more, or its equivalent, are subject to applicable customs cash-declaration rules. Foreign bank accounts and payment accounts may trigger French tax-reporting obligations, including applicable account declarations and disclosures in the annual corporate tax filing; the precise filing treatment depends on account type, holder status, and statutory exceptions.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, within the Eurosystem governed by the European Central Bank; banking and payment-services supervision is principally conducted by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution and, where applicable, the European Central Bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection and operating payments, but not a universal substitute for a French bank. A regulated European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide EUR payment accounts, SEPA transfers, cards, foreign-currency collection, and merchant settlement, subject to its licence scope and safeguarding model. It may not provide ordinary bank deposits, deposit-guarantee protection, unrestricted credit, or statutory capital-deposit services unless the institution and product legally support those functions. Shopify Payments, PayPal, and Stripe onboarding remains separate from corporate-bank onboarding and requires consistent legal-name, tax, beneficial-owner, website, product, refund, and settlement information. Offshore digital EMIs without an applicable European passport, French authorisation, or lawful cross-border service basis present elevated account-closure, safeguarding, data, sanctions, and payment-finality risks. The company should maintain a regulated French or EEA primary bank account and use fintech accounts as controlled secondary rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder equity through a regulated bank account held in the subscriber's legal name into the French SARL capital-deposit account or operational account, using a clear payment reference such as share capital subscription or shareholder loan, as legally applicable. Before transfer, provide the bank with the subscription agreement, draft or executed articles, shareholder identity and beneficial-owner evidence, source-of-funds documents, corporate resolutions, and expected amount and currency. The bank should record the originator, beneficiary, purpose, intermediary banks, and ultimate source of funds. For a capital increase, obtain the required corporate approvals, deposit certificate, updated registration filing, and post-closing capital records. Do not route capital through personal accounts, unrelated third parties, cash, privacy-enhancing instruments, or opaque multi-hop structures. SEPA Credit Transfer is appropriate for EUR transfers within the SEPA area; SWIFT cross-border transfer is appropriate for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions. Use accurate ISO 20022 payment data and retain MT103 or equivalent payment confirmation where issued. Payment-purpose wording must be truthful and must not be used to evade sanctions, reporting, tax, or bank controls.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible EUR suppliers within SEPA, subject to bank limits and fraud controls. Use SWIFT customer credit transfer, historically evidenced by MT103 and increasingly processed through ISO 20022 pacs.008 messaging, for non-SEPA suppliers and foreign currencies. For higher-value or documentary transactions, use a commercial letter of credit subject to UCP 600, with electronic presentation only where the applicable rules and bank agree; use documentary collections under URC 522 where suitable. Match each payment to purchase orders, commercial invoices, customs and shipping documents, supplier master data, and proof of delivery. The bank may request the transaction purpose, HS classification, end-use, country of origin, Incoterms, sanctions screening, and beneficial-owner information. Separate supplier payments, tax payments, payroll, refunds, merchant settlements, and shareholder distributions in the treasury ledger. Conduct sanctions screening before release and maintain dual approval, beneficiary-change call-back, payment limits, and bank-reconciliation controls.Deliverable EUR/foreign-currency forward contracts with an authorised bank or regulated investment firm, supported by documented forecast exposures and subject to MiFID II appropriateness, suitability, product-governance, and client-classification requirements where applicable.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies or jurisdictions where physical delivery is restricted or impractical, subject to legal availability, counterparty policy, market documentation, sanctions screening, and appropriate ISDA or bank master-agreement terms.Currency swaps and cross-currency swaps for managing recurring funding or settlement exposures, subject to counterparty credit limits, valuation, collateral, and applicable European Market Infrastructure Regulation obligations.Foreign-exchange spot transactions for operational conversion of collected revenues and supplier-payment requirements.Currency options, including purchased vanilla options or approved option structures, only where the SARL understands premium, volatility, collateral, liquidity, and loss risks and the regulated counterparty has approved the product.Natural hedging through matching currency receipts and payments, currency-denominated supplier contracts, settlement-account segmentation, and documented exposure netting.Hedging must relate to genuine commercial or financing exposures, be approved under a written treasury policy, be recorded under applicable French GAAP or IFRS accounting rules, and not constitute unauthorised investment activity or speculative dealing.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to High for payment collection and operating payments, but not a universal substitute for a French bank. A regulated European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide EUR payment accounts, SEPA transfers, cards, foreign-currency collection, and merchant settlement, subject to its licence scope and safeguarding model. It may not provide ordinary bank deposits, deposit-guarantee protection, unrestricted credit, or statutory capital-deposit services unless the institution and product legally support those functions. Shopify Payments, PayPal, and Stripe onboarding remains separate from corporate-bank onboarding and requires consistent legal-name, tax, beneficial-owner, website, product, refund, and settlement information. Offshore digital EMIs without an applicable European passport, French authorisation, or lawful cross-border service basis present elevated account-closure, safeguarding, data, sanctions, and payment-finality risks. The company should maintain a regulated French or EEA primary bank account and use fintech accounts as controlled secondary rails.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard rate; 15% reduced rate on first €42,000 of taxable profit for companies with turnover < €10 million

30% standard rate; reduced to 15% (portfolio) or 5% (substantial shareholding ≥10%) under US-France treaty; other treaties vary

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full documentation required if French entity turnover ≥ €50 million or group turnover ≥ €400 million; simplified documentation for SMEs below thresholds

Economic substance scope: France applies General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) and substance requirements for treaty benefits; ESR not applicable (non-offshore jurisdiction), but substance is required to avoid abuse of law

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 40% of understated tax (80% for abuse of law); criminal fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for tax debts if found to have participated in fraud; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions; denial of treaty benefits; penalty of 80% of tax avoided; potential criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for unpaid taxes and penalties; possible criminal sanctions including imprisonment
AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 5% of turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for non-compliance; may face criminal charges and disqualification from management
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income; penalty of 5% of adjusted amount (up to 40% for serious non-compliance); interest on underpayment

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; potential criminal liability for fraud
