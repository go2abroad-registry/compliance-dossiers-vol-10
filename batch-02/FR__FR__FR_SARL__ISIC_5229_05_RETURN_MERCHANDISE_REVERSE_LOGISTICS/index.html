Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du tribunal de commerce having jurisdiction over the registered office; registration in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS) after transmission through the INPI Guichet unique portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Chapter III — société à responsabilité limitée regime under Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43, notably L.223-2 (freedom in fixing share capital), L.223-7 (mandatory one-fifth cash liberation at constitution), L.223-8 (in-kind contribution valuation), and L.223-18 (powers of the gérant); procedural digitalization under Décret n° 2021-1305 (Guichet unique).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts constitutifs (Articles of Association) together with the standardized Déclaration de constitution de société filed electronically on the Guichet unique (digitalized EPJ/M0 declaration, legacy CERFA n° 13891*03).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 2 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital: the SARL share capital is freely fixed in the articles and may be as low as €1 (Article L.223-2 C.com.). When a higher capital is adopted, at least one-fifth (1/5) of the cash contributions must be paid up at subscription and held in a blocked escrow account opened with a licensed French bank or notarial Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the balance must be released within five years (Article L.223-7 C.com.). A capital blocking certificate (attestation de blocage des fonds) is mandatory before registration, except where the capital is fully paid or set at the symbolic minimum.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — the French national tax administration headquartered in Paris; corporate income tax and VAT registration, filings and controls are administered through the professional portal 'Espace Professionnel' at www.impots.gouv.fr; company registration formalities are channelled through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; customs control of export and physical cross-border operations falls under the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) acting on the DELTA customs declaratory platform.

E-government filing portal

Foreign owners of a French SARL (no minimum capital and 100% foreign shareholding permitted) complete incorporation by filing articles of association, the appointed gérant's identity documents, registered-office lease, beneficial-ownership declaration (RBE) and the legal-announcement receipt through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). INSEE then assigns the SIREN/SIRET, which is used as the universal corporate tax identifier; DGFiP automatically allocates, or upon form 1547-F application delivers, the numéro individuel d'identification TVA in the format FR + 11 digits (e.g. FR-123-456-789-01), which must appear on every sales invoice. The SARL has no separate income-tax number: the SIREN links corporate income tax, payroll and VAT obligations in all DGFiP systems. Where the SARL is the buyer of cross-border services it self-assesses French VAT via the reverse charge mechanism; where a foreign supplier (not the SARL) carries on taxable operations in France without an EU establishment it must appoint a représentant fiscal for VAT administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box election, but the following statutory options are available for a French SARL: (i) the article 239 bis AA CGI election enabling small SARLs (no more than seven members, conditions met) to be taxed as pass-through partnerships instead of entity-level corporate tax; (ii) the article 223 A CGI election to enter a 95%-owned French fiscal-integration group (intégration fiscale); (iii) ministerial grants of 'bénéfice mondial' or 'bénéfice par pays' regimes on application; (iv) automatic application of ATAD 2 anti-hybrid rules under article 212 bis CGI, which cannot be 'elected' and operate to deny double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion outcomes created by hybrid financial instruments or hybrid entities; (v) formation of a French VAT group (assujetti unique à la TVA) for eligible related entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Eligibility for the small-enterprise VAT exemption (franchise en base de TVA, art. 293 B CGI) requires annual French-established turnover below EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for supplies of services; from 2025 the exemption thresholds are adjusted to EUR 93,500 / EUR 41,000 under the annual decrees. Reverse-logistics service revenue (ISIC 5229; NAICS 488510) counts against the services threshold. Below the threshold the SARL may register optionally; once exceeded, mandatory VAT registration takes effect from the first euro of the following month. Non-established foreign suppliers have no franchise right and must register from the first euro; B2B reverse-charge chains (subcontracting, construction, immovable services) are likewise excluded from the franchise and require the French recipient to self-account VAT. For cross-border B2B logistics services to non-French recipients, French VAT registration is retained for input VAT recovery and e-reporting compliance even where the supply is located outside France under art. 259-1 CGI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France outside the EU are zero-rated for VAT (art. 262 I 6° CGI) upon production of a customs export declaration (DAE) recorded in DELTA showing actual EU exit, a transport document (CMR/B/L/AWB), transfer of title to the foreign buyer under the agreed Incoterms, and a customs-issued justificatif d'exportation retained before the VAT return deadline. Ancillary logistics services performed in direct connection with export of returned merchandise (handling, quality grading, repackaging, temporary storage pending re-export) qualify for the export-service exemption of art. 262 I 3° CGI when supplied to the exporter/principal bearing the cost, and only if the underlying goods physically leave the Union. Deliveries of goods to EU-based recipients are exempt under art. 262 ter(1) CGI conditional on a VIES-valid customer VAT number and inclusion in the e-Sales List. For the SARL's core B2B reverse-logistics services rendered to a foreign retailer/principal (ISIC 5229 / NAICS 488510), the place-of-supply rule of art. 259-1 CGI locates the supply where the foreign customer is established; no French VAT is chargeable by default, the foreign customer self-accounts under reverse charge, and the SARL retains the right to deduct input VAT attributable to that business. Invoices must state the legal basis (art. 259-1 or art. 262-6° CGI) and the customer's valid VAT ID.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE / Document Administratif Unique) accepted in the DELTA customs declaratory system and confirmed by the Bureau de Douane
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or International Consignment Note (CMR) evidencing physical EU exit of the returned goods and the date of departure
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — French bank attestation (avis d'encaissement) confirming receipt of export proceeds in the SARL's account and foreign-exchange repatriation consistent with Banque de France balance-of-payment reporting
  • Justificatif d'exportation delivered by the customs office (original or certified copy) evidencing departure from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial export contract / purchase order and commercial invoice with Incoterms 2020 data matching the customs line items of the DAE

Mandatory French e-invoicing (LF 2023, art. 289 and 289-0 CGI): as of 1 September 2026 every business must be able to receive structured e-invoices; as of 1 September 2027 every business, including this SARL, must issue domestic B2B e-invoices in Factur-X, UBL or CII formats via an accredited Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the free Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) and transmit invoice metadata to DGFiP. In parallel, cross-border B2B and B2C transactions are subject to periodic e-reporting (transmission des données de transaction et de paiement) through the PPF/PDP. The French model does not impose per-invoice fiscal clearance, digital stamping or prior online validation; it requires: (1) interoperability of PDPs via standard APIs (YAML/JSON specifications), (2) a 6-year audit trail of all electronic workflows, and (3) mandatory certification of billing software under the obligation de mémoire fiscale. The SARL must configure its ERP/API to emit mandatory invoice fields and cross-border e-reporting payloads no later than the staggered September 2026/2027 deadlines.

Under BOI-TVA-CHAMP-40-20 the DGFiP treats export substantiation as a formal verification file: where the justificatif d'exportation is not produced within the inspector's fixed period or the 90-day response window in the audit procedure, the zero-rated amounts are recharacterised as taxable French deliveries subject to TVA at 20%. The reassessment carries intérêts de retard at 0.20% per month plus a graduated penalty of 10% (negligence), 40% (manquement délibéré) or 80% (fraude) under article 1729 CGI. Fraudulent schemes (fake export declarations, fictitious reverse-logistics lines, circular goods movements) expose the SARL and its directors to criminal liability under article 1741 CGI (5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 75,000 fine; aggravated organised fraud: 7 years and EUR 100,000) and to customs penalties of up to three times the evaded duties under the code des douanes. Clawback of previously zero-rated VAT is unlimited in amount; where the tax authorities establish an undisclosed or fictitious export activity, the limitation period is extended to ten years (art. L. 169 LPF) and the DGFiP may enforce joint and several liability of directors for the tax and penalties. Failure to preserve the FX-repatriation / export-payment file is additionally sanctionable under foreign-exchange reporting obligations to Banque de France.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; France maintains an open capital account consistent with Articles 63-66 TFEU. There is no doctrine of capital versus current account convertibility approval because the euro is a fully convertible currency. Profit remittance is not subject to exchange-control authorisation; a French SARL may distribute profits to non-resident shareholders subject only to corporate-law solvency tests, tax withholding under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) or double-tax treaties, and CRS/FATCA reporting. The sole administrative hurdle is a statistical declaration to Banque de France for any inbound or outbound transfer of EUR 10,000 or more to or from outside France. ACPR-supervised banks perform the reporting electronically; no licence or prior approval is required for reverse-logistics trade flows or for a capital injection into a non-sensitive sector. Sectoral pre-approval of foreign investment applies only to sensitive activities listed in Article L.151-3 of the Code monétaire et financier (defence, cybersecurity, dual-use, etc.), which does not cover ISIC 5229 reverse logistics.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France abolished export-proceeds surrender and mandatory conversion requirements in the 1989 liberalisation of foreign exchange. An exporter, including a reverse-logistics operator handling returned goods, is free to hold export proceeds in foreign-currency accounts at an ACPR-licensed bank or through an authorised payment institution, and there is no statutory deadline to repatriate or convert receipts. The only surviving obligations are: (i) the domestic French currency legal-tender rule for EUR-denominated debts; (ii) the threshold-based statistical declaration of cross-border transfers equal to or exceeding EUR 10,000; and (iii) customs declarations for physically exported returned goods (INTRASTAT and the Single Administrative Document under the Union Customs Code).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (the French central bank), acting with the Direction Générale du Trésor of the Ministère de l'Économie for exchange-control policy, and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking licensing and AML/CFT supervision. France applies the Banque de France external-statistics reporting system to all fund transfers between France and foreign countries.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility as a settlement-only companion but legally capped. A licensed EU/UK electronic money institution (EMI) such as Airwallex can hold and transfer e-money, issue virtual IBANs in the company's name, collect marketplace proceeds, and disburse refunds via SEPA Instant or SWIFT; this matches the target payment rails of local ACH plus Airwallex. Legal constraints under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and the French Code monétaire et financier are: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot grant overdrafts or pay interest on client balances; (ii) safeguarding accounts held with a partner credit institution are outside the EUR 100,000 FGDR deposit guarantee; (iii) the EMI must be authorised in an EEA state and either passport into France or operate through an ACPR-registered agent; and (iv) all virtual IBANs must carry the exact legal name of the SARL to avoid AML layering flags. The compliant structure is to keep tax, social-security (URSSAF) and dividend treasury at a tier-1 French bank and to use the EMI only as a collection and disbursement rail with daily zero-balance sweeps.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections and subsequent shareholder loans must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (non-EUR) or SEPA CT with the ISO 20022 purpose code CAPM (capital payments) and remittance reference in the form apport en numéraire - shareholder name - share capital increase. For formation of a new SARL, the cash must first be credited to a blocked account at a bank or at the Caisse des Dépôts, then released by the corporate registrar after the RCS issues the Kbis. The French bank credits the operating account only after checking the corporate resolution, the certificate of deposit of funds (attestation de dépôt des fonds), and the identity of the remitter. Any transfer into France from outside France of EUR 10,000 or more is simultaneously declared to Banque de France by the receiving bank under the balance-of-payments statistical regulation (Code monétaire et financier Articles R.152-1 et seq.); the bank must be given the exact legal name, address and country of the ultimate originator to avoid rejection. A missing purpose code, a generic transfer code, or an unverified UBO structure is the most frequent cause of quarantine by French correspondent banks.For French suppliers and SEPA refunds to e-commerce customers, use SEPA Credit Transfer (CT) with RMA remittance information, or SEPA Instant for sub-second refunds; buyer refunds are settled in EUR to avoid FX conversion. EUR clearing settles over TARGET2 with SEPA Instant finality through TIPS and pan-EU batch clearing via EBA CLEARING STEP2. For non-EEA trade settlements, including payments to foreign carriers, warehouses and customs brokers, use SWIFT MT103 for direct transfers, MT202COV for cover payments, or a commercial Letter of Credit under UCP 600 for large volumes subject to Incoterms 2020. The physical-return outbound flow (re-export) requires EUR or USD settlement through the tier-1 bank trade desk with the Banque de France statistical motive coded as exportation de marchandises or logistics service; physical exports require DGDDI customs declarations. When disbursing via the EMI, each local payout must originate from the safeguarding account with a bank-grade remittance reference (for example TRAD or GDDS) and be fully mirrored on the tier-1 bank statement to avoid being treated as unvouched turnover.Spot FX (change au comptant) for immediate conversion of USD, GBP and CNH e-commerce receipts, typically with T+2 settlement.Outright FX forwards (change à terme) for hedging the refund liability and supplier-payables portfolio, typically up to 12 months, subject to a bank credit line and documented underlying commercial exposure.FX swaps (swap de change) as a liquidity bridge to convert short-dated USD or CNH cash into EUR while avoiding mark-to-market credit risk.Currency options (options de change) permitted only after a MiFID II suitability and expertise assessment by the French bank, with documented hedging rather than speculative intent.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for limited-dealability Asian currencies (e.g., CNY and KRW) where physical delivery of the onshore currency cannot be performed, allowed only through an ACPR or EU-licensed counterparty and subject to EMIR transaction reporting and clearing thresholds.Cross-currency swaps (CCIRS) for longer-term EUR/USD or EUR/CNH balance-sheet hedges, documented under an ISDA Master Agreement or the French FBF Convention-cadre with close-out netting.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility as a settlement-only companion but legally capped. A licensed EU/UK electronic money institution (EMI) such as Airwallex can hold and transfer e-money, issue virtual IBANs in the company's name, collect marketplace proceeds, and disburse refunds via SEPA Instant or SWIFT; this matches the target payment rails of local ACH plus Airwallex. Legal constraints under the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC) and the French Code monétaire et financier are: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot grant overdrafts or pay interest on client balances; (ii) safeguarding accounts held with a partner credit institution are outside the EUR 100,000 FGDR deposit guarantee; (iii) the EMI must be authorised in an EEA state and either passport into France or operate through an ACPR-registered agent; and (iv) all virtual IBANs must carry the exact legal name of the SARL to avoid AML layering flags. The compliant structure is to keep tax, social-security (URSSAF) and dividend treasury at a tier-1 French bank and to use the EMI only as a collection and disbursement rail with daily zero-balance sweeps.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File required if the French entity has annual turnover or gross assets exceeding €400 million, or if the group's consolidated turnover exceeds €50 million (with the French entity being part of the group). Simplified documentation (light file) required for entities with turnover or assets between €400 million and €50 million? Actually, the threshold is: full documentation (Master File and Local File) if the group's consolidated turnover exceeds €50 million and the French entity's turnover or assets exceed €400 million. For entities below these thresholds, a simplified TP documentation (form 2257) is required if the entity's turnover exceeds €152,000 and it has related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE. However, the concept of economic substance is embedded in the anti-abuse rules (e.g., the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) under Article L.64 of the French Tax Procedure Code) and the OECD BEPS actions. A French SARL must have real substance (employees, premises, decision-making) to avoid being challenged as a shell company. The tax administration may disregard transactions lacking economic substance and recharacterize them.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty: up to €500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment (Article 1741 CGI). Civil penalties: 40% to 80% of the evaded tax, plus interest at 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the company's tax debts if they have committed fraud or mismanagement. They may face criminal prosecution, travel bans, and seizure of personal assets. The corporate veil can be pierced under the doctrine of 'abus de biens sociaux' (misuse of corporate assets).
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may disregard the entity for tax purposes, reallocate income to the actual beneficial owner, and impose a 100% penalty for abusive arrangements (Article L.64 A LPF). Additionally, the company may be subject to dissolution under commercial law.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly liable for the company's tax debts if they have created or used a shell structure to evade taxes. They may face criminal charges for tax fraud and money laundering, leading to imprisonment and confiscation of assets.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 5% of turnover for legal entities (Article L.574-5 CMF). Criminal penalties for individuals: up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine. The company may also be subject to a compliance monitor.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly fail to report suspicious transactions. They may also be banned from managing a company for up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty for lack of documentation (Article 1735 ter CGI), and interest. In cases of deliberate mispricing, the penalty can be 80%.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TP documentation may be held personally liable for the additional tax and penalties. They may also face criminal charges for tax fraud if the mispricing is deemed fraudulent.
