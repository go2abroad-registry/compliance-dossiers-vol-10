Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – French General Directorate of Public Finance; official online portal for corporate tax administration: impots.gouv.fr; VAT registration and filings are also handled via the DGFiP portal with mandatory EDI or online filing for all registered businesses.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity seeking to establish a French SAS must first complete company incorporation via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises, which transmits data to the French tax authorities. Upon registration, the company is assigned a SIREN number (national company identifier) and a SIRET number (establishment identifier). Subsequently, the DGFiP issues a French VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) formatted as FR + 2 checksum digits + SIREN, typically within 2-4 weeks after incorporation, upon submission of the standard registration form 'P0i' or its digital equivalent. For permanent establishments or non-resident taxable persons without a French corporate form, a specific 'VAT number without SIREN' may be requested directly from the DGFiP Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères, accompanied by a certificate of fiscal domicile, a signed tax representation mandate, and proof of economic activity in France.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity seeking to establish a French SAS must first complete company incorporation via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises, which transmits data to the French tax authorities. Upon registration, the company is assigned a SIREN number (national company identifier) and a SIRET number (establishment identifier). Subsequently, the DGFiP issues a French VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) formatted as FR + 2 checksum digits + SIREN, typically within 2-4 weeks after incorporation, upon submission of the standard registration form 'P0i' or its digital equivalent. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the entity level. Options available include: (i) election for the integrated tax regime ('régime d'intégration fiscale') under Article 223 A of the French General Tax Code (CGI), allowing a French parent to consolidate the taxable results of wholly-owned French subsidiaries; (ii) option for the 'régime mère-fille' (parent-subsidiary regime), which mitigates double taxation on dividend distributions through a 95% exemption subject to a 5% recapture; (iii) election under the French tax consolidation regime requires prior approval by the DGFiP and must be made before the end of the fiscal year preceding the consolidation. As a rule, no pass-through or 'check-the-box' election exists for a French SAS under domestic law; all elections are administrative options. Hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD 1 and 2) transposed into the CGI (Articles 205 and 209) may apply, but no unilateral election can override them.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,800 for supplies of goods; EUR 36,400 for supplies of services (domestic taxable persons). For non-established taxable persons: no threshold – mandatory registration or reverse-charge mechanism applies from the first euro. For distance sales of goods imported from other EU countries: EUR 10,000 annual threshold; from 1 July 2021 the threshold is monitored at the EU level through the OSS portal. A French SAS is automatically registered upon incorporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 262-I of the French General Tax Code (CGI), exports of goods out of the European Union are eligible for 0% VAT. To qualify for zero-rating, the French SAS must prove that the goods were physically shipped from France to a destination outside the EU, and that physical transfer is documented. Exports to French overseas territories (Guadeloupe, Martinique, Réunion, Mayotte, French Guiana, Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon, and Wallis-et-Futuna) are also treated as exports, subject to the same documentation. For services related to exported goods, including laboratory testing, quality control, and technical analysis of liposomal adjuvants, zero-rating is available under Article 259-A and Article 262-II of the CGI, provided the services are directly linked to the exported goods and performed prior to or at the time of export. Intra-community supplies of goods to taxable persons in other EU member states are exempt under Article 262-I-1° with a valid EU VAT number of the customer and a recapitulative declaration (DEB). Export zero-rating is a privilege, and the burden of proof rests entirely on the exporter.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/EDI), as validated by French customs (Déclaration en Douane – ECS with EORI number), including the exact Harmonized System codes and statistical value
  • Bill of Lading (copy of the maritime or airway bill showing the French port/airport of departure and foreign destination, with consignee name and address)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (for the payment from the foreign buyer, evidencing receipt of the consideration in euros or convertible currency)
  • Commercial Invoice in French or English with full description of goods, terms of delivery (Incoterms 2020), and the explicit statement 'Exportation – TVA non applicable sur justification'
  • Proof of Physical Exit: a signed certificate from the customs broker or forwarding agent (e.g., CMR consignment note, T1 document, or ENS/ICS validation) showing actual exit through a French or EU customs office
  • Copy of the EORI number registration certificate
  • Packing list and weight/volume certificate for the export lot
  • Substantiation of direct link for services: a signed contract or service order tying the liposomal vaccine adjuvant manufacturing services to the exported goods, plus evidence of shipment, delivery, and payment

France has transposed Directive 2014/55/EU and is implementing a phased mandatory e-invoicing reform from 1 September 2026. From that date, all businesses must be able to receive e-invoices in a structured electronic format (Factur-X, UBL, CII) and transmit e-invoices through a registered 'Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire' (PDP) or the public free portal 'Portail Public de Facturation' (PPF). Real-time fiscal clearance is currently applicable to B2B and B2C transactions through the mandatory data transmission of inward supplies under Article 289-II of the CGI and the annually updated 'taxe sur les salaires' control. Payable, no digital stamping or real-time clearance framework like that of Italy's 'Sdi' applies in France; instead, periodic VAT returns (CA3/CA12) must file detailed turnover codes and intra-community transaction declarations (Des CA3). Mandatory e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions becomes fully obligatory on 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises. However, all customers are obliged to receive e-invoices as of 1 September 2026. Additional electronic reporting (e-reporting) is mandatory for B2C transaction data effective the same dates. Failure to issue e-invoices will result in a EUR 15,000 per invoice penalty, capped at EUR 60,000, under Article 1737 CGI.

The French tax administration, through the Direction Nationale d'Enquêtes Fiscales (DNEF) and the customs authority, conducts regular export substantiation audits. Claiming zero-rating on exports without valid proof triggers: (i) immediate VAT repayment demand including interest at the legal rate; (ii) a penalty of 40% for lack of good faith or 80% for deliberate tax evasion (mauvaise foi ou manœuvres frauduleuses) under Article 1729 CGI minus the statutory abatement; (iii) tax shelter prosecution under Article 1741 of the CGI, punishable with up to five years imprisonment and a fine of EUR 500,000. In addition, the loss of the right to issue zero-rated invoices for a period of 12 calendar months may be imposed, and a false customs declaration may result in penalties equal to one to three times the total VAT amount at stake under the Customs Code (Article 414). VAT credits claimed from zero-rating are scrutinised via the pre-refund inspection (contrôle de la demande de remboursement), where the DGFiP may require a bank certificate confirming receipt of foreign currency, the export customs entry, and the customer's complete identification. Retroactive clawback is possible for up to six years preceding the detecting year, limited to a statutory limitation period of six to ten years in cases of fraud or undisclosed activity.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions subject to EU and French law. There is no general French exchange-control approval for foreign investment, intercompany funding, service payments, dividends, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds. Payments remain subject to bank AML, sanctions, beneficial-ownership, tax, transfer-pricing, foreign-subsidy, and financial-accounting controls. A non-resident shareholder may generally subscribe for SAS shares, provide shareholder loans, receive dividends, and repatriate proceeds through a French or EU-regulated bank. Profit remittances require distributable profits, approved financial statements, corporate approvals, applicable withholding-tax analysis, treaty documentation, and evidence that the transaction is not abusive or a disguised distribution. Payments involving sanctioned countries, restricted counterparties, dual-use goods, controlled biological materials, pharmaceutical products, or regulated research activities may require enhanced screening, licences, or may be prohibited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to Banque de France, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring conversion of foreign-currency export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the company's discretion, subject to contractual banking terms, accurate customs and VAT records, accounting recognition, sanctions screening, and tax compliance. The SAS should maintain documentary reconciliation between export declarations, commercial invoices, transport evidence, bank receipts, customer ledgers, and VAT reporting. Foreign-currency balances must be remeasured under applicable French accounting rules, with exchange gains and losses recorded appropriately. Sector-specific export controls, customs obligations, controlled-product licences, and payment-reporting requirements may apply independently of exchange-control rules.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the oversight of the European Central Bank. Foreign-exchange law is principally governed by European Union law, French monetary and financial law, and applicable sanctions and anti-money-laundering rules administered or enforced by the French authorities, including the Direction Générale du Trésor, the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution, TRACFIN, and the customs authorities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational payments and foreign-currency collection; Low as the sole banking solution for a high-risk pharmaceutical manufacturing SAS. An EU or EEA electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide accounts, payment services, SEPA access, safeguarding, and selected foreign-exchange services under its authorisation and passporting rights. It may not necessarily provide a French statutory capital-deposit account, full deposit-taking, credit facilities, commercial letters of credit, specialised trade-finance products, or unrestricted cash-management functionality. Client funds may be safeguarded rather than covered by the French deposit-guarantee scheme. The company should retain a regulated French or EU credit institution as primary bank and use an EMI only after confirming safeguarding, payment-finality, country coverage, sanctions controls, account-closure procedures, data access, and compatibility with the company's pharmaceutical and export profile.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity funding through a regulated French or EU credit institution to the SAS's designated capital-deposit account before incorporation or capital increase, or to the operational account after registration and release of funds. Use a clear payment narrative identifying the SAS, subscriber or lender, legal basis, currency, amount, and purpose, such as share capital subscription, share premium, or shareholder loan. Provide the bank with the subscription or loan agreement, corporate resolutions, updated capitalization table, beneficial-owner evidence, source-of-funds evidence, and remitter-to-beneficiary relationship. For non-euro transfers, use SWIFT payment instructions with complete originator and beneficiary information and convert through the bank under its spot or agreed FX terms. Reconcile the received funds to the notarial or bank certificate, accounting ledger, capital increase filings, and tax records. Do not split transfers to avoid controls, use unrelated third-party remitters, or route funds through unregulated payment channels. No general French FX purpose-code approval is required, but the bank's internal payment-purpose, AML, sanctions, and regulatory-reporting fields must be completed accurately.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT cross-border credit transfer, commonly using the MT103 message or its ISO 20022 successor equivalent, for non-SEPA or correspondent-bank payments. Include complete originator, beneficiary, ultimate-beneficiary where required, invoice number, contract reference, goods description, country information, and bank-account details. For higher-risk or document-intensive transactions, use a commercial documentary letter of credit issued or confirmed by a regulated bank, preferably subject to UCP 600, with documentary requirements aligned to the commercial contract and export-control position. Alternative rails include documentary collections under URC 522 and bank guarantees or standby letters of credit where commercially appropriate. The bank should receive purchase orders, invoices, packing lists, transport documents, customs declarations, certificates of origin, insurance documents, licences, and end-use evidence where relevant. Maintain three-way reconciliation among contract, logistics documents, and payment. Screen banks, customers, suppliers, vessels, goods, jurisdictions, and beneficial owners before release. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million (group turnover) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File; below this threshold, a simplified TP documentation (Form 2257) is required if cross-border transactions exceed EUR 50,000.

Economic substance scope: France applies general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles. While no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist, entities must demonstrate real economic activity (personnel, premises, decision-making) to benefit from treaty provisions and avoid being deemed a shell company. The French tax administration may challenge artificial arrangements under Article L.64 of the Tax Procedure Code.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 (or 50% of evaded tax) and imprisonment up to 5 years (Article 1741 of the French General Tax Code). Civil penalties: 80% of underpaid tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they participated in fraudulent acts; travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment up to 5 years. Piercing the corporate veil is possible under abuse of law doctrine.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 40% penalty on undisclosed amounts (Article 1729 of the CGI). Potential criminal charges for fraud if used for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly facilitated the shell structure; travel bans and prohibition from managing companies in France.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of turnover (Monetary and Financial Code); criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine for money laundering (Article 324-1 of the Criminal Code).

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable for AML failures; personal fines, imprisonment, and disqualification from managing companies. Civil liability for damages to third parties.
