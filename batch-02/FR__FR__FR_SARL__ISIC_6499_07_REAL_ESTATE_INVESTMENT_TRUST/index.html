Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), operating under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; official portal: https://www.impots.gouv.fr; business formation and SIREN/SIRET allocation portal: https://www.guichet-entreprises.fr; EU VAT number validation (VIES): https://ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies; non-resident corporate VAT registration unit: Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), 10 rue du Centre, 93160 Noisy-le-Grand.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine French permanent establishment (PE) exposure. Step 2: If a PE exists, register the foreign entity at the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via Guichet des Formalités Entreprises to obtain the 9-digit SIREN from INSEE and the establishment-level SIRET; then submit a VAT registration application (P0i) to DGFiP to obtain the French intra-Community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + key + SIREN) and validate it on the VIES system. Step 3: If no PE exists, apply directly to SIEE for stand-alone French VAT registration using the P0i teleprocedure; applicants must file the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, identity documents of directors, description of taxable French activity, projected turnover, and, for entities established outside the European Union, a VAT fiscal representative approved by DGFiP under Article 289-A of the Code Général des Impôts who is jointly and severally liable for the principal's VAT. Step 4: For a real estate investment trust deriving French-source letting income, obtain a SIREN/SIRET even without a PE because French corporate income tax filing, land registration (publicité foncière) and the annual tax on immovable property require a French tax reference identifier. Step 5: Normal DGFiP processing timeline is 3–15 working days; SIEE direct registrations for non-established foreign companies generally require 4–6 weeks including fiscal representative due diligence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a check-the-box system; the available elections are: (a) SARL income-tax transparency election (option IR) under Article 239 bis AA CGI for small and medium enterprises meeting the 50-employee / EUR 5 million revenue ceilings, converting entity-level corporate income tax (IS) into partner-level impôt sur le revenu (IR) taxation for a maximum of five periods; sole-member EURLs are transparent by default and may opt into IS. (b) SIIC (REIT) election under Article 208 C CGI: only a société par actions whose shares are admitted to trading on a regulated European stock exchange may elect entity-level exemption from French IS on rental income and capital gains, conditional on distribution of 90% of rental income, 60% of gains from disposals of development/development-assets, and 100% of gains on property disposals no later than the end of the following fiscal year; a SARL is not eligible for the SIIC regime and must convert to SA or SAS before filing the election. (c) VAT option on commercial letting under Article 260-C CGI: a landlord may irrevocably elect for VAT on the letting of bare or equipped commercial premises (élections de taxer) to recover input VAT; the option is valid for at least 9 years and applies to new leases and renewals. (d) Hybrid mismatch treatment: the French implementation of BEPS Action 2 under Articles 212-II and 209 CGI-B disallows deductions or creates inclusion mismatches for hybrid financing instruments and branch payments; the equivalent of a check-the-box outcome must be documented in the liasse fiscale and transfer-pricing file.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general zero-turnover registration threshold for non-established foreign suppliers in France; the triggering rules are: (1) Intra-EU distance sales of goods to French non-taxable persons: EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual turnover threshold under the OSS scheme, above which French VAT registration or OSS registration is mandatory. (2) Intra-EU acquisitions by taxable persons and B2B services with a French place of supply: registration is required from the first transaction with no minimum threshold. (3) Cross-border electronic services (TBE): EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold; above it the French mini/OSS scheme applies. (4) French domestic letting of real estate: letting is exempt absent the Article 260-C option; where the option is exercised, VAT registration is compulsory regardless of turnover. (5) Sales of new buildings or building land by a taxable developer: VAT (TVA immobilière) applies from the first sale with no franchise threshold. (6) The former EUR 35,000 threshold for local distance selling of goods has been abolished and replaced by the EU-wide EUR 10,000 OSS rule since 1 July 2021.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(1) Goods exported outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 262-I-1° CGI provided the seller is the exporter (or acts as such), ownership of the goods is transferred to the non-resident purchaser, the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days of customs acceptance of the export declaration, and the exporter holds documentary proof of exit. (2) Directly connected services relating to exported goods, including transport, handling, valuation and services ancillary to the export, qualify for zero-rating under Article 262-I-2° CGI; services supplied to non-EU taxable persons are placed outside French VAT by the place-of-supply rule in Article 259-A CGI rather than by zero-rating. (3) In the context of a real estate investment trust, French letting of real property is territorially situated in France under Article 260 CGI and is either VAT-exempt (Article 261-C CGI) or, where the option is exercised, taxable at the standard or reduced rate; it is not eligible for export zero-rating, while services supplied to the non-resident REIT by a French manager are reverse-charged to the non-resident client and not exported. (4) The zero-rating claim is conditional on the exporter receiving payment of the export consideration through a formal banking channel and keeping all transport and customs records in a VAT memorandum for six years.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Document Administratif Unique (DAU) registered and released by French Customs through DELTA-X (Douane GUN), evidencing the MRN (Movement Reference Number) and export procedure EX1
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Acte de Consignation / Air Waybill (AWB) or CMR international consignment note demonstrating physical departure of the goods from French and EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or documentary bank credit advice/SWIFT transfer receipt proving settlement of the export invoice on a French bank account or via an authorized foreign exchange channel
  • Commercial invoice annotated 'Exportation hors CEE - TVA non applicable, Article 262 CGI' and stating the buyer's VAT number where applicable
  • Export packing list, signed purchase order or international sales contract, and certificate of origin for preferential tariff claims, if applicable

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing for domestic B2B supplies and B2C e-reporting under the French e-invoicing reform (Ordonnance 2022-1290): (1) Phase-in calendar: large enterprises (headcount above 400 and turnover above EUR 50 million) from 1 September 2026; small and medium enterprises from 1 September 2027. (2) Mandatory formats: Factur-X hybrid invoice or UBL XML conforming to EN 16931; EDI remains permissible only through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP). (3) Clearance architecture: each taxable person transmits invoice datasets and B2C transaction data to DGFiP via an accredited PDP or the Public Billing Portal (Portail Public de Facturation - PPF) using machine-readable APIs; the PPF performs automated fiscal checks, validates SIREN/TVA numbers, and issues a unique stamp/identifier per invoice, enabling real-time audit. (4) B2C e-reporting content: transaction-level data including customer identification, contract number, service/rent type, VAT rate, and payment information; must be submitted within 10 days of the transaction end. (5) Real-estate application: rent invoices issued under the Article 260-C VAT option must be e-invoiced through the same channels; input VAT recovery coefficients (coefficient d'assujettissement) are supported by these datasets. (6) Compliance penalties: EUR 15 per invoice or transaction (maximum EUR 15,000 per control) for non-transmission, plus a potential 50% penalty on the hidden input VAT adjustment and temporary suspension of invoice issuance rights in repeated cases.

(1) Audit scrutiny: export zero-rating claims are a priority review area of the DGFiP Verification Direction and the Direction Nationale des Vérifications de Situations Fiscales (DNVSF); the administration cross-checks French export declarations with entry records in the destination country and with freight forwarder records. (2) Retrospective clawback: if the exporter cannot produce valid exit and payment proof, VAT is reassessed at the standard rate of 20% on the full export invoices retroactively, plus late payment interest (intérêts de retard of 0.20% per month, currently 2.4% annualized) and potential monthly 0.20% late-filing increases. (3) Civil penalties: an insufficiency penalty of 40% applies for unintentional omission (Article 1729 CGI); the 80% penalty applies where the claim is deemed to involve bad faith or fraudulent maneuvers (manœuvres frauduleuses); an additional 5% per month or 40% fixed fine under Article 1728 may apply for failure to file the required VAT returns. (4) Criminal prosecution: misuse of export zero-rating by issuing fictitious export invoices constitutes tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI and customs smuggling under Articles 414 and 423-1 of the Code des Douanes, with penalties of up to 5 years imprisonment, a fine of EUR 750,000 (or up to twice the evaded sums), confiscation of transport vehicles and goods, and publication of the penal decision in newspapers or online. (5) Joint and several liability: the freight forwarder or customs broker acting as exporter's representative is jointly liable for duties on fraudulent exports under Article 393 Code des Douanes; the VAT fiscal representative of a non-EU company is jointly liable for all underlying output VAT. (6) Limitation periods: the DGFiP audit window for VAT is the broader of six years from the due date of the declaration (Article L.169 Livre des procédures fiscales) and, in cases of tax fraud or suspected hidden activity, ten years; the criminal fiscal action period is six years from discovery.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full capital account convertibility under EU Regulation 2015/830 and national decrees. Capital inflows and outflows are unrestricted for residents and non-residents. Non-resident real estate acquisitions require prior tax authority notification and payment of registration duties. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without restriction, subject to 12.8% withholding tax (reducible under double tax treaties) and beneficial ownership declarations per DAC2.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The entity is a real estate investment trust and does not engage in export trade or receipt of export proceeds. No mandatory surrender or conversion of foreign currency receipts is required.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A French SARL must appoint a statutory auditor (commissaire aux comptes) when, at fiscal year-end, it exceeds at least two of the three thresholds: total balance sheet > EUR 4,000,000; net turnover > EUR 8,000,000; average headcount > 50 employees. An audit is also compulsory where the company controls or is controlled by an entity that is subject to statutory audit, and an audit may be requested by shareholders holding at least 10% of share capital.
