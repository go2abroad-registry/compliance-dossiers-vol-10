Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; principal portals are impots.gouv.fr and the professional tax account (espace professionnel). Business formation and statutory registration filings are submitted through the INPI Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises. The competent local tax office is generally the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE).

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered through the INPI Guichet unique before commencing business. The filing produces a SIREN identifier for the entity and a SIRET identifier for each establishment. The DGFiP then assigns or confirms the French VAT identification number, generally formatted as FR plus a two-character key and the nine-digit SIREN. The entity must provide formation documents, registered-office evidence, corporate identity information, beneficial-owner information, business activity details, bank and accounting information, and the expected date and nature of taxable transactions. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the competent French non-established-business tax service for a French VAT number before making transactions requiring French VAT registration. EU-established businesses may use the competent tax administration or applicable EU procedures; non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a French fiscal representative unless an applicable treaty or administrative arrangement removes that requirement. VAT registration does not itself establish a French permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Certain SARLs may elect corporate-income-tax transparency and taxation at shareholder level (impôt sur le revenu, IR), including qualifying family SARLs and, subject to statutory conditions, qualifying newly established SARLs. The ordinary qualifying SARL election is time-limited, while the family-SARL regime can continue while eligibility conditions are satisfied. France has no general US-style check-the-box election for SARLs. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implementing ATAD 2 can deny deductions or require inclusion for specified hybrid arrangements; they are statutory anti-avoidance rules rather than elective entity-classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business providing services, the principal franchise en base de TVA threshold is EUR 37,500 of annual turnover, with a EUR 41,250 tolerance threshold under the applicable rules. The applicable thresholds differ for goods and certain regulated activities. A business may elect to become VAT-liable before exceeding the franchise threshold. There is generally no French turnover threshold for a non-established business making French VAT-taxable supplies that require French registration. For qualifying intra-EU cross-border B2C telecommunications, broadcasting and electronically supplied services, the EUR 10,000 EU-wide annual threshold may apply before destination-country taxation and OSS obligations arise. Apple and Google platform collection does not automatically eliminate the SARL's VAT obligations; the contractual principal, merchant-of-record status, commission model and customer location must be determined.

France is introducing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through approved private platforms connected to the public directory. From 1 September 2026, all businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; large and mid-sized businesses must generally issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from that date. Small and medium-sized businesses must generally issue them from 1 September 2027. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. France's model is not a universal real-time VAT clearance system: domestic B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting will transmit prescribed invoice and transaction data to the tax administration, while cross-border and B2C transactions are generally handled through e-reporting and applicable OSS or other VAT procedures. Electronic invoices must contain required French and EU VAT data, structured invoice fields, identity and VAT-number information, transaction dates, tax bases, rates and amounts, and reliable authenticity and integrity controls.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member state; the EUR is fully convertible with no exchange control approvals required for current account payments. Capital account transactions are broadly liberalised but subject to statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque de France when any single cross-border transaction exceeds EUR 15,000 (excluding inter-EU flows). Dividend and profit remittances by a SARL to its shareholders are freely transferable; French withholding tax may apply for non-EU shareholders unless reduced under a tax treaty, and EU parent-subsidiary directive protection applies for qualifying EU corporate shareholders. No administrative FX licence is required for conversion from EUR to foreign currency for trade or investment purposes, provided the underlying activity is lawful and compliant with anti-money-laundering obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. French law imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds (including digital services revenue). The SARL may retain USD, EUR, or other freely convertible currencies in its treasury accounts. However, for national statistics, export proceeds attributed to French residents must be declared to the Banque de France within the annual balance-of-payments filing when they exceed the EUR 15,000 threshold per calendar year. App-store gross settlements (Apple/Google) are booked as export of digital services and must be traceable in the statutory accounting records.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France); Foreign Exchange Administration de facto delegated to the Direction Générale du Trésor under the Code Monétaire et Financier (Articles L151-1 et seq.)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for day-to-day operational treasury through French-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto or Shine. Legal constraints: these are ACPR-regulated payment institutions under PSD2; they may hold client funds, execute SEPA and SWIFT transfers, and issue payment cards, but may not grant credit or hold escrowed capital at long tenor. Offshore non-EU EMIs are not permitted to provide payment services into France absent an EU branch or licence passported under PSD2; hence the SARL should maintain at least one Tier-1 bank account for statutory capital, dividends, and tax payments. An EMI may be used for app-store recovery settlement and FX conversion only when the payout is legally attributable to the SARL and the EMI performs strict KYB.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: use SWIFT MT103 in EUR to the SARL's French euro account. For EU shareholders, standard SEPA Credit Transfer is sufficient. The bank reference field must state 'Capital Increase - SARL Apport en Numéraire' and identify the share register reference; the purpose code for French balance-of-payments reporting is 'Capital transfer – equity participation or subscription'. The bank must receive: a certified extract of the company registry, minutes of the extraordinary general meeting resolving the capital increase, shareholder list, and a decomposition of the contribution amount. The contribution is held in the statutory capital account and cannot be swept without a further formal act; the bank will verify that the inbound funds originate from the shareholder declared in the KYC file, not a third party. For amounts above EUR 15,000, the SARL, through the bank, must file the French balance-of-payments statistical form (CERFA equivalent) with the Direction Générale du Trésor.Clearing rails: (1) EU and EEA author royalties, licensor fees, and refunds: SEPA Credit Transfer (instant or delayed) using IBAN/BIC, with no FX cost. (2) Non-EU content licensing, author royalties, platform development, and operational invoices: SWIFT MT103 in the settlement currency, with the beneficiary bank routed through correspondents; for volume payments (e.g., Russian, Indian, Brazilian authors), a single MT103 with a structured remittance advice can be batched through the treasury, but each underlying contract and tax withholding certificate must be retained. (3) Commercial LC (documentary credit) is permissible but unusual for webnovel royalties; it is reserved for large-scale IP acquisitions (e.g., film/TV adaptation licenses) where the issuing bank in France may open a confirmed LC in favour of the non-EU licensor. (4) App-store refunds and chargebacks traverse the acquiring rails (Stripe/Adyen/Apple/Google) and appear as reversals against the SARL's funding account; this does not require a separate clearing rail.Outright EUR/USD FX forward contract for hedging monthly Apple and Google payout receipts in USD into EUREUR/USD and EUR/GBP FX Swaps (swap and forward combined) for liquidity bridging among treasury accountsNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) for INR, IDR, TRY, PHP, and similar non-convertible emerging-market currencies arising from content royalties or app-store remittances, settled in EUREuropean-style FX options (calls/puts) on EUR/USD for contingent app-store settlement exposure, subject to bank approval and ISDA/CSA documentation

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on gross dividends paid by a French SARL to non-resident corporate and individual shareholders (CGI art. 119 bis-2), plus a 3% social contribution on distributions borne by the distributing company (CGI art. 235 ter ZCA).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return must be filed by 15 May of the following calendar year. For non-calendar fiscal year-ends, the return is due within 60 days after the close of the fiscal year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly instalments due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December; final balance due with the annual return on the applicable deadline described for Form 2065-SD.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 700 — Bénéfice imposable Taxable income after applying loss carryforwards, deductions, and fiscal adjustments. For an SARL with non-resident shareholders, this box reflects only the entity-level corporate income tax base; shareholding residency has no effect on this field, but proper attribution of dividends must be documented for withholding tax purposes. Late filing: 10% penalty of the tax due plus 0.75% per month late-payment interest; for non-filing after formal notice, ex officio assessment with a 100% surcharge may apply.
Box 712 — Crédits d'impôt utilisés Tax credits available against corporate income tax, including research and innovation credits. Non-resident shareholders may not carry tax credits of the entity; credits apply solely to the SARL and must be substantiated by supporting forms. Late filing: 10% penalty of the tax due plus 0.75% per month late-payment interest; for non-filing after formal notice, ex officio assessment with a 100% surcharge may apply.
Box 714 — Déficits reportables Net operating loss carryforwards and current-year deficit absorption. Loss carryforward positions are part of the French entity's fiscal history and are unaffected by ownership residency, but change-of-control anti-abuse limitations apply under Article 221bis CGI. Late filing: 10% penalty of the tax due plus 0.75% per month late-payment interest; for non-filing after formal notice, ex officio assessment with a 100% surcharge may apply.
Box 735 — Contribution sociale sur l'IS Additional 3% social contribution for companies with taxable income above a statutory threshold. This charge is computed at entity level; no non-resident shareholder filing requirement arises. Late filing: 10% penalty of the tax due plus 0.75% per month late-payment interest; for non-filing after formal notice, ex officio assessment with a 100% surcharge may apply.
Case A — Base de l'acompte Reference corporate income tax base from the preceding fiscal year. For newly established SARLs, the first-year instalment base is calculated on the total corporate income tax estimated for the first fiscal year; non-resident shareholders must ensure cash-flow planning for such prepayments. Late payment penalty of 5% on the unpaid amount plus monthly 1.2% late-payment interest; inadequate instalments may trigger fixed penalties and administrative surcharges.
Case B — Acompte dû Quarterly prepayment amount reflecting the prior-year tax, with possible inter-period reductions. Tax reductions or exemptions must be validly claimed by the SARL; foreign shareholders cannot claim a refund of the entity's prepayments. Late payment penalty of 5% on the unpaid amount plus monthly 1.2% late-payment interest; inadequate instalments may trigger fixed penalties and administrative surcharges.
Case C — Imputation des crédits d'impôt Offsetting of tax credits against the instalment due. No direct application to non-resident shareholders; any excess credit after balance may be carried forward or refunded to the French entity. Late payment penalty of 5% on the unpaid amount plus monthly 1.2% late-payment interest; inadequate instalments may trigger fixed penalties and administrative surcharges.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File documentation is required for French entities belonging to a multinational group with consolidated turnover of or above EUR 750 million. For entities below this threshold, transfer pricing documentation may still be requested by the tax administration during an audit if cross-border controlled transactions are material and lack an arm's-length justification.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code as in UAE. Instead, French tax law applies a substance-over-form doctrine under the abuse of law provision (LPF art. L. 64) and general anti-abuse principles. An SARL with no actual management, premises, personnel, or risks in France may be disregarded for tax purposes or found to be an artificial arrangement lacking economic substance, especially in relation to royalty flows and treaty benefits.
