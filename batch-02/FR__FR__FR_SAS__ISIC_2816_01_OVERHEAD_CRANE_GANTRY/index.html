Name reservation / lock period

France does not operate a formal name-reservation window. If the proposed name is rejected by the greffier, the applicant must obtain a written statement of reasons under Article L.123-10 of the Commercial Code. An appeal may be filed as a summary petition (requête) before the President of the Tribunal de Commerce, or the Tribunal Judiciaire for non-commercial undertakings, within one month of refusal in accordance with Article R.123-78 of the Commercial Code. The petition must include the proposed name, the registrar's refusal grounds, evidence of negative company/trademark searches, and written legal submissions demonstrating absence of confusion. No extension of name reservation is possible; the applicant may immediately re-file under a new name.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SAS has no statutory minimum capital; articles fix the capital freely, often EUR 1 for non-regulated activity. In-kind shares must be fully paid on subscription. Cash shares must be paid up to at least 50% of nominal value at subscription and the remaining 50% within five years. Capital is blocked in an escrow/bank account and released only after RCS registration; an attestation de blocage de fonds must be produced.

Funding & unlock path: Workplace clearance requires compliance with the French Labor Code (Code du travail) on machine safety (Article R.4323-1 et seq.), the 'permis de feu' for welding, and certification of the fixed crane runway's structural compliance (Eurocode 3, EN 1993-6). The SDIS verifies fire resistance of buildings, evacuation routes, gas/solvent storage and the fire water reserve. Occupationally, the site must have a DUERP, a certified emergency plan, and an approved inspection contract for periodic verification (annual) of hoisting gear by an accredited organism (APAVE, DEKRA, etc.).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a eurozone Member State, maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the euro under Articles 63-66 TFEU and the French Monetary and Financial Code. No prior central-bank approval is required for the conversion of export proceeds, profit repatriation, dividends, loan repayment, or capital injections to or from the EUR area. Restrictions only arise from EU/OFAC-equivalent financial sanctions, EU restrictive measures, anti-money-laundering screening, and the exercise of foreign-investment screening in strategic sectors (e.g. ports, security, dual-use equipment), which may affect inward direct investment but not ordinary FX conversion. Profit remittance by an SAS is unrestricted provided corporate formalities (audited accounts, tax filings, UBO registers) are current and the payment is processed through a compliant EU banking channel.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in France. Exporters may hold receivables in EUR or any foreign currency without forced repatriation or sale to the central bank. However, for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, exports of goods must be reported through the customs 'déclaration d'échanges de biens' (DEB) and exports of services through the 'déclaration d'échanges de services' (DES). When a payment transfer between France and a non-resident exceeds the Banque de France statistical threshold (generally EUR 7,500 per transaction), the bank will require a 'déclaration de transfert de fonds' (DTF) to be completed for monetary statistics and sanctions screening. Non-compliance may delay transactions but does not void the underlying commercial agreement.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with prudential supervision undertaken by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); foreign exchange-control policy and financial sanctions enforcement are co-ordinated with the Direction Générale du Trésor and the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Recovery.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for full treasury and trade-finance operations. Authorised EU electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (e.g. specialist French fintechs) can legally open operational payment accounts for an SAS and provide FX payment services via passporting under PSD2, but they are not credit institutions and cannot: accept deposits from the public, grant loans, issue documentary letters of credit, open confirmed LC/standby LC facilities, or provide authorised FX hedging booking. For an industrial port-equipment exporter with LC-backed settlement needs, a tier-1 bank is required. EMI accounts should be used strictly for low-value operative disbursements (payroll, SaaS subscriptions, minor expenses) and must be segregated from capital escrow balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for shareholder loans or equity contributions into the French SAS must use a SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the parent company to the SAS's euro-denominated account at a French tier-1 bank. The remittance information field should indicate 'capital increase / share subscription' or 'shareholder current-account advance' according to the underlying legal act, and the ISO 20022 purpose code should be set to a capital/payment code such as 'CAPC' (Capital Payment) or, where the invoicing purpose applies, 'CORT' (Trade/Credit Settlement) as accepted by the receiving bank. Beneficiary account must be the dedicated Capital Escrow Account if the injection is still subject to legal release conditions. For any single transfer exceeding the Banque de France statistical-declaration threshold (in practice EUR 7,500 and above), the bank will require a completed 'Déclaration de transfert de fonds' (DTF) to accompany the wire; transactions must also pass EU sanctions screening on all counterparties, banks, vessel operators, and intermediate ports.Trade settlements for the import of components and export of gantry cranes should be cleared through: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct low-value supplier payments; (ii) documentary letters of credit issued by the French issuing bank and advised/confirmed by a correspondent in the buyer's country, transmitted via SWIFT MT700/MT710/MT720, with settlement using MT103/MT202 upon presentation of compliant transport and commercial documents; (iii) standby letters of credit and bank guarantees via MT760 for advance-payment or performance risk; and (iv) euro-denominated high-value payments via TARGET2 or EURO1/STEP2 automated clearing for intra-European counterparties. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to domestic exemptions, European Union rules, tax treaties, beneficial ownership requirements, and anti-abuse rules. Distributions to qualifying EU or EEA parent companies may be exempt under the Parent-Subsidiary regime, generally requiring at least 10% ownership, a minimum holding period or binding commitment to satisfy it, and appropriate legal and anti-abuse conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally by the second working day following 1 May for a financial year ending on 31 December. For a non-calendar year, filing is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The tax balance is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, subject to the applicable filing and payment calendar.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following the financial year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million: French entities or French permanent establishments generally within the statutory scope where annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets reach at least EUR 150 million, or where an associated French entity or permanent establishment meets the relevant statutory condition. A local file and master file are generally required for in-scope taxpayers. A simplified annual transfer pricing declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies at the EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, subject to the statutory related-party transaction conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France has no separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance is assessed through French transfer pricing rules, the arm's-length principle, the general anti-abuse rule, the beneficial ownership doctrine, permanent establishment principles, anti-hybrid rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, and tax treaty principal-purpose tests. The SAS should demonstrate real French functions, personnel, premises, decision-making, risk control, assets, commercial purpose, and contemporaneous records proportionate to its overhead-crane manufacturing and project activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, 40% or 80% tax penalties depending on conduct, late-payment interest, and criminal prosecution. Tax fraud may result in up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, with aggravated cases reaching up to seven years' imprisonment and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory aggravating conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability, civil liability for tax debts in cases of fraudulent or seriously negligent management, asset recovery, director disqualification, and seizure. Travel restrictions or imprisonment may arise only under applicable judicial measures and criminal proceedings; the corporate veil is not automatically pierced.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length income, interest limitation or expense denial, transfer pricing penalties, late-payment interest, and penalties for failure to provide statutory documentation. Failure to produce documentation may result in a penalty generally calculated by reference to the reassessed profits, subject to statutory minimums and caps.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur liability where they knowingly approve sham arrangements, conceal records, or organize fraudulent profit diversion. Ordinary transfer pricing disagreement does not by itself create criminal director liability.
Withholding Tax and Profit Repatriation Failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, applicable surcharges, late-payment interest, potential denial of treaty relief, and penalties for inaccurate or late returns. The French payer may remain liable even where the non-resident recipient received the gross amount.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for deliberate or seriously negligent failure to remit tax and criminal liability where non-remittance forms part of a fraudulent scheme.
Shell Company, Beneficial Ownership, and Abuse of Rights

Statutory sanction: Denial of exemptions or treaty benefits, recharacterization of payments, tax reassessment, abuse-of-rights penalties potentially reaching 80% of the reassessed tax, interest, and possible criminal fraud proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face civil recovery, asset seizure, disqualification, and criminal prosecution where they intentionally implement abusive or fraudulent structures. Travel bans and imprisonment require judicial or criminal-law grounds.
Anti-Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing, and Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Regulatory sanctions, administrative fines, account blocking, confiscation, contract invalidation, and criminal penalties depending on the underlying offense. Money laundering may carry imprisonment and substantial fines, with aggravated penalties for organized activity or regulated-professional involvement.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally prosecuted for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or deliberate failure to prevent criminal conduct. Assets may be frozen or confiscated, and judicial travel restrictions or imprisonment may apply.
False Accounting, Bribery, and Public Procurement Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Accounting restatement, tax reassessment, expense disallowance, corporate fines, procurement exclusion, debarment, confiscation, and criminal penalties for bribery, corruption, forgery, or fraudulent accounting.

Director personal liability: Directors, executives, intermediaries, and beneficial owners may face imprisonment, personal fines, disqualification from management, civil restitution, confiscation, and judicial restrictions. Corporate indemnification may be unavailable for intentional misconduct.
Customs, Import VAT, and Intra-Group Goods Misclassification

Statutory sanction: Recovery of customs duties and import VAT, customs fines, seizure or forfeiture of goods, interest, denial of preferential origin, and possible criminal customs proceedings for intentional evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly exposed under applicable customs and criminal rules where they authorized or knowingly tolerated false declarations. Repeated or organized conduct increases seizure, confiscation, and imprisonment risk.
