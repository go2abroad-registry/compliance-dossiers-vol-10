Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permissible through a written domiciliation agreement with an approved commercial domiciliation company under Article L.123-11-1 of the Code de commerce; the agreement must be concluded for a minimum of one year, is renewable, and may be terminated on three months' notice. For the fabless semiconductor design and IP core licensing business, a domiciliated virtual registered office is accepted at inception; however, commercial substance for French tax residence, French VAT enforcement, and future R&D relief requires that management decisions, statutory books and registers (registre des décisions de l'associé unique, registre des mouvements de titres), books and records, and contractual records of IP licenses be physically accessible in France. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS. If share capital is subscribed in cash, at least 50% of such cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation and the balance within five years or within the shorter period fixed in the statuts. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up and, where applicable, reviewed by a contribution auditor. No statutory escrow account is required, but cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked account with a French credit institution and evidenced by a bank certificate before registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – official portal: impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity forming a French SAS initiates registration through the single business formalities portal at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Upon completion of immatriculation at the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés, the DGFiP assigns the SIREN/SIRET statutory identifiers. The VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) is issued subsequently by the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises, with EORI registration required for intra-EU/export customs operations. The corporate tax file is activated automatically with the SIRET, and the SAS must file an initial results declaration under its corporate income tax regime.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity forming a French SAS initiates registration through the single business formalities portal at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Upon completion of immatriculation at the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés, the DGFiP assigns the SIREN/SIRET statutory identifiers. The VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) is issued subsequently by the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises, with EORI registration required for intra-EU/export customs operations. The corporate tax file is activated automatically with the SIRET, and the SAS must file an initial results declaration under its corporate income tax regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French tax law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A SAS is mandatorily subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level. Available statutory elections include fiscal group integration under CGI Article 223 A et seq., VAT grouping under CGI Article 1693 ter (assujetti unique), and the reduced corporate tax rate for small and medium-sized enterprises under CGI Article 219 when statutory conditions are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base thresholds for domestic micro-businesses: EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for services. However, foreign/non-resident suppliers have no threshold and must register for French VAT immediately upon making any taxable supply in France unless the transaction is outside scope or subject to reverse charge under CGI Article 194.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically removed from France to destinations outside the European Union qualify for 0% VAT under CGI Article 262-I-1°, provided transport evidence demonstrates actual exit of the goods within 90 days of dispatch. Semiconductor IP core licensing and fabless design services supplied to recipients established outside France are outside French VAT scope under the B2B place-of-supply rules (CGI Article 259-2 and EU VAT Directive 2006/112/EC), allowing zero-rate treatment. Intra-Union B2B supplies of semiconductor licenses can also be zero-rated when the customer's valid EU VAT number is obtained and recapitulative statements are filed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/ENS – Document Administratif Unique)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and signed export order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate proving foreign currency payment from the overseas buyer

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing and e-reporting to DGFiP through a certified Partner Dematerialisation Platform (PDP) or the public Chorus Pro portal, phased from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for small and medium-sized enterprises. France does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system; transmission of invoice data occurs at issuance, with VAT settlement via Form CA3/3310 through the DGFiP electronic filing system.

DGFiP applies heightened audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. Failure to substantiate the physical export and receipt of payment within the required documentation and 180-day FX repatriation period triggers reinstatement of the 20% VAT with late interest (intérêts de retard). Penalties escalate from 40% for material under-declaration (insuffisance de déclaration) to 80% for deliberate misconduct (manquement délibéré) or concealed activity (exercice occulte). Forgery or falsification of export and customs documents constitutes tax fraud, exposing the SAS and its directors to criminal fines and imprisonment under the French General Tax Code and Customs Code clawback mechanisms.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full euro-zone convertibility applies. Both capital and current-account transactions are liberalised under EU capital-movement rules; no currency inconvertibility or exchange-approval restriction exists for the French SAS. Profit remittance (dividends) is unrestricted subject to AML/KYC verification, potential French withholding tax (30% standard flat tax on outbound dividends, reducible to 15%/5% under applicable double-tax treaties, or 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the shareholder qualifies), and Banque de France statistical direct-investment declarations. Fabless IP-core royalty receipts are classified as current-account business revenue and are freely repatriable without convertibility, licensing or prior-exchange-approval hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export receivables may be retained in any foreign currency. However, French resident corporate exporters are required to file balance-of-payments statistical declarations with the Banque de France for direct investment flows where the transaction or cumulative external position exceeds the €15,000,000 threshold, and ongoing external financial-account declarations apply to foreign receivables above de minimis levels; USD invoicing for semiconductor IP licences is expressly permitted.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting jointly with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) and the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) for foreign-exchange statistical, balance-of-payments and exchange-control surveillance, in accordance with the French Monetary and Financial Code and Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63-66.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., French-authorised Qonto or passporting EU EMIs such as Revolut Business) may support day-to-day euro operational liquidity, SEPA/SEPA Instant transfers, FX conversion and payment cards under the French Monetary and Financial Code. They are, however, not viable as the sole treasury backbone for a fabless semiconductor treasury requiring USD correspondent banking, Commercial Letters of Credit, documentary collections, or ACPR-regulated credit and hedging lines. Non-EU EMIs (e.g., UK or US-based providers) receive a Low viability rating because of French client-money segregation rules, the absence of French IBAN compensation, and bank-in-the-middle KYC frictions for recurring high-value trade flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire-transfer routing for inbound equity or shareholder-loan injections uses SWIFT MT103 for cross-border currencies or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR, with mandatory ISO 20022/EPC purpose codes. Equity subscription transfers must be tagged with the EU Balance-of-Payments direct-investment codification (e.g., code 705101 for share-capital acquisition); the beneficiary French SAS must file a Banque de France direct-investment declaration, and a prior French FDI authorisation is required for non-EU acquirers of control or a 25% voting threshold in the semiconductor sector under Décret n° 2019-1590. Remittance instructions must include the full legal name of the SAS, its SIREN/SIRET identifiers, the nature-of-payment field (remittance information in field 70) stating SUBSCRIBED CAPITAL INCREASE or SHAREHOLDER LOAN, and such inbound funds are subject to bank-side AML pre-clearance, verification against the corporate capital-resolution register, and an anti-money-laundering declaration where applicable.Primary clearing rails for outbound fabless disbursements are SWIFT MT103 high-value wire transfers for IP-core licence fees, design-service payments and foundry disbursements, using the relevant Banque de France balance-of-payments service/trade codes (e.g., licence-fee code 265U). For equipment or material procurement requiring a documentary instrument, Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP 600 and standby LCs are available through the French bank's trade-finance desks; documents-against-payment and documents-against-acceptance collections provide supplementary rails. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax on taxable profits under Article 219 of the French Tax Code (Code général des impôts).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to domestic exemptions and treaty relief. Qualifying distributions to an eligible EU parent may be exempt under the French implementation of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring qualifying ownership, holding-period and anti-abuse conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the standard deadline is the second business day following 1 May of the following year. Corporate income tax instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December. The final balance is generally due on the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a 31 December year-end, the balance is generally due by 15 May.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million. A French entity or French tax group generally falls within the full transfer-pricing documentation regime where its annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets reach at least EUR 400 million, or where it is associated with an entity meeting the statutory EUR 400 million threshold. The full regime generally requires a Master File and Local File. A transfer-pricing summary filing on Form 2257-SD may apply at lower thresholds, commonly including EUR 150 million turnover or gross assets or qualifying group conditions. French entities below the full threshold must still apply the arm's-length principle and may be required to provide adequate documentation on request.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a separate, generally applicable UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Economic substance is nevertheless material under French transfer-pricing rules, the abuse-of-law doctrine, the principal-purpose and anti-abuse rules, controlled foreign company rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, interest limitation rules and general anti-avoidance principles. A French SAS conducting semiconductor architecture and IP licensing should maintain qualified personnel, decision-making capacity, technical records, functional risk control, development evidence, premises or operational infrastructure appropriate to its activities, and contemporaneous ownership and exploitation records for intellectual property.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, tax surcharges generally up to 40% for deliberate conduct and up to 80% for fraudulent or abusive conduct. Aggravated tax fraud may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, increasing in specified aggravated cases to up to 7 years and EUR 3 million. Corporate fines may be multiplied under French criminal-law rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, tax-payment liability in cases of fraudulent or seriously negligent management, civil damages, management bans and asset-seizure measures. Travel restrictions are not automatic; imprisonment requires criminal conviction.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Additional corporate tax, interest, transfer-pricing reassessment, potential 40% or 80% tax surcharge, documentation-related penalties and possible withholding tax on recharacterized payments. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation may attract a penalty generally linked to the higher of a statutory minimum and a percentage of the corrected transaction amount, subject to statutory caps.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for breach of management duties, criminal exposure where falsification or fraud is proven, and personal tax-payment liability in cases of fraudulent management. Veil piercing is exceptional and fact-dependent.
Undeclared permanent establishment or artificial residency position

Statutory sanction: French corporate income tax, business taxes, withholding taxes, payroll and VAT exposure where applicable, interest, late-filing surcharges and potential permanent-establishment reassessment under domestic law and the applicable treaty.

Director personal liability: Managers may be liable for misleading filings, concealment and unpaid tax where their conduct is intentional or seriously negligent. Criminal liability depends on proof of fraud; travel bans and imprisonment are not automatic consequences of a tax reassessment.
Money laundering, corruption and misuse of company funds

Statutory sanction: Money laundering may carry up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 375,000, with aggravated cases carrying up to 10 years and EUR 750,000. Corruption offences carry separate and potentially more severe criminal sanctions. Corporate criminal fines, confiscation and prohibition orders may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners and employees may be prosecuted personally. Asset confiscation, management bans and civil restitution are possible. Travel restrictions may be ordered by a court; imprisonment requires conviction or may arise pre-trial under judicial-control rules.
Beneficial-owner, AML and corporate-record failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative, civil and criminal consequences may include filing injunctions, fines, refusal or suspension of account or transaction services, director-management sanctions and prosecution where the conduct supports fraud, money laundering or false declarations.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives may be personally liable for knowingly false filings or obstruction. Civil restitution and management prohibitions may apply; travel bans or imprisonment require a specific judicial basis and are not automatic.
Unlawful IP royalty or technology-transfer structure

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, denial of deductions or patent-box treatment, withholding-tax liability, interest, penalties, potential abuse-of-law recharacterization and civil IP claims. Unlawful export-control or technology-transfer conduct may create separate criminal or administrative exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for unauthorized exploitation, breach of fiduciary duties or deliberate tax abuse. Criminal liability arises where fraud, counterfeiting, corruption or other specific offences are established.
