Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), transmitting the dossier to the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS) of the registered office.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title III, Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 et seq.; registration formalities under Articles L.123-1 to L.123-12 and R.123-1 et seq.; SARL formation implemented through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de constitution de société (formerly form M0/CERFA 13928) filed electronically on the Guichet Unique, accompanied by the signed statuts, the blocked-capital deposit certificate, and the Journal d'Annonces Légales publication certificate.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 16 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum statutory capital for an SARL; capital is set freely in the articles. Cash capital must be fully subscribed and at least 20% of each cash contribution must be paid up before registration, with the balance payable within five years; in-kind capital must be fully paid up immediately. The cash contribution is placed in a blocked escrow account with a credit institution, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts, and the depositary certificate is attached to the RCS filing.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate or inspection receipt from the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) for any covered vehicle bay or auxiliary premises; structural load inspection certificate for warehouses, loading docks and parking decks; occupational health and safety registration with CARSAT, completion of a Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP), compliance with working time rules, use of certified equipment, and pre-start safety clearance for fuel storage and vehicle maintenance areas.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum statutory capital for an SARL; capital is set freely in the articles. Cash capital must be fully subscribed and at least 20% of each cash contribution must be paid up before registration, with the balance payable within five years; in-kind capital must be fully paid up immediately. The cash contribution is placed in a blocked escrow account with a credit institution, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts, and the depositary certificate is attached to the RCS filing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), the French national tax authority; online portal: impots.gouv.fr (professional access for VAT, CIT and withholding filings). Business incorporation and tax ID registration are filed through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises operated by INPI at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Customs and bonded-transit matters are handled by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), including AEO (Opérateur Économique Agréé) and NCTS T1/T2 transit authorizations. Non-resident entities registering for VAT deal with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) in Montreuil.

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL: (1) file statutory incorporation documents (statuts, registered-office proof, gérant identity) through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; (2) the CFE/INPI transmits the dossier to INSEE for allocation of the 9-digit SIREN and 14-digit SIRET; (3) DGFIP automatically issues the 13-character intra-Community VAT number (format FR + 2 control digits + 9-digit SIREN) upon completion of registration; (4) register as employer with URSSAF if hiring staff; (5) if bonded customs operations are intended, obtain an AEO/Customs transit authorization from DGDDI. For a foreign entity without a French establishment: no turnover threshold applies; the entity must file CERFA Form No. 16782 (demande d'attribution d'un numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) with the SIEE in Montreuil, provide the foreign TIN and proof of business existence, and appoint a French fiscal representative (représentant fiscal assujetti) jointly and severally liable for VAT where French VAT registrations are required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SARL is by default subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at entity level, at 25% above €42,500 of taxable profit and 15% on the first €42,500 for eligible SMEs (≤€7.6 million turnover, capital fully paid up, at least 75% held by individuals). Territorial_Scope: France applies the territoriality principle, so profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment are outside the French IS charge (CGI 209). Tax_Exempt_Zone: no general tax-exempt zone applies to airport feeder trucking; only very narrow ZFU/urban free-zone incentives exist and are not relevant here. — For a French SARL: (1) file statutory incorporation documents (statuts, registered-office proof, gérant identity) through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; (2) the CFE/INPI transmits the dossier to INSEE for allocation of the 9-digit SIREN and 14-digit SIRET; (3) DGFIP automatically issues the 13-character intra-Community VAT number (format FR + 2 control digits + 9-digit SIREN) upon completion of registration; (4) register as employer with URSSAF if hiring staff; (5) if bonded customs operations are intended, obtain an AEO/Customs transit authorization from DGDDI. For a foreign entity without a French establishment: no turnover threshold applies; the entity must file CERFA Form No. 16782 (demande d'attribution d'un numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) with the SIEE in Montreuil, provide the foreign TIN and proof of business existence, and appoint a French fiscal representative (représentant fiscal assujetti) jointly and severally liable for VAT where French VAT registrations are required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SARL is by default subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at entity level, at 25% above €42,500 of taxable profit and 15% on the first €42,500 for eligible SMEs (≤€7.6 million turnover, capital fully paid up, at least 75% held by individuals). Territorial_Scope: France applies the territoriality principle, so profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment are outside the French IS charge (CGI 209). Tax_Exempt_Zone: no general tax-exempt zone applies to airport feeder trucking; only very narrow ZFU/urban free-zone incentives exist and are not relevant here.

Available election forms

1. Check-the-box-style option for pass-through taxation: a SARL with more than 50% of its capital held directly by natural persons may elect, under CGI Article 239 ter AA, to be taxed as a transparent partnership (régime des sociétés de personnes) for 5 fiscal years, renewable, provided the option is filed within the first 3 months of the starting fiscal year. 2. Family SARL election: all shareholders within the family circle may keep the IR (income-tax) regime indefinitely, and may conversely elect for IS. 3. Tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under CGI Article 223 A: a 95%+ parent may consolidate French subsidiaries for IS. 4. Hybrid mismatch: France has no entity-level check-the-box election; ATAD (Directive (EU) 2016/1164 as amended by (EU) 2017/952) hybrid mismatch rules are implemented through the Finance Act 2019, disallowing deductions for deductible-without-includible payments and double-deduction arrangements, and applying anti-reverse-hybrid treatment to certain French-resident entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For French-established taxable persons, the 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption (CGI 293 B) relieves the taxpayer from VAT registration when preceding-year taxable turnover does not exceed €36,800 for services (including road freight transport) or €91,900 for supplies of goods, and projected current-year turnover does not exceed €39,100 or €101,000 respectively; crossing those ceilings triggers compulsory DGFIP registration. For non-established foreign entities, VAT registration is compulsory from the first euro of taxable supplies performed in France with no threshold. For a B2B road-feeder carrier, the reverse charge on intra-Community transport services and on supplies by non-resident providers avoids a local VAT registration unless the entity maintains a fixed establishment in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to export supplies of goods (CGI Article 262-I) and to services directly linked to the export operation, including transport, handling, loading and customs formalities (CGI Article 262-II), provided: (a) the goods physically leave EU customs territory within 90 days from supply/invoicing; (b) the exporter is VAT-registered and holds a valid export customs declaration (DAU) with the EXS 'Goods Released' outcome from DELTA-D; (c) the transport is evidenced by CMR, Air Waybill/Master Air Waybill or Bill of Lading clearly identifying the goods, the exporter/importer, gross weight, HS codes and the customs office of exit; (d) consideration is receivable and export proceeds are repatriated within the statutory 180-day FX deadline; and (e) for the GPS-sealed bonded road-feeder service, the cargo movement is supervised under a T1/T2 or TIR transit procedure, with the airport hub as an authorized customs-bonded facility and the final non-EU destination proven by inbound-control records. Domestic trucking legs within France that are not part of a documented export movement remain taxable at 20%; refunds of excess input VAT on zero-rated exports may be claimed monthly via the 'remboursement de crédit de TVA' procedure on impots.gouv.fr.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU — Document Administratif Unique) lodged electronically in the DELTA-D export module, showing the MRN, the EXS 'Goods Released' status and, where applicable, the NCTS T1/T2 transit movement reference for the bonded truck fleet.
  • Transport and Transit Documents: CMR consignment note (road feeder fleet), Air Waybill/Master Air Waybill (airport hub), or multimodal Bill of Lading, together with the customs transit accompanying document (TAD), the sealed-truck GPS manifest and the outward-office exit certificate (attestation de sortie).
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: French bank certificate of export-proceeds collection (certificat de domiciliation bancaire) or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt in EUR of the export sale within the 180-day deadline, matched to the commercial invoice and the exporter's accounts.

France implements the mandatory e-invoicing/e-reporting reform (Loi de finances pour 2023, Ordonnance 2022-1087): from 1 September 2026, all French taxable persons must be able to receive structured electronic invoices, and large/mid-size enterprises must issue e-invoices (Factur-X or UBL 2.1/cXML) and transmit standardized e-invoice and e-transaction data to the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF/Chorus Pro) directly or via certified Partner Dematerialisation Platforms (PDPs); from 1 September 2027, SMEs and micro-enterprises (including typical SARLs) must issue and transmit as well. The architecture is a 'cocktail model' of interconnection and central storage at the PPF, not real-time per-invoice fiscal stamping, but DGFIP receives transactional data within prescribed 10-day reporting windows and reconciles invoices electronically. Paper invoices lose tax validity for B2B supplies. On the customs side, export declarations and T1/T2 transit movements are fully electronic through DELTA-D/ NSTI and the EU Trader Portal with ICS2 Entry Summary declarations; GPS-sealed trailers must match the transit accompanying document and the customs office of exit in order for the zero-rating to be sustained.

DGFIP and DGDDI jointly verify export zero-rating claims by cross-referencing the export declaration (EXS), the NCTS transit closure and the bank receipts of foreign currency. Unsupported or falsely documented exports are recharacterized as domestic supplies subject to 20% VAT, triggering a 'rappel de TVA' for up to 3 prior fiscal years (extended to 6 years for fraudulent omission or 10 years for hidden activity), plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month (intérêts de retard). Standard penalties: 40% of the reassessed VAT for deliberate insufficiency (CGI 1731), 80% for bad faith or fraudulent maneuvers (CGI 1729), and 100% for abuse of law or use of false documents; aggravated criminal tax fraud (CGI 1741) carries up to 5 years' imprisonment and a €500,000 fine (up to 7 years and €750,000 in organized-fraud form); customs penalties under Code des douanes include fines of 1 to 5 times the defrauded duties, confiscation of the truck and cargo, and suspension or withdrawal of AEO and bonded-warehouse authorizations. The SARL's gérant is jointly and severally liable and may face personal criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EUR is fully convertible under Article 63 TFEU (free movement of capital). Current-account transactions are deregulated; capital-account transactions are also liberalized, subject only to the French declarative reporting regime (régime déclaratif) for cross-border flows exceeding statistical thresholds. Profit remittance requires no exchange-control authorization, but remains subject to the SARL statutory reserve rule (appropriation of at least 10% of annual profits until 5% of share capital is reached) and to domestic or treaty-based dividend withholding exposure for non-resident shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender obligation applies to French-resident exporters. Foreign-currency export receipts may be retained, converted, or transferred freely. However, every payment transaction with a non-resident of an amount equal to or exceeding EUR 50,000 must be reported automatically by the servicing bank to Banque de France for BOP statistics via the GABARI return; the corporate client must maintain the underlying transport, customs-bonded, and invoicing records for audit.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) — acting in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for exchange-control policy and capital-flow regulation, and with the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) for banking conduct and AML supervision. The Banque de France administers balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical reporting through its GABARI reporting system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

High for routine operational accounts and cross-border payment execution via ACPR-licensed or EEA-passported electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business) under PSD2. Fee levels, FX spreads, and API-driven treasury management are commercially attractive. Low for statutory capital-escrow perfection because the blocked-deposit certificate required for SARL incorporation must be issued by a credit institution or a notary. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs have Low viability: without an EU EMI licence and AML/CFT registration in France, they cannot lawfully provide payment services to the French entity nor access national clearing systems.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share-capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.008 in straight-through Euro clearing) to the dedicated blocked capital account, using purpose code 'EQUI' and the Remittance Information field stating 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION – SARL INCORPORATION'. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Eligible small companies may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to statutory conditions, including annual turnover generally not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up share capital, and at least 75% ownership by individuals or qualifying entities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic French withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident corporate recipient is generally 25%, subject to applicable treaty relief and statutory exemptions. Qualifying distributions under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may be exempt where ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. A 12.8% rate generally applies to dividends paid to non-resident individuals, subject to treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150,000,000 of annual turnover or gross assets generally triggers the full French transfer-pricing documentation obligation, subject to statutory ownership, control, tax-group, and transaction conditions. The French master file and local file must be prepared and maintained when applicable. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups meeting the EUR 750,000,000 consolidated revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone economic substance regulation identical to the UAE or certain offshore regimes. Nevertheless, French anti-abuse principles, the general anti-abuse rule, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, permanent-establishment principles, transfer-pricing requirements, and principal-purpose testing can deny benefits or deductions where arrangements lack genuine commercial substance. A bonded trucking SARL should maintain French operational capacity, drivers or contracted transport capability, vehicles or fleet-control arrangements, customs and transport licenses, dispatch and GPS records, personnel or decision-making capacity, bank activity, customer contracts, and commercially consistent risk allocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Administrative tax reassessment, late-payment interest, surcharges generally ranging from 10% to 80%, and criminal prosecution. Tax fraud may result in imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated cases potentially reaching seven years and EUR 3,000,000, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Managers and directors may incur criminal liability, tax-officer liability for fraudulent or persistently unpaid taxes, civil damages, asset seizure, and management bans. A corporate veil is not automatically pierced, but personal liability may follow from intentional misconduct, gross mismanagement, fraud, or commingling. Travel restrictions are not automatic and require a specific judicial or criminal basis.
VAT fraud and carousel-fraud exposure

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, interest, penalties potentially up to 80% for fraudulent conduct, refusal of input-tax recovery, joint liability in statutory cases, and criminal sanctions for aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for intentional participation or facilitation, face asset seizure and management restrictions, and bear civil or tax liability where personal misconduct is established.
Customs and bonded-transport violations

Statutory sanction: Customs duties, import VAT, interest, administrative fines, seizure or forfeiture of goods and vehicles, suspension or withdrawal of customs authorizations, and possible criminal customs prosecution.

Director personal liability: Company officers and operational managers may be personally prosecuted where they ordered, knowingly permitted, or negligently facilitated violations. Customs guarantees and personal or corporate financial liability may be called upon.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Income reassessment, withholding-tax exposure, interest, penalties, documentation penalties, and possible denial of deductions. Failure to provide required documentation may produce a presumptive or adverse assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil and tax liability for deliberate concealment or abusive arrangements. Criminal liability generally requires proof of intentional fraud or participation.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, treaty benefits, parent-subsidiary exemption, interest deductions, or tax credits; recharacterization of income; reassessment and anti-abuse penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for fraudulent management, tax evasion, insolvency misconduct, or asset diversion. Management bans and personal restitution orders may arise; travel bans are case-specific and not an automatic consequence.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing breaches

Statutory sanction: Regulatory warnings, injunctions, administrative fines, professional-license consequences, account restrictions, and criminal sanctions where money laundering or terrorist financing is established.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally liable for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or serious compliance failures. Money-laundering convictions may result in imprisonment, fines, confiscation, and judicial restrictions.
Profit repatriation and withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, interest, penalties, denial of treaty relief, and possible penalties for inaccurate or missing reporting.

Director personal liability: The legal representative may face personal financial liability in cases of fraudulent withholding-tax avoidance, deliberate non-compliance, or serious management fault, in addition to corporate liability.
Employment, payroll, and undeclared labor violations

Statutory sanction: Backdated social contributions, surcharges, labor penalties, loss of public support, administrative closure, and criminal sanctions for concealed work or serious safety breaches.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for concealed work, wage fraud, or serious health and safety failures, with fines, imprisonment, management bans, and civil compensation.
Insolvency and asset-diversion misconduct

Statutory sanction: Clawback of transactions, extension of liabilities to managers in cases of management fault, bankruptcy or liquidation sanctions, asset recovery, and possible criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Courts may impose contribution to insufficient assets, personal restitution, management bans, director disqualification, and imprisonment for fraudulent bankruptcy or asset concealment. Travel bans require a specific judicial order.
Corporate and accounting-record falsification

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, accounting and corporate-law penalties, evidentiary presumptions against the company, and criminal sanctions for forgery, use of forged documents, or obstruction of tax inspection.

Director personal liability: The managing director and responsible officers may face criminal prosecution, civil damages, asset seizure, management bans, and imprisonment where intentional falsification or obstruction is proven.
