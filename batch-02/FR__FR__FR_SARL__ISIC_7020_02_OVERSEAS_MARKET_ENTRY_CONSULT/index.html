Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the official portal at https://www.impots.gouv.fr/

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned entities operating as SARL in France must register with the DGFiP to obtain a SIREN number. Non-resident owners may appoint a French tax representative. Registration involves filing Form 1388-A or equivalent for VAT purposes, providing corporate documents, and obtaining approval. The SIREN serves as the unique tax identifier for corporate and VAT purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box elections available. However, the micro-entreprise regime is available for small businesses under specific turnover thresholds and conditions. France adheres to OECD BEPS guidelines for addressing hybrid mismatches through domestic legislation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

85,000 EUR annual turnover threshold for VAT registration in most cases; reduced thresholds may apply for intra-Community supplies

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT rate applies to exports of goods from France and to services performed outside France and invoiced to non-French customers. Eligibility requires verifiable proof of export or performance abroad, including customs documentation for goods and evidence of service delivery and payment for services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane d'Exportation - DAU)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificat de Règlement Intérieur) confirming receipt of payment from abroad

Mandatory electronic invoicing (facturation électronique) for all B2B transactions since October 2024, requiring real-time transmission of invoice data to the French tax authorities through certified platforms or APIs, with digital time-stamping (horodatage fiscal) mandatory.

Claims of zero-rating must be substantiated; fraudulent or unsubstantiated claims may lead to penalties of 40% of the VAT due, interest surcharges, and potential criminal liability. Retrospective adjustments and clawbacks apply upon audit if documentation is insufficient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France offers full current and capital account convertibility under the eurosystem framework and OECD liberalisation commitments. A French SARL may receive foreign currency proceeds, hold multi-currency accounts, and remit dividends to non-resident shareholders without prior exchange-control approval. Dividend remittance to non-EU shareholders may attract withholding tax unless reduced or eliminated by the relevant double taxation treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. No exchange control barriers apply to trade-related current payments or funding flows relating to market-entry consultancy contracts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio exists for French export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or foreign currency at a French-authorised credit institution without any minimum repatriation timetable. For accounting and tax reporting, foreign currency balances are converted to EUR at the applicable period-end exchange rate under French GAAP and IFRS rules; for balance-of-payments statistics, the receiving bank must report certain euro-area and non-euro-area flows to the Banque de France.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) and the European Central Bank, administers foreign exchange policy and maintains the monetary and macro-prudential framework for euro-area operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Moderate for auxiliary cross-border disbursement and FX conversion; Low for primary operating account and capital escrow functions. Legal constraint: an offshore EMI must hold a valid e-money institution licence or passport through the European Economic Area under the Second Payment Services Directive (PSD2) to provide payment services to a French SARL without triggering unauthorised activity. Non-EEA EMIs without a French/EEA licence cannot lawfully offer commercial account services in France and should be avoided for treasury-critical flows.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Reduced rate of 15% applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying small companies with turnover below EUR 10 million and 25%+ individual ownership.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory dividend withholding rate (prélèvement forfaitaire unique equivalent) for non-resident shareholders, unless reduced by an applicable DTA. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive can reduce this to 0% for a qualifying corporate parent holding at least 10% of the SARL's capital for 24 consecutive months.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months of the fiscal year-end. For calendar-year taxpayers the usual deadline is 3 May, shifted to the next business day if 3 May falls on a weekend or French public holiday.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 group consolidated turnover or balance-sheet total (CGI Article L. 13 AA) triggers mandatory full French TP documentation (Local File, Master File). A simplified TP documentation duty applies when cross-border related-party transactions exceed EUR 1,000,000 during the financial year. Country-by-Country reporting is separately required for groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute in the Anglo-Gulf sense. However, the administration enforces substance through: (a) the permanent establishment attribution test under CGI Article 209(I); (b) abuse of law (abus de droit) under Article L. 64 LPF; and (c) beneficial ownership and substance requirements contained in French DTAs and the EU anti-tax-avoidance directives. A French SARL claiming treaty benefits, patent-box relief or EU Parent-Subsidiary exemption must demonstrate real premises, staff, business decisions, and risk management in France. A shell SARL without any operational substance will be denied the reduced DTA rates and CIT credits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declaration (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: CIT reassessment plus 80% penalty and late interest of 0.20% per month; criminal prosecution under CGI Article 1741 carries EUR 500,000 criminal fine and 5 years imprisonment; additional false-accounting sanctions under Article 1743 may impose EUR 500,000 fine and directors' disqualification.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed fraudulent returns or orchestrated the concealment are jointly and severally liable under CGI Article 1750. Courts routinely impose personal asset forfeiture, 5-year directorship bans, travel bans and, in aggravated cases, immediate imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Establishment

Statutory sanction: A sham company is recharacterised under the abuse-of-law procedure (Article L. 64 LPF), resulting in dismissal of all tax reliefs granted, repayment of claimed credits, and an abusive-tax-scheme penalty of 80% where the primary purpose is tax avoidance. Administrative fines for missing legal substance documents and failing to maintain registered office condition can reach EUR 50,000.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who permitted the use of the corporate vehicle for abusive tax planning may be declared personally liable for unpaid taxes and interest under LPF Article L. 267. The French commercial court may also issue a déclaration de cessation des paiements, leading to an interdiction de gérer and personal travel ban.
Anti-Money Laundering / Tax Compliance Gap

Statutory sanction: French AMF supervision under Code Monétaire et Financier Article L. 561-15 allows administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 15% of the company's own funds for legal persons. Unreported foreign bank accounts trigger automatic taxation of undeclared assets plus a penalty equal to 12.5% of the undeclared account balance.

Director personal liability: Business owners face 5 years imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine for money-laundering offences under Article 324-1 of the Criminal Code. Directors may be placed under judicial control, have French bank accounts frozen, and be prohibited from conducting commercial activities during the investigation.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may re-characterise transaction prices and reassess CIT; failure to produce TP documentation upon audit attracts a fixed penalty equal to 5% of the reassessed amount, compounded by the 40% bad-faith penalty and late interest of 0.20% per month. In a major cross-border manipulation, the CbC and Master File penalty is an additional EUR 20,000 per missing notification.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved one-sided prices without an arm's-length benchmark can be held personally liable for the resulting corporate tax deficiency if the transaction is found to be an abuse of civil personality or company interest. Persistent TP violations trigger professional disqualification, seizure of personal assets, and a travel ban pending completion of the tax audit.
