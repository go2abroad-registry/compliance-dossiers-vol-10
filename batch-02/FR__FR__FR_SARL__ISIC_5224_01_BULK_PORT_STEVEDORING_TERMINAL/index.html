Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital: founders freely fix the capital, including a nominal floor of EUR 1. Where the subscribed capital is below EUR 100,000 it must be fully paid up at incorporation; where equal to or above EUR 100,000, at least 20% must be deposited at incorporation and the balance within five years. All paid-up funds must be placed in escrow on a blocked French bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and technical inspection issued by the local fire service (SDIS) for terminal buildings and electrical installations; structural load inspection and load-bearing certification of quays, pavements, and gantry/crane foundations by an approved engineering surveyor; occupational workplace clearance under the Code du travail validated by the DREETS, including the plan de prévention for mixed stevedoring operations, mandatory worker health clearance by the occupational health service, and the issuance of stevedoring work permits for quayside personnel.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is within the eurozone; the euro is unconditionally and fully convertible on both current and capital accounts pursuant to EU Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63-66 and the French Code Monétaire et Financier. No residual exchange-control authorization is required for capital inflows, profit remittances, dividend repatriation, loan servicing, or liquidation distributions to non-resident shareholders. The only constraints are statistical: all cross-border transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be captured in the Banque de France BDP reporting, which the resident banking intermediary files as 'déclarant relais' automatically. Dividend outbound remittances are subject to French withholding tax at the default statutory rate of 25% (or reduced treaty/EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive rate where applicable), but no currency convertibility or prior-approval impediment exists. Profit repatriation is frictionless from an FX-control standpoint; the only practical hurdle is corporate-tax clearance (attestation de régularité fiscale) sometimes requested by banks for large one-off remittances, which can be resolved via the DGFiP tax-clearance certificate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender obligation exists in France. Since the complete liberalization of foreign-exchange controls (effective 1990), French-resident exporters, including stevedoring and port-terminal entities, are not required to repatriate, convert, or surrender export proceeds within any statutory timeline. Receipts from stevedoring services, dock charges, handling fees, and transshipment revenues invoiced in foreign currency may be retained in foreign-currency bank accounts (comptes en devises) held with authorized credit institutions, provided that each transaction exceeding EUR 12,500 is identified in BDP declarations submitted by the account-holding bank. No conversion ratio applies; the operator may freely choose to convert EUR or hold the original currency for future disbursements. Non-resident-owned French SARLs are equally exempt from any surrender or timing rule after capital is lawfully booked on the balance sheet.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), exercising delegated foreign-exchange policy functions under the European Central Bank's single monetary policy framework; cross-border financial reporting is administered by the Banque de France's balance-of-payments (BDP) directorate, with customs-side trade statistics managed by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary treasury functions (payroll, petty cash, local third-party payables), Low for primary port-stevedoring clearing and statutory trade settlements. Legal constraints: only ACPR-licensed établissements de monnaie électronique or EEA-passported electronic-money institutions may lawfully serve French-resident merchant entities; such EMIs cannot grant credit, issue guarantees, or hold client funds as deposits, and all safeguarded funds must be ring-fenced. For ISIC 5224 bulk-port stevedoring operations, the mandatory statutory license, high counterparty volumes, documentary trade settlement (bills of lading, port-authority tariffs, customs manifests), and the need for MT103/MT700 rails render domestic tier-1 bank clearing essential; offshore non-EEA digital EMIs are effectively barred from opening French payment accounts without a full ACPR passport, and most tier-1 platforms will not route port-authority disbursements through unauthorised EMIs. EMIs may be used only as an ancillary top-up rail, never as the primary clearing channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: equity subscriptions and shareholder loans must enter France exclusively through an established credit institution via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT when originating within EEA). All inbound amounts exceeding EUR 12,500 are automatically incorporated into the receiving bank's BDP declaration to Banque de France; no separate administrative declaration is required of the SARL, but the bank will request a 'nature de paiement' code mapping. Recommended BDP nature codes: for share capital — code 'A3.2 Apports en capital social'; for shareholder current-account advances — 'Prêts d'actionnaires / avances en compte courant d'associés'; for subordinated loans — 'Autres prêts entre sociétés apparentées'. The SARL must present: (a) Kbis extract issued within the last three months; (b) board/shareholder meeting minutes authorizing the capital call; (c) subscription certificates or loan agreement executed by all parties; (d) source-of-funds documentation (bank statements, audit reports, parent resolutions); (e) if funds are used for concession-required infrastructure, the port-authority concession contract. Funds can be credited to a temporary escrow ('compte de construction') and released only against procurement invoices and payroll, or directly to the operational account upon bank validation. Prohibited: cash or non-banking channels (e.g., crypto or unlicensed payment aggregators) for any capital injection.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 for all cross-border payments (shipowners, foreign agents, international equipment suppliers, subcontracted stevedores outside France). Domestic French disbursements (port-authority dockage, harbour light dues, local labour, social charges) clear via SEPA Credit Transfer (MT103-equivalent) or SEPA Instant to the Grand Port Maritime account. Documentary trade settlements for equipment or demurrage claims should use SWIFT MT700 (Commercial Letter of Credit) where the foreign counterparty requires a bank undertaking; standby LCs for performance bonds under stevedoring contracts are issued via MT760. Remittance messages must include the French BIC/IBAN pair, the invoice number and port-call reference, IMO number of the vessel, and clearly state the nature of service (stevedoring, lashing, shore crane hire, storage). The bank will dual-tag the payment for both DGDDI trade-data capture (if customs-related statistic threshold applies) and Banque de France BDP classification; stevedoring services are classified under the BOP services code for 'transport auxiliaire / manutention portuaire'. Settlement currency may be EUR, USD, CNY, or any freely convertible currency; payments in restricted currencies require a correspondent bank capable of executing the conversion and may need a confirmed EUR/USD bridge.Plain-vanilla FX spot and outright FX forwards (termes de change) on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF, EUR/CNY, EUR/BRL and other deliverable pairs — freely permitted without underlying trade documentation for the hedge amount, but banks require a signed ISDA/EMIR-mandated documentation suite.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — permitted where the stevedoring revenue is denominated in a non-deliverable or lightly convertible currency (e.g., West African CFA franc corridor via Paris interbank; selectively for certain EMEA/APAC currencies), provided the bank can evidence a genuine underlying receivables exposure and the NDF is subject to EMIR margin exchange for non-cleared OTC derivatives above the clearing threshold.Cross-currency swaps (simples changes / swaps de devises) — permissible for SARL treasury management of matched EUR/USD or EUR/CNY asset-liability flows, particularly for port equipment financing or concession-fee payment schedules; requires ISDA 2002 Master Agreement, Credit Support Annex, and compliance with EMIR reporting via a registered trade repository.FX options (vanilla European call/put on EUR) — permissible as overlay hedging for confirmed port-terminal receivables; subject to MiFID II suitability assessment and additional capital treatment under French banking practice; not recommended as primary instrument for a mid-sized SARL treasury without a dedicated corporate FX desk.
