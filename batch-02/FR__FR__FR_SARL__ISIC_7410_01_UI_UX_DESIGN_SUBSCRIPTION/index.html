Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 to R.223-31 concerning the constitution, management and operation of a Société à Responsabilité Limitée; complemented by Articles 1832 and 1835 to 1864 of the French Civil Code (Code civil) on the contract of company.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SARL. The articles must fix the capital amount; all shares must be fully subscribed and cash contributions fully paid up before registration. Cash capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account and released only upon issuance of the Kbis extract.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection and record (registre de sécurité) required for premises open to the public; structural load verification by a certified technical inspector if the building receives public access; electrical installation conformity certificate issued by CONSUEL or an accredited inspection body; completion of the Dossier Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP) and occupational medical clearance for employees.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for an SARL. The articles must fix the capital amount; all shares must be fully subscribed and cash contributions fully paid up before registration. Cash capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account and released only upon issuance of the Kbis extract.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — Online portal: impots.gouv.fr; Business incorporation and initial tax identification portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; VAT identifiers administered by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE).

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL: register the company through formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr with the electronic single form (CERFA modified process); the competent company formality center transmits the file to the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE), the commercial court registry (Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce), and the DGFiP. Upon incorporation, the company obtains a SIREN (unique company identifier), SIRET number, and is automatically registered for corporate tax. The DGFiP subsequently issues the French intra-community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) when the company becomes subject to VAT. For a non-resident entity without a permanent establishment, direct application to the local SIE in France is required to obtain a French VAT number and appropriate TIN. The application is made on a French VAT registration form (Cerfa 1529-sd) supported by identity documents, proof of foreign registration, and details of French commercial activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) as a separate tax entity. There is no general check-the-box mechanism under French law. Statutory elections include: (i) option for partnership/flow-through taxation for a family SARL under Article 239 bis AA of the French General Tax Code (CGI), available where all partners are natural persons in the same family; (ii) option for tax-consolidated group treatment (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for groups of 95% or more controlled French entities; (iii) elections applicable under EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules where France applies provisions preventing hybrid mismatch deductions, but no elective check-the-box treatment exists. For small innovative SARLs, a reduced tax rate or loss-carry-forward eligibility may apply, but the default remains entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for a France-established SARL; registration is required upon any taxable economic activity, regardless of turnover. A small-business exemption ('franchise en base de TVA') applies to service providers whose annual turnover does not exceed EUR 37,500 (2025 threshold; higher threshold of EUR 85,000 for goods trading), but this exemption is a relief and does not remove the obligation to establish the company with French tax authorities; it may also require opening rights through the SIE. For non-French suppliers supplying services to French customers, immediate VAT registration is generally required without a de minimis threshold unless the EU OSS/OSS regime applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods: French VAT is chargeable at 0% under Article 262-I CGI when goods are physically delivered from France to a destination outside the EU, provided the supplier retains documentary proof that the goods left the EU within 90 days of delivery. Services supplied by a French business: B2B services supplied to a recipient outside France are generally taxable at the place of supply of the recipient (Article 259-1 B CGI), and therefore French VAT is not chargeable (effective 0% French VAT), provided the service is subject to the laws of the recipient country and the supplier obtains two independent pieces of evidence of the recipient's establishment. B2C services to non-EU customers may also fall outside French VAT if the place of supply is determined as outside France under cross-border rules (Articles 258-A and 259-D CGI). Digital design/subscription services qualify for the 'offshore services' treatment when the recipient is established outside France (EU or non-EU) and the recipient is taxable in its own jurisdiction; supporting documentation must substantiate the customer's tax ID, address, and business activity.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation / DAE) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, CMR or other transport document evidencing departure from France and arrival in a non-EU jurisdiction
  • Commercial invoice quoting the non-EU destination and stating the ‘Taxe sur la Valeur Ajoutée non applicable – Exportation hors Union Européenne’ exemption
  • Signed contract or purchase order with the non-EU customer and supplier delivery records
  • Customer confirmation receipt (Recapitulatif client / attestation de destination) for goods or services
  • Bank account statements and inward remittance certificate from the French bank evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Intra-EU summary declaration (Échange de Biens – DEB) or sales listing (état récapitulatif) where required for intra-EU supplies

France is transitioning to compulsory electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions. From 1 September 2026, all companies must be able to receive e-invoices, and large enterprises must issue them electronically; from 1 September 2027, small and medium-sized enterprises (including SARLs) must issue e-invoices. E-invoicing must be transmitted through the public portal Chorus Pro or through an accredited Private Digital Platform (PDP) using the dual-flow model. E-reporting of B2C and international transactions is also mandatory. There is no continuous real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping at the national tax authority; instead, mandatory structured digital formats are transmitted on a near-real-time basis, with penalties applicable to non-compliant invoicing.

The French tax administration (DGFiP) may apply a standard VAT assessment period of three years (extendable to six years in the case of undisclosed activity or foreign taxpayers). Fraudulent export zero-rating claims result in immediate recovery of unpaid VAT plus late payment interest (currently 0.45% per month), a 40% penalty for deliberate misstatement, and an 80% penalty for organized tax fraud or fraudulent tax maneuvers. Criminal sanctions may apply for falsifying export documents, including a fine of EUR 50,000 and imprisonment in addition to civil clawbacks. For services zero-rating, a failure to establish documentary proof outside France within a reasonable period (normally up to 180 days) exposes the exporter to VAT reassessment on the full proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. As an EU Member State, France applies the free movement of capital under Article 63 TFEU and does not impose general exchange controls, prior licensing for ordinary profit remittances, or registration requirements for dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders. Profit repatriation is achieved through lawful corporate distributions after statutory audit/approval and is subject to the standard 30% flat withholding tax (or a lower treaty rate under income tax treaties). The only material qualification is the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) screening regime under Articles L151-3 et seq. and R153-9 of the French Monetary and Financial Code, which requires prior ministerial authorization for investments in sensitive sectors such as defence, cybersecurity, critical infrastructure, artificial intelligence with dual-use application, and other strategically protected activities. A UI/UX design subscription agency classified under ISIC 7410 / NAICS 541430 does not fall within that protected perimeter; therefore no prior authorization is required for non-resident capital injection. Balance-of-payments reporting obligations apply for statistical purposes only.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline. Export invoices may be denominated in any currency freely agreed between the parties. The only compliance duties are: (i) balance-of-payments reporting to Banque de France via the intermediary credit institution for each cross-border transfer at or above EUR 50,000 (or monthly aggregate reporting at the same threshold); (ii) completion of the Banque de France 'green form' declaration when payments are made without bank intermediation; and (iii) a cash declaration to French customs for physical banknotes and negotiable instruments of EUR 10,000 or more transported across the border. There is no obligation to convert export proceeds into euros.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is the central bank and national authority for balance-of-payments statistics and monetary stability. The Direction Generale du Tresor (French Treasury) administers foreign-exchange policy and the foreign-investment screening regime. The Autorite de Controle Prudentiel et de Resolution (ACPR) licenses and supervises credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions under the EU CRD, PSD2 and EMD frameworks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High as a complementary treasury and collections rail, but not a complete substitute for tier-1 bank accounts. Qonto (French electronic money institution licensed by ACPR), Wise Europe S.A. (Irish-authorised EMI passporting into France under PSD2) and Stripe Billing (French payment institution) can onboard the low-risk UI/UX design subscription company remotely within 24-48 hours, provide a French or European EUR IBAN, execute SEPA Credit Transfers, receive inbound SWIFT MT103 payments, and facilitate multi-currency conversions at competitive rates. Legal constraints: EMIs and payment institutions are not credit institutions; client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts under Article L.526-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code and are not covered by the French deposit guarantee fund (FGDR). EMIs cannot grant overdrafts, working-capital loans, trade finance, or standby letters of credit, and they are subject to restrictions on investment activities. For this entity, the optimal structure is: tier-1 bank for capital escrow, operational treasury, and credit facilities; Stripe Billing as the principal card/subscription collection rail; and Wise for cost-efficient cross-border disbursements and FX execution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the formation of the SARL, shareholders must pay their capital contributions in euros into a blocked capital-deposit account (compte de depot de capital) held either with a French credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Depots et Consignations. The depositary issues the certificat de depot des fonds, which is attached to the M0 application for registration at the Registre du Commerce et des Societes (Greffe). Upon issuance of the Kbis, the blocked account is released and the capital is transferred to the SARL operational account. For a non-resident shareholder injecting funds from abroad, the compliant routing is an inbound SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's regulated bank, retaining the ISO 20022 UETR reference and using the Banque de France balance-of-payments code for direct investment (capital subscription or paid-in equity); the wire narrative must identify the company name, the words 'apport en numeraire', and the reference to the blocked capital account. The receiving bank will apply standard funds-verification and, for transfers at or above EUR 50,000, the transaction will be captured in the balance-of-payments reporting to Banque de France. No prior ministerial authorization is required because the UI/UX design subscription business is not in a protected strategic sector; however, if the foreign shareholder holds sensitive corporate characteristics, the bank may request an FDI statement and additional UBO documentation.Routine trade settlements with EU beneficiaries should be executed through SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for same-day delivery; an SWIFT MT103 is unnecessary within the SEPA zone. For non-EU counterparties such as UK, US, and Asian subcontractors, licensors, cloud providers, and design freelancers, the appropriate clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 with gpi tracking (including UETR) with the invoice reference and a purpose code consistent with cross-border services. For larger enterprise contracts above approximately EUR 300,000, a vendor may request a documentary commercial letter of credit (sight LC issued via SWIFT MT700) or a standby letter of credit (SWIFT MT760) securing the SARL's subscription or milestone-payment obligations; these instruments are available from BNP Paribas or Societe Generale against the client subscription-receivable portfolio with invoice-assignment collateral. All outbound service payments must be supported by a signed service agreement, a compliant invoice, a transfer-purpose annotation, and, where the aggregate transfers to one counterparty exceed EUR 50,000 per month, the bank will include the flows in the Banque de France balance-of-payments file. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High as a complementary treasury and collections rail, but not a complete substitute for tier-1 bank accounts. Qonto (French electronic money institution licensed by ACPR), Wise Europe S.A. Legal constraints: EMIs and payment institutions are not credit institutions; client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts under Article L.526-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code and are not covered by the French deposit guarantee fund (FGDR). EMIs cannot grant overdrafts, working-capital loans, trade finance, or standby letters of credit, and they are subject to restrictions on investment activities. For this entity, the optimal structure is: tier-1 bank for capital escrow, operational treasury, and credit facilities; Stripe Billing as the principal card/subscription collection rail; and Wise for cost-efficient cross-border disbursements and FX execution.
