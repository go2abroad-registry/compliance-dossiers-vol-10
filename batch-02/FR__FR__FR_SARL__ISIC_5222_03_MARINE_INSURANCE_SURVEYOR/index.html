Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The law imposes no prescribed minimum capital; the articles may fix capital at any positive amount. Cash contributions must be paid up to at least one-fifth at subscription, with the balance due within five years of registration, unless a higher early payment is chosen. Prior to registration, cash capital must be deposited in a blocked account with a bank, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary; funds are released after the company is registered.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a fire safety certificate (avis de conformité / commission de sécurité) from the préfecture or an approved inspection body for any premises accessible to staff or the public. Where survey equipment is stored, verify the structure's load-bearing capacity and maintain an inspection register. Register the workplace with the occupational health service, obtain medical fitness certificates for employees, submit the mandatory prior hiring declarations to URSSAF, and perform periodic workplace safety evaluations and health and safety training.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances; official portal: impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

For an FR SARL, the statutory TIN is assigned upon incorporation through the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE), filed via guichet-entreprises.fr. The company receives a SIREN, SIRET and code APE/NAF, and is automatically registered for French VAT by the DGFiP, which allocates a Numéro de TVA Intracommunautaire (format FR + SIREN). Foreign entities not established in France must submit Form SI to the DGFiP and, for non-EU entities, appoint an accredited tax representative; supporting documents include constitutive acts, proof of address and business purpose.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For an FR SARL, the statutory TIN is assigned upon incorporation through the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE), filed via guichet-entreprises.fr. The company receives a SIREN, SIRET and code APE/NAF, and is automatically registered for French VAT by the DGFiP, which allocates a Numéro de TVA Intracommunautaire (format FR + SIREN). Foreign entities not established in France must submit Form SI to the DGFiP and, for non-EU entities, appoint an accredited tax representative; supporting documents include constitutive acts, proof of address and business purpose.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box election. An SARL is by default subject to entity-level French corporate income tax (IS). Under CGI Article 239 bis AA, a 'SARL de famille' composed exclusively of natural persons may elect, for a limited renewable period, to be treated as a tax-transparent partnership taxed at the personal income tax (IR) level. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under ATAD are mandatory, not elective, and are implemented through Articles 205 A et seq. CGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Service providers: €37,500 annual turnover; goods suppliers: €85,000 annual turnover (franchise en base, CGI Art. 293 B). Exceeding the relevant threshold, or the two-year rolling average, triggers mandatory VAT registration and invoicing obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% TVA) applies to goods physically transported outside the EU, subject to proof of export under CGI Art. 262 ter I. Marine cargo survey and inspection services supplied to non-resident underwriters qualify as outside the scope of French VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule (CGI Art. 259-1) where the customer is established outside France. Services directly connected to exported goods may also be zero-rated if the services are performed on or in relation to those goods and are evidenced by export documentation. The claimant must demonstrate the non-French establishment of the customer and the direct link to the export transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / Electronic Export Declaration) validated by French Customs
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR evidencing transport out of the European Union
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods or services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/ bank transfer confirmation proving receipt of export proceeds
  • Marine surveyor's damage inspection report or certificate of insurance loss
  • Customer's written confirmation of non-French establishment, including VAT registration number where available

Under the French e-invoicing reform, from 1 September 2026 all French VAT-registered businesses must receive structured electronic invoices and large/mid-size businesses must issue them; from 1 September 2027 small businesses, including SARLs, must issue e-invoices. Transmission must occur through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) using structured formats (FACTUR-X, UBL). Transaction data and VAT reporting must be transmitted to DGFiP in real time or periodically via PPF APIs; there is no separate digital stamping, but continuous transaction controls apply.

The DGFiP may reassess erroneous zero-rating claims within three years (extended to six years in cases of fraud or obstruction). Improperly claimed zero-rating is clawed back together with late-payment interest at 0.2% per month and penalties of 40% for negligence or 80% for bad faith or dissimulation under CGI Articles 1728 and 1729. Fraudulent export claims may also trigger criminal penalties under CGI Article 1741, including fines up to €75,000 and imprisonment of up to five years. The taxpayer bears the burden of substantiating the export and the non-French status of the customer, and must prove timely repatriation of foreign currency proceeds within the 180-day deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member with full current-account and capital-account convertibility in euros. Capital movements, payments and transfers inside the EU and with third countries are unrestricted under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles L. 511-1 and R. 151-1 et seq. of the CMF, subject only to EU/UN sanctions, temporary safeguard measures, and the foreign-investment screening regime (Code monétaire et financier, Article L. 151-3) for strategic sectors, which do not capture marine cargo surveying activities as currently classified. Profit repatriation is lawful and receives no surrender or conversion constraint; practical hurdles are documentary rather than convertibility barriers, including FATCA/CRS declarations, KYC on beneficial owners, and balance-of-payments reporting through the intermediary credit institution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or forced repatriation deadline applicable to export receipts in France. EUR proceeds arising in France need no declaration as such; foreign-currency proceeds (e.g. USD, GBP) may be retained in designated FX accounts at a licensed credit institution. However, all cross-border payments above the administrative information threshold must be reported by the intermediate French bank to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics under Articles R. 152-1-1 to R. 152-1-5 of the CMF, and parties holding foreign accounts or executing high-value transactions must maintain documentary support of the commercial underlying.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) under the European System of Central Banks; prudential supervision is exercised through the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution); foreign exchange and balance-of-payments regulatory reporting is collected and validated by Banque de France under the framework of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, CMF) and delegated EU powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for auxiliary receipts, Low for core treasury. A French-licensed electronic money institution under ACPR/PSD2 may provide operational payment accounts for low-value surveyor fee collection, but cannot replace a banking license for credit facilities, capital escrow deposits, or complex trade settlement. Non-EU digital EMIs (including Wise and Payoneer) are legally usable for inbound professional fees from foreign underwriters but present elevated KYC and clearing risk, cannot accept escrow deposits, and lack Banque de France BOP reporting responsibility, shifting documentary burden to the SARL. Use of EMIs should be limited to complementary collection rails while tier-1 bank accounts remain the primary treasury spine.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for SARL capital injection uses SEPA Credit Transfer via TARGET2 for EEA-origin EUR funds and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA or hard-currency funds, with the following control points: the remitter must transmit from its own bank account with matching IBAN; the reference field carries the corporate contract or share-subscription reference; the receiving bank requires the SARL's LEI, SIREN/SIRET, EORI-VAT number, and standard settlement instructions. The Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment capital contributions is applied by the bank under the foreign-direct-investment statistical category (capital-in-kind/subscribed capital), and the account designation must clearly flag the temporary blocked status mandated for SARL formation under Articles L. 223-7 and R. 223-3 of the French Commercial Code (Code de commerce).EUR trade settlements up to standard thresholds are cleared through SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and, for urgent low-value disbursements or underwriting advance payments, SEPA Instant; high-value euro transfers are settled via TARGET2. For non-EEA trade disbursements, SWIFT MT103 with correspondent cover through MT202 is the standard corporate banking rail. Documentary trade settlements for marine cargo claims and surveyor services are executed under Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) using MT700 issuance and MT754 acceptance; an Incoterms-aligned and contract-referenced invoice package, certificate of survey, loss-adjustment statement, and bank-compliant transport-document set must accompany each drawing. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding on French-source dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders (Article 119 bis 2 CGI); 12.8% for non-resident individual shareholders, excluding the effect of any additional social levies. Distributions require a shareholder resolution and must be reported in the annual 2065 return.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, filing must occur on or before 3 May of the following year, adjusted to the next business day if that date falls on a weekend or public holiday. For any other fiscal year-end, the deadline is the second business day following the expiry of the three-month period after the close of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 (French entity annual revenue or gross fixed assets triggering Local File and Master File obligations under Article L13 AA LPF); OECD CbC Report threshold is EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue. SMEs (under the EU definition) are exempt from formal TP documentation but must still comply with the arm's-length principle.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate an offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code. Substance is nevertheless decisive through the abuse-of-law procedure (Article L.64 LPF), the sham/simulation doctrine, Article 57 CGI recharacterisation of intercompany arrangements, the EU Parent-Subsidiary and Interest & Royalties anti-abuse clauses, and the EU ATAD/Unshell (ATAD 3) initiatives. A marine insurance surveyor SARL must demonstrate genuine management, qualified personnel, premises, client contracts, professional indemnity cover and local decision-making to preserve treaty and EU directive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude fiscale

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under Article 1741 CGI; aggravated forms (organised gang or combined with laundering) carry up to EUR 1,500,000 fine and 7 years' imprisonment; civil reassessment with an 80% bad-faith penalty plus 0.20% monthly late interest.

Director personal liability: The gérant may be jointly liable for the company's evasion penalties under Article L.267 LPF; criminal conviction triggers interdiction de gérer, travel bans, freezing of personal assets and possible extradition exposure.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under Article 155 A CGI and the abuse-of-law doctrine (Article L.64 LPF) with an 80% penalty and denial of treaty and EU directive benefits; the tax administration may also apply the 75% withholding on payments to non-cooperative jurisdictions.

Director personal liability: The gérant who created or managed the artificial arrangement may be held personally liable for the restated tax debt; a criminal court can impose an interdiction décennale de gérer and sanctions for organised tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing / Non-Arm's-Length Foreign Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Article 57 CGI permits recharacterisation of profits transferred abroad, giving rise to additional corporate income tax, a 40% bad-faith penalty and monthly interest; failure to present the TP file within 30 days of a request adds a fine of up to 10% of the reassessed tax under Article 1735 ter A CGI.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly executed the mispricing risks criminal complicity in tax evasion, personal recovery of the reassessment under the abuse-of-law and management-fault regime (Article L.223-22 Code de commerce), and suspension from commercial management.
AML / Laundering of Illicit Insurance Claim Proceeds

Statutory sanction: Money laundering of insurance fraud proceeds is punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine under Article 324-1 Code pénal; aggravated laundering by an organised gang or through the company's professional activity carries up to 10 years and EUR 750,000, plus confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: The gérant and signing surveyors face criminal conviction, removal from the maritime experts register, civil damages to underwriters, seizure of personal and corporate property, and automatic commercial disqualification under Article L.653-8 Code de commerce.
