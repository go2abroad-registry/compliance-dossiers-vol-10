Name reservation / lock period

France does not provide a pre-incorporation name reservation or a standard 30-day exclusivity window. If the Greffe or INPI rejects the submitted corporate name because it is identical to an existing registered name, misleading, contrary to public order, or contains a protected token, the applicant must revise the name and resubmit the incorporation file through the Guichet Unique des Formalités. There is no administrative appeal to a name registry; the applicant may seek judicial review before the commercial court within the applicable limitation period if the registry refusal is arbitrary or abusive. Early name clearance is achieved by a conflict search in INFOGREFFE/RCS, INPI trademark databases and the EUTM database.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for SAS; shareholders freely determine capital in the Articles of Association. Paid-up capital must be fully subscribed and either deposited in a blocked escrow account at a French bank (Caisse des Dépôts) or with a notary prior to registration, and released upon issuance of the registration certificate (extrait Kbis).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification obtained through the Commission de Sécurité (prefecture section) verifying the installation of fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and a tested fire alarm system in compliance with the Code du Travail occupational safety regulations; structural load inspection conducted by a certified building control office for any heavy duty test benches and overhead cranes; workplace clearance certificate issued by the Inspector du Travail confirming compliance with mandatory workplace ventilation, machine guarding (CE marking on all machining equipment), ergonomic workstations, and worker training records on hydraulic system pressure hazards.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - public portal impots.gouv.fr; company incorporation and statutory formalities via Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); customs administration: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) - douane.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SAS (Société par Actions Simplifiée) by filing statutes and corporate documentation through the Guichet Unique; INSEE allocates the 9-digit SIREN and 14-digit SIRET, and the RCS registration produces the Kbis extract. Step 2: DGFiP assigns the French intra-Community VAT number (format FR + 2 control digits + SIREN) upon VAT registration. Step 3: A foreign enterprise without a French permanent establishment but making taxable supplies in France must register directly with the SIE Non-Résidents (Service des Impôts des Entreprises des Non-Résidents, Noisy-le-Grand) and appoint an accredited fiscal representative under Article 289-A CGI if established outside the EU. Step 4: For export operations, obtain an EORI number (FR-format) and create a DGDDI DELTA account for electronic customs filing. Step 5: Commission the VAT accounting in the normal real regime with monthly or quarterly returns (Form 3310-CA3) unless the simplified real regime is elected.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a check-the-box regime. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) option for territoriality of corporate income tax under Article 209-I CGI, limiting Impôt sur les Sociétés to French-source profits; (ii) intra-group fiscal integration under Article 223 A CGI for groups holding at least 95% of French subsidiaries; (iii) VAT grouping as a single taxable person under Article 1693 ter-0 bis CGI; (iv) election for the simplified real VAT regime (régime réel simplifié) under Article 302 septies CGI where turnover ceilings are met; (v) choice of non-calendar financial year-end in the bylaws; (vi) ATAD hybrid-mismatch compliance filings under Article 212 bis CGI governing payments through hybrid entities or instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-resident persons: any taxable supply made in France, intra-Community acquisition, importation, or distance sale into France triggers mandatory VAT registration under Article 286-I CGI. For domestic micro-enterprises only, the franchise de TVA exempts turnover up to EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services per year under Article 293 B CGI; this franchise is not available to an SAS engaged in export manufacturing nor to foreign-established taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility under French VAT: (i) definitive export of goods outside the European Union under Article 262-I-1° CGI, provided the goods physically leave French/EU territory within 3 months and the exporter holds adequate proof; (ii) intra-Community dispatch of goods to a purchaser identified for VAT in another Member State under Article 262 ter CGI, evidenced by the customer's valid EU VAT number and supported by the recapitulative declaration (DEB) and Intrastat filing; (iii) services directly related to exported goods under Article 258-A CGI; (iv) B2B services the place of supply of which falls outside France pursuant to Articles 259 and 259-A CGI, such as engineering, testing, design, and logistics services connected to high-pressure hydraulic valve production; (v) supplies into customs warehousing or free zones under suspension arrangements. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports remains fully creditable.

B2G e-invoicing via Chorus Pro has been mandatory since 2020. The B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting reform enacted by the 2023 Finance Act applies in phases: large enterprises from 1 September 2026, SMEs and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027. E-invoices must transit through a certified PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) or the PPF (Portail Public de Facturation) in structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII). Transaction-level data on B2B and B2C sales must be e-reported to DGFiP on a near-real-time basis through accredited platform APIs and file-based exchanges (REST/JSON, PEPPOL BIS). France does not operate a Latin American-style pre-clearance digital stamping system; instead, an immutable audit trail, electronic signature or unique invoice identifier, and automated transmission to DGFiP satisfy the fiscal clearance obligation.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to ex-post verification by DGFiP under the statutory audit regime (Articles L. 47 to L. 51 LPF). In the event of missing, false, or insufficient export evidence, the export exemption is overturned, output VAT is reassessed at 20%, corresponding input VAT is clawed back, and intérêts de retard (0.2% per month / 2.4% annually) are levied under Article 1727 CGI. Penalties escalate to 40% for bad faith (mauvaise foi) and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres under Article 1729 CGI. Customs fraud penalties under Articles 410 to 414 Code des Douanes include confiscation of goods, fines up to 3 times the value of the goods, and imprisonment of up to 3 years. Criminal tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI exposes the SAS and its legal representatives to fines up to EUR 500,000 (or EUR 3,000,000 and up to 7 years' imprisonment in aggravated cases), publication of sanctions, and loss of good fiscal standing. The ordinary reassessment limitation period is 3 years (Articles L. 169 and L. 186 LPF), extended to 10 years for undeclared activity or fraudulent conduct (Article L. 187 LPF), requiring retention of export documentation files for at least 11 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member with full capital account convertibility under EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63-66 TFEU). The euro is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance is unrestricted; dividends, royalties, and interest can be repatriated without prior authorization, subject to standard anti-money laundering (AML) and tax reporting obligations. No exchange control approval is required for cross-border transfers, but banks must report transactions exceeding €10,000 to the French Financial Intelligence Unit (Tracfin) for AML purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists for export proceeds in France. Exporters are free to hold foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts, subject to general banking and tax declaration rules. The EU's Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR) and French regulations impose no mandatory conversion or repatriation timeline. However, for statistical purposes, the Banque de France collects data on cross-border transactions via the 'Balance of Payments' reporting system (reporting thresholds apply for transactions above €50,000).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) acting under the authority of the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy; the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) administers foreign exchange controls under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. French-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine, and Lydia Solutions offer multi-currency accounts, SWIFT/SEPA rails, and competitive FX rates. Legal constraints: EMIs must be licensed by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) and are subject to the same AML/KYC obligations as banks. They cannot provide credit or hold client funds beyond e-money issuance. For cross-border liquidity, EMIs are viable for operational accounts but may lack full trade finance (LC) capabilities; a hybrid structure (EMI for daily operations + Tier-1 bank for LC/escrow) is recommended.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound capital injections (shareholder loans or equity contributions) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with accurate purpose codes. For equity contributions, use purpose code 'S010' (Direct Investment) or 'S020' (Capital Contribution) under the French Balance of Payments classification. For shareholder loans, use 'S030' (Financial Loan). Verification: The bank will require a copy of the board resolution approving the injection, the share subscription agreement, and a certificate of deposit from the French notary (if applicable). Transfers above €50,000 must be reported to the Bank of France via the 'Rapport de Change' system. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. A 15% reduced rate may apply to qualifying SMEs on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to revenue not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up share capital, and qualifying ownership conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to exemptions and treaty limits. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption may reduce withholding to 0% if the recipient is an eligible EU or EEA parent, satisfies minimum ownership and holding-period conditions, is subject to a qualifying corporate tax, and is not an abusive or conduit arrangement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending on a date other than 31 December, the tax return is generally due within three months after the fiscal year-end. For a 31 December year-end, the electronic filing deadline is generally the second business day following 1 May of the following year. The final corporate income tax balance is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, subject to the statutory electronic calendar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Comprehensive transfer-pricing documentation is generally mandatory where the French entity or French tax group has annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 150 million, or is controlled by or controls an entity meeting the threshold, subject to statutory conditions. The Local File and Master File must be prepared and available upon request. A simplified documentation requirement may apply below the threshold. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France has no single standalone economic substance regulation equivalent to certain offshore ESR regimes. Substance is enforced through the arm's-length principle, general anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, anti-conduit principles, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, EU anti-hybrid rules, withholding-tax anti-abuse provisions, and the principal-purpose test under applicable treaties. A French SAS should maintain real decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or operational infrastructure, risk control, contractual authority, and commercial evidence proportionate to its functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, and penalties generally ranging from 40% for deliberate conduct to 80% for fraudulent maneuvers or concealed activity. Criminal tax fraud may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated penalties of up to seven years and EUR 3 million in specified serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may incur criminal liability, civil liability for intentional or repeated tax misconduct, and personal liability for tax debts where their management fault made recovery impossible. Court restrictions on management may apply. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from judicial measures or criminal proceedings.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment under the arm's-length principle, interest, and penalties. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation may trigger a penalty generally equal to the greater of 0.5% of the amount of transactions covered by the missing documentation or 5% of the reassessed profits attributable to those transactions, subject to statutory minimums and limits.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil or criminal exposure where the arrangements involve intentional fraud, fictitious services, abuse of corporate assets, or obstruction of a tax audit. Ordinary TP adjustment alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Unlawful dividend or profit extraction

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax adjustment, withholding tax, penalties, repayment of unlawfully distributed amounts, and potential offences relating to fictitious dividends, abuse of corporate assets, or misuse of company property.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore unlawfully distributed amounts and may incur criminal liability for abuse of corporate assets or fictitious dividends. Management bans and imprisonment may apply depending on the offence and intent.
Withholding tax and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, interest, penalties, denial of treaty relief, and possible anti-abuse reassessment. The payer may remain liable even where the foreign recipient received the gross amount.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for intentional non-remittance, fraudulent documentation, or knowing participation in a conduit arrangement. Criminal exposure depends on the underlying fraud and evidence of intent.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, treaty benefits, exemptions, or tax residence claims; reassessment under anti-abuse rules; withholding tax recovery; interest; and penalties. Transactions may be recharacterized based on their actual substance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they knowingly implement abusive arrangements, conceal beneficial ownership, or misuse the company. Corporate personality is not automatically disregarded merely because the entity has foreign shareholders.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial ownership

Statutory sanction: Administrative, professional, and criminal sanctions may include fines, loss of authorization where applicable, confiscation, and imprisonment. Money laundering may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 375,000, with aggravated sanctions in specified circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may be prosecuted personally for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or negligent failures where a statutory duty applies. Asset freezing and confiscation may accompany criminal proceedings.
Customs, import VAT, and product misclassification

Statutory sanction: Recovery of customs duties and import VAT, customs penalties, seizure or confiscation, loss of simplified procedures, and potential criminal customs sanctions for deliberate fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal criminal or civil exposure for intentional customs fraud, false declarations, or organized evasion. Ordinary classification errors generally create corporate liability unless aggravated by misconduct.
Obstruction of tax audit and document destruction

Statutory sanction: Separate procedural penalties, estimated assessment, loss of evidentiary rights, increased tax penalties, and potential criminal prosecution for obstruction or document falsification.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers responsible for withholding or destroying records may incur personal criminal liability and management sanctions. Civil liability may arise for losses caused to the company, creditors, or tax administration.
