Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the Impôt Gestion Publique portal; business registration and VAT procedures are coordinated through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a SARL file the statutory incorporation and registration dossier via the Guichet Unique, which transmits to the CFE/INSEE to obtain SIREN and SIRET numbers. The entity then requests a TVA intracommunautaire number by completing Form 1585-1 (normal or simplified regime) through the local Centre des Finances Publiques; for non-EU residents, a tax representative may be required under Article 326 of the CGI appendix. Inward suppliers of B2B transport services may need simplified VAT registration via Form 1550 or seek reverse-charge treatment if the customer is French VAT-registered.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying SARL may elect, within a limited window or under specific conditions, to be taxed at the level of partners as a personal-income-tax entity (transparent regime under CGI Art. 239 and related rules). Group tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) is available for French sub-groups; hybrid mismatch elections must respect ATAD transpositions, and no US-style check-the-box election exists in French domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France imposes no de minimis registration threshold for B2B supplies by non-established taxable persons; registration is generally required for French established activities. For EU-distance sales of goods the annual cross-border EU threshold is EUR 10,000; for foreign transporters, a fixed VAT registration via Form 1585 is triggered by local operations in France, subject to the reverse charge for imported transport services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rating is available for supplies of goods dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the seller or on behalf of the seller (CGI Art. 262-I), and for ancillary transport services directly linked to export of goods, including international freight by road using TIR carnets, provided the transport service is from French territory to a non-EU destination or is part of an international transit movement. The supplier must hold documentary evidence proving the goods physically left the EU within statutory timeframes and that the service is consumed outside France for export purposes.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DELTA E-express / ENS or normal export clearance)
  • TIR Carnet (validated exit stamp and/or electronic skate)
  • CMR International Consignment Note (contract of carriage evidence)
  • Commercial invoice and transport contract
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent proof of delivery outside EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment proof showing the foreign counterparty settled the transaction

France mandates structured electronic invoicing (Factur-X/UBL) for domestic B2B transactions under the reform sequenced from September 2026 and 2027, with clearance through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or certified dematerialisation platforms (PDP). Inbound and outbound customs goods movements under TIR require the electronic customs declaration (DELTA/E-Customs) and the TIR carnet data exchange; there is no digital stamping for road transport invoices, but real-time e-invoicing reporting and upload of transaction data to DGFiP/EU systems become mandatory on the applicable roll-out dates.

Export zero-rating claims are heavily scrutinised by DGFiP and Customs (douane). Failure to maintain or produce the required export documentation within time limits triggers VAT clawback, interest for late payment, and an 80% penalty under Article 1729 (0) CGI for concealment or abusive use of zero-rating; deliberate false declarations may constitute tax fraud (Article 1741 CGI) and customs offences under the Code des Douanes, carrying criminal fines and potential imprisonment, together with joint liability for the tax representative and service company for the underlying unpaid tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a founding member of the euro area, and the euro is fully and freely convertible. Both capital-account and current-account items are unrestricted: Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union guarantees the free movement of capital between Member States, and France applies the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements without exception for direct investment, profit repatriation, loan servicing or trade settlements. No exchange-control permit is required for a non-resident shareholder to inject equity into a French SARL, nor for the SARL to remit dividends, interest or royalties abroad. The practical constraints are documentary and prudential rather than convertibility-based: (i) all capital injections must pass through the regulated banking channel and are subject to mandatory UBO verification under Directive (EU) 2015/849 as transposed in the Code monétaire et financier; (ii) cross-border transactions reaching or exceeding EUR 50,000 trigger a Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration filed by the instructing credit institution under the Arrêté of 5 August 2015; (iii) dividend remittances to non-resident corporate shareholders attract a 25% French withholding tax unless reduced to 0% for EU/EEA parents meeting the Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions (a holding of at least 5% for at least two years with a valid tax-residence certificate), or otherwise lowered by an applicable double-tax treaty; (iv) the TIR trucking model must remain compliant with EU restrictive measures — no conversion facility or banking channel can legitimise a sanctioned corridor to or through Russia, Belarus or other black-listed territories. Income invoiced in USD, GBP or CHF is freely receivable and may be retained in foreign-currency accounts in France, but physical cash entering or leaving French borders in excess of EUR 10,000 must be declared to French Customs under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in France. The exchange-control regime was fully liberalised in 1990, and a French-resident exporter may keep the entirety of its foreign-currency proceeds in a French compte en devises, use them for payments to third countries, or convert them into euro at any time without penalty. The discipline that replaces surrender is reporting: (i) each transfer of funds with non-residents of EUR 50,000 or more must be tagged with a standard Banque de France nature-of-payment code, and the bank files the balance-of-payments declaration within the statutory deadlines; (ii) movements of goods within the EU are declared through the déclaration d'échanges de biens (DEB), while customs exports are supported by the DAU export declaration; (iii) TIR Carnet operations require discharge at the French customs office of destination within the time-limit set by customs, normally no more than 30 days, otherwise the international TIR guarantee chain is activated as a decisive customs debt; (iv) fuel, tolls and operational disbursements paid outside France in non-euro currencies are ordinary current-account transactions and require no prior authorisation, only proper accounting and VAT reporting.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) — the French central bank and central repository for balance-of-payments statistics and foreign-exchange surveillance; the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy and Finance) administers capital-account liberalisation, foreign-investment screening and financial sanctions; the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) supervises the prudential and AML conduct of banks and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a secondary collections and disbursement rail, LOW as the single treasury spine. An ACPR-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or payment institution passporting under Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2) can lawfully open a professional payment account, provide a French IBAN, execute SEPA credit transfers, direct debits, and sponsor-bank SWIFT operations, and issue commercial business cards for driver expenses. The legal constraints are severe for a TIR trucking entity: (i) an EMI cannot grant credit, therefore no overdraft, factoring, import/export documentary credits, TIR customs bonds or fleet finance can be provided; (ii) funds held with an EMI are e-money, not bank deposits, so they are not covered by the French deposit-guarantee scheme applicable to credit institutions; (iii) EMIs cannot issue customs guarantees or TIR Carnet guarantee instruments — only licensed insurers and credit institutions can stand behind the TIR guarantee chain; (iv) most mainstream EMIs apply restrictive usage policies and reject high-frequency freight invoice turnover or cash-receipt patterns without documentary evidence, and many cannot settle the specialised fuel-card clearing networks operated by DKV, UTA or Eurotoll; (v) from 17 January 2025 the Digital Operational Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) imposes mandatory ICT-resilience obligations on payment institutions, and the entity should verify the EMI's compliance certificate. The practical blueprint is therefore to keep BNP Paribas or Société Générale as the principal clearing and credit relationship and to use a reputable EU EMI only for tactical expense-card and fast-disbursement workflows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a secondary collections and disbursement rail, LOW as the single treasury spine. An ACPR-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) or payment institution passporting under Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2) can lawfully open a professional payment account, provide a French IBAN, execute SEPA credit transfers, direct debits, and sponsor-bank SWIFT operations, and issue commercial business cards for driver expenses. The legal constraints are severe for a TIR trucking entity: (i) an EMI cannot grant credit, therefore no overdraft, factoring, import/export documentary credits, TIR customs bonds or fleet finance can be provided; (ii) funds held with an EMI are e-money, not bank deposits, so they are not covered by the French deposit-guarantee scheme applicable to credit institutions; (iii) EMIs cannot issue customs guarantees or TIR Carnet guarantee instruments — only licensed insurers and credit institutions can stand behind the TIR guarantee chain; (iv) most mainstream EMIs apply restrictive usage policies and reject high-frequency freight invoice turnover or cash-receipt patterns without documentary evidence, and many cannot settle the specialised fuel-card clearing networks operated by DKV, UTA or Eurotoll; (v) from 17 January 2025 the Digital Operational Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) imposes mandatory ICT-resilience obligations on payment institutions, and the entity should verify the EMI's compliance certificate. The practical blueprint is therefore to keep BNP Paribas or Société Générale as the principal clearing and credit relationship and to use a reputable EU EMI only for tactical expense-card and fast-disbursement workflows.
