Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for a French SARL. Cash contributions must be deposited with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations in the name of the company in formation; the registry accepts the bank capital-deposit certificate as proof. At formation at least 20% of each cash share's nominal value must be paid, with the balance payable within five years; in-kind contributions must be fully paid on incorporation. For this passive equity-holding SPV, fully paid-up capital is strongly recommended to satisfy bank AML/KYC requirements and to simplify release of blocked deposits after issuance of the Kbis.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full Eurozone member and applies the principle of full euro convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. Under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier), cross-border payments and capital movements are fully liberalized for EU/EEA residents and are subject to no general exchange control authorization. Profit distributions and dividend remittances by a French SARL to its non-French shareholders are permitted without prior Bank of France approval. Non-EU/EEA transfers may be subject merely to statistical declaration and, in limited cases, to due diligence to enforce EU sanctions or detect irregular financial flows. There is no crown-currency restriction on the holding of bank accounts outside France for French-resident entities, but the SARL must report its cross-border financial flows to the Banque de France when mandated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement. No mandatory repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exist for export receipts. A French exporter or international equity holding entity may retain export proceeds in foreign currency, either onshore in an authorized French bank account or offshore in a fully compliant foreign bank account. However, cross-border payments exceeding €12,500 in value are subject to the French balance-of-payments reporting regime, which requires the entity or the intermediary bank to complete the appropriate statistical declaration (via the Banque de France). Such reporting requirements are declarative and do not constitute exchange control approval, conversion mandates, or surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting in coordination with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) and under the prudential framework of the European Central Bank and the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for licensed EU/EEA Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions passporting into France, and Low for offshore third-country digital EMIs. EU-licensed EMIs such as Qonto, Treezor, or Mangopay can provide French-domiciled payment services and operational euro accounts for treasury movement, but they are not full credit institutions and cannot provide credit facilities, overdrafts, or custody of capital-market instruments. An offshore EMI regulated outside the EEA cannot lawfully carry out payment services or hold funds for a French SARL without obtaining an ACPR-licensed branch or agent arrangement. Completeness of treasury, interbank clearing access, and capacity to handle high-value cross-border capital injections remain materially weaker in digital EMIs; therefore, the primary cross-border banking and clearing relationship should be with a Tier-1 Eurozone credit institution.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Ultimate beneficial owner is a non-European or high-risk nationality resident in a tax-haven or FATF-listed jurisdiction

Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owner is a non-European or high-risk nationality resident in a tax-haven or FATF-listed jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Government-issued photo ID, proof of physical residential address, full UBO declaration in the French STR registers (TCF/INPI), notarised explanation of source of wealth, audited shareholder financial statements, tax residence certificates, and any necessary OFAC/ACPR sanctions screening reports demonstrating clearance.
Pure equity holding SPV with no operating activity, no staff, and no bespoke board minutes

Root cause: Pure equity holding SPV with no operating activity, no staff, and no bespoke board minutes

Defense evidence: Strategic group explanatory memorandum, SARL by-laws with non-trading holding object, board resolution approving the banking relationship, organisational chart, audited or carefully prepared pro-forma accounts showing the treasury role, evidence of passive income streams, and an economic substance and tax compliance note evidencing French tax coverage and directors' oversight.
Large inbound capital contribution from a related shareholder or parent without auditable trail

Root cause: Large inbound capital contribution from a related shareholder or parent without auditable trail

Defense evidence: Notarised share subscription agreement or capital increase deed, parent board minutes approving the financing, audited parent accounts, banking reference of the parent, wire transfer instruction with explicit purpose code, and sworn declaration from the SARL's legal representative that the transaction reflects a lawful equity investment.
Significant beneficial ownership layered through multiple opaque foreign holding companies

Root cause: Significant beneficial ownership layered through multiple opaque foreign holding companies

Defense evidence: Corporate registry extracts from every jurisdiction in the ownership chain, notarised translations into French or English, organisational charts certified by directors, register of beneficial owners (if any), legal opinion from a licensed international law firm confirming transparency and lawful source of funds.
PEP (politically exposed person) status of any officer, shareholder, or family member

Root cause: PEP (politically exposed person) status of any officer, shareholder, or family member

Defense evidence: Self-certification and questionnaire of the competent compliance department, wealth origin documentation including tax returns and asset titles, details of the underlying commercial rationale, notarised declarations from all PEP-related individuals, unqualified references from two independent regulated financial institutions, and enhanced monitoring consent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocols: (1) The parent/shareholder must transmit a cross-border SWIFT MT103 wire from its own established bank account; (2) the wire must be in EUR for direct settlement on TARGET2, or in an acceptable convertible currency for conversion in France; (3) the payment beneficiary is the French SARL's account held at the receiving French Tier-1 bank; (4) the purpose of payment must be encoded as “Capital Subscription / Equity Contribution” or the Banque de France balance-of-payments category “Investissement direct / Capital social - prise de participation”; (5) the bank must receive and register the supporting corporate resolution of the SARL authorising the capital increase or equity injection and the corresponding shareholder resolution; (6) all transaction documents must be originals or certified true copies and must be retained for the Bank's AML file; (7) if the amount triggers French balance-of-payments statistical reporting, either the receiving bank or the SARL must file the appropriate declaration (type DOP or DY/OIP) with the Banque de France; (8) if the foreign contribution exceeds France's European sanctions threshold, full automatic sanctions screening happens both at the correspondent and the receiving bank.The passive equity holding SARL is expected to make recurring outbound payments for group management fees, interest payments, legal fees, future acquisition consideration, dividend remittances to the parent, and repayment of shareholder loans. Acceptable clearing rails are: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR for payment to EU/EEA beneficiaries, using the SARL's IBAN and BIC with mandatory purpose codes; (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA cross-border transfers, carrying the beneficiary bank's BIC, the parent's IBAN or account number, and precise payment details; (3) Trade settlement is not normally performed by a holding SPV, but if a purchase of subsidiary shares requires a documentary payment, the SARL may use a Standard Documentary Letter of Credit (UCP600), unwritten or standby LC, or a Bank Payment Obligation (BPO) only when its bank approves the underlying commercial contract; (4) for truly commercial cost disbursements, direct debits via SEPA Direct Debit are available for recurrent Swiss, French, and euro-area payments after supplier mandate verification.
