Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital for a SARL. Capital must be fully subscribed; at least 20% of each cash contribution must be paid upon subscription and deposited in a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) in the company's name, with the balance payable within five years. The bank releases funds upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: For a standard office not open to the public: fire-safety compliance comprises basic electrical verification, portable fire extinguishers, unimpeded escape routes, smoke detectors where required, and compliance with local building codes. Occupational clearance requires preparation of the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques (DUERP), scheduling of workplace medical visits, emergency plan display, and, for leased offices, coordination with building owners on shared means of evacuation.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum capital for a SARL. Capital must be fully subscribed; at least 20% of each cash contribution must be paid upon subscription and deposited in a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) in the company's name, with the balance payable within five years. The bank releases funds upon RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) - French tax administration; primary portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate and e-invoicing entities portal: www.paiement.pro / Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); customs jurisdiction: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned French SARL: (1) incorporate via the Guichet Unique (formalites-entreprises.gouv.fr), which transmits the registration to INSEE and automatically issues SIREN and SIRET numbers; (2) upon completion of the registration, the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) assigns a French VAT identification number (numero de TVA intracommunautaire) generally without additional application; (3) where no French establishment exists, a foreign supplier of digital services may separately register for French VAT by filing the relevant form at the competent non-resident centre (Service des Impôts des Particuliers, Non-Residents) or elect the OSS (One-Stop Shop) procedure for EU supplies; (4) a company requiring formal proof of its VAT number may request a VAT certificate (extrait au registre des operateurs) from the DGFIP.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL carrying on commercial activity is a separate legal taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) under article 206 CGI. It is taxable on worldwide profits (subject to tax treaty relief) and is a distinct VAT taxable person (assujetti unique) for French VAT purposes. No check-the-box or partnership classification is available by default, and the SARL is not eligible for the micro-entrepreneur individual regime. — For a foreign-owned French SARL: (1) incorporate via the Guichet Unique (formalites-entreprises.gouv.fr), which transmits the registration to INSEE and automatically issues SIREN and SIRET numbers; (2) upon completion of the registration, the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) assigns a French VAT identification number (numero de TVA intracommunautaire) generally without additional application; (3) where no French establishment exists, a foreign supplier of digital services may separately register for French VAT by filing the relevant form at the competent non-resident centre (Service des Impôts des Particuliers, Non-Residents) or elect the OSS (One-Stop Shop) procedure for EU supplies; (4) a company requiring formal proof of its VAT number may request a VAT certificate (extrait au registre des operateurs) from the DGFIP.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL carrying on commercial activity is a separate legal taxpayer subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) under article 206 CGI. It is taxable on worldwide profits (subject to tax treaty relief) and is a distinct VAT taxable person (assujetti unique) for French VAT purposes. No check-the-box or partnership classification is available by default, and the SARL is not eligible for the micro-entrepreneur individual regime.

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box or entity-classification election. Available statutory elections include: (1) under article 239 bis AA CGI, a SARL whose shares are held by natural persons and that does not exceed prescribed size and duration criteria may elect, for a maximum of five years, to be taxed as a partnership (imposition à l'impôt sur le revenu); (2) an election under article 239 bis AB CGI may apply where the SARL exercises only certain specified civil or patrimonial activities; (3) for VAT, an election against the franchise en base may be made to restore full input VAT deduction; (4) ATAD hybrid mismatch rules under article 209 D CGI may require re-characterisation of a French entity where the counterparty jurisdiction applies different classification. This dossier assumes no election is made and the default corporate income tax and standard VAT treatment apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For foreign (non-established) suppliers of digital services there is no monetary registration threshold: a French VAT obligation arises from the first taxable supply in France (notably B2C electronic services under the customer-location rule). Domestic small businesses benefit from the franchise en base de TVA (exemption without input VAT recovery) up to EUR 85,000 annual turnover for goods and EUR 37,500 for services (including digital services) applicable to the French-established party. For Micro-SaaS consumer subscription sales made through Paddle or Lemon Squeezy acting as merchant of record, the platform is generally the VAT collector and registration nexus is managed by the platform.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is claimable for the supply of goods physically exported outside the European Union (article 262(1) CGI) and for intra-Community supplies of goods to a taxable person in another Member State holding a valid EU VAT number (article 262 ter CGI), provided the supplier retains evidence of transport and files the recapitulative statement. For the Micro-SaaS digital service scope, B2B subscriptions to business customers established in another EU Member State are zero-rated in France and reverse-charged in the customer's Member State under article 58 CGI (customer-location rule); B2C subscriptions to consumers residing in the EU are subject to French VAT at 20% unless covered by the OSS regime, while B2C supplies to customers outside the EU may be outside French VAT where effective use and enjoyment is shown to occur outside France.

  • Customs export declaration (Document Administratif Unique / export summary declaration) validated by the DGDDI customs system
  • Transport evidence: signed bill of lading, CMR consignment note, or airway bill proving physical departure from France or the EU
  • Commercial invoice clearly stating the Incoterms, the French zero-rating legal basis and the absence of VAT
  • Payment evidence: inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103, or platform settlement statement issued by Paddle or Lemon Squeezy showing the collected consideration
  • For intra-EU supplies only: the customer's valid VAT identification number and the corresponding EC Sales List (recapitulative statement) confirmation

France does not operate an immediate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. The domestic B2B e-invoicing mandate is being phased in: structured electronic invoicing (Factur-X, UBL or CII via a registered PDP or the public PPF portal) is mandatory from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small and medium enterprises. B2C transaction data and cross-border B2B supplies are subject to e-reporting (données de transaction et de paiement) through API interfaces or the PPF. No continuous certification or fiscal stamp is required; instead, invoice integrity, authenticity and readability requirements under articles 289 and 289 bis CGI apply.

France does not impose a statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; however, a 180-day documentation baseline is applied in this dossier as an internal compliance benchmark. The DGFIP may reassess VAT before the expiry of the general limitation period (normally until 31 December of the third year following the year in question, extended to ten years for tax fraud). Where zero-rating is not supported with valid proofs, VAT is clawed back at the standard 20% rate plus late-payment interest at 0.2% per month. Deliberate inaccuracies attract a 40% penalty (manquement délibéré); abusive tax planning (abus de droit) or fraudulent schemes attract an 80% penalty. Persistent export fraud can lead to criminal prosecution under article 1741 CGI with imprisonment of up to seven years and fines of EUR 1,000,000, and the responsible SARL managers may also incur personal civil liability for the company's tax debt.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has full current-account and capital-account convertibility within the framework of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, particularly the free movement of capital rules. A French SARL may generally receive, hold, convert, and remit EUR and foreign currencies through regulated banks or payment institutions without a general exchange-control approval. Dividends, interest, royalties, intercompany service fees, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds may generally be paid to non-residents, subject to distributable-profit rules, corporate approvals, withholding-tax analysis, transfer-pricing support, beneficial-owner verification, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, and applicable tax reporting. Banks may delay or reject transactions lacking a clear economic purpose, supporting invoices or agreements, source-of-funds evidence, or satisfactory beneficial-ownership information. Payments involving sanctioned persons, sanctioned territories, restricted goods, dual-use items, or high-risk jurisdictions may require blocking, licensing, enhanced due diligence, or prohibition. French residents generally must report qualifying foreign bank, payment, and digital-asset accounts annually to the French tax administration, subject to statutory exceptions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds to France, surrender export receipts to the Banque de France, or convert a prescribed percentage of export proceeds into EUR. No general statutory conversion deadline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to software or SaaS export receipts. Receipts may remain in foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank terms, tax-accounting requirements, sanctions controls, and accurate booking of foreign-exchange gains and losses. Export transactions must nevertheless comply with customs, VAT, invoicing, accounting, and statistical requirements. Physical cash and certain negotiable instruments transported into or out of the European Union at or above EUR 10,000 must be declared to customs. Intra-EU trade-statistics and VAT reporting may apply, including Intrastat where applicable thresholds are met. Export documentation, customer contracts, platform settlement reports, invoices, payment records, and evidence of service performance should be retained for audit and tax-defense purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem and subject to applicable European Union monetary, payment-services, sanctions, and capital-movement rules. Prudential and payment-services supervision is principally conducted by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, while customs and certain statistical controls are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payments and collections; Low as a substitute for a French bank account used for capital formation, regulated escrow, or all treasury functions. A licensed European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, IBANs, acquiring, card services, foreign-exchange conversion, and international transfers under its authorization and safeguarding model. It is not a credit institution unless separately licensed and generally does not provide deposit-taking or bank-credit products. Offshore digital EMIs outside the European Economic Area cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services into France merely through an application interface; they require an applicable French or European passport, local authorization, or a properly authorized service provider. Platform providers such as Paddle and Lemon Squeezy are payment or merchant-of-record channels and should not be treated as the SARL's bank, capital-escrow institution, or unrestricted operating account. Onboarding remains subject to KYC, beneficial-owner identification, source-of-funds checks, sanctions screening, transaction limits, safeguarding restrictions, and possible account termination for high-risk payment flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route share capital, capital increases, and shareholder loans from an account held in the name of the identified contributor to the SARL's designated capital or operating account at a regulated French or EEA credit institution or payment institution. For formation or a capital increase, obtain the bank deposit certificate, execute the subscription and corporate resolutions, complete the required corporate filing, and release or reclassify funds only after the legal conditions are satisfied. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments within SEPA and SWIFT cross-border transfers for non-SEPA currencies. France does not use a general mandatory FX purpose-code regime; the payment narrative should nevertheless clearly state the legal basis, such as 'share capital contribution', 'shareholder loan under agreement dated [date]', or 'intercompany funding'. Match the remitter, beneficiary, amount, currency, and agreement; avoid unexplained third-party funding; preserve SWIFT or bank confirmation, source-of-funds records, board approvals, loan terms, and accounting entries; and separately assess withholding tax, thin-capitalization, interest limitation, transfer pricing, and beneficial-ownership consequences. A foreign-currency receipt should be converted at the bank's agreed rate or booked under the applicable French accounting policy, with realized and unrealized FX differences reconciled.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible EUR supplier and service payments, subject to scheme limits and bank controls. Use SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly evidenced by an MT103 or its ISO 20022 successor equivalent, for non-SEPA payments or currencies. For material or higher-risk purchases, use a commercial documentary letter of credit, standby letter of credit, documentary collection, or bank guarantee with clearly defined Incoterms, shipping or service documents, inspection requirements, discrepancy rules, sanctions screening, and governing law. For SaaS operations, ordinary bank transfer, card acquiring, direct debit, and regulated payment-platform settlement are generally more proportionate than a commercial LC. Every payment should be supported by a contract, invoice, purchase order or service acceptance evidence, tax treatment, beneficiary verification, and three-way reconciliation where applicable. Do not use customer receipts, merchant-of-record settlements, or third-party accounts to pay unrelated suppliers without a documented contractual basis. Apply EU and French sanctions, export-control, VAT, accounting, and fraud-prevention controls before release.Deliverable EUR/foreign-currency forwards with a regulated bank or authorized investment firm, documented under appropriate treasury terms and supported by a genuine underlying exposure.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies that are subject to local convertibility restrictions or where physical delivery is unavailable, subject to the counterparty's authorization, local-law restrictions, sanctions controls, valuation terms, collateral requirements, and EMIR or other applicable reporting obligations.Currency swaps and cross-currency swaps for recurring funding, intercompany loans, or longer-term currency exposure, documented under an ISDA Master Agreement or equivalent bank documentation and approved under the SARL's treasury policy.Purchased currency options, collars, and other permitted option strategies for forecast or committed exposures, subject to suitability, cost, accounting, collateral, and governance controls. Avoid uncovered speculative positions unless expressly permitted by the board-approved treasury policy and accepted by the bank.Hedges should be proportionate to documented commercial exposures, with counterparty credit limits, confirmations, independent valuation, maturity and rollover controls, hedge-accounting analysis where elected, EMIR classification and reporting assessment, and board or delegated-authority approval. The SARL should maintain a hedge register identifying exposure, instrument, notional, currency, maturity, counterparty, valuation, collateral, and termination rights.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payments and collections; Low as a substitute for a French bank account used for capital formation, regulated escrow, or all treasury functions. A licensed European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, IBANs, acquiring, card services, foreign-exchange conversion, and international transfers under its authorization and safeguarding model. It is not a credit institution unless separately licensed and generally does not provide deposit-taking or bank-credit products. Offshore digital EMIs outside the European Economic Area cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services into France merely through an application interface; they require an applicable French or European passport, local authorization, or a properly authorized service provider. Platform providers such as Paddle and Lemon Squeezy are payment or merchant-of-record channels and should not be treated as the SARL's bank, capital-escrow institution, or unrestricted operating account. Onboarding remains subject to KYC, beneficial-owner identification, source-of-funds checks, sanctions screening, transaction limits, safeguarding restrictions, and possible account termination for high-risk payment flows.
