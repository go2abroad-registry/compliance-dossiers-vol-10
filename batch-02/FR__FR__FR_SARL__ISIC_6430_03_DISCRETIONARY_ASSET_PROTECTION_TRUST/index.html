Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital; however, the capital must be fully subscribed and paid up (in cash or in kind) as stated in the Articles. If capital is less than €10,000, it must be fully paid at incorporation; if higher, at least 20% must be paid up, with the remainder within 5 years. Funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account until the company is registered.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies the EU treaty framework of free movement of capital. Both capital and current account transactions are fully convertible for residents and non-residents involving euro or foreign currencies, subject to anti-money laundering (AML), know-your-client (KYC), counter-terrorist financing (CTF), and international sanctions screening. Profit remittances from a French SARL are permissible without prior FX approval provided that distributable reserves are evidenced by audited financial statements, corporate tax obligations are settled or provisioned, and the distribution complies with the SARL's legal and statutory filing requirements. Hurdles are administrative and documentary: proving beneficial ownership, sourcing funds, and demonstrating economic substance in the trust management activity. There is no exchange-control license requirement for standard cross-border payments, but the Banque de France may require statistical declarations for incoming and outgoing capital transfers through the Balance of Payments reporting regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose a statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters and service providers, including fiduciary trustees receiving overseas income, may retain 100% of proceeds in foreign currency or convert as needed. However, all cross-border transfers exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, and file declarations for financial sanctions and asset-freezing checks. For trust-related export-like service fees, the provider must maintain supporting contracts and transfer evidence to substantiate the underlying transaction.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting as the competent national authority for balance-of-payments reporting and monetary operations, with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) supervising banking and payment institutions; the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) administers financial sanctions and foreign asset control.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. France permits Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions authorized under the EU PSD2 to provide IBAN-based accounts for cross-border payments. For a High_Risk_Special_License fiduciary administration company and an irrevocable offshore asset protection trust, most EMIs will decline onboarding due to concentrated counterparty risk, opaque beneficiary structures, and the lack of physical presence in the SARL's address. Specialized trust-focused fintechs may open an account with enhanced AML/KYC and restrictions on cash withdrawals, but they generally cannot provide loans, credit, or complex FX products. Regulatory viability is legally sound under the ACPR regime, yet commercially few EMIs accept this classification; a tier-1 bank with a dedicated fiduciary desk remains the defensible primary clearing gateway.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Discretionary beneficial ownership and lack of fixed beneficiaries

Root cause: Discretionary beneficial ownership and lack of fixed beneficiaries

Defense evidence: Provide the full trust instrument, a structured beneficiary mapping with defined class descriptions, the settlor's letter of wishes, and a legal opinion from a French or foreign-qualified attorney confirming that the trustee has full discretionary power and that the SARL exercises effective management and control. Include audited financial statements and any regulatory approval from the trust licensing authority.
Irrevocable offshore trust domiciled in low-tax or opaque jurisdiction

Root cause: Irrevocable offshore trust domiciled in low-tax or opaque jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Submit tax residence certificates of the SARL and the trust, FATCA/CRS classification codes, economic substance report, and written escalation to the bank's Financial Security officer setting out the commercial rationale for the jurisdiction. Add regulatory license evidence and a list of all professional advisers (lawyers, accountants, regulated custodians).
Origin of funds from asset protection arrangements

Root cause: Origin of funds from asset protection arrangements

Defense evidence: Deliver a sworn source-of-funds declaration supported by bank statements of the settlor covering 36 months, audited financial statements of the underlying commercial entity, sale/purchase agreements for any funded assets, inheritance or gift tax paperwork, and a dedicated legal opinion confirming the trust does not prejudice creditors or violate French fraudulent-conveyance rules.
Cross-border flows to and from high-risk third countries

Root cause: Cross-border flows to and from high-risk third countries

Defense evidence: Maintain a comprehensive transaction flow map, counterparty due-diligence file, commercial contracts, and sanctions screening output. Establish enhanced monitoring via dedicated sub-ledgers, proactive tax transparency, and evidence of WHITE (EU) or OFAC screening of all counterparties before each payment. Contemporaneous board minutes authorizing each outbound disbursement should be retained.
Lack of demonstrated economic substance in the SARL

Root cause: Lack of demonstrated economic substance in the SARL

Defense evidence: Provide the employment contracts of the SARL's personnel, a physical office lease agreement, minutes of board/management meetings held in France, corporate tax and social security filings, and a time-stamped operational calendar of fiduciary decisions, all of which evidence realistic management in France.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital funding for a French SARL acting as trustee should be routed via SWIFT (MT103) or SEPA with a valid EU purpose code. For equity/capital contributions, the standard French balance-of-payments purpose code is 'S301 – Investment in equity including contributions to capital of non-resident enterprises' (or the applicable EU Balance of Payments classification). The remitter must reference the SARL's registration, the trust deed, and the transaction nature. The receiving bank will verify the transaction against the ACPR AML file, perform sanctions screening, and may require an FX conversion in EUR at the spot rate. For funds originating from a discretionary trust, the bank will require a copy of the trust deed, a distribution resolution from the trustee, and an updated list of beneficial owners. All inbound wires must include a clear reference to the SARL's tax identifier and the fiduciary client code to avoid blocking. Where the trust funds pass through multiple intermediary banks, use a single correspondent clearing lane (preferably euro through TARGET2) to expedite compliance.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements and trust distributions should be executed over dedicated clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for single beneficiary payments, (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated payments inside the European Economic Area, and (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits, UCP600) for cross-border trade transactions requiring guarantee mechanics. The French SARL should maintain at least two outgoing clearing channels: a direct bank line to a tier-1 eurozone clearing bank and a USD correspondent channel via New York or London. For each outward wire, the bank requires the beneficiary's IBAN or SWIFT/BIC, legal entity identifier, invoice or distribution deed, and a completed foreign exchange instruction.
