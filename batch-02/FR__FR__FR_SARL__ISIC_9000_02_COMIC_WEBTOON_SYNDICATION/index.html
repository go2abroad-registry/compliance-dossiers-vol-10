Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum share capital (Article L.223-2). Capital may be set freely, typically EUR 1.00. At least 20% of cash subscriptions must be paid up on incorporation, with the balance payable within five years (Article L.223-7). No statutory escrow account is required; cash contributions are deposited with a bank or notary, and in-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's report.

Funding & unlock path: Workplace safety clearance: obtain a fire safety certificate from the competent commission de sécurité for the premises, in accordance with Code du travail Articles R.4227-1 to R.4227-21. For registered office use, conduct a structural load inspection if server equipment is installed, and register a medical and occupational health unit with the Service de Prévention et de Santé au Travail (SPST) of the regional CARSAT.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) for non-established businesses. Online administration is principally provided through impots.gouv.fr; company formation and business-registration filings are submitted through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered through the INPI Guichet unique. The filing generates a SIREN identifier for the legal entity and a SIRET identifier for each establishment, with registration recorded in the Registre national des entreprises. The company then activates its professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr and identifies its competent SIE. A French VAT number is issued by the DGFiP in the format FR followed by a two-character control key and the nine-digit SIREN. The VAT registration request normally requires the Kbis or registration certificate, articles of association, identification of managers and beneficial owners, registered office evidence, business description, projected taxable turnover, bank details, and evidence of economic activity. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the SIEE for a French VAT number where required and appoints a French tax representative only where legally required, including applicable cases involving non-EU businesses. EU-established businesses generally do not require a French fiscal representative but remain responsible for registration, filings, invoicing, and payment obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying SARL may elect temporary taxation under personal income tax (impôt sur le revenu, IR) for up to five fiscal years, subject to statutory conditions concerning age, ownership, workforce, turnover, and activity. A qualifying family SARL may elect IR taxation without the ordinary five-year limitation, provided all statutory family-ownership and consent conditions remain satisfied. The election must be made within the applicable statutory period and notified to the DGFiP. France has no general United States-style check-the-box election. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules apply mandatorily and do not create an elective hybrid classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For an established French business using the VAT exemption for small enterprises, the principal services turnover threshold is EUR 37,500, with a higher tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250; goods and certain accommodation and catering activities generally use thresholds of EUR 85,000 and EUR 93,500. These thresholds concern eligibility for the French small-business VAT exemption rather than a universal exemption from registration. A business making taxable transactions outside the exemption, or a non-established business required to account for French VAT, may need registration regardless of turnover. Digital services supplied business-to-consumer to EU customers are subject to EU place-of-supply rules and may be reported through the One Stop Shop (OSS), while business-to-business services generally follow the customer-location reverse-charge rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the EU may be invoiced without French VAT if the supplier can prove that the goods left the EU customs territory and satisfy the statutory export conditions. The supplier must retain a customs export declaration and transport or delivery evidence linking the goods to the export transaction. Supplies to customers in another EU Member State are generally intra-Community supplies rather than exports and require the applicable customer VAT-number, transport, and recapitulative-statement conditions. Webtoon licensing, digital content, and electronic services require separate place-of-supply analysis: B2B supplies to an established business outside France are generally outside French VAT under the customer-location rule, while B2C electronic services are subject to EU consumer-location rules and may require OSS or local registration. An offshore service is not automatically a zero-rated French export merely because payment is received from abroad.

  • Electronic export declaration filed through the applicable French customs system, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Export accompanying document or equivalent customs evidence showing release for export and exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, contractual terms, and VAT treatment
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, carrier evidence, or other transport documentation demonstrating shipment outside the EU
  • Proof of exit generated or validated by customs, where available
  • Purchase order, contract, delivery records, and accounting evidence linking the export declaration to the invoice
  • Customer VAT identification evidence and transport proof for intra-Community supplies, where the transaction is an EU dispatch rather than an export
  • Bank payment evidence where relevant; a French inward remittance certificate is not generally a statutory prerequisite for export VAT exemption

France does not currently operate a universal real-time invoice-clearance system for ordinary domestic transactions. Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing is being phased in through approved private electronic invoicing platforms: all businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; large and mid-sized businesses must issue them from that date; small and micro-enterprises must issue them from 1 September 2027, subject to legislative or regulatory amendments. Electronic transaction reporting for transactions outside the domestic B2B e-invoicing perimeter is introduced on the same phased timetable. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. Invoices must satisfy French VAT content, authenticity, integrity, legibility, retention, and audit-trail requirements; no general digital tax stamp is presently required for ordinary VAT invoices.

France has no general foreign-exchange control rule requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. Export VAT exemption depends on objective proof of export and correct transaction classification, not on a statutory repatriation deadline. If the DGFiP determines that zero-rating was unsupported, it may assess the unpaid VAT retrospectively, together with late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month, subject to statutory rules and applicable periods. A 40% surcharge may apply for deliberate failure or deliberate misapplication, and an 80% surcharge may apply in cases involving fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights; higher consequences may apply for concealed or fictitious transactions. Customs violations may generate separate customs duties, VAT assessments, administrative penalties, seizure, and criminal exposure. Input-VAT refunds may be denied or clawed back where export evidence is incomplete, inconsistent, fabricated, or not retained. Tax and customs records should be retained for the applicable statutory period, commonly six years for tax audit purposes and longer where accounting or customs rules require it.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a Eurozone member, maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU law. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard tax reporting and anti-money laundering compliance. No exchange control approvals are required for ordinary business transactions, but cross-border capital movements exceeding €15,000 per transaction must be reported to the Banque de France for statistical and balance-of-payments purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds in France. Exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts. However, all cross-border transactions (including export receipts) exceeding €15,000 must be reported to the Banque de France via the appropriate electronic declaration (CERFA form or direct reporting). No conversion ratio is imposed; companies may freely convert EUR to foreign currency or hold foreign currency balances.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Central Bank of France) and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) for foreign exchange regulations and cross-border capital movement reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine, or Lydia for operational accounts, given their streamlined onboarding and digital-first approach. However, for cross-border payments and multi-currency treasury operations, these EMIs may have limitations in terms of FX hedging and large-volume international wire transfers. Offshore digital EMIs (non-EU) are generally not viable due to regulatory restrictions under PSD2 and French banking law; they would require a branch or subsidiary in France to operate legally.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties including up to 5 years imprisonment and fines up to 80% of the evaded tax amount

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability, possible imprisonment, and obligation to repay evaded taxes plus interest
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Tax penalties up to 80% of the tax evaded, plus potential criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil leading to personal restitution orders, fines, and imprisonment
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to 1.5 million euros or 10% of annual turnover, plus criminal sanctions

Director personal liability: Personal fines, disqualification from management roles, and potential jail time for willful non-compliance
