Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), business tax portal: impots.gouv.fr; VAT and corporate tax files administered by the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) within the SIRENE/RNE public registry (sirene.fr); company incorporation, SIREN/SIRET issuance and all statutory formalities operate through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, managed by INPI at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; customs and EORI functions are handled by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via the DELTA-X export platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Formation: file the SARL incorporation dossier through the Guichet unique (INPI); upon validation, INSEE issues the 9-digit SIREN and a 14-digit SIRET establishment code automatically through the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE). Step 2 — Registration: the DGFiP allocates the tax status and activates the business VAT file; the company obtains the numéro de TVA intracommunautaire (format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN) for intra-EU B2B invoicing, export declarations, and VAT refund claims. There is no statutory registration threshold for an established French SARL: registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction. A de minimis domestic-only trader may alternatively benefit from the franchise en base de TVA (turnover exemption) below EUR 85,000 for goods (Art. 293 B CGI), although an e-commerce pet-supplies SARL will normally waive it. Step 3 — Customs identity: apply to the DGDDI for the EORI number (format FR + SIREN) required to lodge export declarations. Step 4 — Portal activation: create the professional account on impots.gouv.fr (espace professionnel) to file VAT returns (CA3/CA12 via EFI), corporate income tax forms (2065/2072), and refund claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SARL exercising the option for Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) or SARL with at least one corporate shareholder, which is subject to IS by law; applies the territorial scope of France including permanent-establishment nexus under Art. 209 CGI; no Tax_Exempt_Zone relief applies to this metropolitan retail scope — overseas-department zone regimes such as La Réunion (8.5% VAT) are N/A for a standard metropolitan SARL).

Available election forms

French law does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime; the available characterization elections are: (i) irrevocable option for corporate income tax (IS) by a SARL whose members are exclusively natural persons (Art. 3-bis CGI, first sentence), converting the default income-tax-transparent SARL into an entity-level taxed corporation — the option must be notified to the SIE and becomes irrevocable after five tax years; (ii) election of corporate income tax grouping (intégration fiscale) for French subsidiaries at least 95% owned, allowing group-wide IS consolidation (Arts. 223 A et seq. CGI); (iii) hybrid mismatch alignment under the ATAD implementation: France applies automatic re-characterization provisions of Arts. 209 B and 209 C CGI (deductible payments subject to a non-included counterparty income test), with mandatory disclosure of cross-border hybrid arrangements under DAC6 rather than elective check-the-box treatment; (iv) voluntary VAT registration election to waive the franchise en base de TVA (Art. 293 B, last paragraph CGI) enabling input VAT deduction and export zero-rating claims.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 annual turnover for tangible goods (franchise en base de TVA, Art. 293 B CGI) and EUR 37,500 for services; EUR 10,000 cumulative EU distance-selling threshold below which distance sales remain taxed at origin (Art. 258 A CGI / Directive 2006/112/EC); operational note: an established e-commerce SARL distributing pet supplies from France is obliged to register for VAT from its first taxable transaction, as the exemption threshold only benefits micro-enterprises selling exclusively to domestic final consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under Art. 262-I (1) CGI to tangible goods dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the seller or on its behalf — including pet-supplies sold by e-commerce and shipped from France directly to end consumers in third markets (Switzerland, UK, USA, APAC/Gulf states). Eligibility conditions: (i) the goods must be physically exported and the export evidenced by customs documentation (DAU/ECS declaration with MRN); (ii) the supply must be made to a non-EU acquirer/consignee; (iii) transport must leave the EU and the seller must hold proof of the effective export prior to or within the admissible evidence window (Art. 74-0 D, Annex III CGI); (iv) for offshore ancillary services, Art. 259-1 CGI zero-rates services directly related to exported goods (transport, loading, transit operations). Claiming the zero rate also requires a positive VAT posture (no franchise) so that any input VAT on French warehouse stocks and freight may be deducted; excess credits are refunded monthly/liquidated through the CA12 form and verified by the SIE.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation via DELTA-X/ECS including the Movement Reference Number — MRN, plus the export summary declaration and any exit-confirmation receipt proving conveyance out of the EU)
  • Transport Document — Bill of Lading (Connaissement), Air Waybill, or CMR international road consignment note naming the exporter/consignee and non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank credit advice or PSP settlement statement (Stripe/PayPal) showing receipt of the proceeds in EUR and tied to the shipment/invoice
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List — export invoice stating HS codes, Incoterms, quantities and precise export weights
  • Contract/purchase-order evidence and proof of non-EU customer identity (customer's VAT ID not required for a private consumer, but delivery address evidence outside the EU is mandatory)

France is implementing a mandatory structured e-invoicing and e-reporting regime (Decree No. 2023-1187 as amended by the 2024 finance law): from 1 September 2026 all large and mid-size enterprises must issue and receive structured e-invoices (Factur-X, UBL, CII) via certified Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires (PDPs) connected to the central Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); from 1 September 2027 the same obligation extends to SMEs and micro-enterprises, including SARLs. The operating model is the 'Y-network' clearance hub: each issuer/receiver connects its PDP to the PPF through secure APIs (REST/JSON, SIREN-based routing, PEPPOL-style addressing), with real-time transmission of invoice metadata, payment data and B2C sales data (e-reporting) to the DGFiP. No 'digital stamping' postage scheme was retained — fiscal clearance is effected through structured-data exchange and the automatic presence of the PPF in the invoice flow rather than by stamping each PDF; nevertheless, each invoice issued by the SARL must satisfy the substantive TVA invoice conditions of Art. 289 CGI and Art. 242-1 B of Annex II, be retained in its original structured electronic format for at least 6 years, and electronic invoices must be received by the buyer in a format meeting the 3-corner integrity requirements.

The zero-rating is conditional on complete export substantiation; if the required bundle is not held by the filing date of the VAT return covering the export (deferred evidence not admitted beyond the regulatory window of Art. 74-0 D, Annex III CGI), the DGFiP retroactively re-imposes French VAT at 20% on the export consideration. On audit, failure of proof attracts the bad-faith penalty of 40% (Art. 1729 CGI) or 80% in cases of organized fraud/manoeuvres frauduleuses, plus monthly late interest of 0.20% (Art. 1727 CGI). Fraudulent export zero-rating schemes (fictitious dispatches, fabricated DAU/ECS declarations, sham invoices not backed by B/L or payment repatriation) may be prosecuted under Art. 1741 CGI as délit de fraude fiscale, punishable by imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines of up to EUR 250,000 for natural-person principals, and are subject to administrative clawbacks over the 3-year standard reprise period (6 years in fraud cases, Art. L.169 CGI) and customs penalties of up to three times the evaded duties under Arts. 410 and 414 Code des douanes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a member of the Eurozone; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no general exchange controls on capital movements within the EU, and profit remittances (dividends, branch profits) are freely transferable subject to standard corporate tax withholding and anti-abuse documentation. Non-resident shareholders may receive dividends without prior authorization, but must provide tax residency certificates to benefit from reduced withholding rates under tax treaties. For e-commerce operations, cross-border payments in EUR and major foreign currencies are unrestricted, but transactions with sanctioned jurisdictions (e.g., Russia, Belarus) are prohibited under EU/OFAC sanctions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists in France. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency earnings in bank accounts, subject to reporting obligations for statistical purposes (Banque de France's balance of payments reporting). For amounts exceeding €50,000 per transaction or equivalent, the exporter must report the transaction to the Banque de France via the electronic reporting system (Téléprocédure). There is no conversion ratio requirement; the exporter may hold or convert funds at market rates.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) under the authority of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; foreign exchange policy is coordinated with the European Central Bank (ECB) and the European Commission.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for cross-border e-commerce operations. French-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine, and Lydia (via Treezor) offer multi-currency accounts, SEPA and SWIFT transfers, and virtual IBANs. They are subject to ACPR (Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority) oversight and are fully compliant for treasury operations. However, for large capital injections or complex trade finance (LCs), a traditional tier-1 bank is recommended. Offshore EMIs (e.g., UK-based or Singapore-based) are viable but may face friction with French tax authorities and local payment processors; they are not recommended as primary treasury accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a French SARL, inbound capital injections (shareholder loans or equity contributions) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA CT (for EUR) with a clear purpose code. For equity contributions, use the purpose code 'S301' (Investment in equity capital) or 'S302' (Investment in mutual funds) under the ECB's Balance of Payments (BoP) classification. For shareholder loans, use 'S401' (Loans and deposits). The bank will require a copy of the shareholders' resolution and the bank account statement of the contributing entity. For amounts exceeding EUR 10,000, the bank must report the transaction to the French Financial Intelligence Unit (TRACFIN) under the anti-money laundering directive. The funds must be credited to the SARL's operational account; a capital escrow account is not mandatory but may be used for proof of capital for certain commercial contracts.For trade settlements (e.g., purchasing pet supplies from Chinese or EU suppliers), the primary clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wire transfers in EUR, USD, or CNY (subject to bank's currency availability); (2) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for EU suppliers within the SEPA zone; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) via SWIFT MT700 for high-value or first-time supplier relationships, typically issued by the SARL's bank and advised through the supplier's bank. For e-commerce refunds to customers, use the same payment processor (Stripe/PayPal) or SEPA Direct Debit (SDD) for EU customers. All outbound payments must be accompanied by the correct purpose code (e.g., 'S401' for trade credit, 'S402' for advance payments) and supporting invoices to avoid delays.Outright FX Forwards (non-deliverable and deliverable) with a licensed French bank or EU investment firm, subject to ISDA or local master agreementFX Swaps (swap de change) for short-term liquidity management, permitted under French financial regulationsCurrency Options (European-style) for hedging against adverse EUR/USD or EUR/CNY movements, available from tier-1 banksCross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) if the SARL has long-term foreign currency debt, though not typical for e-commerceNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for exotic currencies (e.g., CNY) if the bank has a corresponding offshore market access; note that onshore CNY forwards are restricted for non-residents, but NDFs are permissible

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard corporate income tax rate applicable to French branches and resident companies; reduced 15% rate applies to the first EUR 38,120 of taxable profit qualifying for the reduced SME rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid by a French SARL to non-resident corporate shareholders are subject to a statutory 25% withholding tax under CGI art. 187-1. Dividends paid to non-resident individuals are subject to a 12.8% withholding tax under CGI art. 119 bis-2, plus possible social levies. For an EU parent entity holding directly at least 10% of the societe’s capital for at least two years, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive reduces the rate to 0%. Treaty relief may further reduce the corporate rate to 0%, 5%, or 10% depending on the residence clause of the relevant double taxation agreement.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months following the fiscal year-end. For a SARL closing on December 31, the practical EDI filing date is May 3; for other fiscal year-ends, the deadline is three months after closing. Quarterly instalment payments are due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, with the final balance paid with the annual return.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly instalments are due by 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, calculated at 25% (or reduced 15% rate) of the prior year’s taxable income. The annual final balance is paid with the income tax return within three months of year-end (May 3 for calendar-year filers).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box A1 (Cadre A - Résultat imposable) Net taxable income or loss before allocation of carried-forward deficits; includes all business profits from electronically supplied pet product sales, after allowable deductions for e-commerce operating expenses, depreciation, stock costs, and write-offs. A non-resident-owned SARL with a French independent company files as a French tax resident company; all online revenues from French consumers are taxable in France. A foreign parent must not impute losses or profits from non-French activities into this box. Late filing triggers an automatic 10% increase of the tax due (CGI art. 1728-1); a formal notice unpaid for over 30 days adds a monthly 5% penalty up to 50%, plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month. In cases of intentional concealment or fraud, the penalty increases to 40% to 80%, with criminal exposure up to EUR 500,000 and five years’ imprisonment.
Box B1 / B2 (Cadre B - Déficits antérieurs) Carry-forward of tax losses utilized in the current year. Limited to EUR 1,000,000 plus 50% of taxable profit exceeding EUR 1,000,000. Carryback of prior year losses is reported subject to the three-year rule and EUR 1,000,000 cap. Only French-sourced tax losses of the French entity or branch may be carried forward. Foreign branch or parent company losses are not deductible against French taxable income. Late filing triggers an automatic 10% increase of the tax due (CGI art. 1728-1); a formal notice unpaid for over 30 days adds a monthly 5% penalty up to 50%, plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month. In cases of intentional concealment or fraud, the penalty increases to 40% to 80%, with criminal exposure up to EUR 500,000 and five years’ imprisonment.
Box W1 / W2 (Crédits d'impôt et réductions) Declaration of research tax credit, innovation tax credit, family tax credit, foreign tax credits, and withholding tax credits used against corporate income tax. Non-resident companies may claim credits only for taxes from French permanent establishments; treaty foreign tax credits must satisfy the OECD treaty provisions and cannot exceed the corresponding French CIT. Late filing triggers an automatic 10% increase of the tax due (CGI art. 1728-1); a formal notice unpaid for over 30 days adds a monthly 5% penalty up to 50%, plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month. In cases of intentional concealment or fraud, the penalty increases to 40% to 80%, with criminal exposure up to EUR 500,000 and five years’ imprisonment.
Line 10 (Amount of quarterly instalment) Amount of the instalment computed on the previous taxable year’s final CIT assessed, net of the reduced SME rate and tax credits. Foreign-owned SARL must use a French bank account for the tax payment; no offset with DTA foreign tax credits until the annual return is filed. Non-payment of instalments within 30 days triggers a 10% late payment surcharge, monthly late-payment interest at 0.20% per month, plus a 5% increase applied by the tax authority; forged returns attract criminal sanctions under CGI arts. 1729 and 1741.
Line 45 (Balance payable) Difference between total annual CIT and quarterly installments already paid; adjusted for current-year events and tax credits claimed in boxes W1/W2. If the SARL is a permanent establishment of a non-resident foreign parent, the parent’s head office tax credits cannot reduce the French balance.
