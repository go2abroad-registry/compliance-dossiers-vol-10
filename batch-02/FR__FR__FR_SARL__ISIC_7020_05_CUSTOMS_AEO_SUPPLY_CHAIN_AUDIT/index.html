Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry), operating the Registre National du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the INPI Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.210-1 et seq. (general company provisions), L.223-1 to L.223-43 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée / SARL), Decree No. 84-406 of 30 May 1984 (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés), and Order No. 2023-393 (single-window filing regime).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) together with the CERFA M0 form (Demande d'immatriculation d'une personne morale), filed electronically via the INPI single-window portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for an SARL (may be set at EUR 1). Each cash contribution must be paid in to at least 20% (one-fifth) at formation, with the balance payable within five years; cash funds must be deposited with a licensed French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations (escrow) before RCS registration, and in-kind contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation and valued by a commissaire aux apports if the contribution exceeds EUR 30,000 or on shareholder request.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety compliance of the office premises under the Code de la Construction et de l'Habitation (ERP third-category escape routes, fire extinguisher inspection), electrical-installation conformity certificate, DREETS occupational-health-and-safety inspection under the Code du Travail, annual employee safety training, and, for AEO certification, physical security controls including access management, CCTV, tamper-evident procedures and structured supply-chain security screening for the audited customs facility.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for an SARL (may be set at EUR 1). Each cash contribution must be paid in to at least 20% (one-fifth) at formation, with the balance payable within five years; cash funds must be deposited with a licensed French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations (escrow) before RCS registration, and in-kind contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation and valued by a commissaire aux apports if the contribution exceeds EUR 30,000 or on shareholder request.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial operating license is required for ISIC 7020 management-consulting and audited AEO supply-chain compliance activities; workplace compliance is supervised by the DREETS (Direction régionale de l'économie, de l'emploi, du travail et des solidarités) and voluntary customs authorization is administered by the DGDDI (Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects), in coordination with the CCI (Chambre de Commerce et d'Industrie) for trade facilitation.

Category: Kbis extract (RCS registration certificate) as the general business authorization; optional Authorized Economic Operator – Customs Simplifications (AEO-C / AEO-S, now 'AEOF') certificate issued by DGDDI under Articles 38 and 39 of the Union Customs Code for security and safety audit credentialing.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for all IMF Article VIII operations. No prior authorisation, licence or central bank foreign-exchange approval is required for capital injections into a French SARL by non-resident shareholders, for invoicing of overseas AEO clients, for profit/dividend remittance or liquidation proceeds to foreign shareholders, or for maintenance of foreign-currency accounts at home or abroad. The operative constraint is declarative rather than permissive: every cross-border financial flow must be reported by the resident company or its banking counterpart to the Banque de France within 20 days after the end of the month in which the flow occurs, using the authorised purpose codes of the French balance-of-payments nomenclature. Profit-repatriation hurdles are confined to corporate and fiscal formalities: dividends require a shareholders' approval and the closure of accounts; distributions to non-EU/EEA residents attract a 25% withholding tax unless reduced by treaty; qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders may claim the 0% Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption supported by a certificate of tax residence and beneficial-ownership statement. No exchange commission, residual surrender duty or convertibility fee is imposed on the AEO consultancy's ordinary cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory repatriation timeline and no statutory conversion ratio. Exporters and cross-border service suppliers may retain receipts indefinitely in any currency, including on foreign-currency bank accounts, provided the associated operation is declared in the Banque de France monthly balance-of-payments statement using the service-revenue operation code for ISIC 7020 / AEO supply-chain consulting, and invoice plus SWIFT confirmation evidence is retained for the statutory six-year preservation period. French customs law is indifferent to the payment rail; AEO certification and supply-chain audit services are invoiced as services, not merchandise exports, and therefore no export-receipt declaration in the sense used by French customs (declaration en douane de marchandises) is applicable.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Paris) - the national central bank and balance-of-payments statistical authority - acting jointly with the Direction Generale du Tresor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty) under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monetaire et financier, Articles L. 151-1 et seq. and R. 152-1 et seq.). Day-to-day prudential supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions and e-money institutions rests with the ACPR (Autorite de Controle Prudentiel et de Resolution) in coordination with the European Central Bank under France's Eurosystem membership.
