Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full capital account convertibility with no prior approval requirements for profit repatriation or capital movements. Current account transactions are unrestricted. Profit remittance from French SARL to foreign parent entities is permitted subject only to standard corporate governance, tax filing, and AML compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds applies under French law. Companies must retain supporting trade documentation and may file statistical declarations with the Banque de France for exports exceeding €50,000. No statutory conversion ratios or timelines are prescribed.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate (Art. 219 CGI); effective blended rate 25.8% when the 3.3% social contribution is triggered on tax exceeding €763,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory withholding tax of 25% on dividends distributed to non-resident legal entities (Art. 119 bis CGI); 12.8% withholding for non-resident individuals (Art. 117 quater CGI); 0% for qualifying EU parent companies holding at least 10% of the SARL's capital for two years under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive implemented by Art. 119 ter CGI.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months of the fiscal year-end (Art. 223-1 CGI); for a calendar-year company, the filing date is the second business day after 1 May.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Instalments due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December of the fiscal year; balancing payment due at the filing of Form 2065-SD.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File under Art. L.13 AA of the French Tax Procedure Code where the French entity or the group records annual turnover or gross balance-sheet assets above €400 million; Country-by-Country reporting is required for groups with consolidated revenue of at least €750 million. For smaller CAD/BIM service SARLs below these thresholds, no statutory TP file is mandatory, but on audit the burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer when contemporaneous arm's-length documentation is absent, exposing the company to the Art. 1740 ter CGI penalty of 5% of reassessed profits (minimum €10,000).

Economic substance scope: France has not enacted UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations; substance is enforced through the general anti-abuse rule (Art. L.64 of the French Tax Procedure Code), EU ATAD CFC rules (Art. 209 B CGI), anti-hybrid rules, and the OECD treaty-shopping framework. A French SARL performing genuine CAD/BIM drafting, employing local engineers, holding a local bank account, maintaining board minutes and bearing operational risk satisfies the substance requirement; a hollow conduit without employees and premises is rebuttable as an abuse of law and may trigger an 80% penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Art. 1741 CGI: fine up to €500,000 per offender (up to €3 million in aggravated circumstances) and imprisonment from one to five years (seven years where the offence involves organised crime); civil tax consequences include reassessment with 40% bad-faith or 80% fraud increases plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax and penalties under Art. 1840 CGI; criminal courts may impose travel bans, confiscation of assets, and imprisonment; directors may also face civil restitution to the Treasury and disqualification from corporate management.
Shell Company / Abusive Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the arrangement under the abuse-of-law procedure (Art. L.64 LPF) leading to reassessment of hidden profits as dividends (Art. 111 c CGI), application of an 80% penalty (100% where the abuse was concealed), and imposition of the 25% dividend withholding tax on the deemed distribution.

Director personal liability: The managing director is personally and severally liable for the abuse-of-law penalty under Art. 1840 CGI; directors may incur commercial disqualification, personal insolvency proceedings, and criminal liability if the shell arrangement served to launder tax fraud proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the hidden profit transfer with a 5% penalty on the reassessed amount (minimum €10,000) under Art. 1740 ter CGI, plus a 40% bad-faith increase; the burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer when documentation is absent, and the hidden profit may be recharacterised as a constructive dividend subject to withholding tax.

Director personal liability: The director who signed the deficient return or approved the non-arm's-length pricing may be held jointly liable for the penalty under Art. 1840 CGI; failure to implement OECD-compliant documentation can expose directors to professional negligence claims and regulatory sanctions.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions under the Monetary and Financial Code (Art. L.561-1 and following): up to five years' imprisonment and a €375,000 fine for individuals, and a €1,875,000 fine for the company; Tracfin reporting obligations apply; banking relationships may be frozen and the corporate registration suspended.

Director personal liability: Directors face imprisonment for money-laundering of tax fraud proceeds, travel bans, asset seizure, and civil liability to the Treasury; directors may be prohibited from managing commercial companies for up to 15 years.
