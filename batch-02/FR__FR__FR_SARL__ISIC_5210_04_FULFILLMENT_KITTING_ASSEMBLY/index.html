Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for a French SARL; the capital is freely fixed in the Articles. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked share-subscription account at a French credit institution or at the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the depositary certificate is required before filing Form M0. The funds are released only upon issuance of the RCS certificate.

Funding & unlock path: For premises up to 1,250 m² not open to the public, no fire-safety authorization is required, but the company must still install detection, emergency exits, extinguishers, and an evacuation plan and may request the local SDIS (Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours) for advice. A structural load inspection is recommended for high-bay racking and mezzanines. The employment site must have a DUERP completed and displayed labor-code notices; before commencing operations, the DREETS may require a declaration of opening if employees are hired.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for a French SARL; the capital is freely fixed in the Articles. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked share-subscription account at a French credit institution or at the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the depositary certificate is required before filing Form M0. The funds are released only upon issuance of the RCS certificate.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No specific industrial licensing board for light kitting and fulfillment assembly. Relevant oversight authorities are the Direction Régionale de l'Environnement, de l'Aménagement et du Logement (DREAL) for ICPE compliance and the Direction Régionale de l'Économie, de l'Emploi, du Travail et des Solidarités (DREETS) for workplace health and safety.

Category: No mandatory operating license under French law for warehousing and kitting activities below the ICPE thresholds; optional voluntary certifications (ISO 9001, security clearance) may be adopted for commercial purposes.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate an SARL via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), which generates a SIREN/SIRET through INSEE. 2) The DGFiP automatically assigns a French VAT number (numéro d'identification à la TVA intracommunautaire) to the registered entity. 3) For a foreign entity with no French establishment, the procedure is to file form 1542-SD (Demande d'inscription au fichier des organismes étrangers) with the competent SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) or through the Guichet Unique, appointing a tax representative if required for VAT purposes. 4) A SIRET is issued for each place of activity, and the entity must file an initial VAT return (form 1142 or 1143) depending on closing. 5) The VAT number is verified by the French government's intra-community VIES validator.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — 1) Incorporate an SARL via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), which generates a SIREN/SIRET through INSEE. 2) The DGFiP automatically assigns a French VAT number (numéro d'identification à la TVA intracommunautaire) to the registered entity. 3) For a foreign entity with no French establishment, the procedure is to file form 1542-SD (Demande d'inscription au fichier des organismes étrangers) with the competent SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) or through the Guichet Unique, appointing a tax representative if required for VAT purposes. 4) A SIRET is issued for each place of activity, and the entity must file an initial VAT return (form 1142 or 1143) depending on closing. 5) The VAT number is verified by the French government's intra-community VIES validator.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law does not operate a universal check-the-box regime. An SARL is by default chargeable to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Statutory elections allow pass-through taxation in narrowly defined cases: (i) family-owned SARL (SARL de famille); and (ii) a small commercial/artisanal/industrial/professional SARL employing fewer than 50 employees and with turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million may opt for income tax for up to five years (article 239 bis AA CGI). Additionally, foreign entity classification for hybrid mismatch purposes follows article 165 bis CGI and the ATAD provisions, and an SARL may be recharacterized for French tax purposes if its legal form is equivalent to a transparent entity under the criteria of legal equivalence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA: EUR 91,900 for goods-supplying activities and EUR 36,800 for services-providing activities (annual turnover); above these thresholds, registration is mandatory for French-established businesses. Non-established foreign suppliers have no exemption threshold and must register if any taxable supply is deemed to take place in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT at 0% applies to supplies of goods physically sent or transported outside the EU by the seller or a third party for account of the seller, provided the exporter retains evidence of export within 90 days from the chargeable event (or, under recent flexibility, within the time limit to file the VAT return). The goods must leave the EU before the end of the third month following the transfer of power to dispose. For services to non-EU business customers, the place of supply is outside France, thus outside French VAT; certain ancillary services linked to exported goods qualify for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU - Document Administratif Unique or DEX)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent transport document: Air Waybill, CMR)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export payment
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List

Electronic invoicing becomes mandatory for all established taxable persons for B2B transactions from 1 September 2026 (large and mid-size enterprises) and 1 September 2027 (small and micro enterprises). Invoices must be transmitted through a certified PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) or the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation), using the Factur-X hybrid format. Transaction data are transmitted to DGFiP for automated fiscal clearance and pre-filling of VAT returns; there is no per-invoice stamping or real-time clearance in the sense of a continuous control system.

The French tax administration (DGFiP) may challenge the 0% application if the export proof is incomplete, missing, or contradictory, and may reassess VAT at 20% (TVA au taux normal), together with the late payment interest ('intérêts de retard' of 0.2% per month) and penalties: 40% for deliberate infringement (manquement délibéré), 80% for fraudulent behavior or if the taxpayer has not submitted a return within 30 days after a formal notice. In cases of export fraud, criminal sanctions may apply to company representatives, and the tax administration may also claim restitution of deducted input VAT. Retrospective clawback is permitted within the statutory re-assessment period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area jurisdiction with full current-account and capital-account convertibility, subject to European Union financial-sanctions controls, anti-money-laundering requirements, tax rules, cash-reporting rules, payment-services regulation and limited statistical reporting. The euro is freely transferable for ordinary operating payments, imports, exports, dividends, interest, royalties, intercompany settlements and loan repayments. A French SARL may generally hold and use foreign-currency accounts and remit distributable profits to non-resident shareholders without a general exchange-control approval. Profit distributions require finalized annual accounts, an available distributable profit or lawful reserve, shareholder approval, satisfaction of corporate-law restrictions, withholding-tax analysis, beneficial-owner verification and completion of any applicable treaty or European Union directive documentation. Cross-border capital contributions, shareholder loans and treasury transfers may be subject to bank source-of-funds review, related-party documentation, transfer-pricing requirements, thin-capitalization or interest-limitation rules, and tax reporting. Payments involving sanctioned persons, sanctioned jurisdictions, prohibited sectors or unusual payment chains may be blocked or escalated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio and no general deadline requiring a French exporter to convert foreign-currency export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may normally remain in the invoiced currency or be converted at the company’s discretion through an authorized payment-service provider or credit institution. The company must retain commercial invoices, transport and customs evidence, contracts, remittance records and accounting support. Banks may request trade documents and transaction-purpose information under AML, sanctions, fraud-prevention and regulatory-reporting controls. Cash or cash-equivalent movements of EUR 10,000 or more into or out of the European Union require the applicable customs declaration; this rule does not generally apply to ordinary bank transfers, which remain subject to bank monitoring and reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: European Central Bank for monetary policy and euro-system oversight; Banque de France for national central-bank functions, payment-system oversight, balance-of-payments and statistical reporting; Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution for prudential supervision of credit institutions and certain payment institutions; Autorité des Marchés Financiers for financial-market conduct and investment-services regulation; Direction Générale du Trésor for national financial-policy administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium to High for routine EUR collections, SEPA payments, card expenditure, multi-currency balances and treasury administration, subject to the provider’s licensing perimeter, country risk policy, safeguarding model and account-closure rules. Airwallex and comparable electronic-money institutions or payment institutions may provide payment accounts or safeguarded funds but do not necessarily provide a French bank deposit account, French IBAN, deposit-guarantee coverage or capital-deposit services acceptable for every incorporation workflow. Offshore digital EMIs without authorization under the EU Payment Services Directive framework, passporting rights, or an equivalent legally recognized authorization should not be used for regulated payment services to or from the French SARL. Fintech accounts should supplement, rather than replace, a primary French bank account for payroll, taxes, customs, statutory payments, capital deposits and business continuity. The company must verify the provider’s legal entity, authorization register entry, safeguarding arrangements, terms for frozen funds, permitted countries, beneficial-owner requirements and data-access procedures.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route capital contributions through an account held in the exact legal name of the SARL or through the designated capital-deposit account of the incorporating bank. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR transfers from participating institutions and SWIFT cross-border transfer for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions. The remitter name, beneficiary name, legal entity identifier where available, and payment narrative should clearly identify the capital contribution, capital increase or shareholder loan. Do not route funds through personal accounts or unrelated third parties. Reconcile the incoming amount to the accounting entry and retain the payment message, bank advice and supporting corporate documents. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory where the French entity reports annual net turnover above €50 million or total balance-sheet assets above €400 million; Country-by-Country reporting and Master File apply where the multinational group reaches consolidated revenue of €750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation applicable to domestic ordinary trading entities. The French general anti-abuse rule (Article L.64 LPF) and the OECD Principal Purpose Test embedded in tax treaties impose a genuine substance requirement. A fulfillment/kitting SARL with physical premises, storage equipment, and 3PL personnel meets the required operational substance threshold.
