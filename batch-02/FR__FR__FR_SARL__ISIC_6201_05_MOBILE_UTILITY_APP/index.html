UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Primary AML/CFT rules are in Articles L.561-1 to L.561-50 and Articles R.561-1 to R.561-23 of the French Code monétaire et financier. Beneficial-owner identification and filing for commercial companies are principally governed by Articles L.561-46 to L.561-48 and Articles R.561-55 to R.561-63 of the Code monétaire et financier, together with Articles R.123-30-1 et seq. of the Code de commerce and the applicable register-filings regulations. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SARL must have a registered office in France under Articles L.123-11 et seq. of the Code de commerce. A domiciliation company or virtual-office provider may be used if it is legally authorized and the domiciliation contract, proof of address, and required declarations are available. A director may in certain circumstances domicile the company at the director's personal address for the legally permitted period and conditions. A virtual office is generally acceptable for a software and mobile-application business with no inventory, laboratory, customer-facing premises, or regulated operational facility, but it does not replace actual management, accounting, records, data-protection, employment, or operational substance. The company should maintain accessible corporate records, reliable contact details, French tax and accounting records, and evidence of genuine software-development and commercial activity.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI): https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr/

Name reservation / lock period

France does not generally provide a universal statutory company-name reservation period of 30 days through the Guichet unique. The applicant should conduct an INPI availability search, search the Registre national des entreprises, review trademark databases, domain names, and relevant trade names, and file the selected name promptly. If the registry or formalities authority rejects or suspends the filing, obtain the written reason, correct the name or supporting documents, and resubmit through the Guichet unique within the stated correction period. For a formal administrative decision, use the applicable administrative-recourse route: submit a reasoned recours gracieux to the issuing authority, and, where appropriate, a recours hiérarchique or judicial challenge before the competent administrative court within the applicable deadline, normally two months from notification when the decision and applicable rules provide that period. A registry-specific refusal or clerk decision may instead require a commercial-court or designated registry challenge; obtain the decision notice and use the remedy stated there. Separate trademark opposition or cancellation procedures before INPI apply where the issue concerns trademark rights rather than company-name registration.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the judicial district of the registered office, acting through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) and recording the company in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

Code de commerce (French Commercial Code): L.210-1 to L.210-6; SARL applicable provisions L.223-1 through L.223-43; paid-in capital rules under L.223-7; registration and filing procedure under R.123-1 et seq. and R.210-1 et seq.; Décret n°67-236 on the deposit of funds for companies in formation.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic Declaration of Business Formation (Déclaration de création d'entreprise, successor to the Cerfa M0 form) filed via the Guichet Unique, with the signed Statuts and supporting exhibits attached; registration is evidenced by the Kbis extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 8 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for a SARL; the capital is freely set in the Statuts (commonly EUR 1). Under Art. L.223-7 C. com., at least 20% of cash subscriptions must be paid up at subscription and the balance paid within five years. The subscribed funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, released only upon RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – Ministry of Economy and Finance; online portal at impots.gouv.fr; business formalities via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL (or foreign entity establishing a permanent establishment), the following steps apply: (1) file articles of association and registration declaration with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce through the Guichet Unique; (2) receive SIREN and SIRET identifiers from INSEE; (3) after legal registration, the DGFiP automatically assigns a VAT identification number (n° d'identification TVA intracommunautaire) to be used for national and EU VAT purposes; (4) if the entity is a non-established foreign entity, appoint a fiscal representative and apply directly to the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for a VAT ID and a French tax number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SARL (or foreign entity establishing a permanent establishment), the following steps apply: (1) file articles of association and registration declaration with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce through the Guichet Unique; (2) receive SIREN and SIRET identifiers from INSEE; (3) after legal registration, the DGFiP automatically assigns a VAT identification number (n° d'identification TVA intracommunautaire) to be used for national and EU VAT purposes; (4) if the entity is a non-established foreign entity, appoint a fiscal representative and apply directly to the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for a VAT ID and a French tax number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Under the French General Tax Code (Code général des impôts – CGI), a SARL is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by default. However, certain elections are permitted: (i) a newly created SARL may elect to be treated as a pass-through partnership (régime fiscal des sociétés de personnes) for up to five fiscal years under article 239 bis AA, provided it meets the conditions of a 'jeune entreprise' (no public offering, majority of capital held by individuals); (ii) a 'family SARL' (SARL de famille) may definitively elect for income tax (IR) under article 239 bis AA if all shareholders are related within a certain degree. No formal hybrid-mismatch check-the-box election exists in French law; unincorporated joint ventures or GIE structures may choose transparency under specific provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic transactions, VAT registration is mandatory once taxable turnover exceeds the 'franchise en base de TVA' threshold: EUR 91,900 for supplies of goods and EUR 36,800 for services (annual, excluding tax). For mobile utility apps (electronically supplied services), the B2C cross-border digital services threshold of EUR 10,000 per annum applies for supplies to consumers in other EU Member States under the Union OSS scheme. Any B2B supplies in France generally entitle the recipient to self-assess VAT under reverse charge, and the foreign supplier must register if the place of supply is French and no reverse charge applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For tangible goods exported outside the European Union, a 0% VAT rate (exonération à l'export) applies when the supplier retains proof that the goods have physically left EU territory through a valid customs export declaration. For intra-EU supplies, 0% VAT applies if the purchaser provides a valid EU VAT ID and transport evidence. For the mobile utility app (electronically supplied service): (i) supplies to consumers (B2C) resident outside the EU are outside the scope of French VAT; (ii) supplies to customers resident in other EU Member States follow the place-of-supply rules – B2B supplies are reverse-charged to the recipient (customer accounts for VAT), and B2C supplies are subject to French VAT unless the supplier opts for the OSS non-Union/Union schemes and declares VAT in the customer's EU Member State; (iii) supplies to French customers are always subject to French VAT. To claim a 0% VAT treatment for exports of goods, the supplier must (1) obtain an export customs declaration (déclaration d'exportation via DELTA), (2) evidence transport outside the EU (e.g., bill of lading, airway bill, CMR waybill), and (3) retain the commercial invoice and, where applicable, remittance/payment proof.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation via DELTA system)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (original or signed electronic transport document)
  • Commercial Invoice (original)
  • Packing List
  • Proof of Consideration — Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation showing receipt of export proceeds

France is mandating electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises under the French Finance Act 2022. E-invoices must be structured in Factur-X or EDI format and transmitted through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the mandatory Portail Public de Facturation (PPF). A parallel e-reporting obligation applies to B2C and cross-border transactions. This is not a real-time clearance or digital-stamping system; data is transmitted to the tax authority automatically within 5 days of issuance, but there is no immediate validation or stamping in the style of Latin American fiscal clearance models.

The French tax administration (DGFiP) may retrospectively deny the 0% VAT rate and assess French VAT at 20% (plus interest) if export evidence is missing or proves fraudulent. Deliberately false claims or abuse are subject to a 40% to 80% penalty (majore la taxe) under article 1729 of the CGI, together with 0.20% monthly late interest (or 0.40% after 6 months). In serious cases, the criminal fine for tax evasion under the French Tax Procedure Code may reach EUR 750,000 and/or imprisonment of 5 years. For foreign exchange repatriation, exceeding the 60-day deadline for service exports (150 days for goods) without an authorized extension may trigger administrative fines and the forced alignment of the operation under the French exchange-control regime, with penalties up to EUR 30,000 and possible retrospective VAT reassessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full euro convertibility under a floating exchange-rate regime with no separate official or market exchange rate. Capital and current account transactions are broadly liberalised for EU residents; restrictions apply only to transactions with sanctioned jurisdictions, to foreign direct investments requiring prior approval under French strategic-sector regulations, and to the collection of statistical data. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are not subject to foreign-exchange authorisation, but are subject to dividend taxation formalism, possible withholding tax at the domestic rate of 25% or a treaty-reduced rate, and French anti-money-laundering reporting where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation in France. Exporters are not required to repatriate receivables within a fixed period or to convert export proceeds into euros. Export settlement in euro or a freely convertible foreign currency is permitted. Export receipts flowing through French-resident banks may be recorded for balance-of-payments statistical reporting by Banque de France, and banks may require supporting transport, customs, or commercial invoices for sanctions screening and source-of-funds verification.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting with the Direction Générale du Trésor of the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; France operates under EU capital-movement rules including Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, with Banque de France responsible for balance-of-payments statistics, foreign-exchange monitoring, and financial sanctions enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for a French SARL: Medium for ACPR-licensed French electronic money institutions and payment institutions, including Qonto, Shine, and Manager.one, which may operate under PSD2 passporting and provide euro-denominated French IBANs. Feasibility rating for non-EU offshore digital EMIs: Low. Such offshore EMIs are not ACPR-supervised, cannot provide valid French or SEPA settlement participation, may expose the SARL to sanctions and AML de-risking, and are unsuitable for capital escrow or dividend-conduit accounts. A compliant treasury structure should use a tier-1 domestic bank as principal account holder and use a licensed EMI only as an auxiliary disbursement rail for operating expenses and payroll where the EMI is subject to ACPR supervision or an equivalent EU supervisory authority.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from shareholders should be routed in euros using a SEPA Credit Transfer if the originator holds a SEPA account, or a SWIFT MT103 for inbound transfers from non-SEPA countries. The remittance information must accurately state the purpose code, generally 'INTC' for intra-group capital movements or 'CNB' for cash-management transfers where accepted, and must contain the explicit narrative 'Apport en capital / SARL capital contribution' together with the corporate registration identifier. The bank must be provided with the authorising shareholders' meeting minutes, updated extract Kbis, the UBO declaration, French foreign-direct-investment clearance documentation where the sector is sensitive or the investor is a non-EU government-controlled entity, and the source-of-funds proof. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating for a French SARL: Medium for ACPR-licensed French electronic money institutions and payment institutions, including Qonto, Shine, and Manager.one, which may operate under PSD2 passporting and provide euro-denominated French IBANs. Feasibility rating for non-EU offshore digital EMIs: Low. Such offshore EMIs are not ACPR-supervised, cannot provide valid French or SEPA settlement participation, may expose the SARL to sanctions and AML de-risking, and are unsuitable for capital escrow or dividend-conduit accounts. A compliant treasury structure should use a tier-1 domestic bank as principal account holder and use a licensed EMI only as an auxiliary disbursement rail for operating expenses and payroll where the EMI is subject to ACPR supervision or an equivalent EU supervisory authority.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File obligations arise where the French entity's annual gross revenue (excluding VAT) or balance sheet gross assets exceed €400,000,000, or where the multinational group reports consolidated turnover above €750,000,000. A smaller SARL must nevertheless maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing evidence for all cross-border related-party transactions, including intercompany royalty charges and app distribution agreements.

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to UAE or Guernsey. Substance requirements are enforced through the OECD BEPS minimum standard, EU ATAD, and French general anti-abuse doctrine under Article L64 of the Livre des procédures fiscales. The SARL must demonstrate real economic presence (staff, premises, management decisions, operational bank account) to claim treaty benefits and to avoid recharacterization of the in-app revenue structure as a sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine for the legal entity up to €500,000 (or twice the evaded amount), imprisonment up to 5 years for the principal; administrative penalties of 40%–80% of evaded tax plus 0.2% monthly interest; confiscation of sums involved.

Director personal liability: The Gérant or legal director who organized the fraud is personally prosecuted under Article 1741 CGI, may be barred from commercial activity, subjected to travel bans, ordered to pay civil restitution equal to the evaded tax and penalties, and in serious cases imprisoned.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: DGFIP may invoke the abuse-of-law procedure under Article L64 LPB, recharacterize the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, impose an 80% penalty on the reattributed profit, and assess additional corporate income tax with late interest; the SARL may also be deemed to have a French permanent establishment for the true economic owner.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers face joint and several liability for the assessed tax and penalties; personal liability for the entity's debts can arise under veil-piercing principles where the incorporation was fraudulent; travel bans and seizure of personal assets may be ordered in tax recovery proceedings.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €30,000 for the legal entity and/or imprisonment of the responsible officer; administrative penalties for failure to file can reach €50,000; non-cooperative state payments may be subject to 75% withholding under Article 238 A CGI.

Director personal liability: Officers may be disqualified from management, placed under judicial travel restrictions, ordered to repay diverted funds as part of civil restitution, and face imprisonment for knowingly obstructing French AML and tax investigation authorities.
