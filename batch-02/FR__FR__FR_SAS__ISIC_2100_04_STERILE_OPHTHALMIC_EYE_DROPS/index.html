Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

French SAS entities are automatically assigned a SIREN/SIRET number upon registration with the INSEE. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full capital account convertibility under EU law and IMF Article VIII obligations. The euro is freely convertible for current and capital transactions with no prior approval or surrender requirements for profit remittances to shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies under French or EU regulations. Receipts may be repatriated freely within standard commercial terms (typically 90-180 days) subject to AML/KYC verification, tax reporting, and underlying trade documentation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax on taxable profits. Eligible small companies may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject generally to turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up capital, and qualifying ownership conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The general French withholding tax on dividends and comparable distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident is 25%, subject to exemptions and treaty reduction. Dividends paid to a qualifying EU or EEA parent may be exempt under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring a qualifying ownership interest, minimum holding conditions, beneficial ownership, and anti-abuse compliance. A treaty may reduce the rate, commonly to 5% or 15%, depending on the recipient, ownership percentage, beneficial ownership, and applicable treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and tax package are generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the year following the financial year-end. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the deadline is normally in early May, with the exact electronic deadline published annually by the tax administration. A company with a non-calendar year-end generally files within three months after the year-end, subject to the applicable administrative rules. Corporate income tax installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, with the final balance generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following the year-end, or by 15 May for a 31 December year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million of pre-tax turnover or gross assets, or another statutory trigger applying to a qualifying French entity or group, generally triggers comprehensive transfer-pricing documentation comprising a Local File and Master File. A French entity or group meeting the lower EUR 50 million threshold may also be required to file the annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration on Form 2257-SD. Documentation may be requested below these thresholds where the tax administration considers the transactions material or abnormal.

Economic substance scope: France has no separate United Arab Emirates-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance is nevertheless required under French transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-abuse doctrine, beneficial-ownership principles, anti-hybrid rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest-limitation provisions, and treaty anti-abuse standards. The French SAS should maintain genuine French decision-making, qualified personnel, premises or operational capacity appropriate to its activities, manufacturing and quality-control evidence, risk control, asset ownership records, and contemporaneous commercial rationale. A company incorporated in France but directed and economically operated abroad may face transfer-pricing adjustments, permanent-establishment issues, treaty denial, recharacterization, or tax-abuse findings.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax declarations

Statutory sanction: Additional tax generally includes the unpaid tax, late-payment interest, and penalties of up to 80% in aggravated cases. Criminal tax fraud may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, increased in aggravated circumstances to up to 7 years and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who personally participate may incur criminal liability, civil tax liability, director disqualification, and liability for damage caused to the company or creditors. Courts may impose asset seizure and, where legally justified, restrictions affecting travel or liberty; imprisonment is available for qualifying offenses.
Shell company, artificial arrangement, or abuse of rights

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may disregard the arrangement, deny treaty or exemption benefits, reallocate profits, and impose the applicable tax, interest, and abuse-of-rights penalty, generally 40% and potentially 80% for fraudulent maneuvers.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for deliberate misconduct, tax-abuse participation, creditor prejudice, and breach of management duties. Criminal exposure may arise where the arrangement involves false documents, fraud, laundering, or concealment.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Taxable income may be increased under the French abnormal-act-of-management and transfer-pricing rules, with corporate income tax, interest, and penalties. Missing or materially deficient documentation may trigger the statutory documentation penalty, generally the higher of EUR 10,000 or 0.5% of covered transactions, in addition to the reassessed tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable where they knowingly approve sham transactions, false documentation, or unlawful distributions. The company and responsible individuals may also face criminal exposure if records are falsified or tax fraud is established.
Improper profit repatriation and withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The French payer may be assessed for the unpaid withholding tax, late-payment interest, and applicable penalties. Recharacterized hidden distributions may also produce corporate income tax adjustments and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur liability for knowingly authorizing unlawful distributions, misleading tax filings, or withholding-tax evasion. Shareholders may be required to repay unlawful distributions where statutory conditions are met.
VAT and regulated pharmaceutical compliance

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, interest, penalties, suspension or withdrawal of regulatory authorization, product recall, destruction orders, administrative fines, and criminal sanctions may apply. Serious public-health offenses may carry substantial fines and imprisonment under the French Public Health Code.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible managers may face personal criminal liability for placing unsafe or unauthorized medicinal products on the market, obstruction of inspections, falsification of quality records, or deliberate VAT fraud. Civil claims for patient injury and product liability may be substantial.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-ownership failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative and criminal money-laundering sanctions may include confiscation, substantial fines, regulatory measures, and imprisonment. Money laundering may generally carry up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 375,000, with aggravated cases carrying higher penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who facilitate or knowingly ignore laundering may be prosecuted personally. Assets may be frozen or confiscated, and courts may impose management prohibitions and other liberty restrictions.
Accounting and corporate-record falsification

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include rejection of accounts, tax reassessment, corporate sanctions, insolvency proceedings, director-management bans, fines, and imprisonment for qualifying accounting or insolvency offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for insufficiency of assets, contribution claims in insolvency, disqualification, and criminal liability for misuse of corporate assets, fraudulent bankruptcy, or false accounts.
Unlawful dividend or capital distribution

Statutory sanction: The distribution may be void or repayable, with shareholder and director liability, tax recharacterization, and potential criminal sanctions under French company law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore funds, compensate creditors or the company, and may face criminal prosecution for fictitious dividends, misuse of corporate assets, or intentional creditor prejudice.
Failure to preserve tax, transfer-pricing, and quality documentation

Statutory sanction: Deductions and credits may be denied, taxable income reassessed, withholding exemptions rejected, and administrative penalties imposed for missing transfer-pricing or regulatory documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur liability where the documentation failure reflects deliberate concealment, obstruction, reckless management, or participation in a fraudulent filing. Personal exposure is not automatic for an ordinary administrative omission.
