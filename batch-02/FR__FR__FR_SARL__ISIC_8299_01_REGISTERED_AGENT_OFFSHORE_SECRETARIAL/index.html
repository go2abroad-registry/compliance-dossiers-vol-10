Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Permissible for this industry subject to Code de commerce Articles L.123-11-1 to L.123-11-4. A SARL may elect its registered office (siège social) at the premises of a professional domiciliation company, but the arrangement requires: (i) a written domiciliation contract with the provider; (ii) the provider's valid prior declaration with the Préfecture du département (the provider must hold actual operational premises in France, not a post-office box, and must maintain personnel present during working hours); (iii) a renewable initial term not exceeding five years, extendable up to ten years; and (iv) a genuine French operational address distinguishable from a mere letterbox. For corporate secretarial and registered-agent lines of business, the domiciliation provider itself must not be a prohibited or under-sanction entity, and the arrangement must be disclosed to the RNE with the provider's identity. Pure 'virtual-only' postbox arrangements without any human presence or business substance expose the SARL to commercial registry scrutiny and tax residence challenges.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) maintained by the Greffe du tribunal de commerce of the registered-office jurisdiction, receiving filings through the Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises operated by INPI (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), with automatic notification to INSEE, URSSAF and DGFiP.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II — Articles L. 210-1 et seq. (regulated company domiciliation and registered-agent activity).

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for SARL (Law no. 2003-721 of 1 August 2003 abolished the former minimum); capital is freely fixed in the statuts. Cash subscriptions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the SARL in formation with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released only upon RCS registration. At least one-fifth of cash contributions must be paid up on incorporation and the balance within five years; in-kind contributions are fully released upon incorporation and, if their value exceeds legal thresholds, must be appraised by a commissaire aux apports.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls on capital movements within the EU and generally free movement of capital with non-EU countries, subject to EU sanctions and anti-money laundering (AML) reporting. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, but must comply with corporate tax withholding (if applicable) and transfer pricing documentation. No prior authorization is required for dividend distributions, but the company must file annual tax returns and report cross-border payments to the French tax authorities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the central bank or any conversion requirement. Exporters may retain foreign currency in authorized accounts, but must report all cross-border transactions to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics (via the 'Réglementation des changes' reporting). The reporting threshold is €12,500 per transaction or cumulative monthly reporting for larger flows. No statutory timeline for conversion; however, companies must maintain proper documentation for tax and customs purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in coordination with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) and the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) for banking supervision and foreign exchange policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. French-regulated EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Shine, N26 Business) offer streamlined onboarding and multi-currency accounts, but may have lower transaction limits and less robust trade finance capabilities. Cross-border EMIs from other EU states can passport into France under the PSD2 regime, but they must register with the ACPR. For a registered agent/offshore secretarial business, EMIs are viable for operational liquidity and low-volume cross-border payments, but for high-value trade settlements and LC operations, a full-service commercial bank is recommended. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot provide credit or hold client funds for investment; they are limited to payment services.

Defense evidence: Provide a full organizational chart with UBOs (individuals holding >25% shares or control), copies of passports, proof of address, and a declaration of the ultimate beneficial owner. Include the French 'Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs' (RBE) filing confirmation.
Unusual transaction patterns (e.g., high volume of cross-border wires with low substance)

Root cause: Unusual transaction patterns (e.g., high volume of cross-border wires with low substance)

Defense evidence: Provide a detailed business plan, client contracts, and a forecast of transaction volumes. Include a description of the company's role as a registered agent, with evidence of actual office space, staff, and operational expenses. Provide a list of clients and their business purposes, along with any relevant licenses (e.g., registered agent license).
Potential tax evasion or money laundering via offshore structures

Root cause: Potential tax evasion or money laundering via offshore structures

Defense evidence: Provide a tax compliance certificate from the French tax authorities, proof of filing of corporate tax returns, and a copy of the company's tax risk assessment. Include a letter from an external auditor confirming the company's adherence to transfer pricing rules and the absence of aggressive tax planning.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound capital injections (e.g., share capital increase or shareholder loans) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code. For capital contributions, use the French balance-of-payments code '12' (Direct Investment) or '10' (Capital Operations). The bank will require a copy of the shareholders' resolution, a notarized deed of increase, and proof of the source of funds. For shareholder loans, use code '11' (Loans) and provide a loan agreement. All transfers must be accompanied by a 'Déclaration de Transfert de Fonds' (DTF) if the amount exceeds €10,000. The receiving bank will verify the purpose and may request additional documentation to comply with AML regulations.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For payments to suppliers or service providers, use SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers. For documentary trade, use Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) via SWIFT MT700/MT707, with the bank acting as issuing or advising bank. For high-value transactions, consider using the TARGET2 system for euro-denominated payments. For non-euro currencies, use correspondent banking networks. All outbound payments must include a valid purpose code (e.g., '130' for services) and comply with the French foreign trade regulations. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard rate; reduced 15% applies to the first €42,433 of taxable profit for eligible small enterprises under Article 219 CGI)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders (Art. 119 bis(2) CGI); 30% for non-resident individuals (12.8% PFU plus 17.2% social levies); reduced or eliminated under tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

2056-SD – Déficits et amortissements réputés différés Current-year tax loss carryback/carryforward positions and imputation history. Apply the French statutory annual cap: €1M plus 50% of taxable profit above €1M; losses from a non-PE foreign source cannot be reported. Late filing: 5% penalty (10% if not filed within 30 days of formal notice), plus 0.20% monthly interest on tax due; deliberate concealment raises the penalty to 40% or 80%; criminal sanctions under Art. 1741 CGI up to €500,000 fine and 5 years' imprisonment for tax evasion.

Disclose the percentage held by each non-resident shareholder and attach the definitive list of beneficial owners where distributions or DTA relief are claimed. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: French Local File and Master File are mandatory where the French entity has annual turnover or gross assets ≥ €400 million or belongs to a multinational group exceeding €750 million consolidated revenue for Country-by-Country reporting; smaller companies must nevertheless keep contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation to prove arm's-length transactions.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations, but implements an abuse-of-law rule (Art. L.64 LPF) and CFC legislation (Art. 209 B CGI). A French SARL with no real office, employees, meetings, or independent management decision-making is disregarded as a letterbox, and its income is reattributed to the economic owners. Registered-agent-only structures are therefore subject to active anti-shell-company attack.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Non-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty under Art. 1741 CGI: fine up to €500,000 and 5 years' imprisonment; increased to €1,000,000 and 7 years where committed by an organised gang; administrative penalty of 40% or 80% for deliberate concealment, plus monthly interest of 0.20%.

Director personal liability: Directors (gérants) are personally and jointly liable under Art. L. 267 LPF for corporate taxes, penalties and interest where their deliberate mismanagement enabled evasion; travel bans, freezing of personal assets, and extradition are possible for non-resident officers.
Sham Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The French tax administration applies the abuse-of-law doctrine (Art. L.64 LPF) to disregard the SARL, reattributing its income to the foreign parent; the company loses input VAT deductions, incurs 80% penalty for concealment, and may face automatic dissolution for statutory non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors are civilly liable for the resulting tax adjustments; can be prohibited from managing a French company for up to 10 years; non-resident directors may be refused entry to France and have their vehicles or real estate seized under fiscal enforcement.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: ACPR/AMF sanctions up to €5 million or 10% of annual turnover for serious AML/CTF failures; criminal money-laundering penalties under Art. 324-1 Penal Code: up to 5 years' imprisonment and €375,000 fine (aggravated form: 10 years and €750,000); the registered-agent licence may be revoked and the entity dissolved.

Director personal liability: Directors have criminal liability for knowingly organizing money-laundering or circumventing Tracfin suspicious transaction reports; individual fines, confiscation of professional property, and professional interdiction are enforced on their personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment plus 5% transfer-pricing penalty on the recharacterised amount and 0.20% monthly interest; payments to non-cooperative jurisdictions are disallowed under Art. 238 A; criminal exposure arises where false documents are fabricated to support artificial pricing.

Director personal liability: The gérant who intentionally arranged undervalued or inflated intercompany flows is personally liable for the resulting tax credits and penalties; directors may be disqualified from corporate management and their personal bank accounts sealed during the fiscal enforcement procedure.
