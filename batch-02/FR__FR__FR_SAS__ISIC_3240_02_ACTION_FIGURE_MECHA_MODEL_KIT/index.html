Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent greffe of the Tribunal de commerce for the registered office, acting through the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS); filings are submitted through the national Guichet unique managed by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), including Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-20 and R. 123-1 et seq.; beneficial-owner declarations are governed by Articles L. 561-46 and R. 561-55 et seq. of the Monetary and Financial Code.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company-formation formalities submitted through the Guichet unique, requesting RCS registration and filing the beneficial-owner declaration; no separate statutory M0 form is ordinarily filed.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to a SAS; the articles set the capital. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at subscription, with the balance payable within five years after registration. Cash contributions are deposited with an authorized depositary, typically a bank or notary, and evidenced by a deposit certificate; funds are released after RCS registration. Contributions in kind are generally fully paid and require a contributions auditor unless a statutory exemption applies. The stated USD amount is an approximate conversion of the RCS registration charge and excludes the variable mandatory legal-notice publication charge, beneficial-owner filing charge, and any service-provider fees; verify current tariffs.

Funding & unlock path: Before use, comply with building-planning and construction rules, workplace health and safety requirements, fire-prevention duties, and applicable electrical and machinery-safety standards. An establishment open to the public (ERP) requires applicable fire and accessibility approvals; an ordinary industrial workplace does not have a universal standalone fire certificate. Obtain building-owner or authority approvals for structural alterations and equipment loads, and complete workplace risk assessment and required safety inspections.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No general industrial operating licence or industrial licensing board applies to the described manufacturing activity. The competent prefecture and Direction régionale de l'environnement, de l'aménagement et du logement (DREAL) oversee installations classified for environmental protection (ICPE), where applicable.

Category: No general manufacturing operating permit is required solely for producing plastic model kits. Prior ICPE declaration, registration, or environmental authorization may be required depending on the actual processes, equipment, storage, substances, and applicable ICPE nomenclature thresholds.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE); the official online tax portal is impots.gouv.fr. Foreign businesses without a French establishment generally deal with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE).

E-government filing portal

Register the SAS through the business formalities single window (Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, operated via INPI). INSEE assigns the entity a SIREN and establishment-specific SIRET. Obtain a French VAT identification number from the competent SIE or, for a foreign business without a French establishment, the SIEE; the number is generally FR followed by a two-digit key and the SIREN. A non-EU business generally must appoint a French fiscal representative unless an applicable statutory exemption or reciprocal arrangement applies. EU businesses generally register directly without a fiscal representative. VAT registration is required before commencing taxable transactions for which French VAT is due; a foreign taxable person is not generally entitled to rely on the domestic small-business threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS), at the standard rate of 25%, subject to applicable reduced-rate conditions. A qualifying SAS may elect temporarily for income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, IR) for up to five fiscal years if the statutory ownership, activity, age, size, and other conditions are met. There is no US-style check-the-box election. French tax classification and hybrid-mismatch treatment are governed by French law and applicable EU rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For eligible French-established small businesses, the domestic VAT exemption regime generally uses annual turnover thresholds of EUR 85,000 for sales of goods and certain related activities and EUR 37,500 for services, subject to statutory excess thresholds and activity-specific rules. These thresholds do not generally protect foreign taxable persons making taxable supplies in France; a foreign business may need to register from its first taxable transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the EU may qualify for VAT exemption with credit (effectively 0%) when statutory conditions are met and the exporter can substantiate that the goods left the EU. Intra-EU supplies are governed by separate exemption, VAT-identification, reporting, and transport-evidence rules. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore; place-of-supply rules determine whether French VAT applies, and specific exemptions may require additional conditions.

  • Export customs declaration and evidence of exit from the EU customs territory, including the export accompanying document or equivalent electronic customs confirmation
  • Commercial invoice and order or sales contract identifying the goods, buyer, and destination
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, carrier documentation, or proof of delivery
  • Evidence linking the exported goods to the customs declaration and sale, including packing list and shipment records
  • Foreign-exchange or inward-remittance evidence where available and relevant; a French inward remittance certificate is not a general statutory prerequisite for export VAT exemption

France is implementing mandatory domestic electronic invoicing and transaction/payment-data e-reporting through the public invoicing portal or a registered private platform. From 1 September 2026, all businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; issuance obligations apply from that date to large businesses and mid-sized enterprises, and from 1 September 2027 to SMEs and micro-enterprises. E-invoicing applies to covered domestic B2B transactions between parties subject to French VAT; e-reporting applies to specified transactions outside that scope, including relevant B2C and cross-border transactions. France does not impose general real-time pre-clearance or mandatory digital stamping of every invoice. Structured data, transmission, and platform requirements apply under the phased regime.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The taxpayer must retain reliable evidence supporting the export exemption and comply with VAT return, invoicing, and record-retention obligations. If export or other exemption conditions are not proven, the DGFiP may assess VAT, late-payment interest, and applicable tax penalties. Intentional fraud or failure to meet reporting obligations may attract enhanced penalties and, where statutory elements are met, criminal sanctions. Input VAT deductions or refunds attributable to unsupported exempt transactions may be denied or recovered.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies the euro-area framework and generally has no exchange controls restricting ordinary current-account payments or capital movements. Cross-border transfers remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, and statistical-reporting requirements. A SAS may generally receive foreign investment, pay foreign suppliers, and remit dividends or other lawful distributions, subject to corporate-law procedures, available distributable profits, withholding-tax rules, treaty relief documentation, and the recipient’s tax treatment. Banks may require evidence of the transaction’s commercial or corporate purpose and source of funds. Transfers involving sanctioned parties or jurisdictions may be prohibited or require authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated and no mandatory foreign-currency conversion ratio for ordinary commercial exports. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign currency or in an eligible foreign account, subject to applicable banking, sanctions, tax, accounting, and statistical-reporting obligations. The company should record export revenue and foreign-currency balances in its books and retain invoices, customs and transport evidence, and bank records.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France’s central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The French Treasury (Direction générale du Trésor) administers relevant external-financial-relations rules. Prudential supervision of banks is principally conducted by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR), within the applicable European Union framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. An electronic money institution authorized in France or another EEA state and entitled to provide services in France may support payments and foreign-currency services within its authorization and product terms. It is not necessarily a deposit-taking bank, and e-money safeguarding is not equivalent to deposit-guarantee protection. Offshore providers without the required EEA authorization or passporting rights should not be treated as an assured substitute for a French corporate bank. Confirm account ownership, safeguarding, payment limits, supported currencies, client-money arrangements, and ability to process capital contributions and tax or payroll payments.

Defense evidence: Current corporate registry extracts and constitutional documents for each ownership layer; ownership chart identifying natural-person beneficial owners; identity and address evidence; beneficial-owner filings; explanation of control and economic rationale; certified translations or legalization where requested.

Defense evidence: Share subscription and corporate resolutions; investor bank statements; audited accounts, tax returns, sale agreements, or other source-of-wealth evidence; proof of the originating account’s ownership; transfer instruction identifying the investor and the SAS; explanation of any third-party funding.

Defense evidence: Business plan and forecast; supplier and customer contracts; product descriptions and applicable commodity classifications; premises or manufacturing arrangements; customs, transport, and insurance records; tax and VAT registrations where applicable; projected payment volumes and currencies.
Use of personal accounts, unrelated third parties, cash, or multiple intermediaries to fund or settle company transactions

Root cause: Use of personal accounts, unrelated third parties, cash, or multiple intermediaries to fund or settle company transactions

Defense evidence: Board-approved treasury and payment-control procedures; explanation and documentary trail for any exceptional transfer; evidence of company-account ownership; reconciliations linking each transfer to an invoice, capital contribution, or approved distribution; remediation plan discontinuing unsupported routing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a pre-registration share subscription, use a bank or other legally eligible capital-deposit provider and obtain the deposit certificate required for incorporation; funds are released or transferred in accordance with the applicable incorporation process. After registration, route additional equity or shareholder financing to an account held in the SAS’s name, with the legal nature documented as a capital increase, share premium, or shareholder loan. Instruct the sending bank to identify the investor, beneficiary SAS, currency, and precise transaction purpose; use an accurate narrative such as share subscription, capital increase, or shareholder loan rather than an invented or generic regulatory purpose code. Retain subscription documents, corporate approvals, registration evidence, source-of-funds records, and the incoming payment confirmation. France does not generally impose a universal FX purpose-code system for these transfers, but banks may request supporting documents and apply reporting, sanctions, and AML controls. Confirm any applicable statistical-reporting treatment with the bank or Banque de France reporting guidance.Settle supplier invoices through SEPA credit transfer for eligible euro payments and SWIFT cross-border transfers, including MT103 customer-credit-transfer messaging where applicable, for other currencies or corridors. Commercial documentary credits may be issued through a bank using applicable documentary-credit rules, commonly UCP 600, subject to credit approval, fees, and collateral. For foreign-currency payments, document the underlying exposure and agree exchange-rate execution, value date, fees, and any intermediary-bank charges with the bank.
