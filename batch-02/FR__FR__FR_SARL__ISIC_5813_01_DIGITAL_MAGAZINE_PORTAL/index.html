Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances; official tax portal: impots.gouv.fr; business registration portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique).

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, the corporate income tax account (IS), SIREN/SIRET numbers, and VAT identification number are procured through the Guichet Unique incorporation procedure; no separate foreign-entity TIN application is needed for the local SARL. A foreign entity supplying digital magazine services into France must register directly with the territorial Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE), or use the Union OSS for EU taxable persons supplying B2C e-services. Non-EU persons must appoint an established tax representative and apply for a French VAT ID before commencing taxable supplies. The DGFiP issues a French VAT number in the format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: SARL is by default a corporation (personne morale) liable to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at 25% standard rate, with a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit where conditions are met. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: SARL is by default a corporation (personne morale) liable to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at 25% standard rate, with a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit where conditions are met. Territorial scope: profits from French operations and foreign permanent establishments are taxable in France, subject to tax treaties and foreign tax credit relief.

Available election forms

France does not provide a check-the-box or general fiscal transparency election for SARLs. Eligible family SARLs may elect income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu) under Article 239 bis AA, and a parent SARL with at least 95% ownership may elect French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A. Hybrid-mismatch outcomes are neutralized mandatorily under French ATAD implementing legislation, not by elective mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France has no general economic turnover threshold: any taxable person carrying out a taxable business activity in France must be registered for French VAT. The micro-business 'franchise en base' exemption is EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services, but digital publishing services are treated as services for this threshold and the exemption is not available to non-established or foreign suppliers. Non-established EU suppliers of e-services must register or use OSS once the EUR 10,000 Union-wide threshold for B2C distance sales of e-services is exceeded; non-EU suppliers have mandatory French VAT registration from the first French-sourced supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports outside the EU qualify for French VAT exoneration with credit (zero-rating) if the seller proves legal and physical export into a third country within statutory time limits and retains customs exit evidence. Intra-EU dispatches are VAT-exempt where the customer is VAT-registered and the SARL holds the valid EU VAT ID and records EC sales lists. Digital magazine services exported offshore are outside the scope of French VAT when the place of supply is outside France: B2B services supplied to non-French/non-EU entrepreneurs are governed by the customer's jurisdiction and reverse charge; B2C supplies are taxed at the customer's country rate, and the SARL should apply the EU OSS mechanism for EU B2C digital services.

  • Customs Export Declaration / DAU (Déclaration en Douane) with customs movement reference number
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note proving physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export contract / purchase order with Incoterms evidence
  • Bank payment credit advice or foreign exchange inward remittance certificate confirming repatriation of export proceeds
  • EU VAT registration certificate of the customer for intra-EU dispatches, or export license where regulated

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing in France is phased: B2B e-invoices must be issued in Factur-X/UBL/CIUS format through a registered Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public Chorus Pro portal, effective 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size taxpayers and 1 September 2027 for small taxpayers. B2C transaction data e-reporting is transmitted to DGFiP through the same PDP/API architecture. France does not operate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping, but digital magazine portals capturing B2C payments must use NF 525 certified payment/cash-register software and preserve immutable sales records for tax audit.

The DGFiP may retroactively claw back export zero-rating within the statutory tax-reassessment period (normally three years, extended for cross-border and anti-abuse cases) if documentation is incomplete, invoices are falsely zero-rated, or proceeds are not actually repatriated. The clawback restores output VAT at 20%, cancels corresponding input VAT deductions, adds late-payment interest, and applies graduated penalties: 40% for culpable under-assessment, 80% for deliberate concealment, and 100% for fraudulent or abusive operations. Directors and accomplices may be jointly liable for VAT debts, and serious export-fraud cases may trigger criminal tax prosecution under the French Tax Procedure Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a euro-area member, applies full convertibility for both current-account transactions (digital magazine subscriptions, sponsorship placements, advertising, royalties) and capital-account transfers (equity injection, shareholder loans, dividend distributions) under Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are not blocked for balance-of-payments reasons, but the paying French bank must obtain documentary evidence of the underlying corporate purpose, beneficial ownership, tax-residency certificates, and source of funds. The only comparably significant friction for a non-resident shareholder is the 30% French withholding tax on dividends under domestic law, which may be reduced or eliminated under the applicable France-foreign tax treaty and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive in qualifying holding structures. Currency exchange rates are freely negotiated through French credit institutions; the Bank of France does not impose price controls. The remaining material restrictions are EU/UN sanctions lists, French FDI screening in limited sensitive sectors, and anti-money-laundering verification obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in France. A French SARL operating a digital magazine portal and selling online subscriptions or receiving cross-border advertising and sponsored-media payments may hold and convert foreign-currency receipts freely through an authorised bank intermediary. The practical compliance obligation is the balance-of-payments reporting under Regulation of the Banque de France, where the receiving institution must be able to classify each international payment (e.g., services exports, licence fees, advertising proceeds); however, no currency equalisation or forced EUR conversion timeline applies to digital service exports. The invoice and bank reference must accurately state the commercial nature of the service to avoid an automatic transfer-pricing or VAT audit flag.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the European System of Central Banks and in coordination with the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR), the Ministry of Economy and Finance, and the Treasury for exchange-control, balance-of-payments, and sanctions matters under the French Monetary and Financial Code and EU treaties.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility of using offshore digital electronic-money institutions (EMIs) for the French SARL is Low-to-Medium unless the EMI has a current EU/EEA electronic-money licence that is properly passported into France through the ACPR. For example, a UK EMI with Financial Conduct Authority authorisation can continue serving the French SARL only via the temporary or contractual framework agreed between the United Kingdom and France; an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot legally open a payment account or hold funds on behalf of a French resident SARL. A French-licensed EMI (obtained through the ACPR or under the French electronic-money institution authorisation) can provide local IBANs, settlement, and treasury agility, but the SARL must maintain its core operating account at a regulated credit institution to ensure LCR and investor-grade treasury controls.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A non-resident shareholder or parent company should make the capital injection as a SWIFT MT103 wire from an account held in the shareholder's own name. The wire must be denominated in EUR or another freely convertible currency, and the payment reference field must clearly state the true economic purpose in French or English: for a capital increase, use 'Apport en capital / augmentation de capital'; for a shareholder current account, use 'Apport en compte courant d'associé'; for a subordinate loan repayment or profit distribution, use the appropriate commercial description. The French receiving bank will require the KYC file, the written corporate resolution of the SARL granting access to or accepting the capital injection, the signed capital-increase form, if applicable, the source-of-funds confirmation, and the anti-money-laundering declaration where the transfer exceeds regulatory thresholds. Where the injection is made in a currency other than the euro, the bank will clearly mark the reference rate and apply the freely negotiated contract rate, and no exchange-control approval from the Banque de France is required.Trade settlements for outbound licensing, content acquisition, software development, hosting, and subcontractor services should be executed predominantly via SWIFT MT103 for simple service payments and recurring digital-media disbursements. For high-value editorial-content or IP acquisition contracts, the parties may use an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit (UICC) issued and advised through French staff, although a documentary letter of credit is atypical and adds little value for non-goods services. All outbound payment instructions must reference the invoice number, the underlying contract date, the VAT-exempt or VAT-paid status of the French supplier, and the French IBAN of the payee. Cross-border routing to an EEA bank must be made through the SEPA rails where the amount is in EUR and under the SEPA payments threshold; otherwise SWIFT MT103 or MT202 cover payment is used.Plain vanilla OTC FX forward contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY, EUR/CNH) transacted with an ACPR-authorised French credit institution or investment firm at arm's length.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or low-liquidity currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, NGN, EGP) where the counterparty is a licensed financial institution and the settlement is in EUR or USD without physical delivery.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps for treasury balancing in a shareholder-loan structure, always tied to a documented underlying exposure.Market-validated vanilla FX options (put/call, zero-cost collars) used exclusively for hedging the advertising or subscription-currency receipts, provided the instruments are not speculative and are booked with a bank or regulated broker.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard CIT; 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs satisfying the turnover ceiling.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory withholding tax on gross French-source dividends paid to non-resident shareholders (Article 119 bis CGI); 0% for qualifying EU parent companies under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive; reduced rates under applicable DTA (often 0%, 10% or 15%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Calendar-year companies: file by the second working day after 1 May of the following year (usually 3 May). Non-calendar year-ends: file within three months of the balance-sheet date. A short fiscal year is due within three months of its close.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400 million – annual turnover excluding VAT or total balance-sheet assets of the French entity or French group; entities below this threshold are not required to prepare a full Local/Master File but must maintain benchmarked arm's-length documentation when intercompany transactions exist.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime. Substance requirements are enforced through the French GAAR (abus de droit), Article 205A CGI, ATAD implementation, and the beneficial-ownership conditions in French DTAs. A French SARL holding digital publishing contracts but with no employees, no French premises, and no substantive decision-making functions can be disregarded or reclassified as fiscally transparent; treaty protection will be denied for a pure letter-box company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Aggressive Tax Evasion / Deliberate Under-declaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment plus 80% penalty for abusive/fraudulent behavior and 0.20% monthly interest; criminal conviction under Articles 1741 and 1743 CGI: fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, extended to 7 years for structured organised fraud.

Director personal liability: Director may be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt under Articles L. 266–L. 267 LPF; criminal sanctions include imprisonment, confiscation, disqualification from management, and publication of the judgment. Foreign directors are not shielded by the corporate veil when they organised the fraud.
Failure to File / False Accounting

Statutory sanction: 10% late-filing surcharge (40% after formal notice; 80% for repeated failure), €150 fixed penalty for incomplete annexes, automatic tax base estimation by DGFiP; criminal sanction for false accounting under Articles L. 123-12 et seq. Code de commerce.

Director personal liability: In insolvency, the director can be ordered to bear the company's debts under the action en comblement du passif; the court may impose 'faillite personnelle', including travel restrictions, prohibition to manage, and asset attachment; imprisonment up to 6 years for knowingly false accounting.
Artificial Shell Company / Abusive Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Application of Article L. 64 LPF abus de droit procedure: the nullity of the artificial act is opposed, income is reclassified to the actual owner, 80% penalty applies for abuse, with late-payment interest. DTA relief is denied if the ultimate beneficial owner is not established in the treaty jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Director who executes sham contracts commits gestion fautive; courts may impose faillite personnelle and a management ban; the director can also be prosecuted for 'abus de biens sociaux' (Article L. 241-3 Code de commerce) with up to 5 years' imprisonment and €375,000 fine.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment plus 0.20% monthly interest; failure to file TP documentation is punishable by a fine under Article 1735 bis CGI; fraud penalties of 40–80% can apply to artificial TP arrangements. The company loses the deduction for the disallowed payments.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve inflated intercompany invoices can be held civilly liable for the resulting tax shortfall; this can extend to criminal complicity in tax fraud, and to disqualification from management where the conduct caused the company's insolvency.
Beneficial-Ownership and AML Compliance Gaps

Statutory sanction: Failure to file/report the beneficial-owner register triggers a fine up to €15,000 for individuals and €75,000 for legal entities under the French Monetary and Financial Code; DGFiP can combine tax reassessment with distrust penalties, and the SARL can be ordered dissolved in extreme money-laundering cases.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for regulatory fines for non-filing of the beneficial-ownership register; deliberate laundering of tax proceeds triggers criminal liability with imprisonment and asset forfeiture; travel bans can be imposed as part of judicial control.
