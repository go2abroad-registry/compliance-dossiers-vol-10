Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

French Directorate General of Public Finances (Direction générale des finances publiques, DGFiP), acting through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the impots.gouv.fr portal. Business incorporation and enterprise identifiers are administered through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI); customs matters are administered by the Directorate General of Customs and Indirect Taxes (Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects, DGDDI) through the French customs online services.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is incorporated through the Guichet unique, which transmits registration data to the competent registries and tax authorities. The entity receives a SIREN identifier and establishment-level SIRET identifier. The French VAT number is issued by the DGFiP and normally has the format FR followed by a two-character control key and the nine-digit SIREN. The entity must submit the required tax registration and VAT information to the competent SIE, including legal documents, registered office details, beneficial-owner information, business activities, expected turnover, bank details, accounting period, and evidence of taxable transactions where requested. A foreign entity without a French establishment that performs taxable French transactions must apply for French VAT registration with the non-established-taxable-persons service of the DGFiP. EU-established businesses generally do not require a French tax representative; non-EU businesses normally require an accredited French tax representative unless a specific statutory exemption applies. Customs operations additionally require an Economic Operators Registration and Identification (EORI) number obtained through French customs. A customs warehouse or bonded-depot operator must obtain the relevant customs authorisation and comply with the Union Customs Code, including inventory, guarantee, recordkeeping, and control requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) by default. Subject to statutory conditions, a SARL may elect for income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, IR), including the family SARL regime where all shareholders are qualifying family members and the company conducts an eligible commercial, industrial, artisanal, or professional activity. Certain closely held SARLs may also elect temporarily for IR treatment for up to five financial years if the statutory ownership, size, age, and activity conditions are satisfied. France has no US-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework and generally operate as mandatory anti-abuse rules rather than elective classification mechanisms. Separate elections may apply to VAT filing regimes, including the franchise in base, simplified regime, or normal regime, subject to statutory eligibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully and freely convertible in France. Both current and capital account transactions are liberalized within the European Union under Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No prior exchange control authorization is required for equity capital subscriptions, shareholder loans, trade payments, or profit repatriation. Constraints are limited to sanctions screening, customs related identity checks for free trade zone operators, and the corporate law conditions for SARL dividend distributions: annual accounts must be approved by shareholders, distributable profit must exist, and net assets must not fall below unpaid capital plus non-distributable reserves. Neither branch profit remittance nor dividend payment is subject to exchange control approval, though dividends derived from an active logistics entity may carry a domestic withholding tax and require a tax residence certificate when the recipient is treaty-entitled.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France maintains no export proceeds surrender obligation and imposes no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory repatriation timeline. Export receipts may be retained in any currency and converted at market rates. However, under customs law, records of physical and financial flows for goods entered into a bonded warehouse in a free trade zone must be kept in the approved customs accounting regime and presented to the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) on demand. Payment evidence, banking advices, and SWIFT messages must be archived for at least the statutory customs record retention period to demonstrate physical movement, value, and settlement.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting as agent for the European Central Bank for euro monetary policy and as national supervising authority for credit institutions; the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) exercises foreign exchange and external financial sanctions coordination, while the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) supervises banks and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditional feasibility rating: Medium. A fintech electronic money institution (EMI) holding or passported under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and registered with the ACPR may legally provide payment services, issue e-money accounts, and support cross-border euro disbursements. However, EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot hold capital deposits, provide credit, issue documentary credits, or act as escrow agents for share capital in the same manner as banks. For a bonded depot company classified as Medium_Risk_Enhanced, reliance on an EMI is suitable only for narrowly tailored operational payables, not for statutory capital escrow, customs guarantees, supplier LCs, or permanent treasury concentration. Concentration risk and the absence of a credit relationship must be mitigated through a back-to-back arrangement with an ACPR-licensed bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% for dividends distributed by a French company to non-resident corporate beneficial owners (Article 119 bis 2 of the French General Tax Code); 30% for non-resident individual beneficial owners under the single flat levy. An EU qualified parent may benefit from a 0% dividend withholding tax under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive subject to the 5% or 10% holding conditions and required holding period.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed within 60 days after the close of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year entity closing on 31 December, the administrative filing calendar grants a deferral generally until the second business day after 1 May (e.g., 3 May 2024). Non-calendar fiscal years are due 60 days from the fiscal year close.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million in annual net French turnover (excluding VAT) for the French entity, or EUR 400 million in gross assets, or membership in a group with consolidated worldwide revenue exceeding EUR 750 million; at any such threshold, the entity must prepare and maintain a Master File and Local File under Articles L13 AA and L13 AB of the French Book of Tax Procedures. Below the threshold, entities must still retain adequate comparability and arm's-length justification for related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a stand-alone 'Economic Substance Regulation' regime like certain free-trade-zone jurisdictions. Instead, the French tax administration uses the 'abus de droit' doctrine (Article L64 of the Book of Tax Procedures), the anti-hybrid and principal-purpose tests under EU and OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting, and the concept of abuse of law to attack tax-motivated arrangements lacking real substance. A bonded warehouse SARL must demonstrate actual operational presence in France: a valid French customs bonded-warehouse authorisation, owned or leased physical premises, licensed staff exercising management and custody over the inventory, its own stock records, and authority over commercial decisions. A nominally registered SARL acting solely as a conduit or invoicing vehicle for a foreign principal will not be recognised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Undeclared Bonded Sales

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty for fiscal fraud under Article 1741 of the French General Tax Code: a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years, increased to EUR 3,000,000 and 7 years for aggravated fraud involving computer misuse or organised crime. Civil sanctions include the 80% concealment penalty, late-payment interest at 0.20% monthly, and reassessment of the full evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Managing directors and de facto officers who personally conceive, orchestrate or sign false declarations are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax, penalties and interest; personal travel bans, loss of civil rights, restriction of professional activity, and, upon criminal conviction, imprisonment up to 7 years are applicable. The corporate veil may be pierced where the SARL has been used as a sham or fraudulent conduit.
Abuse of Law / Artificial Shell Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Article L64 of the Book of Tax Procedures authorises the tax administration to disregard the artificial act and reattribute the concealed profits to the French entity or principal; the abusive arrangement carries an 80% penalty (majoration d'abus de droit), or 10% if a legitimate non-tax reason exists, plus interest. The relevant customs free-zone status may also be revoked and duties recovered.

Director personal liability: Directors who have personally approved or implemented the sham arrangement may be held personally liable for the reassessed corporate income tax and penalties under the manager's civil liability regime; in aggravated cases, personal indictment for tax evasion or company assets misuse is possible with potential travel bans and managerial disqualification.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Deficient Documentation

Statutory sanction: Tax administration may reconstruct the taxable profit at arm's length; the lack of compliant documentation triggers the penalty regime under Article L13 B of the Book of Tax Procedures, a mandatory 5% penalty on reassessed profits where documentation is not produced, and an automatic 40% bad-faith penalty or 80% gross-negligence penalty, plus late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who have signed false or incomplete transfer pricing declarations or approved artificial service fees can be held personally liable for tax recalculations and penalties; if the TP manipulation constitutes the stripping of profits through sham contracts, directors may additionally face 'abus de biens sociaux' civil liability and professional restrictions.
Money Laundering and Customs Fraud in the Free Trade Zone

Statutory sanction: Under the French Monetary and Financial Code, money laundering by a legal person is punishable by a fine equal to five times the fines applicable to individuals (up to EUR 1,875,000 for individuals? Actually individuals up to EUR 375,000, legal persons up to EUR 1,875,000) and/or up to 5 years' imprisonment for individual operators. Customs fraud sanctions include confiscation of the goods, a fine of one to five times the value of the fraudulent merchandise, and revocation of the customs bonded-warehouse licence.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate, fail to implement AML measures, or allow the SARL to be used for laundering proceeds are jointly civilly liable for fines and forfeiture, personally exposed to criminal money-laundering charges (up to 5 years' imprisonment for individuals and EUR 375,000 fine), and subject to travel bans, professional disqualification, and removal from management positions.
