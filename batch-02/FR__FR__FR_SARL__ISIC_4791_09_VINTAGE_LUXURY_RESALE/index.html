Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de commerce via Guichet Unique Électronique des Formalités des Entreprises (available at guichet-entreprises.gouv.fr), with INSEE issuing SIREN/SIRET identification and DGFIP handling tax registration.

Governing statute

Code de commerce (French Commercial Code), Book II, Title II: Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée), Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10, and implementing RCS rules under Articles R.123-1 et seq.; Decree No. 2007-431 on the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés.

Formation / constitutional filings

Business incorporation declaration (Form M0 / Dossier de création d'entreprise) together with signed statutes, publication certificate, capital deposit certificate, and beneficial-owner declaration submitted through the sole electronic window.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for a French SARL (Art. L.223-2 Code de commerce). The articles set the capital. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and at least 20% paid up at incorporation; the balance must be paid within 5 years. Contributions must be deposited into a blocked bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations before filing and are released only after registration. In-kind contributions are transferred immediately and must be valued in the articles or by a statutory auditor if value exceeds EUR 30,000.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a pure online activity without public physical access. If a commercial office or warehouse is leased, install fire extinguishers and first-aid equipment in accordance with the French Labour Code, verify electrical installations, keep a building safety log, and conduct an occupational risk assessment. No ERP (établissement recevant du public) occupancy permit is required unless customers physically visit the premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for a French SARL (Art. L.223-2 Code de commerce). The articles set the capital. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing board applies to an online luxury resale activity without manufacturing, warehousing, or public-access retail premises. The company must remain registered with the RCS and, where applicable, the local mairie may exercise building and civil-defence powers over the registered office.

Category: No prior operating or manufacturing permit is required. As a trader in second-hand movable goods, the company must nevertheless hold and maintain a police register (registre de police) recording high-value acquisitions and sales to comply with traceability and anti-theft obligations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — impots.gouv.fr; corporate formation and tax ID filings via the single business formalities portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); non-resident VAT registration handled by the DGFiP's Service des Impôts des Entreprises through the NOVA e-procedure

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SARL with the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE) through the single portal to obtain SIREN, SIRET, APE code, and RCS/Kbis registration; the CFE automatically transmits VAT registration data to the DGFiP, which issues the TVA intracommunautaire (FR VAT ID). Step 2: For a foreign parent or foreign individual shareholders, provide notarized statutes, identity documents, and proof of good standing; non-resident ownership is permitted without restriction, but the SARL must appoint a local legal representative for tax filings and a compliance officer for banking AML purposes. Step 3: For intra-EU distance sales of vintage luxury goods, register in the French OSS portal (guichet unique TVA) to declare and pay destination-country VAT; for imported goods consignments up to EUR 150, use the IOSS scheme. Step 4: The DGFiP issues the SIREN/TVA intracommunautaire dossier within 5–15 working days of electronic filing; a positive Kbis extract is the operative VAT-registration certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Default SARL taxation is at entity level under Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS): 25% standard rate, with a reduced 15% rate applied to the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit for qualifying small enterprises (Article 219 CGI). An election under Article 206(3) CGI permits pass-through taxation (régime des sociétés de personnes) for a maximum fixed period of five years when all shareholders are natural persons and the conditions of Article 239 bis AA are met; reversion to IS is then mandatory. No US-style check-the-box election exists in France; hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized under the ATAD implementing rules of Article 212 bis CGI. The SARL may also join the French VAT group regime (assujetti unique) available since 1 January 2023 for financially, economically, and organizationally linked French taxable persons.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 annual taxable turnover for supply of goods (franchise en base de TVA, Article 293 B CGI); once the threshold is exceeded, the SARL must register for VAT from its first taxable transaction. There is no threshold for non-established foreign sellers — VAT registration applies at the first euro of French taxable supplies unless the OSS or IOSS special schemes are used. The intra-EU cross-border distance-sales threshold is EUR 10,000 in annual EU distance sales, above which OSS declaration becomes mandatory for deliveries to other Member States.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

True zero-rating (exemption with input-VAT recovery) is available under Article 262(I) CGI for sales of vintage luxury goods physically delivered from France to a destination outside the European Union, provided the goods are exported by the vendor, by a non-established buyer, or by a carrier on the vendor's behalf, and the exporter holds the prescribed evidence of export by the time the VAT return is filed. Ancillary services directly sold in connection with the export goods (restoration, polishing, authentication tied to the exported item, packing, transport) are likewise zero-rated under Article 262(II) CGI if rendered to the non-EU party and directly related to the exported goods. Intra-EU distance-sales deliveries remain taxable under the destination rules (declarable via OSS), while eligible second-hand/consignment sales may be subjected to the margin scheme (Article 297 A CGI) which taxes only the sale margin and requires specific invoice mentions for each vintage item sold on consignment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX-E/export summary declaration lodged in DELTA-X) with the Export Accompanying Document (EAD) evidencing the external export procedure
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or signed consignee commercial delivery note proving physical movement from France to the non-EU destination
  • Commercial Invoice annotated 'TVA non applicable — exportation hors UE / 0% export VAT'
  • Proof of payment or the inward remittance certificate (or equivalent bank/FX statement from the credit institution or Banque de France)
  • Source acquisition invoices (unit cost and margin-scheme eligibility records) documenting the input-VAT recovery basis
  • Appraisal/authentication certificates for vintage-luxury items retained for customs valuation and anti-fraud evidence

France is implementing a mandatory e-invoicing/e-reporting reform under the 2022 Finance Act (Article 32) and the Order of 20 July 2022. The rollout was postponed by successive Finance Acts and, as currently projected, applies from September 2026–2028: B2B invoices must be transmitted in the Factur-X (EN16931) structured format through a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) that clears mandated invoice fields to the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); B2C transactions (including online marketplace sales) require automated e-reporting to the PPF of transaction data, VAT registration IDs, and turnover figures. France does not operate a real-time per-invoice digital-stamping clearance, but invoices must carry one of the recognized anti-fraud quality seals under Article 289 CGI (encrypted hash, PPF routing, or trusted timestamp) to preserve deductibility and probative value in DGFiP audits.

The export zero-rating is denied if the documentary bundle is incomplete, late, or non-compliant; DGFiP may retrospectively claw back the uncollected 20% output VAT together with late-payment interest (intérêts de retard, 0.2% per month capped at 10%) and graduated penalties of up to 40% for material non-disclosure (manquement délibéré) or 80% for dissimulation (Articles 1728 and 1730 CGI). False export documentation or fictitious Customs declarations constitute tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI (up to EUR 500,000 fine and five years' imprisonment) and customs contraventions under the Code des douanes (up to twice the value of the goods plus seizure/confiscation of the exported luxury items). Export VAT refunds are additionally subject to enhanced documentary scrutiny where the consignee is located in a high-risk non-cooperative jurisdiction or where the vintage-luxury unit value exceeds EUR 10,000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions within the European Union and the euro area. France has no general exchange-control regime requiring prior approval for ordinary import payments, export receipts, capital contributions, dividend payments, loan repayments, or transfers to non-residents. Banks may require transaction evidence, beneficial-owner information, source-of-funds evidence, customs or commercial documentation, and payment-purpose information under anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions, tax-reporting, and statistical-reporting rules. Profit remittances remain subject to French accounting rules, distributable-profit restrictions, corporate income tax, applicable dividend withholding tax, treaty or EU relief, transfer-pricing rules, and bank compliance review. Payments involving sanctioned countries, high-risk jurisdictions, restricted goods, or suspicious trade flows may be delayed, rejected, frozen, or reported.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France or to convert a prescribed percentage of export receipts into euros. No general mandatory conversion ratio or universal statutory repatriation deadline applies to export receipts of a French SARL. Export proceeds may generally remain in euros or foreign currency accounts, subject to the bank's account terms and applicable reporting, sanctions, tax, customs, anti-money-laundering, and accounting obligations. The company should retain contracts, invoices, shipping or delivery evidence, customs records where applicable, payment confirmations, and reconciliation records. Foreign-currency balances must be accounted for under applicable French accounting rules, with exchange differences recognized appropriately.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, within the Eurosystem under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank; payment and electronic-money institutions are supervised principally by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for euro collection, merchant acquiring, foreign-exchange conversion, and international transfers, subject to the provider's risk appetite. An offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or electronic-money services to French customers merely because it is accessible online. The provider must be authorized under the applicable national regime and, for EEA services, benefit from valid EU passporting or establish an appropriately authorized French or EEA entity. A non-EEA provider generally requires an applicable French authorization or a regulated EEA partner for regulated services. Account safeguarding, client-money segregation, payment-services disclosures, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, complaint handling, data protection, and safeguarding of funds must be verified. Fintech balances should not be assumed to be bank deposits or covered by the French deposit-guarantee scheme unless the provider and product qualify.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route paid-in capital through a French bank or regulated EEA payment institution to the designated capital escrow account in the exact legal name of the SARL. Use the shareholder's own account wherever possible and identify the transfer as paid-in share capital or shareholder funding, not customer revenue. Include the company name, registration details, shareholder identity, capital-subscription reference, and transaction purpose in the payment instructions. Retain the bank transfer confirmation, subscription documents, source-of-funds evidence, and escrow certificate. After incorporation and release of funds, transfer the balance to the operational account only against the bank's corporate-document checklist. For shareholder loans, use a written loan agreement, arm's-length terms, repayment schedule, transfer-pricing analysis where applicable, and accounting entries. France does not impose a universal foreign-exchange purpose-code system equivalent to certain controlled-currency jurisdictions; the bank may nevertheless request an internal purpose code or transaction narrative and may report relevant transactions for regulatory or statistical purposes.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area where timing and beneficiary requirements permit. Use SWIFT FIN MT103 for supported cross-border customer credit transfers, with ISO 20022 pacs.008 increasingly used as the corresponding modern payment format. Provide accurate ordering-customer, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, invoice, currency, amount, and purpose information. For higher-value or documentary trade transactions, use a commercial documentary letter of credit subject to the agreed rules, commonly UCP 600, with documentary examination and sanctions controls. Use documentary collections under URC 522 where appropriate, or controlled escrow and payment-on-delivery structures for consignment inventory. Match payments to purchase orders, invoices, customs or shipping evidence, and vendor master data. 25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable tax treaties. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the annual corporate income tax return is generally due on the second business day after 1 May of the following year, subject to the electronic filing calendar and any officially granted extension. For a non-calendar year, filing is generally due within three months after year-end. The corporate income tax balance is generally due by the fifteenth day of the fourth month after year-end; for a 31 December year-end, the balance is generally due by 15 May. Installments are generally due by 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150,000,000 annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets generally triggers the full French transfer-pricing documentation obligation under Article L13 AA of the French Tax Procedure Code, subject to statutory ownership and control tests. The threshold is assessed at the French entity or French tax group level as applicable. A transfer-pricing declaration on Form 2257-SD may be required at lower transaction thresholds, generally where a category of transactions with an associated enterprise exceeds EUR 100,000.

Economic substance scope: France has no single general economic substance regulation equivalent to a standalone ESR regime. Substance is assessed through transfer-pricing rules, the beneficial-ownership requirement, the abuse-of-law doctrine, the principal-purpose test in applicable treaties, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, anti-hybrid rules, and general anti-avoidance provisions. A French SARL should maintain genuine local management, decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or credible operational infrastructure, commercial risk assumption, bank-account control, inventory control, and records demonstrating that related-party transactions reflect actual services and arm's-length value.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties generally ranging from 10% to 80% and potentially 100% in specified cases. Criminal tax fraud may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated cases potentially carrying up to seven years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur criminal liability where they personally participated in the offense. A director may also be declared jointly liable for tax debt where fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated non-compliance made recovery from the company impossible. Civil liability generally requires a personal management fault or a fault separable from ordinary corporate functions. Travel restrictions are not automatic; judicial control, bail conditions, asset-freezing measures, or passport restrictions may arise in criminal proceedings.
VAT fraud and carousel or missing-trader arrangements

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, denial of input tax, late-payment interest, penalties up to 80% in fraudulent cases, possible joint and several liability, and criminal prosecution for aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors can face criminal prosecution for intentional participation and civil or tax liability for personal misconduct. Payment service providers, marketplaces, customs records, and bank data may be cross-checked by the tax administration.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profit, possible withholding-tax exposure, interest, penalties for inadequate documentation, and a documentation penalty generally equal to the greater of EUR 10,000 or 0.5% of inadequately documented transactions, subject to statutory limits and facts.

Director personal liability: Directors generally face corporate liability rather than automatic personal liability, but deliberate falsification, obstruction, fraudulent accounting, or personal participation may create civil or criminal exposure.
Shell company, abuse of law, and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or domestic exemptions, recharacterization of transactions, reassessment, 40% or 80% abuse-of-law penalties depending on the applicable procedure, withholding-tax liabilities, and potential criminal consequences where deception or fraud is established.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for knowingly implementing abusive structures, fraudulent declarations, or asset diversion. Corporate separateness is not automatically disregarded; veil-piercing requires legally recognized grounds such as personal fault, fraud, commingling, or an artificial arrangement.
Anti-money laundering, terrorist-financing, and beneficial-ownership violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative and criminal sanctions may include fines, professional or business restrictions, confiscation, and prosecution under French AML and criminal laws. Obliged professionals may face disciplinary sanctions and reporting failures may aggravate exposure.

Director personal liability: Managers and compliance officers may incur personal criminal liability for knowing participation, concealment, or obstruction. Directors may face management bans and imprisonment where the underlying conduct constitutes money laundering or related criminal offenses.
Counterfeit goods, stolen goods, and provenance failures

Statutory sanction: Confiscation, customs and intellectual-property penalties, damages to rights holders, business closure measures, and criminal penalties. Receiving stolen goods may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with aggravated penalties in specified circumstances; counterfeit offenses may carry separate penalties under intellectual-property law.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly sell, import, store, or facilitate counterfeit or stolen goods may be prosecuted personally. Directors may face imprisonment, fines, confiscation, and management restrictions independently of the SARL's civil liability.
Customs and import declaration violations

Statutory sanction: Recovery of duties and import VAT, customs penalties, seizure or confiscation, and criminal prosecution for serious smuggling or false declarations.

Director personal liability: The company and responsible individuals may be liable under customs law. Personal exposure is fact-dependent and may arise from intentional instructions, fraud, or direct involvement.
Accounting, corporate-record, and asset-diversion violations

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, civil restitution, shareholder claims, insolvency clawback, criminal accounting offenses, and possible management bans or imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Managers may be liable for an insufficiency of assets in insolvency where management faults contributed to the shortfall, and may face personal restitution, asset-freezing, management bans, or imprisonment where criminal conduct is proven.
Unlawful dividend distribution and profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, withholding-tax reassessment, interest and penalties, shareholder or creditor claims, and possible criminal sanctions for misuse of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Managers may be required to restore unlawful distributions and may incur civil or criminal liability for knowingly authorizing distributions contrary to French corporate-law and tax requirements.
