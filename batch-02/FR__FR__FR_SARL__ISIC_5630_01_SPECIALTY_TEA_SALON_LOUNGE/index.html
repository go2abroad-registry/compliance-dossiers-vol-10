Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by default. A qualifying SARL comprising only individuals and meeting statutory conditions may elect income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu), including the family SARL regime where applicable. A qualifying newly established SARL may also elect temporary income-tax treatment for up to five financial years, subject to ownership, size, activity, and filing conditions. There is no general United States-style check-the-box election. The tax treatment of branches, permanent establishments, partnerships, and hybrid instruments follows French statutory classification and anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directives.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France does not provide a universal VAT registration threshold for all transactions. The franchise en base de TVA exemption may generally apply, subject to statutory conditions, where prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 85,000 for goods, restaurant-type supplies, and accommodation activities or EUR 37,500 for services; enhanced current-year thresholds are generally EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250 respectively. Thresholds are subject to legislative amendment and activity classification. VAT registration and reporting may nevertheless be required from the first transaction for imports, certain intra-Community acquisitions, reverse-charge transactions, and transactions outside the franchise regime. A specialty tea lounge must separately assess on-premises food and beverage services, takeaway goods, packaged tea, merchandise, and any ancillary services. The standard rate is 20%, while qualifying food products may be subject to reduced rates depending on their nature, packaging, sale conditions, and immediate-consumption status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from France or the European Union to a destination outside the EU may be invoiced without French VAT if the seller satisfies the French and EU export exemption conditions and retains reliable evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. The usual evidence is a customs export declaration linked to an exit confirmation, together with transport and commercial records. Qualifying intra-Community supplies to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State may be exempt rather than technically treated as exports, provided the customer VAT number, transport, and recapitulative statement requirements are satisfied. Services supplied to a business established outside France may be outside the scope of French VAT under place-of-supply rules, subject to customer-status and evidence requirements. Services physically performed in a French tea salon, including food, beverage, hospitality, and cultural salon services, generally remain French-taxable even if the customer is foreign or payment is received from an overseas account. Zero-rating or exemption must not be claimed merely because the customer is non-resident.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the seller, purchaser, goods, value, currency, delivery terms, and VAT treatment
  • Electronic customs export declaration containing the applicable customs procedure and Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs exit result or equivalent electronic evidence confirming that the goods exited the EU customs territory
  • Packing list and transport document, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or courier delivery record, as applicable
  • Purchase order, sales contract, delivery evidence, and accounting records linking the goods to the export transaction
  • EORI number and any required customs authorizations, licenses, certificates of origin, or product-specific documents
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required EC Sales List or recapitulative statement
  • Bank payment evidence may support the commercial file but is not generally a mandatory French export document, and France does not generally require an Inward Remittance Certificate for export VAT exemption

France is introducing mandatory domestic business-to-business electronic invoicing and electronic reporting in phases. All businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Large and intermediate businesses must generally issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from 1 September 2026, while small and medium-sized businesses, including a typical SARL, are generally scheduled to issue them from 1 September 2027. Electronic reporting applies to relevant business-to-consumer and international transactions under the same phased framework. France generally uses an approved electronic-invoicing platform model rather than universal real-time invoice clearance at the point of issuance. Invoices must contain required VAT data and be transmitted in an accepted structured or hybrid format through an approved platform or authorized public infrastructure. There is no general physical tax stamp requirement for ordinary VAT invoices. Existing electronic recordkeeping, audit-trail, retention, cash-register, and payment-reporting requirements continue to apply.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export VAT relief. Export proceeds may generally be received through ordinary banking channels, subject to anti-money-laundering, customs, accounting, and transfer-pricing controls. If export evidence is missing, inconsistent, fabricated, or obtained after the fact, the DGFiP may reassess French VAT for the full applicable rate, deny input-VAT recovery where connected records are deficient, and charge late-payment interest currently calculated at 0.20% per month, subject to statutory rules. Additional penalties may include a 10% surcharge for late filing or payment, 40% for deliberate non-compliance, and up to 80% for fraudulent conduct, abuse of law, or concealed activity, depending on the facts. Customs infringements may trigger separate duties, VAT, fines, seizure, or other measures under the Union Customs Code and French customs law. Serious intentional tax fraud may create criminal exposure, including imprisonment and fines under French tax-fraud provisions, with enhanced consequences for aggravated conduct and potential separate liability of the legal entity and responsible individuals.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

As a eurozone member, France applies the principle of freedom of current and capital transactions within the EU/EEA under the Monetary and Financial Code; no general exchange controls exist. With respect to third countries, the Banque de France imposes balance-of-payments reporting obligations but does not restrict remittance of profits by an SARL. Profit repatriation requires a valid annual general meeting resolution, compliance with French statutory reserve and distributable-profit rules, and presentation of standard documentation (distribution resolution, audit certificate where required, and tax compliance evidence). Non-resident shareholding in an SARL is freely permitted; no exchange-control authorization is required for a specialty tea salon operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement for export proceeds in France. Exporters are not obliged to convert foreign currency receipts into euros nor to do so within any particular timeline. Exporters may retain and use foreign currency freely, provided that the underlying transactions are accurately declared through the sending/receiving bank for Banque de France balance-of-payments statistics and that all settlement documents carry appropriate commercial purpose codes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (the French central banking and foreign-exchange administration)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for EEA-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) passporting into France under the PSD2 and ACPR supervision; Low-Medium for offshore/third-country EMIs. An EEA EMI is usable for treasury acceleration, cross-border supplier payments, and liquidity topping-up, but it may not provide commercial banking functions such as credit, guarantees, or deposit-taking, and its funds are not covered by the French deposit guarantee (FGDR). An offshore EMI unlicensed in France cannot independently provide payment services; it must act through a licensed French banking intermediary. A specialty tea salon should therefore treat an EMI as a supplementary rail, while maintaining a primary French banking relationship.

For initial formation, subscription funds must be deposited with the bank or a notary to obtain the deposit certificate (attestation de dépôt des fonds), while subsequent equity contributions may be wired directly to the operational treasury account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million (annual revenue or gross assets) for the French entity's Local File; EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue for the Master File.

Economic substance scope: France has no Economic Substance Regulations barometer in the OFC sense, but economic substance is enforced through the abuse-of-law doctrine (abus de droit), the anti-fraud rule of Article L.64 of the Livre des procédures fiscales, EU ATAD substance requirements, beneficial-ownership tests in DTA relief, and transfer-pricing behavioural tests. A specialty tea salon lounge owned by non-residents must demonstrate real local presence, employees, operating assets, stock management, decision-making, and business risk in France; otherwise the administration may disregard the entity or recharacterise the arrangement as artificial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Concealed French Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: 80% fraud penalty on evaded tax plus monthly late interest at 0.20%; criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years under Article 1741 CGI; potential additional penalties for failure to keep accounting records and for mis-declaration of P&L.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in tax evasion may be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt, penalties, and interest under Article L.267 LPF. Courts may impose a management ban, asset freeze, travel restrictions, and in aggravated cases imprisonment of up to seven years.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Frustrated Benchmarking

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment at the French tax administration's reconstructed profit plus late interest; 40% penalty for insufficient documentation or deliberate misstatement, rising to 80% for fraud; additional potential fines under Article 1735 CGI for failure to produce documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for transfer policies can be made jointly liable for adjusted corporate taxes where deliberate concealment or fictitious documentation is established. This may extend to disqualification from management, travel bans during fiscal proceedings, and criminal fines.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Disapplication of the legal entity under abuse-of-law procedure; recharacterisation of transactions; 80% abuse penalty; denial of treaty benefits; possible nullity of certain contracts under civil law; tax reassessment up to the real economic substance.

Director personal liability: Nominee or de facto directors may be held personally liable for the full restored tax and penalty where the entity is considered fictitious or an instrument of fraud; French courts may impose management bans, prohibitions on residency, and imprisonment.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 30,000 and, for serious laundering, imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of EUR 375,000 under Articles 324-1 and 324-2 of the French Criminal Code. Regulatory sanctions may be imposed by the Prudential Control and Resolution Authority.

Director personal liability: Directors of the SARL may be personally prosecuted for money laundering and for failing to comply with KYC/AML obligations. In addition, they may be ordered to pay confiscation of assets and be prohibited from managing a business for up to fifteen years.
Unlawful Dividend Distribution and Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the distribution as taxable income in the hands of the shareholder; withholding tax reassessment against the SARL; late payment interest; 40% or 80% intentional non-disclosure penalties; civil nullity of the distribution resolution.

Director personal liability: Directors who voted or executed the unlawful distribution can be held personally liable to restore the amounts received by shareholders, jointly with the company, to the extent of company losses. Where fraud is shown, imprisonment and personal tax liability follow.
