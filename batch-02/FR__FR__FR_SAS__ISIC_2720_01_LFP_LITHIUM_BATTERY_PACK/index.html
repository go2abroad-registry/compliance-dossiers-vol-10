Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French tax administration; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; companies portal: entreprises.impots.gouv.fr; unified business filings via Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned SAS incorporated in France, the statutory TIN (SIREN/SIRET) and intra-community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) are assigned automatically during registration with the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) through the Guichet Unique. A foreign legal entity without a French establishment that incurs French VAT obligations must apply for French VAT registration directly to the DGFiP (SIE) using form 1547-B (SI), attaching proof of foreign commercial registration, proof of address, and designation of an individual authorised to act for tax purposes; no minimum capital is required for SAS.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (IS) at entity level on worldwide profits for French-resident entities, subject to territoriality rules and impairment rules; non-resident shareholders are not automatically taxable in France solely by reason of ownership. No Tax_Exempt_Zone classification applies to a commercial battery-pack assembly operation in mainland France. — For a foreign-owned SAS incorporated in France, the statutory TIN (SIREN/SIRET) and intra-community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) are assigned automatically during registration with the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) through the Guichet Unique. A foreign legal entity without a French establishment that incurs French VAT obligations must apply for French VAT registration directly to the DGFiP (SIE) using form 1547-B (SI), attaching proof of foreign commercial registration, proof of address, and designation of an individual authorised to act for tax purposes; no minimum capital is required for SAS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (IS) at entity level on worldwide profits for French-resident entities, subject to territoriality rules and impairment rules; non-resident shareholders are not automatically taxable in France solely by reason of ownership. No Tax_Exempt_Zone classification applies to a commercial battery-pack assembly operation in mainland France.

Available election forms

French law has no US-style check-the-box; tax classification follows legal form. SAS may elect for income tax under Article 239 bis AA for certain narrowly defined small trading or real-estate purposes only; available options include French group taxation under Article 223 A (intégration fiscale) by filing an election with DGFiP; hybrid mismatch elections are not elective but anti-hybrid rules under EU ATAD Directive and Articles 209, 210 and 212 bis CGI must be disclosed and complied with.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France's statutory franchise exemption threshold is EUR 85,000 of annual taxable turnover for sales of goods and EUR 37,500 for services; registration becomes mandatory above the threshold, or earlier when making intra-Community acquisitions, exporting, or intending to deduct input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to supplies of tangible goods physically exported from France to destinations outside the European Union, provided the exporter retains complete proof of export within the legal timeframe and records the turnover as exports; intra-Community dispatches are effectively exempt in France with valid EU customer VAT details (Article 262-I-1 CGI). Services connected to LFP battery-pack exports may be outside French VAT under place-of-supply rules if the customer is a business outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU) — Single Administrative Document (SAD) plus transit/security summary declaration where applicable
  • Commercial invoice / customs value declaration
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR transport document evidencing physical export
  • Transport document evidencing arrival at destination outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank proof of receipt of export proceeds

France mandates structured electronic invoicing (e-invoicing) for all domestic B2B transactions ahead of the reform timeframe: from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for small and medium-sized enterprises, with clearance through the Portail Public de Facturation (Chorus Pro) and certified private platforms (PDPs) using PINT/Peppol-based formats. Exports remain subject to mandatory French customs electronic clearance via DELTA/GUN rather than a separate real-time fiscal stamp; no physical fiscal stamp is applied to battery-pack exports.

Export proof must be retained by the exporter for at least six years; where zero-rating cannot be substantiated, DGFiP reassesses output VAT at 20%, applies late-payment interest (0.20% per month), and may impose a penalty of 40% for substantial inaccuracy or 80% for fraudulent manipulation/abuse of right; criminal sanctions and customs fines up to EUR 37,500 or half the value of the goods may also be imposed, with retrospective clawback of previously recovered input VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The euro (EUR) is freely convertible for trade, investment, loan service, and profit remittance. Dividend distributions from an SAS to a non-resident shareholder are exempt from exchange-control authorisation, but the bank must receive the shareholders’ meeting minutes, audited financial statements, and a current French corporate tax certificate to evidence that withholding tax (up to 25% under domestic law, unless treaty-reduced) has been settled. Treasury operations such as repurchase agreements, derivatives, and advanced cross-border payments are unrestricted if the transaction is compliant with EU financial sanctions, anti-money-laundering (AML) rules, and the French Monetary and Financial Code. Convertibility does not eliminate mandatory statistical declarations for payments above €12,500 via resident banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has not imposed export-proceeds surrender or forced conversion rules. Export receivables in foreign currency may remain on a foreign-currency or euro account and be used for operational payments. Under Article R152-6 of the Code Monétaire et Financier, every resident enterprise must report cross-border payments in excess of €12,500 (and other threshold-holding situations) to the Banque de France using the official payment-reports purpose codes (nature de la transaction). The reporting deadline is generally the first business day following the transaction month for companies using bank-mediated declarations. There is no mandatory conversion ratio or minimum repatriation timeline, but proceeds must be recorded in the company’s balance sheet in compliance with French accounting standards.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the French Central Bank, in coordination with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) and the General Directorate of the Treasury (DG Trésor) under the European System of Central Banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. French-licensed or EU-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto and Shine (ACPR-authorised) can offer operational IBANs, SEPA clearing, and cross-border FX. Offshore EMIs not authorised under the EU/EEA passport regime may not legally provide payment services to a French SAS; any such provider must either be regulated by ACPR or rely on a European partner bank. Practical limitations include incomplete SWIFT/LC capabilities, regulatory onboarding restrictions, and absence of derivatives or credit lines, making them viable only as supplementary treasury rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an equity contribution from a non-resident shareholder, instruct the remitting bank to execute a SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.009 transfer to the SAS account in France. Use the ISO purpose code 'EQUI' for equity purchases and, for French balance-of-payments reporting, communicate Banque de France operation code 'A050' (Direct Investment in France) to the receiving bank. The bank requires: ultimate beneficial owner identification; evidence of the parent company's existence; legalised corporate authorisation or resolution; submission of the French UBO declaration form; and confirmation that the incoming funds do not originate from sanctioned entities or shell structures. Any subsequent capital increase must comply with SAS publication rules and be matched against the bank's ongoing customer-due-diligence file. For shareholder loans (compte courant d'associé), use purpose code 'GLIS', provide the executed loan agreement with arm's-length interest, and submit tax documentation for the interest deduction.Supplier settlements and import payments should be routed via: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for euro-denominated payments within the EEA; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA, USD, CNY, or urgent same-day foreign-currency disbursements; and (iii) commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700, with amendments through MT707 and advice or confirmation through MT710/MT720. For every outbound disbursement, the bank must receive the underlying commercial invoice, transport document, and a recognised purpose code such as 'GOOD', 'TRAD', or 'IMPP'. When the beneficiary is an LFP cell supplier in China and the payment is in CNY, the treasury should book a deliverable forward or an NDF to mitigate FX risk. Euro-clearing is settled intraday on TARGET2, while USD clearing requires CHIPS/FEDWIRE correspondent routing; the French bank will perform OFAC, U.S. instructed-party, and EU restrictive-measures screening before releasing funds.Outright deliverable FX forwards with a French-authorised credit institution, documented under ISDA/CSA and cleared through a CCP if the EMIR clearing threshold is breached.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for CNY, USD, and other restricted-deliverability currencies, subject to bank internal limits, legal review, and supporting underlying exposure evidence.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) used to hedge long-term foreign-currency financing or shareholder loans, with EMIR margin rules for non-cleared derivatives.Vanilla FX options and zero-cost structured collars hedging forecast export sales, conditional on the provision of underlying commercial forecast documentation.
