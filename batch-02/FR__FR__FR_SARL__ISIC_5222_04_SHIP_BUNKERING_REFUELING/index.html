Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, operated by INPI; company information is recorded in the Registre national des entreprises (RNE).

Name reservation / lock period

France does not provide a generally applicable statutory company-name reservation period for a SARL; a name search or availability indication does not create an exclusive right. If a filing is rejected or a formality is challenged, obtain the written decision and stated grounds, correct or replace the designation, and resubmit through the Guichet unique. For a formal adverse decision, use the review or appeal route and deadline specified in that decision and applicable procedural law; seek correction through INPI or the competent registry where the issue is a filing error. A third-party name or trademark conflict should be addressed through consent, rebranding, or the applicable civil or intellectual-property procedure, not treated as a registry-name appeal.

There is no comprehensive fixed list of words requiring ministerial approval for all SARLs. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), operating through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); digital portal: impots.gouv.fr. Business registration and statutory tax-ID initiation are executed via the Guichet Unique administered by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Customs and export declaration administration falls under Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), portail: douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

(1) File SARL incorporation with the Guichet Unique (INPI platform). (2) Obtain SIREN (9-digit INSEE identification number), SIRET (14-digit establishment code), and registration in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS). (3) File the statutory administrative registration with the CFE (Centre de Formalités des Entreprises) for social and tax affiliation; the DGFiP automatically assigns the fiscal regime (Impôt sur les Sociétés default). (4) Apply to the local SIE for the TVA intra-communautaire number (FR + 9-digit SIREN + 2-digit key) when the company performs intra-Community sales, purchases, or services subject to VAT, including international marine fuel supply operations. (5) Obtain an EORI registration number (FR + SIREN) from DGDDI for customs export declarations. Foreign parent entities establishing a French SARL may appoint a non-resident legal representative, but the SARL itself remains the principal registered taxable person filing VATEU/CA3 returns through the professional online platform (Espace Professionnel).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the SARL without an option for the family tax regime is subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) at the standard 25% statutory rate (plus possible social surcharge at 3.3% on amounts of corporate tax exceeding €763,000 applicable to entities with turnover above €7,630,000). Territorial scope: France (unitary jurisdiction); international shipping income is governed by domestic territoriality rules and applicable double-tax treaties, with foreign permanent establishment profits exempt from French IS under Article 209 CGI unless placed within the group tax consolidation election. Marine bunkering services and fuel sales realized within French territory or performed from French shore installations remain within the scope of French tax jurisdiction, subject to PE/treaty reliefs. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the SARL without an option for the family tax regime is subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) at the standard 25% statutory rate (plus possible social surcharge at 3.3% on amounts of corporate tax exceeding €763,000 applicable to entities with turnover above €7,630,000). Territorial scope: France (unitary jurisdiction); international shipping income is governed by domestic territoriality rules and applicable double-tax treaties, with foreign permanent establishment profits exempt from French IS under Article 209 CGI unless placed within the group tax consolidation election. Marine bunkering services and fuel sales realized within French territory or performed from French shore installations remain within the scope of French tax jurisdiction, subject to PE/treaty reliefs. Tax-exempt zone status: none applicable in metropolitan France.

Available election forms

As a default IS taxpayer, the SARL may elect for the 'SARL de famille' personal-income-tax regime (Art. 239 bis AA CGI) if all partners are natural persons within the same family group and the SARL qualifies as a small or medium-sized enterprise (fewer than 50 employees and turnover under €10,000,000) — the election is irrevocable unless loss of qualifying status. Standard election under the rules laid out in Article 206 allows placement under the personal income tax (IR) regime; alternatively, group tax integration (option for régime de l'intégration fiscale) under Articles 223 A to 223 U CGI is available at 95% direct ownership for French-resident subsidiaries of the same group. No US-style check-the-box mechanism exists; hybrid entity mismatches are neutralized by the ATAD-based rules of Article 212 bis CGI and Article 205 A CGI applying look-through treatment for fiscally transparent foreign entities. Export and intra-Community supply options must be documented contemporaneously per Articles 262 and 289-0 CGI; the company must choose between per-transaction CA3 reporting and the DEB/EC sales list regime as determined by the activity scale.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.2%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a supply-of-goods business such as marine fuel bunkering: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for the franchise en base de TVA (small business exemption), and EUR 37,500 for service activities; however, the SARL engaged in intra-Community or export operations must be registered for TVA without any threshold exemption, including when performing supplies of goods connected with international sea navigation, which are taxable/exempt at the level of the operator. VAT registration is triggered immediately and compulsorily upon the first intra-Community acquisition exceeding EUR 10,000 per year or upon exercising the option for application of VAT. Bunkering sales to vessels used in international commercial shipping are exempt from TVA with a right to deduct input VAT (exonération de TVA pour les navires de commerce international, Art. 262 CGI, Art. 148 of the Directive).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods destined outside the European Union are exempt with credit (economically equivalent to 0% VAT) under Article 262(1) CGI and Article 146 of Directive 2006/112/EC, provided that physical transfer out of EU territory is proven within 90 days of the supply and the exporter retains the export documentation. For the marine bunkering business, fuel and lubricants sold to a vessel engaged in international commercial navigation (voyages on the high seas or non-EU ports) are exempt from TVA under Article 262(3) CGI and the derogation in Art. 148 of the Directive, provided that the buyer is the vessel's operator or a recognized handler for the vessel and the vessel is not reserved for pleasure craft or exclusively private use. The exemption applies regardless of whether ship refuelling is performed at French ports or offshore within French jurisdictional waters, upon submission of a bunker delivery note (BDN) naming the vessel, the international voyage itinerary, and the place of the operation. If refuelling is made to non-commercial craft or to vessels used for intra-EU cabotage without an international route, standard French TVA at 20% applies.

France has enacted a phased mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting framework (loi de finances 2024, Article 19) effective from 1 September 2026 for large companies (sociétés assujetties tenues d'émettre des factures électroniques) and from 1 September 2027 for SMEs and microenterprises. B2B e-invoices must be exchanged through certified Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs) or the state-operated Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); mandatory data fields per the EN 16931 semantic model (hybrid Factur-X format permitted) and transmission to the DGFiP of e-invoice transactions plus e-reporting data for B2C transactions and specific cross-border flows. France does not currently operate a continuous transaction control (CTC) real-time fiscal clearance with digital stamps (as in Italy); nevertheless, full API-based transmission of invoice and transaction data to the PPF is mandatory within one business day after invoice issuance, and EC sales lists (DEB / Etat récapitulatif TVA) must be filed monthly or quarterly. For bunkering export supplies, the DEB declaration must include the counterpart VAT number, nature-of-transaction codes, and the vessel positioning as export/out-of-scope reference supporting the 0% VAT treatment.

The tax administration ex officio can audit export exemption claims within the statutory assessment limitation period, which is 3 years (Art. L.169 LPF) under regular filing, with extensions to 6 or 10 years in cases of undisclosed business activity or fraudulent tax avoidance, respectively. If export status is unfounded or not substantiated within the 10-year retention obligation (Art. L.102 B LPF), the tax authority retroactively reassesses 20% VAT on the full supply value. Fraudulent or deliberate failure to substantiate an export triggers penalties under Article 1729 CGI of 40% of the additional VAT assessment for mauvaise foi (bad faith), or 80% for manoeuvres frauduleuses (fraudulent maneuvers), plus late-payment interest of 0.20% per month under Article 1731 CGI. In the case of a false or forged BDN, export declaration, or proof of international navigation, additional criminal sanctions exist under Articles 1741 and 1743 CGI (criminal tax evasion) and under Book III of the French Customs Code for documentary falsity, including up to EUR 15,000 fines and up to 5 years imprisonment for enterprise managers, in addition to the trade-level consequences of suspension or loss of the DGDDI authorized shipper status. The ECJ and French administrative jurisprudence (e.g., CE, 24/11/2016, n°389280 and CJEU C-183/14 Radu) require the tax administration to consider the good faith of the supplier and the absence of knowledge of irregularities before applying clawbacks; the burden of proof is reversed to the loss on the exporter to demonstrate that the fuel entered international navigation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current account and capital account under Article 63 TFEU and the French Code monétaire et financier; France abolished its general exchange-control regime in December 1990 (Décret 89-938). There are no licensing, priority, or parallel-market mechanisms. Dividend, interest, loan repayment, and profit remittances from a French SARL are unrestricted, subject only to (i) EU/French sanctions asset-freezing and transfer prohibitions, (ii) Banque de France balance-of-payments and external transaction statistical declarations where thresholds apply, and (iii) customs cash declaration for physical currency movements of EUR 10,000 or more. No prior exchange approval is required for capital injection, trade settlement, or hedging operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable in France. No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio is imposed on export or bunker-supply proceeds received in EUR or foreign currency. The SARL may retain, hold, and deploy such proceeds freely in any account, onshore or abroad, subject only to Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical reporting (BOP-SF1 / external transaction declarations) and the general sanctions and anti-money-laundering framework. France has not reintroduced any form of compulsory repatriation or conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (the French central bank and secretariat of the ACPR), acting jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for external financial relations; the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) exercises banking licensing and AML conformance; the European Central Bank governs the eurozone monetary framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW to MEDIUM for working-capital collection accounts; NOT VIABLE for escrow, credit, standby LC, or documentary trade settlement. European Economic Area digital EMIs passporting into France under ACPR registration (for example Qonto, Wise Business, or Revolut Business) can lawfully provide payment-account services and domestic/SEPA collection, but they are legally prohibited from taking deposits as an investment, cannot issue bank guarantees or standby letters of credit, and commonly decline Medium-Risk Enhanced ship-bunkering clients because of sanctions-nexus complexity. Offshore non-EEA digital EMIs without a valid ACPR/EBA passport have no standing to open a French account and would require an EU-licensed entity, a French registered agent, and a full regulatory license. The blueprint therefore uses a licensed traditional bank as the primary rail and an EMI only for sub-limit operating collections.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax. A reduced 15% rate may apply to qualifying small companies on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to statutory conditions, including turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up capital, and qualifying ownership requirements.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax is generally applicable to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. The rate may be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, commonly where the recipient is an eligible EU parent holding at least 10% of the distributing company for the required period and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. An applicable tax treaty may provide a lower rate, commonly 5%, 10%, or 15%, subject to treaty entitlement and beneficial-ownership requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer-pricing documentation generally applies where the French entity or relevant group meets the statutory EUR 150 million threshold, including the applicable turnover or gross-assets test, or where another statutory trigger applies. Simplified transfer-pricing documentation may apply from the EUR 50 million threshold under current French rules. The threshold does not eliminate the arm's-length principle for smaller entities.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a single standalone economic-substance regulation equivalent to certain offshore ESR regimes. Substance is enforced through the arm's-length principle, abuse-of-law doctrine, beneficial-ownership requirements, anti-conduit rules, controlled foreign company provisions, interest limitation rules, general anti-abuse principles, and EU anti-tax-avoidance measures. The French SARL should maintain genuine French management and operational capacity proportionate to its activities, including competent personnel, decision-making, banking controls, commercial risk management, contracts, inventory or fuel-flow records, and evidence of actual bunkering services. A pass-through entity with no functions, assets, risks, or commercial rationale presents a high reassessment and treaty-denial risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, 10% to 80% administrative penalties depending on conduct, late-payment interest, and criminal tax-fraud sanctions that may include imprisonment of up to five years and fines of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated sanctions potentially reaching seven years and EUR 3 million.

Director personal liability: Managers and directors who personally participate in fraud may face criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, professional-management prohibitions, confiscation, and civil recovery. A director may also be held civilly liable for unpaid taxes in cases of serious and repeated managerial misconduct that made recovery from the company impossible. Travel restrictions are case-specific judicial measures and are not automatic.
Shell company, abuse of rights, and treaty conduit arrangements

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, treaty benefits, exemptions, beneficial-owner status, or tax credits; reassessment under French anti-abuse rules; 40% or 80% penalties where applicable; and late-payment interest. A shell company is not, by itself, a separate criminal offence, but sham transactions and fraud may be criminal.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability, criminal liability for participation in fraud or falsification, management bans, and confiscation where they knowingly implement artificial arrangements. Corporate separateness generally remains effective unless statutory personal misconduct or fraudulent abuse is established.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profit, denial of deductions, withholding-tax reassessment, transfer-pricing documentation penalties, late-payment interest, and penalties generally ranging from 10% to 80% depending on the circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they knowingly approve fraudulent pricing, false documentation, or unlawful distributions. Ordinary adverse transfer-pricing outcomes do not automatically create personal liability.
Anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing breach

Statutory sanction: Supervisory, administrative, and criminal consequences may include business restrictions, fines, confiscation, account restrictions, and money-laundering prosecution. Basic money laundering may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 375,000; aggravated cases may carry up to ten years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 750,000, subject to statutory qualification.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or negligent governance where legally established. Courts may impose management prohibitions, confiscation, and other judicial measures; travel restrictions are fact-specific and not automatic.
Sanctions, export-control, and restricted-vessel exposure

Statutory sanction: Asset freezes, payment blocking, licence or authorization consequences, administrative and criminal fines, confiscation, and imprisonment may apply under EU and French sanctions laws. The exact sanction depends on the measure breached, intent, value, and applicable sectoral restrictions.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees who authorize or knowingly facilitate sanctions evasion may face personal criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, management prohibitions, and confiscation. Corporate approval does not protect an individual participant.
Unlicensed marine-fuel operations and customs or excise violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative closure, withdrawal of licences, seizure or confiscation of fuel and equipment, customs and excise reassessment, duty penalties, environmental sanctions, and possible criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for intentional customs, environmental, or safety offences and may face fines, imprisonment where provided by the relevant statute, management bans, and restitution. Civil liability may extend to remediation and third-party losses.
Illegal profit distribution and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, director liability, insolvency-related clawback, damages, tax reassessment, and potential criminal sanctions for misuse of corporate assets or fraudulent management.

Director personal liability: Managers may face restitution, damages, management bans, and imprisonment and fines where misuse of company assets, fraudulent bankruptcy, or other criminal conduct is established. Corporate limited liability does not shield deliberate personal misconduct.
