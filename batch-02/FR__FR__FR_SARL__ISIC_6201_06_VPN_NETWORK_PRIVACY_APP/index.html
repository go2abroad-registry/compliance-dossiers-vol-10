Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official portal https://www.impots.gouv.fr. Corporate registration data and business identifiers are coordinated through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is formed and registered through the INPI Guichet unique. The filing generally includes the articles of association, registered-office evidence, management identification, beneficial-owner information, capital deposit evidence, and required declarations. Following registration, the entity receives a SIREN number, a SIRET number for each establishment, an APE activity code, and tax-account details from the DGFiP. The French VAT number normally follows the format FR plus a two-digit key and the nine-digit SIREN. The entity must activate its professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr and complete the applicable tax registration questionnaire with the competent SIE. A foreign company with taxable French activities but no establishment must apply to the non-resident tax service or the competent SIE for a French VAT number. The application generally requires the foreign incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, proof of business activity, identity and authority documents, transaction and customer details, and evidence of the French taxable supplies. A business established in another EU Member State may generally use the EU administrative cooperation framework and the One Stop Shop where applicable. A business established outside the EU that is liable for French VAT generally must appoint a French fiscal representative, unless an applicable legal exception applies. VAT registration does not itself establish a permanent establishment for corporate income tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A standard SARL is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS). A qualifying SARL may elect for income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, or IR) if statutory conditions are satisfied, including, depending on the regime, qualifying family ownership or eligibility as a newly created company for the limited-duration start-up option. The election is subject to formal filing deadlines and eligibility conditions and is not an unrestricted check-the-box election. France has no general United States-style check-the-box regime. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework and can deny deductions, impose inclusion, or restrict relief for arrangements producing deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes. These rules do not provide a separate elective entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business, there is no general turnover threshold that permits a taxable business making ordinary taxable supplies to remain outside VAT registration; taxable supplies can require registration from commencement. The domestic franchise en base de TVA thresholds generally relevant to services are EUR 37,500 of prior-year turnover and EUR 41,250 as the tolerance threshold, subject to current statutory rules and legislative changes. The corresponding general thresholds for goods and accommodation activities are EUR 85,000 and EUR 93,500. A VPN or network-security SaaS business must analyze electronically supplied services separately. For intra-EU cross-border B2C telecommunications, broadcasting, and electronically supplied services, the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold can determine whether taxation remains in the supplier's Member State or shifts to the customer's Member State, subject to the supplier's election and the One Stop Shop rules. B2C services supplied to customers outside France may therefore require OSS or local VAT registration in the customer's jurisdiction rather than French VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union can generally be exempt from French VAT, economically equivalent to zero-rating, when the supplier can prove that the goods left the EU customs territory and satisfy the documentary and supplier-status conditions. Evidence normally includes the export customs declaration, the export MRN or equivalent exit confirmation, transport evidence, commercial invoices, and accounting records. Goods supplied to VAT-registered customers in another EU Member State may qualify as exempt intra-Community supplies rather than exports, subject to valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, recapitulative reporting, and other conditions. Offshore B2B services, including certain software and network-security services, are generally outside the scope of French VAT where the customer is a business established outside France and the place-of-supply rules allocate taxation to the customer jurisdiction; this is not technically a French 0% export rate. B2C electronically supplied services are generally taxed where the consumer is located and do not automatically qualify for export treatment. VAT refunds require a valid VAT credit, compliant invoices, accounting support, and evidence that input VAT relates to business activities. French-established taxpayers generally request refunds through their VAT returns, while eligible EU and non-EU businesses use the applicable EU electronic refund procedure or the French non-EU refund procedure, subject to reciprocity and filing requirements.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, and export transaction
  • Electronic export customs declaration and export Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent proof that the goods exited the European Union
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or other transport document
  • Packing list and, where applicable, freight-forwarder or carrier delivery evidence
  • Purchase order, sales contract, and accounting records linking the supply to the export
  • Proof of payment or other commercial settlement evidence where relevant
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification evidence, transport proof, and required recapitulative statement
  • For offshore B2B services: customer business-status evidence, customer location evidence, contract, service records, invoice, and reverse-charge wording where applicable
  • Inward remittance evidence only where required by the relevant banking, contractual, or audit process; France has no universal export-VAT requirement for an inward remittance certificate

France is implementing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through approved private platforms, known as plateformes de dématérialisation partenaires (PDPs), connected to the public directory and the DGFiP system. All French-established businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Issuance obligations apply generally to large and intermediate businesses from 1 September 2026 and to small and medium-sized businesses and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027, subject to the final statutory timetable. E-invoicing applies principally to domestic B2B transactions within the scope of French VAT; B2C and cross-border transactions are generally addressed through electronic reporting rather than the domestic B2B invoice channel. France does not operate a universal real-time pre-clearance model for all invoices. Structured invoice data, mandatory fields, transmission through an approved PDP, and electronic reporting of applicable transaction and payment data will be required. Public-sector invoices continue to use Chorus Pro. A SaaS supplier should maintain customer-status, customer-location, VAT-number validation, place-of-supply, invoice, payment, and OSS evidence.

France does not impose a general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export VAT exemption or refund claims. French tax authorities may refuse or reverse the exemption or refund where the supplier cannot prove export, customer status, transport, place of supply, or transaction authenticity. The unpaid VAT may be assessed retrospectively, generally with late-payment interest at the statutory rate and tax penalties. Deliberate or fraudulent conduct can trigger a 40% penalty for deliberate breach or an 80% penalty for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, subject to the applicable procedural rules. Penalties may also apply for inaccurate or omitted VAT returns, invoice deficiencies, failure to transmit required transaction data, and incorrect OSS reporting. Serious VAT fraud may create criminal exposure, including fines and imprisonment for responsible individuals. Input-tax refunds can be clawed back, and repeated or material non-compliance can lead to enhanced audit activity, denial of VAT credits, joint-liability measures in applicable cases, and loss of evidentiary protection for subsequent periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and applies the EU single-market framework. Current-account payments and capital transfers are generally free of exchange controls, subject to EU and French sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, and reporting rules. A French SARL may generally receive and remit dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, loan repayments, and sale proceeds cross-border without prior foreign-exchange approval. Payments remain subject to corporate-law distribution and capital-maintenance rules, withholding tax where applicable, transfer-pricing requirements, treaty eligibility, beneficial-ownership checks, and bank documentary review. Dividend payments require distributable profits and valid corporate approvals; interest and royalty payments require substantiation and applicable tax analysis. Banks may request contracts, invoices, tax records, source-of-funds evidence, and payment-purpose information. Restrictions may apply to sanctioned parties, jurisdictions, or transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No general French rule requires export proceeds to be repatriated by a statutory deadline or converted into euros at a prescribed ratio. Proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to sanctions, tax, accounting, bank terms, and applicable statistical reporting. Cross-border payments and receipts may be reportable through authorized intermediaries for balance-of-payments statistics under applicable rules; the reporting party, threshold, and filing route depend on the transaction and current Banque de France requirements. Cash or bearer instruments of EUR 10,000 or more carried into or out of the EU must be declared to customs under EU cash-control rules; this is distinct from ordinary bank transfers.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France’s central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. The French Ministry of the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty, including the Direction générale du Trésor, administers relevant financial and external-finance rules; the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution supervises covered banks and payment institutions. French customs authorities administer applicable cash declaration requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for routine EUR collections, SEPA transfers, and eligible foreign-currency payments, subject to provider onboarding and supported-country coverage. An EU-licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution is not a bank: e-money is not a bank deposit and is not covered by the deposit-guarantee scheme; safeguarding applies under the provider’s regulatory regime. Verify the provider’s home-state authorization, French passporting or other lawful service basis, safeguarding arrangements, account-name and IBAN availability, limits, chargeback handling, and permitted business categories. VPN, encryption, privacy, high-volume card subscriptions, elevated refunds, and customers or counterparties in sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions may trigger enhanced review or restrictions. Maintain a French or other eligible credit-institution account for capital deposit, payroll, tax payments, and operational resilience where required or prudent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For share capital, transfer funds from the subscriber’s own verified account to an eligible French capital-depositary account, identify the transfer as share-capital subscription or incorporation capital, and provide subscription documents and subscriber identity evidence. Obtain the capital-deposit certificate, complete registration, then provide the Kbis extract and any requested corporate records to release funds to the operational account. France has no general foreign-exchange approval or mandatory conversion requirement for ordinary equity funding. For subsequent equity injections, use the correct corporate instrument and approvals, document the subscription or premium, and record the transaction in the company’s accounts. For shareholder loans, document lender identity, principal, currency, interest, maturity, repayment terms, corporate approvals, and transfer-pricing and withholding-tax treatment. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments and SWIFT for other cross-border wires; give accurate payer, beneficiary, IBAN/BIC, purpose, and intermediary details. Do not split transfers to avoid screening or reporting. Apply sanctions screening, AML review, invoice and service substantiation, and tax and accounting controls. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File / Local File if group consolidated turnover or gross assets ≥ €400 million. Form 2257-SD filing if company turnover ≥ €50 million or group threshold met. Documentation must be available within 30 days of tax audit request.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). Substance is enforced through abuse of law (abus de droit, Art. L64 LPF), abnormal act of management, GAAR, and anti-treaty shopping. For SARL, French tax authority expects real office, local employees, board decisions, and risk control. Lack of substance can trigger PE recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and 80% penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI: 5 years imprisonment and €500,000 fine; aggravated (organized gang, concealed accounts) 7 years and €2,000,000. Tax adjustment plus 40% or 80% penalty and 0.20% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: De facto or de jure managers jointly liable for taxes and penalties; criminal conviction, travel ban, loss of civic rights, imprisonment.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Abus de droit (Art. L64 LPF): nullity of arrangement, reassessment, 80% penalty, and 0.20% monthly interest. Criminal sanctions if fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors liable for restitution, fines, and imprisonment; veil piercing under Art. L267 LPF for tax debts.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: €10,000 penalty per audited year; increased to €50,000 for non-response to notice. TP adjustment taxed at 25% plus 40% penalty if abuse.

Director personal liability: Managers can be jointly liable for adjusted tax if fault; criminal sanctions for false documentation.
AML/CTF Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Code monétaire et financier: fines up to €1 million or 50% of illicit amounts; 5 years imprisonment for willful failure. ACPR sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for fines and imprisonment; management ban.
VAT Fraud / E-Services

Statutory sanction: 40% penalty for deliberate breach; 80% for hidden activity; 5 years and €500,000 under Art. 1741 CGI; 0.20% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Joint liability for VAT debts under Art. L267 LPF; criminal conviction and imprisonment.
