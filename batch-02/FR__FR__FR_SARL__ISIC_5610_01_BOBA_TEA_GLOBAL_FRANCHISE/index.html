Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) maintained by the Greffier du Tribunal de Commerce with territorial jurisdiction over the registered office; national electronic portal: Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formulaires.entreprises.gouv.fr), coordinated by the Conseil National des Greffiers des Tribunaux de Commerce (CNGTC) / Infogreffe.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title III: Articles L. 223-1 to L. 223-43 (SARL formation, capital and management); L. 223-2 (no statutory minimum capital), L. 223-7 (full subscription; minimum one-fifth payment of cash shares), L. 123-11 and R. 123-30 et seq. (RCS registration); Loi Doubin, Article L. 330-3 C.com (franchise pre-contractual disclosure); Décret n° 2023-796 (Guichet Unique electronic formalities).

Formation / constitutional filings

Form M0 'Demande d'immatriculation d'une personne morale au RCS' together with the executed Statuts (articles of association), submitted through the Guichet Unique; the incorporation is perfected upon entry in the RCS and evidenced by the official 'extrait Kbis'.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 36 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for a SARL (L. 223-2 C.com); capital is freely fixed in the articles. At formation the capital must be fully subscribed and at least one-fifth (20%) of each cash share must be paid up (L. 223-7 C.com); in-kind contributions are fully released upon incorporation and the remaining quota may be called within five years. Cash contributions must be blocked on a dedicated escrow account ('compte bloqué de fonds') with a credit institution, a notary or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the deposit attestation is filed with the RCS and the funds are released only upon production of the Kbis.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: 5th-category ERP (N ≤ 200) clearance by the Commission de Sécurité, including ventilation, emergency exits, fire extinguishers and a kitchen fire-suppression system. Structural: technical control by a certified bureau de contrôle (e.g., SOCOTEC, APAVE) for floor load capacity of the syrup storage area and any mezzanine seating, and verification of the grease-extraction duct. Occupational: affiliation to the statutory occupational health service (médecine du travail), completion of the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP), electrical installation conformity certificate for kitchen equipment, posting of mandatory notices, and the 4-yearly medical aptitude review for the staff.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for a SARL (L. 223-2 C.com); capital is freely fixed in the articles. At formation the capital must be fully subscribed and at least one-fifth (20%) of each cash share must be paid up (L. 223-7 C.com); in-kind contributions are fully released upon incorporation and the remaining quota may be called within five years. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

France – Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Public Accounts; tax portal: impots.gouv.fr; business formalities one-stop: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; customs authority for export procedures: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) – DELTA platform; public e-invoicing hub: Portail Public de Facturation (PPF).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – SARL is taxable on its own account under art. 206-1 CGI at the flat 25% IS rate on profits attributable to French business operations (territorial scope under art. 209 CGI; foreign PE profits excluded with foreign tax credit). No pass-through to members; distributed dividends are subject to the 30% flat withholding (PFU) or the 12.8% option, plus 3% social contribution on distributions. The entity cannot elect any check-the-box flow-through status.

Available election forms

France has no US-style check-the-box or elective hybrid classification. Express legal elections available: (i) option for income-tax regime (art. 239 bis AA CGI) for SARLs with a maximum of 50 individual shareholders and corporate capital not exceeding 10%, irrevocable for five accounting periods; (ii) fiscal integration (art. 223 A CGI) for a chain of at least 95%-owned French subsidiaries headed by an IS parent; (iii) VAT filing simplification (régime réel simplifié, art. 293 CC CGI); (iv) interest deduction cap and anti-hybrid rules (art. 212 bis CGI implementing ATAD) apply mandatorily – no elective carve-out; an EBITDA-based 30%/EUR 3 million limitation is the default statutory ceiling.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise-en-base thresholds in force for 2025: EUR 91,900 annual turnover for supplies of goods; EUR 36,800 for services (franchise royalty income falls in the services category); tolerance ceilings EUR 101,000 and EUR 39,100. The SARL exceeds the EUR 36,800 services threshold in its first fiscal year by the nature of the franchise model (royalties plus syrup supply), so TVA registration is compulsory from the first taxable transaction without any de minimis suspension; the EUR 10,000 EU distance-sales threshold only concerns non-established EU sellers and is not relevant to this entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération avec droit à déduction) applies under art. 262-I-1° CGI to goods definitively exported from the EU customs territory; the French SARL must prove physical exit of the goods using the DGDDI electronic export procedure (DAU accepted and MRN closed by customs, with exit within the customs-prescribed deadlines – presentation generally within 30 days and exit monitored under UCC art. 107). Syrups and supplies shipped to foreign franchise outlets qualify; prepared beverages sold by French domestic franchisees remain taxable in France (10% reduced rate for drink service) while franchise royalties invoiced to foreign franchisees are B2B services placed outside France under arts. 259-259 C CGI (destination principle, reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction) and must carry the mention 'TVA non applicable – art. 259 B CGI'. No statutory French FX-repatriation deadline exists for VAT zero-rating; the 180-day benchmark is an internal treasury/customs audit reference (cross-border cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more are reportable under the Code monétaire et financier).

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU) lodged in the DGDDI DELTA platform with MRN closure
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR consignment note (road) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice stating the export nature, the SARL's TVA intracommunautaire number and the foreign franchisee's VAT/identification number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing payment by the foreign franchisee

Mandatory structured e-invoicing under art. 289 CGI and ordonnance n° 2021-1190, as deferred by décret n° 2023-888: from 1 September 2026 all taxable persons must be able to receive e-invoices, and large enterprises (≥ EUR 150M turnover / 500 employees) and mid-sized enterprises must issue e-invoices; small and medium enterprises must issue e-invoices from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be transmitted through a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) in Factur-X, UBL or CII formats; e-reporting of domestic sales within 1 business day and of intra-EU/export transactions within 10 days is transmitted to DGFiP. France does not apply per-invoice fiscal stamping or real-time pre-approval by the tax authority; compliance is enforced by ex-post analytics on PPF data.

DGFiP and DGDDI cross-match the DAU/MRN data, transport documents and PPF e-reporting; failure to substantiate export triggers reversal of the zero-rating with re-invoicing of 20% VAT, monthly late interest of 0.20% (art. 1727 CGI) and penalties of 40% for bad faith or 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres (art. 1729 CGI); fictitious or non-compliant invoices expose the issuer to a 50% amende (art. 1737 CGI); tax fraud (art. 1741 CGI) may entail criminal fines up to EUR 500,000 and seven years' imprisonment for the gérant, with joint and several liability under art. L.267 LPF; clawback of input VAT improperly deducted is retroactive. The statutory reassessment window is three years for declared operations and six years for undeclared ones (art. L.176 LPF), with a ten-year customs penalty horizon.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies unrestricted euro convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No prior French exchange-control approval is required for ordinary inbound equity injections, franchise royalty collections, intercompany loans, or profit remittances to non-resident shareholders. Residual constraints are limited to EU/UN economic sanctions, the French non-cooperative states (ECNC) regime requiring prior ministerial authorization for certain financial relationships, and the enhanced documentary and beneficial-ownership reporting imposed by French AML/CFT law. Dividends and royalties may be repatriated freely, provided the remitting French bank receives a valid purpose code and the transaction, where applicable, is captured in the Banque de France balance-of-payments and annual liaison financière returns. Related-party remittances must be supported by transfer-pricing substance documentation to withstand both tax and AML characterization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has abolished general export-proceeds surrender and forced-conversion rules. Under the current liberalized framework, export receipts from ordinary jurisdictions may be retained in foreign currency, and no statutory percentage conversion into euros and no statutory repatriation deadline apply. The SARL is expected only to book the proceeds in its accounting records and to allow the collecting French bank to perform the required statistical declaration to the Banque de France. Exceptional mandatory repatriation and euro conversion may be triggered solely for transactions involving countries on the French ECNC list or designated jurisdictions under EU/UN sanctions, in which case prior authorization from the Direction Générale du Trésor is required, and full repatriation through a licensed French credit institution with conversion of the proceeds must be completed within the contractual settlement period.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks, jointly with the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) as the prudential supervisor, the Direction Générale du Trésor of the French Ministry of Economy and Finance for foreign-exchange authorizations relating to non-cooperative states, and TRACFIN as the French Financial Intelligence Unit. The legal basis is the Code monétaire et financier (CMF), in particular Articles L151-1 et seq. CMF and Décret n° 89-938 du 29 décembre 1989 on financial relations with foreign countries.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for auxiliary multi-currency treasury operations, but not acceptable as the sole banking relationship. ACPR-regulated French electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto S.A., Shine S.A. (Groupe Société Générale), and MangoPay S.A. may provide French IBANs, virtual IBANs, SEPA and SWIFT collections, and multi-currency e-money wallets. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot grant credit, receive deposits, or pay interest on balances, and their e-money balances are not covered by the French deposit guarantee (FGDR); EMIs typically refuse SWIFT beneficiary credits from high-risk, sanctioned, or formally restricted foreign jurisdictions; currency conversion must be executed by a licensed payment institution or credit institution rather than the EMI itself; derivatives and structured FX hedging cannot be delivered through the EMI platform. For the global franchise royalty network, the EMI is best used as a treasury dashboard and SEPA collection gateway, whilst the legal settlement backbone must remain a tier-1 French bank holding the capital account and executing hedging documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital injection protocol: (1) The SARL opens a dedicated capital escrow/subscription account (compte bloqué) at BNP Paribas or Société Générale and obtains the bank’s commitment to release funds upon registration; (2) the non-resident shareholder remits via SWIFT MT103 (from a non-EEA jurisdiction) or SEPA Credit Transfer (from an EEA jurisdiction) strictly in EUR, naming the SARL as beneficiary with full legal name and registration number; (3) the payment must contain in Field 70 the remittance information referencing the deed of subscription (bordereau de souscription), the file reference of the French notary or bank, and the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (equity capital) or 'INVL' (investment loan) for shareholder advances; (4) the French bank will verify the deposit certificate (attestation de dépôt des fonds) issued under the French Commercial Code and will ensure the balance-of-payments declaration is transmitted to the Banque de France where the transaction threshold applies; (5) after publication of the incorporation notice, the funds are released into the SARL’s operational account. For remittances originally in USD, RMB, or HKD, the shareholder’s bank must convert the amount into EUR before transmission and state the all-in EUR value in the SWIFT instruction to avoid residual shortfall.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (i) SWIFT MT103 Customer Credit Transfers for non-EEA suppliers, international logistics providers, and overseas franchise support, sent through the French bank’s EUR clearing on TARGET2 or through its USD correspondent network (CHIPS/Fedwire) for USD-denominated contracts; (ii) SEPA Credit Transfers and SEPA Instant Credit Transfers for recurring EEA suppliers of syrup concentrates, packaging, and European sub-franchisees; (iii) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (sight or usance) issued on the SARL’s account under ICC UCP 600 and pre-advised via MT700 by the issuing French bank for large-volume purchases of tapioca, tea leaves, and equipment from Asia; (iv) Standby Letters of Credit under ISP98 or on-demand bank guarantees where the SARL provides security to franchisees or suppliers; (v) Documentary Collections (CAD/DP) for mid-sized non-EEA batches. All non-EUR disbursements above EUR 50,000 must be covered by a confirmed FX deal and the underlying commercial invoice; payments to ECNC-listed or sanctioned jurisdictions must be pre-cleared by compliance; French customs export declarations (DAU/EXA) and certificates of origin must accompany goods-related settlement documents to satisfy Banque de France statistical requirements.Outright FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY) limited to demonstrable commercial exposure arising from franchise royalties, syrup imports, and intercompany flows, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) master agreement with acceptable CSA collateral terms.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or illiquid Asian currencies (CNY, MYR, VND, TWD, KRW) executed through an ACPR-authorized credit institution or an international market-maker, strictly for hedging underlying trade or royalty inflows, with a maximum maturity of 12 months and no leverage.Cross-currency swaps (vanilla fixed-for-fixed or floating-for-floating) to align the currency denomination of the SARL’s intercompany shareholder debt and the future royalty stream in EUR, USD, and RMB, subject to French bank credit approval and prudential documentation.European-style FX options (protective puts, zero-cost collars, risk reversals) for the purchase price of imported goods and forecast royalty receipts; writing uncovered options or entering exotic/leveraged derivatives is unavailable to a non-professional corporate client under MIFID II and would be rejected by French compliance as an unhedged speculative transaction.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for auxiliary multi-currency treasury operations, but not acceptable as the sole banking relationship. ACPR-regulated French electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto S.A., Shine S.A. (Groupe Société Générale), and MangoPay S.A. may provide French IBANs, virtual IBANs, SEPA and SWIFT collections, and multi-currency e-money wallets. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot grant credit, receive deposits, or pay interest on balances, and their e-money balances are not covered by the French deposit guarantee (FGDR); EMIs typically refuse SWIFT beneficiary credits from high-risk, sanctioned, or formally restricted foreign jurisdictions; currency conversion must be executed by a licensed payment institution or credit institution rather than the EMI itself; derivatives and structured FX hedging cannot be delivered through the EMI platform. For the global franchise royalty network, the EMI is best used as a treasury dashboard and SEPA collection gateway, whilst the legal settlement backbone must remain a tier-1 French bank holding the capital account and executing hedging documentation.

25% (standard corporate income tax rate for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2022; prior fiscal years: 26.5% to 27.5%)

25% on gross French-source dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders (Article 119-2 CGI). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File are mandatory if the French company or a French consolidated group exceeds at least one of: (a) total consolidated balance sheet ≥ EUR 400 million, or (b) consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 400 million (Article L. 13 AA CGI). Below these thresholds, a simplified documentation obligation may apply on request; EU-compliant SME groups are generally exempt from a full dossier if fewer than 250 employees and either turnover ≤ EUR 50 million or balance sheet total ≤ EUR 43 million, but must keep transfer pricing records showing the arm's length nature of franchise royalty and syrup-supply charges.

Economic substance scope: France has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in common-law offshore centres. Instead, French tax law applies the abuse of law doctrine (Article L64 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales), the general anti-abuse rule in Articles L64 A and L64 B LPF, and the MLI/principal purpose test. A French S.A.R.L. must prove real local substance: actual management decisions, premises, employees, independent corporate governance, IP ownership, invoicing, and payment flows. If a PE or the French entity is found to lack substance or is used for treaty abuse, the tax administration may disregard it, reallocate profits to the true economic owner, or deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI, up to EUR 500,000 fine and five years' imprisonment (EUR 750,000 and seven years when committed by an organised gang). Administrative penalties include late-filing surcharges (5%–10%), late-payment interest at 0.20% per month, and a 40% penalty for bad-faith deficiency under Article 1729 CGI.

Director personal liability: The gérant may be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt under Article 267 LPF if the fraud is established; criminal conviction may trigger commercial disqualification, seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and exclusion from management in any French company.
Artificial Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Application of Article L64 LPF abuse of law: reassessment of tax plus a penalty of 80% of the avoided tax (or 40% where the abuse is outweighed by non-exclusive legitimate purposes). The tax administration may also apply anti-hybrid and PE recharacterisation rules, deny treaty benefits under MLI, and claw back tax credits and reduced rates.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed declarations or allowed the shell to operate can be held personally liable for the reassessed taxes and penalties under the abuse of law procedure; they may also be prosecuted for tax evasion with criminal sanctions and travel bans if a flight risk exists.
Money Laundering and AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 324-1 et seq. of the French Criminal Code, a maximum of ten years' imprisonment and a EUR 750,000 fine for non-aggravated money laundering; aggravated forms may double both amounts. Administrative sanctions for AML compliance failures under the French Monetary and Financial Code include fines up to EUR 1 million and prohibition orders.

Director personal liability: Individual managers face criminal liability, confiscation of assets, disqualification from exercising commercial or professional duties, travel bans, and possible civil liability toward victims and third parties.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Article 57 CGI permits full tax reassessment treating the price difference as deemed distributions subject to withholding; late-payment interest and a 40% intentional-deficiency penalty under Article 1729 CGI may apply; potential double taxation persists absent mutual agreement procedure.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign non-arm's length arrangements may be personally accountable to the tax administration and to the company for losses arising from penalties; may face criminal charges for false statements if documents are fabricated, and may be subject to travel bans in the context of a criminal investigation.
