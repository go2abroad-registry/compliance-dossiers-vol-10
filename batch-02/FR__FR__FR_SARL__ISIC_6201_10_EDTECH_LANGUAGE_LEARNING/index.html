Competent corporate registry

Greffe du tribunal de commerce territorially competent for the registered office, acting through the Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises administered by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Code de commerce, Book II: Articles L.210-2 (subscription and payment of share capital), L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL formation and governance), R.223-1 to R.223-29; Décret n°84-406 on the registre du commerce et des sociétés; Articles L.123-3 and R.123-1 et seq. on company registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'entreprise using Cerfa n°14777 (M0), submitted electronically as a single formalities dossier through the Guichet unique INPI and transmitted to the commercial registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum capital is freely fixed (statutory floor of EUR 1). All shares must be subscribed at formation; cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% at incorporation and the balance may be paid within five years; in-kind contributions must be fully released upon formation. Where funds are deposited before registration, the manager must open an escrow or blocked capital account and obtain a bank or notarial attestation de dépôt des fonds, which is attached to the incorporation file and released only after issuance of the Kbis.

Funding & unlock path: For the registered office and administrative workplace, the company must hold the landlord's premises occupancy clearance, verify structural and fire-safety conformity, provide fire extinguishers and evacuation procedures under Code du travail Articles R.4227-1 et seq., ensure work access safety under Article R.4224-1, and maintain an electrical installation conformity check under Article R.4216-1. No additional civil-defence clearance is required for a software-as-a-service operation.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum capital is freely fixed (statutory floor of EUR 1). All shares must be subscribed at formation; cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% at incorporation and the balance may be paid within five years; in-kind contributions must be fully released upon formation. Where funds are deposited before registration, the manager must open an escrow or blocked capital account and obtain a bank or notarial attestation de dépôt des fonds, which is attached to the incorporation file and released only after issuance of the Kbis.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy and Finance, France; tax portal: impots.gouv.fr; business registration portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique); customs and export VAT controls: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL: (1) incorporate via the Guichet Unique portal, which automatically issues the SIREN and SIRET identifiers and the intra-community VAT number (FR+11 digits); (2) file the DPAE for the legal representative and complete non-resident director documentation; (3) open a French bank account and notify the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for corporate income tax (IS) registration; (4) for foreign non-established entities supplying digital services to French consumers, register for French VAT via the DGFiP foreign-entities desk (Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères) using the online non-resident registration module, or elect the EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) to obtain an OSS VAT identifier; (5) where physical goods are exported, obtain an EORI number (DGDDI) and, if required, a customs broker mandate; lead time is typically 2 to 6 weeks for standard VAT/IS registration, while OSS registration is granted within a few days upon online application.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SARL: (1) incorporate via the Guichet Unique portal, which automatically issues the SIREN and SIRET identifiers and the intra-community VAT number (FR+11 digits); (2) file the DPAE for the legal representative and complete non-resident director documentation; (3) open a French bank account and notify the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for corporate income tax (IS) registration; (4) for foreign non-established entities supplying digital services to French consumers, register for French VAT via the DGFiP foreign-entities desk (Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères) using the online non-resident registration module, or elect the EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) to obtain an OSS VAT identifier; (5) where physical goods are exported, obtain an EORI number (DGDDI) and, if required, a customs broker mandate; lead time is typically 2 to 6 weeks for standard VAT/IS registration, while OSS registration is granted within a few days upon online application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (IS) at 25%, with a reduced rate of 15% on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs with turnover below EUR 10,000,000. Statutory elections available: (a) option for pass-through taxation under Article 239 bis A of the French General Tax Code (CGI) for close-held small SARLs with fewer than 50 employees and turnover or total assets not exceeding EUR 10,000,000, effective for a maximum of 5 consecutive fiscal years; (b) French fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for a group of companies where the parent holds at least 95% of the subsidiary's share capital; (c) election to be subject to VAT even when below the small-business franchise threshold; (d) no US-style check-the-box mechanism exists; hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatches are instead neutralized through domestic anti-hybrid provisions (Articles 205 bis and 212 bis CGI) implementing EU Directive 2016/1164, which deny deductions and impose symmetrical classification for entities treated as transparent in one jurisdiction but opaque in France.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic franchise en base de TVA: EUR 91,900 for goods and EUR 36,800 for services per year; EdTech subscription services (digitally supplied) are treated as services, so the EUR 36,800 threshold applies to a French-established micro-business; however, EU cross-border B2C digital services supplied by an EU-established supplier trigger mandatory OSS registration once the EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-sales/digital-services threshold is exceeded; non-EU established suppliers have a zero threshold and must register for French VAT or the EU OSS/IOSS from the first euro of French taxable turnover; an active French SARL engaged in EdTech B2C subscriptions must register for VAT immediately unless it validly relies on the franchise, and must in any event register if it intends to charge VAT, issue VAT invoices, or recover input VAT under the normal regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical goods: 0% French VAT applies where the exporter lodges a customs export declaration (DAU), the goods physically leave EU territory within 90 days of supply, and transport documentation is maintained (Article 262-I CGI; EU VAT Directive Articles 146-147). EdTech subscriptions (electronically supplied services): the goods-style zero-rating mechanism is not used; supplies to non-EU B2C customers are outside the scope of French VAT because the place of supply is the customer's country of residence (Article 259 D CGI); supplies to EU or non-EU B2B customers are shifted to the customer's VAT account under reverse charge (Article 259-1 CGI; Article 44 EU VAT Directive); for EU B2C cross-border digital services, the French supplier applies the consumer's Member State VAT rate through the OSS return and cannot claim zero rating; the supplier must substantiate customer location via IP address, bank details, billing address, SIM card country code, landline location, or other commercially reasonable evidence as set out in the EU VAT administrative cooperation guidance; for app-store sales (Apple IAP / Google Play), the platform operator may self-assume the VAT liability as deemed supplier, and the French publisher must reconcile the associated net settlements against the VAT treatment applicable to each app-store territory.

  • Customs export declaration (DAU / Document Administratif Unique) lodged electronically in the DELTA system and accepted by DGDDI
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR road consignment note evidencing physical departure of the goods from EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice incorporating the exact Incoterms, export VAT exempt annotation ('Exportation - Hors champ TVA, Article 262-I CGI') and the customer's industrial/commercial status
  • Proof of settlement and inward remittance certificate (bank SWIFT/IBAN statement or Bank Payment Obligation) evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Supply-chain substantiation file: purchase and delivery records, customs valuation dossier, packing lists, and internal transport instructions retained for audit defense

France is implementing a mandatory structured e-invoicing and e-reporting reform (Loi de Finances 2020; specification published by the arrêté of 29 July 2024): from 1 September 2026, large and mid-size entities must issue B2B e-invoices and all French VAT-registered businesses must receive them electronically; from 1 September 2027, all VAT payers, including foreign companies registered for French VAT, must issue e-invoices; supported formats are Factur-X, UBL, and CII; exchange routes are through a certified Dematerialization Partner Platform (PDP) or the free Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); invoices must be transmitted to the DGFiP via the PDP/PPF within one business day of issuance; e-reporting data for B2C, intra-EU, and export transactions is filed monthly by the 10th day of the following month; there is no real-time digital stamping or serialization of each invoice as in Latin American fiscal clearance models; instead, France applies transactional batch control through the PPF/PDP ecosystem, supplemented by the mandatory FEC (Fichier des Écritures Comptables) accounting-file obligation and certified anti-tamper cash-register software for POS retail; marketplace sellers must also consider the deemed-supplier rules under Article 242 bis CGI where the platform is treated as the taxable seller.

Export VAT zero-rating claims are subject to intensive scrutiny by DGFiP and DGDDI. Failure to produce the mandatory documentation bundle within 30 days of an audit request exposes the exporter to immediate VAT regularisation, output VAT on the full invoice amount, intérêts de retard at 0.20% per month, and penalties of 40% for lack of good faith, 80% for deliberate tax fraud (mauvaise foi / manœuvres frauduleuses), and up to 100% for organized fraud chains (Article 1729 CGI). Customs violations for fictional or false export declarations carry confiscation of the goods and transport means, fines of one to two times the value of the object of fraud (Code des douanes Articles 413-1 to 414-2), and criminal imprisonment, which can reach 10 years for organized smuggling; on the tax side, Article 1741 CGI criminal tax fraud is punishable by a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and 5 years' imprisonment, increased to EUR 3,000,000 and 7 years when committed by an organized group. Claims are clawed back retroactively across the applicable limitation windows (generally 3 years under Article L.176 CGI-LPF for VAT, extended to 6 years where the activity is conducted abroad under Articles L.169-LPF for direct taxes); therefore all export zero-rating evidence, FX repatriation records, and app-store settlement statements must be retained for a minimum of 10 years, with particular attention to the 180-day FX repatriation deadline as a compliance red flag when exceeded.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full member of the Eurozone and the EU, with the euro (EUR) as legal tender. The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. France adheres to OECD and EU liberalization codes, and there are no general restrictions on capital movements. However, certain transactions require reporting to the Banque de France for statistical and balance-of-payments purposes, particularly for direct investment, real estate, and loans exceeding €15,000. Profit remittances (dividends, royalties, interest) are freely transferable subject to standard corporate tax withholding (if applicable) and anti-money laundering (AML) verification. No prior exchange control approval is required for current account payments, but documentation must be retained to evidence the underlying transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency earnings in bank accounts, either in France or abroad, subject to reporting obligations. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation, but all cross-border transactions must be reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics if they exceed €50,000 per transaction or €150,000 per calendar year in aggregate. The euro is the functional currency; conversion of foreign currency receipts is at the discretion of the company, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in coordination with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) and the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) for banking supervision; FX policy is set by the Ministry of Economy and Finance, with operational oversight by the Banque de France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. France recognizes Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) under the EU PSD2 framework, regulated by the ACPR. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., those licensed in Lithuania, UK, or Singapore) can operate in France under passporting rights if they are EU-licensed, but they are not full banks and may not offer credit or deposit facilities. For an EdTech company with subscription revenue, an EMI can handle payment processing and FX conversion, but for treasury management and capital injection, a full bank account is recommended. Legal constraints: EMIs must comply with French AML/CFT regulations and may require a local agent for KYC. They cannot provide overdraft facilities or hold client funds as deposits. For high-volume cross-border transactions, a tier-1 bank is more robust.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For capital injection from shareholders (e.g., from a parent company or investors), the funds must be transferred via SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code. For equity contributions, use the purpose code 'S0101' (Direct investment - equity capital) or 'S0102' (Direct investment - other capital) as per the Banque de France's balance-of-payments classification. The transfer must be accompanied by a copy of the shareholders' resolution and the capital increase deed. For loans, use purpose code 'S0201' (Loans and credits) and ensure the loan agreement is documented. The bank will require a declaration to the Banque de France if the amount exceeds €15,000. The funds must be credited to a French bank account in EUR; if the injection is in foreign currency, the bank will convert it at the prevailing rate, and the company must report the transaction. For capital escrow, the funds are held in a blocked account until the SARL is fully registered, then released to the operational account.For trade settlements (e.g., paying for software licenses, marketing services, or content licensing), the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers. For larger transactions, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) may be used, but for an EdTech company, MT103 is standard. The bank will require supporting documents (invoices, contracts) to comply with French foreign exchange regulations. For recurring payments to EU suppliers, SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area) transfers are efficient and low-cost. For non-EU suppliers, SWIFT is used. The company must ensure that the purpose code on the transfer matches the nature of the payment (e.g., 'S0301' for services, 'S0401' for royalties). For disbursements to Apple and Google (for IAP and Play Billing), the company will receive funds from these platforms, not disburse to them; the outbound flows are for operational expenses. The bank may require a list of expected counterparties and their jurisdictions to pre-clear the account.Forward contracts (FX Forwards) with a maturity up to 12 months, available from French banks for EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and other major currencies. These are OTC derivatives and require an ISDA or French master agreement.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are permissible for currencies with convertibility restrictions, but for the EdTech's main currencies (USD, GBP, CNY), NDFs are not necessary as they are fully convertible. However, if the company has exposure to emerging market currencies (e.g., INR, BRL), NDFs can be used.Currency swaps (FX Swaps) for managing short-term liquidity and rolling over positions. These are allowed under French law and are subject to EMIR reporting requirements.Options (FX options) are also permissible, but they are less common for small companies due to premium costs. The bank will require a credit line or collateral for any derivative transactions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. France recognizes Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) under the EU PSD2 framework, regulated by the ACPR. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., those licensed in Lithuania, UK, or Singapore) can operate in France under passporting rights if they are EU-licensed, but they are not full banks and may not offer credit or deposit facilities. For an EdTech company with subscription revenue, an EMI can handle payment processing and FX conversion, but for treasury management and capital injection, a full bank account is recommended. Legal constraints: EMIs must comply with French AML/CFT regulations and may require a local agent for KYC. They cannot provide overdraft facilities or hold client funds as deposits. For high-volume cross-border transactions, a tier-1 bank is more robust.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2022; qualifying small enterprises benefit from a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividend withholding under article 119 bis 2 of the French General Tax Code (CGI): 25% for non-resident corporate shareholders; 12.8% for non-resident individual shareholders, with applicable social levies in the absence of treaty protection.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File, 'fichier des prix de transfert') applies to enterprises established in France with annual turnover above EUR 50,000,000 or more than 200 employees, including foreign-owned French subsidiaries and groups satisfying those thresholds. Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply Economic Substance Regulations of the offshore model. However, a French SARL must demonstrate genuine substance through its registered office, operational management exercised in France, payroll, premises, decision-making functions, and risk-taking capacity. Substance is enforced through the abuse-of-law doctrine (article L.64 of the LPF), the general anti-abuse rule, CFC rules, anti-hybrid provisions, the OECD BEPS minimum standards, and the Pillar Two global minimum tax of 15% for large groups. A shell SARL whose IP and digital platform functions are managed abroad without French substance will be treated as managed and controlled outside France or disregarded for treaty and directive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under article 1741 CGI: criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, increased to EUR 750,000 and 7 years for aggravated cases (organized gang, use of offshore vehicles, or international concealment). Tax reassessment carries an 80% penalty for hidden activity plus 0.20% monthly interest and confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: The gérant is criminally liable as perpetrator or accomplice. Courts may impose a management ban, travel ban, driving licence suspension, or committal orders for unpaid fines and taxes. The corporate veil is pierced where the manager's gross fault (faute lourde) caused the company's default or where the company is a mere facade for personal enrichment.
Abuse of Law / Artificial Arrangement (Abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: Application of article L.64 of the LPF disregards the abusive transaction and reallocates profits to the French entity; an 80% penalty applies for abuse of law, plus 0.20% monthly interest. The GAAR is complemented by the CFC rules of article 209 B CGI for low-tax passive income.

Director personal liability: The manager who initiated the artificial scheme in his own interest is personally liable for the reassessed tax debt. Criminal prosecution under article 1741 may follow, with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 500,000; asset attachments and exit bans are available coercive measures.
Shell Company / False Treaty or Directive Claim

Statutory sanction: Denial of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive or double tax treaty benefit, recharacterization of the dividend at the statutory 25% withholding rate, plus interest and penalties. The distributing SARL may be held jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax if it failed to obtain, prior to payment, a valid sworn statement and tax residence certificate.

Director personal liability: The French gérant who certified false substance statements is liable for restitution of the unpaid withholding tax. In aggravated patterns, criminal liability for fraud, forgery or issuance of false attestations may apply, with sentences up to 5 years' imprisonment and fines up to EUR 375,000.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of EUR 15,000 per fiscal year for failure to present the transfer pricing documentation; reassessment of shifted profits with a 40% penalty for bad faith or 80% for fraudulent concealment, plus 0.20% monthly interest. Late or missing Country-by-Country notification carries an additional EUR 15,000 fine per entity.

Director personal liability: Where the transfer price was fixed personally by the gérant with gross negligence, the manager can be held jointly liable for the deficiency. Veil-piercing occurs in exceptional circumstances, exposing the manager's personal estate to collection and confiscation measures.
AML / Money Laundering in Digital Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Violations of the French Monetary and Financial Code anti-money-laundering obligations can trigger fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for repeat offences. Tax fraud sanctions under article 1741 CGI are cumulative, and the proceeds of the offence are subject to confiscation orders.

Director personal liability: A natural-person gérant convicted of money laundering (blanchiment) faces up to 5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, plus professional bans and travel bans. Civil liability actions by creditors or the tax administration may reach the manager's personal assets.
