Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French national tax authority; professional tax portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate formation and registration via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) operated by INPI; non-resident VAT direct registration desk: Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), 10 rue du Centre, 93166 Noisy-le-Grand.

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL with foreign shareholders: (1) execute the statuts, appoint a gérant, and block the share capital (minimum capital optional; commonly EUR 1–1,000); (2) file the incorporation dossier through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, which broadcasts the file to the RCS/greffe, INSEE and DGFiP; (3) obtain the SIREN number (9 digits), SIRET (14 digits), extrait Kbis, and automatic ex officio VAT/corporate tax accounts; DGFiP assigns the intra-Community VAT identification number (format FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN), verifiable in VIES; (4) for a foreign entity without a French fixed establishment seeking direct French VAT registration, file an application with SIEE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères) together with foreign corporate registry extracts, identification forms, and supporting documents; SIEE issues the TVA intracommunautaire number normally within 2 to 4 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax — impôt sur les sociétés — at 25% headline rate, with a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit for eligible SMEs); Territorial_Scope applies (French corporate tax is limited to profits attributable to French operations — principle of territoriality); Pass_Through_Eligible exists only under narrow statutory options (e.g., Art. 239 bis A/A bis CGI for family-controlled SARLs, or the option for partnership-style income tax treatment under Art. 239 bis AA CGI); no general Tax_Exempt_Zone applies to this business segment in metropolitan France. — For a French SARL with foreign shareholders: (1) execute the statuts, appoint a gérant, and block the share capital (minimum capital optional; commonly EUR 1–1,000); (2) file the incorporation dossier through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, which broadcasts the file to the RCS/greffe, INSEE and DGFiP; (3) obtain the SIREN number (9 digits), SIRET (14 digits), extrait Kbis, and automatic ex officio VAT/corporate tax accounts; DGFiP assigns the intra-Community VAT identification number (format FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN), verifiable in VIES; (4) for a foreign entity without a French fixed establishment seeking direct French VAT registration, file an application with SIEE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères) together with foreign corporate registry extracts, identification forms, and supporting documents; SIEE issues the TVA intracommunautaire number normally within 2 to 4 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax — impôt sur les sociétés — at 25% headline rate, with a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit for eligible SMEs); Territorial_Scope applies (French corporate tax is limited to profits attributable to French operations — principle of territoriality); Pass_Through_Eligible exists only under narrow statutory options (e.g., Art. 239 bis A/A bis CGI for family-controlled SARLs, or the option for partnership-style income tax treatment under Art. 239 bis AA CGI); no general Tax_Exempt_Zone applies to this business segment in metropolitan France.

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections: (1) option for income tax treatment for SARLs held by a handful of individual shareholders (Art. 239 bis AA and 239 bis A CGI), revocable for a renewable 5-year period; (2) election of the corporate income tax regime for otherwise transparent vehicles; (3) French fiscal consolidation (intégration fiscale, Art. 223 A CGI) for groups holding at least 95% of a subsidiary; (4) micro-BIC / simplified real regime options for small taxpayers; (5) anti-hybrid classification is governed by EU ATAD rules as transposed by the French Finance Acts, so structurally transparent foreign instruments may be recharacterized for French tax purposes; tax advice should confirm that the SARL is not treated as a disregarded entity in any counterpart jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Established SARL: no mandatory turnover threshold — VAT registration is required from the first taxable transaction unless the franchise en base de TVA (Art. 293 B CGI) applies; 2023/2024 franchise thresholds: EUR 91,900 for predominantly goods transactions and EUR 36,800 for services (mail scanning/virtual office services fall under the services threshold); non-established EU suppliers: EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-selling threshold before French VAT registration (or OSS election); non-EU suppliers: zero threshold — mandatory direct registration (often with a VAT fiscal representative under Art. 289 A CGI) from the first taxable sale.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The virtual office / mail scanning activity is a B2B service. When supplied to a taxable business established outside France, the place of supply is the customer's establishment (Art. 259-1° CGI), placing the transaction outside the scope of French VAT (effective 0%); for intra-EU B2B supplies the customer applies reverse charge, and the French supplier must hold the customer's valid VAT number (VIES). Physical export of goods is zero-rated under Art. 262-I CGI only where a customs export declaration (DAU) and transport proof are retained. Caution: supplies to non-taxable private persons established outside the EU are not automatically out of scope; under Art. 259 B CGI they remain taxable in France unless a specific exception (not applicable to CMRA services) applies. Criteria to claim zero/out-of-scope treatment: contract naming the foreign customer, customer business registration/VAT evidence, performance evidence (scanning, mail forwarding logs), and bank-inward remittance proof.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / déclaration en douane) evidenced through the DELTA system, where physical goods are exported
  • Commercial contract and VAT invoice with zero-rated/out-of-scope mention and cross-border B2B clause
  • Client VAT identification extract for EU taxables (VIES validation) or foreign trade registry extract for non-EU taxables
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill for any physical dispatches of scanned mail or archives
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/bank statement evidencing payment from the foreign client account

France is implementing a full e-invoicing/e-reporting mandate (Loi de finances 2022, decree n° 2022-1299): from 1 September 2026 all VAT-registered businesses must be able to receive e-invoices and large/mid-size enterprises must issue structured e-invoices (Factur-X, UBL or CII) via the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP); from 1 September 2027 all businesses (including micro-entities) must issue e-invoices; e-reporting will capture cross-border B2B/B2C data and domestic B2C payment data in parallel; no continuous-transaction-control digital stamping (e.g., Italy/Spain style) exists today, but cash registers and billing software for consumer cash transactions must satisfy Art. 286 CGI quater anti-fraud certification (NF 525); B2G invoicing uses Chorus Pro.

DGFiP audit (vérification de comptabilité) places the burden of substantiation on the exporting supplier; failure to produce contemporaneous export/service evidence triggers reassessment of VAT at 20%, plus default interest (0.20% per month) and penalties of 40% (bad faith) or 80% (fraudulent maneuvers, Art. 1729 CGI), with a standard 3-year statute of limitations (Art. L169 LPF) extendable in hidden-activity or fraud scenarios; criminal tax fraud (Art. 1741 CGI) is punishable by up to EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment. Retroactive clawbacks will disallow previously zero-rated outputs and associated input VAT credits. France does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline, but the 180-day benchmark is retained here as prudent payment-trail discipline: delayed or unmatched inward remittances are a classic red flag for fictitious export claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a Eurozone member, maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (Article 63) and the EU Capital Movements Directive (Directive 88/361/EEC). No exchange control authorization is required for profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments, or trade settlements involving EUR and other freely convertible currencies. However, statistical reporting obligations apply to cross-border flows exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction under the French Foreign Exchange and Financial Relations Code. Profit remittance from a SARL is permitted upon satisfying corporate tax filing obligations and, for dividends, the approval of the shareholders' meeting; a tax clearance certificate is not statutorily required for dividend remittance but may be requested by the paying bank to meet compliance due diligence. Non-EUR transactions are subject to market pricing; no central bank intervention or specific conversion quota applies. Capital account operations such as direct investment, real estate acquisitions, and intellectual property transfers are freely liberalized, subject only to post-event reporting and anti-money laundering checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in France to repatriate or surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France. Exporters are free to hold foreign currency receipts in domestic or foreign bank accounts, provided such accounts are declared to the tax authorities and the cross-border movement of funds is reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes when the individual payment exceeds EUR 12,500. No mandatory conversion ratio applies; companies may retain EUR or convert to foreign currency as commercial prudence dictates. For invoicing in EUR, the settlement is cleared through TARGET2 or correspondent banking. Exporters of goods and services must maintain documentary evidence of the underlying commercial transaction for a period of at least six years to satisfy customs, tax, and AML verification requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with regulatory enforcement authority delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking and prudential supervision; foreign exchange and balance-of-payment reporting is administered jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor under EU capital movement liberalization rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) authorized under the ACPR passporting regime, such as Qonto, Shine, and Lydia, are viable for a SARL's operational banking needs, particularly for virtual office and mail scanning services where low-cost account management and online settlement are desirable. Non-EU offshore digital EMIs without an ACPR license or an EU/EEA passport are not viable for holding client funds or conducting regulated payment services in France; they may only serve as auxiliary software/platform providers without engaging in money transmission. A French SARL must maintain its primary operational bank account with a licensed credit institution or an ACPR-registered EMI. Onboarding viability with EMIs is rated High for day-to-day treasury activity, but the Capital Escrow Account must be opened with a fully regulated bank to ensure credibility with creditors and to avoid liquidity risk. EMIs are subject to safeguarding requirements, and their cross-border clearing is limited to electronic money and payment initiation services; they do not provide commercial lending or trade finance instruments.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership opacity: Nominee shareholders, complex multi-jurisdictional ownership chains, or inability to identify the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) under French AML law (Monetary and Financial Code Article L561-2-2).

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity: Nominee shareholders, complex multi-jurisdictional ownership chains, or inability to identify the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) under French AML law (Monetary and Financial Code Article L561-2-2).

Defense evidence: Provide a notarized and apostilled register of actual shareholders, a full organizational chart tracing funds and control through each layer, copies of passport/ID of all UBOs, a declaration from each UBO confirming no criminal record for financial offenses, and a legal opinion from a French avocat confirming the SARL's ownership structure is not designed to evade transparency. The SARL's Articles of Association must be annotated to identify individuals holding more than 25%.
Business-model red flags: Virtual office / mail scanning classified as 'commercial mail receiving agency' may be perceived as a conduit for shell companies, money laundering, or invoice fraud, given the medium-risk enhanced AML classification.

Root cause: Business-model red flags: Virtual office / mail scanning classified as 'commercial mail receiving agency' may be perceived as a conduit for shell companies, money laundering, or invoice fraud, given the medium-risk enhanced AML classification.

Defense evidence: Submit a detailed commercial contract with each client, proof of a physical service location (leased office or genuine business center), sample of standardized mail handling and scanning procedures, a privacy policy aligned with GDPR, identification of all client companies via KYC checks, prohibition of use for nominee/bank account opening, and a client acceptance policy that rejects high-risk industries. Maintain an audit trail of all mail, package logs, and scanned documents with timestamps.

Root cause: Source of funds ambiguity: Large or sudden capital injections from non-EU jurisdictions, especially in jurisdictions considered high-risk by FATF.

If funds derive from trading operations, provide underlying invoices and customs declarations; if from employment or sale of assets, provide pay slips or notarized sale contracts.
Transaction purpose misalignment: The declared activity being 'mail scanning' is not conclusively linked to the planned wire transfers or high-volume receipts from foreign clients.

Root cause: Transaction purpose misalignment: The declared activity being 'mail scanning' is not conclusively linked to the planned wire transfers or high-volume receipts from foreign clients.

Defense evidence: Present contracts with service recipients, a service-level agreement (SLA) specifying the volume of mail and digital scans, a tariff list for each service, expected monthly recurring revenue, an operational budget projecting cash flows, and a business plan demonstrating that the bank account activity will be commensurate with the declared commercial purpose. Provide client diversity to avoid concentration risk.
Tax or corporate compliance sanctions: Failure to register the SARL's beneficial owners with the Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs or pending tax disqualifications.

Root cause: Tax or corporate compliance sanctions: Failure to register the SARL's beneficial owners with the Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs or pending tax disqualifications.

Defense evidence: Produce the official registration confirmation from the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), the 'extrait Kbis' dated within three months, the current UBO declaration receipt, and a tax compliance certificate issued by the Direction Générale des Finances Publiques confirming no outstanding corporate tax arrears or fraudulent activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the French SARL's corporate account must be initiated via SWIFT wire transfers using the SARL's IBAN (FR76 format) and BIC code. The transfer must include a proper purpose code in accordance with Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting. For direct investment equity contributions, use purpose code 'F1010' (capital share subscription) or 'F1020' (additional paid-in capital); for shareholder loans, use 'S3010' (commercial credits, advances and deposits) or the relevant code for financial operations. The sending bank must receive a copy of the notarized share subscription agreement, corporate resolution, or shareholder loan agreement to release the funds. Inbound transfers must state the economic nature in the 'remittance information' field, such as 'capital increase FR_SARL' or 'shareholder loan'. The receiving French bank will verify the beneficial owner details and may request a certificate of tax residence from the remitting entity to ensure compliance with the EU Anti-Money Laundering Directive and the French Monetary and Financial Code. If the transfer exceeds EUR 12,500, the SARL must report the transaction to the Banque de France within 10 business days using the standard CERFA form or via the online portal.Outbound trade settlements must be executed through SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for direct payments to suppliers, or through a commercial letter of credit (documentary collection) issued by the French bank under ICC UCP600 rules for goods transactions. The SARL's virtual office business, being service-oriented, will primarily utilize MT103 transfers for recurring fees, subcontractor payments, and profit distributions. Each outbound payment must include the correct purpose code and be accompanied, when required, by underlying invoices or contracts. For payments to countries with exchange controls, the French bank will require a compliance review of the transaction against EU sanctions and embargo lists. Commercial letters of credit are permissible for trade settlement of physical goods if the SARL expands into billing and forwarding operations; in that case, the account must be designated as 'trade account' and the bank will issue the LC based on an underlying sale contract and proforma invoice.
