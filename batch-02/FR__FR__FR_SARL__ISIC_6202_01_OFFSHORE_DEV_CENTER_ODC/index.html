Formation / constitutional filings

Official online application for registration of a societe commerciale (legacy Cerfa M0 / demande d'immatriculation au RCS) accompanied by the signed statuts and all statutory annexes

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital; the SARL may be formed with freely determined capital, typically EUR 1. Under Article L.223-7 of the French Commercial Code, at least one-fifth of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation and the full subscribed cash capital must be deposited in a blocked bank account with an attestation de depot des fonds until RCS registration; funds are released to the company after registration.

Funding & unlock path: For leased professional office space, the landlord must provide the building fire-safety certificate and the SARL must maintain extinguishers, emergency exits, evacuation plans and workplace fire-prevention measures under the Labour Code and applicable ERP regulations; if the premises are open to the public, obtain the Commission de securite approval; no structural load certificate is required for ordinary office use.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — impots.gouv.fr; customs and cross-border goods formalities: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) — douane.gouv.fr; business registry and SIREN issuance: Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (INPI) — formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must register for French VAT and obtain a French TIN before making its first taxable supply in France. The application is submitted to the DGFiP through the online business formalities portal (Guichet Unique) or to the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) using form Cerfa n°10456*08 for the régime réel d'imposition. Non-EU/EEA-established foreign companies must appoint a tax representative in France accredited under Article 202 of the French General Tax Code (CGI). Upon approval, INSEE issues a SIREN identifier used as the French corporate TIN and a SIRET number for each establishment; VAT-specific intra-Community identifiers are then issued for cross-border transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must register for French VAT and obtain a French TIN before making its first taxable supply in France. The application is submitted to the DGFiP through the online business formalities portal (Guichet Unique) or to the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) using form Cerfa n°10456*08 for the régime réel d'imposition. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is by default subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the entity level. A small SARL may elect under Article 239 bis AA CGI to be taxed as a pass-through entity at the personal income tax (impôt sur le revenu, IR) level for a maximum of five fiscal years, provided all shareholders are natural persons and the share capital is fully paid up and held under specific ownership conditions; the election is made with the competent tax office and applies to all partners. France does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; however, hybrid entity mismatches are adjusted via the ATAD implementation rules and tax residence is determined by place of effective management and registered office.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For French-established entities: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for supplies of goods; EUR 37,500 annual turnover for supplies of services. There is no registration threshold for foreign-established entities: any taxable operation in France triggers a French VAT registration obligation from the first euro. The general EU distance-selling threshold of EUR 10,000 applies only to intra-EU distance sales of goods; cross-border B2B services are generally taxed at the customer's place of establishment, not by the French threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the European Union qualify for a 0% VAT (exonération) under Article 262-I CGI if the goods physically leave France/EU territory within the statutory time limits (normally within three months, extendable to six months with justification) and the exporter holds probative evidence of export. Intra-EU dispatches to a taxable person are zero-rated provided the purchaser's valid VAT identification number is disclosed and the goods are transported to another Member State. Offshore software engineering and maintenance services supplied to a non-French business customer are treated as supplied where the customer is established under Article 259-1 CGI; the French supplier therefore applies 0% French VAT by excluding the services from French scope, provided it obtains documentary evidence that the customer is a taxable person acting as such (valid EU VAT ID or official foreign tax residence certificate, contract showing business customer status) and that the services are not physically performed for a fixed French establishment of the customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / DAU) filed via DELTA/GUS with customs approval
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / transport document evidencing physical export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of foreign exchange collection / bank transfer credit advice)
  • Commercial invoice bearing the zero-rating reference and the customer's VAT ID
  • Proof of exit from France/EU (e.g., Exit Summary Declaration, transport manifest, or carrier certificate)

France is implementing mandatory structured B2B electronic invoicing under the 2024 reform. From 1 September 2026, all established taxable persons must be able to receive e-invoices, and large and medium-sized enterprises must issue e-invoices; from 1 September 2027, small and micro enterprises must also issue structured e-invoices. Invoicing and e-reporting data must be transmitted through an accredited Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the Public Invoicing Portal (PPF), using EN 16931-compliant formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII). There is no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control stamping, or pre-clearance API with the DGFiP for ordinary domestic invoices; however, customs export declarations must be filed electronically through the DGDDI's DELTA/GUS system. Cross-border B2B and B2C sales are subject to periodic e-reporting to the tax administration from the same implementation date.

Export zero-rating claims are rigorously audited by the DGFiP and DGDDI. If the requisite documentary evidence is missing or false, the French tax authority re-assesses the supply at the standard 20% French VAT, together with late-payment interest at 0.20% per month (CGI Art. 1731) and an additional penalty of 40% for serious tax misstatement or 80% for fraudulent behavior (CGI Art. 1729); an 80/50% penalty can apply for concealed operations. Criminal tax fraud penalties under Article 1741 CGI include fines up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Customs irregularities can trigger administrative fines and confiscation under Customs Code Articles 410 and 413. Refunds of input VAT are available when zero-rated exports are properly substantiated; unjustified refund claims trigger additional fines under Article 1788A CGI.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. France applies EU capital movement liberalization; no prior foreign-exchange authorization is required for current or capital account transactions. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders require no exchange-control permit, provided they are supported by approved financial statements and shareholder distributions; dividends remain subject to French withholding tax unless treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief applies. Banks must report transaction-level data to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics on payments above €50,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds in France. Exporters may hold EUR or foreign currency abroad and in French accounts; however, statistical balance-of-payments reporting to Banque de France applies to individual external transactions equal to or exceeding €50,000. No conversion ratios exist.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury); banking and payment supervision is delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High viability for EU-licensed electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Europe, Payoneer Europe) passporting into France under EEA e-money and payment-services legislation; low viability for offshore non-EU EMIs because they may not accept client deposits or operate without an EU branch/passport. Such platforms are recommended only for operational disbursements, not for statutory capital escrow or principal clearing relationships.

Supporting documents include: Kbis extract, shareholder meeting minutes, board resolution, capital subscription deed, and certified identity/UBO documents. Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box A / SIREN Entity identifiers: SIREN number, APE/NAF classification (6202A / NAICS 541512 equivalent), legal form (SARL), registered office address. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million (annual turnover or total balance sheet assets) triggers mandatory transfer pricing documentation in France: Local File and Master File must be immediately producible; country-by-country reporting is additionally required when the multinational group consolidated turnover equals or exceeds EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain a statutory Economic Substance Regulations return in the manner of offshore centres, but general law and anti-abuse jurisprudence require genuine economic substance. For an Offshore Development Center in a French SARL, substance is evidenced by locally based software engineers, payroll in France, premises physically occupied, local managerial decision-making, and commercial risk retention; artificial arrangements that strip profits to tax-favoured jurisdictions are recharacterised under Article L64 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales and OECD/ATAD principles.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: 40% tax increase for bad faith under Article 1729 CGI, increased to 80% for abusive fraud (manœuvres frauduleuses); late-payment interest at 4.8% per annum; criminal fraude fiscale under Article 1741 CGI carries a fine of up to EUR 50,000 and two years’ imprisonment, with aggravated circumstances extending to EUR 100,000 and five years’ imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the company’s tax debt where fraud or negligent mismanagement is proven; civil restorative orders, personal asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment can follow a criminal conviction.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Administrative recharacterisation under Article L64 LPF; penalty of 80% of the reassessed tax; reversal of artificial legal acts; suspension of the benefit of DTA relief; publication of decisions; additional interest to apply.

Director personal liability: Veil piercing is applied where the corporate personality is entirely artificial; directors may be personally liable for hidden taxes, restitution of diverted profits, judicial exclusion from management, and criminal conviction for misuse of corporate assets.
AML / Aggressive Payment Engineering

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the Code Monétaire et Financier up to EUR 1,000,000 for legal persons; criminal penalties of up to five years’ imprisonment and fines up to EUR 375,000 for individuals; Tracfin referral and freezing of corporate bank accounts; failure to report beneficial ownership triggers separate penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for AML non-compliance, including civil damages, corporate asset freezing, travel restrictions, ineligibility to manage commercial companies, and criminal imprisonment where deliberate concealment is established.
