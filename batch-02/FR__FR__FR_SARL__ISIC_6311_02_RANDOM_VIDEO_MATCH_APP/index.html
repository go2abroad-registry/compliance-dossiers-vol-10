Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL has no statutory minimum capital; the articles can fix any amount in euros with a minimum nominal share value of EUR 1. However, every cash contribution must be fully paid at incorporation and deposited into a blocked bank account (or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations) before filing. The blocked-funds certificate is released after registration and the Gérant may not withdraw funds prior to issuance of the Kbis.

Funding & unlock path: No mandatory fire-safety certificate applies to a pure digital company until a physical office is opened. If an office is used, the employer must perform an initial occupational health and safety inspection under Articles R.4227-1 et seq. of the Code du travail, install fire extinguishers, maintain an updated 'registre unique du personnel', and receive a non-ERP occupational occupancy document from the insurance company. If more than 300 persons are present or the office is open to the public, a fire-safety permit from the Commission de Sécurité (classified ERP) would be required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), the French national tax administration operating through impots.gouv.fr; company formation and initial tax identity are processed electronically by the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) under the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

Established SARL: registration of the company at the RCS via the Guichet Unique automatically generates a SIREN/SIRET; DGFiP then assigns the French intra-Community VAT number (FR + 9 digits) without any separate filing. Non-established foreign digital operator with French taxable supplies and no PE: application for direct VAT registration must be made to the DGFiP Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), including the designation of an accredited fiscal representative where required; alternatively, the EU/non-EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) regimes may cover EU B2C digital services without a French VAT registration. No payment of minimum capital or license is required for the SARL (CGI non-resident ownership permitted).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box system. The SARL is an opaque legal entity default-taxed at corporate level: profits are subject to corporation tax ('impôt sur les sociétés' – IS) under CGI art. 206, with territoriality under CGI art. 209 confining IS to French-sourced/PE-attributable operations. Statutory elections: (i) qualifying small SARLs (unlisted, at least 50% of capital held by individuals and fewer than 50 employees) may elect, under CGI art. 239 bis AA, to be taxed as a transparent partnership for a non-renewable five-year period; (ii) sociétés civiles and family companies may rely on art. 239 bis A transparency in specific cases; (iii) ATAD 2 anti-hybrid matching rules (CGI arts. 209 I quinquies and 212 bis) re-characterize hybrid transparent/opaque mismatches and deny deductions for structured arrangements, meaning a US-style check-the-box election cannot be imported into France.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover registration threshold for an SARL established in France: the company is taxable from its first euro of economic activity (CGI arts. 256 and 286). A small established business may, however, benefit from the franchise en base de TVA exemption if annual service turnover does not exceed approximately EUR 36,300 (tolerance up to EUR 40,300) or goods turnover approximately EUR 91,000 (tolerance up to EUR 101,000), indexed annually; this exemption is irrelevant for the digital app because the SARL is created for taxable platform activity and Apple IAP/Paymentwall volumes will exceed the thresholds. Non-established foreign suppliers of digital services are registrable from the first euro unless the OSS is used.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: exports of tangible goods outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under CGI art. 262 ter I provided the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days (12 months for justified specific movements) and the exporter holds contemporaneous export proof; intra-EU supplies to VAT-registered customers are exempt with right to deduct under art. 262 ter II, subject to reverse charge in the destination Member State. Digital services of the app: the SARL's supplies to B2B customers outside France (EU or non-EU) are outside French VAT by place-of-supply rule (CGI art. 259 A; art. 44 VAT Directive) and are invoiced without 20% VAT if two corroborating proofs of business establishment are retained (valid VAT ID, business address, contract, bank details). B2C supplies to EU consumers are taxed in the consumer's Member State and reported via the OSS portal of the DGFiP; B2C supplies to non-EU consumers are outside French VAT if customer-residence evidence (IP geolocation, billing country, payment records) is kept. Marketplaces/deemed supplier: when Apple App Store (Apple IAP) qualifies as an app-store operator, Apple is deemed to have received the SARL's digital service and to re-supply it to the consumer under VAT Directive art. 14a, so the SARL issues a zero-rated B2B invoice to Apple and Apple accounts for consumer VAT; Paymentwall acts solely as payment intermediary and does not change the place-of-supply analysis.

  • Commercial invoice and delivery/supply log, describing the goods or the digital service performed, the counterparty, the consideration, and citing the VAT exemption provision (CGI art. 262 ter I or II, or place-of-supply rule).
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill or Air Waybill with customs transit documents proving physical exit from the EU.

  • Digital service substantiation file: contract, IP-address geolocation logs, OSS/ViES validation records, Apple iTunes Connect payment reports and Paymentwall settlement statements identifying the customer/residence.

France imposes a full e-invoicing and e-reporting reform: as legislated, all B2B e-invoices for VAT-taxable persons established in France must be issued and transmitted digitally through certified Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs) or the public state platform (Portail Public de Facturation – PPF) with mandatory structured formats (Factur-X hybrid XML/PDF, UBL 2.1, or UN/CEFACT CII). Phased enforcement: 1 September 2026 for large/mid-market enterprises; 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises. The PPF operates a 'Y'-architecture data exchange with State slots and public APIs for direct submission, invoice acceptance/rejection, interruption/outage management and the mandatory audit trail; transaction and payment data for B2C (including digital app sales) are also mandatorily reported (e-reporting) at least monthly, no later than the 10th day of the following month, to the DGFiP through the same certified channels. French law does not use the 'digital stamp' clearance model; instead it combines pre-authorized certification, API-based transmission, and NF 525 certification for cash-register/billing software (applicable to any French billing tool used for the app's payment rails). Non-compliant invoicing is penalized under CGI arts. 1737 and 1737 ter.

French tax and customs auditors (vérification de comptabilité) may reassess incorrectly zero-rated exports within the standard three-year limitation period (LPF art. L. 174), extended to six years where the taxpayer has been guilty of fraud or serious tax offence. Retroactive clawbacks apply in full with intérêts de retard (CGI art. 1727) of 0.20% per month. Quantitative penalties: 40% for deliberate bad faith (mauvaise foi) and 80% for fraudulent maneuvers (manoeuvres frauduleuses) under CGI art. 1729; incomplete or false invoicing attracts separate fines under art. 1737. Criminal exposure: art. 1741 CGI treats fictitious exports/fake zero-rating as tax fraud, carrying a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and up to 5 years' imprisonment (increased to EUR 1,000,000 and 7 years for aggravated cases), plus confiscation, publication of the decision, and joint and several liability of any participant in the fraudulent chain under CGI art. 283(4).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible on both current account and capital account for French residents. Following the liberalisation of French exchange-control legislation (Decree 89-938 and the Finance Act 1989, subsequently embedded in the Code monétaire et financier, Articles L151-1 through L165-2, and in TFEU Article 63), a French SARL requires no prior exchange-control authorisation to receive foreign equity, to repatriate export-of-services proceeds, to pay foreign suppliers, or to distribute dividends to non-resident shareholders. Capital-account restrictions exist only in the targeted sectors listed by the decree on inward foreign investment (Defence, cybersecurity, critical infrastructure, space operations, chemical/biological/radiological/nuclear technologies); a real-time video-matching social app under ISIC 6311/NAICS 519130 is not a sensitive sector and is not subject to prior authorisation. Profit and dividend remittances to non-residents are legally free but carry practical hurdles: (i) the SARL's gérant must evidence a validly approved annual accounts and a shareholders' meeting decision; (ii) the distribution must respect the balance sheet solvency and legal-reserve rules of Code de commerce Articles L232-10 to L232-11; (iii) a dividend withholding tax applies to non-resident shareholders — 25% standard for a non-treaty resident corporate shareholder, 12.8% single income levy plus 17.2% social levies for natural persons when a tax-transparent regime applies, with rates reduced by double-taxation treaties (e.g., Bhutan, Hong Kong, US, Swiss, UK); and (iv) banking KYC expectations require traceability of the profit source, but there is no requirement to obtain an FX licence, a conversion permit, or a 'bona fide' certificate from the Banque de France.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. A French SARL may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in authorised foreign-currency accounts or convert them into EUR at market rates at any time; revenue from the video-matching app (Apple App Store and Paymentwall settlements) is classified as export of services for the purposes of the EU services trade and French statistics. The only statutory overlay is the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting regime: any French resident legal entity that executes a single transfer between France and a foreign country equal to or greater than EUR 7,500 — including export collections, import payments, capital subscriptions, and dividend flows — must declare the operation to the Banque de France under Regulation 91-07 and Directive of 5 September 1991 (Relations financières avec l'étranger, RFE), filed electronically through the TéléRFE portal within the prescribed monthly deadline, using the operation codes applicable to services (e.g., 'G2/Capi' for capital transactions; '1S' services for subscription-based app revenue). There is no requirement to convert proceeds, no minimum holding period, and no surrender certificate, and the EUR is the legal tender currency for the statutory accounts.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the national central bank within the Eurosystem/ESCB and as the sole administrator of French balance-of-payments (RFE) declarations; foreign-exchange and capital-movement policy is co-administered by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy and Finance); banking prudential supervision and AML oversight is exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution), an independent authority attached to the Banque de France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — acceptable as auxiliary rail, insufficient as the sole treasury anchor. French-regulated fintech EMIs (notably Qonto SAS, which holds an ACPR licence as an établissement de monnaie électronique and partners with French banking institutions; Shine as a subsidiary of Société Générale) are legally viable under Articles L521-1 et seq. of the Code monétaire et financier for day-to-day operational accounts, card issuance, and open-API bookkeeping, and they allow fully remote onboarding even for foreign directors under eIDAS-compliant video KYC. However, for a cross-border video-matching app rated Medium_Risk_Enhanced, the following constraints apply: (i) SARL incorporation capital must be deposited in a blocked account held by an ACPR-licensed French credit institution or by a French notary (Code de commerce Article L223-7); an unlicensed or passporting-only foreign EMI cannot hold this statutory escrow; (ii) Banque de France RFE declarations are the client's responsibility and require bank statements with clear counterparty traceability, which passporting offshore EMIs (e.g., Wise European entity in Belgium, Revolut Lithuania, Airwallex/Currencycloud) may not emit with full French correspondent transparency; (iii) ACPR guidance disallows reliance on non-regulated e-money for long-term treasury of enhanced-risk digital companies; and (iv) foreign digital EMI platforms generally do not provide commercial credit lines, trade instruments, or FX hedging ISDA execution. Recommended architecture: anchor settlement liquidity and capital escrow with a Tier-1 bank (BNP Paribas or Société Générale) and use one EU-licensed EMI in parallel for low-value collection sweeps and payroll cards.

There is no prior exchange-control approval; France has removed prior-authorisation screening for this ISIC 6311 digital-services activity. The bank will execute the transfer to a French IBAN on the TARGET2 rail; once the SARL is registered on the RCS (K-bis extract issued), the bank unblocks the funds into the operational current account. Compliance duties: (a) for any single leg exceeding EUR 7,500, the SARL or its bank (under the RFE delegation) must file the balance-of-payments declaration to the Banque de France via TéléRFE using operation code G2/Capi; (b) the shareholder must evidence source-of-funds at the provider bank; (c) use SWIFT gpi with a UETR for tamper-proof tracking across the correspondent corridor; (d) attach the certified board resolution of the foreign shareholder and the SARL's copy of the executed share-subscription deed; (e) permit the French bank's compliance desk to apply the '135-day' review of the capital source under Article L561-6; (f) do not rely on crypto-bridge rails or third-party omnibus accounts — only the SARL's own blocked IBAN is compliant.
