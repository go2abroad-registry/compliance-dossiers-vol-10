Name reservation / lock period

France does not maintain a statutory pre-incorporation name-reservation window. A proposed SARL name is cleared only at the time of article-of-association filing and RCS registration through the Guichet Unique. If the registrar rejects the name as misleading, contrary to public order, or conflicting with an existing RCS name or third-party trademarks, the applicant may (i) revise and resubmit the application immediately through the Guichet Unique; and (ii) file a written petition within 15 days from receipt of the refusal with the judge responsible for supervising the company registry (juge commis à la surveillance du registre) under Article L. 123-5-2 of the Code de commerce. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for SARL; capital is freely determined in the articles. Cash contributions must be subscribed in full, and at least 20% of each cash share must be paid up at incorporation (Article L. 223-7 C.com), with the balance payable within 5 years. No escrow account is required if capital is imposed; funds may be deposited with a bank or notary.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Commission de Sécurité (SDIS) may be required only if office premises are open to public (e.g., showroom), but for pure online office e-commerce, the workplace must obtain an attestation of conformity from the occupational health service (SMVT) confirming fire exits, emergency lighting, and first aid equipment. Structural load inspection is not required for standard office, but the registered office must include a workplace licence (autorisation d’exploitation) under the urban planning code (Code de l'urbanisme) if the commercial activity alters the premises’ use. Covid/occupational clearance is replaced by the general risk assessment (Document Unique) filed with the CSE. The manager must ensure workers receive basic first aid training and establish a workplace inspection register.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for SARL; capital is freely determined in the articles. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) / Online portal: impots.gouv.fr (business portal: www.impots.gouv.fr/accueil; e-invoicing platform: Chorus Pro)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity making taxable supplies in France must register with DGFiP for French value-added tax (TVA). Obtain a French SIREN/SIRET number from the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) if the entity has a permanent establishment; otherwise, register direct for a French VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) using the pre-registration portal of the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects or the EU VAT registration procedure. The application must identify the fiscal representative in France if no permanent establishment and the entity is outside the EU. Required documentation: certificate of incorporation, proof of EU/TIN registration, description of the business activity, and expected French taxable turnover. The French VAT number is normally issued within 2–6 weeks. For domestic SARLs, the TIN is the SIRET number and the VAT number is obtained automatically upon registration at the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident entity making taxable supplies in France must register with DGFiP for French value-added tax (TVA). Obtain a French SIREN/SIRET number from the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) if the entity has a permanent establishment; otherwise, register direct for a French VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) using the pre-registration portal of the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects or the EU VAT registration procedure. The application must identify the fiscal representative in France if no permanent establishment and the entity is outside the EU. Required documentation: certificate of incorporation, proof of EU/TIN registration, description of the business activity, and expected French taxable turnover. The French VAT number is normally issued within 2–6 weeks. For domestic SARLs, the TIN is the SIRET number and the VAT number is obtained automatically upon registration at the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS), qualifying as an entity-level taxpayer. A new SARL may elect, for a maximum period of five fiscal years, to be taxed as a partnership (régime des sociétés de personnes) under Article 239 bis A of the French General Tax Code. Subsequent irrevocable option for IS is possible. France also permits tax consolidation (régime de l'intégration fiscale) for groups holding at least 95% of the subsidiary. No 'check-the-box' election comparable to the US exists; hybrid mismatch rules under European Directive ATAD are implemented, including reverse hybrid classification rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General domestic VAT registration is required without threshold for foreign entities selling goods in France via e-commerce. For intra-Community distance sales of goods by EU-established suppliers, the applicable French distance-selling threshold is EUR 10,000 (annual aggregate across all EU supplies, triggering French registration if exceeded). A French-established small enterprise may benefit from the 'franchise en base' exemption for sales of goods up to EUR 91,900 (2025), reducing the need to charge VAT below that level; this exemption is not available to foreign-established entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods to a destination outside the European Union qualifies for French VAT at 0% if the export is substantiated by proof of removal from the EU within the statutory timeframe (generally 90 days from invoice; exports of goods supplied from France to non-EU customers in the normal course of trade are zero-rated absolutely). Transfer of goods to an EU member state that triggers intra-Community supply is generally exempt with the right to deduct input VAT, not zero-rated, but may be zero-rated under certain conditions. Services directly linked to exported goods (e.g., freight, insurance, loading) may be zero-rated. The customer must be outside France/EU and the goods must physically leave French customs territory; the VAT exemption is denied where the evidence does not establish a genuine external transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU – Document Administratif Unique)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR (International Consignment Note)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Supplier's Export Evidence (Broker's export certificate, customs manifest)
  • Bank Transfer / Payment Document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Proof of Receipt of payment from the non-EU counterparty

France mandates electronic invoicing for all transactions between taxable persons established in France, with a phased implementation from 1 September 2026 (all businesses must be able to receive e-invoices; issuance obligation begins 1 September 2026 for large/medium, 1 September 2027 for small). Invoices must be transmitted as structured electronic documents (Factur-X, PEPPOL, etc.) to the Chorus Pro platform for clearance against the DGFiP. E-reporting of transaction data is required for B2B and B2C cross-border supplies and payment transactions. Real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping are not required for all retail e-commerce; however, online marketplaces must report seller turnover and payments. Since 2023, France has required payment service providers to transmit electronic payment data to the Tax Administration for all cross-border and domestic retail sales, enabling indirect tax monitoring.

Export zero-rating is subject to retrospective audit by DGFiP. Where documentary evidence is false, incomplete, or where goods re-enter the EU or were diverted, the tax authority reassesses VAT at the standard or reduced rate, imposes a 100% fine of the evaded tax (Article 1729 CGI), plus 80% restitution interest, and may apply a 40% penalty for deliberate negligence. Criminal penalties may apply for customs declaration fraud. The foreign exchange repatriation obligation is enforced by customs law (monetary obligations) with non-compliance penalties up to 50% of the unrepatriated sum. Claims for refund of French VAT on export-related input taxes require strict compliance with Articles 271 and 242-0 L of the French tax code and may be rejected upon incomplete documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions; no exchange control authorization is required for EUR-denominated or cross-border payments. Profit remittance is free of exchange restrictions; the only impositions are corporate income tax on French-source profits (25% standard rate, 15% reduced rate for SMEs below thresholds) and dividend withholding tax (25% standard domestic rate, reduced by tax treaties, fully exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU for qualifying corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for 2 years). Repatriation of trade proceeds faces no Bank of France surrender or licensing hurdle; bank statements and MT103 records must be preserved for ACPR/Tracfin inspection.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio applies in France; exporters may hold export proceeds in foreign currency in authorized banks. Balance-of-payments statistical declarations to Banque de France are required for individual cross-border capital transactions (loan, equity, real estate) exceeding EUR 15 million; trade-related payments for goods and ordinary services remain below the reporting threshold and need no declaration. All wire transfers must carry complete remitter and beneficiary information under EU Regulation 2015/847 to support AML screening.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Central Bank); foreign exchange and financial surveillance co-supervised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) and the Direction Générale du Trésor (DGT) of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty. France applies full free movement of capital under Articles 63-66 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Checkout.com Fit: High

High feasibility with strict legal constraints. ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine, and Treezor, and EU-passported EMIs (e.g., SWIIP, Checkout.com) may open French IBANs for the SARL; they provide faster onboarding and are PSD2-compliant. Constraints include: (1) safeguarding of funds rather than bank deposit guarantee, (2) limited credit and FX-Hedging product availability, (3) mandatory ACPR authorization or European passport notification, and (4) e-money accounts cannot be used to benefit from the droit au compte guarantee of Banque de France. Offshore and non-EEA EMIs (e.g., UK-only FCA or US-chartered) have Low feasibility for primary treasury use; they may be used only as secondary collections channels after ACPR cross-border services authorization review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions into France are unrestricted inbound EUR transfers. The sole compliant routing is: (1) the shareholder wires via SWIFT MT103 from a verified bank account in their own name to the SARL's IBAN; (2) the field 70 of the MT103 must carry the purpose code 'S301' or 'APPORT CAPITAL' (or 'Apport en compte courant d'associé' for shareholder loans); (3) for capital increases exceeding EUR 15 million per transaction, the SARL must file a Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration within 20 days, including the French intermediary bank; (4) the beneficiary bank will apply enhanced due diligence on the originator bank; funds are credited only if the SARL's Kbis name matches the IBAN; (5) escrow rules for capital deposit apply if the SARL is not yet registered at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce: funds are blocked on a 'compte de dépôt' until issuance of the Certificate of Registration. Evidence to retain: signed share subscription resolutions, third-party bank reference, notarized certificate of deposit, RBE filing and the MT103 substantiating inflow path.Trade settlements may be executed through (1) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments to EU suppliers (max EUR 100,000 per transaction with instant SEPA; no limit on standard SCT), (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR payments (USD, CHF, JPY) via correspondent banks, (3) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 for high-value watch and jewelry imports (irrevocable, confirmed) to protect prepayment risk, and (4) documentary collections (D/P, D/A) for recurring supplier flows. Add: remittance information must include the vendor's legal name, invoice number and country; EU Regulation 2015/847 requires full originator and beneficiary data; for goods imports subject to customs, the DPE (Déclaration d'Echange de Biens) must be filed within the 10th working day of the month following the transaction; Stripe and PayPal EUR settlements are cleared via Standard SEPA/SEPA Instant to the SARL's main account on a daily or T+1 basis.Outright Forward FX (EUR/USD, EUR/CHF) – fully permitted; executed under the Convention-Cadre FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) master agreement or bank's ISDA-style undocumented spot/forward annex; requires credit line or margin of 2-5% of notional for a SARL; appropriate for USD-denominated watch procurement from Swiss/US distributors.Cross-Currency Swaps (EUR/USD) – permissible for dealers and non-financial counterparties under EMIR (EU Regulation 648/2012); requires a signed ISDA Master Agreement plus Credit Support Annex (CSA) for collateral; used only for hedges of long-term commercial contracts exceeding 12 months; transaction reporting to a registered trade repository is mandatory within one business day.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) – legally permissible in France but commercially irrelevant for EUR pairs because the euro is fully convertible; NDFs may be used only for settlement against non-convertible or lightly traded currencies (e.g., XOF, MAD, INR, CNY offshore CNH) to hedge invoice exposures; settlement is in EUR by net difference, subject to the same FBF master agreement and Banque de France derivatives statistical reporting.

ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine, and Treezor, and EU-passported EMIs (e.g., SWIIP, Checkout.com) may open French IBANs for the SARL; they provide faster onboarding and are PSD2-compliant. Constraints include: (1) safeguarding of funds rather than bank deposit guarantee, (2) limited credit and FX-Hedging product availability, (3) mandatory ACPR authorization or European passport notification, and (4) e-money accounts cannot be used to benefit from the droit au compte guarantee of Banque de France. Offshore and non-EEA EMIs (e.g., UK-only FCA or US-chartered) have Low feasibility for primary treasury use; they may be used only as secondary collections channels after ACPR cross-border services authorization review.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: French Local File and Master File documentation (dossier de prix de transfert) is legally mandatory where gross annual turnover or gross fixed assets reach €400,000,000 (art. L. 13 AA and L. 13 AB CGI). Country-by-Country reporting is mandatory where the consolidating group's annual consolidated revenue reaches €750,000,000. The current SARL, operating below the €400M threshold, is not obliged to file a full TP dossier but must retain contemporaneous arm's-length evidence as Art. 57 CGI empowers the tax administration to recharacterise and adjust any inter-company transaction.

Economic substance scope: France applies an anti-abuse and substance-over-form framework instead of a formal ESR regime: (i) art. L. 64 LPF abuse-of-law doctrine (80% penalty); (ii) the ATAD-inspired general anti-abuse rule and OECD Principal Purpose Test introduced into French domestic law; (iii) the 'place of effective management' residence test (art. 209 CGI) and the 'screen company' case law. The SARL must evidence genuine economic substance in France - a local registered office, an employed management team, board decisions made in France, physical inventory or warehouse arrangements, and real commercial activity - to sustain treaty protection, VAT rules, and non-application of the abuse-of-law penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Concealment (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI: up to €500,000 fine per count and 5 years' imprisonment (increased to €1,000,000 and 7 years for organized-gang fraud); compulsory publication of the condemnation; electronic and passport surveillance measures; automatic referral of the case to the financial prosecutor (PNF) for aggravated circumstances.

Director personal liability: The gérant can be criminally prosecuted as principal perpetrator; travel bans, passport withdrawal, and bank-account seizures are available under judicial control; the tax authorities may pierce the corporate veil for the fraud penalties and seek civil restitution against the manager for the whole evaded tax.
Abuse of Law / Artificial Shell Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Art. L. 64 LPF: 80% penalty on the recharacterized tax base plus monthly late interest; possible criminal referral for abuse of law aggravated by fraud; professional disqualification from commercial activity.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or approve the abusive scheme incur personal liability for the 80% penalty and interest; the commercial court (tribunal de commerce) may ban the manager from managing a company for a period of years; imprisonment up to 5 years applies if the scheme is also characterized as fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Hidden Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Reallocation of the full profit to the French SARL; 40% penalty for deliberate non-filing (manquement délibéré) and 80% for abuse of law; interest at 0.20% per month; possible 10-year limitation extension for undisclosed foreign transactions.

Director personal liability: The gérant who executed the overbilling is liable to civil restitution for the evaded IS, social contributions on fringe benefits and, in flagrant cases, criminal fines and imprisonment for money laundering (art. 324-1 Code pénal: 5 years and €375,000).
AML / Financial Circuit Defects (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: French Monetary and Financial Code L. 561 et seq. violations and Code pénal art. 324-1: corporate fines up to €375,000, confiscation of jewelry, turnover and import bans; the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel and Tracfin may block payment accounts and blacklist the SARL.

Director personal liability: Managers face 5 years' imprisonment, a €375,000 fine, professional prohibition, freezing orders, and travel bans while under investigation; civil liability for all proceeds laundered through the corporate accounts.
Failure to Declare Economic Substance and Non-Cooperative State Payments

Statutory sanction: Art. 238 A CGI: disallowance of deductions and 75% withholding on the payments, reclassification of the full sums as constructive dividends or profit distributions; administrative penalties up to 40-80%.

Director personal liability: Joint and several liability with the company; the director cannot rely on the corporate shield where the payment arrangement was deliberately designed; criminal prosecution for complicity in tax evasion exposes the director to a €1,000,000 fine and 7 years' imprisonment.
