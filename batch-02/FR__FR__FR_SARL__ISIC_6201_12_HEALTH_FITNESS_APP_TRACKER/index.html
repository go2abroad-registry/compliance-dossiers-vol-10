Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for an SARL. Share capital is freely fixed in the articles. Cash contributions must be fully paid up and deposited into a segregated escrow account with a French bank, or held by a notaire, until the company is registered; the depositary releases the funds only upon presentation of the Kbis.

Funding & unlock path: For a standard office or coworking suite, no prior civil-defense permit or structural-load inspection is required. If the building is open to the public, the relevant ERP authorization under the Fire Code must be verified with the local mairie.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official impots.gouv.fr portal. Business creation data are generally transmitted through the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI) Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL obtains its SIREN and SIRET identifiers through registration with the Registre national des entreprises via the INPI Guichet unique. The DGFiP then assigns or confirms the French tax identification details, including the intracommunity VAT number (FR followed by a two-digit control key and the nine-digit SIREN), after review of the registration file. A foreign entity without a French establishment requests tax registration from the competent non-resident tax service or relevant SIE, submitting constitutional documents, proof of business activity, identification of directors and beneficial owners, French representative details where required, and evidence of the French VAT nexus. VAT registration is separate from corporate income tax registration and may be required before taxable transactions commence.

The DGFiP then assigns or confirms the French tax identification details, including the intracommunity VAT number (FR followed by a two-digit control key and the nine-digit SIREN), after review of the registration file. A foreign entity without a French establishment requests tax registration from the competent non-resident tax service or relevant SIE, submitting constitutional documents, proof of business activity, identification of directors and beneficial owners, French representative details where required, and evidence of the French VAT nexus. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). An election for income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, IR) may be available under statutory conditions, including for certain family SARLs and qualifying small companies for a limited period. The election is not a general United States-style check-the-box regime. France does not provide a general elective hybrid-mismatch classification mechanism for SARLs; anti-hybrid rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the domestic French VAT franchise regime, the principal annual turnover threshold is EUR 37,500 for services, including most electronically supplied or digitally delivered services when France is the place of taxation. The enhanced threshold is EUR 41,250. The goods threshold is EUR 85,000, with an enhanced threshold of EUR 93,500. These are exemption thresholds, not a universal VAT nexus rule. Cross-border digital services can require registration notwithstanding domestic franchise thresholds. For EU business-to-consumer electronically supplied services, the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold and the One Stop Shop regime may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be supplied without French VAT if the supplier can prove export and exit from the EU customs territory, normally through electronic customs exit certification or equivalent legally accepted evidence. Intra-EU supplies of goods are generally VAT-exempt with a right of deduction when the customer has a valid VAT number and the transport and reporting conditions are satisfied; this is technically an exemption rather than domestic zero-rating. Services supplied to a business customer established outside France are generally taxed at the customer's place of establishment under the B2B place-of-supply rule and are invoiced without French VAT, subject to customer-status and evidence requirements. Digitally supplied B2C services are generally taxed where the consumer resides, potentially through OSS or a non-Union registration. VAT input refunds require valid invoices, a direct business purpose, proper accounting, and evidence that the related transactions qualify for deduction.

  • Export customs declaration accepted through the French/EU customs system
  • Electronic proof of exit from the European Union customs territory, such as an exit result or equivalent customs certification
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, values, VAT treatment, and applicable customs information
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or carrier delivery record
  • Packing list and relevant export licence or regulatory certificate where applicable
  • For intra-EU supplies: customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required recapitulative statement documentation
  • For offshore services: customer business-status evidence, contractual documentation, service delivery records, and evidence supporting the customer's establishment outside France
  • Accounting records and VAT return schedules supporting the zero-rated or outside-scope treatment

France does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT clearance system. The mandated model uses approved private Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs), with the public invoicing portal serving as the central data-routing and reporting infrastructure. All French businesses must be capable of receiving electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Issuance of domestic B2B electronic invoices is scheduled from 1 September 2026 for large and medium-sized enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro-enterprises. Electronic reporting applies to relevant cross-border B2B and B2C transactions and certain domestic payment data. Invoices must contain mandatory French and EU VAT information, use an approved structured or hybrid format, and be transmitted through the prescribed platform infrastructure.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline applicable to export VAT relief. The taxpayer must nevertheless retain reliable proof of export, customer status, transport, contractual performance, and accounting treatment for the applicable record-retention period. Unsupported or fraudulent export treatment may result in reassessment of VAT, recovery of wrongly deducted input VAT, late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month, and tax penalties commonly ranging from 10% for non-compliance to 40% for deliberate conduct and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, subject to statutory conditions. Customs violations may trigger separate duties, fines, seizure, or other customs sanctions. Serious intentional tax fraud may also lead to criminal prosecution, including imprisonment and substantial fines under the French Tax Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full capital-account and current-account convertibility under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No prior FX licence or exchange-control approval is required for subscription of SARL capital, profit remittance, dividend distribution, royalty payment, or liquidation proceeds transfer by non-resident shareholders. Practical restrictions are not FX controls but bank compliance, TRACFIN suspicious-transaction reporting, and mandatory withholding-tax documentation where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or a bilateral tax treaty is invoked. Funds may be maintained in EUR or any freely convertible currency, and no special non-resident account category is imposed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, forced conversion, or fixed repatriation deadline exists for French export proceeds. A French SARL may collect export receivables in the currency of the invoice and retain them in a normal foreign-currency bank account. The only obligations are statistical reporting of cross-border transactions to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments purposes when above de minimis thresholds and, where an export of digital-health services is zero-rated for VAT, maintenance of commercial evidence of the recipient's EU or non-EU status.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (central bank and balance-of-payments authority), acting with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for bank licensing and the Direction Générale du Trésor for exchange-control and foreign-investment matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High for operational EUR and SEPA flows when the EMI holds a valid EU electronic-money institution or credit-institution passport under PSD2/EMD2 and is registered with ACPR. High for low-cost remote onboarding, but an EMI cannot lawfully grant credit, offer overdrafts, or provide the same depositor protection as a bank; therefore it is not recommended as the sole escrow bank for SARL registered capital. Non-EU or non-passported offshore EMIs are non-viable and should not receive corporate treasury funds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulation regime comparable to the UAE. However, the abuse of law doctrine (abus de droit),, the 'weak link' doctrine (maillon faible), EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, and CFC rules collectively require the French SARL to demonstrate real substance: employees, premises, decision-making, and risk-bearing capacity. Artificial arrangements lacking substance are recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Administrative 80% increase of tax evaded plus 0.20% monthly interest. Criminal penalties: fine up to EUR 1,500,000 for legal entities or 50% of evaded tax, and imprisonment up to 7 years for individuals.
