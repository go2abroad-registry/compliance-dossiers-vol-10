Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; online tax portal: impots.gouv.fr, including the professional taxpayer portal espace professionnel. Corporate formation and enterprise identification are coordinated through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), with SIREN and SIRET numbers issued through the national business register and VAT identification administered by the DGFiP.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS obtains its SIREN and SIRET identifiers by filing incorporation and beneficial-owner information through the INPI Guichet unique. The formation filing is transmitted to the competent tax service, which registers the company for corporate income tax and VAT according to its declared activities and tax regime. The French VAT identification number generally consists of the country prefix FR, a two-character control key, and the nine-digit SIREN. The company must activate an espace professionnel account, file the applicable VAT registration and election forms, and provide its articles of association, Kbis or registration evidence, business address, directors, beneficial owners, bank details, activity description, expected turnover, and intra-Community transaction profile when requested. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in France must register with the competent DGFiP tax service, commonly the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE), unless the transaction is fully subject to French reverse charge or another statutory simplification. An EU-established entity generally does not need a French fiscal representative; a non-EU-established entity generally must appoint an accredited French fiscal representative unless an applicable reverse-charge or treaty-based exception applies. VAT returns are filed electronically, normally through the professional tax portal or an approved tax-accounting intermediary.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is normally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A qualifying SAS that is newly formed, unlisted, not controlled by a listed company, below applicable employee and turnover or balance-sheet limits, and meeting ownership conditions may elect temporarily for partnership-style taxation under the French income tax regime for a maximum period generally limited to five financial years. The election must be made within the statutory deadline and is subject to withdrawal and eligibility rules. There is no general United States-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework and generally deny deductions or impose inclusion where applicable; they do not provide a routine elective hybrid classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general French VAT registration threshold for a non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in France, and a French SAS normally registers from commencement where it carries out taxable activities. The domestic franchise en base de TVA exemption may apply to eligible small businesses below statutory turnover limits, subject to the applicable annual and prior-year thresholds and sector-specific rules; the principal historical thresholds are EUR 85,000 for goods and accommodation activities and EUR 37,500 for services, with higher tolerance thresholds and legislative changes potentially applicable. These franchise thresholds do not generally eliminate registration for imports, intra-Community transactions, or transactions requiring a VAT identification number. For qualifying intra-EU cross-border B2C distance sales and certain electronically supplied services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold is relevant to destination-country VAT and OSS use.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT under the French General Tax Code, principally subject to proof of export and exit from the EU customs territory. The supplier must retain a compliant commercial invoice, customs export declaration, export reference or MRN, transport evidence, and reliable proof that the goods left the EU. Goods supplied to an export intermediary may qualify only where the statutory intermediary and documentation conditions are satisfied. Intra-Community supplies of goods are generally VAT-exempt rather than exports and require a valid customer VAT number, transport to another EU Member State, and recapitulative reporting. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore. Their place of supply must be determined under French and EU VAT place-of-supply rules; B2B services supplied to a non-French business may generally be outside the scope of French VAT, while specific services involving French immovable property, events, transport, passenger services, or other special categories follow dedicated rules. Input VAT connected with exempt exports may generally remain recoverable where the statutory conditions are met.

  • Commercial export invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, Incoterms where relevant, and the VAT exemption reference.
  • Electronic customs export declaration submitted through the applicable EU and French customs system, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN).
  • Customs confirmation of exit or other legally accepted evidence that the goods exited the European Union customs territory.
  • Packing list, transport document, and carrier or freight-forwarder evidence, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent transport record.
  • Purchase order, sales contract, delivery evidence, and warehouse or logistics records linking the goods to the export transaction.
  • Proof of receipt or delivery outside the EU where required to corroborate the customs evidence.
  • Bank payment records or other commercial settlement evidence supporting the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory requirement for export VAT exemption.
  • For intra-Community supplies rather than third-country exports: customer VAT identification validation, transport evidence, and required EC Sales List or recapitulative statement data.

French domestic electronic invoicing reform applies progressively to transactions between French-established VAT-taxable businesses. All businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; large and intermediate businesses must issue them from 1 September 2026, while small and medium-sized businesses generally must issue them from 1 September 2027, subject to final implementation rules and any statutory adjustments. Invoices must generally pass through an approved private platform or the public invoicing portal framework, with structured data transmitted to the tax administration. France does not currently operate a universal real-time pre-clearance system for every invoice. E-reporting of transaction and payment data is required for specified non-domestic and B2C transactions under the phased reform. Electronic VAT return filing and payment are mandatory for businesses within the relevant regimes. Import VAT reporting may be performed through the VAT return for eligible VAT-registered businesses, while customs declarations continue through the French and EU customs electronic systems.

France has no general foreign-exchange control or statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days. Export proceeds should nevertheless be traceable through normal accounting, banking, customs, and anti-money-laundering records. If export VAT exemption is unsupported, the DGFiP may reassess French VAT, deny or claw back input VAT recovery, charge late-payment interest generally at 0.20% per month, and impose tax penalties. The principal penalty may be 40% for deliberate failure or deliberate inaccuracy and up to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards. False, fabricated, or materially misleading customs or tax documentation may also trigger customs sanctions, administrative fines, and potential criminal liability. Recovery of improperly refunded or credited VAT may extend to the relevant reassessment period, with additional penalties where fraud, concealment, or repeated non-compliance is established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility under Articles 63–66 TFEU and the French Code monétaire et financier; no prior authorization, exchange-control licence, or central bank approval is required for equity capital injection, trade settlements, or dividend remittances. The euro is the sole legal tender; EUR/USD, EUR/CNH, and other foreign-currency receipts may be retained indefinitely in authorized foreign-currency accounts (comptes en devises) without mandatory conversion. Repatriation of profits is unrestricted, but a 25% French withholding tax applies on dividends paid to non-French corporate shareholders under Article 119 bis-2 of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI), unless exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Article 119 ter CGI; minimum 10% shareholding held for at least two years) or reduced under an applicable double-tax treaty (commonly 5%–15%). Statistical reporting obligations apply: any single cross-border transaction equal to or exceeding EUR 50,000, or monthly cumulative flows with non-residents equal to or exceeding EUR 150,000, must be reported to Banque de France via the TeleBOP/e-BOP platform.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, forced repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirement exists under French law. Export proceeds from Active Noise Cancelling Headphones and Smart Audio Speakers (ISIC 2640; HS 8518.30 / 8518.62) may be retained in foreign currency abroad or in domestic foreign-currency accounts without time limit. The sole compliance duties are: (i) monthly BOP statistical declaration to Banque de France when the EUR 50,000 per-transaction or EUR 150,000 monthly aggregation threshold is crossed; and (ii) customs/treasury declaration of any physical transport of cash or bearer negotiable instruments equal to or exceeding EUR 10,000 (Article L152-1 Code monétaire et financier). No export licensing or dual-use authorization applies to standard consumer audio equipment (ECCN EAR99 / 5A992 classification).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), functioning within the Eurosystem under European Central Bank monetary policy; prudential licensing and AML/CFT supervision delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); foreign-exchange and capital-movement policy administered jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique. Banque de France acts as the mandatory repository for all Balance of Payments (BOP) statistical declarations by French-resident corporations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium – Lawful only for EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EME) or Payment Institutions (EP) passported into France under PSD2 and registered with the ACPR Regafi register. An offshore non-EEA EMI (e.g., Singapore, UAE, or US-based) may not lawfully open or maintain a payment account for a French-domiciled SAS without obtaining a full ACPR licence; therefore only EU/EEA-licensed platforms (e.g., licensed in Luxembourg or Lithuania) are viable. EMIs are operationally feasible for platform settlement collections (Amazon Marketplace), supplier disbursements, and multi-currency virtual accounts, but cannot substitute a tier-1 credit institution for capital escrow accounts, documentary letters of credit, overdraft facilities, or fiduciary services. Client fund safeguarding and segregation requirements apply; a hybrid treasury model (tier-1 bank + EEA EMI) is recommended for the ANC hardware supply chain.
