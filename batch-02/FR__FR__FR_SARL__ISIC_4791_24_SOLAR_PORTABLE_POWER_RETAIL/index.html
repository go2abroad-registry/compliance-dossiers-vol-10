Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Paris) / Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) through the single electronic window formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Articles L.210-1, L.223-1 to L.223-43, and L.223-5 (SARL capital payment rules); implementing rules under Décret n° 2023-431 (electronic formalities).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) filled in French law form 'M0' (déclaration de constitution de société); registration produces the official Kbis extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for SARL; if share capital is fixed, at least one-fifth (20%) of the cash capital must be paid up at the time of incorporation and blocked with a French credit institution until registration and the issuance of the capital deposit certificate.

Funding & unlock path: No civil-defense permit is required for non-manufacturing e-commerce. If the company operates an office or incidental warehouse, fire safety certification for ERP (établissement recevant du personnel) is required under Articles R.123-1 et seq. of the Code de la construction et de l’habitation; electrical installations must comply with NF-C 15-100 standards, and the company must register with DREETS for occupational health and provide basic workplace safety clearance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for SARL; if share capital is fixed, at least one-fifth (20%) of the cash capital must be paid up at the time of incorporation and blocked with a French credit institution until registration and the issuance of the capital deposit certificate.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
FIRST BOARD RESOLUTION — FRANCE SARL [E-COMMERCE ENTITY]. The undersigned founder, acting as sole shareholder of FR SARL, hereby resolves that: (1) the share capital of EUR 1,500 is duly subscribed, fully paid into the company’s designated bank account, and allocated as shares to the sole shareholder; (2) the registered office is adopted at 128 Rue de la Convention, 75015 Paris, with jurisdiction of the Paris Commercial Court; (3) the Gérant (Manager) is appointed as legal representative with authority to open bank accounts, execute contracts, and represent the company; (4) the company’s object includes online retail and wholesale distribution of portable solar generators and photovoltaic accessories, ISIC 4791; and (5) the Manager shall complete all registrations, licenses, and tax formalities. Adopted unanimously.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable – no industrial or manufacturing permit is required for an ISIC 4791 online direct retail business without on-site production.

Category: No specific operating licence; however, general consumer protection and e-commerce duties under Articles L.111-1, L.121-16 and L.511-1 of the French Consumer Code apply, together with the legal warranty and information obligations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and, for non-resident businesses, the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE); official portal: impots.gouv.fr. Company formation and registration filings are submitted through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), which transmits data to the tax administration. The company receives a SIREN and establishment-level SIRET; a French VAT identification number is issued by the DGFiP.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the SARL and file the formation formalities through the INPI Guichet unique. 2. Obtain the SIREN/SIRET and identify the competent SIE from the registration record. 3. Submit the VAT liability and tax-option information through the company formation filing or directly to the competent SIE using the required business tax registration forms. 4. Provide the articles of association, Kbis or registration extract, registered office evidence, bank details, director identification, beneficial-owner information, business activity description, projected turnover, and evidence of taxable transactions where requested. 5. The DGFiP assigns the French VAT number in the format FR plus two control characters and the nine-digit SIREN. 6. Validate the VAT number through the European Commission VIES system for intra-Community transactions. 7. Register separately for an EORI number through French customs before importing or exporting goods. A foreign company without a French establishment applies through the SIEE or the competent non-resident tax channel. Businesses established in another EU Member State generally do not need a fiscal representative in France; businesses established outside the EU generally must appoint an accredited French fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance exception or special regime applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS) on its taxable profits. The standard territorial rules apply, including taxation of French business activity, French permanent establishments, and specified French-source income. A qualifying small company may benefit from the reduced corporate income tax rate on a specified first tranche of profit, subject to ownership, turnover, and capital-payment conditions.

Available election forms

There is no general US-style check-the-box election. A SARL may elect corporate income tax where it is otherwise eligible for income-tax treatment, and a qualifying SARL may elect temporary income-tax transparency under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code, generally for up to five financial years and subject to statutory conditions concerning age, size, ownership, voting rights, activity, and capital holding. A family SARL may elect income-tax treatment under the family-company regime, subject to qualifying family ownership and activity conditions. These elections are formal statutory options, not unrestricted entity-classification elections. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion where arrangements produce deduction-without-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established retailer supplying goods domestically, the ordinary franchise en base threshold is generally EUR 85,000 of prior-year turnover, with a tolerance threshold generally EUR 93,500 for the current year, subject to the applicable statutory period and legislative changes. A business exceeding the relevant threshold, opting for VAT, making transactions that are not eligible for the franchise, importing goods, or undertaking certain intra-Community and cross-border transactions may require VAT registration irrespective of the domestic small-business threshold. For intra-Community distance sales of goods and certain electronically supplied services to EU consumers, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies, after which French or OSS reporting may be required. The 20% standard rate applies to most portable power generators, batteries, and solar panels sold in France, subject to product-specific reduced-rate rules that must be separately verified.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT where the supplier, carrier, customs representative, or buyer completes the export formalities and the supplier retains evidence that the goods actually exited the EU customs territory. The supplier must establish the link between the invoice, the goods, the customs declaration, and the exit confirmation. Intra-Community supplies to a VAT-identified customer in another EU Member State may qualify for the French VAT exemption where the customer has a valid VAT number, the goods are dispatched to another Member State, and the supplier satisfies documentary, reporting, and recapitulative-statement requirements. Services supplied to a business customer established outside France are generally governed by the place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of French VAT rather than zero-rated; service qualification, customer status, and use-and-enjoyment rules must be documented. Export VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices, proof of VAT payment, business use, and compliance with the applicable refund or VAT-return procedure.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the seller, buyer, goods, values, Incoterms, and VAT treatment
  • Electronic customs export declaration containing the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Export acceptance and exit confirmation from the EU customs system, including the electronic proof of exit or equivalent customs certification
  • Packing list and transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or road consignment note
  • Evidence linking the goods and transport to the VAT invoice, including serial numbers or shipment references where commercially relevant
  • Proof of the purchaser and destination outside the EU, where required by the transaction and risk profile
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required recapitulative statement
  • Bank payment evidence or commercial settlement records where relevant to substantiate the transaction; a French inward-remittance certificate is not a general statutory requirement for export VAT exemption

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and transaction e-reporting through accredited Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs), with a public directory and designated data-routing infrastructure. The current statutory timetable provides that all businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance obligations generally begin on 1 September 2026 for large and medium-sized enterprises and on 1 September 2027 for small and micro-enterprises, subject to legislative or regulatory amendments. Domestic B2B invoices within the scope of the mandate must use structured or hybrid electronic formats and contain the required transaction, VAT, identification, payment, and status data. France does not currently operate a universal real-time pre-clearance model for all invoices. Transaction e-reporting applies principally to B2C and cross-border transactions outside the domestic B2B e-invoicing flow. B2C e-commerce invoices are not generally subject to mandatory issuance in every retail transaction unless required by the customer or applicable sector rules. Customs declarations and import/export records are filed electronically through the French and EU customs systems.

France has no general foreign-exchange control rule requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. Export proceeds should nevertheless be traceable through ordinary accounting, banking, customs, and anti-money-laundering records. If an export exemption or zero-rating claim is unsupported, the DGFiP may reassess the VAT that should have been charged, deny input VAT recovery connected with the transaction, and impose late-payment interest generally calculated at the statutory monthly rate, currently 0.20% per month, subject to applicable rules. Additional penalties may include 10% for certain late or deficient filings, 40% for deliberate non-compliance, and up to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights. Fraudulent or deliberately false customs or tax documentation may also trigger customs sanctions, criminal tax-fraud exposure, director or officer liability, and loss of simplified customs or VAT privileges. The taxpayer should retain export and transport evidence for the applicable French accounting and tax limitation periods, commonly at least three years for VAT assessment purposes and longer where accounting or customs retention rules apply.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France adheres to the Eurozone monetary framework with unrestricted capital and current account convertibility. All profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriations are permitted without prior approval or surrender requirements. No capital controls apply to resident French entities under EU directives.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds is required under EU regulations. Export receipts denominated in EUR may be retained in full. Non-EU exports require standard customs declaration and statistical reporting but impose no conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited feasibility for cross-border electronic money institution activities. French ACPR licensing is mandatory for any EMI providing payment services; offshore digital EMIs are not permitted without specific authorization and full compliance with PSD2 and open banking rules.
