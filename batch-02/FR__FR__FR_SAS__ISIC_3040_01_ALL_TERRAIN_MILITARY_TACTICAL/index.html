Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS (Art. L.227-2 Code de commerce); capital is freely set in the statuts, must be fully subscribed by the shareholders, and the paid-in portion must be deposited in a dedicated blocked account held by a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations pending registration, with the bank's 'certificat de dépôt des fonds' or the notary's attestation attached to the filing. For defense procurement and DGA vetting, a paid capital in the order of EUR 500,000 or more is commercially expected, although the law imposes no floor.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); online corporate portal at impots.gouv.fr; local VAT registration desks are the Services des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); customs and EORI matters are administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent or non-resident shareholder forms the French SAS and registers it at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce through the formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr single electronic window, obtaining the SIREN and SIRET numbers. The SIE then allocates the intra-Community French VAT identification number and, for employers, the URSSAF registration. For a foreign entity, including a non-resident SAS without a French fixed establishment, making its first taxable supply, a direct VAT registration is filed under Article 289A CGI with the non-resident SIE; EU-established sellers may file directly, while non-EU sellers must appoint a tax representative approved by the DGFiP. The exporter also requires an EORI number beginning with 'FR' for customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A French SAS is a commercial company with full legal personality and is, by default, subject to the standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the entity level under Articles 206(1) and 219 CGI. It is tax-resident in France because it is incorporated and managed in France; no check-the-box pass-through status is available under French tax law.

Available election forms

The main available elections are (i) group tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Articles 223 A to 223 U CGI where the French parent holds at least 95% of the SAS; (ii) the régime mère-fille dividend exemption under Article 145 CGI if the SAS holds at least 10% of the paying subsidiary; (iii) a French VAT group election for closely-linked taxable persons; and (iv) standard loss carryforward and depreciation elections. There is no affirmative hybrid-mismatch 'check-the-box' election; hybrid mismatches are neutralised automatically under Article 212 bis CGI and are documented for the annual tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mainland France: EUR 85,000 for principal supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for principal supplies of services under Article 293B CGI, with periodic DGFiP revalorisation. A taxable person established outside France has no quantitative threshold: registration is required from the first taxable transaction carried out in France, subject to applicable reverse-charge or distance-selling exceptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from mainland France to destinations outside the European Union are eligible for the 0% VAT rate under Article 262(I) CGI if (i) the seller is a VAT-registered French taxable person, (ii) the goods are physically transported out of the EU under a customs export declaration, and (iii) documentary proof of export and receipt is obtained within the statutory period. Supplies of services physically performed or enjoyed outside the EU may also be zero-rated under Article 259C CGI where the B2B place-of-supply rules allocate the service outside France. Defense tactical vehicles require a prior French export authorisation issued by the Ministry for the Armed Forces under the Code de la défense, but that authorisation does not replace the VAT conditions.

  • Validated customs export declaration from the DELTA/DAC environment (Déclaration d'Exportation / SAD)
  • EORI certificate and export licence issued under Article L2335-3 Code de la défense for military tactical hardware
  • Original transport document proving physical movement to the non-EU consignee (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR)
  • Commercial invoice containing the export VAT code and zero-rating basis
  • Packing list and certificate of origin supporting customs valuation and classification
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank transfer proof evidencing payment by the non-resident buyer

France mandates structured e-invoicing for B2B and B2C transactions and e-reporting via certified dematerialisation platforms. From 1 September 2026, large and mid-size businesses, and from 1 September 2027, all businesses must issue e-invoices using Factur-X or an equivalent PEPPOL-compatible structure. The Public Invoicing Portal (PPF) carries out near-real-time tax clearance and transaction-data audit; no paper fiscal stamp is required, but every invoice receives a unique technical identifier and must be transmitted to DGFiP through a registered PDP or the PPF. Foreign suppliers without a French establishment must operate through a registered PDP or a French tax representative for e-reporting.

DGFiP and the DGDDI jointly audit export zero-rating claims. The zero rate is provisional until the exporter has supplied full documentary evidence; on failure, VAT is clawed back with French late-payment interest (intérêt de retard). Penalties under Article 1729 CGI are 40% for deliberate inaccuracy or omission and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvre; bad-faith cases may extend the ordinary 3-year limitation period to 6 years, and criminal tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI may apply. For defence goods, an unauthorised export triggers customs smuggling sanctions and suspension or cancellation of the export authorisation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. France maintains no exchange-control restrictions on cross-border payments within the EU/EEA. For non-EU counterparties, capital movements are theoretically free, but transactions related to defence equipment, armored vehicles, and military tactical hardware are subject to restrictive licensing, prior authorisation for inward direct investment in sensitive defence sectors, and comprehensive EU sanctions screening. Profit remittances are permitted without a special FX licence, but dividends must satisfy French corporate tax rules, documentary evidence of distributable reserves, and, if applicable, the parent-subsidiary directive requirements to reduce or avoid withholding tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in France. Export proceeds may be retained in euro or foreign currency accounts, subject to Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting for any cross-border transaction equal to or exceeding EUR 50,000, submitted through an authorised intermediary bank within the monthly reporting cycle. Non-compliance with reporting obligations may result in administrative penalties and heightened AML scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the national central bank and French representative of the European Central Bank (ECB); foreign exchange policy is primarily harmonised at EU level under the eurozone framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for non-French licensed digital EMIs, and Medium only for French-licensed or EU-passported electronic money institutions that offer corporate IBANs. Legal constraints: a French SAS may lawfully hold funds with an EMI, but cross-border digital EMIs without a licensed French or EU establishment are generally unsuitable for high-risk defence-sector treasury operations because they cannot provide the full documentary trail, defence-trade due diligence, and sanctions-screening infrastructure required by the AML/CTF framework. Most mainstream fintechs also refuse businesses operating under ISIC 3040/NAICS 336992 due to their internal weapons and military-application exclusion policies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection uses the SWIFT network with ISO 20022 message types (pain.001 customer credit transfer initiated through the remitting bank; MT103 for correspondent advice). The transfer must state an appropriate purpose code consistent with an equity capital contribution or shareholder loan, such as 'Capital – participation in an enterprise' under the ECB Balance of Payments classification. For non-EU origins, the receiving French bank will require a copy of the share subscription agreement, the SAS afkk/board resolution approving the capital increase, proof of deposit into a blocked capital account if statutory capital is being formed, and the not-arrears/declaration from the notary where applicable. The bank will file the mandatory Banque de France external position/reporting declaration if the amount reaches EUR 50,000 or more.
