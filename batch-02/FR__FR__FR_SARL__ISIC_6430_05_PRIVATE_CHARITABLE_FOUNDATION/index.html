Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – impots.gouv.fr; centralized business formalities portal at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SARL must register via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), selecting the 'société' filing and uploading the articles of association and identity documents. DGFiP automatically issues SIREN/SIRET registration and allocates the French intra-community VAT number. A non-resident entity without a French establishment that makes taxable supplies must file a direct VAT application through the dedicated DGFiP non-resident procedure; a provisional tax identification number (NIF) is issued pending VAT activation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned SARL must register via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), selecting the 'société' filing and uploading the articles of association and identity documents. DGFiP automatically issues SIREN/SIRET registration and allocates the French intra-community VAT number. A non-resident entity without a French establishment that makes taxable supplies must file a direct VAT application through the dedicated DGFiP non-resident procedure; a provisional tax identification number (NIF) is issued pending VAT activation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box regime; tax classification is statutory. A SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (IS, CGI Art. 206). It may elect for a five-year period, under CGI Art. 239 bis, to be taxed as a transparent partnership (IR). A charitable endowment SARL may also qualify for non-profit exemption under CGI Art. 206(1) if management is disinterested and the activity is non-commercial. VAT grouping is available under CGI Art. 256 C ter; ATAD and DAC6 hybrid mismatch rules apply to elective financing arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services under the domestic franchise-in-base exemption (CGI Art. 293 B); non-established foreign suppliers have no threshold and must register for French VAT before making taxable supplies in France, subject to reverse charge or OSS where applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% (exemption with credit) applies to dispatches or transports of goods to destinations outside the EU (CGI Art. 262-I-1) and to certain services directly related to exported goods (CGI Art. 262-I-2) or consumed outside the EU under CGI Art. 259. The supplier must be VAT-registered, hold ownership or export mandate, prove the goods left France/EU under an accepted customs export procedure, and retain complete documentary evidence before claiming zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation via DELTA)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Proof of payment / Inward Remittance Certificate (avis de crédit or attestation de domiciliation bancaire)
  • Export sales contract or purchase order

From 1 September 2026, all French-established VAT-taxable persons must receive electronic invoices via the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified De-materialisation Partner Platform (PDP); issuance obligations phase in from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small enterprises. Structured e-invoice formats (Factur-X/UBL) must be transmitted by API to DGFiP for mandatory e-reporting; no real-time fiscal stamping applies, but the digital transmission pipeline and archiving are legally mandatory.

On audit, if export evidence is incomplete or the export did not in fact occur, VAT at 20% is clawed back plus intérêt de retard (0.20% per month) and statutory penalties under CGI Art. 1729: 40% for deliberate understatement and 80% for organized fraud. Criminal tax fraud under CGI Art. 1741 may result in imprisonment for up to five years and fines up to EUR 500,000.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; France applies the EU free movement of capital and payments regime (TFEU Articles 63-66). Both current and capital account operations are liberalised, with no exchange-control licensing, no surrender obligation and no ceiling on foreign-currency holding for the SARL. Profit or endowment remittances to foreign beneficiaries are unrestricted in FX terms but remain subject to EU/UN sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering due diligence, and, where a beneficiary is a dividend-paying structure, to the French withholding-tax framework on outbound distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion ratio applies to export or service receipts in France; proceeds may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency. The only binding obligation is statistical reporting: the resident entity must report all cross-border transactions to Banque de France for balance-of-payments purposes (monthly ECL/RUBRIQUE declarations where thresholds are exceeded), and cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more across the EU border must be declared to French Customs (DGDDI) under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672. Verification basis: 0% mandatory conversion; 100% free retainability.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), exercising monetary and balance-of-payments statistics authority within the Eurosystem; the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) supervises credit institutions and payment institutions, and the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) administers sanctions and restrictive measures, all under the Code Monétaire et Financier.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for French-licensed EMIs: High for operational accounts (Qonto, Kaiko, Vialink Panenka), but Medium overall because an endowment manager requires multi-currency treasury, segregated custody, and grant-disbursement rails beyond the EMI licence perimeter. EU-passported EMIs (German, Irish, Dutch digital banks) are Lawful Under the ACPR passporting regime and may hold the operational account on behalf of a French SARL. Non-EU and offshore digital EMIs (post-Brexit UK, US, Singapore, Baltic non-bank fintechs without French/EU passport) are rated Low: they cannot lawfully provide payment services to a French resident without ACPR authorisation or a full EU branch, and French banks will not clear or accept them as intermediary references.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Complete source-of-funds file: notarised donation deed (acte de donation), donor identity and passport, upstream bank statements covering 12 months, the SARL's minute book entry approving the capital/endowment increase, audited or accountant-certified financial statements, and a written philanthropic rationale mapping each inflow to ISIC 6430 / NAICS 813211 grant-making activity.
Sanctions and restrictive-measure screening hits on grant beneficiaries or donor counterparties (EU consolidated list, UN lists, US OFAC SDN, French CISLE list)

Defense evidence: Adverse-media-cleared counterparty file: full name, date of birth and tax residency of each beneficiary, a negative screening certificate issued by an independent screening tool, COREP/AML transaction justification, a signed sanctions-compliance warranty clause from the beneficiary, and a documented grant-approval memorandum evidencing legitimate humanitarian or scientific end-use.

Beneficial ownership opacity: nominee shareholder arrangements or a refusal to disclose the ultimate donor chain

Defense evidence: Up-to-date RBE filing receipt, organisational chart and ownership cascade, statutory auditor (commissaire aux comptes) certification of the capital structure, signed declarations of trust or nominee prohibition, and a legal opinion confirming that the SARL holding company for the private charitable endowment is not controlled, directly or indirectly, by a politically exposed person or a shell company in a high-risk jurisdiction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound endowment and capital-contribution wires are routed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA equivalent for EEA donors) to the SARL's French IBAN with the ISO 20022 business purpose code DIPP (Direct Investment) or 'CHAR/DON' where the donated funds are not consideration; the bank file must evidence the notarised capital increase, the source of funds, and the RBE/UBO declarations. Cross-border amounts equal to or exceeding EUR 10,000 carried in cash require the DGDDI cash declaration; bank-transferred sums are covered by the five-day validation of the beneficiary bank plus the automatic Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical reporting. For non-EEA donor jurisdictions, the intermediary must be an EU correspondent bank with clean MT 202 cover statements, and the receiving bank requires an LTV (lettre de venue) or pro-forma funding instruction to avoid quarantine.Grant disbursements within the euro area are executed through SEPA Credit Transfer / SEPA Instant clearing (TARGET2/TIPS), while non-EEA grant distributions and procurement settlements use SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers via the SARL's French correspondent bank, with UETR for end-to-end traceability. Commercial letter-of-credit settlements (UCP 600) are available for large scientific-equipment or humanitarian procurement, opened by the French relationship bank and cleared through banks in Germany, Switzerland, the UK or the United States. All outbound disbursements must carry the EU purpose code for grants (e.g., 'GRANT', 'TRUST', or 'CHAR'), be pre-validated against the EU/UN consolidated sanctions lists, and be reported for Banque de France statistical purposes where aggregate monthly customer transfers exceed the reporting threshold.EUR/USD deliverable foreign exchange forwards executed under an ISDA or FBF master agreement with an ACPR-authorised EU credit institutionNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or non-convertible currencies where the underlying philanthropic exposure requires hedge protectionCross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-term endowment liabilities denominated in USD, GBP or CHFFX swaps (swap de change) for short-term treasury liquidity management between EUR and other freely convertible currenciesOTC European-style FX options (calls/puts) on EUR/USD and EUR/GBP for downside protection, subject to the SARL's MiFID-classified professional status and AMF suitability requirements

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard corporate income tax rate; reduced 15% rate applies to first €38,120 of taxable profit for companies with qualified turnover below €10 million)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax on dividends distributed to non-resident corporate shareholders; 30% flat levy (prélèvement forfaitaire unique) plus social levies for non-resident individual shareholders, subject to treaty overriding.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal year ending 31 December, the return is due by the second working day following 1 May in the next year. For non-calendar fiscal years, the return must be filed within 3 months of the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million group turnover AND (i) EUR 8 million intra-group transactions with the French entity, or (ii) French entity itself exceeding EUR 400 million turnover. Thresholds applied per Article L13 AA of the French Tax Code.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a zero-tier Economic Substance Regulation of the kind seen in offshore financial centres. Instead, the l'abus de droit fiscal (abuse of law) doctrine, beneficial ownership criteria, and the anti-shell company framework under EU ATAD (notably DAC6) apply. A charitable endowment SARL must demonstrate actual grant-making activity, board control, and asset management presence; fiscal substance is proven by payroll, real platform, and decision-making files.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty of up to 80% of tax deficiencies plus late interest at 0.2% monthly. Criminal fine of EUR 450,000 (increased to EUR 750,000 in aggravated cases) and imprisonment of 5 to 7 years. Repeated offenders are barred from public contracts and subsidies.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is available where concealment of assets or criminal intent is proven. Directors face joint civil liability for unpaid taxes and penalties, travel bans against leaving France, and possible citizenship sanctions in treaty states.
Shell Company Abusive Arrangement (abus de droit documentaire)

Statutory sanction: Under Article L64 CGI, the tax administration may disregard the arrangement and reassess taxes under the real substance of the activity. Sanction includes 80% fine for abuse of law, 10% restriction on interest deductibility, and denial of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors and the de facto manager may be held personally liable for the resulting tax deficiencies and 80% penalty; additionally, travel bans and inability to leave the European Economic Area while proceedings are pending.
AML / Terrorist Financing Mitigation Failure (LCB-FT)

Statutory sanction: Under the French Monetary and Financial Code, penalties of up to EUR 1 million for institutions and EUR 2 million for repeated or aggravated violations, plus a prohibition on banking activity. Directors may be subject to a fine of EUR 375,000 and 5 years of imprisonment for money laundering offences.

Director personal liability: The director who knowingly executes an unlawful transfer is jointly and severally liable for the proceeds and for restitution orders issued by the penal court. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed pending trial.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure + Charge Fixation Abuse
