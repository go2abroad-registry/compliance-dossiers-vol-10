Name reservation / lock period

France has no standalone pre-incorporation name reservation; name protection accrues upon effective registration in the RNE. If the INPI Guichet Unique rejects the incorporation filing because the chosen name is confusingly similar to an earlier entity or violates a prohibited token, counsel must: (1) obtain from INPI the written rejection grounds (decision de refus d'immatriculation); (2) within two months from notification, file a motion to rectify the name and resubmit the complete incorporation dossier through the Guichet Unique; (3) where the name dispute involves a third party's prior rights, initiate adversarial proceedings before the Tribunal judiciaire (commercial chamber) under Code de commerce L.123-1 et seq. and the general law of unfair competition (Code civil Articles 1240-1241), or file for cancellation of the prior conflicting trademark with INPI; (4) in parallel, conduct a full RNE and INPI trademark search and secure availability of the desired domain names; appeal of an INPI administrative refusal may alternatively be pursued before the Paris Court of Appeal within the applicable statutory time limits. Practical SOP requires pre-filing clearance via data.inpi.fr and consultation of national and EU trademark databases (TMview) before resubmission.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to a SAS; capital may be set at EUR 1. Cash contributions must be paid up by at least 50% upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash funds must be deposited before registration with an authorized bank, notary, or the Caisse des dépôts et consignations, and are released after registration. In-kind contributions must generally be fully contributed at incorporation; a contribution auditor may be required unless the statutory exemption conditions are met.

Funding & unlock path: Before operation, obtain any required building and planning approvals and comply with French fire-safety and workplace rules. Where the premises qualify as an establishment open to the public (ERP), obtain the applicable fire-safety and accessibility approvals and opening authorization. For industrial premises, document fire-risk controls, emergency access, evacuation, electrical and equipment safety, and any structural or floor-load requirements for freezers, racking, and machinery. Complete required occupational-risk assessments, including the single risk-assessment document (DUERP), staff training, and workplace-health obligations. Any additional fire or structural clearance is site- and classification-dependent.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to a SAS; capital may be set at EUR 1. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility of the euro, consistent with Article 63 TFEU and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. There are no quantitative convertibility limits, no foreign-exchange permits, and no blocking or prior authorisation for ordinary trade payments, dividends, or loan servicing. The euro is freely deliverable in France; French resident companies may freely hold foreign-currency accounts (comptes en devises) and settle in any currency via authorised credit institutions. Profit remittance by the SAS to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted at the exchange-control level; the operative hurdles are documentary and fiscal only: (i) a corporate distribution resolution supported by distributable reserves evidenced in audited accounts; (ii) French withholding tax of 25% (12.8% for non-resident individuals), reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the EU parent holds at least 10% of capital for at least 24 months, or to lower treaty rates (e.g., France-Germany treaty 0% at 10%+ shareholding); and (iii) presentation of the DGFiP tax regularity certificate (attestation de régularité fiscale) for larger dividend flows. All cross-border transfers are reported by the authorised intermediary bank to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics under CMF Articles R152-1 to R152-3; the company itself is not required to file a separate FX declaration for ordinary payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France abolished all mandatory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation deadlines, and conversion ratios by Law No. 90-1098. A French exporting SAS may retain 100% of its export receipts in foreign-currency accounts or convert them at market rates, with no statutory conversion band, no surrender timeline, and no minimum repatriation percentage. The only residual obligations are informational: (i) the French receiving bank records export receipts in the monthly Banque de France balance-of-payments transmission (single DEMO statistical file), using the appropriate current-account motif code; and (ii) physical cross-border transport of cash or bearer monetary instruments equal to or exceeding EUR 10,000 must be declared by the traveller under EU Regulation 2018/1672 and the French CMF Article R152-3 — this is a carrier-level customs declaration, not a treasury obligation. No export licence or foreign-currency repatriation certificate is required for frozen food products (ISIC 1074).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), 31 Rue Croix des Petits Champs, 75001 Paris — Direction Générale de la Stabilité Financière et des Opérations, in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; exercises Eurosystem delegated powers from the European Central Bank (ECB) and administers balance-of-payments statistics, cross-border capital monitoring, and foreign-exchange operational oversight under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier, CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium overall — High for EUR collection and low-value operational payments, Low for cross-border trade finance, documentary letters of credit, and FX hedging. Legal constraints: French and passported European EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business) are licensed under CMF Article L525-1 and PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366); they may provide payment accounts, card acquiring, SEPA/SWIFT payment initiation, and FX conversion at market rates through partner banks. They cannot issue, advise, or confirm documentary credits (MT700/MT710), cannot act as the authorised intermediary for Banque de France balance-of-payments declarations, cannot provide credit or negative balances beyond permitted intra-day arrangements, and are not accepted as depositary for regulatory capital escrow for share subscriptions. For the SAS frozen-food factory, an EMI is strictly complementary and must be linked to a Tier-1 bank account for LC settlement, correspondent USD clearing, and OTC forex contracts.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: non-resident shareholder issues a SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer from its own bank to the SAS capital escrow account at the French Tier-1 authorised intermediary, quoting in Field 70 'Subscription to share capital / capital increase of FR SAS' and in Field 72 the shareholder reference. The French receiving bank assigns the applicable Banque de France balance-of-payments motif code for inward direct investment in equity (category 'Investissements directs — titres de participation') in its DEMO statistical filing. No prior ministerial authorisation is required for the frozen-food manufacturing sector (ISIC 1074) under CMF Article L151-3, as the activity does not fall on the strategic-sector list. Verification pack released after compliance review: (i) certified or notarised statuts incorporating the capital amount; (ii) bulletin de souscription d'actions; (iii) minutes of the extraordinary general meeting resolving the capital increase; (iv) updated registre des mouvements de titres; (v) UBO declaration; and (vi) proof that the funds were received in cleared money from the shareholder's own account, without set-off, compensation, or third-party on-behalf payment.Treasury clearing rails: (A) SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfer / open-account settlement of raw-material imports (flour, shrimp, pork, seasonings) and SME export collections in EUR, USD, GBP, or CNH; EUR settlement clears via TARGET2 (T2) for large-value payments or via SEPA Credit Transfer in ISO 20022 pain.001 for EEA EUR payments below T2 thresholds; USD clears via Fedwire/CHIPS; CNH clears via CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System). (B) SWIFT MT700 (issue) / MT710 (confirmation) documentary letters of credit under UCP 600 for structured trade settlement; the French bank issues import LCs in favour of raw-material suppliers and advises/confirms export LCs received from buyers of the frozen dim sum products. (C) SWIFT MT400 documentary collection for clean collections where LC terms are not required. The factory should maintain a revolving LC line and cash collateral or guarantee facility with the Tier-1 bank, and must ensure all outbound payment instructions include invoice numbers, transport references, and HS/ISIC codes for customs and statistical coherence.Permissible — Outright FX Forward (Change à Terme): OTC contract with any ACPR-licensed credit institution under an ISDA 2002 or FBF 1996 Master Agreement; no statutory underlying-transaction test applies to a French commercial SAS, and trades are subject to EMIR transaction reporting and internal treasury mandate limits.Permissible — Cross-Currency Swap (Swap de Devises): permitted for EUR/USD and EUR/CNH exposure management, documented under an ISDA/CSA master agreement; where a financial counterparty is involved, mandatory margin exchange applies under EMIR uncleared-margin rules (VM/IM with UMR obligations) and transactions must be reported to an EU-registered trade repository.Permissible with caveat — Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF): not a market-standard instrument for EUR because the euro is fully convertible and deliverable; NDFs may be booked exceptionally to hedge non-convertible or restricted currencies (e.g., onshore CNY, RUB, BRL) via an authorised French credit institution settling in EUR in the offshore market, with the resulting position reflected in the Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration. For Chinese renminbi exposure, a deliverable CNH (offshore yuan) forward or swap is the preferred permitted alternative.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 (aggregate group turnover or gross assets) triggering mandatory Local File and Master File; Country-by-Country reporting applies above EUR 750,000,000 consolidated annual revenue.

Economic substance scope: France applies the OECD BEPS substance requirements through the General Anti-Abuse Rule (abus de droit, Art. 64 LPF) and the principle of substance over form. A French SAS must demonstrate actual personnel, premises, decision-making, and commercial risk in France; artificial arrangements without economic substance are disregarded and treaty benefits denied under the Principal Purpose Test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fiscal reassessment plus 80% penalty for intentional tax fraud and late interest at 0.20% per month. Criminal conviction under Art. 1741 CGI may result in up to five years’ imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, or seven years / EUR 1,000,000 in aggravated circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors who initiate, approve, or knowingly benefit from evasion are personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; travel bans, asset forfeiture, and imprisonment may be imposed. In extreme cases, the corporate veil is pierced to attach liability to management.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Rejection of the artificial structure under GAAR (abus de droit) with an 80% penalty; denial of treaty benefits; reassessment of profits attributable to the substance-less entity; possible administrative fines up to EUR 750,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally required to restore sums distributed or trapped in shell structures, and may face commercial court disqualification or civil restitution orders; if fraud is proved, criminal liability attaches.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Automatic reassessment of intra-group prices with 40% penalty for gross negligence and late interest at 0.20%; non-response after formal notice incurs an administrative fine of up to EUR 10,000 plus potential criminal sanctions for obstructing tax control.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable where they knowingly signed false or misleading TP documentation; penalties may be lifted against directors who personalise the corporate fault under the 'faute lourde' doctrine.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of up to 5% of the unlawful cash amount; criminal prosecution for money laundering carrying up to EUR 150,000 fine and imprisonment; increased penalties under the Monetary and Financial Code for repeated or organised infractions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate cash laundering face imprisonment up to seven years, EPC/ACM restrictions, travel bans, and disqualification from corporate management; compliance officers may also be sanctioned.
