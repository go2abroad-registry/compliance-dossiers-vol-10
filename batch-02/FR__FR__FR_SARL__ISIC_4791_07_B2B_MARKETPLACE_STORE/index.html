Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The French Commercial Code imposes no minimum share capital for a SARL, but cash contributions must be subscribed in full and paid up to at least 20% at incorporation; the balance may be paid within five years. Contributions must be deposited on a blocked bank, notarial, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations account in the name of the company in formation, and the funds are released only after RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and structural load inspection are not required for a non-ERP office-based platform. The employer must still comply with workplace health and safety rules under the Code du travail; if the office is open to the public or a warehouse is operated, the appropriate ERP fire authorization and structural clearance must be obtained from the mairie or préfecture.

Paid-in capital rule: The French Commercial Code imposes no minimum share capital for a SARL, but cash contributions must be subscribed in full and paid up to at least 20% at incorporation; the balance may be paid within five years. Contributions must be deposited on a blocked bank, notarial, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations account in the name of the company in formation, and the funds are released only after RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the impots.gouv.fr professional portal and the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Customs export formalities are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI) through douane.gouv.fr and the DELTA customs systems.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is incorporated and registered through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). The registration produces an SIREN identifier for the legal entity and an SIRET identifier for each establishment. The entity must then activate its professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr and register with its competent SIE. The SIE determines the applicable VAT regime and issues or validates the French intra-Community VAT identification number, generally consisting of the FR country code, a two-character key, and the SIREN number. A non-established foreign taxable person must register with the relevant French foreign-business tax service where it makes taxable French supplies that are not fully reverse-charged. The application normally requires constitutional documents, proof of legal existence, identification of directors and beneficial owners, business activity details, French establishment or representative information where applicable, expected turnover, banking information, and evidence supporting the VAT liability. EU-established businesses may generally use the EU VAT registration and One Stop Shop mechanisms where legally available; non-EU businesses may require a French tax representative unless an applicable exemption or reverse-charge rule applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS). An eligible SARL may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu, or IR), generally subject to statutory conditions concerning age, ownership, activity, employee headcount, turnover or balance-sheet limits, and duration. A qualifying family SARL may elect IR without the same five-year limitation, subject to the family-ownership conditions. France does not operate a United States-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive provisions and can deny deductions, defer deductions, or impose inclusion where instruments or entities produce deductible/non-included or duplicate deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business eligible for the franchise in base, the principal turnover thresholds are generally EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and certain related activities and EUR 37,500 for services, subject to statutory tolerance thresholds, activity-specific rules, and legislative amendments. A B2B electronic marketplace may fall within the services threshold depending on its contractual remuneration and supply characterization. There is no general turnover threshold that protects a French-established business from VAT registration once it elects or is required to use a normal VAT regime. A non-established supplier generally has no broad French threshold for taxable French transactions, subject to reverse-charge rules. Intra-Community distance sales of goods and certain electronically supplied services are subject to the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold, after which the destination principle applies, with OSS available for qualifying supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT when the supplier is the exporter or acts under an applicable export arrangement and can substantiate customs exit from the EU. The supplier must retain the export customs declaration and reliable evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. Intra-Community supplies of goods may be exempt from French VAT where the customer is identified for VAT in another EU Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier retains transport and customer-VAT evidence and makes required recapitulative statements. Services supplied to a business established outside France are generally governed by place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of French VAT rather than technically zero-rated; services connected with French immovable property, events, passenger transport, short-term vehicle hire, and other special categories require separate analysis. A marketplace operator must also assess deemed-supplier rules for platforms facilitating certain B2C imported or intra-EU goods transactions.

  • Electronic export customs declaration submitted through the applicable DELTA system, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent electronic proof that the goods exited the European Union
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and VAT treatment
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, air waybill, road consignment note, courier record, or carrier delivery confirmation
  • Packing list and, where relevant, proof of export authorization, licenses, or restrictions compliance
  • Customer order, contract, marketplace transaction record, and proof of delivery where relevant
  • Payment evidence or other commercial evidence supporting the transaction; an inward-remittance certificate is not generally a mandatory French VAT export document

France is implementing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices; large and medium-sized businesses must generally issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from that date. Small and medium-sized businesses, including most SARLs, are generally scheduled to issue them from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be transmitted through an approved private platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire, or PDP); the public invoicing portal is being used as the government platform infrastructure. Cross-border B2B transactions, B2C transactions, and certain non-domestic flows are subject to e-reporting rather than domestic B2B invoice transmission. France is not based on universal real-time pre-clearance of every invoice; mandatory structured data transmission, platform routing, authenticity and integrity controls, and digital archival requirements apply. The required structured formats and data fields are prescribed by French VAT and e-invoicing regulations and may include transaction, payment, customer identification, VAT, and collection-status data.

France does not generally impose a universal 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline as a condition for export VAT exemption or refund. Export VAT treatment is primarily substantiated by customs exit evidence, transport records, commercial documentation, and transaction consistency. If zero-rating or exemption is unsupported, the DGFiP may reassess output VAT, deny or claw back an input VAT refund, apply late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month, and impose tax penalties. The principal penalty rates can include 10% for late or insufficient compliance, 40% for deliberate misconduct, and 80% for fraudulent maneuvers or abuse of rights, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards. False documentation, concealed transactions, or intentional VAT fraud may also trigger criminal proceedings, including imprisonment and substantial fines under the French Tax Code. Repeated or material non-compliance may result in enhanced audit scrutiny, refusal of refund claims, customs enforcement, and recovery of reassessed VAT, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the euro for both current-account and capital-account transactions, consistent with Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the French Monetary and Financial Code. Capital injections into an SARL by non-resident shareholders, profit distributions, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds may be freely converted and remitted abroad without prior foreign-exchange licensing or central bank approval. The only operational requirements are standard bank KYC/AML checks, sanctions screening, and, where applicable, statistical balance-of-payments declaration to the Banque de France for individual transactions exceeding EUR 50,000 in the non-EU reporting file. There is no restriction on profit remittance, no requirement to obtain an exchange-control certificate, and no discriminatory conversion premium for non-resident owners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose any statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory minimum conversion obligations on export proceeds. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in foreign-currency bank accounts held with EU or EEA credit institutions without any conversion requirement. The effitive mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. The only compliance obligations are (i) a statistical reporting duty to the Banque de France for each non-EU foreign-exchange transaction equal to or exceeding EUR 50,000, (ii) inclusion of foreign-currency account balances and receivables in the annual French balance-of-payments statement, and (iii) declaration of any foreign bank account to the French tax administration on the annual tax return (form No. 3916-bis). There is no statutory deadline for converting export receipts; dealers may hold indefinite open positions, subject to normal corporate treasury and transfer-pricing policies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France — the French central bank, together with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the prudential licensing authority and the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) acting as the competent authority for balance-of-payments reporting and exchange-control notifications under the Code monétaire et financier and EU capital-movement rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High for operational accounts and EUR payment collection, Medium for cross-border multi-currency disbursement, and Low for capital-escrow services and documentary-credit instruments. Regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) authorised in France under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) — e.g., Qonto, Shine, Lydia Solutions, and Manager — may open fully digital account structures for an SARL without physical branch visits. However, a French-licensed EMI cannot hold an escrow account that requires credit-worthy custody, cannot issue or receive commercial letters of credit, and, when passporting into France from another EEA state, must comply with ACPR passport-notification requirements. For the B2B marketplace model, EMIs are viable as the primary ‘collection rail’ for seller payouts and charge payments, but a traditional credit institution with SWIFT and LC capability remains mandatory for inbound equity capital and large trade-sponsored transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing of the SARL capital injection or shareholder loan from abroad must use the SWIFT MT103 payment message, dictating a purpose code of 'D' (direct investment/deposit) or the ISO20022 payment description 'Carbon and equity investment' as instructed by the receiving French bank; the bank requires the exact corporate registration number (SIREN of the SARL) and the underlying equity constitution document. The injection must originate from the shareholder's own bank account in a jurisdiction from which EEA banks accept clear AML evidence, and not from an unrelated third party. If the amount exceeds EUR 50,000, the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical return (remise de déclaration Cotes) must be filed by the bank or the entity within twoworking days; capital movements above EUR 12.5M may trigger additional statistical thresholds. For a foreign-currency injection, the euros are converted at the ECB euro foreign-exchange reference rate prevailing on the valuation date, with the foreign-exchange gain or loss being recognised in the SARL's share premium reserve; no prior approval is required for the conversion. To avoid misclassification as commercial turnover, the account must be earmarked as a separate ‘capital account’ or ‘compte courant d’associé’ and the contribution evidenced by a notarial declaration of subscription and payment, a formal board resolution, and a bank credit advice referencing the corporate identifier.Trade disbursements from the French SARL for B2B marketplace procurement use four permitted clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 wire transfer for EUR, USD, GBP, or any convertible-currency settlement to suppliers abroad, carrying the purpose code of commercial credit; (2) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (LC) governed by UCP 600, initiated via SWIFT MT700/MT760 through an issuing French bank and a confirming bank abroad, for high-value or sovereign-risk purchases; (3) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for euro payments to EEA suppliers with the shortest clearing cycles and mandatory IBAN identification; and (4) e-commerce/payment institution rails such as Alibaba Pay or Payoneer for catalogue purchases where the transaction value is low, subject to the bank’s pre-approval of counterparty and daily transaction volume. Each outbound disbursement must be supported by a commercial invoice, purchase order, logistics document, or platform supplier contract, and must not be sent to a country subject to European Union sanctions. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High for operational accounts and EUR payment collection, Medium for cross-border multi-currency disbursement, and Low for capital-escrow services and documentary-credit instruments. Regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) authorised in France under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) — e.g., Qonto, Shine, Lydia Solutions, and Manager — may open fully digital account structures for an SARL without physical branch visits. However, a French-licensed EMI cannot hold an escrow account that requires credit-worthy custody, cannot issue or receive commercial letters of credit, and, when passporting into France from another EEA state, must comply with ACPR passport-notification requirements. For the B2B marketplace model, EMIs are viable as the primary ‘collection rail’ for seller payouts and charge payments, but a traditional credit institution with SWIFT and LC capability remains mandatory for inbound equity capital and large trade-sponsored transactions.
