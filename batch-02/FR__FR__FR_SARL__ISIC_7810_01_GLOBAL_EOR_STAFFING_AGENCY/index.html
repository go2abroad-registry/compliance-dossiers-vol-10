Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Article L.223-2 of the French Commercial Code. The Articles of Association may fix a lower or higher nominal capital; for regulated employment activities the capital should be sufficient to support the financial guarantee obligation under Labour Code Article L.124-3 et seq. The subscribed cash capital must be deposited into a blocked French bank account and evidenced by a bank certificate; funds are released only after RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), official portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate formation and registration via Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; customs and export formalities under Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), portal: douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, statutory incorporation documents are filed through the Guichet Unique (Centre de Formalités des Entreprises). DGFiP issues the SIREN, SIRET, and Registre National des Entreprises registration. VAT identification is automatic for established taxable persons: DGFiP allocates a French intra-Community VAT number (FR + 11 digits) under CGI Article 286 ter, verifiable via the EU VIES system. A non-established foreign enterprise needing direct French VAT registration must submit a registration application to the competent DGFiP local tax office and, if established outside the EU/EEA, appoint an accredited tax representative. A French SARL acting as an Employer of Record is an established taxable person and registers for French VAT from the start of taxable activity without requiring a turnover threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in France. A SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level. Under CGI Article 239 bis AA, a qualifying SARL may elect, for a maximum of the first five fiscal years and subject to conditions including all shareholders being natural persons, to be taxed transparently as a société de personnes. On indirect taxes, a small business may elect to opt out of the franchise en base de TVA exemption under CGI Article 293 B to become subject to VAT. EU/ATAD hybrid mismatch rules impose notification and disallowance obligations rather than elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The general statutory VAT registration/exemption thresholds under the franchise en base de TVA (CGI Article 293 B) are EUR 85,000 for principal activity of supplying goods and EUR 37,500 for supplies of services, with special reduced thresholds applicable to supplies of dwelling-related services and certain liberal professions. These thresholds are exemptions for small taxpayers, not ordinary taxable person registration thresholds: an established SARL carrying on an EOR/staffing business is required to register for VAT from the first euro of taxable activity. Cross-border B2B services supplied to non-French / non-EU customers are subject to place-of-supply rules and reverse charge rather than threshold-based registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies under CGI Article 262.I only to goods physically dispatched or transported by the supplier or on the supplier's behalf to a destination outside the European Union. The exporter must retain documentary proof of export, normally within one year from shipment. EOR and staffing services supplied by a French SARL to non-French or non-EU business customers are not technically zero-rated; they are outside French VAT scope under the B2B place-of-supply rule (reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction) where the customer is a taxable person. Staffing services supplied to a French-established client remain subject to French VAT at 20%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation - EX / DELTA export system)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list

France is phasing in a mandatory B2B electronic invoicing and e-reporting regime under CGI Article 289 bis. From 1 September 2026, large and mid-size enterprises must receive and issue structured electronic invoices (Factur-X, UBL, CII) through a registered Platforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the Public/Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); from 1 September 2027, the obligation extends to small enterprises and micro-enterprises. Cross-border B2B and B2C transactions are covered by e-reporting requirements submitted to DGFiP via API. There is currently no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for continuous transaction authorization, but all e-invoice data must be transmitted in real time or near-real time to the relevant PDP/PPF for audit and clearance purposes.

DGFiP may reclassify improperly zero-rated supplies as taxable domestic supplies and assess VAT at 20% plus late payment interest (0.20% per month) and penalties: 40% where a serious inaccuracy is found, 80% in cases of bad faith, and 100% for fraudulent maneuvers or deliberate dissimulation. Failure to substantiate export within the one-year evidentiary period triggers a retrospective VAT clawback plus interest and penalties. Customs and exchange-control infringements, including false declarations or failure to repatriate export proceeds, may result in administrative fines up to the value of the goods or services involved and, in aggravated cases, criminal sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in France. Capital and current account transactions are liberalised under Articles 63-66 TFEU and the French Monetary and Financial Code. France maintains no exchange-control authorisation, no profit-repatriation queue and no blocked-fund mechanisms. Statutory limitation applies only to sectoral foreign-direct-investment screening in strategic activities (defence, security, gambling, public order), which is irrelevant to an EOR payroll scope. Dividend remittances from the FR SARL: an EU parent holding at least 10% for a minimum 2-year period is exempt from French withholding tax under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; non-EU parents face a 25% withholding tax under Article 119 bis of the French General Tax Code (CGI), reduced by applicable double tax treaties (e.g., 0-5% under the US-France treaty on qualifying holdings). All cross-border flows are traced by the receiving ACPR-licensed intermediary for Banque de France statistical balance-of-payments purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France does not impose statutory surrender timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export or service receipts. Euro and foreign-currency proceeds may be retained in accounts with an ACPR-licensed bank or held abroad; the authorised intermediary reports settlement flows to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics. For direct-investment-related inflows from non-EU residents, transactions at or above the Banque de France monthly declaration threshold of EUR 15 million per operation require a dedicated statistical return; smaller inflows are captured by the intermediary's normal reporting. No forced conversion into euro is ever required.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); prudential supervision and licensing of banks, payment institutions and electronic-money institutions rest with the ACPR (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: HIGH for cross-border contractor disbursement and API-based payout rails; MEDIUM for primary treasury hosting. Wise Europe SA (electronic-money institution authorised by the National Bank of Belgium and passported into France) is viable for batch payroll and contractor payments in 40+ currencies; Qonto SAS and Shine (Société Générale) provide French-IBAN accounts with fully remote opening. Legal constraints: (i) EMI balances are safeguarded e-money, not bank deposits, and are outside the French FGDR deposit-guarantee scheme; (ii) an EMI cannot issue the banker's attestation de dépôt de fonds required to certify the SARL's initial capital, so the capital constitution escrow must be held by an ACPR-licensed bank or a notary; (iii) a French bank account is operationally preferable for URSSAF, DGFiP and DSN payroll debits and for the financial-guarantee evidence required by workplace-staffing regulations; (iv) non-EU offshore EMIs without an EEA passport or French agent cannot lawfully serve as the SARL's statutory corporate treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial capital: transfer EUR by SEPA (within the EU) or by SWIFT MT103 (non-EU) to the SARL's blocked capital account (compte bloqué) maintained with the chosen ACPR-licensed bank or held by a notary in a sequestrated account; the MT103 field 70 narrative should read 'CONSTITUTION DE CAPITAL - SARL [NAME]'. The depositary bank issues the attestation de dépôt de fonds required for RCS/Kbis registration. Subsequent injections: the shareholder passes a resolution (P.V. d'assemblée) and the statuts are updated; the transfer narrative must state 'AUGMENTATION DE CAPITAL' or 'AVANCE EN COMPTE COURANT D'ASSOCIÉ'. The receiving bank automatically reports the flow to the Banque de France as part of balance-of-payments statistics and performs EU/OFAC sanctions screening and UBO verification. Non-EU direct-investment inflows of EUR 15 million or more per operation trigger a specific monthly statistical declaration to the Banque de France; no prior ministry or central-bank authorisation is required for an EOR payroll business.Domestic payroll: the French payroll engine files SEPA Credit Transfers (SCT) and, where required, SEPA Instant Credit Transfers (SCT Inst up to EUR 100,000) for same-day salary crediting; DSN filings fund URSSAF social contributions directly from the operational account. French employees are paid exclusively in EUR from the French IBAN. Cross-border contractor settlements: SWIFT MT103 transfers (beneficiary receives in local currency with shared or half charges) or API-based EMI rails (e.g., Wise Batch) converting EUR at interbank-linked rates, with each payout referencing the underlying contractor agreement and payroll ledger line. For goods/trade settlement if any (e.g., equipment, licences), the French bank can issue an irrevocable documentary letter of credit under ICC UCP 600, with optional confirmation by the beneficiary's correspondent bank. All outflows are reconciled to the payroll register and retained for VAT, transfer-pricing and French commercial-law audit requirements.Outright FX forwards (change à terme) on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP and other liquid pairs with an ACPR-licensed bank under an ISDA 1998 FX or FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) Master Agreement, subject to EMIR trade reporting.FX swaps (combined spot and forward legs) for monthly payroll liquidity management across currencies, documented under the same ISDA/FBF framework.Cross-currency swaps (XCCY) for multi-year euro versus foreign-currency net exposure (typically EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) with applicable EMIR margin and collateral rules.European-style vanilla FX options (purchase only) as premium-limited hedges on material payroll-currency exposures, requiring evidence of underlying treasury exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for blocked or exotic currencies (e.g., BRL, NGN, ARS) may be offered by the bank's trading desk when the EOR's contractor payout in that currency cannot be physically delivered, provided a genuine payroll exposure is demonstrated; NDFs on EUR are not standard because the euro is freely deliverable.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: HIGH for cross-border contractor disbursement and API-based payout rails; MEDIUM for primary treasury hosting. Wise Europe SA (electronic-money institution authorised by the National Bank of Belgium and passported into France) is viable for batch payroll and contractor payments in 40+ currencies; Qonto SAS and Shine (Société Générale) provide French-IBAN accounts with fully remote opening. Legal constraints: (i) EMI balances are safeguarded e-money, not bank deposits, and are outside the French FGDR deposit-guarantee scheme; (ii) an EMI cannot issue the banker's attestation de dépôt de fonds required to certify the SARL's initial capital, so the capital constitution escrow must be held by an ACPR-licensed bank or a notary; (iii) a French bank account is operationally preferable for URSSAF, DGFiP and DSN payroll debits and for the financial-guarantee evidence required by workplace-staffing regulations; (iv) non-EU offshore EMIs without an EEA passport or French agent cannot lawfully serve as the SARL's statutory corporate treasury account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR. Annual form 2257 (transfer-pricing declaration) is required when aggregate controlled transactions with foreign related parties exceed EUR 100,000. Full Master File / Local File documentation is mandatory for enterprises whose turnover or balance-sheet total exceeds EUR 400 million, or belonging to a group whose consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 400 million. Country-by-Country reports apply to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to the UAE. However, the French 'abus de droit' doctrine (LPF art. L64) and the general anti-abuse framework allow the tax administration to disregard artificial arrangements lacking commercial substance. For an EOR, substance must be demonstrable through a French office, local payroll, staff hierarchy, physical asset management, board decisions, and compliance with French labor law.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud (CGI art. 1741): up to 5 years' imprisonment and EUR 75,000 fine; administrative surcharges: 40% for bad faith, 80% for fraudulent intent; late-payment interest at 0.2% per month; potential embargo of bank accounts and seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted as accomplices for personal participation in the tax evasion. Courts may impose travel bans, corporate-director disqualification, and direct personal recovery of the company's unpaid taxes under the 'action en comblement du passif' if the director's fault caused the insufficiency.
Artificial Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Under LPF art. L64, the tax administration may disregard the transaction or the entire legal structure and recharacterize income as a deemed distribution or as taxable income of the beneficial owner. Penalty: 80% surcharge on the reassessed tax; denied deductibility and reclassification of fees as hidden remuneration; possible 3-year statute extension.

Director personal liability: Court may pierce the corporate veil if the company is fictitious or manifestly undercapitalized; director can be held jointly liable for corporate taxes, interest, and penalties, and criminally liable for forgery of books/accounts.
Illegal Employment Staffing

Statutory sanction: Prohibition of illegal labor lending (prêt illicite de main-d'œuvre) and trafficking of labor (marchandage) under the French Labour Code: up to 5 years' imprisonment and EUR 75,000 fine; administrative fine of EUR 4,000 per illegally supplied worker; suspension of activity by the labor inspectorate.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to criminal penalties and may be banned from managing a company for up to 5 years; social-security authorities may also seek civil recovery of unpaid employer contributions against the director in cases of fraudulent practises.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Failure to declare beneficial owner: fine up to EUR 7,500 for a legal entity; unreported foreign bank account: EUR 1,500 per account (EUR 10,000 if the account is held in a non-cooperative state); money laundering (C. pén. art. 324-1): 5 years' imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine.

Director personal liability: Directors can be criminally liable for money laundering and may face asset freezing, travel ban, and personal confiscation orders; civil liability extends to monetary fines and restitution of illegal profits; AML governance failures may lead to a management ban by the regulatory authority.
