Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SARL must have a registered office in France under Commercial Code Articles L.123-11 et seq. A commercial domiciliation company may be used if it is properly authorized and a written domiciliation agreement is maintained. Competent corporate registry

Guichet Unique operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; registration is executed by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés) of the judicial district of the registered office.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée) and R.123-1 ff. (RCS registration); Article 1835 of the Civil Code (contents of articles); Decree No. 2023-138 of 27 February 2023 (Guichet Unique digital filing regime).

Formation / constitutional filings

Demande d'immatriculation principale au registre du commerce et des sociétés (electronic M0 declaration filed via the Guichet Unique), culminating in the Extrait Kbis as the official certificate of legal existence.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital (Law No. 2003-721 of 1 August 2003); capital is freely fixed at EUR 1 or more in the Articles. Cash contributions must be paid up to at least one-fifth upon incorporation (Article L.223-7, Code de commerce), with the balance within five years. Funds must be deposited in escrow with a French credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and are released only upon issuance of the Kbis.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: for any leased office, verify the building's fire classification and, if the premises constitute an ERP (établissement recevant du public) of the 5th category or a workplace under the Code du travail, obtain the fire inspection certificate from the commission de sécurité or the attestation of the lessor. Structural load: verify with the landlord that floor-load capacities accommodate the storage of patent files, servers, and office equipment. Occupational clearance: implement the mandatory DUERP (document unique d'évaluation des risques professionnels) and maintain it on-site for inspection by CARSAT and labour inspectors; register the company with URSSAF for workplace-accident coverage before any employee is allowed to work.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital (Law No. 2003-721 of 1 August 2003); capital is freely fixed at EUR 1 or more in the Articles. Cash contributions must be paid up to at least one-fifth upon incorporation (Article L.223-7, Code de commerce), with the balance within five years. Funds must be deposited in escrow with a French credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and are released only upon issuance of the Kbis.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; operational portal: impots.gouv.fr; company formation registry: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique) with RCS registration effected through the greffe du tribunal de commerce.

E-government filing portal

For a newly formed French SARL: (1) execute the articles of association (statuts), open a French bank account and deposit stated share capital, and prepare proof of registered office; (2) file the complete dossier via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), which transmits to INSEE and the commercial court registry; (3) INSEE issues SIREN/SIRET identification; (4) DGFIP assigns the intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + two-digit control key + 11-digit SIREN and opens the corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) account. For a non-established foreign licensor without a French fixed establishment, direct VAT registration is effected by application to the DGFIP Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE); where no tax cooperation convention exists, appointment of a VAT fiscal representative is mandatory under Article 289 A CGI.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL is by default fully subject to corporate income tax (Articles 206 and 219 CGI) at 25% standard rate; the 15% reduced rate applies to the first EUR 42,622 of taxable profit for companies with annual revenue not exceeding EUR 10 million; taxable profits exceeding EUR 763,000 additionally trigger the 3.3% social surcharge where group turnover exceeds EUR 3.76 million. Net income from qualifying patent and software licensing may be taxed under the Article 238 CGI patent box regime at a 10% effective corporate rate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL is by default fully subject to corporate income tax (Articles 206 and 219 CGI) at 25% standard rate; the 15% reduced rate applies to the first EUR 42,622 of taxable profit for companies with annual revenue not exceeding EUR 10 million; taxable profits exceeding EUR 763,000 additionally trigger the 3.3% social surcharge where group turnover exceeds EUR 3.76 million. Net income from qualifying patent and software licensing may be taxed under the Article 238 CGI patent box regime at a 10% effective corporate rate. Article 239 bis AA CGI pass-through eligibility exists only for SARLs whose members are exclusively natural persons; default characterization nevertheless remains entity-level taxation.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in French tax law. Available statutory options: (i) Article 239 bis AA CGI: a SARL whose shareholders are exclusively natural persons may elect pass-through income-tax treatment for the company's profits; (ii) Article 1655 ter CGI 'SARL de famille' election for family SARLs to be taxed as partnerships; (iii) Article 238 CGI patent box option to apply the 10% preferential rate to net licensing income derived from qualifying patents and qualifying software rights, binding for five years; (iv) Articles 145 and 216 CGI parent-subsidiary (régime mère-fille) election for exemption of qualifying dividend income. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are subject to mandatory disallowance under Article 212-0 CGI with no elective override.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The domestic small-business exemption (franchise en base, Article 293 B CGI) applies up to EUR 91,900 annual supplies of goods and EUR 36,800 annual services; however, a non-established licensor supplying patent or trademark rights to a French VAT-registered business has no minimum threshold because the French customer must self-assess under the reverse charge (Article 283 CGI). A French-established SARL carrying on IP licensing is compulsorily VAT-registered from the first taxable supply with no registration threshold. Additional EU threshold: EUR 10,000 intra-Community distance-sales/OSS annual ceiling before mandatory OSS registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: export outside the EU is exempt with full input-VAT credit (Article 262 CGI) where the exporter holds a customs export declaration and transport evidence showing physical departure from the EU. Services/IP licensing: patent or trademark licensing by the French SARL to a business customer established outside France is outside French VAT (Article 259-1 CGI); for royalties from a non-EU non-business licensee, the supply remains taxable in France unless the effective use and exploitation of the IP occurs outside the EU (Article 259 D CGI), in which case the supply is outside scope and input VAT remains deductible. EU B2B royalty licenses are relieved through the reverse-charge mechanism with no French output VAT. Creditable input VAT is recovered via periodic CA3 returns or, for non-established businesses, through the EU refund procedure under Directive 2008/9/EC.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (for sea/air export)
  • CMR or equivalent land-transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank attestation of repatriation of foreign-currency proceeds)

France operates EU-standard periodic VAT returns (CA3) and does not apply real-time continuous transaction control or per-document fiscal stamping. Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing is phased in on 1 September 2026 (large and mid-size enterprises) and 1 September 2027 (SMEs, including SARLs); invoices must be transmitted as structured data (Factur-X / EN 16931) through a certified Partner Dematerialisation Platform (PDP) or the State Public Portal (PPF) via standardized APIs. Cross-border B2B and B2C transaction data must be electronically reported to the tax administration. Invoice integrity is ensured through electronic signature/seal and the PDP-PPF interoperability framework; retail cash-register software used for B2C receipts must be certified under NF 525.

Unsubstantiated export zero-rating triggers disallowance of input VAT recovery and reassessment of output VAT with a 40% penalty (Article 1729 CGI), increased to 80% for bad faith or fraudulent behavior; invoicing penalties under Article 1737 CGI can reach 50% of the sums involved. Customs penalties under Articles 410 and 413-2 Code des douanes include fines of one to three times the tax evaded and potential confiscation of goods. Fraudulent export-VAT and zero-rating claims expose the legal representative to criminal tax-evasion prosecution under Article 1741 CGI (up to five years imprisonment, EUR 750,000 fines for legal entities, and publication penalties). The DGFIP may retrospectively claw back refunds within the standard ten-year prescription period (Article L.186 LPF), plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a member of the Economic and Monetary Union. The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account; there are no French exchange controls, no central-bank clearance, and no mandatory approval for royalty, dividend, loan, or licence remittances. Current-account payments (royalties, service fees, sub-licence fees) and capital-account transactions (equity injections, intercompany loans, IP acquisitions) benefit from full liberalisation under EU free-movement-of-capital rules (Article 63 TFEU). Residual frictions are limited to: (i) French withholding taxes and treaty or Interest-and-Royalties-Directive formalities on cross-border licence payments; (ii) EU/UN sanctions screening and embargo compliance; (iii) mandatory declaration of cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more; and (iv) prior Ministerial authorisation under Decree No. 2019-1590 for inward FDI in sensitive sectors, which is generally not engaged by a pure IP licensing HoldCo unless the patents relate to listed critical technologies (defence, cybersecurity, critical infrastructure, dual-use R&D). Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is unrestricted provided the annual accounts are approved and the applicable WHT or exemption documentation is filed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable: France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no percentage conversion requirement, and no holding-period restriction on export receipts or royalty income. The SARL may retain a multi-currency account and convert EUR proceeds at freely negotiated market rates with its clearing bank. Compliance obligations are limited to: (i) the legal obligation to declare any physical transfer of cash or bearer instruments of EUR 10,000 or more to the French customs administration (Article L152-2 of the Code monétaire et financier); (ii) statistical monthly balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting to the Banque de France of direct investment and intercompany financial flows when the annual aggregate threshold is crossed; and (iii) standard AML documentation of the economic purpose of each foreign-currency conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) - principal central-bank and foreign-exchange authority, operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential and AML/CFT supervision is delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); balance-of-payments and capital-account statistical monitoring is coordinated with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for authorised EEA electronic money institutions (EMIs) holding a French ACPR licence or exercising a PSD2 passport. An EEA-licensed EMI can lawfully hold the SARL's operational funds, process royalty collections via SEPA and SWIFT, and issue payment cards; it cannot provide commercial credit, documentary credits, capital-escrow or séquestre arrangements, foreign-currency treasury services, or investment products. Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore non-EEA EMIs: direct servicing of French residents without EEA licensing or passporting would breach the French/European payment-services regime, and the presence of such an EMI in the treasury flow creates a serious AML-KYC obstruction at tier-1 French banks. No capital-escrow, LC, or lending capability is available. Practical recommendation: use an EEA EMI only for low-risk receipts collection in EUR/USD corridors, while maintaining the principal treasury and escrow accounts at a tier-1 French bank.

Root cause: High-royalty and low-substance profile: the IP HoldCo generates large licence fees with minimal French payroll, premises, or economic substance (classic IP-box red flag)

Defense evidence: Registered patent and trademark certificates with the full chain of title; executed licence and sub-licence agreements; OECD-compliant transfer-pricing documentation (master file and local file under Article 57 of the Code général des impôts); French substance file including office lease, active payroll records, CFE tax filing, and management minutes demonstrating strategic decisions taken in France; tax returns and financial statements evidencing arm's-length treatment.
Sanctions exposure or high-risk jurisdiction linkage: parent, counterparty, or sub-licence territories under EU/UN sanctions or on the FATF grey list, or suspected treaty shopping through an IP-box jurisdiction

Circular or pass-through treasury flows: royalty receipts rapidly exported to tax- or sanction-optimisation jurisdictions without operational purpose

Defense evidence: Intercompany services and loan agreements; approved cash-pooling or liquidity policy; commercial justification for each cross-border payment (licence invoice, WHT computation); annual transfer-pricing analysis; monthly Banque de France BOP declarations; board-approved budget and cash-flow model.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for authorised EEA electronic money institutions (EMIs) holding a French ACPR licence or exercising a PSD2 passport. An EEA-licensed EMI can lawfully hold the SARL's operational funds, process royalty collections via SEPA and SWIFT, and issue payment cards; it cannot provide commercial credit, documentary credits, capital-escrow or séquestre arrangements, foreign-currency treasury services, or investment products. Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore non-EEA EMIs: direct servicing of French residents without EEA licensing or passporting would breach the French/European payment-services regime, and the presence of such an EMI in the treasury flow creates a serious AML-KYC obstruction at tier-1 French banks. No capital-escrow, LC, or lending capability is available. Practical recommendation: use an EEA EMI only for low-risk receipts collection in EUR/USD corridors, while maintaining the principal treasury and escrow accounts at a tier-1 French bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (IS) under Article 219 CGI; qualifying small enterprises may benefit from a 15% reduced rate on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit, subject to annual turnover below EUR 10 million and 75% ownership by individuals.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Under Article 119 bis 2 of the French General Tax Code, dividends distributed by a French SARL to non-resident corporate shareholders are subject to a 25% withholding tax (increased to 30% for payments made to non-cooperative states); distributions to resident individuals fall under the 30% flat tax, while resident companies may avail of the 99% participation exemption. For an EU/EEA parent company meeting the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions (at least 10% shareholding held for two consecutive years), the withholding tax is reduced to 0%.

The definitive annual return must be filed within three months of the close of the fiscal year. For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the statutory filing deadline is 3 May of the following year; the online filing portal grants a short practical extension of one to two business days.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full three-tier transfer pricing documentation (master file, local file, and country-by-country report, where applicable) is mandatory for a French entity when the consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 400 million, or when the French entity's own annual turnover excluding VAT or gross assets exceeds EUR 400 million. Companies below those thresholds but engaged in cross-border related-party transactions must nevertheless complete the simplified transfer pricing schedule (imprimé 2257-SD) and attach it to the annual corporate income tax return.

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations of the offshore style, but economic substance is indispensable for an IP holding company. The French patent box applies only to IP developed by the taxpayer's own research and development under the OECD-approved nexus principle; passively acquired IP or mailbox sub-licensing structures are ineligible. Further, the general anti-abuse rule (Article L.64 LPF), the EU ATAD provisions, and the OECD BEPS Plan permit the French tax administration to disregard artificial contractual arrangements lacking real staff, premises, local management, or economic risk. A French SARL acting as a mere conduit without substance faces recharacterization, denial of treaty and Directive benefits, and exposure to fraud penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declaration

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions under Articles 1741 and 1743 of the General Tax Code: imprisonment up to five years and a fine up to EUR 250,000 for individuals; legal entities face fines up to EUR 1,250,000, confiscation of assets, and publication of the judgment. Administratively, reassessment is increased by 80% in cases of abuse of law or fraud, plus late-payment interest at 0.83% monthly.

Director personal liability: Under Article L.267 of the Book of Tax Procedures, the gérant (managing director) may be held jointly and severally liable for the SARL's tax debts when serious and repeated irregularities render the company unable to pay. A criminal conviction additionally triggers an interdiction de gérer (management ban) up to ten years, possible travel restrictions, and attachment of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may reassess the royalty income at the arm's length level, resulting in additional corporate tax at 25% plus late-payment interest; a specific penalty of up to 5% of the transfer-pricing adjustment applies, with a minimum fine of EUR 10,000 (Article L.13 AA LPF). Failure to submit form 2257-SD is itself punishable by an automatic fine of EUR 1,000.

Director personal liability: When the documentation deficiency amounts to a management fault (faute de gestion) that causes the company's insolvency or inability to pay the tax debt, the gérant may face civil liability in an 'action en responsabilité pour insuffisance d'actif'; continued intentional under-documentation can also expose directors to fraud charges.
Withholding Tax Evasion and False Treaty Claim

Statutory sanction: The French payer is assessed for the unpaid withholding tax plus late-payment interest; late-filing/underpayment penalties amount to 5% or 10%, and bad faith increases penalties to 40% or 80%. Where the mechanism constitutes tax fraud, criminal penalties of five years' imprisonment and up to EUR 250,000 apply.

Director personal liability: Article 1754 CGI makes directors personally liable for the withholding tax when the company does not deduct and remit the amounts; if the non-withholding is part of fraudulent mismanagement, the gérant may be subject to contribution to the tax debt, a management ban, and a travel ban.
AML Violations and Shell-Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Articles L.574-3 et seq.), failure to report a suspicious transaction carries a maximum fine of EUR 375,000 and imprisonment of up to five years for individuals; corporate fines may reach EUR 2,250,000. Regulatory sanctions include account freezing, dissolution of the legal entity, and confiscation of the proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers who knowingly use the company as an instrument of laundering or tax fraud are jointly and severally liable for penalties; courts may impose an interdiction de gérer up to fifteen years, a ban on residing in France, asset seizure, and travel restrictions within the EU legal framework.
