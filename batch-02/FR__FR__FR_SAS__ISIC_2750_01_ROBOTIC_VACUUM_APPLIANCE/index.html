Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum legal capital for an SAS. The articles of association freely fix the share capital. All issued shares must be fully subscribed and fully paid up in accordance with Article L.227-2. There is no statutory requirement for a bank escrow account for cash contributions, but capital contributions must be effectively available and supported by a certificate of deposit (attestation de blocage / attestation de dépôt des fonds) when the bank is not opened with a notary.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Commission de Sécurité (or certificate of compliance from a certified bureau de contrôle) is required before opening the premises to staff. Structural load inspections must be contracted with a certified structural engineer if installing heavy machinery. Workplace clearance includes the electrical installation conformity certificate, the approval of ventilation and workstation ergonomics, and the labor inspection endorsement from DREETS as to compliance with the Labor Code (Code du travail), including first-aid equipment and emergency evacuation signage.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum legal capital for an SAS. The articles of association freely fix the share capital. All issued shares must be fully subscribed and fully paid up in accordance with Article L.227-2. There is no statutory requirement for a bank escrow account for cash contributions, but capital contributions must be effectively available and supported by a certificate of deposit (attestation de blocage / attestation de dépôt des fonds) when the bank is not opened with a notary.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) — French national tax authority; primary portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate online portal: www.gdfs.fr; company registry with SIREN/SIRET attribution: INSEE via the Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); dedicated VAT registration platform for non-residents: DGFIP Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity establishing a French SAS: (1) file statutory incorporation documentation (statutes, proof of registered office, identity of legal representatives, confirmation of no minimum share capital) via the Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises; (2) the CFE forwards the dossier to the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) for issuance of the SIREN company number and SIRET establishment number; (3) DGFIP automatically allocates a French VAT identification number (intra-community FR TVA number) and the standard corporate taxpayer reference; (4) the SAS must file a 'déclaration d'existence' (form M0 variant) within one month of incorporation and register for Corporation Tax (IS) and VAT with the local Entreprise (SIE) in the competent territorial department; (5) for non-resident companies acting directly without a local establishment, direct VAT registration before the SIEE is required using the simplified 'cerfa n°13670' procedure, with documentary proof of economic activity in France; (6) all tax returns and payments must be lodged through the DGFIP professional messaging service 'Espace Professionnel Securisé'.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SAS is a corporation resident in France subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) on worldwide profits, with territorial scope limited to profits attributable to French establishments and activities actually carried out in France; the commercial-law autonomy of the SAS does not alter its tax transparency status; it is a separate taxpayer from its shareholders. — For a foreign entity establishing a French SAS: (1) file statutory incorporation documentation (statutes, proof of registered office, identity of legal representatives, confirmation of no minimum share capital) via the Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises; (2) the CFE forwards the dossier to the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) for issuance of the SIREN company number and SIRET establishment number; (3) DGFIP automatically allocates a French VAT identification number (intra-community FR TVA number) and the standard corporate taxpayer reference; (4) the SAS must file a 'déclaration d'existence' (form M0 variant) within one month of incorporation and register for Corporation Tax (IS) and VAT with the local Entreprise (SIE) in the competent territorial department; (5) for non-resident companies acting directly without a local establishment, direct VAT registration before the SIEE is required using the simplified 'cerfa n°13670' procedure, with documentary proof of economic activity in France; (6) all tax returns and payments must be lodged through the DGFIP professional messaging service 'Espace Professionnel Securisé'.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SAS is a corporation resident in France subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) on worldwide profits, with territorial scope limited to profits attributable to French establishments and activities actually carried out in France; the commercial-law autonomy of the SAS does not alter its tax transparency status; it is a separate taxpayer from its shareholders.

Available election forms

The SAS may not elect partnership-style pass-through taxation, but French law provides the following elections: (1) optional IR (Impôt sur le Revenu) income-tax transparency for SAS/SARL family companies with fewer than 50 shareholders under article 239 bis AA CGI for a maximum of 5 fiscal years; (2) election for parent-subsidiary group integration ('régime d'intégration fiscale') under article 223 A CGI, allowing the parent SAS to consolidate the results of at least 95%-owned French subsidiaries; (3) election for the group VAT regime (option de groupe TVA) under article 1693 ter CGI, permitting a single VAT taxpayer group; (4) no US-style 'check-the-box' election exists, but anti-hybrid mismatch rules under articles 212 bis A through 212 bis H CGI (ATAD implementation) re-characterize disregarded entities and permanent establishments with no elective effect for entity classification; (5) R&D tax credit ('crédit d'impôt recherche') and reduced-tier IS rates are statutory not elective; (6) fiscal year can be elected to depart from the calendar year but must be declared to DGFIP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The French domestic small-business TVA exemption (franchise en base) applies to taxable turnover of EUR 85,000 for supply of goods (including manufacturing and distance sales) and EUR 37,500 for supply of services, but manufacture and intra-Community acquisitions of robotic floor-cleaning appliances require mandatory registration if the EU-wide distance-selling threshold of EUR 10,000 (annual cross-border B2C distance sales) is exceeded; any foreign supplier holding stock in France or using a French fulfilment warehouse is subject to mandatory French VAT registration from the first euro regardless of turnover; non-EU suppliers with goods sold via a marketplace may be registered under the non-union OSS scheme or subject to deemed-supplier rules under article 283 CGI (effectively no minimum turnover threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating under article 262 CGI applies to supplies of goods that are physically transported outside the EU by the seller or on behalf of the seller, provided the seller retains documentary proof of the export, including the customs export declaration and transport documents evidencing departure of the goods from French customs territory within the statutory deadline; intra-Community supplies of goods (articles 262 ter CGI, 258 A CGI) are equally zero-rated when the purchaser communicates a valid EU VAT identification number and the goods are dispatched to another Member State; supply of offshore services (including assembly, repair, software and technical consulting relating to robotic appliances for non-EU clients) falls under the B2B reverse-charge rule of article 283-2 CGI and is zero-rated when the customer is identified for VAT in another country; export VAT zero-rating may additionally benefit from the 'export cash-flow scheme' set out in article 262 CGI for exporters with regular operations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane, DAU/ENS, including the electronic summary export declaration in DELTA-G or DES), Bill of Lading (Connaissement) or CMR consignment note or Air Waybill, Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation de rapatriement des devises / bank credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency payment on French territory)

France is implementing a mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting regime under the Finance Act for 2024 (article 279 LPF), as restructured by the ordinance of 17 September 2024, with phased entry into force: mandatory e-invoicing issuance and receipt for large enterprises from 1 September 2026, and for mid-size and small enterprises (including SAS manufacturers) from 1 September 2027; all invoices must be exchanged through one of the Government-approved 'Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires' (PDP) or through the public portal 'Portail Public de Facturation' (PPF / Chorus Pro); each invoice must transmit structured business data and tax data (FACTUR-X, UBL 2.x, CII formats) to the PPF, which performs real-time fiscal clearance, validation and circuit transmission; e-reporting of transaction and payment data (outside the invoicing chain) is mandatory in parallel; invoices must additionally conform to French authentication and archiving requirements (article 289 CGI), and digital stamping is not generally required for VAT receipts in France, but the PPF automatically validates the invoicer's TVA status and SIREN.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to heightened DGFIP audit scrutiny through cross-referencing of the export file, the customs export summary and the incoming foreign-currency remittance; if the exporter cannot produce the statutory proof bundle, DGFIP re-characterises the supply as domestic, assesses the full 20% VAT plus late-interest (intérêt de retard at 0.40% per calendar month under article 1727 CGI) and applies the intentional-misconduct penalty of 40% (article 1729 CGI) or the concealment-of-activity penalty of 80% in case of dissimulated export fictions; fraudulent arrangement to obtain unjustified export VAT refunds or zero-rating invokes article 1788 A CGI penalty of EUR 750 per false document, the abuse-of-law procedure (abus de droit, article L.64 LPF) allowing assessment on the actual economic substance, solidary liability of the exporter and the fiscal representative under article 283 CGI, and a potential criminal fine of EUR 37,500 plus five years' imprisonment under articles 1741-1743 CGI for fraudulent VAT evasion; refunds already paid are retrospectively clawed back with penalties for up to six years (or ten years in case of offshore concealment).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million in annual turnover or total balance sheet plus more than 100 employees triggers the mandatory French Local File; the Master File becomes mandatory when the worldwide consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750 million. During audit, the DGFIP may demand local transactions documentation for French related-party flows exceeding EUR 100,000 even if the full documentation threshold is not met.

Economic substance scope: France applies no autonomous Economic Substance Regulations comparable to United Arab Emirates or Bermuda; however, the French abuse-of-law doctrine (Article L64 of the LPF), EU anti-hybrid rules, CFC legislation and BEPS Action 6 principal-purpose test require real offices, employees, business equipment and management decisions in France to support low-risk distributor or contract-manufacturer characterisation and to secure treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Article 1741 CGII)

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties of 40% for serious understatement, increasing to 80% for abusive or concealed operations, plus late-payment interest at 0.20% monthly. Criminal sanctions: up to five years' imprisonment and EUR 250,000 fine for de jure and de facto directors; aggravated fraud committed in an organised group or online raises the sanctions to seven years' imprisonment and EUR 500,000 fine.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SAS president who instigate or conceal fraud may be declared jointly and severally liable for corporate income tax, penalties and interest; judicial travel bans, asset seizure, confiscation and imprisonment are available, with referral to the Comité national de lutte contre la fraude for criminal prosecution.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law (Article L64 LPF)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the arrangement as an abuse of law, 80% penalty on the additional tax resulting from the concealed purpose, statutory interest, and denial of treaty benefits; transactions are re-taxed as if the artificial entity did not exist, including additional withholding tax on undisclosed profit distributions.

Director personal liability: The de facto or de jure directors who established the artificial shell are jointly and severally liable for the recomputed tax, withholding tax, penalties and interest; criminal courts may impose fines, travel bans, confiscation and imprisonment where the shell is used to facilitate tax fraud or money laundering.
Money Laundering / Tax Fraud Proceeds (Article 324-1 Code Pénal)

Statutory sanction: For natural persons: up to five years' imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine; for legal persons: a corporate fine equal to five times the statutory natural-person fine, together with confiscation of moveable and immovable property; failure to comply with suspicious transaction reporting obligations may additionally trigger financial-administrative sanctions and TRACFIN enforcement.

Director personal liability: A president or compliance manager who knowingly participates in, directs or facilitates the laundering scheme may be prosecuted as principal or accomplice, with personal imprisonment, fines, confiscation, disqualification from managing companies, travel bans during investigation, and civil restitution to the State for taxes and penalties evaded.
