Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies; EUR 1 is legally sufficient, subject to adequate capitalization. For cash contributions, at least 20% must be paid upon incorporation and the balance within five years. Paid-in cash is normally deposited in a blocked bank or notarial account, and the deposit certificate is filed for registration. The incorporation filing fee excludes legal-notice publication, beneficial-owner filing, bank charges, and professional fees. The USD amount is an indicative conversion of the applicable commercial registry fee and is exchange-rate dependent.

Funding & unlock path: No general pre-opening industrial clearance applies to a virtual retail business. A public-facing premises may be an établissement recevant du public and require municipal fire-safety and accessibility compliance, with commission review where applicable. Any warehouse or workshop must satisfy fire-prevention, emergency-access, electrical, structural-load, machinery, and occupational-health requirements. The employer must prepare and maintain the Document unique d'évaluation des risques professionnels, provide required training and protective measures, and complete workplace inspections or certifications required by the premises and equipment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – foreign business desk: Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), Noisy-le-Grand; portal: impots.gouv.fr / portailpro.gouv.fr. Business formalities and VAT registration are routed through the Guichet unique formalités (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) and the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE).

E-government filing portal

A French SARL receives a SIREN/SIRET number automatically upon incorporation, along with a VAT intra-community number (FR + SIREN-based key) from DGFiP. For a foreign entity establishing a branch or selling in France, submit French VAT registration (form 1547 – 'procédure simplifiée de régularisation') to the SIEE, attaching proof of existence, original power of attorney, and a projection of French turnover. Non-resident businesses have a zero registration threshold: VAT registration is required from the first taxable supply in France, unless the supplies are covered by the EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) regime for distance sales.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A French SARL receives a SIREN/SIRET number automatically upon incorporation, along with a VAT intra-community number (FR + SIREN-based key) from DGFiP. For a foreign entity establishing a branch or selling in France, submit French VAT registration (form 1547 – 'procédure simplifiée de régularisation') to the SIEE, attaching proof of existence, original power of attorney, and a projection of French turnover. Non-resident businesses have a zero registration threshold: VAT registration is required from the first taxable supply in France, unless the supplies are covered by the EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) regime for distance sales.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a 'check-the-box' regime. A small SARL may elect income-tax transparency (imposition à l'impôt sur le revenu – IR) for up to five fiscal years under CGI 239 bis AB, subject to turnover and ownership ceilings. No other elective hybrid classification exists. France applies ATTAD/hybrid mismatch adjustment rules under CGI 212 bis, which are mandatory and do not constitute an election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business 'franchise en base' exemption: EUR 85,800 annual taxable turnover for goods (retail POD apparel) and EUR 37,500 for services; figures apply to established French entities. Non-established suppliers have a zero threshold – registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply unless OSS or an EU import scheme applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rate VAT applies to goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the seller within three months of the transfer of ownership or dispatch. Qualifying conditions: the exporter holds a customs export declaration (DAE) accepted in DELTA-G, transport documents proving departure from the EU (e.g., Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR), a commercial invoice, and a signed purchase contract/payment record. For POD custom apparel, the SARL may zero-rate the supply if it directly exports the finished articles to a non-EU consumer or business. Electronically supplied services to non-EU consumers are outside the scope of French VAT rather than zero-rated. Export evidence must be retained for at least ten years (CGI art. 286-3).

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE / DELTA-G) with EX-EAES / ETS acceptance number
  • Bill of Lading (original sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing physical removal from the EU
  • Commercial invoice / sales contract and payment receipt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation de rapatriement de recettes d'exportation) required by French foreign-exchange controls where export proceeds exceed EUR 12,500 or the 180-day repatriation deadline applies

Mandatory French e-invoicing reform deployed in stages: from 1 September 2026, large and medium enterprises must issue structured electronic invoices (FACTUR-X / Peppol BIS) via an approved PDP or the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); from 1 September 2027, all SMEs including SARL must receive and issue e-invoices. Concurrently, transaction data must be transmitted to DGFiP through the PPF for audit and VAT-filing purposes. Retail POD B2C transactions require NF 525-compliant cash-register software or invoicing system; no real-time fiscal stamping applies to sales invoices, but customs export declarations must be filed electronically using the DELTA-G clearance system.

If export proof is incomplete or false, DGFiP recovers the unpaid VAT at the standard 20% rate plus late-interest at 0.05% per month and raises penalties: up to 80% of the tax under bad faith (mauvaise foi) or 100% for organised dissimulation (manœuvre frauduleuse). Customs authorities may impose parallel fines and seize goods. Criminal liability for customs or VAT export fraud reaches fines up to EUR 500,000 and ten years' imprisonment for organised fraud. The ordinary reassessment period is six years; in cases of tax abuse (abus de droit) the clawback window can extend to ten years. Export records must be made available for electronic audit upon DGFiP demand.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full euro convertibility under EU free movement of capital. Both current-account transactions (trade in goods/services, royalties, intra-group fees) and capital-account transactions (equity subscription, shareholder loans, dividend remittances) are unrestricted. There are no administrative FX approvals for profit remittances; the only constraints arise from corporate law solvency tests, beneficial-ownership disclosure, tax withholding/reporting, and EU/UN sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender regime exists for French exporters. Export receivables in convertible currencies may be kept in foreign-currency accounts or converted at freely negotiated rates without any compulsory timeline or conversion ratio. Export proceeds must be recorded in Banque de France balance-of-payments statistics when individual transaction thresholds are met, but no repatriation certificate or government consent is required.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France; foreign exchange administration and balance-of-payments oversight: Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor), Ministry of Economic, Financial and Industrial Sovereignty

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for EU/EEA-authorized e-money or payment institutions (EMIs/PIs) passporting into France under PSD2 with an ACPR-registered agent; Low-to-Feasible only via partner bank sponsorship for offshore/non-EEA EMIs. An offshore digital EMI cannot directly open a French IBAN or settle payment services for a French SARL; it may process retail card payouts, but the SARL should maintain its primary settlement account at an ACPR-licensed bank or a passported EU EMI to avoid treasury isolation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Eligible small companies may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to French ownership, turnover, and paid-in-capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident, subject to applicable treaty relief, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief, and anti-abuse conditions. French resident individual shareholders may be subject to a separate personal tax regime rather than the non-resident dividend withholding regime.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the fiscal year. For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the ordinary deadline is the second business day after 1 May of the following year. Payment of the final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end. Electronic filing is mandatory for French companies subject to corporate income tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer-pricing documentation, including Local File and Master File obligations for qualifying entities or French groups, generally applies where annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets reach at least EUR 400 million, or where the entity is controlled by or controls an enterprise meeting the EUR 400 million threshold. A separate simplified transfer-pricing declaration generally applies from the EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, subject to statutory conditions and current filing rules.

Economic substance scope: France has no single standalone economic-substance regulation equivalent to some offshore ESR regimes. Substance is enforced through the arm's-length principle, abuse-of-law doctrine, principal-purpose testing, beneficial-ownership rules, controlled foreign company rules, permanent-establishment principles, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation, and general deductibility requirements. A French SARL should maintain French decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or documented operational infrastructure, accounting records, commercial rationale, inventory and fulfillment evidence, and board and management records proportionate to its POD retail functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, 40% surcharge for deliberate conduct, up to 80% surcharge for fraudulent maneuvers or abusive concealment, default interest at 0.20% per month, and potential criminal tax-fraud penalties of up to five years' imprisonment and EUR 500,000 fine. Aggravated cases can reach seven years' imprisonment and EUR 3 million fine, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur criminal liability where they personally participated in the conduct. Courts may impose civil restitution, damages, asset seizure, management bans, and other judicial restrictions. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from judicial-control measures or criminal proceedings. A court may impose director liability for unpaid tax where serious and repeated management misconduct caused the company's tax insolvency.
VAT evasion and carousel-fraud exposure

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, interest, penalties generally ranging from 10% to 80% depending on conduct, denial of input-tax recovery, joint-liability measures in qualifying cases, and criminal prosecution for organized or fraudulent conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for intentional participation and may face fines, imprisonment in aggravated cases, asset seizure, management prohibition, and civil contribution claims. Travel bans are case-specific and not an automatic consequence.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Income-tax reassessment, withholding-tax exposure, interest, 10% documentation-related penalty where applicable, 40% or 80% tax penalties for intentional or fraudulent conduct, and possible denial of deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for deliberate misstatements or fraudulent documentation. Civil restitution, management bans, and criminal sanctions may apply where conduct constitutes tax fraud or falsification.
Shell company and artificial substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or directive benefits, reallocation of profits, permanent-establishment findings, tax reassessment, abuse-of-law penalties up to 80%, withholding-tax recovery, and potential criminal referral where documents or declarations are fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for fraudulent implementation, asset diversion, or serious management misconduct. Civil claims, asset seizure, management bans, and imprisonment may apply where separate criminal offenses are established. Travel restrictions require judicial action.
Beneficial-ownership and anti-money-laundering failure

Statutory sanction: Corporate and individual administrative or criminal sanctions, beneficial-owner registration enforcement, account restrictions, confiscation, AML fines, and potential imprisonment depending on the underlying offense.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally liable for knowing participation, concealment, or failure to implement required controls. Asset confiscation, civil restitution, management prohibition, judicial-control travel restrictions, and imprisonment may apply according to the offense.
False invoicing and payment-platform concealment

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, VAT and corporate-tax penalties, withholding-tax exposure, bookkeeping sanctions, potential seizure of records and funds, and criminal fraud or forgery penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face criminal liability, civil restitution, asset seizure, management bans, and imprisonment where fraud, forgery, or money laundering is proven.
Undisclosed related-party financing and profit extraction

Statutory sanction: Interest limitation, recharacterization as distributions, withholding-tax recovery, corporate-tax reassessment, penalties, and potential application of anti-abuse and non-cooperative-jurisdiction rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for misuse of corporate assets, fraudulent bankruptcy conduct, or deliberate tax evasion. Civil restitution, asset seizure, management bans, and imprisonment may apply to proven criminal conduct.
Failure to preserve accounting and tax records

Statutory sanction: Estimated assessment, rejection of deductions and input VAT, penalties for incomplete or inaccurate declarations, audit extensions, and potential criminal consequences for concealment or falsification.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally responsible for intentional destruction or falsification of records and may face civil claims, management bans, asset seizure, and imprisonment where a criminal offense is established.
Unlawful distribution or insolvency misconduct

Statutory sanction: Dividend recovery, creditor claims, insolvency proceedings, director contribution to liabilities, asset-recovery actions, management bans, and potential bankruptcy-related criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to contribute to all or part of the company's liabilities where serious management misconduct caused insufficiency of assets. Fraudulent bankruptcy may result in criminal fines, imprisonment, asset seizure, and judicial management prohibition. Travel restrictions are discretionary and case-specific.
