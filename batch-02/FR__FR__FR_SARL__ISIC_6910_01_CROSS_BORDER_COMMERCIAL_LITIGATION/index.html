Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital for SARL; share capital is fixed freely by the articles (commonly €1). Any subscribed capital exceeding €0 must be fully paid up at subscription and, if in cash, lodged in a blocked escrow account with a French bank, notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts; the capital is released upon registration of the company with the RCS.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for premises receiving the public (ERP) issued by the Commission de Sécurité if the capacity exceeds 100 persons or as imposed by prefectural orders; structure must comply with the French Construction and Housing Code load-resistance requirements; workplace clearance includes ergonomic inspection and verification of electrical installations and work equipment conforming to the Labour Code; disabled-access conformity is mandatory and subject to favorable opinion by the accessibility commission.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum capital for SARL; share capital is fixed freely by the articles (commonly €1). Any subscribed capital exceeding €0 must be fully paid up at subscription and, if in cash, lodged in a blocked escrow account with a French bank, notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts; the capital is released upon registration of the company with the RCS.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the impots.gouv.fr portal; business entity registration files are processed through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) single digital window (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) which assigns the SIREN/SIRET and triggers automatic VAT registration with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE).

E-government filing portal

1) File incorporation dossier (M0/statuts/Kbis) with the INPI CFE portal to obtain SIREN, SIRET, and Code APE 6910Z; the DGFiP then automatically assigns the French intra-Community VAT number (format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN) and publishes it on the EORI/VIES registries. 2) For a foreign entity establishing a SARL branch or permanent establishment without separate incorporation, direct registration via form 1478-SD and a request to the SIE is required. 3) Obtain the professional license for litigation chambers (Ordre des Avocats / Bâtonnier) and the CARPA trust authorization, which is a regulatory prerequisite to operate the IOLTA_Trust_Account payment rail. 4) Validate the VAT ID through the VIES system and register for the French e-invoicing platform (Portail Public de Facturation) within the statutory delay. 5) Appoint a tax representative if invoicing French VAT when the SARL elects the non-resident simplified regime (not applicable to a resident SARL).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A resident SARL is mandatorily subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at the standard rate of 25% (reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs meeting the statutory size tests). The pass-through fiscal transparency regime (Impôt sur le Revenu under Article 239 bis AA CGI) is available only by election for small SARLs (fewer than 50 employees, annual turnover below EUR 10,000,000, shares held by individuals), and even when elected, the entity remains subject to VAT at entity level. Territorial scope: France has unitary national taxation; no French tax-exempt zone applies to a commercial litigation SARL, and the General Tax Code excludes legal services from the franchise en base de TVA exemption.

Available election forms

1) Pass-through election under Article 239 bis AA CGI for small SARLs eligible to be taxed as IR-transparent; option is filed with the annual return and is irrevocable for five fiscal years. 2) Election for IS taxation under Article 206 CGI for SARLs initially outside the IS scope (rare; not applicable where the entity is IS by default). 3) VAT grouping election under Article 256 C ter CGI allowing closely linked French entities to form a single VAT person (assujetti unique) with one VAT number and consolidated intragroup reporting. 4) ATAD 2 hybrid mismatch rules (Articles 209 B and 212 bis CGI) are mandatory, not elective; there is no US-style check-the-box election, and a French SARL cannot unilaterally reclassify itself to exploit hybrid mismatches. 5) Optional 3% payroll tax and CVAE/CFE local assessments elections are not relevant to tax identity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for legal and litigation services: VAT registration is mandatory from the first French taxable transaction because legal services are expressly excluded from the franchise en base de TVA (Article 293 B CGI). The general small-enterprise VAT exemption thresholds of EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services do not apply to this activity. For non-resident suppliers, the EU OSS distance-sales threshold of EUR 10,000 per calendar year applies only to B2C e-services/goods, not to B2B litigation services, which trigger reverse-charge VAT treatment in the customer's jurisdiction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border commercial litigation and arbitration services are zero-rated for French VAT on the export of services when the place of supply is located outside France under Article 259-1 CGI (B2B general rule: services supplied to a customer established in a third country are deemed supplied outside the EU and therefore outside the scope of French VAT, allowing 0% VAT invoicing with full input VAT recovery). Services to EU business clients are addressed by reverse charge (autoliquidation) in the client's member state, not by French zero-rating. Zero-rating is denied when: (i) the service relates to immovable property situated in France, (ii) the service is physically performed in France for a private individual (B2C), (iii) the client's French fixed establishment is the recipient, or (iv) the litigation exclusively concerns domestic French proceedings without an export substance; in such cases the standard 20% French VAT must be charged.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU - Document Administratif Unique) transmitted electronically via the DELTA export system evidencing the physical exit from EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (connaissement) or equivalent transport document (CMR consignment note, air waybill, or filed export manifest) proving the cross-border movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate confirming the collection of export proceeds and foreign exchange repatriation
  • Client contract and engagement letter establishing the beneficial recipient's place of establishment outside France
  • Attestation de résidence fiscale / non-EU taxable person declaration (or VIES validation screenprint for EU clients) evidencing the client's status
  • Fee invoice endorsed with the statutory exoneration mention 'Article 259-1 CGI - Exportation de services'

Mandatory structured e-invoicing is being phased in under the Loi de Finances 2023. All French B2B invoices must be issued and transmitted electronically through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP). Compliance dates: 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises (obligation to receive and issue), and 1 September 2027 for all SMEs (including the SARL). Accepted formats are Factur-X (EN 16931 hybrid), UBL, and CII over the PEPPOL network. Data completion is mandatory in real time for e-reporting of B2C and international transactions, including export zero-rated supplies. No digital stamping is imposed; the system relies on continuous electronic clearance of transactional data to DGFiP. Non-compliance penalties under Article 1737 CGI range from EUR 15 to EUR 15,000 per invoice, with graduated sanctions for failure to issue, transmit, or include mandatory fields.

The DGFiP audits export zero-rating claims with strict economic-reality testing under Article 283-1 CGI, reviewing the actual establishment of the client, the delivery of the legal service, and the provenance of the remittance. On a successful challenge, the tax authority retroactively re-subjects the supplies to 20% French VAT with interest for delay (intérêts de retard at 0.20% per month). Statutory penalties apply under Article 1729 CGI: 40% for material inaccuracy or misstatement (insuffisance de déclaration) and 80% for fraudulent maneuvers or fictitious export schemes; input VAT credits claimed on related costs are simultaneously rejected under Article 1731 CGI. Criminal liability under Article 1741 CGI exposes directors to a fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years for tax fraud. The abuse of law procedure (Article L.64 LPF) permits the administration to ignore sham export structuring, and in cases of fraud the clawback period extends to 12 years (Article L.186 LPF).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a Eurozone member, applies full current- and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Articles L151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier). There is no prior authorisation, no quantitative restriction, and no dual-rate exchange regime for euro conversions. Profit remittance for a French SARL is unrestricted after the ordinary shareholders' meeting, subject to (i) corporate income tax at 25%; (ii) the 12.8% dividend withholding tax reduced by treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; (iii) the 17.2% social levies under the single flat-tax regime of Article 200A CGI; and (iv) the standard financial-reporting formalities. The only practical hurdles are the mandatory Banque de France statistical declaration for any cross-border transfer exceeding EUR 12,500 and compliance with EU/OFAC financial sanctions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline. Eurozone membership and EU capital-movement liberalisation preclude any surrender or conversion obligation. The residual statutory requirement is purely statistical: every cross-border receipt (including legal-service export fees, settlement awards, and reimbursements of disbursements) exceeding EUR 12,500 must be declared to the Banque de France DRFI within 20 days after the end of the month of receipt. Export proceeds may be retained in the invoicing currency or freely converted at the market rate.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting with the Direction Générale du Trésor of the Ministry of Economy and Finance for exchange-control policy and foreign-investment screening; prudential supervision is delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution); balance-of-payments statistics are administered by the Banque de France Direction des Relations Financières Internationales (DRFI); monetary policy and Eurosystem settlement are exercised through the European Central Bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for client-fund escrow and regulated litigation deposits, MODERATE for operational disbursement accounts. French AML law and the statutory-licence obligations require IOLTA/CARPA-type client money to remain at a fully ACPR-supervised credit institution. A fintech may operate lawfully in France only under a French credit-institution licence or an EEA passport under PSD2 (payment institution) or the E-Money Directive; offshore digital EMIs without a French/EEA branch or authorised agent cannot hold client funds, provide French IBAN-clearing, or substitute for the regulated escrow account. Qualifying French/EEA fintechs such as Qonto, Treezor, or Mangopay are acceptable for operational expense flows but must not be used for the segregated litigation-client account.

Defense evidence: Evidence bundle: (1) detailed memoranda of fees (mémoires d'honoraires) and disbursement schedules (débours et frais de justice); (2) the client mandate letters and case references; (3) the segregated escrow ledger and account-reconciliation report; (4) a cash-flow forecast and staged disbursement plan approved by the gérant; (5) the firm's annual financial statements demonstrating economic proportionality; (6) copies of the underlying Court orders or arbitral directions supporting each payment.
Crypto-currency or unregulated payment rails: a client proposes settlement or fee payment through digital-asset exchanges, unlicensed money-service businesses, or non-bank value-transfer channels.

Defense evidence: Evidence bundle: (1) an internal anti-crypto/AML policy statement signed by the compliance officer; (2) proof of DASP/PSAN registration with the AMF and ACPR if the firm offers digital-asset services under the PACTE law, or evidence of refusal of crypto receipts where no registration exists; (3) the client's fiat-bank source-of-funds history demonstrating the legitimate origin of any converted amounts; (4) procedures requiring digital-asset conversion solely through a French/EEA-regulated service provider with fiat proceeds moved strictly into the regulated escrow account; (5) the bank's written acceptance of the policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a non-resident shareholder's capital increase, shareholder loan, or working-capital injection: route via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA credit transfer for EUR) to the SARL's operational corporate account using the French IBAN format FR76..., the bank BIC, a UETR/end-to-end reference, and remittance narrative 'CAPITAL INJECTION / APPORT EN COMPTE COURANT / [SARL NAME]'. For a formal SARL capital increase the funds must first pass through a blocked account held with the bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and remain immobilised until the statutory increase formalities are completed; the wire must be accompanied by the gérant's attestation of contribution (attestation de versement) or a notarial certificate. The transaction is exempt from prior FX authorisation, but the bank will apply the EU statistical purpose code for capital transfers and the SARL must ensure the Banque de France DRFI balance-of-payments declaration is filed for every transfer exceeding EUR 12,500; OFAC/EU sanctions screening of the sender applies.Outward litigation disbursements use the following clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for EUR payments to EEA-based courts, experts, bailiffs, and counsel, using IBAN-to-IBAN instructions with the invoice and case reference; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border USD, GBP, or CNH payments to foreign counsel, experts, and settlement counterparties, with UETR and a purpose narrative 'LEGAL PROFESSIONAL FEES / LEGAL DISBURSEMENTS / SETTLEMENT'; banks require the underlying invoice, the retainer agreement, and the Court or arbitral order where amounts are atypical; (iii) commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) and standby letters of credit (SWIFT MT760) where a Court-ordered escrow or settlement guarantee so requires; (iv) TARGET2/RTGS for high-value euro transactions. Each outward transfer above EUR 12,500 is captured in the Banque de France BOP declaration, and the SARL must maintain a payment-support folder (facture, convention d'honoraires, bordereau de créance, judgment) for audit; a Tracfin déclaration de soupçon is mandatory where laundering indicators are present.Plain-vanilla foreign-exchange forwards (change à terme) with the ACPR-licensed credit institution, documented under a standard ISDA or French Federation FX master agreement and limited to genuine commercial or escrow exposuresFX swaps (swap de change) for rolling short-dated liquidity and tenor management of multi-currency client-fund balancesCross-currency swaps (currency basis swaps) for long-dated foreign-currency escrow positions, subject to EMIR reporting to an EU trade repository and margin requirements where a financial counterparty is involvedPurchased vanilla FX options (call/put, no naked short structures) in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, or EUR/CHF for settlement contingencies in cross-border litigationNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only for non-deliverable or offshore currencies (e.g., offshore RMB/CNH, and certain emerging-market currencies) where the underlying litigation receipt is denominated in that currency; executed through the bank's FX desk under a 1998 ISDA FX Novation/NDF confirmation, with on-demand evidence of hedging capacity provided to the bank's risk department

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for client-fund escrow and regulated litigation deposits, MODERATE for operational disbursement accounts. French AML law and the statutory-licence obligations require IOLTA/CARPA-type client money to remain at a fully ACPR-supervised credit institution. A fintech may operate lawfully in France only under a French credit-institution licence or an EEA passport under PSD2 (payment institution) or the E-Money Directive; offshore digital EMIs without a French/EEA branch or authorised agent cannot hold client funds, provide French IBAN-clearing, or substitute for the regulated escrow account. Qualifying French/EEA fintechs such as Qonto, Treezor, or Mangopay are acceptable for operational expense flows but must not be used for the segregated litigation-client account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (IS) rate for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2022; 15% reduced rate on the first EUR 38,120 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs with turnover below EUR 10 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% French-source dividend withholding tax for non-resident recipients (Prélèvement Forfaitaire Unique); 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying corporate shareholders holding at least 5% for 24 months; lower treaty rates may apply.

For 31 December fiscal year-end, file by the second business day following 1 May; for off-calendar year-ends, file within three months after the close of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 3 million in intra-group transactions triggers the French Local File drawing obligation; consolidated group turnover or total assets of EUR 400 million triggers Master File and Local File preparation; Form 2257-SD transfer pricing declaration filing applies from the same thresholds.

Economic substance scope: ESR in the Gulf/OECD sense is not directly applicable in France; however, French law requires genuine economic substance as a condition for treaty and EU directive benefits through Article L.64 LPF (abus de droit), Article 1655 bis CGI, and OECD BEPS Principal Purpose Test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Corporation tax reassessment plus 80% abuse-of-law penalty (CGI Art. 1729); criminal fine up to EUR 75,000 and imprisonment up to five years (Art. 1741), increased to EUR 1,000,000 and ten years for aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held civilly liable for corporate tax debts under the action en comblement du passif; criminal courts may impose travel bans, asset confiscation and imprisonment; French courts pierce the veil where the SARL is a mere instrumentality.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under Art. L.64 LPF with 80% penalty; refusal of treaty reduced withholding; cancellation of corporate registration; criminal exposure for fake corporate seat.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution of misused corporate assets; personal bankruptcy (faillite personnelle), travel bans and director disqualification; creditors may seize personal property of shadow managers.
AML / Client Trust Account Misuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Monetary and Financial Code, fines up to EUR 15,000 for individuals and EUR 1,000,000 for legal persons; criminal imprisonment up to five years; Bar Council disciplinary withdrawal of license.

Director personal liability: Managing director personally responsible for segregated client funds; travel ban and asset freeze after criminal indictment; civil liability for conversion of client money and disgorgement of fees.
Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment under Art. 57 CGI with 40% deliberate-misconduct penalty; late interest; administrative fine up to EUR 50,000 under Art. 1735 ter CGI for TP documentation failure.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP certificates face personal assessments to unpaid taxes if reckless mismanagement of the company treasury is shown; criminal penalties for false documentation; travel restrictions in serious fraud investigations.
Beneficial Owner Concealment

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 15,000 and six months imprisonment under Art. L. 561-3-2 CMF for beneficial-ownership non-declaration; automatic increase to AML penalties if concealed owner is connected to money laundering or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Nominee shareholders and directors are jointly liable; civil liability for restitution of hidden profits; travel bans and seizure orders may be issued against both the nominee and the actual beneficial owner.
