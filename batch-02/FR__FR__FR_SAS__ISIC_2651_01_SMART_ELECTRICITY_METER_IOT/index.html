Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registry of the Commercial Court) having territorial jurisdiction over the registered office; all incorporation filings are submitted electronically through the INPI Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), which routes the dossier to the competent Registry.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title II, Chapter VII — Articles L.227-1 to L.227-24 (Société par Actions Simplifiée), Articles L.210-2 and L.210-6 (registration and legal personality), Articles L.225-12 and L.225-13 (share capital payment rules applicable through L.227-2); Book I, Title II, Chapter III (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés, Articles L.123-1 et seq.); Décret n°2007-431 of 25 March 2007; M0 / Cerfa n°14793#03 administrative form.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'une société (M0 form — Cerfa n°14793#03), filed as a fully dematerialized creation dossier via the Guichet Unique national single-window portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for SAS (capital freely fixed in the articles); capital must be fully subscribed and at least 50% of in-cash contributions (numéraire) must be paid up at incorporation and deposited into a blocked escrow account with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the balance must be paid within five years; in-kind contributions are fully paid up and must be valued in the statutory auditor's report (rapport du commissaire aux apports).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and occupational clearance: mandatory inspection and certification by the departmental fire and rescue service (SDIS) with an approved fire safety visit; CONSUEL electrical conformity certificate for the production unit; structural load inspection confirming the building's suitability for electronic assembly, battery storage, and testing benches; workplace health and safety clearance by the labour inspectorate (DREETS) under Labour Code Books IV and V covering emergency exits, first aid, ventilation, personal protective equipment, and the single health and safety risk assessment document (DUERP); the company must maintain on-site the mandatory safety registers and post the employee safety instructions.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for SAS (capital freely fixed in the articles); capital must be fully subscribed and at least 50% of in-cash contributions (numéraire) must be paid up at incorporation and deposited into a blocked escrow account with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the balance must be paid within five years; in-kind contributions are fully paid up and must be valued in the statutory auditor's report (rapport du commissaire aux apports).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), including the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Tax administration portal: impots.gouv.fr. Business formation and registration filings are generally submitted through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the INPI Guichet unique. The filing produces the legal registration and SIREN identifier, with an establishment-level SIRET identifier where applicable. The company requests or confirms its intra-Community VAT identification number through the competent SIE or the applicable online DGFiP procedure. A foreign entity carrying out taxable transactions in France must identify its French VAT obligations, appoint a French tax representative where legally required for a non-EU entity, submit the required registration documentation, and obtain a French VAT number before the relevant taxable transactions or within the applicable statutory period. Typical supporting documents include constitutional documents, foreign tax-registration evidence, proof of business activity, details of the French establishment or taxable operations, identity and authority documents for the representative, and banking information. The French VAT number generally uses the format FR followed by a two-character validation key and the entity's SIREN number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A French SAS is registered through the INPI Guichet unique. The filing produces the legal registration and SIREN identifier, with an establishment-level SIRET identifier where applicable. The company requests or confirms its intra-Community VAT identification number through the competent SIE or the applicable online DGFiP procedure. A foreign entity carrying out taxable transactions in France must identify its French VAT obligations, appoint a French tax representative where legally required for a non-EU entity, submit the required registration documentation, and obtain a French VAT number before the relevant taxable transactions or within the applicable statutory period. The French VAT number generally uses the format FR followed by a two-character validation key and the entity's SIREN number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by default. An eligible SAS may elect corporate income tax treatment to be replaced temporarily by income tax treatment for up to five financial years, subject to statutory conditions concerning ownership, management, employee headcount, turnover or balance-sheet limits, business activity, and shareholder consent. The election must be filed within the applicable deadline with the competent tax authority and is generally irrevocable after the permitted period. France has no general United States-style check-the-box election for SAS entities. Separate elections may apply for VAT regimes, including the franchise in basis, the simplified regime, or the normal regime, subject to statutory eligibility and turnover conditions. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion where arrangements produce deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business, VAT applies based on the nature and location of taxable transactions. The principal franchise-in-basis thresholds are generally EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods, sales for consumption on premises, and accommodation activities, and EUR 37,500 for services and non-commercial activities, subject to statutory increased thresholds and annual legislative adjustments. Exceeding the applicable threshold or voluntarily opting into VAT generally triggers VAT collection and filing obligations. Imports, intra-Community acquisitions, and certain cross-border transactions may create VAT obligations independently of domestic turnover thresholds. A smart-meter manufacturer ordinarily falls within the goods threshold, but installation, maintenance, software, and other services must be analyzed separately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT, provided the exporter can substantiate the export and satisfy the statutory conditions. Required evidence normally includes the customs export declaration, an exit result or equivalent customs confirmation, and commercial and transport records linking the goods to the transaction. Supplies to customers in another EU Member State are generally treated as intra-Community supplies rather than third-country exports and require valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, and appropriate recapitulative reporting. B2B services supplied to a business established outside France may be outside the scope of French VAT under place-of-supply rules, rather than technically zero-rated, subject to exceptions for services connected with French real estate, events, transport, and other special categories. Refund or deduction claims require valid purchase invoices, proper accounting, and a direct connection with taxable transactions.

  • Customs export declaration submitted through the applicable electronic customs system
  • Master Reference Number (MRN) and customs exit confirmation or equivalent proof of exit from the European Union
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and transaction reference
  • Packing list and product description, including serial numbers or model references where relevant
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or other transport document
  • Transport, delivery, freight-forwarder, and logistics records linking the goods to the export declaration
  • Proof of payment or contractual and accounting records supporting the transaction where requested by the tax authority
  • Export sales ledger and VAT return records showing the exempt export with right of deduction
  • Inward remittance evidence only where relevant to a contractual, banking, financing, or audit requirement; France has no general VAT rule requiring an inward remittance certificate for export exemption

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and transaction reporting in phases. All French-established businesses must be capable of receiving compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Issuance obligations apply from 1 September 2026 to large and medium-sized businesses and from 1 September 2027 to small and micro-enterprises, subject to the statutory definitions and any official transition measures. Invoices must generally pass through an approved private platform or the public invoicing platform architecture designated under French rules. France does not generally require real-time fiscal clearance of every domestic B2B invoice. Transaction data and, where applicable, payment data for transactions outside the domestic e-invoicing perimeter must be reported electronically under the e-reporting regime. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. Electronic records must preserve invoice integrity, authenticity, readability, auditability, and required VAT information.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no general requirement to obtain an inward remittance certificate as a condition of VAT exemption. The exporter must retain reliable evidence that the goods left the European Union and that the transaction was correctly reported. If zero-rating or exemption is unsupported, the DGFiP may assess output VAT retroactively, deny or recover related input-VAT deductions or refunds, charge late-payment interest generally at 0.20% per month, and impose tax penalties commonly ranging from 10% for specified late or corrective situations to 40% for deliberate failure and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, depending on the facts and applicable provision. Customs infringements may generate separate duties, VAT, fines, seizure, or other customs measures. Intentional tax fraud may also create criminal exposure, including imprisonment and substantial fines, subject to the applicable aggravated-fraud rules. The taxpayer bears the burden of preserving export and transport evidence and must correct unsupported claims through amended VAT returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions in France. No exchange control restrictions are imposed on trade or investment flows; however, cross-border fund movements must comply with EU customs declarations, economic sanctions, and balance-of-payments reporting obligations. Dividend distributions and shareholder current-account repayments are freely permitted provided corporate formalities are satisfied, distributable reserves are available, and the relevant tax clearance and anti-avoidance declarations are current.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency onshore or offshore in designated FX deposit accounts. All export-related fund flows must nonetheless be declared under EU customs rules and, for resident entities, reportable monetary statistics must be filed with the Banque de France for cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 50,000 per direct investment transaction or EUR 50,000 in capital movements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) and Direction Generale du Tresor (French Treasury) acting as the foreign exchange regulatory authority under the French Monetary and Financial Code

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility of using offshore digital EMI providers is Medium, subject to strict operational limits. An EMI incorporated within the EEA holding an e-money licence may passport into France and provide ancillary payment accounts; however, it cannot replace a tier-1 French commercial bank for treasury functions requiring trade finance, statutorily compliant escrow arrangements, or local LC settlement. Legal constraints include the AMF/BAC-FIN AML reporting framework, PSD2 conduct rules, and the prohibition on using an offshore account without a correspondent banking relationship where settlement is presented in France. Such EMI accounts are therefore viable only for travel/operational expenses, name-discrepant small-value payments, and temporary FX liquidity, not for capital-injection escrow or large-scale LC disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for shareholder capital injection into an SAS: settle EUR via SEPA Credit Transfer or SWIFT MT103; classify the transaction under CMF as 'investissement direct' and use purpose code E (foreign direct investment) or PF; complete the declaration de transfert de fonds (DTF) to Direction Generale des Douanes et Droits Indirects and the Banque de France for equity inflows exceeding EUR 50,000; keep board minutes evidencing capital increase and issue unissued shares concurrently; do not commingle operational receipts with capital injection. For USD inbound payments, pre-clear via a US correspondent bank by providing the ultimate beneficiary's IBAN, SWIFT BIC and the exact purpose wording 'Capital contribution to a French SAS'.Preferred clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 with cover payment for regular supplier disbursements under open-account trading; (2) irrevocable, confirmed commercial letter of credit under UCP 600 and eUCP 2.0 for project-milestone payments to international counterparties; (3) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EUR supplier payments across EEA; (4) SWIFT gpi tracker overlay for real-time cross-border payment transparency. All outbound remittances must carry a mandatory UETR, presend the correct purpose code mapped to the French PEEC/BNC or OECD service classification, and be supported by underlying contract, invoice, and customs documents.
