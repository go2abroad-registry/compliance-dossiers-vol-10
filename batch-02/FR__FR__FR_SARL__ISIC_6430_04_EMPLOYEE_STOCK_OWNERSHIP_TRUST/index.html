Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital is prescribed for a French SARL. The capital is freely fixed in the articles. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and at least 20% paid up at incorporation; the balance must be paid within five years. All cash contribution must be deposited into a blocked bank account with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations or held by a notary until registration, then released to the gérant.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable – no industrial civil-defense clearance or fire-safety certification is required for a pure administrative ESOP trust. The standard workplace safety and fire-prevention rules of the French Code du travail (Articles L.4121-1 et seq.) and local fire safety regulations for tertiary office premises apply, including suitable evacuation and smoke-detection measures.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministry of Economy and Finance; principal online portal: impots.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity without a French permanent establishment must apoint a VAT representative established in France and submit the registration application to the DGFiP via Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) using Cerfa form n° 15903 (corporate tax filing registration) and Cerfa form n° 11263*01 (TVA/VAT registration). The application must provide the foreign entity's certified constitutional documents, proof of economic activity in France or French taxable supplies, and identification of the statutory tax representative. Upon issuance, the DGFiP assigns a SIREN number and an intra-Community TVA identification number (FRXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX), which must be stated on all tax filings and customer invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under French law. SARLs are default corporate taxpayers subject to corporate income tax (IS) under Article 206 of the Code Général des Impôts. A SARL may opt for partnership-style transparency under Article 239 bis AA or Article 239 bis AB only in strictly limited statutory circumstances (e.g., newly created enterprises or SARL holding companies meeting specific conditions), which makes the profits taxable directly in the hands of the individual shareholders. Hybrid mismatch adjustments apply under the ATAD rules as implemented in the CGI (Article 205 A, 209 B and related provisions), limiting deduction of payments that give rise to hybrid mismatches. No available check-the-box election exists for non-resident shareholders to alter SARL classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic TVA franchise thresholds: EUR 85,000 for principal supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for principal supplies of services. Optional simplified reporting applies to small businesses; for intra-Community distance sales and electronic services supplied to non-taxable persons the distance selling threshold is EUR 10,000. Foreign suppliers with taxable supplies in France must register for TVA immediately without entitlement to the franchise below these thresholds when a French taxable nexus exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to exported goods that are physically dispatched outside the European Union, provided the exporter receives proof of departure within 12 months of dispatch (CGI Art. 262-I). Exporters must hold a valid customs export declaration and transport evidence showing exit from the EU. Intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer are also zero-rated where the French supplier states the customer's EU VAT identification number and satisfies Art. 262 ter CGI conditions. Offshore services (B2B) fall outside French VAT jurisdiction when supplied to a business established outside France or when the customer is liable for VAT in the country of establishment; for services supplied to a taxable person established in the EU, the reverse-charge mechanism shifts VAT to the customer. The ESOP trust acting as administrative intermediary must document the specific service or goods flow and retain signed declarations from its foreign clients to support a zero-rated or no-VAT position.

From the 2026-2027 e-invoicing reform, domestic B2B invoices must be issued electronically in Factur-X, UBL, or CII formats and transmitted through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) approved by the DGFiP. Real-time fiscal clearance (pre-clearance or digital stamping) is not implemented; instead, transaction data must be reported periodically through the PPF in the required E-Report format. Exports continue to require the issuance of an electronic export invoice and may be cleared through the DELTA/Douane processes, but no separate VAT digital stamp applies.

The French tax administration routinely scrutinises export zero-rating claims and requires full documentary support. If export formalities are absent or fraudulent, TVA may be immediately reclaimed from the taxpayer together with interest at 0.20% per month (retard), a 40% penalty for bad-faith failure, and an 80% penalty for abuse or manoeuvres frauduleuses under Article 1729 CGI. Penalties can be raised to 100% in cases of fraudulent collections. Criminal liability under Articles 1741 and 1743 of the CGI follows for false declarations, with penalties of up to EUR 750,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years. The reassessment window is extended from the ordinary 3-year period to 10 years for tax fraud and when the taxpayer is subject to anti-abuse investigation; VAT credit refunds arising from zero-rated exports may be clawed back with retroactive effect plus interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a eurozone member state; the euro is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions. Capital movements within the EEA are unrestricted under EU Treaty liberalisation; transactions with non-EEA jurisdictions are generally free subject to statistical reporting, tax transparency declarations and, in exceptional circumstances, EU safeguard or selective restriction measures. Profit remittance and dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders are not subject to FX conversion approval; however, a French SARL acting as an ESOP trust must evidence the source of funds and the underlying share-plan documents for the bank's compliance assessment. The effective hurdles are AML/KYC and OECD/FATCA reporting, not exchange control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose any mandatory surrender, forced conversion or timeline for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in a designated account or convert to EUR freely. For statistical balance-of-payments purposes, the French resident must report to the Banque de France any cross-border financial transaction with a non-EEA counterparty that exceeds the regulatory de minimis threshold using the monthly External Financial Reporting (EFR/LaREP) channel; the reporting deadline is the 15th business day following the month in which the transaction settles. The appointed French bank typically files this declaration on behalf of the resident for export receipts, thereby removing the necessity for a separate client-side filing.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB); banking supervision and AML/CFT oversight is exercised by the Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR); exchange control and external financial reporting is administered by the Direction Générale du Trésor under the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

For an ESOP trust established as a French SARL, non-French Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) authorised in an EEA state may passport into France under ACPR/EBA rules, but must have a registered agent or branch; offshore EMIs outside the EEA are considered high risk and are not permitted to provide settlement services in EUR or maintain French securities accounts without a full ACPR licence. Feasibility rating: Medium for ACPR or EEA-passported EMIs; Low for non-EEA digital-only providers. Legal constraints include the requirement for a licensed custody or escrow arrangement when holding employee share proceeds, and compliance with Article L.525-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code prohibiting unlicensed money transmission.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound contributions to the ESOP trust's French capital account are routed through the SWIFT network using MT103 customer credit transfers with EU Balance of Payments purpose code G160 for direct investment in equity or G170 for direct investment loans, accompanied by the underlying share-plan or financing agreements. For EU/EEA-origin funds, SEPA Credit Transfers settled on Target2 are acceptable; for non-EEA contributors, the French bank may require an advance foreign-exchange declaration to the Banque de France when the transaction exceeds the regulatory threshold. All inbound flows undergo payment screening, source-of-funds verification and sanctions filtering. Where the contribution forms part of a foreign direct investment in a French company, the additional thresholds and screening of the EU Regulation on Foreign Direct Investment may trigger a notification to the Direction Générale du Trésor if the ESOP trust acquires a controlling interest in a sensitive sector.Outbound operational disbursements and participant distributions are cleared primarily via SEPA payments for EUR-denominated transfers within the EEA, and via SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA beneficiaries; for large banking transfers, MT202 covers interbank settlement. Documentary commercial letters of credit and standby letters of credit may be opened using MT700 messages for cross-border share purchases and trade settlement. The preferred rails are: Target2/SWIFT gpi for traceability, French CVA/SEPA Payroll Payment Scheme for recurring employee distributions, and MT103 in strict SWIFT-compliant format for non-EEA wire transfers. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal year ending 31 December, deadline is the second business day after 1 May; for other fiscal year-ends, within three months of the year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Net profit (bénéfice net) before tax adjustments Report French-source income only; apply treaty provisions if applicable. Late filing penalty: 10% of tax due plus 0.5% monthly interest; additional fixed penalty of €150 for failure to file; criminal sanctions for intentional omission.
Box 2 Net loss (perte nette) before tax adjustments Report French-source losses; carryforward rules apply. Late filing penalty: 10% of tax due plus 0.5% monthly interest; additional fixed penalty of €150 for failure to file; criminal sanctions for intentional omission.
Box 3 Taxable income after deductions and adjustments Include all French-source income; deduct expenses directly related to French operations. Late filing penalty: 10% of tax due plus 0.5% monthly interest; additional fixed penalty of €150 for failure to file; criminal sanctions for intentional omission.
Box 4 Corporate income tax due (25% standard rate or 15% reduced rate for eligible SMEs) Apply reduced rate if turnover < €10 million and profit ≤ €42,500; otherwise standard rate. Late filing penalty: 10% of tax due plus 0.5% monthly interest; additional fixed penalty of €150 for failure to file; criminal sanctions for intentional omission.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400 million (annual turnover or total assets) for full Local File/Master File; simplified documentation required for smaller entities.

Economic substance scope: France applies substance-over-form and General Anti-Abuse Rules (GAAR); no specific ESR regime, but entities must demonstrate real economic activity to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: 40% penalty on understated tax (80% for abuse of right); interest at 0.2% per month; criminal fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for unpaid corporate taxes if found to have participated in fraudulent acts; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, denial of treaty benefits, 40% penalty for abuse of law; potential nullification of tax advantages.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for tax debts if company is deemed fictitious; criminal charges for fraud if intent proven.
AML / Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for entities; criminal penalties for directors up to 5 years imprisonment; confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; travel bans and prohibition from managing companies may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty on additional tax, plus interest; potential double taxation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; criminal sanctions for fraud in extreme cases.
