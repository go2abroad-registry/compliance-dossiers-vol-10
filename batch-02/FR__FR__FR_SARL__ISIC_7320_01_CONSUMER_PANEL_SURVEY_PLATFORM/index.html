Name reservation / lock period

France does not provide a general statutory company-name reservation period equivalent to a 30-day reservation in some other jurisdictions; the value 0 therefore means not applicable. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL has no legally prescribed minimum capital; the shareholders freely fix the capital in the articles of association, subject to a statutory floor of EUR 1. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and are conventionally transferred to a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) at a credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations. The blocking certificate is issued for incorporation, and the funds are released only upon delivery of the Kbis certificate and RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: the registered office, if not open to the public, must maintain at least two clear emergency exits, adequate emergency lighting, manual fire extinguishers and appropriate fire-alarm means; if the premises are open to the public even incidentally, a 5th-category ERP certificate or declaration may be required. Structural load: standard office floor-loading limits apply and no special civil-defense inspection is needed for a data-processing business. Workplace clearance: the employer is required to comply with the Labor Code rules on workplace hygiene, electrical safety verification of IT installations, first-aid equipment, and ergonomic workstations; such compliance is subject to inspection by DREETS and CARSAT at any time.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), operating through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and, for non-established foreign businesses, the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). The principal online portal is impots.gouv.fr, including the professional account and applicable VAT filing and payment services. Corporate legal registration is initiated through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), which transmits registration data to the competent authorities.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL obtains its legal identifiers through the INPI Guichet unique filing. Following registration in the Registre national des entreprises, the entity receives a SIREN number for the legal person and a SIRET number for each establishment. The DGFiP then assigns or confirms the French tax account and corporate income tax filing obligations. A French VAT number is generally issued as an FR prefix followed by a two-digit control key and the nine-digit SIREN number. The SARL must activate its professional account on impots.gouv.fr, identify its competent SIE, submit the applicable corporate tax and VAT registration information, and provide constitutional documents, registered-office evidence, beneficial-owner information where requested, bank details, activity details, and expected taxable turnover. A foreign entity without a French establishment that becomes liable to French VAT must register with the SIEE. An EU-established taxable person generally does not require a French fiscal representative solely because it is non-established in France; a non-EU taxable person generally must appoint a French fiscal representative unless an applicable legal exemption or reciprocal arrangement applies. French VAT registration does not by itself create a permanent establishment for corporate income tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A standard SARL is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS). There is no US-style check-the-box classification election. A SARL may elect corporate income tax where otherwise eligible, and certain qualifying SARLs may elect taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu, or IR). The IR election may be available to a family SARL whose conditions are satisfied, including qualifying family ownership and activity requirements; qualifying newly formed companies may also elect IR for a limited period subject to statutory conditions, including size, age, ownership, and commercial activity requirements. The election is made with the tax administration and is subject to revocation and duration rules. Ordinary SARLs are not automatically fiscally transparent. French anti-hybrid mismatch provisions under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework can deny deductions, include income, or otherwise neutralize arrangements involving hybrid instruments, entities, permanent establishments, or imported mismatches; these rules are not a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 25,000 annual turnover is the principal 2025 franchise-en-base threshold generally relevant to service businesses, subject to the applicable tolerance threshold, activity-specific rules, legislative amendments, and transitional provisions. This threshold is not a universal safe harbor: VAT registration may be required without regard to domestic turnover where the business makes transactions that are not eligible for the French small-business exemption, performs taxable transactions requiring identification, opts for taxation, acquires goods or services under EU rules, or has cross-border transactions subject to reverse-charge or reporting obligations. For B2B services supplied by a French SARL to a business customer established outside France, place-of-supply and reverse-charge rules generally apply rather than a turnover-based exemption. The SARL must monitor its domestic taxable turnover, customer status, place-of-supply analysis, and VAT election status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

French VAT is generally not charged on qualifying exports of goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union when the supplier can prove that the goods physically left the EU customs territory and that the statutory export conditions were satisfied. The supplier must retain a customs export declaration and evidence of exit or equivalent customs confirmation, together with commercial and transport evidence linking the goods to the invoice. Qualifying intra-EU supplies of goods are generally exempt with the right to deduct, rather than technically zero-rated, when the customer is identified for VAT in another Member State, the goods move from France to that Member State, and the supplier holds the required evidence and recapitulative reporting. Services are generally governed by place-of-supply rules: B2B services supplied to a business established outside France are normally outside the scope of French VAT, while qualifying services supplied to EU business customers are generally subject to the reverse charge in the customer's Member State. B2C services, electronically supplied services, intermediary services, and services connected with immovable property, events, transport, or cultural activities require separate place-of-supply analysis. A market-research SaaS platform must distinguish access to software, electronically supplied services, consultancy or research deliverables, advertising, data licensing, and platform intermediation before applying zero-rating, exemption, or reverse-charge treatment.

  • French customs export declaration containing the Movement Reference Number (MRN) or equivalent customs reference
  • Electronic customs exit confirmation or equivalent evidence that the goods exited the European Union customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, VAT treatment, and delivery terms
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, courier record, or freight-forwarder confirmation
  • Packing list and order or contract records where necessary to reconcile the shipment with the invoice
  • Proof of delivery or destination evidence where customs exit evidence alone does not establish the complete transaction chain

France does not currently operate a universal real-time invoice-clearance system for all transactions. The statutory reform introduces mandatory electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions established in France and electronic transaction reporting for relevant B2C and cross-border transactions. From 1 September 2026, all businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices; large enterprises and medium-sized enterprises are generally scheduled to issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from that date. Small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises are generally scheduled to issue them from 1 September 2027, subject to statutory implementation changes. Invoices are to be exchanged through an approved private platform (plateforme agréée), with the public invoicing portal providing the public infrastructure functions specified by law. Required data fields, structured formats, platform routing, authenticity, integrity, archival, and e-reporting obligations apply. The reform does not convert every invoice into an advance tax-clearance requirement. Public-sector e-invoicing through the applicable public procurement platform remains separately governed. The SARL should maintain compliant invoice numbering, customer VAT identification, supply classification, reverse-charge wording, digital audit trails, and retention records.

France has no general foreign-exchange-control rule requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days, and no universal inward-remittance certificate is required to claim export VAT treatment. The supplier must nevertheless prove the export, the customer's status, the place of supply, and the commercial reality of the transaction. If zero-rating, exemption, or out-of-scope treatment is unsupported, the DGFiP may reassess unpaid VAT, deny input VAT recovery, charge late-payment interest, and apply tax penalties. The principal penalty can be 40% for deliberate failure, 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law, and potentially 80% for concealed activity, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards; other penalties may apply for inaccurate returns, missing documentation, failure to report intra-EU transactions, or invoice violations. The tax administration may conduct a multi-year audit and recover VAT retrospectively within the applicable limitation period, generally three years for VAT assessments, subject to extensions, interruptions, fraud, concealed activity, or other statutory exceptions. Criminal prosecution may arise in cases involving deliberate tax fraud, forged customs evidence, false invoices, organized fraud, or other qualifying conduct. Export files should be retained for the statutory accounting and tax retention periods and made readily reconcilable from contract through invoice, customs evidence, transport, payment, and VAT return.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a eurozone member state; the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no administrative exchange controls, no rationing of foreign currency, and no prior approval requirement for profit remittance, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation by a SARL. Statutory restrictions apply only to anti-money laundering documentary verification and balance-of-payments statistical reporting. Direct quarterly balance-of-payments declarations to the Banque de France are required only for transactions individually equal to or above EUR 50 million; all sub-threshold flows are captured via the intermediary bank's FITIS monthly report.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export or service proceeds in France. The SARL may freely retain foreign-currency receipts in domestic or foreign accounts and convert them to euro at any time. Obligations are limited to statistical reporting: any inbound transfer for export/services is transmitted to the Banque de France through the receiving bank's balance-of-payments report, and cash settlements above the EUR 50 million threshold must be declared directly by the company.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the prudential licensing and conduct authority; foreign exchange policy coordination is exercised by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) within the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial Sovereignty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident capital subscription contributions are freely admitted under EU capital liberalisation. Routing is via SWIFT MT103 for non-euro funds or SEPA Credit Transfer / SEPA Instant for euro funds, carrying ISO 20022 purpose code C034 'Investment in equity-capital-like instrument' and reference narrative 'Apport capital SARL FR_SARL + RCS number'. The receiving bank verifies the remitter's identity, UBO consistency, and the registered share capital before releasing the blocked capital escrow account; the Banque de France statistical reporting is discharged by the receiving bank for all amounts below the EUR 50 million direct reporting threshold. For a non-EU shareholder, remittance must be accompanied by the signed share subscription minutes, the Kbis extract, the RBE filing receipt, and a capital declaration to the RCS within one month.Outbound settlements for the market-research platform use a tiered rail: (i) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) up to EUR 100,000 for EU-based panelists and vendors; (ii) SWIFT MT103 with purpose code C039 'Research services' plus contract reference for cross-border panel incentives and researcher fees in non-euro zones; (iii) Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700, advised and confirmed via MT710) for physical goods procurement requiring document compliance; and (iv) batch mass payouts through PayPal Mass Payout API or Wise Business batch payment API, each mapped to the supporting contract and CNIL-compliant beneficiary file.Outright FX Forwards (change à terme) on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP and EUR/CHF with credit-line confirmation from a tier-1 counterparty bankNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for non-deliverable emerging-market currencies (e.g., INR, NGN, VND) where the underlying panel payout exposure cannot be net-settled in EURFX Currency Swaps (swap de change / tom-next rollover) for short-term liquidity pairing in EUR and USDCross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CIRS) for hedging any future external financing in non-euro currencyFX Options and zero-cost collars (cap/floor structures) on EUR/USD quoted by the house bank with a documented hedge policy approved by the SARL gérant

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for operational payment flows via EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions or Payment Institutions (e.g., Wise Europe S.A., PayPal (Europe) S.à r.l., Revolut Business S.A.) under the PSD2 passport. Legal constraint: an EMI cannot hold client funds as deposits, cannot provide credit, and cannot operate a capital escrow account for subscription of SARL shares; therefore the EMI may serve only as a secondary liquidity rail for panel payouts, while the primary treasury and capital accounts must be held with a licensed credit institution. Onboarding for EMIs is fully remote with no physical attendance mandate.
