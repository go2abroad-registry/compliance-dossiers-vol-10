Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) of the commercial court having jurisdiction over the registered office; electronic filing is made through the mandatory Guichet Unique managed by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 for SARL formation and governance, supplementary provisions of Articles 1832 et seq. of the Civil Code, and Code monétaire et financier rules on blocking of capital contributions.

Formation / constitutional filings

Certified Articles of Association (statuts) together with the standardized M0 creation declaration filed through the Guichet Unique portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 11 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies. Cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% at incorporation, with the balance due within five years. Funds must be deposited in a French bank account in the SARL's name prior to RCS filing and remain blocked until the registration certificate (extrait Kbis) is issued; the bank issues a certificate of capital deposit.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for the leased office workplace should be obtained from a licensed prevention bureau (bureau de contrôle) if the premises are open to the public or exceed applicable ERP thresholds; structural load inspections and building compliance are verified under the Code de la construction et de l'habitation; occupational health and safety clearance under Articles L4121 et seq. of the French Labour Code is required for all employees, including emergency exit signage, smoke detectors, and first-aid equipment.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies. Cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% at incorporation, with the balance due within five years. Funds must be deposited in a French bank account in the SARL's name prior to RCS filing and remain blocked until the registration certificate (extrait Kbis) is issued; the bank issues a certificate of capital deposit.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP); portal www.impots.gouv.fr / 'Gérer mon entreprise'

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, incorporation is completed through the 'Guichet Unique' electronic one-stop shop managed by INPI, which assigns the SIREN and SIRET numbers and notifies the DGFIP. The VAT identification number (intra-community) is issued by the DGFIP service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) after registration and is displayed as FR followed by a two-letter/key and the 9-digit SIREN. Foreign entities without a French establishment must submit a simplified registration (form MO 'Siège') to the DGFIP for remote sellers, nominate a tax representative in certain cases, or obtain a direct intra-community VAT ID under Article 286 XI of the CGI.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SARL, incorporation is completed through the 'Guichet Unique' electronic one-stop shop managed by INPI, which assigns the SIREN and SIRET numbers and notifies the DGFIP. The VAT identification number (intra-community) is issued by the DGFIP service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) after registration and is displayed as FR followed by a two-letter/key and the 9-digit SIREN. Foreign entities without a French establishment must submit a simplified registration (form MO 'Siège') to the DGFIP for remote sellers, nominate a tax representative in certain cases, or obtain a direct intra-community VAT ID under Article 286 XI of the CGI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime. A SARL is irrevocably subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) unless it qualifies for the 5-year option for income tax (impôt sur le revenu) under Article 239 bis AA CGI applicable to qualifying small SARLs. Hybrid mismatch elections require self-assessment and documentation under Article 212 bis CGI and Article 206 ter; no elective hybrid classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For French domestic small businesses: €25,000 for service providers (€34,400 for certain liberal/B2C services) below which the 'franchise en base de TVA' derogation may apply. For cross-border distance sales, an EU-wide €10,000 annual threshold applies; above it French VAT registration or OSS return is required. For B2B supplies of services by a non-French supplier, no turnover threshold applies if the French customer is taxable (reverse charge under Article 196 CGI).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% French VAT applies to exports of qualifying goods when the goods physically leave the EU within 90 days of supply and the exporter retains proof of dispatch or transport (Articles 262-I-1 CGI and 73 CGI Annex III). B2B SaaS and digital services supplied to non-EU customers are not subject to French VAT because the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction (Article 259-1 CGI); the French supplier must evidence the customer's business establishment and obtain a written statement/self-certification. EU and French VAT refunds for non-established foreign suppliers are available under the 13th Council Directive (outside EU) and the EU VAT refund procedure (EU suppliers) but require annual application and strict documentary support.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / ENS) with customs registration number and exit confirmation
  • Commercial Invoice describing goods/services, parties, and VAT exempt basis
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR or other transport/tracking evidence proving physical departure from French customs territory
  • Proof of payment and bank remittance records, plus customer VAT/GST identification or corporate registry extract for services

France mandates structured electronic invoicing for all domestic B2B supplies between French taxable persons from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized enterprises, and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises. Invoices must be transacted via a registered Dematrialisation Platform (PDP) or the public portal (Chorus Pro) using the Factur-X/CII-PEPPOL format. There is no real-time tax clearance stamping, but mandatory transmission of invoice data to DGFIP with periodic e-reporting of B2B and B2C transactions creates a continuous transaction control (CTC) footprint.

The French tax administration (DGFIP) may retrospectively disallow the zero-rating or place-of-supply treatment if export documentation is incomplete, INCO terms are inconsistent, or the 90-day physical departure condition is not proved. Reassessment gives rise to VAT plus infringement interest, and penalties under Article 1729 CGI (40% for bad faith, 80% for abusive concealment) plus a possible 100% penalty for confirmed fraud. False export declarations attract customs enforcement under Article 414 Customs Code and potential monetary fines in criminal courts. While France does not impose a hard statutory FX repatriation deadline, regional customs and banking due-diligence standards commonly expect settlement within 180 days; failure to repatriate can trigger money-laundering scrutiny and administrative penalties under the Monetary and Financial Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is a fully convertible currency for current and capital account transactions. France applies the principle of free movement of capital across EU/EEA states; transfers to and from third countries are unrestricted except for temporary safeguard measures, financial sanctions, or EU restrictive measures. Profit remittance by a French SARL to non-resident shareholders is generally free, subject to anti-money-laundering documentation and reference to the company's distributable reserves. A percentage of the tax on dividends is withheld at source (30% flat tax) unless reduced by tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign-currency receipts and are required to report all cross-border transactions and balances through the Banque de France's balance-of-payments reporting service when periodically exceeding EUR 1,000 per transaction for indirect reporting, or EUR 12.5 million annual turnover for direct reporting. There is no minimum surrender ratio.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French Central Bank), in conjunction with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Recovery; monetary policy is governed by the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) under the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

High for an ACPR-licensed or passported EU/EEA EMI that provides French IBANs (e.g., Qonto, Revolut Business, Mangopay). Legal constraints require the EMI to hold an electronic money institution license from the ACPR or another EU authority benefiting from passporting under Directive 2009/110/EC. The EMI must comply with French AML/FT customer due diligence standards and must not be a personally opaque 'offshore' provider without EU presence. Strong viability for LegalTech SaaS; however, for escrow or capital injections, a traditional bank with a licensed deposit-taking status is preferable.

Appointment is also compulsory where the SARL controls or is controlled by one or more companies, at the request of shareholders holding at least 10% of the share capital, or by court order. Below these thresholds the SARL is exempt and may appoint an auditor on a voluntary basis.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File are required for French entities whose net turnover or gross assets exceed EUR 400 million on a standalone or consolidated basis; Country-by-Country reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million. All French-connected cross-border related-party transactions must be documented contemporaneously at audit request even below the threshold (Article L.13 A LPF).

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) framework, but the general anti-abuse rule (abus de droit, Article L.64 LPF), the abnormal management act doctrine (acte anormal de gestion, Article 39 CGI) and the Article 205 CGI GAAR prevent artificial structures. The French SARL must have actual business premises, operational personnel, independent management decisions, and economic ownership of its software contracts; otherwise the tax administration may recharacterize the entire outsourcing arrangement, deny deductions, and apply an 80% abuse-of-law penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI exposes the offender to up to 5 years imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine; the tax deficit is increased by 100% under Article 1732 CGI. Criminal proceedings are run by the JIF (interregional court) and may be coupled with corporate liability.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed or executed the fraudulent billing structure are personally prosecutable; civil courts may lift the corporate veil to order restitution; travel bans, asset freezes and six-month-plus prison sentences are common sanctions in aggravated cases.
Abusive Profit-Shifting / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: The fiscal administration disregards the arrangement under Article L.64 LPF and reallocates the income to France; penalty for abuse of law is 80% of the additional tax, or 40% where the scheme lacks sound commercial justification under the general anti-abuse rule.

Director personal liability: Directors who are identified as the authors or main actors of the abuse are jointly and severally liable for the reincorporated tax and penalties; where foreign bank accounts were not declared, criminal sanctions for undeclared offshore accounts may be added.
Withholding Tax Failure on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The paying French SARL is liable for the unpaid withholding tax plus late-payment interest; Article 1731 CGI adds a 5% penalty rising to 40% in the case of deliberate underpayment. The tax authority may refuse treaty relief and issue a 100% penalty if the certificate is fabricated.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is personally liable for failure to collect and remit withholding taxes only where the failure is coupled with wilful bad faith; administrative courts may impose a personal liability order under Article L.267 LPF in such cases.
AML / Banking Transparency Failure

Statutory sanction: The French Monetary and Financial Code (Article L.561-1 and subsequent) imposes AML obligations; failure to report a suspicious transaction to Tracfin can result in criminal fines up to EUR 300,000 and imprisonment for executives, plus administrative sanctions after a warning if the AML programme is ineffective.

Director personal liability: Executives who knowingly approve or ignore anomalous flows through the payment rails face personal criminal liability, including two-year imprisonment and economic disqualification; in serious cases thecourt may substitute a travel-ban and strip the management rights of the SARL.
