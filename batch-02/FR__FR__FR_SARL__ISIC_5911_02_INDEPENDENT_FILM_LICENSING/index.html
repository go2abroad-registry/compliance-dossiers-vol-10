Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SARL (capital may be set at EUR 1); at least 20% of the cash subscription must be paid up at incorporation and the balance within 5 years (Article L.223-7, Code de commerce). Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked account held by a licensed credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and are released upon issuance of the Kbis certificate; in-kind contributions must be valued in the statuts and, when not waived unanimously, verified by a statutory auditor (commissaire aux apports).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and occupancy clearance: where office premises are open to the public, the ERP classification (Type L, école/établissement de loisir or Type R) must be declared to the Mairie and inspected by the commission de sécurité; for closed workplaces, compliance with the Labour Code Articles R.4222-1 to R.4227-14 (lighting, electrical installations, fire extinguishers, emergency exits, accessibility) suffices. Interior refit works exceeding 20 m² require a déclaration préalable de travaux filed at the Mairie; no structural load certification is required for non-assembly office use under the Construction and Housing Code.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SARL (capital may be set at EUR 1); at least 20% of the cash subscription must be paid up at incorporation and the balance within 5 years (Article L.223-7, Code de commerce). Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked account held by a licensed credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and are released upon issuance of the Kbis certificate; in-kind contributions must be valued in the statuts and, when not waived unanimously, verified by a statutory auditor (commissaire aux apports).

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing board has competence for this activity; the business is confined to office-based film copyright distribution and licensing. The only sectoral administrative register is the CNC — Centre national du cinéma et de l'image animée, where a voluntary registration as a cinematographic distribution company may be effected to access French film-support mechanisms.

Category: None — no operating permit or manufacturing licence is required by law for international motion-picture copyright distribution and licensing. Voluntary CNC registration as a distribution company (Code du cinéma et de l'image animée) is recommended but does not condition lawful operation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. France has abolished all exchange-control restrictions since 1989-1990 (the last remnants of the 'réglementation des changes' were lifted in that period). The Code monétaire et financier (CMF, Articles L. 151-1 to L. 151-3 and R. 152-1 et seq.) guarantees freedom of financial relations with foreign countries in line with TFEU Articles 63-66 (free movement of capital and payments), subject only to limited safeguard derogations. In practice there is: (a) no prior authorisation for inbound capital or outbound profits/royalties; (b) no currency allocation, interest-equalisation or compulsory conversion regime; and (c) no regulatory distinction between current-account and capital-account transactions apart from statistical coding. The only hurdles for profit remittance from a French SARL to non-resident shareholders are corporate-law formalities (shareholders' resolution, verification of distributable reserves) and, depending on the shareholder's status, French withholding tax: a default 25% on dividends under Article 119 bis of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI) (12.8% for individuals; potentially reduced by tax treaty or exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate shareholders). None of these constitutes an exchange-control or convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio exists in France. A French exporter may lawfully retain licensing income and royalties in foreign currency, convert them to EUR at free-market rates, or hold them on external accounts without any deadline or authorisation. The sole statutory obligation is the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical declaration (reporting des changes): each cross-border payment reaching a resident account in an amount of EUR 12,500 or more is declared automatically by the receiving/issuing bank under the 3C reporting mechanism, and the company may be asked to confirm the underlying economic nature (goods, services, royalties, capital flows). Repeat small-value flows may be aggregated under a simplified quarterly declaration, but no individual conversion, repatriation, or surrender requirement applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as the national central bank, acting together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, part of the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique) on foreign-exchange and international-financial-relations matters; since France is in the euro area, exchange-rate policy is also governed by the European Central Bank (ECB) under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU, Articles 127-133) and the Eurosystem framework.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Declarations must be filed within one month following the earlier of the 6-month corporate income tax filing deadline (déclaration de résultats) that is no later than the second business day following 1 May after the fiscal year close. For fiscal years ending 31 December, the filing deadline is the second business day after 1 May. Payment of corporate income tax is made through four quarterly instalments due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, with the balance due on the filing deadline. If the entity is classified as a large company, the balance payment deadline may be extended to the second business day following 1 May, while the annual return (liassez fiscale) must be filed electronically by the same deadline.

Additional 40% penalty for deliberate misconduct and 80% for dissimulation or abusive tax avoidance under Article 1729 CGI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000

Economic substance scope: The EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) and French Economic Substance jurisprudence require that the French licensing entity demonstrates real economic substance. Although France does not maintain formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) akin to the UAE or Cayman, the French General Tax Code (Article 155-A, 1655-A, and 209-B) and the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) framework require the French SARL to show substantive activity, including: (i) local management and decision-making in France; (ii) employees with technical competence in film licensing, contract negotiation, and copyright supervision; (iii) premises and operational equipment commensurate with the volume of licensing contracts; (iv) actual control over film rights including registration in France, and (v) assumption of material economic risk. A French-resident licensing SARL that merely channels film royalties to a foreign parent without staff or substance is vulnerable to recharacterization as an abuse of law under L-64 Livre des procédures fiscales, or to taxation of the profits in the hands of the non-resident beneficiary under Article 155-A when the entity is interposed and controlled by non-residents unless the SARL demonstrates a legitimate commercial purpose and actual economic substance. The French tax administration also applies OECD BEPS Action 3 controlled foreign company (CFC) rules and the principal purpose test under the EU ATAD. A written substance file with employment contracts, board minutes, invoices, licensing agreements, and the bank account records must be contemporaneously maintained.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of EUR 500,000, which may be increased to 50% of the evaded tax amount and up to EUR 3,000,000 in aggravated cases under Article 1741-1 CGI. Additional 40% penalty for deliberate misconduct and 80% penalty for abusive tax avoidance under Article 1729 CGI, plus late payment surcharges and daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be criminally prosecuted personally; the corporate veil may be pierced. They may be ordered to pay the evaded tax and penalties jointly and severally with the company. The court may impose travel bans, prohibitions on managing a commercial enterprise (Article L-653-8 Code de commerce), and imprisonment of 5 years. Directors who deplete corporate assets to frustrate tax recovery may be subject to bankruptcy sanction of bankruptcy (faillite personnelle) with a ban of up to 15 years from directing and managing a business.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law (Abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may set aside the artificial arrangement and tax the profit in the hands of the true beneficiary; a penalty of 80% of the reassessed tax may be imposed under Article 1729-b CGI. In addition, a specific administrative abuse procedure (Article L-64 A LPF) allows sanctions against professional advisors who design abusive tax arrangements, with fines up to EUR 10,000 or 50% of the professional fees. The company is subject to recharacterization with all tax benefits revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in setting up or maintaining an artificial shell entity are jointly liable for the resulting tax liability under Article L-266 LPF, including penalties and interests. Failure to file annual returns may trigger administrative dissolution proceedings; in cases of fraudulent dissimulation, directors face up to 5 years imprisonment and personal fines of EUR 75,000 under Article 1741 CGI. In insolvency proceedings, a manager may incur the entire unpaid corporate debts where the management conduct has contributed to the insufficiency of assets under Article L-651-2 Code de commerce.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of profits to the arm's-length amount under Article 57 CGI. Unjustified transfer pricing adjustments trigger a 40% good-faith penalty or 80% penalty for abuses of rights. Documents may be required under Article L-13 B LPF; failure to submit TP documentation within 30 days of a formal request results in a fine of EUR 15,000, plus the application of a 5% penalty on the reassessed amount if the documentation obligation is not fulfilled, capped at EUR 10,000 per transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors who prepare or approve fraudulent transfer pricing documentation may be liable for the additional assessments due to their gross negligence. Criminal exposure arises where documentation is knowingly falsified: penal sanctions apply under Article 1741 CGI and corporate directors could face jail terms of 5 years and fines of EUR 500,000. In cross-border group restructuring, directors may also face personal liability for illegal distributions if the French SARL is unable to pay its creditors.
Non-Filing and Failure to Cooperate with Tax Audit

Statutory sanction: Fine of EUR 10,000 for failure to file financial statements at the Commercial Registry; a 5% or 10% late-filing penalty on corporate income tax as described under Article 1728 CGI; 100% penalty on undeclared tax where bad faith is established. For unregistered commercial activities, tax evasion sanctions under Article 1741 CGI apply. Simple non-filing of the annual return for more than 60 days after the formal demand triggers an automatic 10% increase.

Director personal liability: The legal representative may be subject to a tax audit penalty of up to EUR 1,000 personally under Article 1732 CGI when the representative fails to answer tax inquiries. Where the failure to file causes losses for the tax authority, the director is jointly and severally liable for taxes, penalties, and interest; imprisonment is possible in aggravated cases involving concealment. In respect of the registration duty on bad-faith non-filing, the person may be banned from managing a commercial company for up to 5 years.
Anti-Money Laundering / Financial Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Violations of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Article L-561-42) carry fines of up to EUR 750,000 for entities and EUR 375,000 for individuals; criminal sanctions for money laundering under Article 324-1 of the French Criminal Code may result in imprisonment of up to 10 years and a fine of EUR 750,000, increased to EUR 5,000,000 where the laundering has an organized-crime dimension. Violation of EU restrictive measures triggers individual criminal liability and asset freeze enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers face personal criminal prosecution for AML breaches; corporate veil does not shield fraudulent transfers. Directors may be imprisoned up to 10 years and subjected to confiscation of assets. They are also exposed to civil liability under Article L-651-2 Code de commerce if they have misused corporate assets in facilitating laundering schemes, with travel bans and professional disqualification up to 15 years.
