Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registry of the Commercial Court) of the territorial jurisdiction of the registered office, acting through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), operated by INPI.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title III, Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL); Articles L.210-2, R.123-31 et seq. (registration); French Monetary and Financial Code, Articles L.522-1 et seq. and L.522-11-1 (payment institution authorisation for the corporate virtual-card issuing activity).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts constitutifs de la Société à Responsabilité Limitée (Articles of Association) executed by all shareholders, accompanied by the electronic creation declaration (Déclaration de création d'entreprise) transmitted to the Greffe via the Guichet Unique.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 34 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital applies to a French SARL (Code de commerce, Article L.223-2); however, the payment-institution activity requires initial own funds of at least EUR 125,000 under Article L.522-11-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code. Before registration, at least 20% of cash capital contributions, and here the full EUR 125,000, must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the balance, if any, must be released within five years, and for ACPR licensing purposes the full amount must remain available.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety compliance: issuance of the ERP/Code du travail fire certificate for office premises open to staff, including the building owner's fire-load attestation and evacuation plan. Structural clearance: landlord's floor-load capacity attestation and post-fit-out structural inspection if vaults or heavy racks are installed. Occupational clearance: completion of the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP), workplace medical fitness visits for employees, and mandatory access-control and secure-area clearances for the card-spending control and monitoring room.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to a French SARL (Code de commerce, Article L.223-2); however, the payment-institution activity requires initial own funds of at least EUR 125,000 under Article L.522-11-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code. Before registration, at least 20% of cash capital contributions, and here the full EUR 125,000, must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the balance, if any, must be released within five years, and for ACPR licensing purposes the full amount must remain available.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The subscribers extend the corporate foundation as follows: (1) adoption of the Articles of Association of the Société à Responsabilité Limitée, whose corporate name and registered office in France are set out therein; (2) subscription in full of the share capital of EUR 125,000, split into equal parts sociales, and approval of the blocked escrow deposit with the chosen depository bank until official registration; (3) appointment of [Name Surname] as Gérant for the Company's duration, with full powers of management and representation toward third parties; (4) authorization given to the Gérant to publish the mandatory legal notice, file the incorporation dossier via the Guichet Unique, procure the Kbis, and initiate the ACPR payment-institution license application.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) of the French Ministry of Economy and Finance; central taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; non-resident and special-licence unit: Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), 10 Rue du Centre, 93160 Noisy-le-Grand; corporate and customs identifiers are issued via the Guichet Unique operated by INPI (RCS registry) and French Customs (Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects).

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL: (1) incorporate through the Guichet Unique INPI electronic procedure, obtain statutory SIREN/SIRET and RCS registration certificate (extrait Kbis); (2) DGFiP automatically attributes the TVA-intracommunautaire VAT ID (format: FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN) and the EORI number (FR + SIREN) for customs declarations; (3) for foreign entities without a French permanent establishment, register directly with SIEE using the CE form or via the EU One-Stop-Shop portal; (4) virtual-card issuers with a high-risk licence must also present the SIREN, VAT ID, and EORI certificates to the ACPR/Banque de France licensing file and card-network due diligence (Mastercard/Visa).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — France is a unitary state with a single national corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) and no sub-national corporate tax. A SARL is by default subject to IS at the 25% standard rate, with a reduced 15% rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs with turnover below €10 million; this fits the tax classification model for a commercial B2B virtual-card issuing fintech. — For a French SARL: (1) incorporate through the Guichet Unique INPI electronic procedure, obtain statutory SIREN/SIRET and RCS registration certificate (extrait Kbis); (2) DGFiP automatically attributes the TVA-intracommunautaire VAT ID (format: FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN) and the EORI number (FR + SIREN) for customs declarations; (3) for foreign entities without a French permanent establishment, register directly with SIEE using the CE form or via the EU One-Stop-Shop portal; (4) virtual-card issuers with a high-risk licence must also present the SIREN, VAT ID, and EORI certificates to the ACPR/Banque de France licensing file and card-network due diligence (Mastercard/Visa).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — France is a unitary state with a single national corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) and no sub-national corporate tax. A SARL is by default subject to IS at the 25% standard rate, with a reduced 15% rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs with turnover below €10 million; this fits the tax classification model for a commercial B2B virtual-card issuing fintech.

Available election forms

There is no US-style check-the-box election. Relevant statutory mechanisms include: (1) Article 239 bis AA CGI — 'SARL de famille' pass-through election at the partners'/members' level, restricted to family-held companies meeting shareholding conditions; (2) Article 223 A CGI — tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) for French groups; (3) Article 145 CGI — mother-daughter participation regime for dividend income; (4) VAT options: régime réel normal, régime simplifié de l'encaissement (cash-basis accounting), or option for the franchise regime where eligible; (5) ATAD 2 anti-hybrid declarations under Articles 202 A ter and 212 bis CGI for cross-border hybrid structures involving French credit-card investment vehicles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA (Article 293 B CGI): SME exemption thresholds of €85,800 annual turnover for goods and €34,400 for services, revalued to €91,900 and €36,800 as of 2025 (tolerance bands €101,000 and €39,100); a separate €10,000 EU OSS threshold applies to cross-border distance sales from other Member States; non-resident suppliers are subject to indefinite VAT registration without exemption; a virtual-card issuer supplying exempt financial services will typically hold registration for recovery and B2B reporting purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For this B2B virtual-card issuer, the applicable mechanism is not classic goods-export 0% rating but: (a) tangible goods exports — 0% VAT under Article 262 CGI with mandatory proof of physical export from the EU; (b) cross-border intra-Community supplies — 0% under Article 262 ter CGI, conditional on the customer's valid EU VAT ID and movement evidence; (c) financial services such as card issuing, payment processing, and interchange settlement — VAT-exempt without input credit under Article 261 C 3° CGI when performed in France, and outside the French VAT scope for B2B services to non-French taxable persons under the place-of-supply rule of Article 259-1 CGI, triggering reverse charge in the customer's country; services to non-EU non-taxable clients fall outside French territorial VAT; consequently, the entity claims 'exemption/outside scope' rather than 'export zero-rating' for its core fee and interchange revenue.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU) and, for intra-EU transports, the EXS declaration or Export Summary Declaration
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR road consignment note proving physical removal from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and the virtual-card service contract showing the B2B nature and place of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation de rapatriement des devises) and bank statements demonstrating FX repatriation to the French treasury account
  • Payment network settlement statements (Mastercard/Visa) evidencing interchange and scheme fee flows for the exported card services

Mandatory structured e-invoicing and e-reporting under Article 289 bis CGI (French 'Facturation électronique' reform): Phase 1 from 1 September 2026 — all VAT-registered companies must receive PEPPOL-compliant structured e-invoices (Factur-X hybrid, UBL or CII), and large and mid-cap companies must issue them; Phase 2 from 1 September 2027 — SMEs and microenterprises must issue; invoices are transmitted via certified PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) platforms or the free public portal (PPF) connected to Chorus Pro, with mandatory electronic reporting of B2B, B2C, and transaction data to DGFiP; France does not apply a fully real-time digital stamping clearance as in Italy or Poland, but the PPF architecture enforces peer-to-peer clearance, structural data validation, and near-real-time reporting for payment and financial-transaction flows.

DGFiP tax audits (vérification de comptabilité) apply heightened scrutiny to zero-rating claims and to VAT-exempt financial supplies, particularly against sham-export and artificial routing structures; documentation must be retained for 10 years (Article L.102 B of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales). Penalties: Article 1729 CGI imposes a 40% penalty for underdeclaration in bad faith (mauvaise foi) and 80% for manifestly abusive fraud (manœuvres frauduleuses); Article 1727 CGI adds late-payment interest of 0.20% per month; reclaims and VAT credits are clawed back with interest, and criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGIDP may result in fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, in addition to publication of sanctions and possible suspension of the financial-services licence by the ACPR.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. France maintains no exchange controls on capital movements, but requires statistical reporting to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments purposes. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate tax withholding (if applicable) and compliance with anti-money laundering (AML) regulations. Non-resident investors may freely repatriate dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts, but must report all cross-border transactions exceeding €12,500 to the Banque de France for statistical and balance-of-payments monitoring. No minimum conversion ratio is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in coordination with the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR) and the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Cross-border Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed in other EU/EEA states may passport into France under the PSD2 regime. However, for a corporate virtual card issuer, the entity itself must hold an EMI or payment institution license from the ACPR or a passporting license. Using an EMI as a banking partner is viable for operational accounts, but capital escrow accounts typically require a full credit institution to ensure safeguarding and regulatory compliance. Legal constraints include adherence to ACPR AML/CFT requirements and the need for a local agent or branch if passporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require SWIFT MT103 with purpose code '1100' (Direct Investment) or '1101' (Equity Capital) for capital contributions. The remitter must provide supporting documentation including board resolution approving the injection, share subscription agreement, and proof of source of funds. The receiving bank will verify the purpose code and may request additional KYC documents. For EU-origin transfers, SEPA transfers are acceptable for amounts below €100,000, but SWIFT is recommended for larger sums. All transfers must be reported to the Banque de France if exceeding €12,500.Clearing rails for trade settlements include SWIFT MT103 for direct payments, and Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for documentary trade. For virtual card issuer operations, outbound disbursements to merchants are typically executed via card network rails (Mastercard/Visa) but for non-card trade payments, SWIFT MT103 is standard. Use of MT202 for interbank transfers is also permissible. All outbound payments must comply with EU sanctions and AML regulations, and be reported for balance-of-payments statistics.Outright forwards (deliverable) in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and other major pairsCurrency swaps (cross-currency interest rate swaps) for long-term exposureFX options (vanilla and exotic) for hedging contingent exposuresNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for currencies with convertibility restrictions, but since EUR is fully convertible, NDFs are not typically used; however, they may be allowed for exotic currencies if the counterparty is a licensed bank.
