Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); central portal: impots.gouv.fr; online professional space: 'Espace professionnel' / 'Gérer mon entreprise'.

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, the statutory tax identification number is the SIREN obtained from INPI/Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés at incorporation; the intra-community VAT number is obtained by filing CERFA no. 3310-CA (or, for non-EU operators, CERFA no. 15738) with the local service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) or the DGFiP central office; non-EU entities without an establishment in France must also appoint an accredited fiscal representative to complete the VAT registration procedure and submit periodic returns.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SARL, the statutory tax identification number is the SIREN obtained from INPI/Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés at incorporation; the intra-community VAT number is obtained by filing CERFA no. 3310-CA (or, for non-EU operators, CERFA no. 15738) with the local service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) or the DGFiP central office; non-EU entities without an establishment in France must also appoint an accredited fiscal representative to complete the VAT registration procedure and submit periodic returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law does not provide a check-the-box regime. A SARL is by default subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level; a qualifying small SARL (SARL de famille or eligible SME) may elect for pass-through treatment under Article 239 bis AA of the French General Tax Code, in which case profits are taxed at member level; ATAD hybrid mismatch elections are limited to the specific carve-outs and disclosure forms required under Article 212 bis CGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 annual taxable turnover for goods and EUR 37,500 for services under the domestic franchise en base; however, for a VAT-established entity such as a French SARL, registration is generally mandatory when carrying on a taxable activity, and non-established foreign suppliers have no de minimis threshold – they are registrable from the first taxable euro unless reverse-charge or special scheme applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

International transport of goods to non-EU destinations and related loading, unloading, handling, or ancillary services directly connected to such exports are zero-rated under Article 262-1-II of the French CGI when the service provider is a taxable person established in France and possesses documentary evidence proving physical export from the EU; intra-Community transport is not zero-rated but is generally subject to reverse charge in the destination Member State.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/EBE) accepted by French customs or exit summary declaration
  • Proof of physical exit from the European Union (electronic exit confirmation or customs-endorsed transit document)
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, EORI number, and description of goods and destinations
  • Transport contract and consignment waybill (single airway bill or CMR consignment note) for the international shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT confirmation, or bank statement proving receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer

France mandates B2B electronic invoicing and e-reporting through the national Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Private Dematerialization Platform (PDP) with API connectivity; phased implementation starts on 1 September 2026; invoices must be transmitted electronically within the statutory deadlines and e-reporting data sent to DGFiP; France does not operate a real-time fiscal-clearance or digital-stamping model akin to Italy or Latin American jurisdictions, but the PPF/PDP pipeline provides periodic validated transmission and audit-ready data.

DGFiP and French customs can retrospectively claw back improperly claimed zero-rating with VAT assessment, 0.40% monthly late-payment interest, and administrative penalties of 40% for intentional misstatement or 80% for organized fraud or abuse of right; false customs declarations may trigger separate customs fines, seizure of goods, and criminal liability for senior management; documentary discrepancies in the outward shipment trail are treated as an audit red flag.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible for current account transactions under Articles 63 and 64 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier). France applies no general exchange-control licensing for ordinary commercial payments in EUR or freely convertible foreign currencies. Capital account movements are liberalized within the EU/EEA; outward transfers of funds to non-EU/EEA persons are unrestricted except for: (i) mandatory anti-money laundering reporting, (ii) EU sanctions and asset freezes, (iii) restrictions on transfers involving non-cooperative jurisdictions (FATF black/grey lists), and (iv) the foreign-investment screening regime (IEF – Investissements Étrangers en France) under Articles L151-1 to L151-8 for investments in sensitive sectors; courier/logistics is not a protected sector unless it touches dual-use or data-security activities. Profit remittance by a SARL to a foreign parent is lawful without dividend pre-clearance; only the standard withholdings/distribution formalities apply. Current-account convertibility is therefore complete; capital-account convertibility is effectively complete outside defense, dual-use, and personally sensitive data activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes NO mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Export receipts in EUR or foreign currencies may be retained, held in foreign-currency bank accounts (comptes devises), or converted freely. Statutory reporting applies instead of surrender: all cross-border transfers to/from non-EU countries exceeding EUR 10,000 must be declared by the paying/receiving bank to French customs and the DGDDI (Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects) under Article L152-1 of the Code monétaire et financier. Additionally, the Banque de France requires balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical filing for direct investment transactions where cumulative flows exceed EUR 15 million annually per counterparty/transaction; the SARL must also maintain an accurate register of receivables and FX positions, but no conversion ratio or repatriation timeline applies to express-courier export invoices.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) within the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique; sole monetary and prudential authority plus balance-of-payments statistical control; the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) supervises banks and fintech EMIs for AML/KYC enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Moderate. French-licensed fintech EMIs (établissements de monnaie électronique) — e.g., Qonto, Shine, and international EMIs passporting into France (Revolut Business, N26 Business) — are viable for day-to-day operations, SEPA/instant credit transfer collections, and multi-currency settlement up to the degree they offer physical or virtual FX accounts; however, they are legally prohibited from accepting demand deposits tied to credit creation, cannot grant overdrafts or working-capital credit, and generally do not execute export-proceeds reporting for customs/Banque de France. An EMI account is acceptable as a secondary operating rail but insufficient as the sole treasury and clearing hub for an international express-courier SARL. The compliance risk-tier of the courier industry (Medium_Risk_Enhanced) means ACPR-regulated EMIs will additionally apply enhanced due diligence; onboarding headroom improves if the SARL holds its principal account at a Tier-1 bank and uses the EMI solely for POS/acquirer settlement. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA are Non-Viable for principal treasury functions because they lack ACPR authorization to hold French SARL funds, cannot offer IBAN-based SEPA clearing, and create tax transparency risks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity and shareholder-loan inflows into the French SARL must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with a remitter reference explicitly indicating 'APPPORT / CAPITAL SOCIAL' or 'COMPTE COURANT D'ASSOCIÉ', and must include the SARL's IBAN and the corporate purpose code compatible with the Banque de France BOP statistical coding, primarily: 'Investissement Direct Étranger (IDE)' – code 6C1P for equity participation and 6C1Q for shareholder loans, or for advances: 'Crédit Commercial' codes. The capital subscription must be deposited in accordance with Article L223-7 of the French Commercial Code: funds are either placed in an escrow account at a French bank, or, for an SARL, remitted directly to the manager against a receipt and presented with the 'attestation du dépositaire' to the RCS registrar. The installing bank must complete: (i) beneficiary-side verification of source of funds; (ii) automatic reporting to customs where inbound amount exceeds EUR 10,000 from a non-EU country; and (iii) quarterly BOP reporting to the Banque de France via the ORME (Observatoire pour la Réforme du Marché des Entreprises) portal if the inbound cumulative direct investment exceeds EUR 15 million. For amounts below EUR 15 million, no prior authorization is required; the sole obligation is bank-held record-keeping and, if the counterparty is a listed 'non-cooperative' jurisdiction, the bank will apply a mandatory procuration/registration blockade under Article R152-1 of the Code monétaire et financier.Clearing rails for trade settlements are stratified as follows: (A) primary rail – SWIFT MT103 for all non-SEPA cross-border beneficiary payments, with mandatory structured reference (41A order, 50K ordering customer, 72 remittance info) and full sanctions/beneficiary-account-name verification; (B) EU/EEA operations – SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) using IBAN-to-IBAN rails, no FX cost, execution within T+0/T+1; (C) documentary settlement – Commercial Letter of Credit (USance L/C 30–90) confirmed by CACIB or HSBC Continental Europe when the SARL imports courier technology/sorting equipment or settles high-value subcontracting receivables; (D) ACH Direct as secondary national French collection rail for recurring domestic invoices via SEPA Direct Debit Core, not for international clearing; and (E) cheque/trésorerie optional rail discretional on the bank. All outward disbursements require treasury management standing instructions such that every MT103 covers the courier service invoice reference, country routing, and the 4-digit DGDDI purpose code to prevent bank rejections and to preserve corporate VAT/export-compliance audit chain.Deliverable FX Forwards (forwards de change) up to 12 months tenor against EUR, executed under standard ISDA/FEBI conventions with a French credit institution, fully documented under EMIR with trade reporting where the collateral threshold is exceededFX Swaps (simples) – EUR/USD and EUR/GBP swap points quoting by BNP Paribas or Société Générale Global Markets, permissible for matching the courier settlement cycle without limitations on depthCurrency Options (Call/Put European-style) and order-driven options bought to hedge unexpected airborne/parcel volatile exposures; selling naked options not permitted against prudent treasury policyCross-Currency Swaps limited to fixed-floating EUR vs. USD/GBP maturities under 24 months for the financing of customs prefinancing and regional distribution hubsNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) admissible only as a fallback for secondary-currency exposure (e.g., emerging-market routes such as TRY, BRL, EGP) where an onshore deliverable forward is functionally impossible; executed back-to-back with a top-tier regional bank and subject to Banque de France statistical declaration if the notional exceeds reporting thresholds

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty of up to 5 years imprisonment and fines up to 80% of the evaded tax (or higher in aggravated cases)

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment, fines, and disqualification from office
Failure to File or Late Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine ranging from €250 to €10,000 or 0.5% to 5% of turnover, plus daily penalties of €20 per day

Director personal liability: Senior directors can be held personally liable for fines and, in cases of gross negligence, criminal sanctions
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 500% of the tax adjustment or specific TP penalties of €10,000 to €100,000 per infringement

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-compliant arrangements may face personal restitution orders and civil liability
Shell Company or Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS)

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions including fines up to €1 million and imprisonment for serious cases

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil leading to personal civil and criminal liability, including travel bans in extreme cases
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million or 5% of annual turnover, plus potential criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may incur personal fines and imprisonment for willful AML breaches
