Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporation of the SARL via the Guichet Unique (guichet-entreprises) triggers issuance of the SIREN/SIRET (French business identifiers) by INSEE. Step 2: VAT registration (TVA intracommunautaire number) is obtained by filing the standardized DGFiP notice of VAT commencement (form/declaration under Article 286-ter of the CGI) either through the Guichet Unique or directly with the local DGFiP Business Registration Service (SIAV – Service de l'Immatriculation des Assujettis à la TVA). Step 3: Foreign-established entities selling electronically supplied services into France may alternatively register under the Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for B2C supplies or the reverse-charge mechanism for B2B supplies, avoiding a full French fixed-establishment VAT profile; a physical French establishment, such as a SARL branch, mandates full local VAT registration and issuance of a FR+ identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

By default, a SARL (other than a societe unipersonnelle SARL) is subject to Corporation Tax (impôt sur les sociétés – IS) on its worldwide-deemed French-source taxable income at the rate of 25% (reduced rate 15% on profits up to EUR 42,500 under conditions). It may elect, under Article 206-3 CGI, pass-through taxation (régime des sociétés de personnes / impôt sur le revenu – IR) only if strictly constituted as a family or small SME enterprise (limited to 50 employees and EUR 10 million turnover). No direct 'check-the-box' election exists; instead, companies choose taxation status at formation/amendment through the Guichet Unique and, once taxed as IR, must obtain DGFiP consent to switch back to IS. Hybrid mismatch elections are constrained by the EU ATAD/ATAD2 rules transposed into the French CGI (Articles 212-1 bis and following), which neutralize double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion outcomes; election must be documented with a compliant GAAP/French accounting basis and prior-year permanent files.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full current and capital account convertibility for EUR-denominated and cross-border transactions. Capital movements are fully liberalized under EU law, subject only to general derogations for public policy, national security, and EU sanctions. Profit remittance (dividend distribution, repayment of shareholder loans, royalty and service payments) is lawful without prior foreign exchange authorization, but is subject to French corporate withholding tax on outbound dividends (generally 25% or 5% under a tax treaty), transfer pricing documentation requirements, and mandatory reporting to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics (tableau de bord). The euro is freely convertible in both current and capital accounts; there are no exchange control barriers, surrender requirements, or bi-lateral clearing restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio to EUR. Exporters may retain balances in foreign currencies in authorized foreign currency accounts; however, all receipts must be booked in EUR for statutory accounting purposes under Article L.123-22 of the French Commercial Code, and all cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments compilation. Payment instructions for export settlements must satisfy EU Regulation (EU) 2015/847 on information accompanying transfers of funds, including full originator and beneficiary identification.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with prudential supervision delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), operating within the European System of Central Banks and under the legal framework of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier) and EU Capital Movements Regulation (Article 63 TFEU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal constraints. Regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions (PIs) authorized by the ACPR or passporting into France under PSD2 may open French IBAN accounts and provide treasury clearing services for cybersecurity SaaS receivables. Well-capitalized EMIs such as Qonto, Shine, or Currencycloud-backed platforms are viable for low-risk operational collections and cross-border vendor payments. However, EMIs cannot provide credit, deposit products, or documentary letters of credit; they are unsuitable as capital escrow banks for the legal share capital of an SARL, which must be held with a credit institution licensed under Article L.511-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code. Offshore EMIs are subject to French AML/KYC transfer-of-funds rules and are legally less defensible for high-ticket contract escrow and parent-subsidiary cash pooling.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard CIT rate applicable to all taxable profits for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2022)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Article 119 bis-2 CGI on gross dividends paid by the French SARL to a non-resident shareholder; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Article 119 ter CGI) when the parent is an EU/EEA company holding at least 5% of the SARL for an uninterrupted two-year period; DTA-reduced rates commonly range from 0% to 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months of fiscal year-end. For a SARL with a 31 December fiscal year-end, the online filing deadline is the second business day following 1 May of the next year (e.g., 31 December 2024 year-end due on 3 May 2025); paper filing deadline is the first business day after 1 May.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Instalments due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December of the tax year; any balance payable or recoverable is settled with the annual Form 2065-SD.

Compute on the same entity-level French result as the annual return; instalments cannot be reduced merely because the parent resides abroad unless treaty relief has been formally granted. Unpaid or under-paid instalment: late penalty of 5% of the missing amount plus monthly interest at 0.20%; the French administration may automatically assess the estimated instalment basis if no return is filed by 1 December.
Box B — Crédits et abattements (Offsets) Deduction of CIT prepayments, research tax credit and any withheld tax credits available against instalments. Foreign tax credits attributable to a foreign branch of the SARL may be offset only to the extent provided by the applicable DTA; the underpaid balance attracts interest. Unpaid or under-paid instalment: late penalty of 5% of the missing amount plus monthly interest at 0.20%; the French administration may automatically assess the estimated instalment basis if no return is filed by 1 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: France imposes no general Local File/Master File obligation on a SARL of this size; mandatory full TP documentation applies to entities exceeding EUR 400,000,000 annual turnover or EUR 2,000,000,000 balance-sheet total. However, the SARL must pre-comply with Article L.13 AA/AB LPF and Annual Form 2257-SD whenever cumulative intra-group transactions with foreign related companies exceed EUR 200,000 per year, requiring a concise arm's-length report and contemporaneous evidence.

Economic substance scope: France does not administer a standalone 'ESR Certificate' regime in the style of the UAE; economic substance is enforced through the general abuse-of-law doctrine (Article L.64 LPF), the French CFC rules (Article 209 B/CGI), and the 'effective management seat' requirement. The SARL must maintain genuine local substance: French registered office, board/gérant decisions taken in France, French payroll with operating personnel, local servers or documentation, and commercial contracts negotiated in France. A purely circular arrangement involving a foreign shell subcontractor with no employees, premises or decision-making autonomy is vulnerable to CIT recharacterisation and 80% 'abus de droit' penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Deliberate Concealment of French CIT/VAT Base (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full French tax base plus a 40% or 80% penalty under Article 1729 CGI and monthly 0.20% late-payment interest; criminal conviction under Article 1741 CGI carries imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, increased to up to seven years and EUR 2,000,000 where the fraud involves organised crime or cross-border concealment; aggravated sanctions include confiscation of the concealed assets, publication of the judgment, and suspension of professional licences.

Director personal liability: The gérant (manager) may be held jointly and severally liable for the SARL's unpaid taxes under Article 267 LPF; criminal conviction of the gérant applies for signing fraudulent declarations. The corporate veil may be pierced via 'gestion de fait' where a foreign controlling shareholder exercises real management without formal office; civil restitution, travel bans and imprisonment follow.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell Conduit Characterisation

Statutory sanction: CIT adjustment reallocating the full margin to the SARL, plus a 40% deliberate-misstatement penalty under Article 1729 CGI; failure to produce TP documentation in response to a formal notice carries a EUR 15,000 fine under Article L.13 AA LPF; payments to a non-cooperative jurisdiction may be disallowed with a 75% final withholding penalty.

Director personal liability: The gérant who executes the non-arm's-length contracts exposes personal assets to civil liability for management fault under Article L.223-22 of the French Commercial Code; an 'abus de biens sociaux' finding (Article L.241-3) triggers five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with the tax judge empowered to disregard the SARL's separate legal personality as a sham conduit.
Money Laundering and Concealment of Digital-Asset Receipts (AML)

Statutory sanction: Article 324-1 of the French Penal Code: simple money laundering carries five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine; aggravated laundering (habitual, professional, or organised) carries ten years' imprisonment and a EUR 750,000 fine; the SARL as a corporate entity is exposed to a fine five times the statutory maximum (up to EUR 1,875,000 to EUR 3,750,000) together with confiscation of the connected cryptocurrency and real-estate assets.

Director personal liability: The gérant who structures, converts or transfers tainted cryptocurrency assets is subject to the same criminal penalties personally; a travel ban, freezing of personal bank accounts, and confiscation of personal property are available. The AML compliance officer's personal criminal liability is engaged for deliberate failure to declare suspicious cross-border transactions.
Illicit Dividend Repatriation — Withholding Tax Evasion on Profit Distributions

Statutory sanction: The SARL is liable as withholding agent for the entire un-withheld amount, plus a 5%-10% late-filing penalty and 0.20% monthly interest under Articles 1728 and 1731 CGI; if the arrangement is characterised as tax evasion, an 80% penalty applies and the shareholders' personal tax position is reassessed in France.

Director personal liability: The gérant who signs the dividend distribution knowingly without WHT becomes personally liable for the corporate WHT debt; presence of fraudulent intent leads to criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI, restrictions on leaving France while the debt subsists, and disqualification from corporate management.
