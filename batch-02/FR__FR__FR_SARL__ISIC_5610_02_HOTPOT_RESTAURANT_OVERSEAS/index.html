Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), operated with Infogreffe and the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI); national name availability screening via data.inpi.fr and the Registre National du Commerce et des Sociétés (RNCS) database.

Name reservation / lock period

France does not operate a statutory 30-day name reservation certificate. A name is provisionally protected from the date of electronic submission via the Guichet Unique until registration is completed (typically 4-10 business days), and definitively protected upon RCS registration. Upon rejection by the Greffier (registrar) for identity, confusing similarity, or public policy grounds, the applicant may: (i) resubmit an amended name through the Guichet Unique at no additional statutory fee; (ii) file a formal challenge against the registrar's decision before the President of the competent Tribunal judiciaire (commercial chamber) within 15 days of notification, by requête; and (iii) for trademark-based conflicts, initiate opposition before the INPI within 2 months of publication of the conflicting French trademark application. Legal counsel should concurrently purchase priority through INPI trademark filing in class 43 to secure the hotpot brand.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) of France; official portal: impots.gouv.fr; company/VAT registration entry: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor forms the SARL through the Guichet Unique (INPI) portal; after incorporation INSEE assigns SIREN/SIRET, and DGFiP issues the TVA intra-community number. Non-established suppliers registering directly for VAT must submit Form CA1 to the local SIE and, if outside the EU, appoint a fiscal representative and obtain an individual VAT number under article 286 CGI.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign investor forms the SARL through the Guichet Unique (INPI) portal; after incorporation INSEE assigns SIREN/SIRET, and DGFiP issues the TVA intra-community number. Non-established suppliers registering directly for VAT must submit Form CA1 to the local SIE and, if outside the EU, appoint a fiscal representative and obtain an individual VAT number under article 286 CGI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no general check-the-box regime. An eligible SARL de famille may elect, under article 239 bis AA CGI, to be taxed as a pass-through partnership; otherwise the SARL is subject to corporate income tax. Anti-hybrid mismatch elections are not available; statutory anti-hybrid classification rules apply ex officio.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Established taxable persons must obtain a VAT number irrespective of turnover. Franchise exemption thresholds are EUR 36,800 for services and EUR 91,900 for goods, but do not exempt registration; non-EU sellers have a zero threshold and any physical supply or restaurant activity in France creates immediate VAT nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to a non-EU destination are zero-rated under article 262 CGI when the seller proves material dispatch/transport out of the EU. Intra-EU supplies to VAT-numbered business customers are zero-rated under article 262 ter I CGI. Cross-border B2B services (including supports for overseas branches) are French VAT-excluded by place-of-supply rules, if the customer is established outside France.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / ECS)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note
  • Commercial invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 confirming repatriated export payment
  • Proof of dispatch or export license/permit applied to the goods

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing and B2C e-reporting phased in from 2026/2027 through a certified platform (PDP) and the State public portal (PPF); all B2C cash receipts in restaurants require anti-fraud certified cash-register software that captures and archives transaction data with digital signature sequence; no separate real-time stamping yet, but fiscal data must be available on DGFiP request.

Zero-rating is rigorously audited by DGFiP; unsupported credits are delinked with 0.2% monthly interest and 5% late-filing surcharge. In bad faith, a 80% penalty under CGI art. 1729 applies; organized evasion triggers 100% penalty and publication of the sanction. Criminal tax fraud under art. 1741 CGI is punishable by up to five years imprisonment and €75,000 fine.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions in France. Cross-border capital movements between EU Member States are liberalised under the free movement of capital provisions of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, and France applies no systemic exchange-control approvals for ordinary corporate financing, equity contributions, supplier settlements, or profit remittances. The main practical hurdles are not convertibility but compliance verification: proof of lawful source of funds, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and confirmation that the counterparty is not subject to EU or French sanctions. Dividends distributed by the French SARL may be remitted freely to the foreign shareholder without prior Bank de France approval; however, a final withholding tax may apply under Article 119 bis-2 of the French General Tax Code unless reduced by an applicable double-tax treaty or exempted under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if the shareholder owns at least 10% of the SARL and satisfies all holding-period and anti-abuse conditions. A French company receiving foreign exchange income is not obliged to obtain a licence, but all accounting entries must ultimately be measured and reported in euros under French GAAP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds into euros. Export receipts in foreign currency may be credited to a foreign-currency account or converted at market rates at the SARL's discretion. There is no statutory timeline within which export proceeds must be repatriated or converted. Nevertheless, all foreign-currency-denominated receivables and cash balances must be valued in euros for French financial statements and tax reporting. Any physical import or export of euro-denominated or foreign-currency banknotes, securities, cheques, or other bearer negotiable instruments having a total value of EUR 10,000 or more must be declared to French Customs pursuant to Article L152-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code and Regulation (EC) No 1889/2005. Failure to declare may lead to customs seizure and penalties.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (central bank and monetary authority); Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), the French banking and conduct-supervision authority attached to Banque de France; Direction Générale du Trésor, Ministry of Economy and Finance, for foreign-exchange policy, capital-movement oversight and balance-of-payments reporting. Banque de France operates interbank clearing oversight and payment-system supervision in France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a supplementary operating account, but Low as the sole banking relationship and Low for statutory SARL capital escrow. Licensed French and EU electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as ACPR-authorised fintechs or EU-passported alternative payment institutions are viable for day-to-day supplier disbursements, payroll execution, and cross-border treasury concentration because they offer fast onboarding, virtual IBANs, and lower fees. Legal constraints under the French Monetary and Financial Code and the EU Electronic Money Directive are material: an EMI is not a credit institution, cannot accept deposits that require a banking licence, cannot grant credit, cannot issue the statutory certificate of deposit required for SARL incorporation, and must safeguard client funds in separate accounts held at an authorised credit institution. For French tax levies, social-security contributions, payroll direct debits, and merchant acquiring for a walk-in hotpot restaurant, a full bank current account remains necessary. EMIs are therefore recommended for inbound-outbound payment routing only and never as a proxy for a French banking-relationship mandate.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the foreign shareholder or parent entity should be routed through established correspondent banking channels using SWIFT MT103, or SEPA Credit Transfer where the investor is within the Single Euro Payments Area. The remittance information field must contain an explicit equity-capital narrative such as capital contribution to share capital and share premium, or shareholding loan. The French receiving bank will require a completed KYC file, the signed capital-deposit certificate for the SARL's incorporation if the funds are lodged in the statutory blocked account, and a board/shareholder resolution authorising the transaction. For statistical balance-of-payments purposes, the SARL is required to report direct-investment transactions to the Banque de France when the applicable reporting threshold is exceeded (typically EUR 15 million of annual gross foreign-direct-investment flows or the acquisition of a controlling direct-investment relationship); a restaurant-operation injection will almost always remain below this threshold, but the bank must be consulted because threshold definitions are adjusted and the reporting obligation is measured at group level. Verification protocols should include source-of-funds statement, high-resolution scanned invoices or valuation for the paid-in capital, and confirmation that none of the shareholder jurisdictions trigger enhanced sanctions screening.The principal clearing rails for outbound trade settlements are SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated supplier payments within the EEA, SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer for non-EUR cross-border payments to suppliers of hotpot ingredients, and irrevocable documentary or commercial letters of credit issued by the SARL's French bank for high-value or first-time vendor relationships in Asia or non-EEA condiment markets. A commercial letter of credit should be issued subject to UCP600, naming the seller as beneficiary and requiring presentation of clean transport documents, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and any sanitary or phytosanitary certificate. For smaller recurring payments, an ordinary MT103 must contain the beneficiary bank SWIFT/BIC, IBAN or account number, the bank's correspondent routing references, invoice number, and a purpose narrative matching the underlying food-import contract. Euro interbank settlement is effected through TARGET2 and French SEPA clearing operated by STET for French bulk payments; foreign-currency settlement passes through the French bank's correspondent network and, where applicable, CLS-eligible currency settlement. Funds should never be released without rendering invoices and customs evidence, because French clearing rules and AML controls treat a payment as an independent transaction subject to full traceability.Plain-vanilla outright FX forward contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/CNH, EUR/GBP) permitted without exchange-control approval when a deliverable foreign-currency payment for hotpot ingredients or overseas franchise obligations is anticipated and can be documented.FX swaps and swap-style rollovers executed with a licensed French credit institution to manage temporary mismatches between EUR receipts and USD or CNY payables, subject to the SARL's treasury mandate and an underlying trade exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with onshore capital-account restrictions (for example certain Asian currencies with limited offshore delivery), permitted when executed through an ACPR/ESMA-authorised bank or investment firm and correlated strictly to the SARL's monetary liabilities; speculative NDF positions without physical underlying trade exposure are not acceptable to French compliance departments.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) to hedge medium-term capital or equipment-finance exposure where a non-EUR loan is used to finance restaurant expansion and fit-out, subject to EMIR collateral-margining requirements and board treasury authorisation.European-style FX options and plain-vanilla collar structures permitted for qualified corporate treasuries, but only when the SARL holds a derivatives trading line, appoints a designated treasury manager, and provides board minutes approving the hedging velocity; options linked to unperformed forecast cash flows are de facto restricted by the bank's suitability and product-governance review under MIFID II.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €75,000 per infringement plus 5% of adjustment amount and interest

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for restitution, disqualification, and criminal liability under tax evasion provisions
Income Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Penalties of 80% of evaded tax plus interest, possible imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability, fines, and obligation to pay tax plus penalties
Failure to Withhold Taxes

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to WHT due plus 10% surcharge plus possible criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable to pay withheld tax and penalties, possible jail time
Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €75,000, potential dissolution, and tax reassessment

Director personal liability: Personal liability for damages, possible criminal sanctions including imprisonment
Tax Evasion through Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion sanctions including fines and imprisonment plus civil penalties

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil leading to personal restitution, travel bans, and up to 5 years imprisonment
