Competent corporate registry

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), with registration in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés maintained by the competent commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 to R.223-36 governing sociétés à responsabilité limitée; Articles L.123-1 to L.123-9 and R.123-1 et seq. governing registration and business formalities; Articles L.561-46 and R.561-55 et seq. governing beneficial-owner filings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of company formation and application for registration in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés submitted through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, together with the signed articles of association and beneficial-owner declaration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital is required; capital may be EUR 1 or more. For cash contributions, at least 20% of subscribed capital must be paid upon incorporation and the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Funds are deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts, which issues the certificate of deposit. The funds become available after registration. Contributions in kind generally require valuation by a commissaire aux apports, subject to statutory exemptions.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupying or modifying premises, obtain applicable building and planning approvals, fire-safety review, and an opening or compliance opinion for establishments receiving the public where relevant. Maintain emergency exits, fire detection and extinguishing systems, evacuation plans, electrical and lifting-equipment inspections, structural load verification for storage areas, and occupational-risk documentation. Apply the Labour Code requirements for workplace safety, training, accident prevention, and occupational-health monitoring. Separate dangerous-goods, rail-terminal, customs, or warehouse approvals may be required according to the physical operations.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital is required; capital may be EUR 1 or more. For cash contributions, at least 20% of subscribed capital must be paid upon incorporation and the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Funds are deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts, which issues the certificate of deposit. The funds become available after registration. Contributions in kind generally require valuation by a commissaire aux apports, subject to statutory exemptions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

French General Directorate of Public Finances (Direction Générale des Finances Publiques – DGFiP); online portal: impots.gouv.fr (professional space); customs authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) for EORI/export clearance; company registration via the Single Business Portal (Guichet Unique) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr and the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File incorporation with the Guichet Unique, which automatically registers the SARL with the RNE, INSEE (allocation of SIREN and SIRET identifiers) and the RCS. The SIREN is the statutory French corporate tax reference. Step 2: File the initial corporate income tax return (n°2065-SD) and obtain the competent local tax office (SIE) for Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at the registered office's jurisdiction. Step 3: VAT — the SIREN is the base VAT identifier; apply for the French intra-Community VAT number (FR + 2-digit key + SIREN) through the DGFiP upon the first intra-EU transaction (Article 286 CGI). Non-resident suppliers without an EU establishment must appoint a tax representative under Article 289A CGI (Form N°3595-SD). Step 4: INSEE automatically assigns the NAF activity code matching the business purpose, i.e., 52.29B (Affrètement et organisation des transports). Step 5: Obtain an EORI number (FR + SIREN) from DGDDI and subscribe to the DELTA-X electronic export clearance platform. All registrations are completed within 5–15 business days via the Guichet Unique.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Pass_Through_Eligible (Default: SARL is fiscally opaque and subject to corporate income tax (IS) — standard 25%, reduced 15% on the first €42,500 of taxable profit per year for SMEs meeting EU turnover criteria (turnover ≤ €10M). Optional income-tax transparency under Article 239 bis AA CGI for qualifying SARLs whose capital is held by natural persons and with limited corporate investors. Territorial scope under Article 209-I CGI: foreign permanent-establishment profits are taxable in France with a foreign tax credit, or exempt if the territoriality election is made. No tax-exempt zone applies to the registered office).

Available election forms

(a) Election for income-tax transparency under Article 239 bis AA CGI (IR) for eligible closely-held SARLs; (b) Family SARL (SARL de famille) option under Article 239 bis A CGI giving shareholders the treatment of a partnership; (c) Tax group (intégration fiscale) under Articles 223 A et seq. CGI for a French parent holding at least 95% of the subsidiary; (d) Hybrid mismatch re-characterisation under Articles 212 bis and 44 CGI (transposing ATAD Directive 2016/1164 and EU 2017/952) for payments with deduction/non-inclusion imbalances, requiring the SARL to align French relief with the payee's tax treatment; (e) No 'check-the-box' classification exists in French law; foreign tax classifications (e.g., US check-the-box entities) are re-assessed through the DGT under general anti-abuse rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for an established French SARL — VAT liability arises from the first taxable supply (Article 256 CGI). For B2B freight-forwarding and intermodal rail services taxable in France, no turnover threshold applies: the supply is taxed at the place of establishment of the customer (Article 259-1° CGI, Article 44 VAT Directive); a non-established supplier must register or the French customer applies the reverse-charge mechanism (Article 283-1 CGI). For intra-EU B2C distance sales of goods to French consumers, the Union-wide threshold is €10,000 per calendar year (OSS scheme available), and IOSS applies to consignments ≤ €150 imported from third territories.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) export of goods outside the EU customs territory — including goods carried by block train under a CIM/SMGS rail consignment contract evidenced by the EU exit confirmation — and (ii) services directly linked to those exports, namely rail freight carriage, transhipment, customs brokerage, loading/handling and forwarding services performed for the exporter or on his behalf (Article 262-I-1° and Article 266-1°(b) CGI). The commission de transport (freight-forwarding service) is zero-rated only when the underlying transport operation itself is an EU export and is ancillary to the export; the French-established SARL must prove that title in the goods passed to a non-resident buyer before the transport begins and that the service is for the direct needs of the goods. Intra-EU transit legs of an intermodal export movement are zero-rated proportionally; any inland haulage not forming a single export operation is standard-rated. Rail freight to non-EU destinations (e.g., China via the Eurasian corridor) qualifies only upon authentication of the EU exit declaration in the delta-X export system.

  • French Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration En Douane via DELTA-X) with ECS/ICS2 rail manifest movement reference and MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • CIM/SMGS international consignment note (lettre de voiture CIM/SMGS) or railway waybill evidencing exit from the EU customs territory at the relevant border office
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract proving transfer of title to the non-resident buyer
  • Packing list and cargo manifest (train consist) documenting goods in transit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer receipt demonstrating that the export proceeds have been received from the non-resident buyer (SWIFT wire or Letter of Credit)
  • Statutory declaration of the SARL (attestation) confirming the service was directly attributable to the export and that the zero-rating conditions under Article 262 CGI are satisfied

Mandatory e-invoicing reform (Ordonnance n°2022-1416, as amended by the 2025 Finance Act; transposition of Directive (EU) 2020/285): B2B and B2G electronic invoicing becomes compulsory from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-cap entities and from 1 September 2027 for SMEs, using the free Public Invoicing Portal (PPF) or certified E-invoicing Service Operators (PDP). Structured XML formats: Factur-X, UBL, CII. E-reporting of B2B, B2C and foreign transactions must then be transmitted to DGFiP in near-real time (within 5 days after invoice issuance). No digital stamping or fiscal visa applies; the DGFiP relies on real-time data clearance for rail cargo, requiring the invoice to contain the client VAT number, freight contract references (CIM/SMGS consignment number), and the export customs declaration reference.

French customs and tax audits verify export zero-rating against the DELTA-X exit records, the CIM/SMGS rail consignment and the bank credit of proceeds (a 180-day collection window is the administrative benchmark for export proceeds repatriation). Where the 0% rate is incorrectly claimed, the DGFiP re-assesses French VAT (20%) under Article 272-2 CGI and applies the 80% penalty for deliberate bad faith (mauvaise foi — 80% under Article 1729(b) CGI) or 100% for abusive concealment; criminal sanctions of up to €500,000 fine and 7 years' imprisonment (Article 1741 CGI) apply for fraudulent VAT schemes. The clawback is retrospective for 3 years for ordinary assessments and 6 years in cases of undeclared or concealed tax fraud (Article L.169 LPF); the statutory manager is personally liable under Article L.267 LPF, and the forwarding agent may be jointly and severally liable for the exporter's VAT under Article 1694 CGI. Compliance files must therefore be retained for at least six years (Article L.102 B LPF).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains a fully convertible Euro regime. Current account transactions (trade, services) are unrestricted and freely convertible without prior approval. Capital account transactions, including profit remittances, dividends, and equity contributions, are convertible subject only to standard tax clearance and reporting thresholds administered by the Direction Générale du Trésor. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal; repatriation is permitted after corporate tax payment with routine documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds applies in France. Exporters retain full flexibility to retain foreign currency or convert to EUR at their discretion. Statutory obligations are limited to timely customs declarations for statistical reporting and complete disclosure in annual corporate tax returns. Non-declaration may trigger penalties but does not compel conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2024

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a French SARL to non-resident shareholders (comprising 12.8% final income tax and 17.2% social levies under the flat-tax regime, CGI Art. 119 bis). Reduction to 0% applies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the beneficiary is an EU/EEA company holding directly at least 10% of the SARL's capital for at least 24 months (or with a sworn commitment to hold that period). Comprehensive DTA rates: 0% for substantive shareholdings under certain treaties (e.g., Germany, Luxembourg), 5% for qualified corporate shareholdings under most OECD-based treaties, and 15% for portfolio investments. A non-EU or non-treaty recipient generally suffers the full 30%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File apply where the FR_SARL, alone or with related entities (including the foreign parent group), has gross revenue or balance-sheet total assets of at least EUR 400 million, or holds directly or indirectly 50% or more of the capital or voting rights of an entity meeting that threshold, or is itself 50% owned by such a threshold-met entity. The Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Below these thresholds, the tax administration may still demand transfer-pricing documentation within 30 days under Article L.13 B of the French Tax Procedures Code; therefore the SARL should maintain a proportionate documentation file whenever related-party transactions exist with the foreign parent, including intermodal haulage fees, capacity booking commissions, and rebilling of rail charges.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a codified economic-substance regime comparable to the UAE ESR, but substance is determinative across three legal fronts: (i) permanent establishment analysis under CGI Art. 209 and DTA Art. 5, (ii) the Principal Purpose Test under Article 7 of the Multilateral Instrument and BEPS Action 6, and (iii) the abuse-of-rights doctrine (abus de droit, L.64 of the Tax Procedures Code). The FR_SARL must demonstrate in France, through premises, employees, board minutes, client contracts, credit-risk management, customs-timeline control, and dispatch decisions, that the Eurasian rail expedition activity is genuinely managed from France; a paper-only contract processor or voyager structure is recharacterized as a shell (société écran) and the taxable margin is re-allocated to the foreign principal, with penalties up to 80%.
