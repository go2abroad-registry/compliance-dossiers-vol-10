Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for SAS. Capital must be fully subscribed by shareholders and, if contributed in cash, deposited in a blocked escrow account opened with a French or EU bank; a certificate of deposit (attestation de dépôt de fonds) issued by the bank is required. If the capital is less than EUR 50,000, the schedule for releasing contributions may be fixed in the statuts; any capital equal to or above EUR 50,000 requires immediate full payment of cash contributions.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification delivered by the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) for the assembly plant, battery storage room and maintenance bay; structural certificate from the local urban planning office or building health and safety commission (CODERST) verifying load capacity; workplace clearance by the inspection du travail (labour inspectorate) for occupational health and safety compliance, including the registration of the operations in the 'Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques' (DUERP) and compliance with EU machine safety directives (2006/42/EC).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for SAS. Capital must be fully subscribed by shareholders and, if contributed in cash, deposited in a blocked escrow account opened with a French or EU bank; a certificate of deposit (attestation de dépôt de fonds) issued by the bank is required. If the capital is less than EUR 50,000, the schedule for releasing contributions may be fixed in the statuts; any capital equal to or above EUR 50,000 requires immediate full payment of cash contributions.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
At the inaugural meeting of the sole shareholder and acting as the governance body of the SAS, it is resolved that: (1) the share capital as stated in the statuts is hereby subscribed in full by the shareholder, and the cash contribution has been deposited into the company's blocked account; (2) the registered office is established at the address indicated in Article 4 of the statuts; (3) the following officers are appointed: President of the SAS, General Manager if necessary, and the statutory auditor where legally required; (4) all necessary incorporation filings are approved and the President is authorized to execute and file the incorporation dossier with the competent registry; and (5) the officers undertake to open a business bank account and to comply with all French commercial, tax and social obligations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), including the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official impots.gouv.fr portal. Corporate formation and unified business registration are administered through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS, submit the incorporation and beneficial-owner information through the INPI Guichet unique. The registry process generates the SIREN identifier for the legal entity and SIRET identifier for each establishment; the entity is then recorded in the Registre national des entreprises. Open the corporate tax account with the competent SIE through impots.gouv.fr and provide the articles of association, registration extract, registered-office evidence, director identification, beneficial-owner filing, business activity description, bank details, and foreign shareholder documentation where applicable. Obtain the French VAT identification number from the DGFiP after VAT registration or confirmation of VAT liability; the number generally consists of the country prefix FR followed by a two-digit key and the nine-digit SIREN. A non-established foreign entity must apply to the competent non-resident tax service or designated SIE before commencing taxable French transactions, unless an applicable intermediary or special registration rule applies. Obtain an Economic Operators Registration and Identification (EORI) number through French customs for import or export customs operations. Registration does not itself establish a permanent establishment; that determination follows French domestic law and applicable tax treaties.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). There is no US-style check-the-box regime and no general French election to classify an SAS as a fiscally transparent entity. Subject to statutory conditions, an SAS may elect temporary taxation under personal income tax rules for up to five financial years. The election generally requires that the company be created for less than five years, conduct an eligible commercial, industrial, artisanal, or professional activity, be unlisted, remain below the applicable employee and turnover or balance-sheet limits, and satisfy ownership and management participation requirements, including specified individual ownership and management thresholds. The election requires unanimous shareholder approval and notification to the tax administration within the prescribed period. A family-company election available to certain partnerships does not generally apply to an SAS. France has anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implementing ATAD II; these are statutory denial or inclusion rules, not elective entity-classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French business supplying goods, including manufactured electric cargo bicycles and step-through scooters, the domestic franchise en base de TVA generally applies below EUR 85,000 annual turnover, subject to the applicable prior-year and current-year thresholds and statutory tolerance rules. A higher threshold of EUR 93,500 generally applies as the current-year tolerance limit for goods activities. The thresholds are distinct from the service thresholds of EUR 37,500 and EUR 41,250. VAT registration is also required irrespective of the small-business threshold for transactions or situations for which the franchise is unavailable, including certain intra-Community acquisitions, imports, and transactions giving rise to French VAT liability. EU cross-border SME exemption rules may apply only if their separate Union-wide and national conditions are satisfied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption under the French implementation of the EU VAT rules, provided the supplier establishes that the goods actually left the EU customs territory and satisfies invoicing, accounting, customs, and documentary requirements. The supplier must generally be the exporter or act through an authorized exporter and must retain the export declaration and electronic customs exit evidence, together with commercial and transport records linking the goods, customer, invoice, and shipment. A sale to an EU customer is generally not an export: it may qualify as an exempt intra-Community supply only where the customer has a valid VAT number in another Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, the reporting requirements are met, and the supplier holds evidence of transport. B2B services supplied to a business established outside France are generally outside the scope of French VAT under the place-of-supply rule rather than technically zero-rated; exceptions apply to services connected with immovable property, passenger transport, events, short-term vehicle hire, and other special categories. Input VAT may be recovered through French VAT returns where the statutory deduction conditions are met. Export exemption or input-tax recovery may be denied where evidence is incomplete, inconsistent, late, or connected with fraud.

  • Electronic export customs declaration accepted by French customs, including the Movement Reference Number or equivalent declaration reference
  • Electronic confirmation of exit from the European Union customs territory, such as the Export Accompanying Document and exit results
  • Commercial invoice identifying the French supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and export transaction
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, airway bill, CMR, or equivalent carrier evidence
  • Evidence linking the goods and invoice to the shipment, including purchase order, sales contract, serial or batch records where relevant, and proof of delivery or destination
  • EORI number and customs representation or power-of-attorney records where a customs agent is used
  • For an intra-Community supply rather than an export, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number, recapitulative statement data, and documentary proof of transport to another Member State
  • Banking and accounting records supporting consideration and transaction reconciliation; an inward-remittance certificate is not generally a mandatory French export-VAT document

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all French-established businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices through a registered Platform for Electronic Invoicing (Plateforme agréée), formerly referred to as a Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP); issuance obligations apply first to large companies and tax groups, with small and medium-sized enterprises generally entering the issuance phase from 1 September 2027. Business-to-government invoicing continues through the Chorus Pro system. The French model is not a universal real-time pre-clearance system: invoices are transmitted through an approved platform, while required transaction and payment data are reported to the tax administration. Invoices must contain mandatory French and EU VAT information, permit structured electronic processing, and support archiving, authenticity, integrity, and auditability. Imports and exports remain subject to customs electronic systems and EORI requirements. Timing and technical specifications remain subject to implementing legislation and official DGFiP guidance.

France has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. Export VAT exemption is substantiated primarily by customs exit and transport evidence, not by a mandatory foreign-exchange repatriation certificate. If the goods did not leave the EU, the evidence is deficient, or the exemption was fraudulently claimed, the DGFiP may reassess VAT, recover improperly refunded or deducted input VAT, charge late-payment interest, and impose tax penalties. The penalty may generally be 40% for deliberate failure, 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, and 80% for fraudulent activity, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards; other penalties may apply for inaccurate invoices, missing records, or reporting failures. Customs duties, import VAT, customs penalties, and joint or representative liability may also arise. Criminal prosecution may apply in serious tax-fraud cases. Clawback periods are generally governed by French VAT limitation periods, commonly three years, with longer periods possible in specified fraudulent or criminal circumstances.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is in the euro area and is fully liberalised under Articles 63–64 TFEU: the euro is freely convertible for current and capital account operations, and the French SAS has no exchange-control restriction on receiving export proceeds, paying import obligations, servicing trade debt or distributing dividends to non-resident shareholders. The only functional hurdles are: (i) the Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration for each cross-border transaction exceeding EUR 12,500, filed by the executing bank on the company's behalf; (ii) the French dividend withholding tax regime for distributions to foreign shareholders (standard 30% or treaty-reduced rate; EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption at 0% when the EU parent holds at least 10% of the SAS for a continuous two-year period and the intermediary holding conditions are met); and (iii) EU sanctions and the FDI screening rules of Articles L151-1 to L151-5 of the French Monetary and Financial Code, which do not cover an electric cargo bicycle/ISIC 3091 manufacturer and therefore permit unrestricted inbound capital injection and outward profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. France imposes no surrender or mandatory conversion of export receipts; the SAS may hold export receivables in EUR, USD, CNY or any other currency on a free account with an authorised bank or abroad. For statistical compliance: (i) each payment instruction or receipt above EUR 12,500 executed with a resident bank is reported by the bank in the Banque de France single balance-of-payments return; (ii) intra-EU merchandise dispatches above the annual threshold of EUR 460,000 must be lodged via the DEB (Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens) by the 10th working day of the following month; (iii) extra-EU export dispatches are captured by the customs export declaration (DAU) and the EU Export Control System; and (iv) any foreign bank account held by the SAS or its controlling shareholders must be declared annually on French tax form 3916.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) — French central bank and Eurosystem member acting as national competent authority for TARGET2, payment statistics and balance-of-payments reporting; the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance exercises exchange-control policy, foreign-exchange regulation and foreign investment screening; the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) licenses and supervises credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic-money institutions.
