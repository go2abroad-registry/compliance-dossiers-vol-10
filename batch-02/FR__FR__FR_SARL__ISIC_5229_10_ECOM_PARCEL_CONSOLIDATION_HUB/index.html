Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - Ministry of Economy and Finance; taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr / impots.gouv.fr/pro. Customs and indirect duties authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI); portal: douane.gouv.fr and pro.douane.gouv.fr (DELTA export filing). Large-entity file handling: Direction des Grandes Entreprises (DGE) when applicable.

E-government filing portal

For a French-incorporated SARL (subsidiary of foreign parent): (1) incorporation - execute statuts, appoint gérant, deposit share capital (no legal minimum under French law; EUR 1 acceptable), obtain registered-office proof and beneficial-ownership (RBE) details; (2) registration with the Guichet Unique operated by INPI (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) - file statutory incorporation forms (M-description), statuts, manager acceptance, domiciliation agreement, identity documents and RBE declaration; INSEE then allocates the SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) identifiers and the RCS K-bis extract is issued; (3) VAT identification - DGFiP automatically attributes the French intra-Community VAT identifier (format FR + 2-digit key + SIREN) upon RCS registration; the number is retrievable through the professional portal; (4) For a foreign entity registering a French fixed establishment/branch (non-SARL), Form 81032 is filed with the competent SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) to obtain the VAT ID; (5) Non-EU-established foreign principals carrying on taxable French supplies without an EU establishment must appoint a fiscal representative and may be required to post a VAT guarantee (Article 302-2 CGI); the SARL itself being French-established needs no fiscal representative; (6) Customs/export registration for DAE/DAU filing via DELTA (pro.douane.gouv.fr) with appointment of an EORI number (FR + SIREN) if any customs operations are performed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax (Article 206-1 CGI); territoriality principle applies so only profits attributable to French operations are taxable (Article 209-I CGI); small-SARL pass-through (IR) election is available under Article 239 bis AA CGI (conditions: fewer than 50 employees and turnover or balance sheet total below EUR 10 million); the entity is a French tax resident with unlimited liability for French-sourced income and limits French-source taxation on foreign activities.

Available election forms

France does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory. Available elections: (i) Article 239 bis AA CGI - small SARL option to be taxed as a partnership (pass-through to members) for a 5-year irrevocable renewable period, subject to the 50-employee/EUR 10-million tests; (ii) Article 223 A CGI - election to form a French tax group (intégration fiscale) where the French parent holds at least 95% of the SARL; the group files a single corporate tax return; (iii) Article 256 C bis CGI - VAT group election (assujettissement unique à la TVA) for closely linked French entities, effective from 2023; (iv) Article 206-3 CGI - election by partnerships to be subject to corporate tax instead of pass-through. Hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatches are not elective: Article 212 bis CGI (ATAD 2/BEPS Action 2) applies automatically, imposes deduction denials/income inclusions, and requires disclosure on Form 2257-SD.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under a fully liberalised capital and current account framework pursuant to the EU Treaty and the French Monetary and Financial Code. The euro is fully convertible; no exchange control licences are required for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or loan repayments. Hurdles are limited to tax formalities (withholding tax on outbound dividends, interest or royalties), substance/beneficial-ownership documentation, and anti-money laundering verification that delays bank processing rather than convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory obligation exists to surrender export proceeds or repatriate them within any prescribed timeline. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency in a permitted foreign currency account. A monthly balance-of-payments reporting declaration is required for cross-border transactions exceeding €750,000 (or equivalent) per month under Banque de France regulation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting in coordination with the European Central Bank and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for EU-licensed EMIs holding an ACPR or EEA passport under PSD2 (e.g., Airwallex, Qonto, Lydia); Low for offshore EMIs without French or EU authorisation. Legal constraint: a payment institution providing services in France must be licensed or passported; unlicensed offshore EMIs are prohibited from operating and may trigger correspondent bank restrictions and ACPR enforcement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.00% standard corporate income tax (CIT) rate on all profits of a French tax-resident SARL; reduced rate of 15.00% applies to the first EUR 42,500 of annual taxable profit where the SME ownership and turnover tests are satisfied, with the excess taxed at 25.00%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.00% withholding tax on dividends distributed by a French SARL to non-resident corporations (Article 119 bis (2) CGI); non-resident individuals are subject to the aggregate 30.00% flat levy comprising 12.80% withholding plus 17.20% social levies unless exempt by treaty or EU coordination; the full 25.00% rate is dispensed in favor of EU/EEA corporate parents satisfying the Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the annual return must be filed by the second working day immediately following 1 May; for any other fiscal year-end, the return is due within three months of the closing date; the first return of a newly created SARL is prorated from incorporation to the chosen closing date and filed within three months of that closing date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 gross turnover or gross balance-sheet total triggers the mandatory preparation of a full Local File and Master File (Article L13 AA LPF); groups whose consolidated worldwide revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000 must additionally file Country-by-Country reporting via Form 2258-SD; every French CIT filer, regardless of size, must submit the simplified Form 2257-SD transfer pricing declaration and maintain adequate contemporaneous evidence of its intercompany pricing.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute; substance is nonetheless a hard precondition embedded in the general abuse-of-law rule (Article L64 LPF), Article 155 A CGI on transfer of income to foreigners, the EU ATAD general anti-abuse clause, and treaty anti-abuse jurisprudence (PE, beneficial ownership, Limitation on Benefits). A parcel consolidation hub is treated as a French tax resident because its effective management, warehouse employees and linehaul contracting are physical in France; if substance is hollowed out, the tax administration may disregard the entity, reattribute profits to the foreign parent, deny EU directive or treaty benefits, and apply the 80% abuse-of-law penalty. The hub must therefore maintain genuine payroll, lease, banking, and operational records evidencing real sorting and injection activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Concealment of Revenue (CGI Articles 1729 b and 1741)

Statutory sanction: Civil uplift of 40% of the avoided tax for intentional bad faith or 80% for fraudulent maneuvering; criminal fine of EUR 500,000 and up to 5 years imprisonment for simple tax fraud; aggravated fraud involving organized, complex or cross-border schemes carries up to EUR 3,000,000 fine and 7 years imprisonment; late-payment interest of 0.20% per month and court-ordered publication of the judgment.

Director personal liability: The gérant may be held personally liable for the company's tax debt under Article L223-22 of the French Commercial Code where the omission results from faute de gestion, with civil restitution of the unpaid taxes and penalties; in aggravated cases the director faces criminal conviction, asset seizure, exit/travel prohibitions, and imprisonment up to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Hidden Profit Extraction (CGI Article 57)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the French taxable base, denial of deduction of the non-arm's-length expenses, upward adjustment of profit, 0.20% monthly interest, 40% penalty for deliberate mispricing, and a specific documentation penalty of 5% of the primary adjustment with a EUR 10,000 minimum per fiscal year; the statute of limitations extends to 12 years for transactions with entities located in non-cooperative states.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign abusive intra-group agreements can be judicially ordered to contribute personal funds to the shortfall under the comblement de passif action; repeated misconduct triggers disqualification from management, travel bans in cross-border collection, and criminal liability where the pricing arrangements are used to conceal fraud.
Abuse of Law and Sham Shell Structure (Article L64 LPF)

Statutory sanction: The administration disregards the artificial steps, re-files the tax base in the true beneficial owner's hands, imposes the 80% abuse-of-law penalty on the avoided tax, and recovers the underlying withholding tax at source with interest; disclosure obligations and extended assessment windows compound the exposure.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil where substance is fictitious, the gérant is held jointly and severally liable for the reinstated tax and 80% penalties, professional disqualification of up to 15 years may be ordered, travel bans and asset-freezing injunctions are available, and imprisonment up to 5 years applies if the sham is part of an intentional evasion scheme.
Unlicensed Payment Services and AML Non-Compliance (Monetary and Financial Code)

Statutory sanction: Illegal exercise of payment services is a criminal offense punishable by up to 7 years imprisonment and a EUR 750,000 fine, together with confiscation of the related funds; AML violations trigger administrative fines set by the ACPR, prohibition of activity, and mandatory reporting sanctions; intermingling of customer funds with corporate treasury constitutes embezzlement under criminal law.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or tolerate the unlicensed settlement flow face the same criminal penalties, personal civil restitution to affected customers, preventive attachment of personal assets, professional disqualification, and cross-border travel restrictions pending criminal proceedings.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Dividend Repatriation (CGI Articles 119 bis and 119 ter)

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid withholding tax, 40% bad-faith penalty, 0.20% monthly late interest, and refusal of the directive exemption with retroactive effect; the paying SARL is treated as the withholding agent and bears primary liability for the uncollected amount.

Director personal liability: The gérant who signs the general meeting distribution resolution and executes the payment is personally liable for the un-withheld tax under the Commercial Code; criminal prosecution for evasion of source-state tax may attach where the distribution was deliberately disguised, leading to civil restitution, travel bans and imprisonment.
