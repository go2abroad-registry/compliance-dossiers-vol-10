Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office in France under French Commercial Code Articles L.123-11 and following. Domiciliation at a qualified commercial domiciliation provider is generally permissible, subject to an approved domiciliation contract and the provider's legal and regulatory obligations. A virtual office is not sufficient where it is merely a mail-forwarding address without compliant domiciliation documentation. For manufacture and export of rugged industrial computers and edge gateways, the registered office may be domiciled separately from the manufacturing, warehouse, testing, customs, or operational premises, but the company should maintain demonstrable commercial substance, appropriate premises or outsourced operational arrangements, accounting records, personnel or service contracts, product-compliance files, customs records, and accessible management records. A purely nominal office presents heightened banking, tax, customs, AML, and substance risk.

Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation procedure with a universal reservation period. Availability checks are non-binding and do not create exclusive rights. If the incorporation filing or name is rejected, obtain the INPI or registry deficiency and rejection notice, identify whether the issue concerns prior rights, prohibited wording, misleading activity, missing legal-form wording, or an administrative defect, and submit corrected documents through the Guichet unique within the stated correction period. For an adverse administrative decision, request reconsideration from the competent registry or authority and, where appropriate, file an administrative challenge before the competent administrative court within the applicable time limit, normally two months from notification for an administrative decision. A separate civil or commercial action may be necessary for trademark, trade-name, or unfair-competition disputes. A new compliant name may be filed without waiting for a statutory reservation period.

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry) acting on the dossier routed through the Guichet unique électronique des formalités d'entreprises operated by INPI (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); the company is enrolled on the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital for an SAS; the statuts freely set the share capital (Article L.227-2 Code de commerce). At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid up on incorporation and the balance within five years. Cash subscription funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a licensed bank, a notaire or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and are released only upon issuance of the Kbis. In-kind contributions must be fully provided and, if their value exceeds EUR 30,000 or exceeds half the share capital, assessed by a commissaire aux apports unless waived unanimously.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – official portal: www.impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a SAS must complete business registration through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). The online application automatically allocates a SIREN and SIRET number via the INSEE registry and subsequently registers the company with DGFiP for corporate income tax (IS), VAT (TVA), and the normal tax system. Non-resident companies acting without a French permanent establishment must register directly with the DGFiP non-resident tax service (Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères) to obtain a French VAT identification number (TVA intracommunautaire) and, where required, a corporate tax registration file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is by default subject to French corporate income tax at entity level. Available elections include: (i) option for tax consolidation (régime d'intégration fiscale) under Articles 223 A to 223 U of the French General Tax Code (CGI), enabling a parent SAS to integrate wholly-owned subsidiaries; (ii) option for small and medium-sized enterprises (PME) to pay reduced corporate income tax rates; (iii) option for VAT exemption on qualifying intra-group transactions or on rental income under limited conditions; and (iv) no US-style check-the-box election exists under French law, but hybrid mismatch election mechanisms apply for entities qualifying under EU ATAD provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€91,900 for supplies of goods (CA annuel), €36,800 for providers of services, with no threshold applied to non-resident suppliers; intra-EU distance sales are subject to the One Stop Shop (OSS) regime for annual cross-border turnover below €10,000 (EU-wide threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) dispatches of goods to another EU Member State where the transferee is identified for VAT, supported by a valid intra-company or third-party documentation; and (ii) definitive exports of goods outside the EU under Article 262-II of the CGI. Services directly linked to exported goods and certain qualifying offshore services supplied to non-residents may also benefit from zero-rating under Articles 259 A and 259 B of the CGI. To validly zero-rate, the supplier must hold full documentary evidence of the physical export and, for intra-EU supplies, record the customer's valid EU VAT number and the accuracy of the value and transaction in the recapitulative statement (DEB).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Document Administratif Unique / DAU form EX)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR international transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation proving settlement in foreign currency from the export contract
  • Commercial invoice including Incoterms 2020 and export declaration number
  • Intra-EU acquisition statement (for intra-EU zero-rating) or customs validation of exit from the EU customs territory

Mandatory electronic invoicing will be phased in from 1 September 2026 for French domestic B2B transactions, requiring clearance through the DGFiP approved invoicing platforms (Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires) and the Public Billing Portal (Portail Public de Facturation, Chorus Pro). Real-time element validation and attestation of invoice authenticity are required; tax data is transmitted to the DGFiP for automated fiscal control. Temporary digital stamping applies only to certain excise goods; VAT invoices are subject to mandatory digital transmission for audit purposes.

If zero-rating is claimed without full documentary proof, DGFiP may retroactively reassess VAT at 20%, apply a 40% tax crime penalty (majoration de 40% in cases of lack of good faith) or a 80% penalty (mauvaise foi or entrave au contrôle). Interest for late payment applies monthly. In cases of fraudulent export zero-rating schemes, criminal penalties of up to 3 years imprisonment and €45,000 fine may be imposed (Article 1741 CGI), and the taxpayer is jointly liable for unpaid VAT and penalties. The FX repatriation deadline is strictly monitored under French customs and tax doctrine; failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days without justified cause triggers the reversal of exports for VAT purposes, withdrawal of zero-rating, and additional penalties, including potential suspension of the taxpayer's VAT clearance status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a eurozone member. EUR is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts under EU free movement of capital (TFEU Art. 63). There are no exchange controls, no prior authorisation, and no profit-remittance quotas for a French SAS. Dividend, interest, royalty and liquidation proceeds may be remitted freely to non-resident shareholders after satisfaction of French withholding tax (if any) under applicable tax treaties. Residual constraints are limited to EU/UN sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering reporting, and, for non-EU inbound direct investment in sensitive sectors, the French foreign-investment screening regime (IEF 151-3 et seq. Code monétaire et financier).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or repatriation of hard-currency export proceeds is imposed on French exporters. Statutory conversion ratio to EUR is 0% – 0% of export receipts must be converted; exporters may retain foreign currency accounts (comptes en devises) with French authorised banks. Balance-of-payments reporting (FDE / déclaration de la Banque de France) is statistical only and is generally discharged by the banking intermediary for transactions above materiality thresholds; the exporter retains an obligation to answer the Banque de France annual survey where its cross-border flows exceed reporting thresholds. There is no statutory time limit for repatriation, but EUR-denominated invoicing is market-standard.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as central bank, exercising delegated foreign-exchange monitoring on behalf of the European Central Bank (ECB); the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor, Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty) retains competence for international financial sanctions and investment-control authorizations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW for primary treasury via third-country EMIs; MEDIUM for EU/EEA-licensed EMIs operating under PSD2 passporting into France. Legal constraints: a non-EU EMI (e.g., UK or Singapore EMI) may not provide payment-account services to a French resident SAS without an ACPR authorisation or an EU-authorised EMI branch/agent. EU/EEA EMIs (licensed under the EU Electronic Money Directive 2009/110/EC, passported into France) can technically host EUR IBANs and process SEPA/MT103-like transfers, but cannot issue or confirm documentary Letters of Credit (Swift_LC) because they lack banking-credit powers under French banking law (L.311-1 Code monétaire et financier). EMIs are therefore viable only for incidental disbursements, not for the Swift_LC/TT_Wire trade-finance backbone of this industrial exporter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the non-resident shareholder into the French SAS must be routed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the dedicated Capital Escrow Account (compte bloqué) opened prior to RCS registration, or to the operational EUR account post-registration. Regulatory protocol: (i) reference must expressly cite 'Apport en capital – souscription d'actions [entity name]' plus the share-subscription deed number; (ii) use ISO 20022 purpose code 'CABB' (Capital Contribution in Cash) or 'EQUI' (Equity-linked instruments) as instructed by the receiving bank; (iii) after execution of the resolution and payment, the RCS registration (Kbis update) must be delivered to the bank as utilisation proof; (iv) the Banque de France balance-of-payments survey obligation is satisfied by automatic bank reporting, and no prior exchange-control authorisation is required under TFEU Art. 63. Banks will conduct a sanctions and source-of-funds verification on the contributing legal entity; a confirmatory email of intended timing should be sent to the relationship manager 48 hours before wiring.Primary clearing rails for trade settlements of the industrial edge-computing exporter are: (i) TT/Wire settlement via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'TRAD' (Trade-related) or 'INVC' (Invoice payment) for low-value supplier payments, cleared in EUR via TARGET2 and in USD via Fedwire/CHIPS with the French bank's US correspondent; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit – import LC to component suppliers via SWIFT MT700, MT707 amendments and MT754/MT752 advice of acceptance, and export LC issuance is typically routed through the confirming bank in the buyer's country; with standard documentation packs (commercial invoice, AWB/CMR/BL, packing list, certificate of origin (EUR.1/EX-A under EU trade agreements), insurance certificate, and pre-shipment inspection certificates where required). Documentary collections (MT400/MT475) are permitted as a secondary rail. All EUR clearing uses TARGET2; no dedicated French vostro account is required if a tier-1 foreign branch is used, but non-EUR accounts in the same bank optimise cost.Outright deliverable FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNY) commencing up to 12 months, documented under a French ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) Master Agreement with an ACPR-licensed credit institution.FX swaps (swap de change) for intra-month liquidity conversion between EUR operational accounts and USD disbursement accounts.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (XCCY) solely for matched balance-sheet financing of long-term USD receivables or shareholder loans.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only for currencies without deliverable onshore markets (e.g., offshore CNY/CNH, INR, KRW) and executed via the French bank's offshore desk, subject to EMIR margin rules.OTC FX options (physical or cash-settled premiums) purchased from an ACPR-authorised counterparty; writing of options is not permitted by this entity as a non-financial counterparty without MiFID licensing.All OTC derivatives are subject to EMIR trade reporting, margin/clearing rules (risk-mitigation techniques) and the new EU SFTR/financial transactions tax review; documentary-collateral annexes (CSA) must be negotiated pre-execution.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: LOW for primary treasury via third-country EMIs; MEDIUM for EU/EEA-licensed EMIs operating under PSD2 passporting into France. Legal constraints: a non-EU EMI (e.g., UK or Singapore EMI) may not provide payment-account services to a French resident SAS without an ACPR authorisation or an EU-authorised EMI branch/agent. EU/EEA EMIs (licensed under the EU Electronic Money Directive 2009/110/EC, passported into France) can technically host EUR IBANs and process SEPA/MT103-like transfers, but cannot issue or confirm documentary Letters of Credit (Swift_LC) because they lack banking-credit powers under French banking law (L.311-1 Code monétaire et financier). EMIs are therefore viable only for incidental disbursements, not for the Swift_LC/TT_Wire trade-finance backbone of this industrial exporter.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. Qualifying small and medium-sized companies may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit if, among other conditions, annual turnover does not exceed EUR 10 million, capital is fully paid up, and at least 75% of capital is held by individuals or qualifying companies meeting the ownership test.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid by a French SAS to a non-resident are generally subject to 25% withholding tax for corporate recipients, subject to treaty relief and statutory exemptions. A 12.8% domestic rate generally applies to individual recipients, subject to the applicable rules. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may provide an exemption for qualifying EU parent companies meeting ownership, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

File within three months after the end of the financial year. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the statutory filing deadline is generally the second working day after 1 May; the tax administration commonly allows an additional 15 calendar days for electronic filing as an administrative tolerance, not a statutory extension. The final corporate income tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end (15 May for a 31 December year-end). Installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December for a calendar-year company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Comprehensive transfer-pricing documentation under Article L.13 AA of the French Tax Procedures Code is generally required where the French entity, or a qualifying related entity, has annual turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, or meets specified ownership or control links to an entity meeting the threshold. A reduced transfer-pricing declaration (Form 2257-SD) generally applies to entities meeting the lower statutory threshold, including turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 50 million, or qualifying links to an entity meeting that threshold. Verify the current statutory perimeter and filing rules for the relevant year.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. French rules nevertheless require transactions and entities to have genuine commercial substance and may deny benefits or deductions under transfer-pricing, permanent-establishment, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, anti-hybrid, controlled-foreign-company, and abuse-of-law rules. Related-party pricing must satisfy the arm's-length principle. Maintain evidence of functions performed, assets used, risks assumed, personnel, decision-making, and business rationale.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax fraud and false tax filings

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessments, late-payment interest, and conduct-based surcharges may apply. Tax fraud can be prosecuted under the French Tax Code and Penal Code; an individual may face up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, with higher penalties, including up to seven years and EUR 3 million, in aggravated cases, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: A director is not automatically liable for company tax debts solely by holding office. Personal criminal liability may arise from personal participation or complicity. Under Article L.267 of the French Tax Procedures Code, a director may be held jointly and severally liable where fraudulent maneuvers or serious and repeated tax-law violations make collection impossible. Civil recovery, asset seizure, and criminal restrictions may follow a court judgment; travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty.
Transfer-pricing noncompliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustments may increase taxable profit and trigger interest and penalties. Failure to provide documentation after formal notice can attract a penalty generally equal to the greater of EUR 10,000 or 0.5% of transactions not documented, subject to the statutory ceiling and rules; incomplete or inaccurate documentation may attract a penalty generally equal to 0.5% of transactions concerned or 5% of transferred profits, subject to minimums and statutory conditions. Additional general tax penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: The company is ordinarily liable for assessments and penalties. Directors may face personal criminal liability for intentional participation in fraud and may be subject to Article L.267 joint and several liability where its conditions are met.
Undeclared permanent establishment or concealed activity

Statutory sanction: French-source profits may be assessed with interest and penalties, including the 80% surcharge for concealed activity where statutory conditions are met. VAT, payroll, registration, and other obligations may also arise.

Director personal liability: The foreign enterprise and any French taxpayer may be assessed under applicable rules. Personal director liability requires a separate statutory or criminal basis; it is not automatic.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, interest, and applicable penalties. Treaty relief may be denied if residence, beneficial ownership, documentation, or anti-abuse requirements are not met.

Director personal liability: The paying company is primarily responsible. Directors may incur personal criminal liability for intentional evasion or joint and several liability where a specific statutory provision applies; ownership or directorship alone does not create automatic personal liability.
Shell-company, beneficial-ownership, or AML violations

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include registration corrections, account restrictions, transaction rejection, tax reassessment, administrative sanctions, and criminal prosecution for money laundering or related offenses. Money laundering may be punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with increased penalties in aggravated cases, subject to the facts and applicable law.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate may face personal criminal and civil liability. Corporate limited liability does not protect an individual from liability for personal criminal conduct; travel bans or imprisonment require an applicable legal basis and judicial process.
Failure to preserve accounting records or obstructing a tax audit

Statutory sanction: The administration may reconstruct taxable income, reject unsupported deductions, apply assessment penalties and interest, and impose applicable fixed or tax-based fines. Criminal penalties may apply to intentional falsification or obstruction.

Director personal liability: The company is subject to tax adjustments and administrative penalties. A director may be personally liable for intentional misconduct or under a specific statutory rule, but is not automatically liable for every company-level assessment.
