Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum for a SARL (Article L.223-2 of the Code de commerce; the capital may be set freely, even at €1). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements, and IMF Article VIII status. The euro is freely convertible and there are no quantitative limits on profit remittance, dividend distribution, interest payments, royalty payments, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds to non-residents. Practical constraints are documentary: the platform must file tax-residence documentation, apply treaty relief forms, and comply with sanctions screening, but no prior administrative authorization or conversion restriction applies to any treasury flow. All financial transactions must be routed through an authorized intermediary bank under Articles L.151-1 to L.151-7 of the Monetary and Financial Code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio apply in France. Export receipts, loan collections, interest income, and platform fees may be retained in EUR or in any freely convertible foreign currency and may be repatriated without penalty. The only binding regulatory requirement is statistical: any cross-border transaction exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported by the SARL's French bank to Banque de France in the monthly balance-of-payments bulletin de liaison; banks are the declarants, and the corporate treasury's duty is to provide correct purpose codes and supporting documents so the bank can classify the operation under the CN 2023 statistical nomenclature.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting as the French foreign-exchange statistics authority and balance-of-payments reporting centre under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier); the euro-area monetary policy authority is the European Central Bank within the Eurosystem, while prudential licensing and supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions, and e-money institutions rests with the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) and exchange-control policy oversight sits with the Direction Générale du Trésor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM HIGH. A non-French EU-licensed EMI or payment institution can passport e-money and payment services into France under PSD2 via the ACPR notification procedure, which resolves the payment-rail and wallet layer. However, e-money and payment-service passporting alone cannot authorize P2P micro-lending activity in France: the lending platform itself requires an ACPR authorization as a European Crowdfunding Service Provider (ECSPR / prestataire de services de financement participatif) with ESMA registration and an ORIAS-listed intermediation status, and no EMI licence on its own authorizes the granting of credit. The workable structure is an ACPR-licensed ECSPR borrower-lending platform holding client funds under the safeguarding rules in a French credit institution or an EU EMI, with the EMI operating SEPA Direct Debits and local wallet payouts. French regulators apply enhanced scrutiny to outsourcing, white-labeling, and sub-agent arrangements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 for EUR and non-EUR injections from the non-resident parent to the SARL's EUR operational account (IBAN FR76...). The payment order must carry the French balance-of-payments reason code under the CN 2023 nomenclature: for an equity increase, use the direct-investment equity code for participation in the capital of a resident enterprise; for a shareholder advance, use the direct-investment intercompany-advance code. The French bank will record the bulletin de liaison automatically for any cross-border operation above EUR 12,500, so the remitter must include a clear purpose description in the Remittance Information field. Supporting documents to pre-submit to the bank's Financial Crime Unit include the notarized capital-increase deed or shareholders' resolution, the bank certificate of deposit des fonds, the share-subscription statement, and beneficial-owner declarations. For EUR flows within the SEPA area, a standard SEPA Credit Transfer with identical purpose text is sufficient, while non-EUR flows require the structured SWIFT MT103 with notarized source-of-funds evidence; any inbound wire with an ambiguous purpose code or missing voided-documentation package will be rejected or frozen.Clearing rails available through the French bank: (1) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border vendor, partner, interest, and dividend payments, with the correct CN 2023 reason code, full beneficiary legal name, IBAN or account number, and applicable tax documentation for reduced withholding; (2) irrevocable commercial letters of credit subject to UCP 600 and documentary collections under URC 522 for trade-linked settlements where the platform purchases software, equipment, or services cross-border; (3) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EEA disbursements, including wallet payouts to borrowers executed through the licensed ECSPR or EMI partner; (4) SEPA Direct Debit Core and B2B mandates for collecting micro-loan installments from borrower accounts; (5) local-wallet and payment-institution settlements via the EMI partner's clearing infrastructure. All transactions settle in TARGET2 or EURO1 at the repository bank, and all outbound payments above EUR 12,500 are captured in the bank's monthly balance-of-payments return to Banque de France, requiring accurate purpose codes and documentary evidence retained in the treasury archive.Outright FX forward (change à terme ferme) in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and other freely convertible major currencies, with tenors matched to the platform's lending-cycle and funding-maturity profile.FX swap (swap de change) for short-term liquidity management between EUR and USD or GBP clearing floats.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) for hedging non-convertible or restricted emerging-market currencies such as CNY, INR, or NGN where the platform holds genuine non-EUR collection or investment exposure, subject to treasury-policy approval and documented hedge intent.Cross-currency interest-rate swap (CCIRS / swap de devises et de taux) for matched-funding of cross-currency shareholder advances, loans, or bond-style funding.Vanilla FX option (option de change) purchased for risk management of committed foreign-currency receipts; writing of naked options is not permitted under conservative French bank treasury policies.FX collar and participating-forward structures available at the bank's bilateral discretion, provided the position is demonstrably non-speculative and aligned with the SARL's approved treasury and hedging strategy.
