Competent corporate registry

French Commercial Code, Articles L.223-1 et seq. (SARL); Articles L.123-1 et seq. (commercial register); and Articles R.123-1 et seq. (registration formalities).

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic business-creation formality submitted through the INPI Guichet unique, resulting in registration in the Registre national des entreprises and, for a commercial SARL, the Registre du commerce et des sociétés.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital; capital may be set at EUR 1. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance paid within five years of registration. Cash contributions are deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released after registration. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed; a commissaire aux apports is generally required unless the statutory unanimous waiver conditions are met.

Funding & unlock path: Before operations, obtain applicable vessel safety, seaworthiness, crew qualification, and manning documentation; secure port or site access and any required fire-safety approval for shore facilities. Shore premises must meet applicable building, fire, and workplace requirements, including employer risk assessment and occupational-safety duties. Structural-load inspection or facility-specific civil-defence clearance is required where applicable to the premises or project, not as a universal SARL incorporation condition.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital; capital may be set at EUR 1. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance paid within five years of registration. Cash contributions are deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released after registration. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed; a commissaire aux apports is generally required unless the statutory unanimous waiver conditions are met.

No general industrial licensing board applies to offshore towage and salvage. The competent maritime authorities include the Directorate General for Maritime Affairs, Fisheries and Aquaculture (DGAMPA), the relevant regional maritime authority and departmental directorate for territories and the sea (DDTM), and the competent maritime prefect and port authority, according to vessel, port, and operating location.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) or, for non-established businesses, the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). Principal digital portal: impots.gouv.fr. Corporate formation and business registration are filed through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). Customs registration and EORI administration are handled by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI) through douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

File the initial business tax registration information with the competent SIE through the applicable French tax-registration process. 3. Apply for a French VAT identification number consisting of the prefix FR followed by a two-digit control key and the nine-digit SIREN number. The number is issued by the DGFiP after verification of the entity, activity, management, premises, and expected taxable transactions. 4. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS), currently at a standard rate of 25% subject to applicable conditions and reliefs. Certain SARLs may elect temporary income-tax treatment under the statutory partnership-style regime, generally for up to five fiscal years and subject to legal eligibility conditions, including qualifying ownership, size, age, and activity requirements. A qualifying family SARL may elect income-tax treatment without the ordinary five-year limitation, subject to the family-ownership and activity conditions. France has no United States-style general check-the-box election. Entity classification is determined by French company law and tax statutes. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the relevant EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive provisions and can deny deductions, include income, or otherwise neutralize mismatches; they are not elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The principal French domestic franchise-in-base threshold for services is EUR 37,500 of prior-year turnover, with a higher tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250 subject to statutory conditions. For activities principally involving supplies of goods, the ordinary thresholds are EUR 85,000 and EUR 93,500 respectively. These thresholds concern eligibility for the small-business VAT exemption and do not eliminate VAT registration obligations arising from imports, intra-Community transactions, reverse-charge transactions, taxable French establishments, or special sector rules. Offshore towing and salvage revenues must be analyzed transaction-by-transaction because the place of supply and maritime exemptions may apply independently of turnover thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT where the goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU by the seller or by the customer, and the exporter can substantiate the export through customs evidence and matching commercial records. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for exemption rather than domestic zero-rating where the statutory customer VAT-identification, transport, and reporting conditions are satisfied. Services supplied to a business established outside France are generally outside the scope of French VAT under the B2B general place-of-supply rule, rather than technically zero-rated; the customer’s status and location must be documented. Services connected with vessels, towing, salvage, freight, and emergency maritime operations require separate analysis under the specific place-of-supply and exemption provisions. Relevant exemptions may apply to qualifying sea-going vessels, their equipment, and specified services, but they are conditional and do not automatically cover every offshore contract. A French VAT refund or deduction claim requires valid purchase invoices, direct business use, correct VAT treatment, accounting records, and proof that any export or exempt activity satisfies the applicable legal conditions.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and MRN, with customs exit confirmation or equivalent electronic evidence of exit from the European Union
  • Commercial invoice, contract, purchase order, and detailed description of the goods or maritime equipment
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, sea waybill, or equivalent carrier evidence where applicable
  • Evidence identifying the exporter, consignee, destination outside the European Union, and transport chain
  • Evidence of delivery or receipt outside the European Union where required to corroborate the export file
  • Applicable export authorisations, licences, sanctions-screening records, and vessel or cargo documentation
  • VAT records linking the export declaration, invoice, accounting entry, customs MRN, and transport evidence
  • Bank payment evidence or other commercial settlement evidence; France does not generally require a standalone inward-remittance certificate as a statutory condition for export VAT exemption
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer’s valid EU VAT number, transport evidence, and required EC Sales List reporting records

France does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system for all domestic transactions. The enacted electronic-invoicing reform requires all businesses established in France to be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Issuance obligations are phased: large enterprises and in-scope intermediate-sized enterprises from 1 September 2026, and small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027, subject to legislative implementation details and permitted extensions. Electronic invoices must generally use a compliant structured or hybrid format and be transmitted through an approved private platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire, PDP), with the public directory supporting routing. Electronic reporting of relevant transaction and payment data, including certain cross-border transactions and transactions with non-business customers, will apply on the applicable reform timetable. Current VAT invoices must contain mandatory particulars, permit reliable identification of the issuer and recipient, preserve integrity and authenticity, and be retained for the statutory period. No general digital stamp or pre-issuance DGFiP clearance is presently required.

The DGFiP may deny or reverse the VAT exemption or deduction and assess the unpaid VAT, late-payment interest, and applicable penalties where export, transport, customer status, vessel qualification, or place-of-supply evidence is incomplete or false. The standard tax penalty may be 40% for deliberate failure and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights; a 5% penalty may apply in specified invoicing or reporting situations, while a 10% penalty can apply to certain late-payment or filing defaults. Late-payment interest is generally charged at 0.20% per month, subject to statutory rules. False or fabricated customs or tax documents may trigger customs penalties, criminal tax-fraud exposure, sanctions-related consequences, and recovery from the relevant transaction date. Ineligible input-VAT refunds may be clawed back with interest and penalties. The administration can conduct documentary or on-site audits, require production of customs and transport records, compare VAT returns with customs data, examine bank and accounting evidence, and apply joint-and-several liability or intermediary liability where legally available.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full Eurozone member with complete current account and capital account convertibility under EU law. Profit remittance is unrestricted for EU parent entities; for non-EU shareholders, France applies no quantitative FX controls but imposes mandatory balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting to the Banque de France for cross-border flows exceeding EUR 750,000 per calendar month or for all direct investment transactions. Dividends to non-resident companies are subject to withholding tax unless reduced by tax treaty (commonly 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive with minimum holding requirements; otherwise 25% / treaty-reduced). No material hurdles to profit repatriation exist beyond standard corporate tax, dividend withholding tax, and anti-abuse compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose any statutory timeline for surrender of export proceeds or mandatory conversion ratios. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted freely. However, all cross-border direct investment transactions and any transaction exceeding EUR 750,000 require submission of BOP declarations to the Banque de France via the FIBI reporting system. For marine salvage services rendered to non-residents, proceeds must be booked as services exports and reported under BOP heading 'Services - Transportation / Other services' as applicable; no FX conversion is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) and Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for balance-of-payments reporting; Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with significant legal constraints. French-regulated EMIs (licensed under ACPR with e-money passport) such as Qonto, Shine, or Lydia/SumUp may open accounts remotely and facilitate EUR clearing, but they generally do not provide SWIFT correspondent banking, letters of credit, or escrow-grade maritime salvage services. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Lithuania-licensed, UK-licensed, or non-EEA) face restricted viability: French AML/KYC rules (Code Monétaire et Financier, Art. L561 et seq.) treat such providers as higher-risk non-bank counterparties; French banks frequently reject transfers from unregulated or offshore EMIs; and commercial counterparties in salvage/towage (Lloyd's market, insurers, shipowners) typically require settlement through a licensed EU bank. Rating: Medium for local ACPR-licensed EMI as operational sub-account; Low for offshore EMI as primary clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for shareholder capital injection: 1) Use SWIFT MT103 wire from investor's bank to the SARL's EUR account in France (BNP Paribas, Société Générale, or Crédit Agricole). 2) Purpose code must state 'Direct Investment in Capital' — under French reporting use BOP form 'Déclaration de transaction (FIBI)' with тип 'CAPITAL / DIVIDEND / TRANSFER'. Use the official French purpose code '51' for investments / contributions to capital, or mention 'capital increase / numéraire' in free text field. 3) For transfers exceeding EUR 750,000 per month, the bank will require the BOP reporting declaration to be completed by the account holder within 20 days following the end of the month in which the transfer occurred; otherwise the bank may block the wire. 4) Verify the resident investor identity and UBO beneficial owner; a French SARL with non-resident UBO must produce the 'Gérant' identity, corporate registry Kbis, and a certified copy of the shareholder resolution. 5) For escrow structures involving Lloyd's salvage security, funds should be tagged in the account reference as 'ESCROW - Salvage Security' and a separate escrow sub-account opened at the same banking institution to avoid commingling.Preferred clearing rail for outbound service/disbursement: (a) Standard commercial trade settlements for maritime services are executed via SWIFT MT103/2 and target2/SEPA in EUR; for non-EUR settlement use SWIFT MT103 with USD/GBP nostro accounts. For salvage-casualty disbursements, require payment in accordance with Lloyd's Standard Form terms (if contractually specified): payment to the salvor's nominated Euro account via MT103 with purpose code 'Settlement of salvage remuneration' (Services - Other business services). (b) For contracts involving the physical repair of tugboats or equipment procurement, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) is recommended under UCP600 with bill-of-lading or delivery certificate as documents; given service nature, standby LCs (ISBP745) for security guarantees also apply. (c) For subcontracting disbursements (fuel, ship chandlery, crew wages), use direct MT103 with references to the contract line item and pre-cleared beneficiary banking compliance. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with significant legal constraints. French-regulated EMIs (licensed under ACPR with e-money passport) such as Qonto, Shine, or Lydia/SumUp may open accounts remotely and facilitate EUR clearing, but they generally do not provide SWIFT correspondent banking, letters of credit, or escrow-grade maritime salvage services. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Lithuania-licensed, UK-licensed, or non-EEA) face restricted viability: French AML/KYC rules (Code Monétaire et Financier, Art. L561 et seq.) treat such providers as higher-risk non-bank counterparties; French banks frequently reject transfers from unregulated or offshore EMIs; and commercial counterparties in salvage/towage (Lloyd's market, insurers, shipowners) typically require settlement through a licensed EU bank. Rating: Medium for local ACPR-licensed EMI as operational sub-account; Low for offshore EMI as primary clearing rail.
