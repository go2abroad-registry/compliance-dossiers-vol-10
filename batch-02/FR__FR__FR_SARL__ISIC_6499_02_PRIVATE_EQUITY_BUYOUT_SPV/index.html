Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the official portal at https://www.impots.gouv.fr/

E-government filing portal

French SARL entities obtain SIREN/SIRET and VAT ID (FR followed by SIREN) through mandatory registration with the tax authorities. Non-resident owners may require a fiscal representative. The process involves online submission via guichet-entreprises.fr or impots.gouv.fr, providing legal incorporation documents, registered office address, activity code (NAF/APE), and capital details. Foreign-owned entities follow the same procedure as domestic entities under French law.

Non-resident owners may require a fiscal representative. The process involves online submission via guichet-entreprises.fr or impots.gouv.fr, providing legal incorporation documents, registered office address, activity code (NAF/APE), and capital details. Foreign-owned entities follow the same procedure as domestic entities under French law.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations qualify for 0% VAT rate. Offshore services may qualify under specific conditions if the service is performed outside France and the recipient is non-EU. Proof of export is mandatory, including delivery to a third country or use by non-EU customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation en Douane - DAU)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment from foreign customer

Mandatory implementation of electronic invoicing (Factur-X standard) with real-time transmission to tax authorities via APIs. Digital stamping (horodatage) required for all B2B invoices. Non-compliance can result in fines and loss of VAT deduction rights.

Fraudulent zero-rating claims lead to full repayment of erroneously zero-rated VAT plus interest, administrative fines up to 5% of the VAT amount, and potential criminal sanctions including imprisonment. Retrospective audits can claw back benefits if documentation is insufficient or export not proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a fully liberalised euro-area jurisdiction: there is no general exchange-control authorisation, no quantitative convertibility limit and no resident/non-resident distinction for current or capital account transactions. The applicable legal framework is the EU free movement of capital (TFEU Art. 63), the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, Arts. L151-1 et seq. and L152-1 et seq.). Capital and current account euro flows are fully convertible; profit remittances from a French SARL are freely executable by dividend, share buyback or reserve distribution once the French mandatory legal reserve (5% of annual profits up to 10% of share capital) has been constituted and the gérant/EGM formalities are completed. French withholding tax applies to distributions to non-EU corporate shareholders unless reduced under an applicable tax treaty; EU corporate shareholders benefit from the Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption. The only operational hurdles are statistical and screening-related: (i) French balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque de France applies where the cumulative monthly cross-border transactions of the company with non-residents equal or exceed EUR 750,000; and (ii) investment by a non-EU investor in a French entity requires assessment of the EU/French FDI regime, prior ministerial authorisation being required only for sensitive activities listed in Art. R151-3 CMF—ISIC 6499 is non-sensitive, so no prior authorisation is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline and no forced conversion of export proceeds into EUR. Exporters and trading companies may freely hold and use foreign currency receipts abroad or in French bank accounts, without any obligation to repatriate or convert within a statutory period. The only relevant duties are (i) statistical reporting to the Banque de France for monthly cumulative cross-border transfers of EUR 750,000 or more, filed by the account-holding bank; and (ii) customs cash declaration for physical cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more under EU Regulation 2018/1672. A private-equity buyout SPV with ISIC 6499 status has no export- surrender obligations whatsoever.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the Eurosystem and under the policy framework of the European Central Bank. Banking supervision is exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution), an independent authority organically attached to the Banque de France. Foreign-exchange policy, capital-movement oversight and foreign-direct-investment screening are implemented by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) within the Ministry of Economy and Finance, in co-operation with the Banque de France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM and strictly auxiliary. Fintech Electronic Money Institutions (établissements de monnaie électronique authorised by the ACPR under PSD2) may legally open payment accounts, issue e-money, execute SEPA and SWIFT transfers and issue cards. However, an EMI is not a credit institution: it cannot accept deposits, grant credit, pay interest, operate a true M&A escrow (séquestre) or participate directly in TARGET2/T2 settlement. For an ISIC 6499 leveraged buyout SPV, the EMI channel is viable only for low-value operating expenses (consultancy invoices, domiciliation fees, regulatory costs); all acquisition consideration, loan drawdowns, equity injections and intercompany funding must be held at a licensed French or EU credit institution to comply with French banking and safeguarding rules.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Multi-tier non-resident ownership structure (Cayman/Luxembourg fund → intermediate holdco → FR SARL) rendering ultimate beneficial owners opaque; French banks classify the entity as a shell-company risk.

Root cause: Multi-tier non-resident ownership structure (Cayman/Luxembourg fund → intermediate holdco → FR SARL) rendering ultimate beneficial owners opaque; French banks classify the entity as a shell-company risk.

Defense evidence: Complete ownership chain from each natural-person UBO to the SARL; certificate of incumbency for each intermediate entity; French RBE (registre des bénéficiaires effectifs) extract; LEI certificate; group organigram; LP subscription book; notarised passport copies and proof of residential address for all UBOs; source-of-funds certification signed by the fund's general partner; and the executed SPA or term sheet proving the real economic purpose of the buyout.
Sudden high-volume inbound wires (capital calls and debt drawdowns) exceeding EUR 750,000 within compressed timelines, inconsistent with the substance-light SPV profile and triggering suspicious-transaction reporting to Tracfin.

Root cause: Sudden high-volume inbound wires (capital calls and debt drawdowns) exceeding EUR 750,000 within compressed timelines, inconsistent with the substance-light SPV profile and triggering suspicious-transaction reporting to Tracfin.

Defense evidence: Executed LBO facility agreement and lender commitment letters; capital call notices; acquisition SPA and price-adjustment protocol; escrow agreement; shareholder resolutions approving the financing; lender security checklist; accounting trail evidencing that inbound funds are recorded as share capital or shareholder advances; and a transaction-specific explanatory memorandum describing the acquisition timeline.
A non-resident PEP or a UBO associated with a FATF high-risk or sanctioned jurisdiction appears as an indirect limited partner, exposing the bank to EU restrictive-measures and AML reputational risk.

Defense evidence: Licensed domiciliation contract or lease; French SIREN/SIRET registration; annual accounts filed with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce; designation of a French- resident gérant; staffing and professional-services agreements; opening balance sheet and business plan; shareholders' meeting minutes appointing management; and evidence that the ISIC 6499 buyout SPV actively utilises treasury, escrow and notarial services.
MT103 ordering or beneficiary references mentioning a sanctioned party or an embargoed jurisdiction, including legacy-group counterparties from high-risk regions.

Root cause: MT103 ordering or beneficiary references mentioning a sanctioned party or an embargoed jurisdiction, including legacy-group counterparties from high-risk regions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity and shareholder-loan injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 (non-EEA payer) or SEPA Credit Transfer (EEA payer) to the SARL's French IBAN (format FR76 + bank code + branch code + account number + key) with the beneficiary name exactly matching the registration and the SIREN/SIRET in the reference field. The Remittance Information field (field 70) must clearly state 'APPORT EN CAPITAL' or 'AVANCE EN COMPTE COURANT D'ASSOCIÉ'; the bank maps the narrative to the Eurosystem balance-of-payments standard purpose code for equity injection or intra-group lending. Supporting documentation required by the receiving bank: (i) executed subscription resolution (PV d'assemblée or décision du gérant); (ii) capital-increase deed or shareholder-loan agreement; (iii) up-to-date UBO/ownership chart and LEI; (iv) source-of-funds statement for the injecting fund; and (v) for a non-EU investor, evidence that the French target is not in a sensitive activity under Art. R151-3 CMF, thereby avoiding prior ministerial authorisation; the investment is then captured by the regular balance-of-payments survey transmitted by the bank to the Banque de France when the monthly cumulative cross-border flow reaches EUR 750,000. Narrative consistency with corporate documentation is decisive to prevent the bank from re-characterising the inflow as undeclared revenue or financing proceeds requiring additional scrutiny.Acquisition consideration, including purchase price and post-closing adjustments, is disbursed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV from the SARL's operational or escrow account to the seller's bank; for France-to-France deliveries, settlement is made by SEPA credit transfer contemporaneously with the delivery of shares on the target's register (delivery-versus-payment). Cross-border LBO payments are typically cleared via TARGET2/T2 for EUR legs and via CLS for USD/GBP legs where the corresponding FX hedge is in place; substantial transactions may be routed through the bank's payments factory to align the MT103 cover with the syndicated facility drawdown. Documentary credits (SWIFT MT700) and standby letters of credit (MT760) are used only where portfolio-company trade flows require purchase-price support or performance guarantees, typically under URDG 758 or ISP98. No French central-bank pre-authorisation is required for ordinary disbursements; the bank will register outbound flows in the monthly French balance-of-payments survey where the cumulative amount with non-residents equals or exceeds EUR 750,000, and each payment leg is subject to real-time sanctions and PEP screening.FX Forward Contracts (change à terme) - OTC, EUR/USD and EUR/GBP, governed by ISDA 1998 FX Definitions and French banking federation (AFB) master agreements, with physical delivery of EUR against the foreign currency at maturityNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) - EUR/CNY, EUR/INR and EUR/BRL style exposures, cash-settled in EUR under ISDA 1998 FX Definitions, authorised only where the underlying currency is non-convertible or subject to settlement restrictionsCross-Currency Swaps (CCS) - fixed/floating EUR-USD or EUR-GBP swaps matching LBO debt service and acquisition funding flows, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Credit Support Annex for collateral exchangeFX Options (vanilla and barrier structures) - for acquisition-price downside protection in foreign currency, with premium charged to the operational account and settlement via physical delivery or cash settlementFX Swaps (swap de change) - spot/forward combinations for short-term liquidity management between EUR and trade-currency balancesCLS-settled Spot FX - EUR/USD, EUR/GBP and EUR/JPY spot legs executed through the bank's single-dealer platform with Continuous Linked Settlement to eliminate settlement risk
