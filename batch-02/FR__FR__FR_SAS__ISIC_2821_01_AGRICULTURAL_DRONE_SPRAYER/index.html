Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) of the Commercial Court in the jurisdiction of the registered office; electronic filing administered through INPI's single portal formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 governing the Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS), together with Articles R.227-1 et seq. and applicable notarial and registry decrees.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts constatant la constitution de la SAS (Articles of Association) accompanied by the official electronic M0 declaration form and individual attestations of corporate officers and beneficial owners.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum share capital is imposed for an SAS; capital may be fixed freely. Where capital is subscribed in cash, at least half of the subscribed cash amount must be paid in before filing and held in a blocked (escrow) account with a credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, with the balance payable within five years of registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: commission de sécurité visitation and final fire certificate for occupied premises, including emergency exits, fire alarms, extinguishers and hazardous storage compartments; structural load inspection required for any elevated test platform, drone hangar or landing pad; occupational clearance: DREETS workplace inspection including electrical installation verification, first aid provisioning, noise exposure assessment, and compliance with the Labour Code on working conditions and medical surveillance of operators.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), the French national tax authority under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; primary portals: impots.gouv.fr, guichet-entreprises.gouv.fr, and the unified registration portal le-guichet-unique.inpi.fr operated by INPI; customs and excise matters for export verification are under the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS, tax identity is obtained ex lege at incorporation: (1) file statutory articles of association and the appointed president via the INPI 'Guichet unique' portal within 30 days; (2) publish the incorporation notice in a legal gazette (journal d'annonces légales); (3) INSEE automatically issues the SIREN (9-digit entity number) and SIRET (establishment number); (4) DGFiP automatically assigns the corporate income tax account (n° fiscal) and the VAT intra-Community identification number (format FR plus 2-digit DGFiP key plus SIREN, 13 characters total); no separate VAT registration certificate is required in France, and no minimum capital is imposed for SAS formation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SAS, tax identity is obtained ex lege at incorporation: (1) file statutory articles of association and the appointed president via the INPI 'Guichet unique' portal within 30 days; (2) publish the incorporation notice in a legal gazette (journal d'annonces légales); (3) INSEE automatically issues the SIREN (9-digit entity number) and SIRET (establishment number); (4) DGFiP automatically assigns the corporate income tax account (n° fiscal) and the VAT intra-Community identification number (format FR plus 2-digit DGFiP key plus SIREN, 13 characters total); no separate VAT registration certificate is required in France, and no minimum capital is imposed for SAS formation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not maintain a US-style check-the-box regime. Relevant elections for an SAS include: (1) fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under CGI Art. 223A, available to a parent holding at least 95% of eligible French subsidiaries, requiring a joint 5-year irrevocable election; (2) parent-subsidiary participation exemption (régime mère-fille) under CGI Art. 145/216, yielding 95% dividend exemption; (3) the option for the reduced corporate income tax rate on small and medium enterprise profits; (4) election of the export-oriented 0% VAT treatment under CGI Art. 262; and (5) ATAD hybrid mismatch compliance declarations for cross-border hybrid arrangements (CGI Art. 209 VII-X), filed on the annual corporate return with marginal anti-avoidance elections where the French payer or French investor is the counterparty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

French-established traders benefit from the franchise en base small-business exemption (CGI Art. 293 B) up to EUR 91,900 annual turnover for goods and EUR 36,300 for services (2025 thresholds); intra-EU B2C distance sales are registered in France via OSS until EUR 10,000 annual cross-border turnover; non-established foreign suppliers must obtain French VAT registration from the first taxable euro and generally must appoint a DGFiP-approved fiscal representative; exports of goods outside the EU are zero-rated and do not trigger registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under CGI Art. 262-I for goods physically exported outside the European Union customs territory and under the place-of-supply rules (CGI Art. 259-1 and 262-II) for services directly connected to export goods or supplied to non-EU B2B customers; the exporter must evidence removal of goods from EU territory within 90 days of dispatch, supported by a valid DAU export entry processed through the DELTA-X electronic customs system; intra-EU dispatches are exempt under CGI Art. 262-0 with submission of the EC Sales List and the French déclaration d'échanges de biens (DEB); refund of accumulated input VAT is claimed via the dème de remboursement de crédit de TVA (form 3519 or monthly periodic refund procedure) supported by export invoices, DAU receipts, transport documentation, and bank credit evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration - DAU (Document Administratif Unique) processed via DELTA-X
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading, House Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note proving exit from EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice bearing the zero-rated export VAT exoneration reference under CGI Art. 262
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/TT credit advice evidencing actual receipt of foreign export proceeds
  • Export licensing records for dual-use or civil UAV/drone-specific controlled goods where applicable under DGDDI strategic goods regulation

France implements a mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting clearance model rather than an Italian-style live fiscal stamping: all B2B e-invoices must use structured semantic formats (Factur-X, UBL, or CII) and be transmitted through the free Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a DGFiP-accredited private platform (PDP) with certified REST/API interfaces; transactional data is cleared to DGFiP simultaneously with issuance, and B2C payment data is e-reported; the reform timetable requires all taxable persons to be able to receive compliant e-invoices from 1 September 2026 and to issue them from 1 September 2027 for large and mid-cap companies and from 1 September 2028 for small and micro companies; no digital fiscal stamp or QR clearance code is affixed to French invoices.

Exporters must retain substantiation for at least 6 years; customs verification may extend retrospectively up to 3 years and tax authorities up to 6 years in fraud cases. Where zero-rating is invalidated (false export declaration, fictitious consignee, missing exit proof, or non-repatriation of proceeds within the reference window), DGFiP will reassess French VAT at 20% with 0.20% monthly late interest and may apply an 80% bad-faith or fraudulent-misrepresentation penalty under CGI Art. 1729; DGDDI may separately impose customs penalties under Code des Douanes Art. 414-426, including confiscation of the goods, fines up to twice the value of the fraud, and imprisonment up to 3 years for smuggling or misdeclaration; refunds previously granted may be clawed back with the same penalties, and serious cases may be referred to the French tax fraud prosecution authorities (Parquet National Financier) for criminal trial.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under a fully convertible regime for both capital and current accounts. The Euro faces no capital controls or prior approval requirements for cross-border transfers. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are unrestricted provided standard corporate tax compliance, beneficial ownership declarations, and AML reporting obligations are met.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds applies under French law. Export receipts are declared for statistical purposes via Banque de France reporting mechanisms where applicable, but conversion occurs at market rates without quotas or prescribed ratios.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax on dividends distributed by a French SAS to non-resident corporate shareholders outside any applicable treaty or directive. The rate is reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a qualifying EU/EEA parent holding at least 5% of the SAS for at least two years, and may be reduced under France's income tax treaties to rates commonly ranging from 0% to 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year financial period ending 31 December, the return must be filed by the third working day following 1 May. For an off-calendar financial year, filing is due by the third month after the close of the financial year. If the statutory deadline falls on a non-working day, the one-day rule is postponed to the next working day.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File obligations arise for French entities and permanent establishments forming part of a multinational group where the French entity has more than 100 employees and has an annual turnover or total gross assets exceeding EUR 50 million. Country-by-Country Report filing applies to groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more. Agricultural drone companies below these thresholds are still required to prepare contemporaneous arm's-length evidence sufficient to satisfy French tax audit standards.

Economic substance scope: France does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulation in the Gulf Cooperation Council sense, but economic substance is a binding precondition for treaty benefits, EU directive benefits, and transfer-pricing recognition. The French 'abus de droit' doctrine, ATAD Article 6 general anti-abuse provisions, and the substance prerequisites of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive require the French SAS to have actual management, decision-making, operational staff, fixed assets, and real drone assembly or R&D functions in France, not merely a registered office shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of undeclared tax with a 40% bad-faith penalty or an 80% fraudulent-manoeuvres penalty, plus late-payment interest. Criminal prosecution may lead to a fine of EUR 500,000, or EUR 1,000,000 where aggravated, and imprisonment of up to five years, or seven years where the offence is committed by an organized group.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the French SAS is used to disguise the tax fraud of its directors or beneficial owner. Directors risk civil restitution for the unpaid corporate tax and penalties, travel bans, asset freezes, seizure of passports, disqualification from managing a French company, and imprisonment for complicity in fraud.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may recharacterize transactions, adjust the French taxable profit, apply the arm's-length principle under Articles 57 and 238A CGI, and impose late-payment interest plus an administrative penalty of up to 40% for serious misrepresentation or 80% where abusive. Non-production of documentation can independently attract penalty and audit-cost burdens.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve transfer-pricing manipulation without economic substance may be assessed personally for taxes and sanctions if they benefited from the pricing scheme. In criminal cases, directors who made false statements to the administration may face personal fines and imprisonment, and the same sanctions as tax-fraud offenders where the mispricing is part of a fraudulent concealment.
Artificial Shell Company and Abuse of Treaty / EU Directive Relief

Statutory sanction: Denial of the domestic law tax treaty benefit and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption; application of the French anti-abuse doctrine to recharacterize the whole arrangement; 40% or 80% penalties, interest, and potential nullity of the corporate entity if fictitious. In certain cases, charges may include unlawful tax benefit and fraudulent registration.

Director personal liability: Directors may be found personally liable for the French tax reassessment and penalties where the shell company is a vehicle for abuse. Sanctions include a French management ban (interdiction de gérer) of up to 15 years, asset confiscation, travel restrictions, and personal criminal fines for intentionally organizing the sham arrangement.
AML, Export Control, and Financial Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalties under the French Monetary and Financial Code, criminal fines of up to EUR 375,000 and imprisonment of up to five years for money laundering, confiscation of the export proceeds and aircraft, withdrawal of the manufacturer/operator licence, and liquidation of the French entity where the permits are invalid.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally liable for AML and sanctions breaches involving the SAS. The corporate veil may be pierced where the structure is used for international money laundering or sanctions evasion; consequences include personal asset confiscation, travel bans, passport denial or revocation, prohibition from banking operations, and imprisonment.
