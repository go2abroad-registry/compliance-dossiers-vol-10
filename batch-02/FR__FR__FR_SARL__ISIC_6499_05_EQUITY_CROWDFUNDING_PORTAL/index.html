Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

French law sets no minimum legal capital for a SARL; the share capital is freely determined by the founders. Cash contributions must be deposited in a temporarily blocked bank account or with a notary and cannot be withdrawn until the company is registered with the RCS. For the crowdfunding service provider, after registration the platform must maintain either own funds or professional-indemnity insurance cover satisfying Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2020/1503.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office must satisfy French building, fire-safety, and disabled-access requirements applicable to commercial premises (Code de la construction et de l'habitation). No structural-load or workplace-atmosphere certificate is required for an office-only digital platform, but the company must maintain a health-and-safety register (registre de santé et de sécurité au travail), conduct an annual fire-safety review, and ensure the premises are covered by the employer's occupational-health declaration to the DREETS labour inspectorate.

Paid-in capital rule: French law sets no minimum legal capital for a SARL; the share capital is freely determined by the founders. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — impots.gouv.fr; business portal 'Mon espace professionnel' dedicated to corporate and VAT declarations

E-government filing portal

For a French-established SARL: incorporate via the Guichet unique/CFE using Form M0; DGFiP subsequently assigns a SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit), and if the SARL executes taxable supplies the SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) attributes an intra-Community VAT number (FR + key + SIREN) under CGI Art. 286 ter. For foreign entities not established in France but making taxable supplies, mandatory registration is effected through the Mini-Guichet étranger procedure under CGI Art. 286 quater; the non-resident VAT application is filed with the local SIE competent for non-residents or via the online demat portal. There is no separate statutory TIN apart from the SIREN/VAT number for corporate taxpayers.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (impôt sur les sociétés at 25% standard rate; reduced 15% for eligible SMEs on the first €42,500 of taxable income); Territorial_Scope: France (worldwide taxation for French-resident SARL with foreign tax credit relief under tax treaties); Tax_Exempt_Zone: No special territorial exemptions are applicable to equity crowdfunding platform activity, whether in mainland France or otherwise. — For a French-established SARL: incorporate via the Guichet unique/CFE using Form M0; DGFiP subsequently assigns a SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit), and if the SARL executes taxable supplies the SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) attributes an intra-Community VAT number (FR + key + SIREN) under CGI Art. 286 ter. For foreign entities not established in France but making taxable supplies, mandatory registration is effected through the Mini-Guichet étranger procedure under CGI Art. 286 quater; the non-resident VAT application is filed with the local SIE competent for non-residents or via the online demat portal. There is no separate statutory TIN apart from the SIREN/VAT number for corporate taxpayers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (impôt sur les sociétés at 25% standard rate; reduced 15% for eligible SMEs on the first €42,500 of taxable income); Territorial_Scope: France (worldwide taxation for French-resident SARL with foreign tax credit relief under tax treaties); Tax_Exempt_Zone: No special territorial exemptions are applicable to equity crowdfunding platform activity, whether in mainland France or otherwise.

Available election forms

No general check-the-box regime exists in France. Statutory elections available to a SARL include: (i) the option under CGI Art. 239 AA to be taxed as a partnership (régime translucide) where all shareholders are natural persons within a same family; (ii) the election under CGI Art. 223 A for fiscal consolidation (intégration fiscale) if the SARL holds more than 95% of French subsidiaries; (iii) for a real-estate or financial holding context, an option under CGI Art. 206-5 for certain co-ownership structures. Hybrid mismatch elections are not elective: the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) and French domestic provisions apply mandatory, non-elective disallowance and non-deduction rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic SARL established in France: €37,500 per annum for supplies of services and intellectual rights (CGI Art. 293 B micro-enterprise exemption threshold; the threshold is €85,000 for supplies of goods, with a reduced threshold of €34,400 for certain services). Foreign taxable persons without French establishment: no registration threshold—the first taxable act in France triggers compulsory registration, except under the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) regime where a non-zero threshold applies to EU-distance sales of goods and certain services supplied to non-taxable persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods exported from the EU, French VAT is zero-rated under CGI Art. 262-II-1° where the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the EU by or on behalf of the supplier and proof of export is produced within the statutory time limit. For services, zero-rating applies only where the place of supply is outside France under the B2B rule (customer established outside France/EU) or, for e-services to non-EU private consumers, where the effective use and enjoyment is outside the EU. The equity crowdfunding portal's qualitative services (listing fees, due diligence, advisory) to non-EU issuers may consequently be outside French VAT; to reinvest EU input VAT, the non-resident portal operator must file a VAT refund application under the EU 13th Directive or the French refund mechanism before the 30 September of the following year.

  • Uniform Customs Export Declaration (DAU) with movement reference number
  • Transport document proving physical exit from the EU: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR
  • Inward remittance certificate / documentary evidence of payment from the non-EU counterparty

E-invoicing reform (originally 2024, postponed by 2023 plan): mandatory B2B domestic e-invoicing begins 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises and 1 September 2027 for SMEs/micro-enterprises. Invoices must be structured (XML/EDI) and transmitted through a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP); data must flow to the Portail Public de Facturation (PF) of DGFiP for exchange and audit. There is no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime in France; the DGFiP performs post-issuance analytics. B2B transactions with non-EU customers remain subject to French e-reporting or in-scope invoicing rules. Equity crowdfunding portals must additionally maintain AMF-mandated investor transaction statements, but these are not fiscal clearing instruments.

The French tax and customs authorities rigorously audit export zero-rating claims; absence of compliant documentation within any 3-year verification window leads to retroactive VAT assessment (claw-back) plus collection of interest. Penalties range from 25% for minor errors to 40% for deliberate inaccuracy, and 80% for manoeuvres frauduleuses; criminal sanctions include imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to €50,000 (CGI Art. 1741). Repeated violations can lead to a permanent loss of the export exemption and, for crowdfunding portals, additional AMF disciplinary sanctions, including fines and withdrawal of the crowdfunding license. The 180-day inward remittance deadline, where contractually imposed by the underlying investment agreement, is not a French statutory FX deadline but is applied as a prudential control by the financial regulator in verifying cross-border crowdfunding round closings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts, anchored in Articles 63-65 TFEU and CMF Articles L.151-1 et seq. No exchange-control authorization is required for foreign direct investment, equity injections, dividend remittances, or crowdfunding flows. Statutory reporting to Banque de France applies to cross-border payments and balance-of-payments statistics (monthly direct-investment declarations per Banque de France Notice No 2021-05; thresholds: transactions >= EUR 50,000 for trade/payments and direct-investment flows). Dividend repatriation from the SARL is unrestricted once the statutory solvency test is met (Code de Commerce Art. L.232-11), subject to the 25% domestic withholding tax reduced under the applicable double-tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France is a Eurosystem member; export receipts may be kept in euros or foreign currency indefinitely with no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio. Reporting obligations only: export payments and receivables exceeding EUR 50,000 must be declared for balance-of-payments statistics to Banque de France by the resident exporter; there is no legal timeline for conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with prudential and AML supervision delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) jointly administers cross-border capital surveillance under the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW for statutory client-fund segregation. Under Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 (Articles 10-11), client monies of a crowdfunding service provider must be held at a credit institution, an eligible payment institution, or an investment firm, with strict segregation and a prohibition on reuse. Non-EU offshore digital EMIs (e.g., US/UK neobanks) cannot serve this statutory function. Feasibility: MODERATE for ancillary operational-expense accounts with EU-licensed EMIs (Qonto, Treezor, or Sella/Soledair), which are acceptable for payroll/vendor payments but never for investor escrow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for an equity injection into the French SARL: SEPA Credit Transfer (euros) or SWIFT MT103 (non-EEA currency) quoting the BOP purpose code for direct investment - French balance-of-payments code 'Investissement direct - Apports en capital' (BPM6 direct-investment equity category). Supporting documents for the receiving French bank: extraordinary general meeting resolution approving the capital increase, updated share register, attestation of contribution release, and UBO declarations; the bank files the mandatory Banque de France monthly direct-investment declaration. For third-country wire transfers, include full remittance data (shareholder name, nature 'Capital Increase', beneficiary SARL name and SIREN) to avoid classification as a loan or re-invoicing flow.Clearing rails for disbursements by the crowdfunding portal: SEPA Credit Transfer for EU/EEA euro payments and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA transfers; each project company receives funds through a segregated sub-account, with remittance information carrying the deal code, portfolio company identifiers, and the governing funding agreement reference. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal years ending 31 December, the return must be filed by the second working day after 1 May of the following year. For fiscal years ending on any other date, the return must be filed within three months of the fiscal year-end. Instalment payment dates under the standard calendar are 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, with the balance due at the same time as the annual return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 (annual turnover or total balance-sheet assets of the French entity or consolidated group triggering formal Local File and Master File obligations under Article 223 quinquies C of the French General Tax Code). A smaller French SARL remains required to maintain contemporaneous arm's length documentation whenever it enters material cross-border transactions with related parties, particularly service fees paid to foreign fintech or platform affiliates.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). The functional equivalent is the French abuse-of-law doctrine under Article L64 of the French Book of Tax Procedure, ATAD anti-hybrid and CFC rules, permanent establishment thresholds, and the OECD BEPS minimum standard. For an equity crowdfunding portal, real operational substance requires an AMF or ECSPR regulatory licence, local employees and decision-making, authentic escrow and client-asset controls, robust AML/KYC procedures, and contemporaneous records demonstrating commercial activity. Failure to demonstrate substance may result in recharacterisation as a shell or conduit, denial of treaty benefits, and joint liability of the gérant.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 40% for intentional misstatement and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law, plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month. Criminal liability under Article 1741 of the French General Tax Code exposes the taxpayer to imprisonment of up to five years and fines up to EUR 75,000, with additional court-ordered publication and forfeiture sanctions.

Director personal liability: The SARL gérant or de facto director may be jointly and severally liable for penalties caused by intentional fault. A criminal conviction can trigger asset seizure, travel restrictions, disqualification from managing companies, and civil restitution to the tax administration and investors.
Unlicensed Crowdfunding / Unregulated Financial Activity

Statutory sanction: AMF administrative injunctions and fines, regulatory licence revocation, and criminal sanctions under the French Monetary and Financial Code for unlawful financial intermediation. Penalties may include fines of up to EUR 100 million or statutory ceilings based on unlawful proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers can be held civilly liable for investor losses, barred from managing regulated financial entities, subjected to AMF fines, and in serious cases face criminal imprisonment and travel bans during investigation.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law and Fictitious Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as an abusive arrangement under Article L64 LPF with an 80% penalty, denial of treaty and EU directive benefits, reassessment of profits to the French taxpayer, and retroactive tax collection with late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the legal form conceals the true principal or is used abusively. The gérant can be jointly liable for tax liabilities, subject to civil restitution to misled investors, and may face disqualification, asset freezes, and criminal sanctions for fictitious operations.
Transfer Pricing / Related-Party Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Arm's length price adjustment reallocating profits to the French SARL, disallowance of excessive deductions, a 40% penalty for deliberate mispricing, and additional transfer pricing documentation penalties where local/master file obligations are not satisfied. Failing to provide documentation on request may also trigger formal assessment procedures.

Director personal liability: The gérant may be jointly and severally liable for penalties resulting from fraudulent or abusive arrangement, and may face criminal charges for tax evasion where fictitious service fees are documented. Civil liability extends to reimbursement of falsely deducted sums and compensation to minority shareholders or investors.
AML / Money Laundering Through Escrow and Crowdfunding Rails

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under the French Monetary and Financial Code and AML regulator decisions, including fines, licence suspension or withdrawal, closure orders, and criminal prosecution for money laundering. Money laundering offences carry heavy criminal fines and imprisonment up to ten years in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers face personal criminal liability for deliberate non-reporting or complicity in laundering. Courts may order asset confiscation, travel bans, professional disqualification, and civil damages to defrauded investors and regulatory authorities.
