Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full member of the euro area and maintains fully liberalised currency and capital controls under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The euro is freely convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. Profit remittances by an SARL in the form of dividends are generally permitted without prior exchange control authorisation, but may be subject to French withholding tax (typically 30% where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive does not apply) and to the standard banking documentation evidencing the shareholder distribution (e.g., board minutes, financial statements, and tax attestations). Cross-border remittances above €15,000 are monitored by credit institutions for balance-of-payments statistics and anti-money-laundering purposes, but this does not constitute a convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement under French law or ECB regulations to surrender export proceeds or to convert them into euros. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts held with authorised intermediaries (banks established in France or elsewhere in the EU/EEA), or freely sold for euros at negotiated commercial rates. Receipts credited to accounts maintained outside France must be individually declared in the annual 'déclaration de compte à l'étranger' part of the French income or corporate tax return. Banks are required to report cross-border payment data to the Banque de France for statistical purposes under the European Balance of Payments framework, but no conversion or repatriation timeline is mandatory.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (together with the Direction Générale du Trésor and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for non-core clearing operations, subject to strict limitation. European Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex and certain fintech platforms benefit from the EU passporting regime and may hold funds for a French SARL, but they are not licensed deposit-taking banks and do not participate in the French deposit guarantee scheme (FC - FGDR). A French SARL engaged in Amazon FBA cross-border commerce may use such EMIs as settlement sub-'rails' for supplier disbursements and treasury pooling purposes, provided the EMI is properly authorised by its home state regulator and has filed for a French passport under Directive 2009/110/EC. However, for statutory capital compliance, parent-guarantee instruments, and invoicing transactions requiring a French IBAN, a Tier-1 or Tier-2 French credit institution remains the mandatory anchor. The use of an offshore EMI without an EU/EEA passport is legally unviable for receiving French-sourced operational inflows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions and shareholder loans must be executed by conventional SWIFT MT103 or SEPA CT (for euro within the SEPA zone) directly into the SARL's French operating bank account. The beneficiary bank will require the beneficial owner and the transaction 'purpose code' to be accurately declared. For equity subscriptions, the transfer reference should read 'Apport en capital – Fondateur' and be accompanied by a resolution of the shareholders (PV d'AG) and a certificate from the gérant (attestation de dépôt des fonds) proving the share premium and the nominal contribution. For shareholder loans, the wire narrative must be tagged 'Compte courant d'associé' and supported by a formal advance agreement, a liquidity plan, and an amortisation schedule. French banks will conduct a funds-origin validation step; therefore, the remitting bank must include a full sender reference (name, address, account number) and the SARL must file the mandatory 'Déclaration de mouvement de fonds' if the transaction qualifies under anti-money-laundering thresholds.Trusted supplier payments and the refinancing of cross-border commercial debt are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for US-dollar-denominated, pound-sterling, or other non-euro settlements; for euro supplies to EU counterparties, SEPA Instant/Restricted Transfer rails are the preferred low-cost route. For major import transactions with manufacturing partners where the SARL negotiates better trade terms, a Commercial Letter of Credit (under UCP600) opened by the French bank is used. All outbound trade disbursements must be accompanied by underlying commercial documentation (purchase orders, supplier invoices, transport and customs documentation when applicable, and Amazon payment settlement statements). For payments above €10,000, bank approval for 'BOP code' declaration under the Banque de France reporting regime is required. Amazon FBA fees and storage charges are usually debited automatically by Amazon from the seller's disbursement account; the SARL must maintain adequate local, SEPA, and SWIFT liquidity buffers to cover both supplier and Amazon fee cycles without triggering overdraft breaches.FX Forward Outrights (plain vanilla forward contracts on EUR/USD and EUR/GBP) with a French counterparty bankFX Swaps for short-term treasury liquidity and inventory cycle managementCross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (fixed or floating, on eligible euro and foreign-currency balance sheet exposures)EUR/USD, EUR/GBP Currency Options (vanilla options held by the treasury function, subject to board approval and non-speculative underlying exposure)Rolling FX hedges using a layered maturity ladder, with maximum tenor of 24 months aligned to Amazon FBA inventory cycle
