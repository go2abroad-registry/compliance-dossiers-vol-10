Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), operating the impots.gouv.fr taxpayer portal and the professional space titled Le Compte Fiscal en Ligne (CFL). Company formation and tax identifier clearance are routed through the one-stop shop of the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI Guichet Unique); inscription is made in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce; INSEE assigns the SIREN (9-digit national identification) and SIRET (site establishment identification) used as the official French tax identification references; DGFIP automatically issues the intra-Community VAT number to the registered SARL.

E-government filing portal

The French SARL is a domestic tax resident and obtains its TIN/VAT ID without a separate discretionary application: file the incorporation dossier containing the signed statutes, registered office proof in France, appointment of the gérant, and legal publication certificate through the INPI Guichet Unique; INSEE then emits the SIREN/SIRET; DGFIP attributes the TVA intracommunautaire in the format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN. Non-resident corporate shareholders do not require a French TIN merely to hold SARL interests. A non-established foreign service provider or intra-group supplier that independently incurs French VAT obligations must apply through Cerfa form n 3527bis to the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) and, if located outside the EU, must additionally appoint a DGFIP-approved representative for value-added tax (CGI art. 289 A). The gérant must in parallel register for personal French income tax formalities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SARL is an autonomous legal entity subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the standard 25% rate; it is not fiscally transparent. Being resident in France by reason of legal seat and effective management, it is taxable on worldwide profits except profit attributable to foreign permanent establishments benefiting from an exemption clause under an applicable double-taxation treaty (territorial scope of treaty-based PE relief). The pass-through family undertaking election (SARL de famille) is not available because ownership is not confined to a close circle of natural persons in the required family relationship.

Available election forms

France does not operate a check-the-box regime; classification follows the General Tax Code. Permit-granting elections relevant to the SARL include: (i) the parent-subsidiary tax regime (régime des sociétés mères, CGI art. 145) electable to benefit from a 95% participation exemption on dividends received from qualifying subsidiaries; (ii) the French tax consolidation option (intégration fiscale, CGI art. 223 A) for a French head company that owns at least 95% of French subsidiaries — a foreign parent must route consolidation through a French-resident intermediary; (iii) the VAT distinct taxable person election (assujetti unique, CGI art. 256 C III) allowing tightly linked French establishments to create a VAT group; and (iv) binding advance rulings (rescrit fiscal) to confirm the VAT place of supply of a BPO service, the existence of a permanent establishment, or the treatment of a cross-border payment. EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules are mandatory rather than elective: mismatched deductions, inclusions and double-deductions are automatically neutralized, and the taxpayer cannot opt out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR — no turnover threshold applies to French-established enterprises: the SARL is a VAT taxable person from incorporation, although the small-business exemption (franchise en base, CGI art. 293 B) permits invoicing without VAT up to EUR 91,900 of annual turnover for goods and EUR 36,800 for services. For EU-established suppliers of remote digital services or distance-selling goods to French consumers the EU-wide cross-border threshold of EUR 10,000 applies; non-EU persons have zero threshold and become registrable from the first taxable French transaction unless the customer self-accounts under reverse charge. Inbound B2B services purchased by the SARL from foreign suppliers are governed by the reverse-charge mechanism in France, avoiding French registration of the foreign supplier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside the EU are exempt from French VAT with full right to deduct input VAT (de facto zero-rating) under CGI art. 262-I-1° and Annex II art. 74-0 C, provided the exporter holds proof that the goods left the EU within three months after the supply. For remote BPO and call-center services, no customs export zero-rating exists; the relevant rule is a supply-of-services displacement: when the customer is a taxable person (B2B), the place of supply is the customer's country (CGI art. 259-1), so the French SARL invoices without French VAT; an EU customer accounts for VAT under reverse charge and a non-EU customer is generally outside French VAT. When the call-center service is rendered to a non-taxable person located abroad, French VAT may still be due under the default B2C rule (CGI art. 259A), except electronically supplied services under CGI art. 259B whose place of supply is the consumer's country and which require OSS registration and reporting. Input VAT deduction is preserved for costs attributable to export, out-of-scope and reverse-charged revenues; full credit is typically available to the BPO operations as long as the supporting documentation identifies the foreign customer, the service performance and the payment flow.

  • Customs export declaration (DAU / Déclaration en Douane) with Movement Reference Number (MRN) and destination customs office outside the EU
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill naming the French SARL as exporter and evidencing the port or airport of departure from the EU
  • Commercial invoice issued at 0% VAT with the applicable Incoterms and the statutory reference of the export exemption
  • Effective export proof such as a signed delivery note, arrival certificate or foreign customs stamp confirming arrival in the destination country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or corresponding bank credit advice evidencing collection of the export receivable within the repatriation period
  • For services: executed BPO contract, statement of work, service delivery logs, client acceptance report, and remittance advices matching the invoiced amounts

France is rolling out a mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting reform (Finance Act 2024, decree n 2022-428 and implementing decrees). From 1 September 2026, all VAT-registered businesses must accept electronic invoices, and large and medium-sized enterprises must emit structured e-invoices; from 1 September 2027, small and micro-sized enterprises must emit them. Invoices must be issued in Factur-X (hybrid) or UBL/CII XML format, transmitted over a certified dematerialization platform (PDP) or the free public portal (PPF), with e-invoicing and e-reporting data passed to DGFIP for structured data matching and VAT return alignment. France does not impose real-time fiscal stamping or centralized government clearance technology; it uses structured continuous transmission and post-clearing audit. Sanctions of EUR 15 per non-compliant invoice (maximum EUR 15,000 per year) and EUR 250 per missing or erroneous e-reporting dataset (maximum EUR 15,000 per year) apply.

DGFIP may audit export and out-of-scope evidence for three years, extended to six years in cases of undisclosed activity or fiscal fraud. If the SARL fails to furnish admissible proof of export within three months, or if the zero-rating/out-of-scope treatment is found to be documentary-only or fraudulent, the VAT is prospectively and retrospectively reassessed: the previously credited or refunded VAT is clawed back together with statutory late-payment interest (intérêt de retard at 0.20% per month from the due date). Additional penalties escalate from 40% for deliberate misstatement (manquement délibéré) to 80% for fraudulent maneuvers or abuse of law (manœuvres frauduleuses, abus de droit) and 100% for opposition to tax control; criminal prosecution under CGI art. 1741 may result in five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, or seven years and EUR 3,000,000 in aggravated circumstances. The appointed tax representative and the gérant of the SARL incur joint and several liability for the company's VAT debts and penalties under CGI arts. 1754–1755, and corporate criminal liability can additionally be imposed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 group consolidated annual revenue (after elimination of intra-group sales) triggers mandatory Local File / Master File under Article L.13 AA LPF; CbC Report is required when the consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000; an entity with cross-border related party transactions less than EUR 50,000 annual flow may file a simplified form 2257-TD but must retain documentary evidence upon audit.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation akin to the Gulf regimes; nevertheless the abuse-of-law doctrine (Article L.64 LPF) permits the DGFiP to disregard transactions and corporate vehicles lacking genuine economic substance, genuine premises, employees and business purpose. A French SARL acting as an empty booking hub while the BPO directors, risk functions and servers are abroad is recharacterised as artificially transferring profits, leading to re-integration, withholding and abstention penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Failure to File

Statutory sanction: Re-instatement of the French taxable base, 0.20% monthly late interest, 10% automatic late-filing penalty, 40% penalty for intentional misstatement, 80% enhancement for abusive concealment; criminal prosecution of tax evasion carries a fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment of five years (Article 1741 CGI).

Director personal liability: Under Article L.267 LPF, directors who directly and knowingly participate in the tax fraud may be held jointly liable for the company's taxes; criminal conviction leads to personal fines, travel bans, freezing of personal assets and disqualification from managing French businesses.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Deduction of management fees, telecom re-charges and software royalties is denied; a 25% constructive dividend withholding is applied to the whole shifted margin; abuse-of-law penalty of 80%; under commercial insolvency law the company may be liquidated as a fictitious structure.

Director personal liability: Directors risk the 'action en comblement du passif' in judicial liquidation; the court can impose a ban on exercising commercial activity, up to 10 years, and a travel restriction; in the criminal branch, participation is treated as aggravated tax fraud with imprisonment and fines.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Administration corrects the transaction under Article 57 CGI, upwardly adjusting the French profit at 25%, plus monthly interest 0.20% and a 40% penalty for serious misevaluation; further indirect tax consequences: VAT re-characterisation and potential denial of input VAT recovery on procurement.

Director personal liability: Managers who signed or ratified transfer pricing returns without a reasonable basis may be personally reassessed for penalties; in the event of default, they are conjoined debtor in respect of 40% penalties; a court may declare them personally liable in the payment of the company's tax liabilities.
AML / Payment Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Breach of the French Monetary and Financial Code: administrative fine up to €5,000,000 or 5% of turnover, penal sanction for money laundering up to five years' imprisonment and a fine of €375,000; asset freeze, confiscation, and a prohibition from providing financial or commercial services for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors found to have knowingly routed fraudulent or disguised funds face personal criminal liability, restitution orders to affected clients, civil damages, international travel bans and disqualification from corporate directorship across the EU.
