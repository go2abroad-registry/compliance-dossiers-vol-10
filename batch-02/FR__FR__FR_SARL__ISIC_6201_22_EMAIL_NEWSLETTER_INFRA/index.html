Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) held by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered office arrondissement; filings are submitted through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), operated by INSEE with transmission to the Greffe, DGFiP and URSSAF.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL), L.210-1 to L.210-10 (general company law), R.123-30 et seq. (RCS), and implementing Decree n° 2021-300 (Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises); legal publicity governed by Decree n° 67-236.

Formation / constitutional filings

Company registration file consisting of the M0/MBO declaration (formulaire M0), certified articles of association (statuts), proof of publication in a Journal d'Annonces Légales (JAL), capital deposit attestation and registered office consent; final proof of registration is the Extrait K-Bis.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum statutory capital for SARL; the articles of association freely fix the amount. All shares must be fully subscribed by the associates, and each cash contribution must be paid in to at least 20% at incorporation (Article L.223-7, Code de commerce), with the remaining 80% payable within five years. The paid-up sums must be deposited and blocked on a dedicated company account opened with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations (CDC), released only upon presentation of the K-Bis.

Funding & unlock path: Office-only occupancy prevails: no public-assembly fire certification (commission de sécurité ERP) is required for non-ICP business offices. The company must nevertheless maintain workplace fire-safety compliance under the Code du travail (evacuation plan, emergency lighting, smoke detectors, fire-fighting equipment), keep a registre de sécurité, obtain an electrical conformity attestation per NF C 15-100, comply with occupational health monitoring (médecine du travail) for employees, and carry adequate civil liability insurance for property and personnel.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum statutory capital for SARL; the articles of association freely fix the amount. All shares must be fully subscribed by the associates, and each cash contribution must be paid in to at least 20% at incorporation (Article L.223-7, Code de commerce), with the remaining 80% payable within five years. The paid-up sums must be deposited and blocked on a dedicated company account opened with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations (CDC), released only upon presentation of the K-Bis.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — no industrial licensing board for purely digital ISIC 6201 businesses in France. Sector policy supervision is exercised by the Direction Générale des Entreprises (DGE), while data protection activity is overseen by the CNIL.

Category: None required (unlicensed activity). Recommended voluntary certifications for email infrastructure credibility: ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2 Type II, and CNIL registration for newsletter data processing. Transactional email transmission does not constitute a telecommunications activity requiring an ARCEP licence under the Code des postes et des communications électroniques.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministry for Economy and Finance; professional tax portal: impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel / EDI-TVA). Company formation and tax-identification filings are lodged through the single window Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the French SARL, the M0 filing through the Guichet Unique automatically triggers INSEE registration (SIREN/SIRET) and DGFiP issuance of the French VAT number in the format FR + two-digit control key + nine-digit SIREN. A non-resident enterprise without a French establishment must apply to the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) or the Centre des Impôts des Non-Résidents (Noisy-le-Grand) using the SIFI fast-track procedure or Form 3310-CA3 before the first taxable transaction; VAT numbers are typically issued within one to two weeks for SIFI filings. The SARL's primary corporate income tax account is managed by the competent SIE based on the registered office location.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) under Article 206(1) CGI on worldwide profits, with a foreign permanent establishment exclusion option under Articles 209 and 214 CGI. There is no automatic pass-through unless a valid transparency election is made. France applies no sub-national income tax; the territorial scope is unitary at national level. — Upon incorporation of the French SARL, the M0 filing through the Guichet Unique automatically triggers INSEE registration (SIREN/SIRET) and DGFiP issuance of the French VAT number in the format FR + two-digit control key + nine-digit SIREN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) under Article 206(1) CGI on worldwide profits, with a foreign permanent establishment exclusion option under Articles 209 and 214 CGI. There is no automatic pass-through unless a valid transparency election is made. France applies no sub-national income tax; the territorial scope is unitary at national level.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (i) five-year transparency election to be taxed as a partnership (impôt sur le revenu) under Article 239 bis AA CGI for SARLs with fewer than 50 individual shareholders and an industrial, commercial, artisan, agricultural or liberal activity; (ii) election for foreign permanent establishment net-exemption under Articles 209/214 CGI; (iii) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 256 C III CGI for closely linked resident entities; (iv) OSS/IOSS/PPP schemes for EU distance sales and B2C electronic services; (v) ATAD hybrid and reverse-hybrid reporting via annual Form 2258-SD with interest deduction limitation under Article 212 bis CGI and CFC rules under Article 209 B CGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold applies to an established French SARL: VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction and is obtained at incorporation (SIREN/VAT number). For foreign suppliers established outside France supplying B2C digital services ('services électroniques'), the EU-wide distance-sales threshold for use of the OSS scheme is EUR 10,000 annual turnover (Article 298 sexdecies F CGI); above that threshold, the place of supply is the consumer's Member State and the supplier must register or use OSS. Since the Email_Infra_SaaS activity is predominantly B2B, most supplies will be subject to reverse-charge in the customer's jurisdiction, with no French output VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports outside the EU qualify for the 0% (exonération) rate under Article 262-I-1 CGI, subject to proof of physical export being established within 90 days of dispatch (Article 74 Annexe III CGI). For services, the email-API and newsletter hosting platform constitutes electronically supplied services; B2B supplies are excluded from French VAT by place-of-supply rules (Article 259-1 CGI) and are subject to reverse charge in the customer's country, B2C supplies to EU consumers are taxed at the destination Member State's rate via OSS, and B2C supplies to non-EU consumers fall outside the French territorial scope under Article 259 C CGI. No French VAT is therefore chargeable on export services, provided the SARL maintains documentary evidence of the customer's business status, establishment and actual consumption location.

  • DAU (Déclaration Administrative Unique) — electronic export declaration lodged via the DELTA/GUN customs clearance system
  • Commercial invoice with export references, Incoterms and zero-rating legal basis
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill or CMR transport document evidencing physical dispatch from the EU (for goods)
  • Customs exit confirmation or visa record proving goods left the EU within the 90-day deadline
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation de change) or SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency

France mandates structured e-invoicing under the facturation électronique reform: from 1 September 2026 all VAT-registered businesses must be able to receive B2B electronic invoices and large enterprises must issue through certified Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDP) or the Public Invoicing Portal (PPF); from 1 September 2027 mid-caps and SMEs (including this SARL) must issue. B2C transactions are subject to monthly e-reporting of transaction and payment data with a maximum transmission delay of 30 days. Mandatory formats are Factur-X, UBL and CII. There is no digital stamping and no real-time fiscal clearance comparable to the Italian SDI; however, anti-fraud cross-matching is performed on transmitted data, and non-compliance triggers penalties up to EUR 15,000 per infringing invoice under Article 1737 CGI.

DGFiP audit rights follow the standard three-year limitation (Article L.169 LPF), extended to six years for undeclared or partially declared activity and ten years for arrangements involving undeclared foreign accounts or offshore assets. Where export zero-rating or off-scope treatment is not substantiated, VAT is re-assessed at 20% with late interest of 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI) and penalties of 40% for bad faith (Article 1729(a) CGI) or 80% for fraudulent maneuvers (Article 1729(b) CGI). Fraudulent export declarations additionally trigger customs sanctions under Articles 413-1 and 414 of the Code des Douanes, including confiscation of goods, fines up to three times the value of the fraud object, suspension of export privileges, and criminal liability; the SARL's legal representative may also be held personally liable for deliberate tax offenses under Article L.267 LPF.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is in the euro area. The euro is freely convertible for current and capital transactions, subject to EU and French sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, tax rules, and applicable statistical or regulatory reporting. No general exchange-control approval is required for a French SARL to receive foreign investment, hold foreign-currency accounts, pay non-resident suppliers, or remit dividends abroad. Dividend distributions remain subject to distributable-profits, corporate approval, withholding-tax, treaty, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse requirements. Banks may require evidence of the transaction's commercial or investment purpose and source of funds. Transfers involving sanctioned persons, restricted jurisdictions, or controlled goods and services may be prohibited or require authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in euros or foreign currency, subject to contractual terms, sanctions, tax, accounting, and applicable reporting requirements. The French Monetary and Financial Code contains reporting obligations for certain cross-border transactions and foreign assets; thresholds, exemptions, and filing procedures should be checked for the transaction and reporting period. Cash physically entering or leaving the EU with a value of EUR 10,000 or more must be declared to customs. This cash declaration is not a surrender or bank-transfer rule.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France's national central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. The Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR), hosted by the Banque de France, supervises French credit institutions and certain payment institutions. The Direction générale du Trésor administers French external financial-relations rules and related reporting under the Monetary and Financial Code.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for routine euro collections and payments where the provider is an appropriately authorized EU payment institution or electronic-money institution passporting into France. An EMI account is not a bank deposit and generally does not provide deposit-guarantee protection; safeguarded funds are subject to the applicable safeguarding regime. Provider terms may restrict high-volume email, marketing, or technology-related activity, foreign-currency corridors, cash services, and certain jurisdictions. Use a French or EU credit institution for core operating liquidity, payroll, tax payments, and any capital deposit requiring a bank certificate. Verify the provider's authorization, passporting status, safeguarding method, permitted business activities, and account-holder name before relying on the service.

Route subscriptions and shareholder loans from an account in the investor's or lender's verified name to the SARL's account, using SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area or SWIFT for other cross-border payments.
