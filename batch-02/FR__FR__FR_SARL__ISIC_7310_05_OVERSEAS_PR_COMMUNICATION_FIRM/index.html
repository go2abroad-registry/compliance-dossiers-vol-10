Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered-office department, receiving filings through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises operated by INPI.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL formation and operation) and R.223-1 to R.223-13 (drafting and filing); Decree No. 84-406 of 30 April 1984 on the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) submitted as an electronic dossier via the Guichet Unique portal; the legally authenticated registration record is the RCS extract K-bis.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

French law sets no minimum share capital for an ordinary SARL (Article L.223-2); the capital is freely determined in the Statuts, but every cash contribution must be fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation (Article L.223-7). The funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a licensed credit institution or notary and may be released only after RCS registration and issuance of the K-bis extract.

Funding & unlock path: Compliance with Articles R.4227-1 et seq. of the Labor Code (fire detection, extinguishers, evacuation instructions); ERP safety clearance if premises are open to clients; structural load and electrical conformity certified by the building owner; occupational health check with the Service de Prévention et de Santé au Travail following each employee's initial medical examination.

Paid-in capital rule: French law sets no minimum share capital for an ordinary SARL (Article L.223-2); the capital is freely determined in the Statuts, but every cash contribution must be fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation (Article L.223-7). Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); primary online portal impots.gouv.fr; VAT/EORI registration and e-invoicing clearance via Chorus Pro and accredited Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must file a registration declaration with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) having jurisdiction over its French place of business or, absent a French establishment, with the SIE for non-residents at the DGFiP service designated for foreign operations. The registration triggers issuance of a SIREN number and, where applicable, a French VAT number. Non-resident entities effecting taxable supplies in France are required to register before the first relevant supply; they may be required to appoint a fiscal representative if not established in the EU/EEA. The application must identify the foreign legal form, registered office, beneficial ownership, and expected French activity under Article 286 I of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on its worldwide income unless a temporary income tax option applies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on its worldwide income unless a temporary income tax option applies. Foreign shareholders are taxed on dividends and capital gains under treaty allocation rules; the entity is not a pass-through vehicle.

Available election forms

A SARL may elect under Articles 239 bis AA and 239 bis AB CGI to be taxed as an income tax (IR) entity for the first five fiscal years if capital is not held by legal entities above certain thresholds and shares are not listed, with mandatory conversion to IS thereafter. France has no check-the-box regime; hybrid mismatch arrangements involving French entities are subject to the EU ATAD rules, and cross-border mismatching deductions are denied or re-characterized under Article 239 quinquies f CGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For locally established small businesses, registration is mandatory when the annual turnover exceeds €85,000 for sales of goods or €37,500 for services. Non-resident suppliers generally have no threshold: VAT registration is required from the first taxable supply performed in France or to French consumers, unless the supply falls under the OSS/IOSS simplification or the French small business franchise en base de TVA is available to an EU taxable person meeting the threshold conditions. Intra-Community distance selling thresholds and cross-border B2B reverse charge mechanisms apply separately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate under Article 258(C) and 262 CGI where the supplier proves physical departure from the EU within 90 days of dispatch. Intra-Community dispatches to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number are similarly zero-rated. International media relations and public communication services supplied to a business customer established outside France (or outside the EU where the recipient is a taxable person) are placed outside the French VAT scope or are zero-rated under Article 259(A) and 259-D CGI, provided the place of supply is determined as the customer's establishment and the invoice contains the appropriate mention. Services connected with exports and ancillary to international trade may also qualify if directly linked to the export transaction.

  • Customs export declaration on the Single Administrative Document (DAU/EXA) bearing the export customs office stamp or electronic clearance status confirming exit from the EU; this declaration must reference the corresponding export invoice and consignee.
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR with signature and delivery documents showing the consignee outside France and the actual external frontier crossing; the transport document must be contemporaneous with the declared dispatch date.
  • Commercial and payment substantiation: signed export contract, commercial invoice, the customer's foreign VAT registration details, and an inward remittance certificate or SWIFT bank confirmation demonstrating receipt of payment from abroad in a foreign account within the statutory FX repatriation period.

Mandatory e-invoicing and real-time e-reporting apply to B2B transactions between taxable persons established in France under the 2023 Finance Act framework. Large and medium-sized enterprises must issue structured electronic invoices through a registered PPP/PDP or the Chorus Pro portal as of 1 September 2026; small and micro enterprises follow on 1 September 2027. Each invoice must comply with the EN 16931 semantic data model, be transmitted automatically to the DGFiP via accredited platforms, and contain an immutable electronic invoice number sequence. There is no physical digital stamp of a general government clearance point; fiscal clearance is achieved through the platform transmission and DGFiP acceptance of the reporting flow.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to strict documentary examination under Articles 289 F and 286 quinquies CGI, and the taxpayer must retain the complete substantiation bundle for at least six years. If the DGFiP invalidates the zero-rating, the full VAT is immediately clawed back, input VAT credits are reversed, and interest at 0.20% per month is assessed. Civil penalties escalate by degree of firmness: 40% of the understated tax for a mere inaccuracy, 80% for deliberate bad faith, and 100% for tax fraud with intent to conceal, pursuant to Articles 1728, 1729, and 1729-0 A CGI. Criminal liability, including imprisonment and fines, may be pursued under Article 1741 CGI and the Ligne de procédure fiscale L. 267 LPF for false declarations or the use of fictitious export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member with full convertibility of the euro on both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange-control permits, no prior FX authorisation, and no restrictions on profit remittance for non-resident shareholders. In practice, outbound dividends and loan repayments are freely executable, but require FATCA/CRS reporting, proper purpose codes, and, where applicable, treaty relief against the 25% dividend withholding tax; banks will demand a complete source-of-funds trail for any large or high-frequency remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies in France: export proceeds in foreign currency can be retained in multi-currency accounts or converted at market rate. The binding constraints are commercial law (receivables must be collected on agreed terms and, for supplies to EU businesses, generally within 60 days under the French Commercial Code/European Late Payment Directive), and Banque de France FECB declarations for every cross-border transaction with non-EU/EEA states above EUR 15,000 per transaction or cumulatively in a calendar month.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the Eurosystem and under the European Central Bank; French prudential supervision and AML/CFT enforcement for credit institutions are exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution); French external financial statistics, including the FECB cross-border transaction reporting regime, are administered by Banque de France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High for operational treasury: EU/EEA-licensed digital EMIs and electronic-money institutions (Qonto, Shine, Wise Business, Revolut Business) can open accounts remotely, provide IBANs, multi-currency wallets, and fast cross-border arrangements, but they are not a full substitute for a French credit institution and are assessed as Medium-to-High for a non-resident-owned PR firm; Low for statutory share-capital escrow, because the SARL capital block requires a French bank, notary, or a credit institution eligible under Article L.227-1 et seq. of the French Commercial Code. All EMIs must hold an ACPR/e-money licence and must not be used as the sole treasury rail for high-value client retainer flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 for non-EU injections into the French SARL's BNP Paribas or Société Générale account, or an instant/SEPA SCT only if the parent is EU/EEA-based. Purpose code must reflect 'capital subscription / share premium' or 'shareholder loan (compte courant d'associé)' with a clear beneficiary reference; for FX conversion, convert at the receiving bank's commercial FX desk after cash confirmation. The French entity should retain the capital-block certificate and notary/registrar receipts for VAT and corporate tax records.
