Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), French Ministry of Economy and Finance. Primary tax portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; business formalities and SIREN/VAT identifier registration via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; local operational contacts are the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and, for non-established foreign companies, the Service des Impôts des Entreprises des Non-Résidents (SIE DRN).

E-government filing portal

For a domestic French SARL: (1) file incorporation or branch registration with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce through formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, which automatically assigns the SIREN/SIRET identifiers and issues the Kbis extract; (2) the DGFiP subsequently delivers the definitive French tax identification number (TIN) and, where a taxable activity exists, the intra-Community VAT number (format FRXXSIREN); (3) the SARL must file the VAT formality on the impots.gouv.fr professional account and submit periodic VAT returns (CA3/CA12) from its first taxable operation. For a non-established foreign entity with French VAT exposure (e.g., B2B services deemed supplied in France or B2C distance sales, and services physically performed in France): apply directly for VAT registration using Form No. 15771-SD with the SIE DRN under Article 286 ter CGI; where the entity has no EU establishment, the DGFiP normally requires appointment of an accredited tax representative (Article 302 D CGI), who assumes joint and several liability for VAT and may be required to post a guarantee. No minimum-capital preface applies to obtaining tax IDs because a SARL has no statutory minimum capital requirement.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. A French SARL is by default a fully taxable French corporate resident subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) on profits exploited, commercialised, or put to use in France under the territoriality rules of Articles 209 and 209 B CGI. Standard IS rate: 25% (financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2022); reduced rate of 15% applies to the first €42,500 of taxable profit where corporate turnover is below €10,000,000, the share capital is fully paid up, and at least 75% of capital is held by natural persons. The SARL is not a pass-through by default and does not benefit from a tax-exempt zone status for consulting income; profits are determined under book-tax accounting (plan comptable général) with a separate tax balance sheet. — For a domestic French SARL: (1) file incorporation or branch registration with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce through formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, which automatically assigns the SIREN/SIRET identifiers and issues the Kbis extract; (2) the DGFiP subsequently delivers the definitive French tax identification number (TIN) and, where a taxable activity exists, the intra-Community VAT number (format FRXXSIREN); (3) the SARL must file the VAT formality on the impots.gouv.fr professional account and submit periodic VAT returns (CA3/CA12) from its first taxable operation. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. A French SARL is by default a fully taxable French corporate resident subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) on profits exploited, commercialised, or put to use in France under the territoriality rules of Articles 209 and 209 B CGI. Standard IS rate: 25% (financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2022); reduced rate of 15% applies to the first €42,500 of taxable profit where corporate turnover is below €10,000,000, the share capital is fully paid up, and at least 75% of capital is held by natural persons. The SARL is not a pass-through by default and does not benefit from a tax-exempt zone status for consulting income; profits are determined under book-tax accounting (plan comptable général) with a separate tax balance sheet.

Available election forms

France has no US-style check-the-box classification; however, several binding statutory elections exist: (1) option for income tax (IR) instead of IS for small SARL de famille satisfying the conditions of Article 239 bis AA CGI (close-ended capital, ≤10 partners, all family members and undisclosed and unincorporated associations of family members, not subject to IS), with the option generally irrevocable except in limited circumstances; (2) election for the reduced 15% CIT rate under Article 219 CGI for eligible SMEs; (3) election to form a fiscal group (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI where the parent holds at least 95% of capital and voting rights of qualifying 95%-or-more-owned French subsidiaries, enabling offset of profits and deficits within the group; (4) VAT elections under Article 287 CGI, including the option for cash accounting (TVA sur les encaissements), option to charge VAT on otherwise exempt financial-type services, and the OSS/IOSS regime election for cross-border distance sales and TBE digital services; (5) no elective shelter exists against hybrid mismatch rules transposed from the ATAD Directive (Articles 212 bis to 212 sexies CGI), which apply compulsorily. All elections are filed electronically through the professional tax account or with the SIE.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France imposes no turnover threshold before a non-established foreign supplier must register for VAT: liability arises from the first taxable transaction (supply of services deemed taxable in France under Articles 259 and 259 A CGI, B2C distance sales of goods, and intra-Community acquisitions). For an established French business, the small-business franchise (franchise en base de TVA) under Article 293 B CGI provides a registration/taxation exemption up to €85,000 annual turnover for supplies of goods and €37,500 for supplies of services, with tolerance ceilings of €93,500 and €41,250, but the franchise is lost where the business issues VAT invoices, makes intra-Community acquisitions, supplies services not eligible for exemption, or performs cross-border activities. A resident SARL in the medical-device consulting sector is therefore registrable and must charge VAT from the first taxable euro of domestic turnover unless the franchise provision is validly claimed. For pan-EU distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services to non-taxable persons, an EU-wide mini-OSS threshold of €10,000 applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: 0% VAT exemption under Article 262(I) CGI applies where goods are physically dispatched or transported by the seller, or on behalf of the seller, to a destination outside the European Union, evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration and supporting transport documents; the supplier must obtain and preserve proof of exportation within 3 months of the tax point (fait générateur) and must file the corresponding information in the recapitulative statements/intrastat declarations where applicable. Intra-EU dispatches are likewise exempt under Article 262 ter CGI (subject to a valid customer VAT ID, transport evidence, and statutory EC-sales-list reporting). Services (including FDA 510(k) and CE-mark regulatory consulting supplied by a French SARL): where the service is rendered to a taxable person established outside France, the supply is placed outside the scope of French VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule of Article 259-1 CGI; where the customer is a non-taxable person outside the EU, the supply may be outside France if the service is effectively used or exploited outside the EU. In all service-export cases, the exporter must substantiate the customer's status and establishment through documentary evidence (foreign VAT ID or non-EU corporate registry extract, contract, engagement letter, and bank/payment records).

  • Customs Export Declaration / Single Administrative Document (DAU) or the electronic customs file evidencing export validation by the French or EU customs authorities
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading (B/L) or telex release for seafreight, IATA Air Waybill (AWB) for airfreight, or CMR consignment note for road transport, together with proof of goods leaving the EU territory
  • Intrastat / Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens (DEB) and recapitulative statement data where the destination is another EU member state
  • Commercial invoice, export order, and contract evidencing the offshore buyer or recipient of the goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/MT103 / bank-credit evidence demonstrating that the export proceeds have been received on a French bank account within the contractual term
  • For services: signed engagement letter, foreign taxable-person certificate (foreign VAT number or non-EU corporate registry extract), and full fee/remuneration records proving the offshore performance or use

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting reform in force under Article 289 CGI and the implementing decrees (ordonnance n° 2022-1087 and associated decrees), with a 5-corner PEPPOL-style decarbonated API model: (i) by 1 September 2026 all taxable persons established in France must be able to receive structured e-invoices via the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or an accredited Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP); (ii) large enterprises (grandes entreprises) and mid-caps (ETI) must issue e-invoices in the crucial formats (Factur-X, UBL, or CII) and submit e-reporting from 1 September 2026; (iii) SMEs and microenterprises (PME/TPE) must issue e-invoices and transmit transaction e-reports from 1 September 2027; (iv) non-established foreign suppliers liable for French VAT must also route their invoice and transaction data through the PPF/PDP system according to the same schedule. France does not operate a real-time per-invoice digital stamping or pre-clearance system; compliance is enforced through the DGFiP's data-exchange API flows (Y1/Y2), cross-reconciliations of invoices with VAT returns, and automatic penalties for missing or non-compliant e-invoices. The mandate also expressly covers the information to be transmitted for the 'régularisation' of supplies to B2C end customers and the annual compulsory data audit trails for certified cash-register software under Article 465 bis CGI.

France's monetary-control framework does not impose a fixed statutory FX-repatriation window for ordinary current-export proceeds; the 180-day reference is applied by the DGFiP and customs authorities as a prudential internationally accepted maximum period for evidencing export-payment collection in export-exemption audits and transfer-pricing documentation. If export zero-rating is claimed without the required documentation, or is later clawed back, the DGFiP may: (i) reassess VAT on the entire turnover within the normal 3-year limitation period of Article L.169 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales (6 years in cases of disguised concealment of activity and 10 years where the taxpayer is not registered); (ii) apply late-payment interest of 0.20% per month under Article 1727 CGI; (iii) impose a negligence penalty of 40% of the reassessed VAT under Articles 1729 and 1730 CGI, increased to 80% for deliberate tax fraud or organized concealment under Article 1729(b) and Article 1729-0 CGI; (iv) apply automatic statutory fines for invoicing defects, including absence of mandatory invoicing information, up to 50% of the disputed invoice sums under Article 1788 A CGI; (v) reject input-VAT deductions and, in cross-border cases, engage mutual-assistance recovery under EU Directive 2010/24/EU; and (vi) refer the dossier to the competent criminal-chamber prosecutor with exposure for fraud under Articles 1741 and 1743 CGI (increased penalties for organized customs/export evasion), including imprisonment of up to five years and fines up to €500,000 for corporate officers and participating parties, plus confiscation of goods and prohibition of professional practice. The DGFiP cross-references customs exit data (NSTI / CUS), foreign-bank information-exchange agreements, and transfer-pricing documents to detect artificial, staged, or sham export structures, particularly in high-consulting-fee regulatory service invoices routed through low-tax jurisdictions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France belongs to the euro area; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital transactions. EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital apply, and there is no foreign exchange control regime for movements between EU member states and third countries, except for targeted international sanctions (e.g., CFSP/European Council restrictive measures) and statistical declarations to the Banque de France. Dividends, branch profits, and capital repatriation to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted from a currency control perspective, subject only to French corporate income tax, withholding tax where applicable, and anti-money-laundering / know-your-customer documentation. Capital account transactions require monthly reporting solely for balance-of-payments statistics where the annual transaction threshold exceeds EUR 12,500,000 for resident businesses or when specifically requested.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds into euro. French exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in accounts opened with EU-licensed credit institutions; the only obligation is accurate invoicing, customs declaration of export transactions (via the Single Administrative Document), and inclusion in the French balance-of-payments reporting when thresholds are met. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) acting within the Eurosystem; banking and financial supervision delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution); exchange control and balance-of-payments reporting oversight conducted by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under Articles L.151-1 to L.151-4 of the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility as supplementary payment gateway: French-licensed EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Shine, or Luxembourg/UK EMIs with EU passporting) can handle inbound/outbound wire transfers and card transactions. Legal constraints: an EMI does not provide a 'compte bancaire' in the strict legal sense (no credit institution license), so it cannot hold client funds with deposit insurance, cannot provide short-term treasury investment products, and is not suitable for the formal capital deposit requirement of a SARL. For transactional volume under EUR 1,000,000 per month and low-risk medical device regulatory consulting, an EMI is operationally acceptable but must be accompanied by a tier-1 bank account for statutory and creditor confidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan transfers into the SARL's French EUR account must be made via standard SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT for euro within EEA). The calling bank must specify the beneficiary bank (BNP/SocGen/Crédit Agricole) with the IBAN (FR76...). Compliance with French Balance of Payments reporting requires the wire to include in field 70 (remittance information) the reference 'Apport en capital – SARL' or 'Shareholder Loan – European Investment Bank Agreement', together with the unique corporate registration number (SIREN/SIRET). The French receiving bank will code the transaction under its BOP survey; for non-EEA transfers exceeding EUR 12,500 (single or cumulative), a specific BOP declaration (liaison financière avec l'étranger) may be triggered. Sanctionable practice is to avoid any split-wire or false purpose code.For B2B trade settlements (payments to US/EU suppliers, clinical research organizations, or certification bodies), the primary clearing rail is the SWIFT network via MT103 for cross-border euro or foreign currency transfers. For documented goods or services where a performance guarantee is required, the SARL can instruct its bank to issue a commercial Letter of Credit (Documentary L/C, UCP600) or Standby L/C. Alternative rails include SEPA Instant Credit Transfers for EEA payments of less than EUR 100,000, corporate card payments (Visa Business / Mastercard Corporate) for sub-€10,000 costs, and cross-border EUR transfers via ENTERGINE, RT1 or TIPS. Each outward wire must be accompanied by a compliant purpose code (commonly 'P' for services or the applicable BOP export/import category) and complete invoice/purchase order documentation, to be retained for AML/KYC and French tax audit purposes.EUR/USD delivered forward transaction (FX Forward) under a standard ISDA or French FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) master agreementEUR/USD FX swapEUR/CHF and EUR/GBP non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with limited convertibility in the French retail marketCross-currency interest rate swap (IRS) where the SARL holds floating-rate offshore liabilitiesPlain-vanilla currency options (calls/puts) with repo-style collateral arrangementsSpot transaction execution with split order or algorithmic execution through the bank's liquidity pool (with no change of purpose)

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility as supplementary payment gateway: French-licensed EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Shine, or Luxembourg/UK EMIs with EU passporting) can handle inbound/outbound wire transfers and card transactions. Legal constraints: an EMI does not provide a 'compte bancaire' in the strict legal sense (no credit institution license), so it cannot hold client funds with deposit insurance, cannot provide short-term treasury investment products, and is not suitable for the formal capital deposit requirement of a SARL. For transactional volume under EUR 1,000,000 per month and low-risk medical device regulatory consulting, an EMI is operationally acceptable but must be accompanied by a tier-1 bank account for statutory and creditor confidence.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (IS) rate for FY2024; 15% reduced rate applicable to the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs with turnover below EUR 10,000,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders (Art. 119 bis 2 CGI); 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds >= 5% for 24 months; non-resident individuals: 12.8% PFU plus 17.2% social levies (30% combined) subject to treaty and social-security coordination.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 annual pre-tax turnover (Local File/Master File); EUR 150,000 share capital and >50% foreign shareholding triggers the documentation duty irrespective of revenue; Country-by-Country report required where the group's consolidated revenue >= EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a Gulf-style ESR regime, but the GAAR (abus de droit, LPF Art. L.64), the MLI Principal Purpose Test, and substance-over-form case law deny treaty benefits and deductibility to artificial arrangements. A MedTech regulatory consultancy must demonstrate real French substance: qualified sign-off staff, contracts actually performed in France, premises, and decision-making for FDA 510(k)/CE-Mark submissions, rather than pass-through fee invoicing to offshore affiliates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and 5 years' imprisonment for individuals; legal persons fined up to EUR 2,500,000 (Art. 131-38 CP); tax reassessment at 80% penalty rate for bad faith plus 0.20% monthly interest (Art. 1729 CGI).

Director personal liability: Directors prosecuted as principals or accomplices (Art. 1741 CGI); personal civil solidarity for unpaid taxes, bank-account freezes, travel bans, and disqualification from company management for up to 15 years.
Late / Non-Filing of IS Returns

Statutory sanction: 10% automatic surcharge plus 0.20% monthly interest; 40% penalty for bad faith; 80% for fraudulent maneuvers; ex-officio assessment (taxation d'office) and withholding of VAT refunds.

Director personal liability: The managing director (gérant) becomes personally liable for the persistence of the filing default; personal fine up to EUR 1,500 and potential in solidum liability where the failure results from gross mismanagement.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law (Abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under LPF Art. L.64 with a 80% penalty on reassessed sums; refusal of treaty exemptions under the PPT; forfeiture of the 15% SME rate and CIR/CII credits; reclassification of distributions as non-deductible.

Director personal liability: Directors liable in solido for the reassessed tax under Art. 1807 CGI; judicial freezing of personal assets, travel restrictions, and disqualification from managing French companies.
AML / False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: False-invoicing offense: up to EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years' imprisonment (CGI Art. 1743; CP Art. 313-2); money laundering: up to 10 years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine for legal persons (CP Art. 324-1); 80% fiscal penalty plus asset seizure.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers exposed to personal restitution, travel bans, account-freezing orders, and imprisonment; civil liability toward creditors under the corporate-veil piercing doctrine (action en comblement de passif).
