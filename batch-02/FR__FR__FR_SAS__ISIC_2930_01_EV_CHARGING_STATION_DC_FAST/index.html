Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies to the SAS; the Articles set the capital freely. At least 50% of cash subscriptions must be paid up at subscription and the remaining 50% within five years of registration. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, customs, foreign-investment-control, and reporting requirements. A French SAS may generally receive foreign capital, pay foreign suppliers, repatriate dividends, repay documented shareholder loans, and transfer operating profits offshore without a general exchange-control approval. Dividend distributions require distributable profits, approved financial statements, corporate approvals, and applicable withholding-tax analysis. Interest, royalties, service fees, and intra-group payments must be commercially justified, arm's-length where applicable, properly documented, and reviewed for withholding tax, beneficial ownership, transfer pricing, deductibility, and anti-abuse rules. Payments involving sanctioned persons, jurisdictions, dual-use goods, or controlled technology may require blocking, licensing, or enhanced review. Foreign direct investment authorization may apply to activities falling within French protected sectors; ordinary manufacture of EV charging equipment is generally outside the protected sectors unless the business also involves regulated energy infrastructure, critical technologies, national-security-sensitive systems, or other controlled activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no universal statutory deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may normally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's treasury discretion. The company must retain commercial, customs, invoicing, transport, and payment records and comply with applicable Intrastat, customs, statistical, tax, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering reporting obligations. Individual banks may impose contractual documentation, transaction-monitoring, or account-crediting requirements. Cash receipts and certain cross-border payments may trigger French monetary-financial reporting obligations, and bank-specific reporting may apply to transactions involving non-EU jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, within the Eurosystem; foreign-exchange policy is principally governed by the European Union, the European Central Bank, French monetary and financial legislation, and the French Treasury. Prudential and conduct supervision of banks and payment institutions is principally performed by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operating payments and treasury administration, but not a complete substitute for a French commercial-bank relationship. A licensed European Economic Area payment institution or electronic-money institution may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, SEPA transfers, foreign-exchange services, and sometimes international wire access through regulated correspondent arrangements. It generally cannot provide bank deposits, conventional lending, statutory capital-deposit certificates, or unrestricted cash-management functionality unless separately licensed for those services. Offshore digital EMIs outside the EEA cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to French customers merely through an offshore application; they require an applicable French or EEA authorization, passporting basis, or authorized local arrangement. EMI onboarding may be restricted for high-value capital injections, complex ownership chains, non-EEA counterparties, cash-intensive activity, sanctioned-country exposure, or transactions requiring documentary trade-finance review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route subscriptions for SAS share capital through a French bank or another institution authorized to issue the required capital-deposit certificate. Use a blocked capital account before incorporation or a capital increase, identify the subscriber and the French SAS precisely, and state the legal purpose as share-capital subscription or capital increase. After registration or completion of the capital increase, provide the Kbis or equivalent registry evidence, updated articles or capital records, shareholder and board resolutions, and the capital-deposit certificate so funds can be released to the operational account. Route shareholder loans through a documented intercompany-loan or financing agreement, with arm's-length terms, lender and borrower account ownership evidence, transfer-pricing support, and any required French loan, tax, or statistical filings. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro transfers within the SEPA area and SWIFT MT103 or equivalent ISO 20022 payment messaging for non-SEPA wires. Include complete originator, beneficiary, IBAN or account number, BIC where applicable, purpose narrative, invoice or agreement reference, and UBO-consistent payer details. Do not split payments to avoid screening or reporting. Reconcile each receipt to the general ledger and retain the payment trail, contracts, bank statements, and accounting evidence.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments and SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 cross-border payment instructions for non-SEPA currencies and jurisdictions. For documentary trade finance, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit transmitted through authenticated bank channels, typically SWIFT MT700, with amendments through the issuing and advising banks; use confirmed letters of credit where country or bank risk warrants confirmation. Match payment instructions to purchase orders, contracts, invoices, Incoterms, customs declarations, transport documents, insurance documents, certificates of origin, and sanctions or export-control screening. For open-account trade, use controlled supplier onboarding, dual approval, verified beneficiary-account changes, payment limits, and three-way matching. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic French withholding tax rate on dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident legal entity is generally 25%, subject to exemptions and treaty relief. Dividends paid to a qualifying EU or EEA parent company may be exempt under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring the relevant ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, and anti-abuse conditions. Dividends paid to a French corporate shareholder are generally not subject to withholding tax, although participation-exemption and add-back rules apply at recipient level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the statutory filing deadline is generally the second business day following 1 May of the following year. Corporate income tax installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a 31 December year-end, it is generally due by 15 May.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File documentation is generally mandatory where the French entity's annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets are at least EUR 400 million, or where it is controlled by, controls, or belongs to a group containing an entity meeting the EUR 400 million threshold. Documentation may also be required under a formal tax audit request. The annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies to entities meeting the relevant EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold or belonging to a qualifying group, subject to the statutory conditions. Separate country-by-country reporting generally applies to the reporting entity of a multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France has no single standalone economic-substance regime equivalent to a comprehensive ESR applicable to all companies. Substance is assessed through transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-abuse rule, the principal-purpose test in treaties and EU law, controlled foreign company rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, permanent-establishment principles, interest limitation rules, and anti-hybrid rules. A French SAS should maintain adequate French decision-making, personnel, premises, operational assets, risk control, accounting records, banking activity, and commercial evidence proportionate to its EV-charger manufacturing and distribution functions. Conduit entities, circular financing, artificial royalty structures, and arrangements lacking commercial rationale are high-risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Administrative tax reassessment, late-payment interest, 40% penalty for deliberate breach, and up to 80% penalty for fraudulent maneuvers or abuse of law. Tax fraud may be criminally punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and EUR 500,000 fine, increasing in aggravated cases to up to seven years and EUR 3 million, subject to the applicable Criminal Code provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, civil liability for intentional or grossly negligent misconduct, management-ban orders, and tax-liability transfer under French tax procedure where they made collection impossible through fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated tax-law breaches. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered by a court in criminal proceedings.
Fraudulent transfer pricing and profit diversion

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment under the arm's-length principle, 25% corporate income tax, interest, applicable tax penalties, and documentation penalties. Missing or materially incomplete documentation may trigger the statutory penalty generally equal to the greater of EUR 10,000 or 0.5% of covered transactions, without limiting the reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they knowingly approve sham arrangements, falsified documentation, or deliberate profit concealment. Civil claims may include restitution of losses and damages; criminal exposure depends on proof of fraud, forgery, laundering, or participation in another offense.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under anti-abuse principles, denial of treaty or EU withholding relief, denial of deductions, withholding-tax assessments, corporate tax reassessment, and applicable penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for breach of management duties and criminal liability if the structure is used for tax fraud, money laundering, forgery, or concealment. A corporate veil is not automatically pierced merely because the company is thinly staffed.
Withholding-tax non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, late-payment interest, applicable penalties, denial of treaty relief, and potential penalties for inaccurate or missing declarations. The payer may remain liable even where the non-resident recipient received the gross amount.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal exposure where non-remittance is deliberate, records are falsified, or payments are made to conceal assets or distributions. Ordinary administrative withholding errors normally remain corporate liabilities unless aggravated misconduct is proven.
VAT and customs fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT and customs reassessment, import duties, interest, administrative penalties, seizure, and potentially criminal prosecution for organized or deliberate fraud. Customs penalties depend on the offense, goods, value, and applicable Union Customs Code and French customs provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal and civil exposure for intentional customs or VAT fraud, including restitution, fines, management bans, and imprisonment in serious cases. Personal liability is fact-dependent and is not automatic for a routine classification error.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist financing

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is generally punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine, with aggravated cases subject to higher penalties, including up to ten years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine. Regulated entities may also face supervisory sanctions, including significant monetary penalties, business restrictions, and license consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees may be prosecuted for participation, complicity, concealment, or negligent control failures where legally relevant. Courts may order confiscation, asset freezing, management bans, and restrictions on movement; travel bans are discretionary and case-specific.
Accounting fraud and false corporate records

Statutory sanction: Rejection of accounts, tax reassessment, civil damages, regulatory consequences, and criminal penalties under company and criminal law for falsification, presentation, or publication of inaccurate accounts.

Director personal liability: Presidents, directors, de facto managers, and persons approving false accounts may face personal civil liability, criminal fines, imprisonment, management bans, and contribution claims from creditors or insolvency practitioners.
Illegal dividend distribution and insolvency misconduct

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful dividends, damages, corporate-law penalties, and possible insolvency proceedings. Directors may be ordered to contribute to insufficient assets where management faults caused or aggravated the deficiency.

Director personal liability: The SAS president and de facto managers may incur restitution, damages, asset-contribution orders, management bans, and criminal liability for unlawful distributions or bankruptcy-related offenses. A dividend must be supported by properly approved accounts and distributable reserves.
Export controls, sanctions, and dual-use goods

Statutory sanction: Administrative measures, transaction blocking, asset freezes, customs seizure, significant fines, and criminal penalties under applicable French, European Union, and United Nations sanctions regimes.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, management bans, and confiscation where they knowingly authorize prohibited transactions or deliberately ignore sanctions controls.
Bribery and commercial corruption

Statutory sanction: Criminal bribery penalties may include imprisonment and substantial fines, confiscation, exclusion from public procurement, and corporate compliance measures. Corporate liability may be imposed independently of individual liability.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees may face imprisonment, fines, confiscation, civil restitution, management bans, and travel restrictions ordered by a court. The Sapin II framework also requires qualifying companies to maintain anti-corruption prevention controls.
