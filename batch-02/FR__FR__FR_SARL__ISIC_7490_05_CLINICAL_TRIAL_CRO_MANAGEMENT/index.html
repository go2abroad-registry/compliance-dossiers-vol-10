Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent clerk of the Commercial Court (Greffe du tribunal de commerce) or, where applicable, the competent commercial chamber of the Judicial Court; the National Register of Enterprises is administered by the National Institute of Industrial Property (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L223-1 to L223-43 and R223-1 to R223-22 governing sociétés à responsabilité limitée; Articles L123-1 et seq. and R123-1 et seq. governing registration and business formalities; Law No. 2019-486 of 22 May 2019 establishing the national electronic business-formality portal.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to a SARL. The capital is freely determined in the articles of association. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations account before registration; contributions in kind must be transferred under the articles and may require a contribution auditor unless a statutory exemption applies.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupation, verify building-use compliance, fire-safety systems, evacuation routes, emergency lighting, accessibility, electrical installations, and, where applicable, public-access building requirements with the competent municipality and departmental fire and rescue service. Maintain workplace risk assessment documentation (document unique d'évaluation des risques), occupational-health arrangements, electrical and equipment inspections, chemical and biological risk controls, and structural-load verification for laboratories, archives, or specialized equipment. No separate industrial clearance is normally required for ordinary office premises.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), French Ministry of Economy and Finance; online portal: impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, the TIN and VAT number are issued automatically at incorporation via the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises. Foreign entities not established in France must complete prior registration with the DGFiP using the dedicated forms for non-resident professionals (notably Form 1616 TD for non-resident companies) and obtain a French VAT number before performing any taxable supply in France. Supporting documents include corporate proof, non-EU VAT certificate, statutory representative identity, and a statement of intended activity. Processing normally takes 4 to 6 weeks, with effective date coordinated with the registration application.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Société à Responsabilité Limitée is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) as a separate legal entity by default) — For a French SARL, the TIN and VAT number are issued automatically at incorporation via the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises. Supporting documents include corporate proof, non-EU VAT certificate, statutory representative identity, and a statement of intended activity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Société à Responsabilité Limitée is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) as a separate legal entity by default)

Available election forms

France does not maintain a check-the-box regime. A SARL may elect for fiscal transparency (impôt sur le revenu) if it qualifies as a small enterprise under Article 239 bis AA of the Code Général des Impôts, or if it is a family SARL (SARL de famille) under Article 239 bis AA. Hybrid mismatch elections under ATAD are not available for entity classification; all such mismatches are addressed through specific EU/French adjustment rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For foreign non-established suppliers: no registration threshold — any taxable supply in France triggers mandatory VAT registration. For domestic businesses eligible for the franchise en base: €85,000 for goods, €37,500 for services (annual turnover, after exclusions). Once the threshold is exceeded, VAT registration and collection become compulsory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods physically leaving the European Union qualifies for 0% TVA provided the exporter holds a valid customs export declaration and proof of departure. For CRO and clinical trial services, zero-rating may apply where services are supplied to a customer established in a third country and the benefit of the service is effectively enjoyed outside the EU, or where the service is directly linked to goods exported. Evidentiary requirements include the written contract, clinical trial protocol, confirmation of non-EU customer status, and documentation demonstrating the place of business of the customer and the absence of use within France.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR or airway bill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer proof of export payment

France mandates mandatory B2B e-invoicing via the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) with phased enforcement: large and mid-size taxpayers from 1 September 2026, and small/micro taxpayers from 1 September 2027. Electronic invoices must comply with structured data standards (Factur-X, UBL, CII) and B2B/B2C cross-border data must be transmitted to the tax authority through e-reporting. The regime is continuous fiscal data submission rather than physical real-time clearance; no per-invoice digital stamp is required.

Export zero-rating is closely audited by the DGFiP. Incorrect or fraudulent claims expose the taxpayer to immediate VAT clawback, late payment interest at 0.20% per month, and an automatic penalty of 80% for concealement if a false claim is deliberate (Article 1729 CGI). In case of tax fraud, criminal penalties under Article 1741 CGI include up to five years’ imprisonment and a €500,000 fine, plus denial of deduction of the export-related input VAT.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a Eurozone member state, maintains full current account and capital account convertibility under Articles 63–65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, the EU Capital Movements Regulation and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. The euro is fully convertible, and there are no prior foreign exchange authorisations for export receipts, clinical-trial service fees, intercompany services, profit remittances, capital subscriptions or dividend distributions. The capital account is equally liberalised: non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of a French SARL without exchange-control approval. The practical restrictions on profit repatriation are taxation and reporting filters rather than FX controls: (i) corporate income tax at the standard French rate of 25% on net taxable profits; (ii) dividend withholding tax, generally 25% for non-resident corporate shareholders and 30% for non-resident individuals, unless reduced or exempted under the relevant double-taxation treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; (iii) the statutory Banque de France cross-border transfer reporting certificate (DTS) triggered for each payment exceeding EUR 12,500; and (iv) transfer-pricing documentation substantiating the arm's-length nature of the CRO's cross-border remuneration. No separate 'profit remittance tax' or exchange-rate surrender charge is levied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory repatriation timeline for export receipts. Proceeds from cross-border clinical-trial CRO services may be collected in EUR, USD, GBP or other convertible currencies and may be retained in domestic accounts, foreign currency accounts, or accounts held abroad without any obligation to convert into euro. The residual administrative obligations are: (i) submission of the Banque de France DTS declaration for any cross-border financial transfer exceeding EUR 12,500, including the statement of purpose and supporting commercial documentation; (ii) declaration to customs of any cross-border movement of cash or bearer negotiable instruments of EUR 10,000 or more; (iii) full AML/KYC and EU/UN/OFAC sanctions screening of the counterparty for every receipt; (iv) annual tax declaration of foreign bank accounts, if any, under articles 1649 A and 1736 of the French General Tax Code (form 3916-bis); and (v) compliance with French VAT invoicing and electronic invoicing requirements for B2B supplies of services. In practice, therefore, the permissible retention ratio is 100%, with no central-bank timeline attached.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating as a national central bank within the Eurosystem and the European Central Bank (ECB) framework; foreign exchange regulation, capital movement statistics and balance-of-payments reporting are administered jointly by Banque de France and the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty. Prudential supervision of credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions is exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), with significant institutions supervised under the ECB Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a fallback channel, High for speed of remote onboarding and low-value disbursements. Under the Revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2), an electronic money institution (EMI) that is licensed in another EEA state and passported into France may lawfully provide payment accounts and cross-border payment services to a French SARL without establishing a French branch. However, a French or passported EMI is not a credit institution and therefore cannot accept repayable deposits, issue documentary credits, provide bank guarantees, arrange commercial letters of credit, or offer proprietary FX hedging beyond plain conversion. The EMI must safeguard client funds in a segregated account and is subject to ACPR or home-state supervision; it also lacks the balance-sheet capacity to support the large milestone wires, investigator site payments in emerging markets, and sponsor-mandated financial instruments that a clinical-trial CRO typically requires. An offshore EMI without an EEA licence must not be used for regulated French payment services unless it operates through an authorised ACPR-licensed agent or a licensed partner bank. The prudent structure is to maintain primary treasury banking with an ACPR-licensed Tier 1 commercial bank and use a passported EMI only as a contingency for rapid vendor payments, investigator reimbursements and emergency disbursements when the primary bank's KYC review is still pending.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection is executed by SWIFT MT103 international wire transfer in EUR, USD or GBP from the shareholder's regulated bank account to the SARL's account at the chosen French Tier 1 bank. For EUR-denominated injections from within the SEPA area, a SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) to the French IBAN is sufficient; for USD injections, the funds are routed from the US bank via Fedwire or CHIPS to the French bank's US correspondent and then credited to the French account after conversion to EUR if required. France requires no prior exchange-control approval, but the receiving bank must classify the payment in its Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting as 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital' or 'Shareholder Loan — Intra-Group Financing' as applicable. If the transfer exceeds EUR 12,500, the statutory DTS declaration (Cerfa No. 12578) must be completed, stating the purpose and the French corporate identifier (SIREN). The supporting verification package must include: notarised board or shareholders' resolution approving the capital increase and the issuance of shares; a bank reference or account statement from the investor showing the source of funds; the SARL's Kbis extract and INPI beneficial-ownership register; identity documentation and tax-residency certificates for the subscriber; and, where the injection is a shareholder loan, a written loan agreement setting out an arm's-length interest rate, maturity, and repayment schedule for tax and DTS purposes. The transfer should refer, in SWIFT field 70 remittance information, to the precise purpose, e.g. 'Subscription to share capital of SARL ... (capital increase dated ...)' or 'Shareholder current-account advance for working capital'.Outbound disbursements use two principal clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) settled through TARGET2/STEP2 for all euro payments to EU/EEA beneficiary banks, covering investigators, clinical sites, suppliers and EU subcontractors; and (ii) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer for non-EEA and foreign-currency payments, routed through the French bank's correspondent network (CHIPS/Fedwire for USD, CHAPS for GBP and other hubs). For clinical-trial CRO operations, commercial letters of credit are not suited to service disbursements; where the sponsor requires payment certainty, the appropriate instrument is a first-demand bank guarantee or standby letter of credit securing the CRO's milestone performance obligations. All outbound transfers must include beneficiary bank identifier (BIC), beneficiary IBAN or account number, and a purpose narrative complying with the French DTS statistical categories; the bank will also require source documentation for any single transfer above the applicable AML threshold (typically EUR 10,000–12,500). The charge-allocation code should generally be 'OUR' for site payments to ensure the full contract amount reaches the beneficiary without intermediary-bank deductions. Payments denominated in USD should be instructed with strict routing details to avoid multiple correspondent deductions. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a fallback channel, High for speed of remote onboarding and low-value disbursements. Under the Revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2), an electronic money institution (EMI) that is licensed in another EEA state and passported into France may lawfully provide payment accounts and cross-border payment services to a French SARL without establishing a French branch. However, a French or passported EMI is not a credit institution and therefore cannot accept repayable deposits, issue documentary credits, provide bank guarantees, arrange commercial letters of credit, or offer proprietary FX hedging beyond plain conversion. The EMI must safeguard client funds in a segregated account and is subject to ACPR or home-state supervision; it also lacks the balance-sheet capacity to support the large milestone wires, investigator site payments in emerging markets, and sponsor-mandated financial instruments that a clinical-trial CRO typically requires. An offshore EMI without an EEA licence must not be used for regulated French payment services unless it operates through an authorised ACPR-licensed agent or a licensed partner bank. The prudent structure is to maintain primary treasury banking with an ACPR-licensed Tier 1 commercial bank and use a passported EMI only as a contingency for rapid vendor payments, investigator reimbursements and emergency disbursements when the primary bank's KYC review is still pending.
