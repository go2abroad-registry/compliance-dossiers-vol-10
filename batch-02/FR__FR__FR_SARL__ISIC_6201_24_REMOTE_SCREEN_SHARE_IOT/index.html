Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry) of the department of the registered office; electronic intake through the Guichet Unique operated by INPI (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL), L.210-6 (legal personality), R.123-1 et seq. (RCS registration); Décret no. 2021-1738 relating to the Guichet Unique.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'entreprise (formulaire M0) with annexed signed Statuts, capital deposit certificate, legal notice receipt and domiciliation proof; registered at the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a SARL (capital is set freely in the Statuts). Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked account with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released only upon RCS registration; in-kind contributions require a commissaire aux apports report and are fully paid at subscription. A legal reserve of 10% of the capital is mandatory.

Funding & unlock path: For the ordinary office lease, the premises must carry a technical completeness certificate from the landlord; office workplaces are subject to fire safety rules of the Code du travail (Articles R.4216-1 et seq.) and, if open to the public, to 5th-category ERP safety checks by the communal fire commission; an annual workplace inspection by the occupational health service (service de santé au travail) is required once staff are hired, and electrical installations must be verified regularly with an electrical inspection certificate kept at the premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a SARL (capital is set freely in the Statuts). Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked account with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released only upon RCS registration; in-kind contributions require a commissaire aux apports report and are fully paid at subscription. A legal reserve of 10% of the capital is mandatory.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) — French national tax administration; electronic filing and taxpayer portal at impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel); business formalities portal operated by INPI: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; customs documentation portal: douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned French SARL must first complete legal incorporation via the INPI Guichet Unique, obtaining SIREN, SIRET and NAF/APE codification within approximately 5 business days. The DGFIP then automatically attributes the intra-Community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) for established companies; if not assigned automatically, the SARL files Form CEI or activates the VAT identity module in the Impôt Professional online portal. EORI activation (n° EORI FR + SIREN) on the French Customs EORI portal is required for cross-border goods operations. A non-established foreign operator supplying taxable remote digital services in France must register with the DGFIP under Article 286 CGI and, if outside the EU, appoint a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) jointly liable for VAT under Articles 289-A and 289-I CGI; an established SARL does not require a fiscal representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a commercial SARL is by default taxed as a corporation with full legal personality, subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) on its French and effectively French-source income, with a standard IS rate of 25% (reduced rates apply to small profits) and a 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for eligible SMEs; the SARL is fiscally transparent only if a valid family SARL election (IR) is made; territorial scope: French-resident companies are taxed on worldwide income from their French operations (subject to CbC and PE rules for foreign branches); new SARLs cannot elect pass-through treatment except for SARL de famille or SARL d'exercice libéral under the CGI. — A foreign-owned French SARL must first complete legal incorporation via the INPI Guichet Unique, obtaining SIREN, SIRET and NAF/APE codification within approximately 5 business days. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a commercial SARL is by default taxed as a corporation with full legal personality, subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) on its French and effectively French-source income, with a standard IS rate of 25% (reduced rates apply to small profits) and a 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for eligible SMEs; the SARL is fiscally transparent only if a valid family SARL election (IR) is made; territorial scope: French-resident companies are taxed on worldwide income from their French operations (subject to CbC and PE rules for foreign branches); new SARLs cannot elect pass-through treatment except for SARL de famille or SARL d'exercice libéral under the CGI.

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available elections include: (1) option for IS by otherwise transparency-eligible entities under CGI Articles 206 and 239; (2) IR election for SARL de famille under Article 239 bis AA where all quota holders are natural persons of the same family group; (3) fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) election under Articles 223 A/B for a French parent holding at least 95% of a French subsidiary, enabling group-wide loss offset and dividend elimination; (4) mandatory ATAD hybrid-mismatch constraints under Articles 209B and 212 bis CGI, which are not elective design tools but require documentation of reverse-hybrid and double-deduction avoidance; (5) transfer-pricing filing and CbC report designations where the group exceeds the €750 million consolidated revenue threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-resident suppliers of French-taxable remote digital/IoT services have zero registration threshold and must register from the first euro. A French-established SARL may rely on the VAT franchise en base exemption: for services (relevant to ISIC 6201 remote support software) the base threshold is €37,500 for 2024 and €39,100 from 1 January 2025, with a tolerance threshold of €41,000 (2024) and €43,100 (2025); for goods the base thresholds are €85,000 (2024) and €91,900 (2025), with tolerance at €93,500 (2024) and €101,000 (2025). The franchise is lost if either threshold is exceeded, in which case VAT registration at the ordinary 20% rate becomes compulsory. Intra-EU cross-border B2C digital-services or distance-selling turnover up to €10,000 may be reported via the OSS election without direct French registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical goods exports outside the EU qualify for French zero-rating under CGI Article 262-I provided the goods are dispatched outside the EU within 3 months and the exporter holds the statutory evidence (transport, delivery to an independent third party abroad, and absence of French transformation). Intra-EU B2B dispatches are zero-rated when the customer supplies a valid EU VAT number and the goods are listed in the monthly DEB recapitulative statement; services of remote screen-sharing and IoT telemetry software (ISIC 6201) supplied to a non-French established business customer are outside the scope of French VAT ('hors champ TVA') under the Article 259 A-1 B2B place-of-supply rule, which equates to an export zero-ratified position; B2C remote digital services fall outside French VAT when the customer's habitual residence is outside the EU under Article 259 D CGI. Exporters may recover input VAT on export costs (including hardware delivered abroad and foreign distribution costs) under Article 271 CGI, exercising the monthly export VAT-credit refund route rather than the normal quarterly cycle, using the Cerfa 3559 refund request form and supporting export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / Déclaration en Douane) accepted by French Customs under export code 10 or 11 with a valid MRN
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR transport document naming the foreign consignee and establishing EU exit of the goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with Incoterms and export valuation
  • International Transaction Annex (ITA) or six-copy export invoice set where applicable
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 bank evidence showing the export proceeds have been repatriated to France within 180 days
  • Intra-EU transfer documents: VIES-validated customer VAT number and the monthly recapitulative statement (DEB) filed by day 10
  • Service export substantiation: the underlying service contract, remote-delivery system logs, technical acceptance by the non-French client, and an attestation of effective use and benefit outside France for remote-screen-share/IoT telemetry services

Mandatory structured e-invoicing for all French domestic B2B transactions under the Y-clearance model: factur-X (PEPPOL BIS 3.02 / UBL or CII) invoices must be exchanged through a certified partner dematerialization platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire — PDP) connected to the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); mandatory timeline: large enterprises from 1 September 2026, SMEs and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027; domestic B2C transactions (typical for remote-screen-share subscriptions) are subject to e-reporting of transaction and payment data to the PPF via PDP APIs. This is a data-clearance/audit schema, not an invoice-by-invoice real-time fiscal stamping system; there is no Annex-style state-issued stamp. Cross-border B2C supplies can be declared through the French OSS quarterly return (guichet unique TVA on impots.gouv.fr) with a one-time unlimited election. Non-compliance penalties are €15 per invoice, capped at €15,000 per calendar year, plus VAT reassessment exposure and denial of deduction rights.

DGFIP export-audit teams under the BOI-TVA-EXP doctrine require full export substantiation within 30 days of a formal audit request; failure to produce the documentation triggers automatic re-instatement of VAT at 20%, with 0.20% monthly late interest, a 5% statutory increase for late payment, and a 40% bad-faith penalty under Article 1729(b) or 80% penalty for abusive/maneuver conduct under Article 1729(a). Fraudulent zero-rating or refund claims additionally expose the company and its management to criminal tax prosecution under Article 1741 CGI, with penalties of up to €3,000,000 and 7 years' imprisonment in aggravated cases, asset seizure, court-ordered publication of the conviction, tax injunction orders, and joint civil liability of directors; the DGFIP also coordinates with the French Customs anti-fraud taskforce and EU OLAF on carousel-style VAT fraud involving cross-border digital services, retrospectively clawing back input VAT credits and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. The Euro is legal tender; France applies the EU Treaty free movement of capital (TFEU Articles 63-66) with no general licensing for current or capital account transactions. Inbound direct investment into the new SARL requires no prior authorisation (the foreign-investment screening decree of 2019-1590 only applies to sensitive defence/security or critical-infrastructure sectors and is not expected to capture ISIC 6201 remote-software activities). Profit and dividend remittances are unrestricted from an FX standpoint; only statistical balance-of-payments reporting (Banque de France 'liaison financière' declarations for transactions ≥ EUR 15,000) and standard French dividend withholding tax (default 25% for corporate recipients or 30% for individuals, treaty relief available) apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no minimum conversion ratios, and no repatriation deadlines. Foreign-currency receipts may be retained in an authorised foreign-currency account (compte en devises) at an ACPR-licensed bank. The SARL must only comply with the monthly financial-relations declaration to Banque de France when an individual cross-border transfer, or cumulative transfers to the same non-resident counterparty in a calendar month, reaches EUR 15,000.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (central bank), acting jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) under the Code monétaire et financier, Articles L151-1 to L165-1 and R151-1 to R165-1.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High for operational settlement accounts: ACPR-licensed or EU-passported EMIs (Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business, Wise Business, N26 Business) can onboard a low-risk SaaS SARL fully remotely. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs do not hold client funds as classic deposits and are not covered by the Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution, so funds are safeguarded subject to a higher unsecured credit risk; (ii) the SARL constitution capital block must be deposited with an ACPR-licensed credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, meaning an EMI cannot issue the required attestation de dépôt des fonds for the registration block; (iii) offshore non-EEA digital EMIs without an EU banking licence or ACPR passport are not viable for the French registered entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share capital deposit: funds are wired by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the SARL's blocked capital account (compte bloqué) at an ACPR-licensed credit institution, or placed with a notary or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the depositary issues the attestation de dépôt des fonds required for Kbis registration, and the block is released upon extraction of the Kbis. Shareholder loans and current accounts: SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR or SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR with OECD BPM6 purpose code 'K.1 Equity capital' or 'K.2.2 Other capital' and remittance information referencing the shareholder resolution. A monthly Banque de France EFI (liaison financière) declaration is mandatory once individual or cumulative cross-border flows reach EUR 15,000. Verification controls: beneficiary name must exactly match the SARL's IBAN title, the Kbis certificate must be filed with the bank, and source-of-funds evidence retained for AML inspection.EU supplier payments via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant with structured creditor references; non-EU supplier and professional fees via SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) with BPM6 service-purpose code 'K.2.4.2.2 Other business services' and underlying invoices. SaaS platform settlements: Paddle (Merchant of Record) nets end-customer receipts and remits the residual to the SARL as platform/revenue-share fees, requiring an inbound MT103 or SEPA credit with a business-services purpose code; Stripe payouts settle multi-currency operating revenue to the operational account, reconciled monthly against platform settlement statements. Outbound licensing, dividends, or shareholder-loan repayments require SWIFT MT103 plus board minutes, intercompany agreements, form 2777 withholding certificates, and double-tax-treaty relief documentation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High for operational settlement accounts: ACPR-licensed or EU-passported EMIs (Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business, Wise Business, N26 Business) can onboard a low-risk SaaS SARL fully remotely. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs do not hold client funds as classic deposits and are not covered by the Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution, so funds are safeguarded subject to a higher unsecured credit risk; (ii) the SARL constitution capital block must be deposited with an ACPR-licensed credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, meaning an EMI cannot issue the required attestation de dépôt des fonds for the registration block; (iii) offshore non-EEA digital EMIs without an EU banking licence or ACPR passport are not viable for the French registered entity.
