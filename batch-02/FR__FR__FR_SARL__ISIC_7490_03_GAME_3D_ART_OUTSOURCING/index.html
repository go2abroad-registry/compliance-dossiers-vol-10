Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital (freely set in the articles; 1 EUR suffices) following the Loi PACTE 2019. Cash contributions must be released to at least 20% at incorporation, the balance within five years; in-kind contributions must be fully released immediately. The released amount must be deposited in a blocked escrow account at a French bank or with a notary and can only be withdrawn after the Kbis is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety per Labour Code R.4227: annual inspection of fire extinguishers by an accredited body, emergency lighting, evacuation plan, two unobstructed exits, smoke detection where required; structural load certificate from a technical controller (bureau de contrôle) if installing heavy server or rendering racks or a mezzanine; the Single Occupational Risk Assessment Document (DUERP) finalised and registered with the occupational health service (SPST); workplace accessibility for disabled workers; and civil liability insurance covering the premises.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – French tax administration; online portal: impots.gouv.fr. Customs and export controls are administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via the national single window DELTA.

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor incorporating a French SARL registers through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises; the registration file is transmitted to the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés), which issues the statutory identifiers SIREN, SIRET, and APE/NAF code. The Greffe transmits the data to DGFiP, which automatically allocates the French intra-Community VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) under Article 286 ter of the CGI. A non-resident foreign entity without a French permanent establishment that makes taxable supplies in France must file a direct application for VAT registration with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises using the official VAT registration form (Cerfa n°13789), and must appoint a fiscal representative established in France if it is outside the European Union. The application must identify the foreign entity, its foreign tax number, the nature of supplies, and the French agent or representative, after which the DGFiP issues a French VAT number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: A French SARL is by default a corporation subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on its worldwide profits when its place of effective management is in France. It is not a pass-through entity by default. Territorial scope: French-resident IS taxpayers are taxed on worldwide income, with foreign branch income relieved by tax treaties or by the territoriality rules of Article 209 CGI where applicable. The SARL is not located in a tax-exempt zone and is not eligible for patent box treatment unless specific reduced rates for intellectual property apply. — A foreign investor incorporating a French SARL registers through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises; the registration file is transmitted to the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés), which issues the statutory identifiers SIREN, SIRET, and APE/NAF code. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: A French SARL is by default a corporation subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on its worldwide profits when its place of effective management is in France. It is not a pass-through entity by default. Territorial scope: French-resident IS taxpayers are taxed on worldwide income, with foreign branch income relieved by tax treaties or by the territoriality rules of Article 209 CGI where applicable. The SARL is not located in a tax-exempt zone and is not eligible for patent box treatment unless specific reduced rates for intellectual property apply.

Available election forms

French law does not provide a check-the-box election. However, a SARL meeting the statutory conditions of Article 239 bis A CGI may elect to be taxed as a transparent partnership (régime des sociétés de personnes) for a limited period, generally up to five fiscal years, if the members are individuals or eligible corporate shareholders. This option must be filed with DGFiP before the end of the relevant fiscal year. Additionally, a French parent company holding at least 95% of a subsidiary may elect l'intégration fiscale (fiscal integration), allowing a tax group to offset taxable profits and losses among members. Hybrid mismatch elections are not discretionary; France applies mandatory anti-hybrid rules under Article 212 bis CGI implementing ATAD, which override contractual classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general economic turnover threshold for a non-established foreign supplier making taxable supplies in France: VAT registration is required as soon as the first taxable supply is made unless an express exemption applies. For small French-established businesses, the franchise en base de TVA exemptions apply up to EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for supplies of services per calendar year, but the franchise is an exemption, not a registration threshold. For intra-Community distance sales of goods by another EU taxable person, the statutory OSS distance-sales threshold is EUR 10,000; sales below that threshold may remain subject to the supplier's home Member State VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to a destination outside the European Union are zero-rated under Article 262 CGI when the supplier proves that the goods have physically left EU customs territory. The physical departure must generally occur within 90 days from the supply date, or within a longer contractual period not exceeding two years where the supply involves processing, assembly, or consignment. The 0% rate applies to the sale only if a valid customs export declaration has been accepted and the supplier holds transport documents evidencing exit from the EU. For services, B2B services supplied to a recipient established outside the EU are outside French VAT territorial scope under Article 259-1 CGI, and therefore not subject to French VAT, provided the supplier has obtained the recipient's valid foreign VAT or equivalent business registration number and contractual evidence. Offshore game 3D art outsourcing services to a non-EU game publisher can qualify as zero-rated or VAT-excluded services if the recipient is a business established outside the EU and the supply is not effectively linked to a French fixed establishment.

  • Accepted customs export declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation / DEX) with a valid Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice (original) stating exporter, consignee, incoterms, value, nature of goods, and destination
  • Packing list detailing carton weights, counts, and shipping marks
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR/rail waybill evidencing physical transport from France or EU to a non-EU destination
  • Proof of arrival in the third country, such as customs entry declaration, port of destination confirmation, or delivery receipt
  • Intrastat / Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens (DEB) filing when the goods remain within EU statistical thresholds
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing receipt of export consideration within the statutory foreign-exchange repatriation period

France mandates structured electronic invoicing and e-reporting for domestic B2B transactions. The B2G segment already uses the Chorus Pro portal. In the B2B segment, all taxable persons established in France must, from 1 September 2026, issue electronic invoices through a certified dematerialization platform (PDP) or the public portal Chorus Pro, and simultaneously transmit invoice and transaction data to DGFiP under the e-invoicing/e-reporting reform. The reform will be phased for small enterprises from 1 September 2027. There is no per-transaction real-time fiscal stamping in the manner of a continuous clearance system; however, each invoice must contain the mandatory data set, including SIREN, SIRET, VAT identification number, invoice category, supply date, unit price, VAT amount, and a unique invoice number. Export invoices are reported on the French VAT return and, when relevant, in the French Intrastat/DEB declarations. Customs export declarations are processed electronically through the DELTA system with a valid MRN.

DGFiP and DGDDI cross-check customs declaration data with physical freight manifests, exit records, and recipient-country entry evidence. If the exporter cannot substantiate the physical departure of the goods or the non-EU establishment of the service recipient, the zero-rating is retroactively disallowed, and the exporter must pay the missing 20% French VAT and file an amended VAT return. Where the French tax authority finds a deliberate misstatement, the statutory penalties under Articles 1728 to 1729 CGI include: late-payment interest (intérêt de retard) at 0.20% per month; a 5% to 10% penalty for late filing, increasing to 40% for insufficient declarations; a 40% penalty for deliberate inaccuracies; and an 80% penalty for abusive scheme or organized fraud. Criminal prosecution for tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI may lead to a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of up to five years. False customs declarations are also sanctionable under the Customs Code Articles 414 to 416 with confiscation of the goods and fines of up to twice the value of the fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a founding member of the Eurozone; the euro is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account in accordance with TFEU Articles 63-66 on the free movement of capital. There is no prior exchange-control approval requirement for inbound FDI into an SARL, for profit remittances to non-resident shareholders, or for payment of cross-border trade invoices. Legal restrictions are limited to EU autonomous sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, and balance-of-payments statistical reporting. Profit remittance is unrestricted; however, dividends paid by an SARL to non-resident shareholders may be subject to withholding tax under Article 119 quater CGI unless reduced by an applicable double taxation treaty. Enterprises must maintain documentary evidence of the underlying transaction and comply with ACPR customer due diligence when any transfer reaches mandatory reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. France abolished its exchange-control surrender regime under Ordonnance n° 67-1121 of 22 December 1967 and fully liberalized external transactions under Loi n° 86-793 of 25 July 1986. There is no statutory deadline requiring repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio of foreign currency receipts into euros. An exporting SARL may lawfully retain USD or other foreign currency balances in authorized foreign-currency compte devises at a licensed French credit institution and may convert them at its own commercial discretion. The sole ongoing obligation is indirect statistical reporting: French banks transmit cross-border payment data to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments purposes when monthly transaction volumes exceed thresholds set by the Bank; this is a reporting requirement, not a surrender or conversion rule.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the national central bank, the exclusive issuer of euro banknotes, the competent authority for balance-of-payments statistical compilation, and the supervisory coordinator for payment systems. Licensing and prudential/AML enforcement for credit institutions is delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution). Residual exchange-control policy powers rest with the Direction Générale du Trésor under the Code monétaire et financier, particularly Articles L151-1 to L165-1.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

High for licensed French payment institutions and e-money institutions such as Qonto and Shine, which offer full French IBANs, robust API treasury feeds, and smooth AML onboarding for low-risk creative BPO entities. Medium for passported EU EMIs such as Revolut Business, N26 Business, and Bunq, which retain fiduciary safeguarding accounts at partner banks and may restrict certain payment purposes. Low for offshore digital EMIs and UK-centred neo-banks after Brexit, as they generally cannot hold dedicated French IBANs nor access the Banque de France retail payment system directly; any reliance on such entities must be structured via a French licensed intermediary. Legal constraint: EMIs cannot grant credit and, under the e-money directive transposed into Articles L525-4 and following of the Code monétaire et financier, client funds must be safeguarded in a segregated account, which may complicate high-value escrow and capital-blocage procedures. Recommended architecture: a licensed Tier-1 French commercial bank as the primary clearing account, with one domestic EMI wallet for operational convenience and PayPal as an invoice-level settlement gateway for small contracts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for a non-resident shareholder wiring capital to FR_SARL: (1) execute the SARL statutes and, if applicable, use the bank's dedicated capital escrow account opened in the company's name; (2) transmit a SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank with a clear purpose reference to the corporate deed, using the Banque de France balance-of-payments classification code for capital operations (category 'Investment — capital of companies', code 301 under the BDR framework); (3) ensure the instruction identifies the SARL by exact legal name, SIREN number, and French IBAN beginning with FR76; (4) the receiving bank will require, as verification, the Kbis extract, the executed statutes, the RBE filing, the identity of the transferring shareholder, and the source-of-funds evidence; (5) for amounts exceeding prevailing ACPR/statistical thresholds, the bank files a balance-of-payments declaration and may request a formal declaration of fund origin under Article L561-15 of the Code monétaire et financier; (6) upon receipt, the account is credited to the capital sub-ledger and may be used only after verification of the legal capital obligation, with any surplus held in a current-account shareholder loan recorded by a written convention de compte courant; (7) for all future invoices, inbound USD payments from overseas publishers are routed via SWIFT MT103 with settlement through the correspondent banking network and converted to EUR at the bank's commercial rate or via a pre-agreed FX forward.Clearing-rail hierarchy for the SARL's outflows: primary rail is the SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA SCT and SEPA Instant) for EUR disbursements to European contractors and domestic suppliers, providing same-day or immediate settlement with full IBAN validation; international non-EUR disbursements use SWIFT MT103 with MT202 COV cover payments, ensuring correspondent-bank transparency and fee predictability; for high-value contract settlements to overseas game-art subcontractors, the SARL may establish an irrevocable confirmed documentary letter of credit (Commercial LC) at its Tier-1 bank, with presentation of invoices, artwork-delivery acceptance certificates, and the applicable INCOTERMS 2020 clause (e.g., DAP or EXW); PayPal Business serves as a fallback micro-rail for engagements below EUR 2,500 or for clients mandating PayPal settlement, subject to PayPal's KYC on each counterparty and its own FX conversion margin; all outbound wires must reference the underlying commercial invoice and be matched in the treasury file with the purchase order and proof-of-delivery. It is legally impermissible to mix personal funds with corporate treasury; inter-company payments to related entities must be supported by a formal services agreement and arm's-length invoices.EUR/USD outright FX forward (change à terme ferme) executed with a licensed French credit institution, covering confirmed USD receivables from overseas game publishers without requiring any regulatory authorization.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) denominated in restricted or exotic currencies (for example BRL, KRW, INR or onshore CNY) where physical delivery is legally impossible; permitted when the reference currency exposure arises from a bona fide commercial service contract.European-style plain vanilla FX options (change optionnel à prime octroyée) purchased to cap adverse USD/EUR moves while retaining upside, treated as an eligible hedge of the SARL's trade-debt portfolio.Cross-currency swap (XCCY) concluded under an ISDA-style master agreement converting a USD-denominated receivable or liability stream into EUR cash flows for multi-year studio agreements.FX swap (change de devises simultané) used for short-term liquidity management, converting temporary EUR excess balances into USD only when a corresponding USD payment is due within the swap's ten-month window.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High for licensed French payment institutions and e-money institutions such as Qonto and Shine, which offer full French IBANs, robust API treasury feeds, and smooth AML onboarding for low-risk creative BPO entities. Medium for passported EU EMIs such as Revolut Business, N26 Business, and Bunq, which retain fiduciary safeguarding accounts at partner banks and may restrict certain payment purposes. Low for offshore digital EMIs and UK-centred neo-banks after Brexit, as they generally cannot hold dedicated French IBANs nor access the Banque de France retail payment system directly; any reliance on such entities must be structured via a French licensed intermediary. Legal constraint: EMIs cannot grant credit and, under the e-money directive transposed into Articles L525-4 and following of the Code monétaire et financier, client funds must be safeguarded in a segregated account, which may complicate high-value escrow and capital-blocage procedures. Recommended architecture: a licensed Tier-1 French commercial bank as the primary clearing account, with one domestic EMI wallet for operational convenience and PayPal as an invoice-level settlement gateway for small contracts.
