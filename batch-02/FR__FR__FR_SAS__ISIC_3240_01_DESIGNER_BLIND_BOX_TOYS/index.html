Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SAS under French law. The Articles of Association freely set the share capital; founders may contribute cash, in-kind assets, or industry/irreducible contributions. Paid-in capital must be fully released only for cash contributions subject to a two-year deferral or five-year deferral under conditions set by the Articles; bank escrow (blocage des fonds) is mandatory on the designated French bank account until the Registrar confirms incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: application of the Fire Code (Code de l'Intérieur) requiring a self-prevention of fire in Établissements Recevant des Professionnels (ERP) if the warehouse area > 300 m²; for industrial plants, must obtain attestation from the SDIS (Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours) for conformity of fire-fighting systems, evacuation plans and flammable material storage; structural load inspection: engineer attestation of floor capacity for casting machines and resin modules, if plant load > 500 kg/m²; workplace clearance: DREETS occupational health inspection to Laplace Labor (labor inspectorate), following the decree on workplace rules to ensure extractive ventilation (vitesse d'aspiration > 0.5 m/s at emission point), glass-face protection for airbrushing booths, declaration of premises to the occupational physician (médecine du travail) within 30 days of registration; issuance of the compliance certificate (Certificat de conformité) by a certified bureau de contrôle (SOCOTEC or APAVE).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP); professional tax services are accessed through the official impots.gouv.fr portal. The competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) handles domestic businesses; the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) handles specified non-established businesses.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is generally registered through the formalities single window (Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises), after which a SIREN is assigned by INSEE and tax registration data are transmitted to the DGFiP. The company obtains or activates its professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr and requests a French VAT number from the competent SIE if it makes transactions requiring VAT registration. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the SIEE, providing evidence of legal existence, tax residence, activities and, where applicable, a French fiscal representative. A non-EU entity may be required to appoint a fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement provides an exemption. EU-established businesses generally do not require a French fiscal representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Eligible newly formed companies may elect temporary income-tax transparency (impôt sur le revenu, IR) for up to five fiscal years, subject to statutory ownership, activity, age, employee and turnover or balance-sheet conditions. A qualifying family-owned company conducting an eligible commercial, industrial, craft or agricultural activity may have a separate IR option, subject to statutory conditions. France does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion or other corrective treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a France-established business eligible for the domestic small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base), the principal thresholds are generally EUR 85,000 for sales of goods and specified accommodation activities and EUR 37,500 for services, with higher tolerance thresholds generally EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250, respectively. Thresholds and eligibility depend on activity and applicable current-year rules. A non-established taxable person generally cannot rely on these domestic thresholds for French taxable transactions; registration may be required from the first such transaction, subject to reverse-charge rules and applicable EU schemes. For intra-EU cross-border consumer distance sales, the separate EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualifying exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the EU may be exempt with credit (effectively zero-rated) under the French VAT export rules, provided the supplier can substantiate export and exit from the EU and satisfies the statutory conditions. Intra-EU supplies are governed by separate requirements, including customer VAT identification and proof of transport. Services are not zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore: place-of-supply rules determine whether French VAT applies, and specific exemptions or export-related rules may be relevant.

  • Export customs declaration and MRN, with electronic confirmation of exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and records identifying the goods, buyer, destination and transaction
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, courier record or other reliable transport evidence
  • Proof of payment or other accounting records supporting the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory prerequisite
  • Customer VAT identification and transport evidence for qualifying intra-EU supplies, where applicable

France is implementing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction/payment e-reporting through DGFiP-approved or registered partner platforms. Under the enacted timetable, all affected businesses must be able to receive e-invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance obligations begin on that date for large and intermediate-sized businesses and on 1 September 2027 for small and micro businesses. This is not a general real-time tax-clearance system: invoices are exchanged through the platform framework, with required data transmitted to the tax administration. Applicable format, platform and reporting requirements should be confirmed for the transaction and taxpayer category.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Unsupported or ineligible export exemption may result in assessment of unpaid VAT, late-payment interest and applicable tax surcharges. Deliberate breaches may attract a 40% surcharge; fraudulent conduct may attract an 80% surcharge, subject to statutory conditions. Certain false-invoice or fictitious-transaction cases may attract a 100% surcharge, and specific VAT documentation failures may trigger separate fixed or proportional penalties. Criminal sanctions may apply to tax fraud. Export and transport evidence should be retained for the applicable statutory limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full euro convertibility under Article 63 TFEU. Both current and capital account transactions are liberalized; no exchange permit, quota, or central-bank approval is required for euro/USD or euro/CNY settlement, including dividend and capital profit remittances. Remittance is constrained only by standard French withholding taxes of 30% for non-treaty beneficiaries or reduced rates under double tax agreements (e.g., China-France treaty) and by the parent-subsidiary regime where applicable. A non-resident investor may freely capitalize a newly formed SAS because designer-toy manufacturing (ISIC 3240) is not a strategic sector subject to prior authorization under Decree 2019-1590; however, a statistical FDI declaration to the French Treasury is mandatory for foreign control or thresholds exceeding 10% of capital or voting rights.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no compulsory repatriation period. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or foreign currency in France or abroad. Nevertheless, French-resident companies must declare any foreign bank account annually (French Tax Code Art. 1649 A, Form 3916), and all cash or wire transactions exceeding fixed thresholds, especially around EUR 10,000 or suspicious flows, trigger reporting to TRACFIN. The SAS may therefore hold USD or offshore CNY balances from Shopify/Stripe payouts, but banking counterparties will require a documented treasury policy for anti-money-laundering purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), exercising treasury statistics, currency convertibility monitoring, and prudential oversight through the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution). The Directeur Général du Trésor (French Treasury) administers foreign exchange and foreign-direct-investment declaration requirements under the Code Monétaire et Financier.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for complementary liquidity utilities; Low for primary treasury mandate. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Multitude/Pleo) may legally hold funds for the French SAS via an EU/EEA EMI passport (Directive 2009/110/EC), and they offer efficient multi-currency payout rails for Shopify/Stripe proceeds. However, they are not licensed French credit institutions and cannot serve as statutory capital escrow accounts or issue the 'Certificat du Dépositaire' required during RGS incorporation filings. Constraints include foreign IBAN rejection by certain French suppliers, stricter internal AML gating on high-volume flow-through, and no access to French-led CNH or commercial documentary credit facilities. Such EMIs should be limited to sub-ledger receivables collection and small disbursements while all compliance-critical treasury sits in a Tier-1 French bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident shareholders inject equity by an international SWIFT MT103 transfer to a dedicated 'Compte Bloqué' (capital escrow account) at the Tier-1 French bank; the wire instruction must contain the ISO purpose code for equity capital and a narrative referencing 'Apport au Capital Social de la SAS' and the company K-bis register number. The bank will require the SAS's incorporation deed, UBO declaration, a board or shareholder resolution, CFE FDI statistical filing acknowledgment (for foreign investments exceeding 10% of control), and source-of-funds documentation for the ensuring 8-figure remittance. Dividend or shareholder-loan conversion may be routed via purpose code loan/equity subcodes but must not be mistaken for share capital in the bank's ownership register. FX conversion of inbound USD/CNY to EUR may be executed at the French bank's spot desk after the escrow account is credited, with a formal trade invoice and internal conversion. No pre-authorization is needed for the toy industry, but the French Treasury's simplified notification must be completed within 30 days of the operation.Disbursements to French/EU artists, designers, and finishing studios are settled via SEPA Credit Transfer (or SEPA Instant for sub-100k amounts). For overseas contract manufacturers (typically Chinese rotocasting and injection factories), the primary rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 plain telegraphic transfer (T/T) in EUR, USD, or offshore CNY/CNH against commercial invoices; (2) Documentary Letter of Credit (UCP600, MT700) issued by the French bank and advised/confirmed through a correspondent (e.g., Bank of China, ICBC, HSBC) for high-value batch orders; (3) occasional cross-border card payouts for sample procurement and e-commerce marketplace fees via Stripe/Shopify. Each transfer is matched to a three-way dossier comprised of the supplier invoice, the air/sea waybill, and the French customs declaration (Déclaration d'Échange de Biens / Export Control System). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium for complementary liquidity utilities; Low for primary treasury mandate. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Multitude/Pleo) may legally hold funds for the French SAS via an EU/EEA EMI passport (Directive 2009/110/EC), and they offer efficient multi-currency payout rails for Shopify/Stripe proceeds. However, they are not licensed French credit institutions and cannot serve as statutory capital escrow accounts or issue the 'Certificat du Dépositaire' required during RGS incorporation filings. Constraints include foreign IBAN rejection by certain French suppliers, stricter internal AML gating on high-volume flow-through, and no access to French-led CNH or commercial documentary credit facilities. Such EMIs should be limited to sub-ledger receivables collection and small disbursements while all compliance-critical treasury sits in a Tier-1 French bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate (impôt sur les sociétés) for FY 2025; reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 38,120 of taxable profit for eligible small and medium-sized enterprises.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax for dividends distributed to non-resident corporate shareholders (article 119 bis CGI); 12.8% for non-resident individual shareholders (PFU rate); reduced to 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate parents holding at least 5% for 24 months; treaty rates typically 5-15% where applied.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File/Local File for a French entity with annual turnover or total balance sheet above EUR 400,000,000, or where the entity holds a majority interest in a foreign enterprise and its own turnover/total assets exceed EUR 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Report mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: France applies the OECD arm's-length standard and the abuse-of-law doctrine (abus de droit); artificial or fictitious arrangements may be recharacterised under LPF Article L.64. The Patent Box and CIR regimes require actual French economic substance, including continuous R&D activity, qualified staff, and management decisions in France; a design-and-manufacturing SAS performing rotocasting, injection moulding and airbrush finishing must demonstrate real production premises, inventory, and active supervision to withstand recharacterisation as a letterbox company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Omission (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (7 years and EUR 500,000 for organised fraud) under article 1741 CGI; additionally, confiscation of assets, 10-year exclusion from public procurement, publication of the judgment, and loss of certain tax advantages; administrative late-filing penalties up to 80% may apply concurrently.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally prepare or sign knowingly fraudulent declarations are criminally liable; civil courts may pierce the corporate veil and impose joint and several recovery of unpaid corporate tax, interest, and penalties against the director, including travel bans under the French tax collection procedure.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of profits under article 57 CGI; penalty of 40% on the additional tax if the mispricing was not performed in good faith, or 80% if the arrangement has no actual purpose; late-payment interest at 3.07% per annum; the French tax authority may issue a unilateral TP adjustment and obtain bank account seizure.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable in civil action for the amount of tax evaded if they knowingly engineered the transfer-pricing scheme; criminal prosecution for complicity in tax evasion carries imprisonment, fines, and professional disqualification; travel ban may be imposed to secure the collection.
Abusive Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: DGFiP is authorised to disregard the artificial transaction under LPF article L.64; the resulting tax saving is subject to an 80% penalty, plus a EUR 10,000 fine where non-genuine transactions are involved; reopening of previous fiscal years and rectification of all downstream profits.

Director personal liability: The managing director of the SAS may be jointly and severally liable for the penalties if the abuse has no purpose but to avoid tax; in serious cases the director's personal assets may be attached after a criminal conviction for tax evasion, and an exit/travel ban can be ordered during judicial investigation.
Disguised Profit Distribution (Distributions occultes)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the value transferred as a deemed distribution under article 111 CGI; withholding tax at 25% (or treaty-reduced rate) is reasserted on the grossed-up amount; civil penalties of 80% for deliberate misdeclaration under article 1728 CGI plus late-payment interest; the taxable basis is recalculated with addition of hidden profits.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or receive disguised distributions are personally liable for restitution, such as repayment of unlawful dividends; in the commercial field, abuse of corporate assets (abus de biens sociaux) under article 241-3 Code de Commerce exposes the director to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, plus civil liability to the company and creditors; travel ban and provisional arrest may also be ordered.
