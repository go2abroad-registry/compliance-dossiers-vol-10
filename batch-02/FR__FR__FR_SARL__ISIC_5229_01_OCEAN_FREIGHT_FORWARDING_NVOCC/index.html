Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de commerce (Commercial Court Registry) of the registered-office department, operating the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); all filings are transmitted electronically through the Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises operated by INPI (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

Code de commerce (French Commercial Code) – Book II: Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (general provisions), L.223-1 to L.223-43 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée – SARL), R.123-1 et seq. (Register of Commerce and Companies – RCS); Décret n°84-406 of 30 April 1984 (RCS filing rules); Code des transports – L.1412-2 for auxiliary freight-forwarding registration. The SARL is validly formed even with a nominal stated capital because French law sets no mandatory minimum share capital.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de constitution d'une société à responsabilité limitée (formulaire M0) filed via the INPI Guichet unique, accompanied by the signed Statuts (Articles of Association), a certificate of publication of the 'Avis de constitution' in an authorized Journal d'Annonces Légales (JAL), a capital-deposit attestation, and the Declaration of Beneficial Owner (DBE).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital under French law; the capital is freely set in the Statuts. If cash capital is stated, at least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at formation and the balance within 5 years (Art. L.223-7). The paid-up funds are blocked in escrow at a credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations (CDC) and are released to the gérant only upon presentation of the RCS Kbis extract.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: for tertiary (office) premises, initial and annual fire-safety verification by a qualified body (APAVE, SOCOTEC, Bureau Veritas) under the Règlement de sécurité and Code du travail R.4222-1 et seq.; maintain consignes incendie, emergency exits, fire extinguishers, and registre de sécurité. If any reception area is open to the public, the premises become an ERP and require a collective fire-safety commission visit. Electrical installation inspection and verification per Code du travail R.4226-1 series. Workplace clearance: structural-load inspection is not required for normal office use, but the employer must maintain a liaison register and conduct an annual air-quality and ventilation check. Warehouse operations, if added, will trigger the ERP chapter for the specific category, structural load certificates for racking, and compliance with civil-defense requirements in the local fire prevention plan.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital under French law; the capital is freely set in the Statuts. If cash capital is stated, at least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at formation and the balance within 5 years (Art. L.223-7). The paid-up funds are blocked in escrow at a credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations (CDC) and are released to the gérant only upon presentation of the RCS Kbis extract.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), through the professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr. Business formation and registration formalities are submitted through the Guichet unique operated by INPI; the resulting SIREN and SIRET identifiers are allocated through the national business-register and INSEE processes. VAT administration is handled by the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE), including the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères for qualifying non-established businesses.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered through the Guichet unique and receives a SIREN and establishment-level SIRET; its French VAT number is generally assigned by the DGFiP after the business and VAT position are recorded. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in France must register with the competent French tax office before or when its French VAT obligations arise. A business established in another EU Member State generally has no French fiscal-representative appointment requirement. A business established outside the EU must ordinarily appoint an accredited French fiscal representative, unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement or legal exception removes that requirement. Applications commonly require formation and registry documents, proof of business activity, identity and authority documents, and evidence of the French taxable activity. Registration obligations may arise without a turnover threshold where the non-established supplier is liable for French VAT; reverse-charge treatment can alter who accounts for VAT on particular transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying SARL may elect the temporary partnership-style income-tax regime (impôt sur le revenu, IR), generally for up to five financial years, subject to statutory ownership, activity, age, size, and consent conditions. A qualifying family-owned SARL conducting an eligible activity may elect IR treatment without that five-year limit while the conditions remain satisfied. A single-member SARL owned by an individual may, subject to conditions and election, be taxed under IR rules. France does not provide a US-style check-the-box election for ordinary French entities; classification and cross-border hybrid treatment are governed by French law, EU rules, and applicable tax treaties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For an ordinarily established French business eligible for the domestic VAT exemption regime (franchise en base), the general thresholds are EUR 85,000 for sales of goods and specified transactions and EUR 37,500 for services, subject to statutory tolerance thresholds and special rules. These domestic exemption thresholds do not generally protect a non-established supplier that is liable to account for French VAT. Cross-border EU distance sales and services are subject to separate rules, including the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold for specified intra-EU distance sales and telecommunications, broadcasting, and electronic services, and the Union/non-Union OSS regimes. A SARL carrying on freight-forwarding activities should determine VAT treatment by supply, customer status, route, and place-of-supply rules rather than relying on a single turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the EU may be exempt from French VAT with deduction rights where the statutory export conditions are met and the supplier can prove that the goods left the EU customs territory. Intra-Community supplies may qualify for exemption with deduction rights where the customer has a valid VAT number in another Member State, the goods are dispatched there, and reporting and documentary conditions are met. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer or payment is offshore: place of supply, customer status, and applicable exemptions determine treatment. International freight and transport-related services may benefit from specific exemptions or related treatment only where the statutory conditions and documentary evidence are satisfied. Freight forwarders acting as disclosed agents and those acting as principals may have different VAT outcomes; the contractual and invoicing capacity must be established.

  • Customs export declaration and export MRN with electronic confirmation of exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, sales or freight-forwarding contract, and records identifying the supplier, customer, goods, and destination
  • Bill of lading, sea waybill, or other transport document evidencing carriage and delivery outside the EU
  • Proof of dispatch, delivery, and export, including carrier or customs-system evidence appropriate to the transaction
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT-number validation, transport evidence, invoice, and required recapitulative statement reporting
  • Payment and accounting records supporting the transaction; an inward-remittance certificate is not a general French VAT export requirement

France is implementing domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction/payment-data reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, businesses within scope must be able to receive electronic invoices; issuance obligations apply from that date to large and intermediate-sized enterprises and generally from 1 September 2027 to small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises. In-scope invoices are transmitted through a government-approved platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire, PDP), with required data reported to the DGFiP. France's model is not a universal real-time tax-clearance or digital-stamping system. E-reporting applies to specified transactions outside domestic e-invoicing, including relevant cross-border and consumer transactions, subject to the statutory scope and timetable.

France imposes no general 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export exemption claims require contemporaneous evidence of export or other applicable qualifying conditions; inadequate evidence can result in assessment of output VAT, interest, and applicable tax penalties. Deliberate or fraudulent conduct may attract increased penalties and, where the statutory elements are met, criminal sanctions. Input VAT deductions or refunds may be denied or recovered where the underlying transactions are fictitious, mischaracterized, or unsupported. The applicable assessment period and penalties depend on the facts, the type of breach, and whether bad faith or fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is in the Eurozone and has fully liberalized both current and capital account transactions. No exchange control approval is needed for trade-related payments, profit remittance, or capital injections. Profit repatriation via dividends or shareholder loans is unlimited and unrestricted, subject only to mandatory tax withholding and anti-money laundering (AML) checks. Reporting duties apply for statistical/balance-of-payments purposes, not authorisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement exists in French law. Export proceeds and freight earnings in foreign currency may be held freely in local or offshore bank accounts. The only statutory obligation is a Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration for cross-border transfers exceeding €25,000, which is filed by the instructing bank on behalf of the company. No conversion ratio or mandatory timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) jointly with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) under the European Central Bank's monetary policy framework; foreign exchange control powers are primarily exercised by Banque de France in accordance with the Code Monétaire et Financier (Articles R. 916-1 et seq.)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Medium – French-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) (e.g., Qonto, N26, Revolut Business, or Eversend) are authorised under PSD2 by the ACPR and offer payment accounts, FX, and cross-border SWIFT-like transfers. They are operationally viable for daily payroll, supplier disbursements, and VAT clearances. However, they are generally unsuitable for a capital escrow account or for high ticket NVOCC transactions because of lower transaction caps, plus enhanced due diligence for the freight forwarding industry, and some EMIs explicitly exclude NVOCC brokers as high-risk. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot provide credit or guarantees, and must comply with the French and European AML/CTF rules on non-resident UBOs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the French SARL must be routed through a fully regulated bank (tier-1) using SWIFT MT103/MT202. Purpose codes must align with the Banque de France's nomenclature: for an equity injection use 'Capital contribution / equity' (Nomenclature code 4000 – Investment), and for a shareholder loan use 'Loan including lease-in' (code 4020 or 4030 depending on maturity). The bank automatically files the balance-of-payments declaration for transfers above €25,000. Supporting documents must include a board resolution from the shareholder, the French SARL's share subscription or loan agreement, the notarial deed (if capital increase), and the Kbis. Verification is also required against the SARL's UBO register and any prior declaration of non-resident shareholding.Clearing rails for outbound trade settlements are primarily via SWIFT MT103 for third-country suppliers (especially in USD, CNY, and JPY). Within the SEPA zone, a SEPA payment (E-Mandat) is used for EUR settlements with EU harbours, terminals, and logistics partners. For large charter payments or advance freight discounts requiring documentary protection, a commercial letter of credit (MT700/707) may be used, or a standby L/C as a guarantee for port fees. USD disbursements are typically cleared via Fedwire or CHIPS through the correspondent bank using the SWIFT MT103 network, with the bank's cost-and-benefit analysis supported by a daily FX conversion into the final payment currency.FX Outright Forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, EUR/GBP) – available under grouping/settlement netting agreements with licensed French credit institutions.FX Swaps (short-dated EUR/USD or EUR/CNY) – commonly used for managing intra-quarter working capital gaps.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) – permissible for converting Euro floating-rate loans into USD fixed-rate obligations if the SARL holds a long-term unhedged USD asset.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – permitted for over-the-counter transactions on restricted/inconvertible currency pairs (e.g., onshore CNY/offshore CNH, INR) only when executed by a French-licensed credit institution with dedicated balance-of-payments reporting and SBRC (Conformité aux réglementations).
