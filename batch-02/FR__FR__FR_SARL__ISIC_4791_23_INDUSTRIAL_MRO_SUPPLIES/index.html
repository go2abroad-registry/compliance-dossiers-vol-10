Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Paris; portal: impots.gouv.fr; entity registration via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) and INSEE.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is incorporated through the Guichet Unique, which transmits the dossier to the RCS and INSEE; after delivery of SIREN and SIRET numbers, the DGFiP automatically assigns the intra-community VAT identification number (format: FR + two-digit key + SIREN) under Article 286 ter of the French General Tax Code (CGI). A foreign entity not established in France must apply for a simplified VAT registration using Form 1546 (immatriculation simplifiée) filed with the DGFiP before its first taxable French supply, or register under the Non-Union OSS, Union OSS, or Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) regime for distance and B2C service supplies. A foreign entity establishing a permanent establishment in France must file Form P0(C4) with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises and obtain a full French VAT identification number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The SARL is subject by default to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) at the standard rate of 25%, unless the company validly elects for the income-tax regime under Article 239 bis AA CGI. — A French SARL is incorporated through the Guichet Unique, which transmits the dossier to the RCS and INSEE; after delivery of SIREN and SIRET numbers, the DGFiP automatically assigns the intra-community VAT identification number (format: FR + two-digit key + SIREN) under Article 286 ter of the French General Tax Code (CGI). A foreign entity establishing a permanent establishment in France must file Form P0(C4) with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises and obtain a full French VAT identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The SARL is subject by default to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) at the standard rate of 25%, unless the company validly elects for the income-tax regime under Article 239 bis AA CGI.

Available election forms

Election for partnership-style income taxation (Impôt sur le Revenu, IR) under Article 239 bis AA CGI for eligible closely-held or family SARLs meeting statutory conditions (employee headcount, turnover and balance-sheet ceilings, shareholder composition, and absence of commercial rental activity); option for the IR regime of a SARL de famille; election for fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) for a SARL at least 95% held by a French tax-integrated parent under Article 223 A CGI; hybrid mismatch alignments under the transposition of ATAD 1 and ATAD 2 (Articles 212 bis and 209-0 B CGI) deny deductions in respect of hybrid entity and hybrid transfer mismatches and treat the French SARL as an opaque entity under domestic law; the IR/IS election operates as the functional French equivalent of a check-the-box classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A domestic-established SARL is subject to compulsory VAT registration from the date of incorporation, with no minimum turnover threshold; however, an eligible small French-established business may elect for the franchise en base de TVA only where annual taxable turnover remains below €91,900 (principal supplies of goods and hospitality/food services) or €37,500 (provision of services), in which case it may not deduct input VAT. For foreign suppliers: a non-established supplier of B2C services must register in France from its first euro of taxable sales (Non-Union OSS); imported distance-sold goods are covered by IOSS where consignment value does not exceed €150; an EU-established supplier making intra-EU distance sales must register under Union OSS when annual cross-border distance sales exceed the €10,000 Union threshold. The SARL's B2B MRO catalogue supplies to French customers are taxable at the 20% standard rate from the first euro.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods outside the European Union are VAT-exempt with credit for input tax (zero-rating) under Article 262-I-1° CGI where the goods are dispatched or transported by the seller, or on the seller's behalf, to a destination outside the EU. Intra-community supplies of goods to a customer identified for VAT in another Member State are exempt under Article 262-0 CGI, subject to retention of the customer's valid EU VAT number and filing of the EC Sales List (état récapitulatif de TVA / DEB). Export-related transport and ancillary services are zero-rated under Article 262-I-2° CGI where they are directly linked to physically exported goods. The SARL's B2B MRO export sales to non-EU industrial clients qualify for zero-rating where the SARL is named as exporter on the customs declaration and the goods physically depart the EU; intra-EU dispatches to VAT-registered industrial resellers and end-users are zero-rated with DEB reporting. Substantiation must be complete within the statutory documentary window to preserve the 0% benefit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Document Administratif Unique / DAU or electronic DELTA-X and ECS export declaration) naming the SARL as exporter and evidencing exit from EU customs territory
  • Transport documentation proving physical export: Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), CMR road consignment note, or combined transport document (DCT)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, export sales contract and INCOTERM (EXW, FOB, CIF, etc.)
  • Intra-EU evidence: customer's valid EU VAT number, EC Sales List (DEB) filing confirmation, and transport proof such as CMR
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (relevé de domiciliation bancaire) or SWIFT MT103/MT950 wire confirmation linking the export customer's payment to the corresponding export invoice
  • For export-linked services: customs transport declaration and the underlying export contract demonstrating a direct link to the exported physical goods

France is deploying a mandatory B2B e-invoicing and B2C e-reporting framework under the 2024 Finance Law and CGI Article 289 bis. Mandatory issuance and receipt of structured electronic invoices applies to large enterprises and mid-sized enterprises (ETI) from 1 September 2026, and to all VAT-taxable persons, including SMEs and micro-enterprises, from 1 September 2027. E-invoices must be exchanged through a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the free Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); accepted structured formats are Factur-X (recommended hybrid UN/CEFACT and UBL semantics), UBL 2.1 and CII 16B. B2C transaction data and cross-border supply data are transmitted to the DGFiP through the e-reporting regime. The 2018 anti-fraud cash-register law separately requires certified, tamper-proof POS software (NF 525 or equivalent) for over-the-counter retail sales; France does not operate an Italian-style real-time fiscal clearance stamp, but the DGFiP conducts risk-based electronic audits and may access transaction-level e-reporting data.

Where the SARL cannot demonstrate the physical export or the customer's valid intra-EU VAT status, the DGFiP re-characterises the supply as taxable French VAT at 20% and applies late-payment interest (0.20% per month) plus penalties under Article 1728 CGI: 40% for deliberate under-declaration (manquement délibéré) or 80% for concealment of taxable operations (transaction dissimulée) under Article 1788 A CGI, rising to 100% for fraudulent manoeuvres. Retrospective clawback of output VAT, denial of input VAT recovery, and joint and several liability are asserted in carousel or missing-trader fraud chains; criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI may lead to five years' imprisonment and a fine of up to €500,000, aggravated under Article 1743 CGI for habituality or organised fraud. The French administration audits zero-ratings with enhanced risk-scoring, targeting fabricated export documentation and circular goods flows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in France; capital movements, current payments and profit remittances are protected by Articles 63 and 65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and by the French Monetary and Financial Code. No exchange authorization, usance requirement, or import licensing is applicable to ordinary B2B MRO supplies. Dividends and proceeds from a SARL may be remitted to foreign shareholders freely, but the French paying company must apply the relevant withholding tax (generally 25% under Article 119 bis CGI or treaty-reduced/zero rate), verify the economic substance of treaty claimants, and file the withholding return and beneficiary statement. Interest paid to non-resident shareholders is deductible only if Article 212 CGI thin-capitalisation, arm's length and market-rate conditions are met. The only practical restriction is documentary: banks and payment providers will request KYC, source-of-funds, beneficial-ownership and trade-backup evidence before releasing outbound dividends or loan repayments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion of export receivables into EUR, and no time limit for repatriating revenues earned by a French SARL. Export receipts may remain in foreign currency at the company's treasury risk. For Banque de France external-statistics purposes, the bank executing the settlement reports the transaction's country, sector and category from the payment field; exporters should therefore maintain invoices, customs declarations, proof of transport and bank statements for each shipment. No surrender certificate, Central Bank pricing confirmation, or prior license is required for standard MRO hardware export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) is the central bank and the competent monetary and balance-of-payments statistician; exchange-control supervision is carried out under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique (French Treasury), while prudential control of credit institutions and payment institutions is exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution), and financial intelligence/AML enforcement is coordinated through Tracfin.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

For a low-risk, non-licensed SARL in MRO e-commerce, a regulated EU fintech or Electronic Money Institution (EMI) is MEDIUM viable for operational and controlled disbursement accounts, but LOW viable as the exclusive treasury rail for capital escrow, substantial cross-border settlements and letters of credit. An EMI passporting into France under the E-Money Directive 2009/110/EC and PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) may open segregated client accounts, issue IBANs, process SEPA/Instant and SWIFT payments, and supply inexpensive online banking. The decisive legal constraints are: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and therefore does not offer protected bank deposits through the French Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution; (ii) not all EMIs support documentary credits, MT103 with full correspondent code or high-value USD clearing; (iii) the French registry/notarial form for the company's capital deposit normally requires a deposit with a bank (établissement de crédit) or a notary rather than an EMI; and (iv) Tracfin reporting and frozen-asset rules still apply, so an EMI cannot reduce the underlying KYC burden.

Defense evidence: Notarised articles of association, up-to-date Kbis, signed shareholder register, capital-subscription certificate, bank's blocked-account certificate (if used), business plan covering ISIC 4791/NAICS 423840 MRO catalogue sales, evidence of warehouse or e-commerce platform, and source-of-funds statements for the paid-up capital.
Non-resident corporate shareholder or UBO in a tax-haven or high-risk FATF jurisdiction

Root cause: Non-resident corporate shareholder or UBO in a tax-haven or high-risk FATF jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Apostilled certificate of incorporation/existence, certificate of good standing, group structure chart identifying all UBOs, audited financial statements or company confirmation, certified ID and proof of residential address for individuals, and a reasoned commercial-purpose paper explaining the French market-entry.

MRO trade description mismatches between invoices, customs declarations, and bank payment narratives

Defense evidence: Purchase orders, supplier proforma and commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading or airway bills, customs Import/Export declarations (DAU/ENS), Incoterms clause, and a product catalogue identifying the HS codes within ISIC 4791 and NAICS 423840; payment narratives must constantly state 'spare parts / industrial MRO hardware' rather than generic 'goods'.
Dividend or interest remittance to a foreign shareholder triggering treaty-reduced withholding

Root cause: Dividend or interest remittance to a foreign shareholder triggering treaty-reduced withholding

Defense evidence: DTA residence certificate, Form 5000/5051 (depending on treaty), full ownership and holding-period evidence under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where applicable, Article 119 bis analysis, board minutes approving the distribution, and a confirmation that the recipient is the beneficial owner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from a foreign shareholder must be routed by SWIFT MT103 in EUR (or USD converted by the bank) to the SARL's French IBAN. The sending bank should use a transparent trade/capital narrative in Field 70 within the 140-character limit, e.g. 'CAPITAL INCREASE / AUGMENTATION DE CAPITAL - SARL [company] - SIREN [number] - shareholder resolution [date]', and the receiving French bank will map the transaction to the Banque de France balance-of-payments category for direct investment/capital increase. No French Central Bank prior approval is required, but the bank may require a 'capital/equity injection compliance pack' comprising the notarized resolution, subscribed capital attestation, the shareholders' source-of-funds evidence, and a signed depository instruction for blocked release. For a loan instead of equity, the funds must be booked under a 'compte courant d'associé' agreement with an interest rate calculated on an arm's length basis, registered in the annual accounts and, if significant, disclosed to the bank for the balance-of-payments statistics.The recommended clearing hierarchy for MRO trade settlements is: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for Euro-denominated payments to EEA suppliers; (2) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border EUR, USD, GBP or exotic-currency wires, always with invoice and contract reference numbers in Fields 70/72; (3) a documentary Letter of Credit opened or transferred by SWIFT MT700/MT707 under UCP600 for high-value or Asian/overseas suppliers, with first benefiting bank being a Tier-1 French bank; (4) standby letters of credit (MT760) for large distribution contracts; and (5) corporate purchase cards/credit cards for low-ticket e-catalogue purchases, subject to supplier category restrictions. The French SARL should maintain an approved-settlement matrix: wire under EUR [threshold] on trade documents, LC above threshold, and card only for predefined vendors.
