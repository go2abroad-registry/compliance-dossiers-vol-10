Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), under the Ministère de l'Action et des Comptes Publics; online portal: impots.gouv.fr (professional taxpayer portal). Customs aspects are administered by Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), at douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS: prepare and file the incorporation package through the electronic Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). The system transmits data to INSEE for issuance of the SIREN/SIRET identifiers and to DGFiP for taxation and VAT accounts. A taxable French SAS automatically obtains an intra-Community VAT number. For a foreign entity not established in France: apply directly to DGFiP (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) before commencing taxable French transactions; submit constitutional documents, foreign registration certificate, appointment of a French fiscal representative where applicable, and obtain a French VAT identification number under Article 286 CGI. No statutory minimum capital is required for an SAS.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SAS: prepare and file the incorporation package through the electronic Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). The system transmits data to INSEE for issuance of the SIREN/SIRET identifiers and to DGFiP for taxation and VAT accounts. A taxable French SAS automatically obtains an intra-Community VAT number. For a foreign entity not established in France: apply directly to DGFiP (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) before commencing taxable French transactions; submit constitutional documents, foreign registration certificate, appointment of a French fiscal representative where applicable, and obtain a French VAT identification number under Article 286 CGI. No statutory minimum capital is required for an SAS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An SAS is a capital company automatically subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés) at entity level. No check-the-box election exists. Relevant statutory elections include: (i) option for an unusual 12-month fiscal year; (ii) election to be the parent of a tax-consolidated group (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI where the SAS holds at least 95% of voting rights in French subsidiaries; (iii) automatic application of the simplified real VAT/income regime below statutory turnover thresholds, with the option for the full real regime; (iv) no election is permitted to treat an SAS as tax transparent. Hybrid mismatches are adjusted under ATAD-derived rules against cross-border structuring.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic French taxpayers: basic franchise en base de TVA thresholds are EUR 91,900 annual taxable turnover for principal supplies of goods and EUR 36,800 for services (enlarged limits EUR 101,000 and EUR 39,100 respectively). For foreign or non-established taxable persons carrying out a first taxable transaction in France, registration is compulsory from the first transaction with no exemption threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating/exemption with right to deduction applies to supplies of tangible movable goods, including plush toys, dispatched or transported by the seller or on the seller's behalf to destinations outside the European Union, provided that (i) the goods physically leave French customs territory, (ii) the power to dispose of the goods is transferred to the foreign customer, and (iii) the exporter possesses the statutory documentary proof. Services directly connected with exported goods and ancillary export services are also exempt under Articles 262, 264 and 275 CGI. Input VAT recovery on export costs requires the same export evidence as the export exemption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en douane d'exportation, DAU/ECS with MRN number)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing departure from France
  • Commercial Invoice / Pro Forma Invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation de rapatriement de devises / bank credit advice showing FX receipt)
  • Proof of delivery or transfer outside the EU where customs warehousing applies

B2G e-invoicing is mandatory via Chorus Pro. Domestic B2B e-invoicing is phased under the e-invoicing reform from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized enterprises, and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro-enterprises; all qualifying invoices must be transmitted to the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified private platform via API/EDI. Mandated formats include Factur-X, UBL and CII. B2C transactions are subject to e-reporting of transaction data. There is no fiscal digital stamping or real-time tax clearance; VAT settlement is made through periodic electronic returns (Form 3515 / CA3).

French tax administration scrutinizes export zero-rating substantiation strictly. Where the exporter fails to produce the required export evidence, the supply is recharacterized as domestic taxable, output VAT is assessed, and input VAT previously recovered in respect of the exported goods is clawed back with interest. Penalties include late-payment interest (Article 1727 CGI) at 0.20% per month; a 40% insufficiency penalty for bad faith (Article 1729 CGI); an 80% penalty for deliberate fraud or fictitious invoicing (Article 1732 CGI); and criminal tax fraud sanctions (Article 1741 CGI) of a fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Fabricated or incomplete export documentation exposes the taxpayer to customs enforcement and loss of export facilitation status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the EU free-movement-of-capital principle, subject to public-policy reservations and national-security screening. Profit remittances and dividend transmissions from a French SAS require no foreign-exchange authorisation, but banks complete balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the Banque de France. Transfers to non-cooperative territories may be blocked, delayed, or subject to enhanced documentary checks; no material EUR/USD or EUR/CNY convertibility impediments exist for trade transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has abolished forced surrender and fixed conversion ratios for export receipts. Export proceeds must, however, be repatriated into France within commercially reasonable payment terms, must be booked in freely convertible currencies, and must be reported to the Banque de France as part of balance-of-payments statistics when entering the French banking system. Export invoices should name the importing entity exactly and carry trade reference numbers to avoid classification as uncollected export receivables.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, functioning as the monetary and foreign-exchange authority within France; banking and prudential supervision are implemented jointly with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), while cross-border capital movement is governed by European Union treaties and the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — EEA-authorised electronic money institutions and payment institutions passporting into France may provide IBANs, currency conversion, and B2B payment rails; however, they are not permitted to take deposits, issue escrow documentation for share capital, or provide documentary collection and import LCs. Fintech EMIs are viable for auxiliary checks and FX settlement but lack the legal standing for capital formation escrow and substantial trade-credit risk.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

UBO disclosure mismatch on the Register of Beneficial Owners (Registre des bénéficiaires effectifs)

Root cause: UBO disclosure mismatch on the Register of Beneficial Owners (Registre des bénéficiaires effectifs)

Defense evidence: Notarised certificate of incorporation, Kbis extract, signed UBO declaration filed with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce, corporate shareholders' register, and explanatory documentation resolving all ownership-chain discrepancies.
High-value or repetitive SWIFT flows to PRC manufacturing counterparties without a transparent commercial rationale

Root cause: High-value or repetitive SWIFT flows to PRC manufacturing counterparties without a transparent commercial rationale

Defense evidence: Executed purchase contracts, commercial invoices and packing lists, transport documents (bill of lading or airway bill), customs declarations for both EU import and non-EU export, supplier bank details, factory audit evidence, and goods description mapping to the registered ISIC 3240 scope.
Absence of a physical office and operational substance in France

Root cause: Absence of a physical office and operational substance in France

Defense evidence: Registered office agreement or professional domiciliation contract, use of a real licensed service provider or commercial lease, board-meeting and management-decision records, payroll and social-security registrations, French tax accounts, and a substance questionnaire showing treasury-management control in France.
Product-description mismatch or suspicion that trade involves dual-use controlled goods

Root cause: Product-description mismatch or suspicion that trade involves dual-use controlled goods

Defense evidence: Detailed product technical spec sheet confirming toy-manufacturing nature, EN71 / ASTM F963 certificates, photos of finished plush mascots, supplier declaration of non-source codes, license of ISIC 3240 with no statutory restriction, and a written confirmation that all goods bear compliant trademarks without military, surveillance, or restricted-export attributes.
Unusually rapid scaling of incoming capital injections during pre-formation and operating phase

Root cause: Unusually rapid scaling of incoming capital injections during pre-formation and operating phase

Defense evidence: Shareholders' subscription agreements, proof of underlying corporate assets in the SAS accounting plan, formal capital-allocation resolution, bank-to-bank reference letters from the remitting institution, and transaction narrative referencing capital subscription in compliance with Banque de France reporting thresholds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: shareholder remits funds via SWIFT or SEPA with reference 'FR_SAS – CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION' and the SAS's LEI or corporate identifier; the receiving bank opens a temporary capital deposit account for the sole purpose of blocking subscription proceeds until the notary or legal counsel issues the 'certificat du dépositaire des fonds'. Upon registration of the SAS and issuance of the Kbis, the blocked funds are released to the company's operational account. All direct inward remittances are subject to bank KYC review and, where applicable, Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical reporting; purpose codes should identify 'Equity Capital / Capital Subscription' rather than 'Trade Settlement' to prevent misclassification. The expected residency/currency conversion rule is maintained through explicit wire narration and supporting corporate documents in French or translated form.Outbound payments for plush-mascot import or intermediary manufacturing should be executed through SWIFT MT103 messages, with invoice, purchase order, and contract references inserted in field 70, after full economic-sanctions, OFAC, and EU-restrictive-measures screening. For large or secured procurements, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (commercial LC) advised or confirmed by the beneficiary's bank is the preferred clearing rail, backed by proforma invoices, inspection certificates, transport documents, and packed lists. French banks also accept documentary collection (D/P and D/A) and advance payment guarantees, subject to the corporate account's trade-line availability.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards on EUR/USD and EUR/CNY matched to confirmed trade receivables or payablesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted offshore currencies, including off-shore CNH variants and speculative-availability exoticsCross-currency swaps for aligning EUR corporate liabilities with USD or CNY supplier flowsPlain-vanilla FX options when approved under the SAS treasury policy and classed as cohesive operational hedges under EMIR, with mandatory clearing and reporting thresholds verified by the ACPR
