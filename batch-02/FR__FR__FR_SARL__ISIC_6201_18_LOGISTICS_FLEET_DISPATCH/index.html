Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital (L.223-2: capital freely fixed, may be €1). Cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% upon subscription (L.223-7); the balance must be released within 5 years. Until registration (Kbis), cash funds must remain blocked in an escrow account opened with a bank, a notaire, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and are released for company use only upon issuance of the Kbis extract.

Funding & unlock path: Office occupancy for IT/telematics staff requires no formal administrative clearance. Nevertheless, the employer must file a DPAE with medical follow-up, comply with Code du travail premises conditions (Articles L.4211-1 et seq.: ventilation, lighting, fire safety, emergency exits), and, if office floor area exceeds 200 m² or receives the public, obtain fire-safety visits by the commission de sécurité (ERP rules) and maintain a registre de sécurité. For any physical server/telecom room, a structural load certificate is needed if racks exceed floor loading capacity; periodic checks of electrical installations (Code du travail Art. R.4226-14) must be scheduled.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital (L.223-2: capital freely fixed, may be €1). Cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% upon subscription (L.223-7); the balance must be released within 5 years. Until registration (Kbis), cash funds must remain blocked in an escrow account opened with a bank, a notaire, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and are released for company use only upon issuance of the Kbis extract.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; VAT registration is handled by the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for companies established in France.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered with the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE) upon incorporation and automatically obtains a SIREN and SIRET number. VAT identification is subsequently allocated by the DGFiP when the entity declares taxable activity. Foreign entities with no establishment in France must apply for a French VAT number using the standard VAT registration request (Form 10963*) through a French individual fiscal representative appointed under Article 289 A CGI; the representative must be approved by the DGFiP and the application must be accompanied by the commercial register extract, proof of actual business premises or permanent establishment, and constitutional documents. Approval is granted after legal, financial, and tax probity checks, generally within 7–14 days.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A French SARL is registered with the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE) upon incorporation and automatically obtains a SIREN and SIRET number. VAT identification is subsequently allocated by the DGFiP when the entity declares taxable activity. Foreign entities with no establishment in France must apply for a French VAT number using the standard VAT registration request (Form 10963*) through a French individual fiscal representative appointed under Article 289 A CGI; the representative must be approved by the DGFiP and the application must be accompanied by the commercial register extract, proof of actual business premises or permanent establishment, and constitutional documents. Approval is granted after legal, financial, and tax probity checks, generally within 7–14 days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is taxed by default at entity level under corporate income tax (IS). A limited election is available under Article 239 CGI enabling certain family SARLs to be treated as pass-through vehicles for personal income tax (IR), subject to approval by all partners and to a five-year irrevocable commitment. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under Article 209 B CGI are mandatory and not elective; they implement ATAD 1 and ATAD 2, and no check-the-box or equivalent U.S.-style election exists under French domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no economic turnover threshold for registration of an established business: a French SARL performing any taxable activity must be registered before its first taxable supply. For very small businesses, the franchise en base exemption thresholds are EUR 91,900 per annum for sales of goods and EUR 36,800 per annum for services; exceeding them triggers mandatory VAT registration. For intra-community distance sales of goods, an annual cross-border threshold of EUR 10,000 applies; once surpassed, VAT registration is required in France unless the supplier elects for the OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A French VAT taxable person may apply the 0% export rate to: (i) tangible goods physically dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union, provided the goods are shipped out within one year from the time of supply and the transfer of ownership occurs to a purchaser established outside the EU; (ii) services connected to exported goods, such as logistical services directly linked to international transport; and (iii) digital services and SaaS supplied to a non-EU business customer, which are outside French VAT under the place-of-supply rules (Article 259-A of the French General Tax Code, TTC) because the recipient belongs outside the EU. For B2C digital services (fleet optimization subscriptions sold to foreign end users), French VAT is inapplicable when the effective use and enjoyment of the service occurs outside the EU. Zero-rating is conditional upon the supplier obtaining, before the end of the fifth month following the year of export, the full export and transport documentary evidence required by Article 275 CGI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation) bearing a customs office health and electronic movement reference
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR road consignment note showing actual exit from the EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency (e.g., SWIFT MT103, bank certificate)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract identifying the foreign purchaser and the agreed Incoterms ruling the transfer of ownership
  • Transport and delivery timestamped records aligned with the custom declaration to confirm physical shipment and destination

Mandatory B2B electronic invoicing and e-reporting pursuant to the 2026–2027 reform. From 1 September 2026 all taxable persons must be able to receive e-invoices, and large and mid-size enterprises must issue e-invoices through a certified PDI (Partenaires de Dématérialisation) or the French Public Data Exchange (PPF) and transmit e-reporting data to the DGFiP. From 1 September 2027 all small and micro enterprises must issue e-invoices in Factur-X, UBL, or CII formats and transmit transaction data. The PPF exposes secure REST APIs for structured invoice exchange and e-reporting; data is transmitted at least every ten days, not in real-time clearance. Authenticity and integrity are assured by digital stamping/electronic signature and compliance with the EDI or PEPPOL-like standards adopted by the PPF.

Unjustified zero-rating is retroactively corrected and the exporter is reassessed for domestic VAT at 20% on the full consideration, plus statutory late-payment interest. Under Article 1729 CGI, knowingly false export declarations incur a penalty of 80% of the understated tax, increased to 100% in cases of abusive manoeuvres or dissimulation. Fraudulent use of export exemption may also trigger criminal liability under Article 1741 CGI (fine up to EUR 750,000 and imprisonment of five years). The DGFiP may claw back output VAT for the preceding three years, extended to six years for concealed fraud, and may deny future refund entitlements in the absence of a compliant customs and foreign-exchange documentation chain.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a eurozone member and the euro is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions under EU law, in particular Article 63 TFEU. There is no general exchange-control regime, no prior approval requirement for domestic or cross-border financing, and no restriction on holding foreign-currency bank accounts. Capital and profit remittances from a French SARL are permitted subject to corporate law formalities and tax compliance; dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are generally subject to a 25% domestic withholding tax, reduced or exempted under applicable tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when the qualifying participation threshold is met. There is no FX convertibility barrier, but banks must complete ordinary sanction screening, FATCA/CRS reporting, and balance-of-payments statistical classification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory surrender percentage, and no forced local-currency conversion for export receipts. A French exporter may freely receive and retain foreign currency in any authorised bank account. Reporting obligations arise under the Banque de France balance-of-payments framework, with resident banks reporting transactions above prescribed thresholds; sanctions screening under EU regimes and Regulation (EU) 2015/847 payments traceability rules apply. Export proceeds are at the company's free disposal upon receipt, subject to the corporate treasury policy and bank-specific confirmation requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), acting with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the French Ministry of Economy and Finance and, for macroprudential oversight, the Haut Conseil de Stabilité Financière (HCSF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for ancillary payment disbursements, operational flows and supplier payouts; Low for statutory capital-account activity and long-term treasury custody. Legal Constraints: under the French Monetary and Financial Code and Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2), an electronic money institution (EMI) holding a European Economic Area passport may provide payment services in France through an ACPR-registered branch or notification, but an EMI is not a licensed credit institution. It cannot grant credit, may not offer certain deposit-taking treasury products, and is not automatically integrated into the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting chain in the same manner. Consequently, the SARL should retain at least one tier-1 domestic credit institution as its principal banking relationship and use a reputable cross-border EMI solely as an auxiliary clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require the non-French parent to send a SWIFT MT103 through a licensed credit institution to the SARL's French tier-1 bank, or, if both entities are in the EEA, a SEPA Credit Transfer. The remittance must carry a clear purpose code: for equity capital, the Banque de France balance-of-payments category for capital participation or capital contribution must be indicated; for intercompany financing, the category for intercompany loans or financial operations must be used. The SARL must produce shareholder and/or board minutes authorising the capital injection, statute updatings or planned notarial formalities, proof of RBE declaration, source-of-funds evidence, identity documents of the remitter, and a compliant tax memorandum if the injection qualifies for treaty or EU directive protection. Any non-EUR capital injection is converted by the receiving bank at a posted commercial FX rate; the bank must retain the transfer reference and verify that the transaction does not breach sanctions restrictions or the Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 cash-regulation thresholds.For the logistics fleet-dispatch model, outbound settlements are cleared through the primary French bank relationship. Direct supplier payments should be executed via SWIFT MT103 (outside SEPA) or SEPA Credit Transfer (within the EEA), carrying invoice numbers and a clear goods or services descriptor. For imported fleet hardware, use a commercial documentary letter of credit issued under ICC UCP600, preferably with a confirmed obligation from a tier-1 French bank; documentation must include commercial invoice, bill of lading, insurance certificate, packing list and certificates of origin.
