Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory legal minimum for a SARL under Code de commerce Article L.223-2 (the articles may fix the issued capital freely). However, for the payment-institution authorization under CMF L.522-1 et seq., the applicant must maintain a regulatory initial capital per CMF R.522-4, ranging from EUR 20,000 to EUR 125,000 depending on the exact catalogue of payment services declared (acquiring/clearing gateway at the applicable tranche). The amount must be fully paid in cash into a blocked escrow account opened with a French credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts, and must remain blocked (attestation de dépôt des fonds) until the company is registered; release is only permitted after production of the K-bis or earlier if the incorporation fails.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and civil defense certification: commission de sécurité (permis de feu) and ERP classification if any client-receiving area; structural load clearing for the data/server room floors (beyond ELS standards); occupational clearance by the Inspection du Travail of the workplace, mandatory medical surveillance and safety training of staff, and compliance with the Code du travail workplace health-and-safety checklist (DUERP — Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels), electrical installation conformity certificate, and annual training for emergency evacuation.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory legal minimum for a SARL under Code de commerce Article L.223-2 (the articles may fix the issued capital freely). However, for the payment-institution authorization under CMF L.522-1 et seq., the applicant must maintain a regulatory initial capital per CMF R.522-4, ranging from EUR 20,000 to EUR 125,000 depending on the exact catalogue of payment services declared (acquiring/clearing gateway at the applicable tranche). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — sole competent authority for corporate income tax and French VAT (TVA) assessment, collection and audit; Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) — competent for customs export controls and foreign-exchange repatriation enforcement. Portals: www.impots.gouv.fr (filing/payments), www.formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (incorporation and tax-ID issuance), and www.douane.gouv.fr (export declarations).

E-government filing portal

For a locally incorporated SARL, tax registration is automatic: (1) file incorporation documents via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; INSEE immediately assigns the 9-digit SIREN, which serves as the universal taxpayer ID (numéro de gestion) for IS, VAT, and employer taxes; (2) the SIRET establishment code is added per registered office; (3) the French intra-Community VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 2-digit key + SIREN) is allocated automatically by DGFiP upon incorporation and appears in the RCS extract within approximately 7–10 days; no separate VAT registration application is required for a French entity; (4) foreign entities without a French establishment must file Form 12184-SD (demande d'immatriculation au fichier national des assujettis) with the competent SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises), while EU distance sellers may declare French B2C VAT via the OSS portal instead of direct registration; (5) a foreign PSP creating a French branch or PE must complete the same SIREN/SIRET procedure and obtain a French VAT number before commencing taxable activities; (6) the PSP must concurrently register with the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) as a payment institution, referencing the SIREN in its licensing file.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SARL is compulsorily subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at 25% headline corporate rate (effective 25.825% including the 3.3% social surcharge on IS exceeding €763,000). Territorial scope: resident SARL is taxable on worldwide profits, with foreign PE profits relieved by tax treaties under Art. 209 CGI; conversely, a non-resident PSP with a French PE is taxable only on France-source profits. Pass-through is not the default: the SARL's profits are taxed at entity level, then distributions are taxed again in shareholders' hands (30% flat tax or progressive scale, with treaty relief). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SARL is compulsorily subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at 25% headline corporate rate (effective 25.825% including the 3.3% social surcharge on IS exceeding €763,000). Territorial scope: resident SARL is taxable on worldwide profits, with foreign PE profits relieved by tax treaties under Art. 209 CGI; conversely, a non-resident PSP with a French PE is taxable only on France-source profits. Pass-through is not the default: the SARL's profits are taxed at entity level, then distributions are taxed again in shareholders' hands (30% flat tax or progressive scale, with treaty relief). Tax-exempt zone: not applicable to a mainland-France payment gateway; no special 'offshore' regime exists for PSPs.

Available election forms

France has no US-style check-the-box. Statutory elections available to a SARL: (1) Article 239 bis AA CGI — newly created small SARLs (≤50 employees, turnover <€10M, created within the last 5 years, natural persons holding ≥50% of capital) may elect for a maximum 5 years to be taxed as quasi-transparent (income allocated to shareholders); this election is generally unavailable/applicable to a foreign corporate-owned PSP and is commercially inadvisable because it forfeits double-tax-treaty benefits and leaves undistributed profits taxed in shareholders' hands; (2) Article 239 bis AB CGI — family SARL (SARL de famille) election to remain under the income-tax regime; (3) VAT grouping election under Article 256 C III bis CGI — allows the SARL and its French subsidiaries to form a single taxable person (assujetti unique) filing one VAT return; (4) VAT filing-period elections under Art. 287 CGI (monthly or quarterly); (5) IS instalment and consolidated (intégration fiscale) election under Art. 223 A CGI for 95%+ French subsidiaries; (6) hybrid mismatch adjustments under Art. 212 bis CGI are mechanical, not elective; no equivalent of a hybrid entity check-the-box exists.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a member of the Eurozone, maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63-66 TFEU). Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-terrorist financing (CTF) verification. No exchange control approval is required for cross-border transfers, but reporting obligations apply for statistical purposes (Banque de France balance of payments reporting).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds is imposed. Exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts. However, all cross-border transactions exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Banque de France for balance of payments statistics. There is no statutory conversion ratio; companies may freely convert or hold foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking and payment supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. France has a robust EMI (Electronic Money Institution) regime under ACPR supervision. EMIs like Qonto, Shine, or Treezor can provide multi-currency accounts and payment services. However, for a high-risk PSP (ISIC 6619), EMIs may impose stricter due diligence and may not offer full treasury services (e.g., FX hedging). Legal constraints: EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot provide lending or certain treasury products. For cross-border clearing, they rely on partner banks, adding counterparty risk. A full banking license or partnership with a tier-1 bank is often required for high-volume settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'CAPITAL' (or 'DIV' for dividends) and reference to the SARL's registration number (SIREN). For EU transfers, use SEPA with purpose code 'CAPITAL' (e.g., 'CAPT' under ISO 20022). Verification: Provide the bank with the corporate resolution authorizing the injection, proof of UBO identity, and source of funds documentation. For amounts exceeding €10,000, the bank will conduct enhanced due diligence and may require a copy of the capital increase deed (acte de souscription).Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wire transfers to suppliers; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for high-value transactions, issued via SWIFT MT700; (3) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EU-based payments; (4) For PSP operations, use card network clearing (Visa/Mastercard) via acquiring banks, and Fedwire for USD settlements (if applicable). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Statutory full Master File / Local File regime is mandatory at EUR 400,000,000 consolidated group annual turnover or balance-sheet total (Art. L13AA LPF). Below that threshold, DGFiP may require simplified transfer-pricing documentation where controlled cross-border transactions are commercially material; Country-by-Country Reporting threshold is EUR 750,000,000 consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation of the type found in common-law financial centres, but applies substantive anti-abuse doctrines: place of effective management under Art. 209-1 CGI, permanent establishment principles under OECD tax treaty rules, and Art. L64 LPF 'abus de droit'. A PSP SARL must demonstrate local management, physical premises, personnel, adequate capital and a functioning AML/compliance function; absence of such substance allows DGFiP to re-characterize profits and treat the SARL as a conduit whose earnings are taxable in the hands of the real beneficiary.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Corporate Income Tax Fraud (Art. 1741 CGI)

Statutory sanction: Administrative re-determination of taxable profit, 80% penalty for bad faith, monthly late-payment interest, criminal fine for legal persons up to EUR 2,500,000 and imprisonment up to five years for the responsible director.

Director personal liability: The gérant/director is subject to criminal prosecution, personal civil liability for assessed tax and penalties, disqualification from corporate management for up to five years, travel bans and enforcement measures against personal assets where corporate assets have been diverted.
Artificial Shell Company / Abuse of Law (Art. L64 LPF)

Statutory sanction: DGFiP may invalidate the abusive arrangement, restore the French taxable base, apply the 80% abuse-of-law penalty, and where criminal intent is established make the arrangements subject to Art. 1741 sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for restitution of the tax deficiency, face penal fines and imprisonment, may be stripped of management rights for five years, and non-resident directors can be subjected to immigration/travel prohibitions.
Unlicensed Payment Services / AML-CFT Failure

Statutory sanction: ACPR enforcement measures: formal warning, reprimand, licence withdrawal, director suspension and administrative fines up to EUR 4,000,000 or up to a percentage of annual turnover. Criminal money-laundering penalties reach five years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 for individuals; legal persons face fines up to the statutory quintuple.

Director personal liability: The gérant, compliance officer and de facto directors risk personal criminal liability, custodial sentences, disqualification from financial-sector management, travel bans and court-ordered confiscation or restriction of assets.
Unsubstantiated Transfer Pricing / Hidden Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: DGFiP may make an upward adjustment to French taxable profit, levy the 40% serious-failure or 80% bad-faith penalty, impose late-payment interest, and enjoin the taxpayer to produce the full transfer-pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the adjusted tax when they participated in the manipulation, and face civil restitution, criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax returns, bans from management and travel restrictions.
