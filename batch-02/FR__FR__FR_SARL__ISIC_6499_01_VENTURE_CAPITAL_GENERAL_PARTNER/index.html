Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — portal: impots.gouv.fr; entity formation filings through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr)

E-government filing portal

A French SARL receives its statutory TIN automatically at incorporation: the Guichet Unique transmits the registration dossier to the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce and to INSEE, which issues the 9-digit SIREN number and the SIRET establishment number. The DGFiP then assigns the intra-community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) for the SARL's main establishment. A foreign entity without a French establishment that needs a French VAT ID must file Form 15769 (or use the EU non-established VAT registration portal) with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), appointing a fiscal representative if required under Article 302 quater of the CGI. The SARL's tax ID is the SIREN/SIRET; the VAT ID is referenced on the French registration certificate (extrait Kbis) and in impots.gouv.fr login.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not use the US check-the-box system. A SARL defaults to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés or IS) on its French profit. It may irrevocably elect to be taxed as a partnership/pass-through (option pour l'impôt sur le revenu, Article 239 bis AA CGI) only if it meets the small-enterprise definition (fewer than 50 employees and annual turnover below €10 million for enterprises with commercial/industrial/artisanal activity, or €5 million for service activity); this IR option is limited to five years for existing SARLs. Alternatively, a family SARL (SARL de famille, Article 239 bis CGI) may opt for income tax with fiscal transparency among family members. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognised for French-tax-resident SARLs; anti-hybrid rules under ATAD/DAC6 disallow deduction/non-inclusion outcomes using hybrid instruments or entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established SARL, VAT registration is mandatory when taxable turnover exceeds €85,000 for supplies of goods, €37,500 for supplies of services (including management fees from a venture capital fund), or €47,600 for certain legal and accounting services; a non-established foreign entity has no de minimis threshold and must register before the first taxable supply in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union are eligible for 0% VAT (exonération à l'export) under Article 262-I of the French General Tax Code (CGI), provided the exporter is the established supplier, the goods physically leave the EU within the legally required delay (normally within 90 days from dispatch), and the exporter holds a valid customs export declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation or DEX) and transport documents. Services directly linked to exported goods (e.g., international carriage, loading, handling) and services supplied to non-EU recipients (delivered outside the EU) may also qualify for zero-rating or territorial exclusion under Articles 258-B and 259-A CGI. A venture capital GP's management fees for advising a non-EU fund are generally taxable in France unless the effective use and enjoyment is outside the EU and the service is not linked to French property; offshore services can be zero-rated only if all territoriality conditions in Article 259 CGI are strictly met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane DX/DEX or AAS status)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (justifying receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer)

France is implementing mandatory e-invoicing (e-invoicing) and e-reporting under the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF). The rollout is staged: large taxpayers from 1 September 2026, mid-sized enterprises from 1 September 2027, and small entreprises from 1 September 2028. B2B transactions between French VAT-registered taxpayers must be transmitted via the PPF or an accredited PPF platform use-compatible API or Factur-X format. Customs export declarations, ESC (Déclaration d'Échange de Biens) filings, and VAT return reporting remain required; real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping are not yet in force for standard goods exports, but the PPF platform demands machine-readable invoicing data for all domestic e-invoiced B2B transactions.

Fraudulent or abusive zero-rating claims expose the SARL to retrospective VAT assessment with interest (intérêts de retard, Article 1727 CGI), a monetary penalty of 80% of the tax evaded for intentional concealment (Article 1729-a or 1729-b CGI), and criminal penalties for tax fraud (Article 1741 CGI) of up to €500,000 fine and up to five years' imprisonment in aggravated circumstances. The tax administration may also claw back refunded VAT credits and require a guarantee from the GP, and can apply a 100% penalty for trafficking in customs documents or fictitious invoices.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has no general exchange controls on lawful current-account or capital-account transactions. The euro is freely convertible and cross-border dividend, interest, service-fee, loan, capital-contribution, and redemption payments are generally permitted through regulated payment institutions or banks. Remittances remain subject to corporate-law distributions, distributable-profit and statutory-reserve rules, transfer-pricing requirements, withholding-tax analysis, beneficial-owner screening, sanctions controls, anti-money-laundering procedures, and documentary evidence of the underlying transaction. A SARL acting as a venture-capital general-partner or fund-management entity may require authorization or registration under the Alternative Investment Fund Managers Directive framework, French Monetary and Financial Code rules, and AMF supervision, depending on whether it manages alternative investment funds, provides portfolio management, or performs regulated investment services. Foreign direct investment screening may apply to acquisitions or investments in sensitive French sectors; it is not a general restriction on ordinary capital funding of a French SARL. Payments involving sanctioned persons, high-risk jurisdictions, opaque ownership structures, or unexplained third-party funding may be delayed, rejected, or reported.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed deadline for converting export receipts into euros. Export receipts may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the holder's discretion, subject to bank controls, accounting requirements, tax reporting, sanctions screening, and any contractual financing covenants. Goods and services exports must be supported by commercially credible invoices, contracts, customs records where applicable, and evidence of performance. Cross-border cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more into or out of the European Union require customs declaration by the person carrying the cash or equivalent instruments. Banks may request transaction-purpose information and may apply enhanced monitoring to fund-management fees, capital calls, distributions, and payments involving non-EU counterparties.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank, with foreign-exchange and capital-movement rules principally derived from European Union law, French monetary and financial law, the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, the Autorité des marchés financiers, and the French Treasury Directorate General.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary treasury and payment operations; Low as the sole relationship bank for a regulated or potentially regulated venture-capital GP. An electronic-money institution or payment institution authorized in France or another European Economic Area state may provide euro payment accounts, safeguarding, SEPA transfers, and selected foreign-currency services under passporting or local authorization. It may not provide ordinary bank deposits, insured deposit-taking, unrestricted credit, or all correspondent-banking services. Offshore digital EMIs outside the European Economic Area generally cannot provide regulated payment services to French customers without an applicable French or EEA authorization, passport, or local agent arrangement. A fintech may reject investment-fund activity, pooled client money, capital-call proceeds, third-party collections, or payments involving high-risk jurisdictions. The company should maintain a regulated French or EEA bank as primary account provider and use an EMI only after confirming safeguarding, segregation, settlement, chargeback, sanctions, account-closure, and access-to-funds terms.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder capital, shareholder loans, and fund-related corporate funding through an account held in the SARL's legal name at a regulated French or EEA credit institution or payment institution. Use the legal entity's exact registered name, IBAN, BIC, registered address, and a consistent payment reference such as 'share capital contribution', 'shareholder loan under agreement dated [date]', or 'management-fee funding'. France does not operate a universal statutory purpose-code system equivalent to certain exchange-control jurisdictions; the bank may nevertheless require a payment purpose, underlying agreement, source-of-funds evidence, UBO information, and regulatory-status documentation. Capital contributions should be supported by the subscription decision, articles or amendment, deposit certificate requirements, and accounting entries. Shareholder loans should be documented with principal, maturity, interest, repayment terms, transfer-pricing support, and any French related-party or thin-capitalization analysis. Do not route investor money through the GP's unrestricted operating account where segregation, fund documentation, regulatory rules, or contractual obligations require a dedicated fund or client-money account. Reconcile all inbound wires to the general ledger and retain SWIFT messages, bank advices, agreements, and compliance evidence.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT cross-border transfers, commonly evidenced by an MT103 or equivalent ISO 20022 payment record, for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions. Use a commercial letter of credit, documentary collection, open-account payment with trade-credit controls, or bank guarantee where the transaction involves goods, technology, customs exposure, or material counterparty risk. For investment-management operations, use contractual management-fee, administration, legal, accounting, software, and professional-service payments supported by invoices and executed agreements rather than trade documentation. Each payment file should contain the beneficiary's legal name, address, bank details, invoice or contract, sanctions-screening result, tax treatment, and approval record. Banks may request customs declarations, bills of lading, certificates of origin, proof of delivery, service evidence, or fund documents. Avoid unexplained third-party payments, split payments designed to evade controls, cash settlement, and payments to personal accounts. A fintech may reject investment-fund activity, pooled client money, capital-call proceeds, third-party collections, or payments involving high-risk jurisdictions. The company should maintain a regulated French or EEA bank as primary account provider and use an EMI only after confirming safeguarding, segregation, settlement, chargeback, sanctions, account-closure, and access-to-funds terms.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard rate; reduced 15% on first €38,120 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs; effective rate rises to 25.825% for groups with annual turnover above €7,630,000 (3.3% social contribution).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends distributed to non-residents under Article 119 bis CGI; 75% where the recipient's State is listed as non-cooperative (ETNC); treaty rates generally 0-15% (e.g. 15% under US-France for portfolio; 5% for qualified participations; 0% for certain EU parent-subsidiary situations).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 of French or consolidated group turnover triggers the formal Local File / Master File documentation obligation under Article L.13 AA LPF; CbC Report threshold EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue. A venture-capital GP below these thresholds must nevertheless be able to evidence benchmarking of management fees, carried interest and shareholder loans on request, and must include arm's-length pricing analysis in internal accounting records.

Economic substance scope: No separate ESR code exists in France; anti-abuse law and ATAD principles apply. For a VC GP, substance is evidenced by French registered office, a French-resident gérant, discretionary independence of the management committee, French payroll and bank accounts, minutes of meetings and traceable decision-making files. Artificial, nominal or mailbox-company arrangements will be challenged as abuse of rights (abus de droit) and may lose treaty/Directive benefits for lack of beneficial ownership.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude fiscale

Statutory sanction: Increase of tax to 80% plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month; criminal fine up to €75,000 for an individual and up to €375,000 for a legal entity acting through the manager, with imprisonment of up to 5 years; publication of the conviction and temporary suspension from public contracts.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil is allowed under Article L.267 LPF: a gérant who has committed serious, repeated misconduct or organized insolvency may be made personally liable for all unpaid corporate taxes and penalties, and may face travel bans, bans on managing any company (interdiction de gérer) and civil restitution.
Failure to File / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of 10% if the delay is under 30 days; 40% if in excess of 30 days; 80% for hidden activity; automatic fixed fine of €150-€1,500; monthly interest at 0.20% on the underpaid tax and additional assessment penalties up to 80% for bad faith.

Director personal liability: The court may order the manager to pay the company's tax debts where recovery through the company proves impossible; for false auditing of the GP, the gérant may also be criminally prosecuted for fiscal fraud and for presentation of inaccurate accounts.
Transfer Pricing and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of cross-border income under Article 57 CGI; adjustment plus 40% penalty for deliberate failure to disclose, or 80% for abuse of law; in cases of incomplete deductible documentation, the administration may deny deduction for the unsubstantiated amount and the same penalties apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign non-arm's-length invoices or false registrations expose themselves to separate criminal sanctions, personal civil liability for resulting tax shortfall, and observation of the company's assets to attach management-company receivables.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law (ATAD and Beneficial Ownership)

Statutory sanction: The administration will apply the abuse of rights procedure (Art. L.64 LPF), disregard the French entity, reallocate all income to the true beneficiary, impose the 80% penalty and deny treaty relief for lack of beneficial ownership; the entity may also be compulsorily dissolved as a fictitious company under commercial law.

Director personal liability: Accomplice directors are exposed to personal liability for the company's taxes and to criminal penalties for fictitious incorporation; individual response includes asset seizure, travel bans, and a prohibition from holding a management office for up to 15 years.
AML/Capital-Call Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: French Monetary and Financial Code imposes criminal penalties of up to 5 years' imprisonment and €375,000 fine for laundering of capital derived from tax evasion, aggravated money laundering increases to 10 years and €750,000; in addition, corporate penalties and confiscation of the funds and profit-generating assets.

Director personal liability: The gérant is subject to personal criminal proceedings, extended seizure of assets, travel bans, and professional disqualification; the supervisory authority may revoke any statutory AIFM license of the GP and the manager's own 'agrément' or declaration.
