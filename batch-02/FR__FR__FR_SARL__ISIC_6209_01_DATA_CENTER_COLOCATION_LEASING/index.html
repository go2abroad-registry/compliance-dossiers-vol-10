Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); online portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate incorporations filed via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, operated by INPI); customs and EORI administration: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (douane.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned French SARL: (1) file formation through the Guichet Unique, receiving the SIREN (national company ID) and SIRET (establishment ID); (2) the competent SIE opens the corporate tax file and issues the French VAT intra-Community number (FR + 2-digit key + SIREN) and a personal tax identification reference (n° fiscal); (3) obtain the EORI number (FR + SIREN) from Customs before moving goods; (4) where a foreign entity is not established in France, form CA 3557 must be filed with the DGFiP Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) — suppliers established outside the EU must appoint DGFiP-approved fiscal representation under CGI Art. 293 G, and no de minimis threshold applies to services connected with French immovable property.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation default for SARL: profits are taxable to the company under French corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at 25% standard rate (15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of annual profit for SMEs meeting the turnover and ownership conditions). France applies its territoriality principle (Territorial_Scope): the IS generally covers French-source profits only, with foreign-branch profits exempt (subject to ATAD CFC rules). No general tax-exempt zone applies; the SARL is not pass-through by default. — For a non-resident-owned French SARL: (1) file formation through the Guichet Unique, receiving the SIREN (national company ID) and SIRET (establishment ID); (2) the competent SIE opens the corporate tax file and issues the French VAT intra-Community number (FR + 2-digit key + SIREN) and a personal tax identification reference (n° fiscal); (3) obtain the EORI number (FR + SIREN) from Customs before moving goods; (4) where a foreign entity is not established in France, form CA 3557 must be filed with the DGFiP Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) — suppliers established outside the EU must appoint DGFiP-approved fiscal representation under CGI Art. 293 G, and no de minimis threshold applies to services connected with French immovable property.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation default for SARL: profits are taxable to the company under French corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at 25% standard rate (15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of annual profit for SMEs meeting the turnover and ownership conditions). France applies its territoriality principle (Territorial_Scope): the IS generally covers French-source profits only, with foreign-branch profits exempt (subject to ATAD CFC rules). No general tax-exempt zone applies; the SARL is not pass-through by default.

Available election forms

France has no US-style check-the-box election. A one-person or family SARL satisfying CGI Art. 239 bis A / 239 bis AA conditions may elect income-tax transparency (régime des sociétés de personnes), making profits taxable in shareholders' hands; the election is filed with the SIE and locks in for at least five years. For all other entities, entity-level IS is mandatory. EU ATAD hybrid-mismatch provisions are implemented in the French General Tax Code and neutralize mismatches (deduction/non-inclusion or double deduction) involving French residents, overriding elective outcomes; no direct election to create hybridity is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€25,000 (annual French taxable turnover excluding VAT for service businesses, including data center and colocation services — franchise en base, CGI Art. 293 B); €85,000 threshold for supplies of goods. Registration becomes mandatory in the calendar year the threshold is crossed. Non-established foreign suppliers have no threshold and must register from the first euro for services connected with French immovable property or supplies to French non-taxable persons; a taxable person below the threshold may voluntarily register (option for taxation, Art. 293 C CGI).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available where goods are physically exported outside EU customs territory and evidenced as such (exportation exonérée, CGI Art. 262-1-I). For the data center colocation model, dedicated rack/cage leasing constitutes a supply of services connected with immovable property (EU VAT Directive Art. 47, place of supply = France): the SARL must charge French VAT at 20% even to foreign B2B customers; no export zero-rating applies. Pure bare-metal and remote server-management services that confer no right to occupy identified physical space follow the general B2B place-of-supply rule (Art. 44) and are outside French VAT where the customer is a taxable person established abroad, provided the SARL holds documentary evidence of the customer's VAT status, contract, and settlement — such services are effectively exported services but are excluded from French VAT rather than zero-rated, and no French input VAT credit arises on the corresponding revenues except through the ordinary deduction mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/Déclaration administrative unique, lodged electronically through the DELTA system) evidencing the 'EX' export procedure, exporter identity and physical exit of the goods
  • Bill of Lading (Connaissement maritime) or Air Waybill (Lettre de transport aérien) proving dispatch outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Attestation de rapatriement des devises / bank statement evidencing settlement of the export receivable within the statutory currency-control deadline)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (Facture commerciale et liste de colisage)
  • Intrastat dispatch declaration for intra-EU movements and, where applicable, dual-use or strategic export licences for server/network equipment crossing EU borders

Mandatory structured B2B electronic invoicing (facturation électronique) applies under a phased French mandate: large enterprises and mid-size enterprises (ETI) from 1 September 2026, SMEs/TPEs from 1 September 2028 (CGI Art. 289 bis). Invoices must comply with the EN 16931 semantic standard (Factur-X or UBL) and be transmitted via the Public Facturation Portal (PPF) or certified dematerialization partner platforms (PDPs), which forward e-invoice and transaction data to DGFiP in near-real time (e-reporting). DGFiP does not pre-clear or stamp invoices; non-transmission or non-conformity triggers procedural fines (€15 per invoice capped by statute) and places input VAT deduction at risk for the recipient.

Export exemption and reverse-charge positions are priority audit targets of DGFiP and French customs. Failure to substantiate an export triggers re-assessment of 20% French VAT, late-payment interest at 0.20% per month, and penalties: 40% for inaccuracies without bad faith, 80% for bad faith, fraudulent maneuvers, or abuse of right (CGI Arts. 1729 and 1729-0 A). Clawback extends to wrongly deducted input VAT credits, and the right of recovery runs three years, extended to six years for concealed activities and up to ten years for fraudulent schemes (LPF Arts. L. 169, L. 176 and L. 187). Criminal tax fraud penalties reach five years' imprisonment and a €500,000 fine (natural persons, increased for legal persons) under CGI Art. 1741; customs false-declaration sanctions include confiscation of goods and fines computed on the customs value (Code des douanes Arts. 410 et seq.). All supporting export documents must be retained until the expiry of the recovery period to avoid retrospective clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. France fully liberalises capital movements under Article 63 TFEU; profits of an FR SARL may be remitted without exchange-control approval. Operative frictions are limited to (i) the monthly balance-of-payments statistical declaration to the Banque de France for any cross-border transfer exceeding EUR 7,500 (Bic-Open reporting system), and (ii) the prior authorisation regime applicable to non-EU investors acquiring control of a French entity active in a sensitive sector listed in CMF Articles L151-3 and R151-3. A pure Tier-3/4 rack, power and space leasing activity aimed at commercial tenants is not automatically captured by the sensitive-sector list; however, the same activity may trigger authorisation where the hosted infrastructure contributes to public safety, national defence, electronic communications, or the protection of information systems. There is no limitation on dividend remittance beyond standard corporate formalities: statutory audit, shareholder approval of financial statements, and availability of distributable reserves.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France imposes no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation obligation on export proceeds, whether received in EUR or in foreign currency. Foreign-currency receivables remain freely usable by the SARL, subject only to the Banque de France statistical report for each flow exceeding EUR 7,500 (due by the 15th day of the following month) and to the accurate statement of the transaction purpose code on the underlying MT103 or SEPA instruction.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in its capacity as national central bank and balance-of-payments statistical authority, together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy and Finance) as the competent foreign-exchange and foreign direct investment (FDI) screening authority; legal basis: Code monétaire et financier (CMF) and EU Regulations on free movement of capital.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a secondary collection rail, but Low as the primary treasury and clearing anchor. EU-licensed electronic-money institutions operating under CMF Article L522-1 (e.g., Qonto SAS, Wise Europe SA, Revolut Business, Shine) can provide French IBANs, SEPA Credit Transfer, SEPA Instant, SEPA Direct Debit, and commercial-rate FX conversion. However, they are not credit institutions: they cannot legally hold client funds in escrow, cannot open a conventional capital-escrow account, and are not covered by the Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution (FGDR) in the same manner as bank deposits. For a capital-intensive colocation business relying on escrow commitments and monthly SWIFT inflows, EMIs should be limited to low-value, high-frequency receivable collections; settlement-grade balances must remain with an ACPR-licensed credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections by the non-EU shareholder must reference the Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose code for equity contributions to a resident company and the payment narrative 'apport en capital social'; the beneficiary must be the SARL with its French IBAN. The physical transfer is executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202. Prior to the injection, the non-EU investor must assess whether the SARL's effective activities are captured by the French FDI screening mechanism (CMF L151-3 / R151-3); a pure rack-leasing activity normally falls outside the list, but an ex-ante authorisation request is mandatory if the site serves or hosts systems contributing to critical public services or national security. After crediting, the SARL must complete the capital-increase filing with the Guichet Unique within one month and file the statistical FDI report with the Banque de France where applicable. Shareholder loans from abroad are routed via MT103 with the purpose code 'avance en compte courant d'associé' and must respect arm's-length interest to avoid French thin-capitalisation and deemed-dividend recharacterisation.Revenue collection: intra-EU tenants settle by SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant; extra-EU tenants settle monthly invoices via SWIFT MT103. Disbursements: (i) facility, power and cooling vendors by SEPA Direct Debit or SEPA Credit Transfer; (ii) international hardware and network suppliers by MT103, or by sight or standby commercial letters of credit issued in EUR or USD under UCP 600 (SWIFT MT700) where documentary compliance is required; (iii) clearing of USD invoices through the bank's USD nostro account with same-day value-date instruction. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility as a secondary collection rail, but Low as the primary treasury and clearing anchor. EU-licensed electronic-money institutions operating under CMF Article L522-1 (e.g., Qonto SAS, Wise Europe SA, Revolut Business, Shine) can provide French IBANs, SEPA Credit Transfer, SEPA Instant, SEPA Direct Debit, and commercial-rate FX conversion. However, they are not credit institutions: they cannot legally hold client funds in escrow, cannot open a conventional capital-escrow account, and are not covered by the Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution (FGDR) in the same manner as bank deposits. For a capital-intensive colocation business relying on escrow commitments and monthly SWIFT inflows, EMIs should be limited to low-value, high-frequency receivable collections; settlement-grade balances must remain with an ACPR-licensed credit institution.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) rate for fiscal years closing on or after 1 January 2022; 15% reduced rate applied to the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs with turnover below EUR 10 million

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding tax under Article 119 bis CGI on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds at least 10% of the SARL's capital for at least two years and both companies are genuinely established in the EU; for individual non-resident shareholders, a 30% flat levy (12.8% tax plus 17.2% social contributions) applies subject to treaty reduction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending on 31 December: the second working day following 1 May of the following year. For any other fiscal year-end: the second working day after the expiry of three months following the close of the fiscal year. The balance of the corporate income tax (solde de l'IS) is payable by direct debit on 15 May, or on the 15th day of the month following the statutory filing deadline for off-calendar fiscal years.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year taxpayers: instalments are due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December. For off-calendar fiscal years: each instalment is due on the 15th day of the month following each three-month period from the fiscal-year opening. No instalment is payable when the estimated annual tax is zero; the balancing payment is due with the 2065-SD annual return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 consolidated group turnover or total assets (Article L.13 AA LPF) triggers the mandatory preparation and filing of the French Master File and Local File documentation together with the electronic transfer pricing declaration on Form 2257-SD; groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000 must additionally file the Country-by-Country Report on Form 2258-SD. All documentation must be supplied to the DGFiP within 30 days of a written request.

Economic substance scope: France applies no registration-based Economic Substance Regulation of the UAE/offshore type; substance is instead enforced through the General Anti-Abuse Rule (abus de droit, Article L.64 LPF), the recharacterization provision of Article 155 A CGI for artificial leasing/service structures, and the BEPS MLI Principal Purpose Test when treaty relief is claimed. A data-center colocation SARL must demonstrate genuine premises, operational staff, active server management and decision-making in France; an empty shell without local substance will be disregarded, its intercompany deductions denied, and its income re-taxed at 25% without treaty reduction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: 80% bad-faith penalty (majoration pour manœuvres frauduleuses) on the evaded tax plus 0.20% monthly late-payment interest; criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and 5 years' imprisonment under Article 1741 CGI, increased to EUR 1,000,000 and 7 years when committed by an organized group; mandatory publication of convictions.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or caused the false return are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt under Article L.267 LPF; criminal courts may order restitution, confiscation of data-center assets, travel bans, and disqualification from managing a French company; in insolvency proceedings, directors face 'comblement du passif' for faute de gestion.
Artificial Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: DGFiP invokes Article 155 A CGI and re-taxes the entire lease income in the hands of the true French beneficiary at 25% plus the 80% fraud penalty and interest; DTA relief is denied under the MLI Principal Purpose Test; administrative fines up to EUR 5,000 apply for failure to disclose the effective beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors who established the artificial shell face personal liability for the full corporate tax shortfall, asset sequestration, travel bans, and up to 5 years' imprisonment for aggravated tax fraud when the shell is stripped of its capital before assessment.
