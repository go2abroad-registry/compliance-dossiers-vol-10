Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Code de commerce (French Commercial Code) Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 et seq.; Décret n° 84-406 on the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) executed by all partners, the standard incorporation form (formulaire M0), and all supporting annexes.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum capital for a SARL. Capital may be set as low as EUR 1; cash contributions must be fully subscribed and deposited into an escrow account with an approved French bank or notary before RCS filing, and can be withdrawn only upon issuance of the registration certificate. In-kind contributions are fully paid automatically.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance and periodic inspection by SDIS (Service départemental d'incendie et de secours); structural load conformity certified by an approved inspection bureau (Bureau de Contrôle); electrical installation safety certification; and the mandatory workplace health and safety assessment (Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques) filed with CARSAT and the labor inspectorate (DREETS). These local clearances confirm the physical, structural, and occupational fitness of the cross-dock facility.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – France; business portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate registration greffe: RCS via Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce; INSEE issues SIREN/SIRET and VAT identification through DGFiP.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first appoint a French tax representative (if non-EU) and file the DGFiP form 'Formulaire de demande d'identification TVA' (form 1545) or register through the DGFiP Pro online service. French-established entities obtain a SIREN/SIRET via the Centre de Formalités de Entreprises (CFE) and subsequently receive a VAT intra-community (TVA) number from the Centre des impôts. For a SARL, the SIREN/SIRET is granted upon RCS registration; the VAT number is issued automatically to the registered business address.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must first appoint a French tax representative (if non-EU) and file the DGFiP form 'Formulaire de demande d'identification TVA' (form 1545) or register through the DGFiP Pro online service. French-established entities obtain a SIREN/SIRET via the Centre de Formalités de Entreprises (CFE) and subsequently receive a VAT intra-community (TVA) number from the Centre des impôts. For a SARL, the SIREN/SIRET is granted upon RCS registration; the VAT number is issued automatically to the registered business address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

SARL (Société à Responsabilité Limitée) is subject to French corporate income tax (IS) by default. A SARL may elect for pass-through / income tax treatment (IR) under Article 239 bis AA CGI for small businesses (turnover < €10 million) and satisfying 50+ individual shareholder conditions. Additionally, an EURL (single-member SARL) may apply for quasi-partnership transparency. Hybrid mismatch elections are governed by the EU ATAD rules incorporated in French tax law (Article 212 bis CGI), applying to both inbound and outbound payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for establishment; a French SARL must register for VAT from its first taxable transaction. For small businesses, an optional exemption ('franchise en base de TVA') applies below an annual turnover of €91,900 for goods (including cross-dock warehouse resale) and €36,800 for services (including fulfillment services), but this is an option and does not apply to cross-border or intra-community acquisitions. Mandatory registration applies immediately if the entity engages in taxable activities above the de minimis handling.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically leaving the EU are eligible for 0% VAT under Article 262 I CGI, provided the supplier obtains proof of export within 90 days. Cross-border B2B services (e.g., logistics, warehousing, cross-docking) provided to a taxable customer outside France but within the EU are zero-rated under Articles 259, 259-0, and 262-0; services related to immovable property are taxed in the country of location. A French SARL supplying 3PL services to an overseas Amazon vendor (non-taxable or localized outside France) must verify the customer's VAT number and maintain a recapitulative statement for intra-community supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EAD / DAU) with validated exit from France
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing transport outside the EU
  • Intra-community Sale Invoice and any supporting delivery order issued in the cross-dock warehouse
  • Proof of payment of freight or third-party transport document (CMR / CIM)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT statement proving payment transfer

France mandates electronic invoicing for all B2B domestic transactions as of 1 September 2026 (large taxpayers) and 1 September 2027 (SMEs). E-invoicing is transmitted through a government-certified 'PDP' platform interfacing with the DGFiP; e-reporting is required for B2C and international sales via PDPs. There is no real-time digital stamping; instead, the French clearance model uses an E-invoice data warehouse (Archives). Failing to issue e-invoices through a certified PDP will result in penalties under Article 1737 CGI.

The French tax administration may reassess within a standard limitation period of three years (6-10 years for fraud). Incorrect zero-rating claims lead to VAT recovery plus interest (intérêts de retard) at 0.2% per month, a 40% ‘insuffisance de déclaration’ penalty (80% for deliberate misconduct under Article 1729 b CGI) and potential criminal fines up to €750,000 and imprisonment. Customs may also claim duty deficiencies and recover VAT under Articles 410-411 of the French Customs Code for false export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. France imposes no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Articles 63-66 TFEU and the French Monetary and Financial Code (CMF Articles L151-1 et seq.) guarantee free movement of capital, trade settlement, profit remittance, shareholder loans and capital repatriation. No FX authorisation is required. The only material remittance hurdle is the standard 25% domestic withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, commonly reduced under double tax treaties. Statistical, not restrictive, balance-of-payments declarations apply when monthly cross-border flows to a single non-resident counterparty exceed 12,500 EUR.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio exist for export receipts. A French SARL may freely retain foreign-currency proceeds in multi-currency accounts or convert to EUR via the interbank market. The only compliance duties are (i) monthly Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting for cross-border receipts above 12,500 EUR per counterparty per calendar month, and (ii) customs declaration for physical cash movements equal to or above 10,000 EUR under EU Regulation 2018/1672.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as central bank and balance-of-payments statistics authority within the Eurosystem; foreign exchange policy is set jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) and the European Central Bank.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. A lower treaty rate or an exemption may apply. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption may be available for a qualifying corporate parent, generally requiring a qualifying ownership interest, minimum holding period, subject-to-tax and anti-abuse conditions. French domestic anti-abuse rules and beneficial-ownership requirements apply.

For a fiscal year ending on a date other than 31 December, the return is generally due within three months after the fiscal year-end. Corporate income tax installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, with the final balance generally due on the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a 31 December year-end, the balance is generally due on 15 May.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The principal threshold for mandatory full French transfer-pricing documentation is generally EUR 400,000,000 of annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets, or membership in or control by an enterprise or group meeting the statutory threshold. A French entity below that threshold may still need to demonstrate arm's-length pricing and may be subject to the simplified documentation regime and annual transfer-pricing declaration where the applicable EUR 50,000,000 threshold or other statutory conditions are met.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a single standalone economic substance regulation equivalent to certain offshore ESR regimes. Substance is nevertheless material under French transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-abuse rule, principal-purpose and treaty anti-abuse standards, beneficial-ownership analysis, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules and interest limitation provisions. The SARL should maintain French operational substance proportionate to its activities, including premises or operational capacity, personnel or outsourced-function governance, decision-making, inventory and risk controls, contracts, accounting records, customs evidence and commercial rationale.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest and penalties that may reach 80% for fraudulent or abusive conduct, together with criminal tax-fraud penalties generally including up to five years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 500,000. Aggravated fraud may carry up to seven years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 3,000,000, with higher penalties potentially linked to the proceeds or organized conduct.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur criminal liability, personal civil liability in cases of fraudulent or repeated tax misconduct under French tax-enforcement rules, director disqualification, professional prohibitions and asset-enforcement measures. Travel restrictions or imprisonment require judicial or criminal measures and are not automatic consequences of an ordinary tax adjustment.
False invoicing and fictitious intercompany charges

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions, withholding-tax reassessment, transfer-pricing adjustment, penalties generally ranging from 40% to 80% depending on intent and conduct, late-payment interest and possible criminal prosecution for forgery, fraud or tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may be prosecuted where they authorized or knowingly participated in the conduct. Corporate separateness does not protect intentional personal wrongdoing or statutory management liability.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment based on the arm's-length principle, late-payment interest, penalties for inadequate or missing documentation and a specific documentation penalty that may be significant and is generally linked to the greater of statutory fixed amounts or a percentage of the adjusted profit, subject to statutory caps.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal exposure for deliberate concealment, obstruction, fraudulent accounting or knowingly approving materially false documentation. Ordinary documentation failure normally creates corporate tax liability rather than automatic imprisonment.
Undeclared permanent establishment or concealed French activity

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax, VAT, payroll and other tax assessments, interest, penalties, possible withholding-tax liability and criminal consequences where concealment is deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for fraudulent management, obstruction or tax evasion if they intentionally structured or operated the activity to conceal French taxable presence.
VAT, customs and import-evasion violations

Statutory sanction: Recovery of customs duties and import VAT, interest, administrative fines, seizure or forfeiture of goods, suspension or withdrawal of customs authorizations and criminal penalties for serious customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally prosecuted for intentional customs fraud, forged documents or organized evasion. Civil recovery may extend to persons who personally guaranteed or enabled the unlawful conduct.
Money laundering and terrorist-financing controls

Statutory sanction: French money-laundering offenses may carry imprisonment and substantial criminal fines, with aggravated sanctions for habitual, organized or professional conduct. Assets and proceeds may be seized or confiscated. AML reporting and cooperation failures may also trigger supervisory sanctions where the entity is within an applicable regulated perimeter.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees may be personally prosecuted where they knowingly participate, facilitate or conceal laundering. Imprisonment, confiscation and judicial restrictions are possible following criminal proceedings.
Shell company, beneficial-owner concealment and sham residence

Statutory sanction: Loss of treaty benefits, denial of deductions, tax reassessment, anti-abuse penalties, beneficial-owner and registry sanctions, possible accounting or fraud offenses and confiscation where criminal conduct is established.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil damages, management bans, criminal prosecution and personal liability for fraudulent declarations or participation in concealment. Travel bans or imprisonment are possible only where ordered under applicable judicial or criminal procedures.
Failure to maintain accounting records and obstruction of tax audit

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment by estimation, increased penalties, rejection of accounting records, procedural fines and possible criminal liability for obstruction, forgery or fraud.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and de facto manager may be personally prosecuted for deliberate obstruction or falsification. A court may impose management prohibitions, civil restitution and criminal sanctions.
Improper dividend distribution or unlawful profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unlawful distributions, withholding-tax assessments, interest, penalties, possible director liability and criminal consequences for misuse of corporate assets or false accounts.

Director personal liability: Managers may be required to restore unlawfully distributed amounts and may be liable for abuse of corporate assets, breach of fiduciary duties, fraudulent accounting or tax evasion.
