Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises, with registration in the Registre National des Entreprises and the competent Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés maintained by the commercial court registry.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.123-1 et seq. for a Société à Responsabilité Limitée; French Insurance Code, Articles L.310-1, L.321-1 and applicable Solvency II provisions for insurance and reinsurance authorization. A SARL may not itself operate as an authorized reinsurance undertaking in France; the regulated underwriting vehicle must use a legally eligible form and obtain ACPR authorization.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of company formation submitted through the Guichet Unique, accompanied by executed SARL articles of association, beneficial-owner declaration, capital-deposit certificate, registered-office evidence, and prescribed identity and management documents.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 42 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to a French SARL. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions must be deposited into a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts account before filing; the deposit certificate is released after registration. A regulated reinsurance undertaking is subject to separate ACPR authorization, own-funds, solvency, governance, and financial-resource requirements that materially exceed the SARL rule.

Funding & unlock path: No separate industrial clearance is normally required for a standard office. The landlord or operator must maintain applicable fire-safety systems, evacuation arrangements, emergency lighting, accessibility measures, and electrical controls. Any company-controlled premises or fit-out may require fire-safety review, building or occupancy approval, structural-load verification, workplace risk assessment, occupational-health arrangements, and compliance with the French Labour Code. A physical underwriting or data facility may trigger additional building, security, business-continuity, and fire-authority requirements.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies to a French SARL. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions must be deposited into a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts account before filing; the deposit certificate is released after registration. A regulated reinsurance undertaking is subject to separate ACPR authorization, own-funds, solvency, governance, and financial-resource requirements that materially exceed the SARL rule.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
As a SARL has no statutory board, the shareholders and appointed gérant resolve as follows: (1) the company accepts the subscriptions stated in the articles and confirms payment of the required cash portion into the designated blocked account; (2) the registered office is adopted at [full French address]; (3) [name, identification details] is appointed gérant for an indefinite term, subject to the articles and French law; (4) the gérant is authorized to complete the Guichet Unique filing, obtain the registration extract, open operating accounts, and execute ancillary incorporation documents. The resolution is approved and signed by all required parties.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR), acting under the French Insurance Code; the Ministry for the Economy and Finance is the responsible ministerial authority for the applicable insurance regulatory framework. No industrial manufacturing board is applicable to a purely financial captive reinsurance structure.

Category: ACPR authorization to conduct reinsurance operations under the French Insurance Code. The authorization is not available to a SARL as the underwriting entity; the applicant must use an eligible regulated legal form and satisfy governance, business-plan, technical-provision, own-funds, solvency, risk-management, and fit-and-proper requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member and operates a regime of unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under TFEU Articles 63-66; there is no general exchange-control authorisation for inward or outward payments, profit remittance, capital injection, or reinsurance premium flows. Residual compliance duties: (i) each transfer of funds between France and abroad exceeding EUR 15,000 must be reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics (Art. R. 152-5 CMF), and this is filed automatically by the presenting bank; (ii) profit remittance by a SARL is free once financial statements are approved within six months (Art. L. 223-21 Commercial Code) and the distribution does not impair share capital; non-resident dividends attract a 25% French withholding tax, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying corporate parents, or to a treaty rate; (iii) all flows remain subject to EU/OFAC sanctions screening, FATCA-CRS classification, and ACPR source-of-funds scrutiny. No licences or exchange permits are otherwise required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds applies; France abolished exchange-control surrender duties under EU capital-liberalisation rules. Reinsurance premium receipts, treated as exports of insurance services, may be retained indefinitely in EUR or in the invoiced foreign currency within a French resident foreign-currency account, with no statutory repatriation deadline and no minimum conversion ratio. The only binding constraint is prudential: under the congruence rule (Art. L. 343-1 Code des assurances) the captive must hold assets representing technical reserves in the same currency as the underlying liabilities, or obtain an ACPR derogation; net unmatched FX exposure must be quantified in the ORSA and hedged where material.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French central bank), acting jointly with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for prudential licensing, AML/CFT supervision and enforcement over the captive reinsurer; the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) retains exceptional safeguard powers under Articles L. 151-3 et seq. of the Code monétaire et financier to suspend or restrict transfers involving non-EU states in crisis circumstances.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: NON-VIABLE for underwriting-reserve custody and capital escrow; PARTIALLY VIABLE (Medium-Low) for low-value operational disbursements and FX-execution only. Under Directive 2009/110/EC (EMD2) as transposed into the Code monétaire et financier, an electronic-money institution may not take deposits and is legally incapable of holding statutory technical reserves. The captive reinsurer is required by Art. R. 332-2 of the Code des assurances to affirm technical reserves within an authorised credit institution. A non-EEA EMI (e.g., a UK-only licensed entity) has no passporting rights after Brexit and cannot operate in France without a dedicated ACPR branch licence; an EEA-passported EMI such as Wise E.U. (Belgian licence) or a French licensed EMI such as Qonto may be used solely as an accessory payment rail, with daily reconciliation to the principal bank and no reserve balances held overnight. Use of unfenced EMI balances would lead to ACPR capital add-ons and a finding of unauthorised deposit-taking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital and premium inflows must route through a licensed French credit institution or EEA branch with activated EUR/USD clearing capacity. Use SWIFT MT103 for all non-EEA originators and SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA originators, with the remitter populating field 70 of the MT103 using the Banque de France balance-of-payments classification ('Direct investment - capital subscription' for equity; 'Reinsurance premiums ceded' for underwriting flows). The receiving bank automatically files the mandatory EUR 15,000 transfer declaration with the Banque de France. Verification documents to be presented: certified copy of the capital-subscription deed; ACPR agrément; board resolution of the ceding parent; signed reinsurance treaty or slip; and audited IFRS accounts of the parent. Escrowed equity is held in a segregated Capital Escrow Account and may be reclassified to the statutory technical-reserve register only upon completion of RNE registration and fulfilment of ACPR conditions.Outbound claim payments to EEA beneficiaries clear through T2 (the new euro RTGS system) and SEPA Credit Transfer, with SEPA Instant for time-critical claims; the payment must carry the mandated purpose code and reference the claim bordereau. For high-value retrocession collateralisation, the captive may procure a standby letter of credit under URDG 758 or an MT760 guarantee instead of pre-funding reserve assets, preserving liquidity. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard rate for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2022; reduced rate of 15% applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for small enterprises meeting specific criteria, not applicable to captive reinsurance SPVs)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (standard withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents, unless reduced by tax treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal year ending 31 December, the return must be filed by the second business day following 1 May (typically 3 May). For other fiscal year-ends, the deadline is the second business day following the 3rd month after the fiscal year-end. Payment of corporate income tax is made in four quarterly instalments (due 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, 15 December) with a final settlement on the filing deadline.

Non-resident entities with a permanent establishment in France must file the same form, reporting only French-source income attributable to the PE. For a captive reinsurance SPV, ensure all underwriting income and investment income from French risks are included. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million (annual consolidated revenue or balance sheet total) for the group to be required to prepare a Master File and Local File. For entities below this threshold, a simplified documentation requirement applies if the entity's turnover exceeds EUR 50 million or intercompany transactions exceed EUR 1 million. Captive reinsurance SPVs typically fall under the full documentation requirement if the group exceeds the threshold.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE, but applies general anti-abuse rules (e.g., Article 155-A of the French General Tax Code) and the OECD BEPS principles. A captive reinsurance SPV must demonstrate real economic substance in France, including adequate staff, premises, and decision-making functions. The French tax authorities may challenge the existence of a French PE or the allocation of profits if the SPV is considered a shell or conduit. The SPV must comply with the French insurance regulatory requirements (ACPR) and maintain sufficient capital and reserves.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: up to EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment for individuals; for companies, fine up to EUR 2 million (or 10% of turnover). Civil penalties: 40% to 80% of the understated tax, plus interest at 0.2% per month. Publication of the sanction may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they have committed fraud or gross negligence. They may face civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment. The corporate veil can be pierced if the company is used as a sham or for fraudulent purposes.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The French tax authorities may disregard the entity for tax purposes, recharacterize transactions, and impose penalties for abusive tax avoidance (up to 80% of the tax avoided). The entity may also be subject to administrative dissolution if it fails to meet regulatory substance requirements under the ACPR.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the company's tax debts if they have acted in bad faith or committed a serious breach of duty. They may face disqualification from managing companies, travel restrictions, and criminal sanctions for complicity in tax fraud.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities. Criminal penalties for money laundering: up to 10 years imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine for individuals; for companies, fine up to EUR 3.75 million. The ACPR may also revoke the insurance license.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable for money laundering if they knowingly participate or fail to prevent it. They may face imprisonment, fines, and personal asset seizure. The corporate veil may be pierced to recover proceeds of crime.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income by the tax authorities, plus a 5% penalty on the adjusted amount (or 10% if the documentation is not provided). Interest of 0.2% per month applies. In cases of serious non-compliance, penalties can reach 80% of the tax understated.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant transfer pricing policies. They may face civil claims for damages and, in extreme cases, criminal charges for tax evasion.
