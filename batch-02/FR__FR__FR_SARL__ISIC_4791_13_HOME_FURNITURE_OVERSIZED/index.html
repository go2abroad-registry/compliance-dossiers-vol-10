Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum SARL capital. Cash contributions must be at least 20% paid-up at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years; in-kind contributions must be fully paid-up at incorporation. Capital funds must be deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: No formal fire brigade clearance is required for a non-public warehouse, but the building must comply with Code du travail workplace safety rules: fire doors, emergency exits, portable extinguishers, first-aid equipment, electrical installation conformity, and structural load certification from the building owner. If the premises are open to the public as an ERP, a municipal fire safety authorization may be required.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum SARL capital. Cash contributions must be at least 20% paid-up at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years; in-kind contributions must be fully paid-up at incorporation. Capital funds must be deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – impots.gouv.fr; business registration through Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises (INSEE issues SIREN/SIRET); VAT individual number issued by DGFiP or MSA

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities with a French permanent establishment must apply for a SIRET number using the formalities portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) and then obtain a VAT individual number (FR + 11 digits and a two-digit key) from DGFiP. Non-established foreign persons selling goods to France may apply directly to the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for a French intra-EU VAT number, filing form 1573-SD and providing proof of establishment, identity and business activity. The SARL domestic procedure is identical: statutory incorporation, CFE/guichet registration, INSEE issuance of SIREN/SIRET, then automatic VAT registration unless eligible for the micro-BA franchise.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities with a French permanent establishment must apply for a SIRET number using the formalities portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) and then obtain a VAT individual number (FR + 11 digits and a two-digit key) from DGFiP. Non-established foreign persons selling goods to France may apply directly to the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for a French intra-EU VAT number, filing form 1573-SD and providing proof of establishment, identity and business activity. The SARL domestic procedure is identical: statutory incorporation, CFE/guichet registration, INSEE issuance of SIREN/SIRET, then automatic VAT registration unless eligible for the micro-BA franchise.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

SARL is default subject to French corporate income tax (IS). Small closely-held SARLs may elect, under Article 239 bis AA CGI, to be taxed as partnerships (impôt sur le revenu) for a renewable maximum of five years. No US-style check-the-box classification election is available for French SARLs; hybrid mismatch restrictions under French transposition of ATAD (Law No. 2019-1479) apply to deny deductions for disregarded hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for foreign non-established suppliers (first euro of French taxable supplies). Domestic SARL may benefit from the franchise en base de TVA (zero VAT return obligation) up to EUR 91,900 annual turnover for goods online resale; beyond that mandatory VAT registration. EU distance-selling pan-European threshold for non-established EU merchants is EUR 10,000 (across all cross-border distance sales).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Sales of movable goods physically dispatched or transported outside France or the EU qualify for 0% TVA provided title passes to the purchaser and the exporter obtains proof of the effective exit within 90 days from the time of dispatch (Article 262-I-1 CGI). Services ancillary to export (freight, handling, transport, packing) are zero-rated under Article 262-I-2 CGI. Other B2B services to taxable customers abroad are generally outside the scope of French VAT rather than zero-rated if the place of supply is the customer's member state. No zero-rating is allowed without documentary proof of the export or cross-border transport; the online supply platform (marketplace) may be deemed seller for VAT purposes under the deemed supplier rules for imports below the consignment threshold.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE) filed via DELTA-SIC and validated by French customs with exit confirmation or MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice with export VAT rate 0% and the seller's French VAT identification number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank/payment-provider record proving foreign receipt of consideration
  • Transport contract, delivery note or warehouse receipt proving physical carriage outside the European Union

France has phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting reform (reforme de la facturation électronique). Receipt of e-invoices becomes compulsory from 1 September 2026 and e-emission from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be transmitted through registered Dematerialization Platforms (PDP) connected to the Public Invoice Portal (PPF), in CII, UBL or Factur-X formats. Export sales must be backed by customs exit data and are subject to e-reporting of counterpart data, but do not require on-invoice digital fiscal stamping because France has not adopted a real-time transaction clearance regime such as continuous taxation control.

DGFiP and French Customs (Direction Générale des Douanes) may audit export transactions retrospectively. If the exporter fails to substantiate the physical exit, 0% treatment is reversed, the normal French VAT is clawed back, and the taxpayer faces a 40% deliberate under-declaration penalty (80% for procedural abuse/fraud), monthly late-payment interest of 0.20% (Article 1727-1728 CGI), plus possible criminal customs penalties up to EUR 500,000 and five years imprisonment under the French Customs Code for false customs declarations. For the SARL and its managers, civil tax liability for directors may also arise in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. As a euro-area member applying Articles 63-66 TFEU, France maintains no exchange controls on current operations, foreign direct investment, profit repatriation, or cross-border payment flows; non-EU/EEA transactions are equally liberalized, subject only to (i) mandatory balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the resident bank to the Banque de France, (ii) foreign-investment prior authorization in narrowly defined sensitive sectors (not applicable to home-furniture e-commerce), and (iii) residual financial-sanctions and FATF-based screening. Profit remittance is unrestricted at the exchange-control level; the nominal hurdle is fiscal only, with statutory WHT of 30% on non-treaty outbound dividends, reducible to 25% under most tax treaties or 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. France imposes no surrender, forced conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds; the mandatory conversion ratio is zero and a French SARL may freely hold EUR or any foreign currency in authorized French bank accounts. Cross-border receipts are, however, captured by the resident bank in the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting framework (monthly reporting obligation lies on the bank, not the enterprise).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) and the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) as the prudential and exchange-policy authorities of the French Republic within the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rated MEDIUM for operational treasury and LOW for statutory share-capital escrow and documentary trade finance. ACPR-licensed French EMIs and neobanks (Qonto, Shine) permit fully remote SARL onboarding, provide French IBANs and PSP-friendly settlement, and are suitable auxiliary clearing accounts; EEA-passported offshore EMIs (Revolut Business via Lithuanian banking licence, Wise Business via Belgian EMI) operate lawfully in France under Directive 2009/110/EC passporting rules, but are treated only as secondary clearing rails, never as the sole treasury account, because marketplace/BNPL float concentration and non-resident manager structures may trigger abrupt account freezes. A tier-1 bank remains legally and practically mandatory for the SARL capital deposit certificate, FX forwards, commercial letters of credit, and large warehouse disbursements. Post-Brexit UK EMIs require a French registered agent and additional ACPR confirmations where they do not hold an EEA passport.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign capital contributions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA for EEA investors) to an ACPR-authorized bank holding the French operational account; the receiving bank automatically records the operation in the Banque de France balance-of-payments statement. The SWIFT purpose code should reference the capital nature of the payment, typically CAPT (capital transfer for participation in capital) or the bank-designated code for capital increases, with the French SIREN number and exact EUR amount in the additional information field. Where the injection is denominated in CNY or USD, the French bank converts into EUR on the client's spot instruction at the ECB reference rate plus European Banking Federation spread. Supporting documents include the shareholder capital-increase resolution, subscription minutes, and notarized affidavit of source of funds. No French central-bank approval is required for non-sensitive retail e-commerce, but the Banque de France direct-investment statistical declaration obligation applies to non-EEA investors and is executed by the bank; the French FDI screening mechanism under Article L151-3 CMF is not triggered because online furniture retail is outside the regulated sensitive sectors.Preferred clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant for EU/EEA suppliers using standard IBAN/BIC and contract references; (ii) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer for Chinese and third-country suppliers using full 12-digit CNAPS bank code and purpose code for trade payment for goods, with reference to the underlying sales contract and customs declaration; (iii) irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit under UCP 600 (SWIFT MT700/MT707) for large furniture container shipments, requiring bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, insurance certificate, and pre-shipment inspection certificate, recommended where the purchase order exceeds EUR 50,000; (iv) SEPA Direct Debit with creditor identifier for recurring French fixed charges such as rent and utilities; (v) daily sweeps of PSP and BNPL settlement floats into the tier-1 treasury account before net outward funding.
