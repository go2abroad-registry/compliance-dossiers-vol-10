Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Registry of the Commercial Court) in the territorial jurisdiction of the registered office, interfaced through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

Code de commerce (France), Articles L. 223-1 to L. 223-43 (SARL formation and governance), Article L. 123-36 et seq. (Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises), Articles R. 123-1 to R. 123-12 (RCS registration), Decree n° 84-406 (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés procedural rules), Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 (Digital Services Act) as applicable to online intermediation transparency.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form M0 (immatriculation d'une personne morale) filed electronically through the Guichet Unique; the constitutive instrument is the 'Statuts constitutifs' (Articles of Association), and the post-registration certificate is the 'K-bis' extract issued by the Greffe.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for a SARL under Article L. 223-2 of the Code de commerce; the Articles may fix capital at any freely determined amount, commonly between 1 EUR and 1,000 EUR. Where capital is 10,000 EUR or more, only 20% of cash contributions must be blocked and paid up at subscription, with the balance payable within five years. Where capital is below 10,000 EUR, 100% of cash contributions must be fully blocked and paid up before registration. Cash contributions must be deposited in a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations on a dedicated blocked account in the name of the company in formation; funds are released to the Gérant only after RCS registration. Non-cash contributions (apports en nature) must be fully released at subscription and, in principle, valued by a commissaire aux apports.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable for manufacturing. For the registered office, the Gérant must ensure general workplace safety under Articles L. 4121-1 et seq. of the Code du travail, including fire extinguishing equipment and adequate first-aid measures. If the offices are open to the public, the ERP regime (établissement recevant du public) under the Code de la construction et de l'habitation would require a fire safety certificate and a commission de sécurité visit; no such permit is needed for a closed back-office digital publishing operation.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) for corporate income tax, VAT, withholding taxes and overall French tax administration; filings are made via the impots.gouv.fr professional portal (Espace Professionnel). Business registration and statutory filings are initiated through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. VAT non-resident registration is handled by the DGFiP Non-Résidents Tax Service (Service des Impôts des Entreprises – Etrangers Résidents Non-Résidents).

E-government filing portal

A newly formed French SARL obtains its statutory TIN through the one-stop business registration platform: the legal representative files the creation dossier with the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). INSEE then assigns the SIREN (9-digit identification number) and SIRET (14-digit establishment number); DGFiP simultaneously issues the French VAT intra-Community number (numéro d'identification TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + a two-digit key + SIREN) and the corporate tax account number (numéro de gestion). A non-resident entity with no French establishment and no fixed place of business in France that makes taxable supplies in France must register for VAT using the simplified non-resident registration procedure: complete and file form 1475-SD (or 3550-SD for the special OSS registration) with the DGFiP Non-Résidents Service, providing corporate constitutional documents, evidence of foreign tax registration, and proof of identity of the legal representative; if the foreign entity is established outside the EU/EEA and has no EU intermediary, it must appoint a French tax representative (représentant fiscal) jointly responsible for VAT collection. No French residence or local partner is required for a foreign-owned SARL; the minimum capital requirement has been abrogated, though statutory and article-of-association capital must be stated in the registered deeds.

No French residence or local partner is required for a foreign-owned SARL; the minimum capital requirement has been abrogated, though statutory and article-of-association capital must be stated in the registered deeds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) under Article 206 CGI, and there is no general US-style check-the-box election. Statutory elections available are: (i) the 'SARL de famille' pass-through election under Article 239 bis AA CGI, available only when all partners are natural persons belonging to the same family, where the company may opt to be taxed as an income-tax partnership (impôt sur le revenu) unless an IS option is exercised; (ii) the reverse option to corporate income tax by entities that would otherwise be fiscally transparent, pursuant to Articles 206 and 239 bis A CGI; (iii) the French VAT group election (assujettis uniques) under Articles 1693 ter and 267 quinquies CGI, allowing a French legal entity and its closely held foreign or domestic establishments to form a single VAT taxable person; and (iv) tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI, requiring 95% or greater shareholding in French subsidiaries, separately available for corporate income tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch elections are governed by EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) rules transposed into Articles 212 bis and 209 CGI, and do not permit elective entity classification outside the statutory corporate law forms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established SARL, VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction; there is no general turnover threshold for ordinary taxable persons. The small-enterprise VAT exemption (franchise en base de TVA) under Article 293 B CGI applies only to French-established businesses whose annual turnover does not exceed €85,000 for supplies of goods, €37,500 for supplies of services, and €85,000 for certain low-margin businesses, provided the supply is not within hotel, catering, transport, or other excluded sectors; as soon as the applicable threshold is exceeded, registration becomes compulsory. Non-resident suppliers without French establishment generally have no registration threshold and are required to register in France from the first taxable French transaction, except where EU special schemes (OSS/IOSS for distance sales of goods and electronically supplied services) permit a single EU registration; the EU-wide €10,000 annual threshold applies only to intra-EU distance sales of goods and services for suppliers established in a Member State, and registration in France is required immediately if the threshold is exceeded or if the supplier opts into a French registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For tangible exported goods, a 0% French VAT rate applies when the goods leave the European Union territory physically within 90 days of the supply and the exporter retains contemporaneous documentary evidence of the dispatch or transport outside the EU (Article 262 CGI and Articles 74-0 A to 74-0 C of the French VAT annex). For intra-EU supplies of goods to a business customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number and with the goods transported to another Member State, the supply may be zero-rated as an intracommunity supply if the seller has complete Article 289 quinquies declarations and the customer provides its VAT number, with aggregation in the recapitulative sales list (Récapitulatif des Livraisons, DEB). For digitally published video games distributed through Steam and PlayStation Store, the place of supply under Article 58 CGI is where the customer belongs: B2C sales to a French consumer are taxable at 20%; B2C sales to non-EU consumers are outside the scope of French VAT (not merely zero-rated) because the customer is not established in France, and the supplier is not required to use French VAT, although it may be obliged to register and charge VAT in the consumer's foreign jurisdiction. B2B sales of digital content to a French business customer are subject to a reverse-charge mechanism if the supplier is non-established, while B2B sales to a non-French/non-EU business customer using that customer's VAT number or equivalent business registration are outside French VAT. To claim French zero-rating for digital exports, the publisher must demonstrate that the customer is a non-established purchaser with foreign address and, where applicable, a valid foreign VAT registration number, and that the payment card billing address, IP address, device country and bank payment routing are consistent with a foreign destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation) filed electronically through the DELTA/DX system, evidenced by the assigned MRN (Movement Reference Number), proving the goods have left the customs territory of the EU
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, or other transport document (CMR/customs consignment note) showing the actual movement of commodities from France to a non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice containing the complete description of goods, sale price, terms of delivery and the export VAT zero-rating reference under Article 262 CGI
  • Packing list and, where relevant, the export accompanying document (DAU-Export) or customs value declaration
  • Bank transfer receipt / inward remittance certificate (certificat de rapatriement de devises) evidencing full receipt of the export proceeds from the foreign buyer within the statutory period
  • Foreign buyer registration or VAT number certificate and, where the business customer is a non-EU entity, proof of business nature in the customer's country
  • For digital store exports, the documentation bundle consists of payment provider transaction logs showing the ultimate payee's billing country, Valve/Steam or PlayStation Store payout statements, geolocation/IP-country logs attached to the consumer's account, purchase confirmation emails, and the platform's VAT report showing that French VAT was not charged on non-French non-EU transactions; no customs declaration is applicable to purely digital supplies

France has enacted a mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting regime for all domestic B2B transactions and B2C transaction reporting, replacing the previous derogatory acceptance of paper/PDF invoices. Under Articles 289 bis, 289 quinquies and 289 septies CGI, a French-established VAT taxpayer must issue structured e-invoices and transmit invoice data to the DGFiP through one of two channels: the public free portal (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF) or a certified private dematerialization partner platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire, PDP) exposed via REST APIs using a secure token. E-invoices must comply with either Factur-X (EN 16931-compliant structured hybrid) or EDI format (EDIFACT/custom EDI conforming to EN 16931). The mandatory transmission flows phase in from 1 September 2026 for large and medium-sized enterprises (grandes entreprises and ETI) and from 1 September 2027 for SMEs and microenterprises. France does not currently operate a real-time fiscal clearance model requiring pre-approval or cryptographic digital stamping before invoice issuance; instead, the PPF and PDPs provide continuous transaction control by near-real-time structured data exchange, with a monthly aggregation to DGFiP for VAT reporting. B2C sales of digital gaming content are subject to e-reporting (identification of the customer, transaction amounts, VAT rate) even when no structured e-invoice is required; OSS returns for EU-wide B2C digital services are filed quarterly electronically. Non-established suppliers without French establishment that use OSS are not subject to the French B2B e-invoicing mandate for their B2C OSS sales, but all domestic B2B transactions requiring French VAT must flow through e-invoicing channels if the supplier has a French establishment or is obliged to file French VAT returns.

Export and intracommunity zero-rating is subject to intense documentary surveillance by DGFiP's anti-fraud and cross-border divisions. The taxpayer must be able to produce, upon request and without prior notice, all export proof including transport contracts, customs declarations, bank receipts and foreign customer files. If the statutory documentation is incomplete, contradictory, or does not establish the actual dispatch outside the EU, the tax administration retroactively recharacterizes the supply as taxable in France at 20% VAT under Article 271 IV CGI. The assessed VAT is immediately recoverable (clawback) together with late-payment interest calculated at 0.2% per month from the original due date and administrative penalties: a 40% increase for deliberate error or unfounded claim, an 80% increase in cases of abuse of right, dissimulation, destructive concealment or organized tax fraud, and additional penalties up to €750,000 plus a five-year term of imprisonment for criminal tax evasion pursuant to Articles 1729 and 1741 CGI. For fraudulent export schemes, the DGFiP may also apply anti-abuse measures, deny the right to deduct input VAT, issue a tax lien with security measures, initiate criminal proceedings for money laundering if funds are routed offshore, and publish a public notice of fraudulent claims. Refund claims based on export zero-rating are scrutinized by DGFiP's refund unit (Service des Restitutions d'Impôts) and may be suspended until the exporter supplies full digital and documentary evidence, especially for newly registered or high-risk exporters operating through marketplaces and payment aggregators.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions under the TFEU free movement of capital regime. France imposes no general exchange-control approval for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repayments. Practical hurdles are limited to anti-money-laundering documentation, verification of beneficial ownership, tax residence certificates, and the absence of EU/UN financial sanctions exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or minimum mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in France. Digital-platform sales proceeds, including Steam and PlayStation payouts, may be held in foreign currency in a French bank account or converted to euro at market rates at the SARL's discretion. Banks report cross-border flows to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics via the direct reporting system, but the entity has no separate reporting obligation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French central bank), exercising supervision and balance-of-payments oversight together with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) and the Direction Générale du Trésor for foreign-exchange statistics and investment-control purposes

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic-money institutions licensed in France or passporting under PSD2 are legally viable for complementary operational liquidity, but not recommended as the sole treasury rail for a publishing SARL. Providers such as Qonto, Shine, and Blank offer fast onboarding, SEPA Instant, and multi-currency available balance accounts; however, EMIs do not hold a banking license, cannot always provide full SWIFT MT103 participation, and may not support the large inbound capital escrow or notarized share-subscription receipts required for statutory capital. A hybrid stack using a tier-1 bank for capital escrow/long-term clearing and a fintech EMI for day-to-day disbursements is feasible and low risk.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Certified RBE extract from the French commercial register (RCS), notarized share-register ledger, corporate organisational chart signed by the gérant, passport or national ID of each UBO, and a sworn declaration of control under Article L.561-2-2 of the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Sanctions, PEP, or adverse-media alerts triggered by a shareholder or director in a non-EEA jurisdiction

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

EUR capital injections for the SARL are routed through the SEPA Credit Transfer scheme using ISO 20022 XML pain.001 messages to the designated corporate IBAN (FR76 + BIC). Non-EUR injections are cleared via SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.009 with the purpose code 'EQUI' for equity subscription; the narrative must state 'souscription au capital et prime d'émission' and reference the notarial or general-meeting approval. For capital increases exceeding thresholds requiring a statutory auditor, the bank will require a copy of the auditor's report on the capital increase. The receiving bank independently verifies remitter/beneficiary consistency, applies the Banque de France balance-of-payments code for direct investment, and may place funds on a blocking account until full KYC completion.Royalty and fee payments to EEA licensors or middleware providers are settled by SEPA Credit Transfer. Non-EEA disbursements are cleared via SWIFT MT103 using purpose code 'TRAD' and supported by commercial invoices or contractual schedules. A Commercial Letter of Credit is legally permissible and can be issued by the French bank for high-value engine-source-code escrow or milestone-based advance payments; however, digital-game publishing royalties normally settle clean against invoices and platform payout statements rather than through documentary letters of credit. Steam and PlayStation Store payouts are received on the treasury account through SWIFT or SEPA, reconciled against platform payout reports, and then deployed via the same clearing rails.EUR/USD deliverable FX forwards with maturities aligned to quarterly Steam and PlayStation settlement cyclesEUR/USD FX swaps for temporary liquidity management between the operational account and foreign-currency payout legsEuropean-style plain vanilla FX optionsParticipating forwards and ratio forwardsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for subordinate non-convertible or illiquid emerging-market currencies, subject to the entity's documented underlying exposure and bank credit approval

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €50 million annual consolidated turnover for Master File/Local File requirements

Economic substance scope: France implements Economic Substance Regulations aligned with OECD standards; relevant for holding companies or IP holding entities, but generally applicable to entities with passive income

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties including fines up to 500,000 euros and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil restitution of evaded taxes

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability, possible imprisonment, and disqualification from management roles
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as non-resident or denial of deductions; penalties up to 500,000 euros

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; directors may face civil and criminal sanctions
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustments by tax authorities increasing taxable base by 10-20%; penalties of 80% of additional tax

Director personal liability: Potential personal liability if willful misconduct
