No statutory minimum capital is required; the share capital may be set at EUR 1 or any higher amount, but each share must be fully subscribed. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the official portal https://www.impots.gouv.fr; company and VAT identification formalities are processed through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises at https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned French SARL, the statutory registration path requires submission of the incorporation dossier (statuts, nomination of manager, proof of registered office, declaration of beneficial ownership) to the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (CFE). The CFE transmits the file to the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE), the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), and the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Upon completion, the SARL obtains its SIREN/SIRET numbers. For VAT purposes, the DGFiP verifies the economic activity and, where the entity is not covered by the small-business exemption, allocates an individual VAT identification number (numéro d'identification individuel) and, for intra-EU transactions, the FR VAT number reported to the VIES system. Non-established foreign entities carrying out taxable supplies in France may apply directly to the DGFiP for a VAT registration using form SI-355/FR.

The CFE transmits the file to the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE), the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), and the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Non-established foreign entities carrying out taxable supplies in France may apply directly to the DGFiP for a VAT registration using form SI-355/FR.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at entity level. Under Article 239 bis AA of the French General Tax Code (CGI), a small SARL meeting statutory criteria (fewer than ten employees, turnover below the thresholds set by decree, and ownership by natural persons) may elect, for a maximum of five fiscal years, to be taxed as a partnership (société de personnes) with profits passed through to shareholders (impôt sur le revenu, IR). The option must be notified to the tax authorities. Additionally, certain SARL de famille may be taxed as IR entities. Hybrid mismatch consequences are addressed through mandatory disclosure and neutralisation rules under EU ATAD implementation, Article 212 bis CGI, and B2B notification obligations under DAC6; France does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

French VAT registration is generally mandatory for any taxable person established in France making taxable supplies, without an annual turnover threshold; however, the statutory small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA) under CGI Article 293 B applies to resident taxable persons whose annual turnover (exclusive of VAT) does not exceed EUR 91,900 for principal supplies of goods and EUR 36,800 for principal supplies of services. For intra-EU remote supplies of digital services and distance sales of goods to non-taxable persons, the pan-EU registration threshold is EUR 10,000 of aggregate cross-border turnover, above which the supplier must register for VAT in each relevant Member State or use the One-Stop Shop (OSS) simplified regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the supply of goods physically exported from France to destinations outside the European Union, provided that the exporter or the acquirer dispatches or transports the goods outside the EU within the statutory time frame and retains evidence of export (CGI Article 262 and Annex I Article 74 A). Services physically performed in France but directly related to exported goods (e.g., handling, transport, inspection) may also be zero-rated under French law. For B2B digital services such as stock photography licensing supplied to a customer established outside the EU, the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction and the supply is outside the scope of French VAT; the French supplier may deduct attributable input VAT, subject to standard substantiation rules. For B2C digital services supplied to non-EU consumers, the supply is also outside the scope of French VAT. To claim zero-rating or out-of-scope treatment, the taxpayer must hold sufficient documentary evidence identifying the destination of the goods or the establishment of the customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation via DELTA) with MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / CMR / consignment note) evidencing physical dispatch from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and contract evidencing the terms of sale and the foreign customer's identity
  • Bank transfer or inward remittance certificate evidencing the receipt payment in foreign currency or euro from the non-resident customer
  • Supporting evidence of customer establishment for out-of-scope services (e.g., VAT registration certificate, extract of foreign commercial register)

France has implemented a mandatory electronic invoicing and e-reporting regime, phasing in from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-market entities and from 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises. All B2B invoices must be issued as structured electronic invoices (Factur-X, UBL, or CII) through an accredited Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public Portal Public de Facturation (PPF), which transmits invoice data in near-real-time to the DGFiP. E-reporting of B2C transaction data is also mandatory. France does not operate a fiscal pre-clearance stamping model; instead, it requires API-based structured data transmission ensuring end-to-end integrity and auditability. Non-resident suppliers subject to French VAT must either register in France or, for intra-EU B2C digital services, use the OSS portal, with equivalent e-reporting obligations.

French tax authorities aggressively audit export zero-rating claims and out-of-scope service treatments. If the required export evidence is absent, defective, or fraudulent, VAT becomes immediately collectible together with interest (intérêts de retard) at the statutory monthly rate. Penalties under CGI Article 1788 and Article 1728 include a 10% to 40% increase for omissions or inaccuracies; an 80% penalty applies for bad faith (manvais foi) or obstruction; a 100% penalty applies for abusive arrangements under CGI Article 1729 and Article 1731. Criminal liability may arise under Articles 1741, 1742, and 1743 of the French General Tax Code for fraudulent tax evasion, with imprisonment up to five years and fines up to EUR 75,000 per individual and EUR 375,000 for legal entities. Retrospective clawbacks may extend to up to five fiscal years (or ten years in the event of abuse of law), with the burden of proof shifted where the taxpayer fails to maintain credible documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Member State of the Economic and Monetary Union; the euro is fully convertible for both current-account operations (royalties, trade in services, freelancer remuneration) and capital-account operations (share capital injections, dividend distributions, repayment of shareholder loans). No prior exchange authorization is needed for payment in or out of the country, including for non-resident shareholders. The only practical limitations are EU/OFAC sanctions compliance and a mandatory monthly Balance-of-Payments (BoP) reporting obligation to the Banque de France for any individual cross-border monetary transfer equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500, usually filed by the banking intermediary. Profit remittances to an EU shareholder are unrestricted; remittances to a non-EU shareholder are free but must pass standard anti-money-laundering checks and will be automatically exchanged with the shareholder's tax authority under Common Reporting Standard (CRS) obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

French law does not impose any statutory surrender or mandatory foreign-exchange conversion ratio for export proceeds, regardless of currency. The French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier, Art. L.112-4, R.112-1) permits free retention of foreign currency in bank accounts with a French credit institution or abroad, provided the underlying commercial operation is documented and the associated cross-border flow above EUR 12,500 is reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics. The SARL may invoice and receive settlement in EUR for all exports, or in any foreign currency agreed by the parties. Commercial prudence suggests converting or hedging non-EUR receipts to eliminate currency mismatch, but no regulatory timeline exists for repatriation once the receivable is recorded.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), under the Eurosystem framework, in coordination with the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) for prudential and conduct supervision, and the Direction Générale du Trésor for foreign-exchange and investment regulatory policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — French licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine, or Pennylane are feasible as a supplementary operating account for SEPA collections and low-value royalty payouts, but they cannot execute documentary trade instruments, provide committed FX facilities, or act as a capital-formation deposit bank under CMF Art. L525-1. For the target ISIC 7420 stock-photo AI business with expected cross-border MDR (merchant discounts) and international payout streams, an EMI is acceptable for aggregated receivables under EUR 20,000/month, but a robust treasury requires a Tier-1 bank for FIC-regulated BoP reporting, correspondent banking, and bare-metal FX settlement. EMI onboarding is legally valid via ACPR-approved video-identification; no physical presence is required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the French SARL, whether initial or subsequent, must be credited to a French bank account held in the corporate name. Transfer protocols: send SWIFT MT103 where the remittance information field clearly states 'Apport en capital / Subscription to shares' and includes the corporate corporate identifier (SIREN/LEI) of the SARL. For any inflow exceeding EUR 12,500 in one calendar month, the receiving bank will execute the balance-of-payments declaration (Balances des Paiements) to the Banque de France; no separate client filing is required, but the company must retain a copy of the transaction letter for tax and legal audit. If the shareholder is outside the EU, the capital injection may trigger a declaration of direct investment to the Ministère de l'Économie (form CERFA n° 10874*) only if the activity falls under a sensitive sector (which ISIC 7420 does not) and the investor acquires more than 25% of the company's voting rights. No prior special authorization applies to a stock-photo licensing business; however, the bank will request evidence that the injection derives from legal funds, and for a contribution in kind (such as a software licence or photolibrary valuation), a statutory auditor's report (commissaire aux apports) is mandatory.For trade settlements—including royalty payments, stock-photo licence fees, artificial-intelligence model dataset purchase agreements, and any all-in price from foreign content creators—the preferred clearing rails are: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (MT103 or ISO 20022 pain.001) for EEA beneficiaries in EUR; (ii) ordinary SWIFT MT103 with correspondent bank for non-EEA beneficiaries; (iii) local ACH/FPS clearing for USD/GBP if the collection account holds multi-currency balances to minimize bank charges; and (iv) international wire using a designated beneficiary celling for payouts above USD 100,000. Commercial Letters of Credit are not required for software/royalty flows and are not business-as-usual for a stock-photo marketplace; standby letters of credit or documentary collections (URC 522) appear only when the SARL acquires an entire photographic asset library or content portfolio from a foreign legal owner. Every outbound disbursement over EUR 10,000 is automatically screened by the bank for terrorism and sanctions; the airway bill, copyright transfer memorandum, or platform payout report must be attached to the instruction when a non-standard remittance (e.g., buy-out of catalogues) is requested. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) is mandatory where the group's consolidated turnover exceeds €400 million and the French entity's intra-group transactions exceed €400 million per year. Simplified documentation (a one-page summary) is required for small and medium enterprises (PME) with cross-border intra-group flows exceeding €50,000 per year. Under French law, documentation rules apply to entities subject to corporate income tax with related-party transactions, regardless of turnover, if the tax administration requests it.

Economic substance scope: France applies the EU Economic Substance Requirements indirectly through the EU blacklist and DAC6 mandatory disclosure rules. A French SARL must demonstrate real economic substance in France: actual offices, personnel, management decisions, and local operational activity. Artificial arrangements with no substance that shift profits to zero-tax jurisdictions are subject to the General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article L.64 of the French Tax Procedure Code), the altered substance doctrine under Article 155-A, and the Principal Purpose Test in DTAs. The stock photo AI platform must maintain records of hosting, servers, staff, and customer contracts in France to avoid being re-characterised as a shell or conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (under Article 1741 of the French General Tax Code). Administrative penalty: 40% bad-faith penalty, 80% fraudulent concealment penalty, plus late payment interest at 0.2% per month. Publication of the sanction may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may be jointly and severally liable for the tax debts and penalties if they personally participated in the evasion; imprisonment and travel bans apply. Civil restitution of evaded taxes with interest.
Shell Company and Artificial Arrangements (Abus de droit / Société écran)

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment under the abuse of law procedure (Article L.64) with a penalty of 80% if abusive, or 40% for lack of economic substance. The tax administration may disregard the arrangement and tax the true beneficiary. Extended statute of limitations (10 years instead of 6).

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for the recomputed tax if the corporate veil is pierced; they may also be charged with money laundering or tax evasion if the shell structure is used to hide the true principal.
Transfer Pricing Adjustment and Deficient Documentation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of profits to arm's length plus 40% penalty for missing or materially inaccurate documentation (increased to 80% in cases of bad faith or fraud). Interest at 0.2% per month applies. Directors can be civilly liable if they knowingly signed misleading returns.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for TP adjustments, but if they intentionally set non-arm's length conditions for personal benefit or to evade tax, they can be personally liable and prosecuted for tax fraud.
Withholding Tax Evasion (Dividend Stripping / Non-payment of WHT)

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax plus late interest; 80% penalty for concealed distributions. Criminal prosecution under Article 1743 for non-payment of tax withheld at source: imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to €75,000. As a withholding agent, the SARL is primarily liable.

Director personal liability: The legal representative (gérant) is personally liable for the withheld tax if they knowingly failed to remit it; criminal liability and travel bans apply. Civil liability to the tax authority for the full unpaid amount.
AML & Sanctions Violations (Money Laundering, Search of Terrorist Financing)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Articles L.561-1 et seq.) up to €1,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for entities subject to AML obligations. Criminal money laundering penalties: imprisonment up to 5 years (7 years for aggravating circumstances) and fines up to €375,000.

Director personal liability: Directors can be criminally prosecuted for money laundering if they knowingly processed proceeds from illicit activities. Travel bans, asset freezes, and seizure of company assets. Beneficial owners are subject to investigation and may face extradition or direct financial liability.
Incorrect Use of Tax Credit or Reduced VAT Regime

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the undue tax credit or VAT with interest, 40% penalty for inaccurate returns, and 80% for deliberate fraud. Criminal sanctions in severe cases. Directors may be personally liable if they signed incorrect declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors are responsible for the completeness and accuracy of tax returns; personal liability arises where gross negligence or wilful misrepresentation is proven, triggering joint and several recovery and, in extreme cases, criminal penalties.
