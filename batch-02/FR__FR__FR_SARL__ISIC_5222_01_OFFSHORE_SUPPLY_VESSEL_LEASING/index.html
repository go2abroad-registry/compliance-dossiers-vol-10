Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre National des Entreprises (RNE), administered through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) Guichet Unique, with registration in the competent Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) maintained by the commercial court registry.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L223-1 to L223-43 and R223-1 to R223-24 governing SARLs; Articles L123-1 to L123-9 governing registration; Articles L561-46 et seq. governing beneficial-owner declarations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of company formation submitted through the INPI Guichet Unique, supported by signed SARL articles of association, beneficial-owner declaration, and required supporting documents.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 62 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions are deposited in a blocked bank or notarial account until registration. Capital contributions in kind require valuation by a commissaire aux apports unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain vessel and premises fire-safety compliance through approved inspections, maintain firefighting and lifesaving equipment, and satisfy the ISM and applicable SOLAS requirements. Shore offices, warehouses, workshops, and bases require building, occupancy, accessibility, and fire-safety compliance; establishments open to the public may require an ERP authorization. Conduct structural load and electrical inspections through competent or accredited bodies where required. Complete workplace risk assessment, occupational-health registration, seafarer medical fitness, training, personal-protective-equipment, emergency-plan, and labor-inspectorate compliance under the French Labour Code.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions are deposited in a blocked bank or notarial account until registration. Capital contributions in kind require valuation by a commissaire aux apports unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

Direction générale des affaires maritimes, de la pêche et de l'aquaculture (DGAMPA), supported by the relevant maritime prefecture, DDTM maritime service, French Customs, and port authority. France has no single generic industrial manufacturing licence for vessel leasing; maritime operation and vessel-specific approvals are mandatory.

Category: French vessel registration and applicable title of navigation (titre de navigation), together with vessel safety, security, radio, insurance, and maritime-employer approvals required for the vessel's flag, size, service, and operating area. A transport authorization or armateur registration may apply depending on the commercial operation and vessel status.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); central portal: impots.gouv.fr; French company registration portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

A French SARL must first be registered with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises, which issues the SIREN and SIRET identifiers. After RCS registration, the DGFiP automatically allocates the French Tax Identification Number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire). A foreign entity without a French establishment but carrying out taxable operations in France must apply to the DGFiP's non-resident tax department (Service des Impôts des Entreprises - non-résidents) using Form 1303-SD (demande d'attribution d'un numéro de TVA) together with supporting evidence of the taxable activity. For vessel chartering and maritime services, the entity must confirm its French VAT registration status before issuing invoices and may need a specific 'service en douane' code for customs and shipping formalities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a SARL is ipso facto subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) under Articles 206-1 and 209 of the CGI; it is not eligible for pass-through treatment. French-resident companies are taxed on worldwide profits, with a territorial adjustment for foreign-source profits attributable to a foreign establishment (Art. 209 I-A CGI). — A French SARL must first be registered with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises, which issues the SIREN and SIRET identifiers. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a SARL is ipso facto subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) under Articles 206-1 and 209 of the CGI; it is not eligible for pass-through treatment. French-resident companies are taxed on worldwide profits, with a territorial adjustment for foreign-source profits attributable to a foreign establishment (Art. 209 I-A CGI).

Available election forms

A SARL cannot elect check-the-box pass-through taxation. Available elections include: (i) tax consolidation ('intégration fiscale') under Art. 223 A et seq. CGI for groups holding at least 95% of the subsidiary's shares; (ii) election for the 'régime mère-fille' (parent-subsidiary regime) under Art. 145 and 216 CGI; (iii) option under Art. 239 bis A CGI for civil-purpose SARLs — unavailable for maritime/commercial leasing activities; (iv) ATAD hybrid-mismatch declarations, where applicable, requiring Form 2257 to be filed with the DGFiP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established SARL, VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable transaction; there is no de minimis registration threshold. The 'franchise en base' small-business exemption applies only to domestic turnover until it exceeds EUR 85,000 for supply of goods and EUR 37,500 for supply of services (USD/EUR: EUR 10,000 annual EU-wide distance-selling threshold under the One-Stop-Shop). A foreign supplier to French B2B customers is caught by the reverse-charge mechanism and does not require French VAT registration unless it makes B2C supplies of services or distance sales above the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically leaving EU territory are VAT-exempt under Art. 262-1-I CGI, provided the seller proves that the goods have left the EU within one month (or in justified cases, within a longer period). Additionally, under Art. 262-VI and 262-VII CGI, the sale, lease, and charter of commercial vessels used for operational activities on the high seas — including offshore platform supply vessels serving maritime installations — is VAT-exempt where the vessel is used for industrial or commercial purposes at sea and the charterer operates it outside territorial waters. The charter must be a marine charter with the vessel supplied, crewed, and operated for offshore logistics services; pure coastal or treacherous-navigation charters inside French territorial waters do not qualify. Zero-rating documentation must be retained for 10 years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/ENS) lodged via the DELTA-X electronic customs system showing the consignee and the 'export' authorisation
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing the high-seas destination and physical departure
  • Cargo manifest and Port Departure Certificate (certificat de départ) issued by the French customs authority at the departure port
  • Commercial invoice referencing the VAT clause 'EXONÉRATION ART. 262 CGI' and the nature of the marine charter
  • Bank document or fiche domiciliation export (for EUR remittance) proving inward receipt of the export consideration / Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Charter-party agreement and end-user certificate confirming offshore platform operations outside French territorial waters
  • Proof of arrival at the foreign or high-seas destination (e.g., offshore operations report, platform delivery note, or third-country customs entry)

France will introduce mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting for all VAT-registered taxpayers under the 'réforme de la facturation électronique': from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises (including taxpayer groups), and from 1 September 2027 for small and medium-sized enterprises. Invoices must be issued in structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, or CII) through an approved Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF). Transaction data for all B2B and B2C supplies must be transmitted to the DGFiP in (near) real-time as e-reporting, including bank transaction detail. Although France does not operate a fully real-time fiscal clearance/stamping system, the DGFiP will automatically cross-check e-invoice and e-reporting data with VAT returns.

If the exporter fails to substantiate the export within 90 days after the expiry of the dispatch period under Art. 272(2) CGI, or fails to repatriate foreign-exchange proceeds within 180 days of shipment, the VAT exemption is retrospectively clawed back. The DGFiP re-assesses VAT on the full consideration, plus intérêts de retard (monthly late payment interest at 0.20% per month, accruing interest), under Articles 1727 and 1728 CGI. Penalties escalate based on intent: 40% for lack of good faith (insuffisance de bonne foi), 80% for abusive manoeuvres (manœuvres frauduleuses) or opposition to tax inspections; criminal penalties for customs false declarations under Art. 414 of the French Customs Code may also apply, including imprisonment of up to three years and fines. The DGFiP maintains a dedicated risk filter for maritime chartering and applies a presumption of missing export evidence if the vessel's GPS/port records do not match the declared itinerary.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. France, as a Eurozone member, adheres to EU free movement of capital provisions. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard anti-money laundering (AML) and tax reporting obligations. No prior authorization is required for cross-border transfers, but declarations are mandatory for transactions exceeding €10,000 or for any transfer to non-EU jurisdictions under the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds is imposed. Exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts. However, all export receipts must be declared to the French customs and tax authorities for statistical and tax compliance. The reporting deadline is within 30 days of receipt, and the exporter must provide supporting documentation (e.g., commercial invoices, transport documents) to the bank for the transaction to be processed.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as the central bank, with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) under the Ministry of Economy and Finance exercising primary authority over foreign exchange regulations and capital controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) can provide cross-border payment services and multi-currency accounts. However, for high-value maritime charter transactions, traditional banks are preferred due to established correspondent banking networks and maritime trade expertise. EMIs may face limitations on escrow services and complex trade finance instruments (e.g., letters of credit). Legal constraints include the need for a full EMI license (not just payment institution) to hold client funds, and compliance with the EU's 5AMLD for beneficial ownership transparency.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Use SWIFT MT103 for equity injections from shareholders. The purpose code must be '110' (Direct Investment) or '111' (Equity Capital) as per the French Balance of Payments classification. The bank will require a copy of the shareholder resolution, the capital increase deed, and the Kbis extract. For loans from non-resident shareholders, use purpose code '120' (Loans) and provide a loan agreement. All inbound transfers must be declared to the French Treasury via the 'Déclaration de Transfert de Fonds' if exceeding €15,000. Verification: The bank will perform a source-of-funds check and may request the underlying contract.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct payments to suppliers, charterers, or service providers; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for high-value vessel acquisitions or major equipment purchases, typically issued via the bank's trade finance desk; (3) Documentary collections (D/P, D/A) for lower-risk transactions. All outbound payments require a valid purpose code (e.g., '130' for services, '140' for goods) and supporting documentation (invoices, contracts). For payments to sanctioned jurisdictions, additional screening is mandatory.Forward contracts (FX Forwards) with maturities up to 12 months, available from tier-1 banks for EUR/USD and EUR/GBP pairs.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are not commonly used for EUR as it is freely convertible; however, they may be offered for exotic currency pairs (e.g., EUR/CNY) on a case-by-case basis.Currency swaps (FX Swaps) for managing short-term liquidity mismatches between EUR and USD, particularly for charter payments in USD.Options (FX Options) for hedging against adverse exchange rate movements, though these are less common for operational treasury and require board approval.
