Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) held by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (or Tribunal Judiciaire) in the territorial jurisdiction of the SARL's registered office.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title III, Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 et seq.; Civil Code Article 1832; Order No. 2023-489 on the Guichet Unique.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (articles of association) and a Déclaration de constitution de société (form M0), filed electronically via the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 12 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The share capital is freely fixed (minimum €1). Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (bank, Caisse des Dépôts, or notary) before filing; at least 20% of each cash contribution must be paid up upon incorporation, the residual within 5 years. In-kind contributions must be fully released upon signature. The escrow deposits are released only after the company obtains its K-bis extract.

Funding & unlock path:

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
At the constitutive meeting of the shareholders of the Company, held at the fixed registered office, the subscribers, acting as sole members under Article L.223-18 of the French Commercial Code, resolve: First, the articles of association are adopted unchanged. Second, the share subscription list, signed by each subscriber, is approved and the allocation of quotas to each shareholder is ratified. Third, the registered office is fixed at the address set in the articles, which shall serve as the legal and administrative seat. Fourth, the shareholders jointly appoint the designated Manager as first gérant, with full powers to bind the company in ordinary operations. This resolution is adopted unanimously.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — portal: impots.gouv.fr; business formalities via guichet-unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr)

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL: (1) incorporate via the Guichet Unique to obtain SIREN/SIRET, Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés, and RBE filing; (2) automatically obtain a VAT intracommunautaire (TVA) number from DGFiP when the entity is liable for French VAT; for a non-resident entity without French establishment, register through the French VAT department (SIE) and appoint a fiscal representative for VAT purposes if required; (3) open a professional tax space on impots.gouv.fr for filing CIT and VAT; (4) for EU distance sales or OSS use, register on the One-Stop-Shop portal; (5) for import/export operations, obtain an EORI number via DGFiP/douane.gouv.fr.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS); restricted pass-through election exists for qualifying small/family SARLs under specific statutory provisions.

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box system. Available elections: (a) option for transparent partnership taxation (impôt sur le revenu) for small SARL de famille or under Article 239bis AA CGI for the initial tax years; (b) option for VAT consolidation (assujetti unique) to treat France group entities as a single taxable person; (c) option to designate a permanent establishment for VAT purposes and for services place-of-supply reverse charge mechanisms; (d) notification and documentation requirements for hybrid mismatch transparency under ATAD/Article 212 bis CGI; all elections must be filed with DGFiP and maintained in tax records.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Article 293 B CGI: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for supplies of goods and certain services; EUR 37,500 for taxable services (excluding goods), including patent and IP services; for EU cross-border distance sales of goods and B2C digital/remote services, an EU-wide EUR 10,000 registration threshold applies through the OSS scheme; foreign suppliers without a permanent establishment are generally required to register in France from the first French taxable supply absent applicable simplification.

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing in France is being phased in (large enterprises from 1 September 2026; small/medium enterprises from 1 September 2027) through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF), with API integration via PDP partners. Invoices must be issued in structured electronic format (Factur-X / PEPPOL BIS / UBL) and e-reporting of B2C/intra-EU transactions is required; there is no real-time digital stamping system, but fiscal clearance occurs through the PPF receiving workflow and obligations for archiving, invoice integrity, and TVA identification.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full capital account convertibility and current account convertibility under EU regulations and the Monetary and Financial Code. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are permitted without prior approval, subject only to standard tax reporting and beneficial ownership disclosure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For service exports including PCT patent applications and international trademark registration, no mandatory surrender of proceeds is required. Invoices are issued in EUR and funds credited directly to corporate accounts. Mandatory surrender applies solely to goods exports, typically within 90 days at official rates.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés); reduced 15% rate applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs; a reduced 10% patent-box effective rate applies to net qualifying intellectual property licensing income of approved patent assets.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Domestic law: 30% withholding tax on dividends distributed by a French tax-resident SARL to non-resident shareholders (Article 119 bis CGI). The statutory rate may be reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the non-resident corporate shareholder holds at least 5% of the SARL's capital for at least 24 months. Under many French tax treaties the applicable rate is 0%, 5% or 15%, depending on the shareholding percentage and the beneficial-ownership test.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual IS return must be filed within three months of the fiscal year-end. For a SARL whose fiscal year closes on 31 December, the return is due no later than the second working day following 1 May of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory French Local File and Master File documentation applies where the French taxpayer or the controlled group has annual turnover or gross balance-sheet assets of at least EUR 400 million, or where the French entity controls, or is controlled by, an entity abroad whose turnover or assets exceed the same threshold. Country-by-country reporting obligations arise for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulations filing regime comparable to the Gulf cooperation council ESR framework. However, French abuse-of-law doctrine, anti-hybrid rules, transfer-pricing rules and the Multilateral Instrument's Principal Purpose Test require a French IP prosecution SARL to demonstrate real economic substance: an office, employees, decision-making authority, board/governance meetings, bank accounts, accounting records and commercial activity in France. An IP-holding or patent-licensing structure using a hollow French SARL without substance is vulnerable to treaty denial, patent-box denial, recharacterisation and heavy penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent under-declaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment plus the 10% late filing penalty, 40% penalty for deliberate misdeclaration, 80% penalty for abuse of law and 0.20% monthly late-payment interest. Criminal tax fraud carries a sentence of up to five years' imprisonment and a criminal fine of up to EUR 500,000, with the fine capable of being doubled or increased to half the value of the concealed assets.

Director personal liability: The gérant and de facto managers may be held personally liable for the SARL's tax debts and penalties where the fraud or management irregularity caused the company's insolvency or where assets were concealed in violation of tax collection rules. Courts may impose a travel ban, asset freezing, a prohibition from managing a company, and imprisonment.
Shell company and artificial patent-holding arrangement

Statutory sanction: The French tax authority may invoke Article L.64 CGI abuse-of-law procedure to recharacterise the arrangement. An 80% abuse-of-law penalty applies to the undeclared tax, and a 100% penalty may apply where the taxpayer cannot show a non-tax purpose. The patent-box regime is denied retroactively and treaty benefits may be withdrawn under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate form is used solely as a sham, courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold the directors or shadow directors jointly and severally liable for the unpaid taxes, penalties and interest. Criminal sanctions, travel restrictions and disqualification orders may be imposed on individuals who knowingly used the shell structure to defraud the French Treasury.
Transfer-pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may disregard the undocumented intra-group transaction, reassess profits using indirect methods, and shift the burden of proof to the taxpayer. Failure to produce the required transfer-pricing documentation can result in a fixed fine of EUR 10,000 per accounting year, and a 40% or 80% penalty if deliberate misstatements or fraudulent documentation are established. A transfer-pricing adjustment may also attract late-payment interest of 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false or incomplete documentation, or who cause the French entity to enter into non-arm's-length contracts for the purpose of tax evasion, may be made personally liable to the extent of the tax evaded under personal liability proceedings, and may face criminal fraud sanctions including fines and imprisonment.
Money laundering and financial flows through shell bank accounts

Statutory sanction: French anti-money-laundering rules impose reporting obligations, customer due-diligence requirements and record-keeping duties on relevant professionals. Failure to file a suspicious-transaction report with Tracfin may lead to administrative fines and criminal penalties; money laundering of tax fraud proceeds is punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 375,000, increased to EUR 1 million or more in sophisticated or organised cases.

Director personal liability: Company directors and beneficial owners can be prosecuted personally for AML violations. They may be subject to corporate-veil piercing, confiscation of assets, prohibition from managing commercial enterprises, travel bans and imprisonment. A pure corporate-veil defence is not available where the individual knowingly directed or benefited from the illicit flows.
