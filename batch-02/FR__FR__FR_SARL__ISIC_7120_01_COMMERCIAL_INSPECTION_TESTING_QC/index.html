Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SARL. Every share must be fully subscribed; cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% at incorporation, with the balance due within five years. Funds are escrowed in a blocked capital deposit account ('attestation de dépôt des fonds') held with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and are released only after the company obtains RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and evacuation certificate issued by the local service de sécurité / commission de sécurité for any premises open to the public; structural load compliance certificate for installed equipment; occupational health clearance from the local occupational health service (médecine du travail) covering the mandatory physical-exams programme and the written 'Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels'.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) with primary portals impots.gouv.fr and portailpro.gouv.fr; SIREN/SIRET issuance by Institut National de la Statistique et des Études Économiques (INSEE) via Répertoire National des Entreprises et des Établissements, interfaced through the INPI/Guichet Unique registration platform; FR VAT number issued separately by the DGFiP Service des Impôts des Entreprises of the competent territorial department.

E-government filing portal

Foreign investor forms a domestic French SARL through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), obtaining (1) a SIREN 9-digit identifier and SIRET 14-digit establishment ID via automatic INSEE notification, (2) a Confirmation d'Immatriculation from the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés, and (3) an intra-community VAT number. The VAT number is applied for with DGFiP (form CERFA n°14463 for normal VAT regime or via the Guichet Unique when establishing the SARL). The applicant must provide: certified copy of the articles of association (statuts), proof of the registered office address, power of attorney for the non-resident manager, identity documents of shareholders/managers, and declaration of intended activity. VAT registration is delivered in approximately 10-15 working days; the format is FR followed by a 2-digit key and the 9-digit SIREN. For purely intragroup services or subcontracted EU/free-zone functions, a permanent establishment VAT registration could alternatively be required; however, for the standard commercial inspection agency SARL, full ordinary-law TIN/VAT-ID procurement via the SARL is the operative path.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. A SARL is unconditionally subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, Article 206(1) Code Général des Impôts). Optional election: a 'family SARL' owned exclusively by natural-person relatives may elect for partnership-style taxation with 40% abatement under Article 239 bis AA CGI, causing profits to be taxed at the shareholder level (each member taxed on distributable share in the progressive personal income tax schedule); the election is revocable under conditions. Anti-hybrid elections under Article 212 bis CGI (ATAD transposition) apply to hybrid financial instruments and hybrid entities: French entities must classify each foreign partnership or transparent entity for French tax purposes according to its foreign legal form, and an election to re-characterize a disregarded foreign entity as transparent or opaque may be made where the foreign treaty classification so provides. Additionally, a SARL may elect between the simplified real VAT regime (régime réel simplifié) and the normal real VAT regime (régime réel normal) on form CA 707, but such elections do not alter entity-level income tax classification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 262 CGI, exports of tangible goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union are exempt from French VAT (zero-rated) when the direct proof of physical exit from EU customs territory is held. Intra-Community supplies of goods are exempt pursuant to Article 262 ter CGI if the transferee supplies a valid French or other-Union VAT number and movement justifying evidence is compiled. For a factory-inspection/QC agency, the following qualifying scenarios apply: (i) testing, inspection and audit services physically supplied on goods that are then exported and provided to a non-French principal are zero-rated if the services directly relate to the goods and are covered by the Article 262 zero-rating shadow; (ii) B2B services rendered to a customer established outside the EU fall outside the territorial scope of French VAT under Article 259 A CGI (place of supply is the customer's place of business), without application of the 20% rate and without requiring a separate invoice; (iii) services to EU B2B customers are taxable in the customer's Member State where the customer is established and do not trigger French VAT output. Entry cannot be claimed by mere declaration; the taxpayer must retain contemporaneous, consistent trade documents.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Document Unique d'Accompagnement / Document Administratif Unique generated through the DELTA-H/ Télé-Douane system, with registered MRN)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR International Consignment Note evidencing physical transportation
  • Commercial invoices, the fixed-price export contract and signed inspection/QC reports or certificates of conformity
  • Proof of delivery and customs exit certificate (certificat d'origine, transit documents or customs stamp)
  • Inward remittance certificate (relevé de remise bancaire) and corresponding SWIFT/Wise settlement records matching invoice currency and debtor name

France is implementing mandatory nationwide e-invoicing and e-reporting reform in phases: B2B domestic e-invoicing and B2C e-reporting obligations begin 1 September 2026 for large enterprises (grandes entreprises) and 1 September 2027 for SMEs/general taxpayers. All VAT-registered persons established in France must transmit structured e-invoices via an accredited Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the State platform (Portail Public de Facturation, 'Chorus Pro'). Mandatory formats are Factur-X (hybrid), UBL, or CII. There is no digital fiscal stamp or real-time transaction clearance server; instead, invoice lifecycle statuses (issued, received, paid) and invoice registration data are transmitted to DGFiP in near-real-time, under the FNN (Facturation dans le Nuage National) architecture. Parallel obligations require French accounting book files to be produced in the FEC (Fichier des Écritures Comptables) standard and retained for at least 6 years (10 years for real estate activities, situational).

The French tax authority rigorously audits export zero-rating claims under Articles 262 and 262 ter CGI and can claw back VAT, impose intérêt de retard of 0.20% per month (2.4% annualized), a 5% late-payment penalty, and an additional 40% bad-faith penalty (Article 1729 CGI) or 80% for organised abuse or simulated acts (Article 1729-0 CGI). Material misstatements trigger fines of €500 per irregular document under Article 1737 CGI and up to €750 per documentary falsehood under the general penal provisions. Tax fraud prosecution under Article 1741 CGI carries imprisonment of up to 5 years and fines up to €500,000 (extended to 7 years and €2,000,000 when committed by an organized gang). The use of artificial arrangements solely to obtain export exemption is recharacterized under Article L64 LPF (abus de droit) with a 80% penalty plus interest. Retention of the export documentation bundle is compulsory for at least 6 years under Article L102 B Livre des Procédures Fiscales, and refunds may be audited retrospectively within the normal three-year limitation period, extended to six years where tax fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member with unrestricted euro convertibility. Under Article 63 TFEU and the EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC), France fully liberalises both current-account and capital-account transactions for non-residents; no exchange-control approvals apply to equity injections, loans, dividends, royalties, or service fees denominated in EUR or foreign currency. Practical residual hurdles: (i) commercial banks require a documented economic purpose and complete KYC for each flow; (ii) dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders attract French withholding tax at 30% (or a reduced treaty rate) under Code Général des Impôts Article 119 bis; (iii) all international transfers must be effected through banking intermediation under the Code Monétaire et Financier; (iv) foreign-currency accounts may be held, but the underlying business justification must be evidenced. Profit remittance is therefore legally unconstrained, subject only to tax documentation and anti-money-laundering controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender rule and no statutory conversion ratio or repatriation deadline. Export invoices may be settled in EUR or any freely convertible currency, and proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts opened with French credit institutions. Residual obligations: (i) all export receipts and trade payments must flow through a licensed credit institution (financial intermediation rule); (ii) invoices and underlying contracts must evidence economic reality when requested by the bank; (iii) a balance-of-payments declaration must be filed with the Banque de France for any year in which aggregate cross-border financial flows equal or exceed EUR 750,000, with detailed reporting per counterparty; and (iv) individual transfers of EUR 10,000 or more remain subject to automated AML screening and documentary retention.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (central bank and balance-of-payments statistical authority); ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) as banking licensing and supervisory authority; Direction Générale du Trésor within the Ministry of Economy and Finance exercises capital-movement policy. The Banque de France operates the external financial statistics (balance des paiements) declaration regime and is the authority to which corporate cross-border financial flows are reported.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for routine low-value treasury operations through French-registered EMIs (Qonto, Shine, PENEXO) and EU-passported EMIs (Wise Europe S.A. under Belgian EMI licence) for collections, supplier payouts, and expense cards; LOW for statutory SARL formation because the capital-blocking requirement for apport en numéraire mandates a licensed credit institution or a notary, not an electronic money institution. Legal constraints: EMIs under PSD2 may issue e-money but may not take deposits, issue documentary letters of credit, execute FX forwards or options, or serve as the main correspondent trade-clearing channel; balance-of-payments reporting remains the French bank's intermediary duty. Recommended architecture: French tier-1 bank as house bank and statutory escrow provider; an EMI used only as a supplementary low-value disbursement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) open a statutory capital blocked account (compte bloqué) at a French credit institution or deposit funds with a notary before SARL registration; (2) remit via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for an EU-resident parent) from the shareholder's own account, quoting ISO 20022 purpose code IVCA (Investment Capital) and the free-text reference 'APPORT EN CAPITAL — FR_SARL — SIREN PENDING'; (3) use TARGET2 settlement through the French bank's correspondent for non-EEA wires, with the beneficiary IBAN of the blocked account and the bank's French BIC; (4) no Banque de France FX approval applies, but enhanced AML verification is triggered when the remitter name does not match the shareholder register or when the amount equals or exceeds EUR 10,000; (5) the French bank issues the certificat de dépôt des fonds enabling the gérant to apply for company registration; (6) funds are released to the operational treasury account only upon production of the Kbis and filed Articles of Association; (7) the SARL records the contribution in the shareholders' register and, where annual aggregate cross-border flows reach EUR 750,000, files the Banque de France direct-investment declaration for 'investissement direct — apport en capital en numéraire'.Clearing rail strategy for factory inspection, QC testing, and audit payables: (a) EU/EEA subcontractors and calibration laboratories are settled through SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR with purpose code COMC/TRAD and a VAT reverse-charge invoice reference; (b) non-EEA vendors are settled through SWIFT MT103 in the invoice currency (USD, GBP, CNY, EUR) via the house bank's correspondent network; (c) high-value export-critical deliverables are secured through irrevocable sight or usance Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600, with payment against presentation of inspection certificates, test reports, and cargo documents; (d) low-value operational payments below EUR 10,000 may use the Wise Europe EMI rail, but Wise cannot provide documentary LC or FX forward capacity; (e) every disbursement is matched to an approved purchase order, inspection deliverable, and signed acceptance report; (f) restricted-country counterparties require additional OFAC/EU sanction screening and end-use statements; (g) foreign-currency payouts are settled either from forward contracts or from natural hedging where the company holds matching USD or GBP inspection receivables.Outright FX forward (change à terme) on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CNY for maturities up to 12 months, subject to an approved treasury limit and board resolution.FX swap (swap de change) for short-term intra-month liquidity alignment between inspection-fee receivables and vendor payouts.Cross-currency swap (swap de devises) for matched-term hedging of long USD-denominated master service agreements and structural balance-sheet exposure.Plain-vanilla FX option purchases (long call and long put) with premium settled upfront, permitted on a hedge-only basis with no naked option writing.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) limited to currencies without full convertibility (onshore CNY variants, INR, BRL, VND) where a documented underlying inspection contract exists in that currency and settlement is effected through EUR offset with full audit trail.

Recommended architecture: French tier-1 bank as house bank and statutory escrow provider; an EMI used only as a supplementary low-value disbursement rail.
