Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital for an SAS. The articles must state the issued share capital freely, but any cash contributions above stated minimum must be deposited in a blocked French bank account until the RCS registration certificate (Kbis) is issued, after which the president may release funds. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up upon subscription and must be valued by a statutory contribution auditor unless the statutory exemption applies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the municipal Commission de Sécurité or the local inspection authority after on-site inspection; structural load certification verifying floor loading for palletized inventory; workplace clearance from DREETS covering health and safety, emergency routes, and the general prevention plan required under the French Labor Code. Occupational risk assessment (DUERP) and workplace medical surveillance must be established before operations commence.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) or Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE); primary portal: impots.gouv.fr. Customs and EORI matters are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI) through douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS incorporated through the national one-stop business formalities portal receives a SIREN and establishment-level SIRET from the Institut national de la statistique et des études économiques (INSEE). The SAS must then be registered with the competent SIE for corporate tax and VAT administration. DGFiP issues a French VAT identification number consisting of the prefix FR, a two-digit validation key, and the SIREN number. A non-established foreign entity carrying out taxable transactions in France must apply to the SIEE before or upon commencing the relevant activities, provide constitutional documents, ownership and management information, business contracts, bank details, proof of taxable activity, and a French or EU fiscal representative where legally required. An EORI number must be obtained from DGDDI for customs imports and exports. VAT registration, corporate tax registration, payroll registration, and customs registration are separate compliance processes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Subject to statutory eligibility conditions, including limits on age, ownership, workforce, turnover, and balance-sheet size, a qualifying SAS may elect temporarily for taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu), generally for a maximum period of five financial years. France has no general United States-style check-the-box election. A SAS may also elect for the VAT franchise regime only where the statutory turnover conditions are satisfied; the election does not eliminate VAT obligations for transactions outside the franchise conditions. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive apply to relevant cross-border arrangements and do not constitute a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business eligible for the domestic VAT franchise, the principal turnover threshold for supplies of goods and comparable commercial activities is EUR 85,000, subject to the applicable prior-year and current-year thresholds and statutory tolerance rules. A French-established business exceeding the applicable threshold must register and account for VAT. A non-established business generally has no general de minimis threshold for French VAT registration when it makes taxable supplies in France, unless a specific reverse-charge or other statutory exception applies. Reduced VAT rates may apply to qualifying food products, but manufactured self-heating meal products require transaction-specific classification and packaging analysis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union may generally qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT under the French rules implementing Article 146 of the EU VAT Directive, provided the exporter can prove exportation and satisfy documentary and timing requirements. The exporter must be the supplier or act through an authorized intermediary, and the goods must physically leave the EU customs territory. Intra-EU supplies may qualify for VAT exemption where the customer has a valid VAT number in another Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier retains transport and recapitulative statement evidence. Services supplied to a business established outside France may be outside the scope of French VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rules rather than technically zero-rated; exceptions apply for services connected with French immovable property, admission to events, transport, and other specifically regulated categories. Input VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices, business-purpose evidence, and compliance with the applicable French or EU refund procedure.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, Incoterms, and VAT treatment
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, airway bill, road consignment note, or equivalent
  • Proof of exit from the EU customs territory generated by the customs system or equivalent carrier and customs evidence
  • Purchase orders, sales contracts, delivery records, and customer or freight-forwarder correspondence linking the goods to the export
  • Evidence of payment or commercial settlement where relevant; France does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a condition for export VAT exemption
  • For intra-EU supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required EC Sales List or recapitulative statement

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices, and large and mid-sized businesses must issue domestic B2B electronic invoices and submit required transaction data. From 1 September 2027, small and micro-enterprises must issue domestic B2B electronic invoices. Invoices will generally be exchanged through an approved private platform connected to the public invoicing directory; France is not currently operating a universal real-time pre-clearance model for all VAT invoices. E-reporting applies to specified B2C and cross-border transactions. Electronic invoices must contain required French VAT invoice data, structured or hybrid formats where applicable, and traceability, authenticity, and integrity controls. Export customs declarations remain processed through the French and EU customs systems and are not replaced by the e-invoicing mandate.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption may be denied or retrospectively clawed back if exportation, EU exit, customer status, transport, or transaction substance is not proven. Reassessed VAT is generally payable with late-payment interest, currently calculated under the applicable statutory rate, and may attract a 40% penalty for deliberate non-compliance or an 80% penalty for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights; lesser penalties may apply for other procedural failures. False invoices, fabricated customs evidence, or knowingly fraudulent VAT claims may trigger criminal tax-fraud proceedings, administrative fines, customs sanctions, seizure, and liability of responsible officers. Input VAT refunds and related deductions may be disallowed, and assessments may extend to the applicable statutory limitation period, with longer periods or criminal exposure in cases of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a core Eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, which guarantees unrestricted free movement of capital and payments. No exchange-control approval, central-bank clearance, or individual investment license is required for current-account flows, capital subscriptions, loan repayments, or profit remittances. Repatriation of profits is unfettered, subject only to the ordinary corporate income tax (25% standard rate as of 2024), the participation exemption benefits for qualifying subsidiaries, and the 30% withholding tax framework on non-treaty outbound dividends, with treaty relief available under the France-PRC, France-Hong Kong, and France-US DTCs as applicable. Statistical balance-of-payments declarations to the Banque de France are due for cross-border transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 12.5 million per calendar year, and inward direct investments exceeding EUR 15 million held by non-EU investors may be screened under EU Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) Regulation 2019/452 and French Decree No. 2019-1590.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender, no statutory repatriation deadline, and no forced conversion ratio into euro. Exporters may retain foreign-currency balances freely in designated foreign-currency accounts held with French-resident banks. The only obligations are statistical: each bank must report to the Banque de France all cross-border settlement flows used for balance-of-payments compilation, with threshold-based declaration of individual payments above EUR 12.5 million per annum and the annual survey of direct investment positions. Trade settlements with counterparties in jurisdictions subject to EU restrictive measures are separately prohibited under the Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP) framework and EU Regulation 833/2014 as amended.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in conjunction with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for balance-of-payments statistical oversight; prudential licensing, AML/CTF enforcement, and banking supervision are exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), pursuant to the Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier) and EU Regulation No. 1024/2013 (Single Supervisory Mechanism).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for secondary transaction accounts and customer collections; Low for core treasury, capital escrow, and documentary trade operations. EMIs currently active in France operate under PSD2 licenses (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Finom) or under EU passporting rights (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business); they may open corporate accounts through fully remote embedding. However, offshore EMIs domiciled outside the EEA or not passported under PSD2 are legally prohibited from providing payment services in France without an ACPR branch license; their accounts cannot receive share-capital deposits or serve as trust/escrow for the SAS capital injection, and they are unsuitable instruments for LC issuance, cross-border guarantees, or SWIFT-based trade settlement. The SAS should maintain its primary clearing and capital account at a tier-1 ACPR-licensed commercial bank and use fintech EMIs exclusively for collections, expense disbursements, and multi-currency petty treasury accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the SAS paid-in capital injection: (i) remitter transfers via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank cover) to the SAS's euro-denominated IBAN account held at the tier-1 French bank;
