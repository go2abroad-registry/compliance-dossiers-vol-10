Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

French INPI Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises, connected to the Registre National des Entreprises

Name reservation / lock period

France does not generally provide a standalone statutory company-name reservation with a universal 30-day validity period. A proposed SAS name is submitted as part of the incorporation formalities; any temporary blocking or filing workflow is procedural rather than a guaranteed statutory reservation. Before filing, conduct INPI trade-name, trademark, RCS, domain-name, and public-register searches. If INPI or the competent registry rejects or suspends the filing, obtain the written reason, correct the name or supporting evidence, and resubmit through the Guichet Unique. For a formal administrative decision, file an administrative claim with the issuing authority and, where appropriate, a recours gracieux or recours hiérarchique within the applicable deadline, followed by a judicial challenge before the competent administrative court. Registry disputes concerning commercial-registration decisions may require proceedings before the competent commercial court. Use French counsel to preserve deadlines and distinguish a registry refusal from a trademark or unfair-competition dispute.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), principally the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE); online tax procedures are administered through the DGFiP professional portal at impots.gouv.fr. Corporate formalities and business identifiers are processed through the national business formalities one-stop shop at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, with registration data recorded in the Registre national des entreprises (RNE) and the national business register.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS formed in France completes its formation formalities through the national business formalities one-stop shop. Following registration, it receives a SIREN identifier and establishment-level SIRET identifier; the competent tax office assigns or confirms its tax accounts and VAT status. A French VAT identification number normally consists of the country prefix FR followed by a two-digit key and the SIREN. The company must register for professional access to the DGFiP portal and submit applicable corporate tax and VAT returns. A foreign business with taxable transactions in France must register with the competent DGFiP service for foreign businesses, the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE), or the competent SIE as applicable, and provide formation, identity, activity, representative, and transaction documents. A non-EU business may be required to appoint a French fiscal representative unless an applicable statutory exemption or mutual-assistance arrangement applies. EU businesses generally register directly without appointing a French fiscal representative. Registration and VAT reporting obligations can arise without a general turnover threshold for a non-established taxable person making transactions taxable in France.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Eligible SAS companies may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax (impôt sur le revenu, IR) regime for up to five fiscal years, subject to statutory conditions, including limits on age, workforce, turnover or balance-sheet size, ownership, and the proportion of voting rights held by individuals and qualifying managers. The election is not a US-style check-the-box election and is unavailable where the eligibility conditions are not met. No general French election permits a SAS to elect transparent treatment solely by selecting a tax classification. French rules implementing EU anti-hybrid-mismatch provisions may deny deductions or require income inclusion in defined cross-border arrangements; they do not constitute a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to a French-established business conducting ordinary taxable activities: VAT registration and compliance are generally required from commencement of taxable activity, subject to the small-business franchise en base de TVA and other specific exemptions. The mainland-France franchise thresholds generally applicable to domestic small businesses are EUR 85,000 for sales of goods and similar activities and EUR 37,500 for services, subject to statutory qualification rules, prior-year/current-year tests, and legislative changes. These domestic franchise thresholds do not generally relieve a non-established supplier of French VAT registration where it makes transactions for which it is liable to account for French VAT. Separate EU cross-border small-enterprise rules and transaction-specific exemptions may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with a right to deduct input VAT, provided the statutory conditions are met and the supplier retains evidence that the goods actually left the EU. Intra-EU supplies of goods may qualify for exemption only when the substantive conditions are satisfied, including transport to another Member State, a valid customer VAT identification number, and required reporting. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer or supplier is offshore: place-of-supply rules determine whether French VAT applies. Many B2B services supplied to a taxable person established outside France are outside the scope of French VAT, subject to exceptions; services taxable in France remain subject to the applicable French VAT treatment. A VAT credit refund is subject to the applicable return, evidence, and refund procedures.

  • Export customs declaration and export-result confirmation, including the MRN and exit confirmation where available
  • Commercial invoice, order or contract, and records identifying the goods, buyer, and transaction
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route, such as bill of lading, CMR, air waybill, carrier confirmation, or delivery evidence
  • Proof of payment or accounting records supporting the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory export-VAT document
  • For intra-EU exempt supplies, the customer's valid VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required EC Sales List reporting

France is introducing domestic business-to-business electronic invoicing and electronic transaction-data reporting in phases. Under the legislated timetable, all in-scope businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance obligations begin on that date for large and intermediate-sized enterprises and on 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises. In-scope invoices must use compliant structured electronic formats and be transmitted through the public invoicing portal or an approved private platform connected to the national system. The regime is not a universal real-time pre-clearance or digital-stamping system. Applicable transaction-data and payment data reporting requirements depend on transaction type and taxpayer category. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. Cross-border transactions and specified transactions outside the domestic e-invoicing scope may remain subject to electronic reporting rather than domestic e-invoice issuance.

France imposes no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. A zero-rating or exemption claim unsupported by evidence of export or by the applicable transaction conditions may be denied, with output VAT assessed retrospectively, interest for late payment, and applicable tax penalties. Penalties depend on the nature of the error, disclosure, and conduct; deliberate fraud or fraudulent documentation can attract enhanced tax penalties and, where statutory elements are met, criminal sanctions. Input VAT refunds may be adjusted or recovered if the underlying entitlement is not established. The taxpayer should retain contemporaneous customs, transport, invoice, accounting, and payment evidence for the applicable French tax-record retention period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France fully liberalised current and capital account transactions under EU law. The euro is freely convertible; no general quantitative limits apply to profit remittances, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation to non-EU shareholders. Foreign investors may transfer funds in and out without exchange control authorisation. The only mandatory requirements are documentary reporting to Banque de France for cross-border financial flows above statutory thresholds, compliance with international sanctions, anti-money laundering rules, and the absence of any embargoed counterparty. Capital account transactions are likewise unrestricted but must be reported for statistical purposes on the French balance-of-payments (échanges extérieurs).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds in France. Exporters may freely retain export receivables in authorised foreign-currency accounts (comptes en devises) or in euro-denominated accounts. No statutory timeline for conversion exists, and no minimum conversion ratio applies. Cross-border export payments must be included in the enterprise's monthly balance-of-payments declaration to Banque de France when aggregated transaction volumes exceed the regulatory reporting threshold.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Central Bank) in coordination with the Directorate General of the Treasury (DG Trésor) for balance-of-payments and direct investment reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is medium-high for a non-deposit operating account, but legally constrained. An EU/EEA-authorised e-money institution (EMI) may passport into France under ACPR notification and provide payment accounts, e-money issuance, and often SWIFT MT103 routing. However, an EMI cannot offer escrow, credit, or deposit facilities, and most cross-border digital EMIs lack direct French IBAN and TARGET2 access. For marine-engine trade settlements and documentary LC operations, a licensed credit institution remains mandatory. EMIs are therefore acceptable only for low-volume ancillary disbursements, not for capital escrow or trade clearing.
