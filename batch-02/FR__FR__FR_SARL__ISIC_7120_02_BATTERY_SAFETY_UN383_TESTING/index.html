Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); VAT registration is handled by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Portal: impots.gouv.fr / pro.impots.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities without an establishment in France must apply for VAT registration through the DGFiP’s SIE using Form CO (Cerfa n°14871*01) or via the EU’s One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for distance sales and certain B2B services. Non-EU entities must appoint an individual tax representative (assujetti unique) who is severally liable for VAT. The application requires an EORI number, proof of business activity, identity documents, and details of the fiscal representative. The SIE issues a French TIN and, where applicable, a TVA intracommunautaire number (FR followed by a two-digit control key and the SIREN number).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities without an establishment in France must apply for VAT registration through the DGFiP’s SIE using Form CO (Cerfa n°14871*01) or via the EU’s One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for distance sales and certain B2B services. Non-EU entities must appoint an individual tax representative (assujetti unique) who is severally liable for VAT. The application requires an EORI number, proof of business activity, identity documents, and details of the fiscal representative. The SIE issues a French TIN and, where applicable, a TVA intracommunautaire number (FR followed by a two-digit control key and the SIREN number).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at entity level. It may elect, under strict statutory conditions (Art. 239 bis AA CGI), to be treated as a transparent pass-through entity for its first five fiscal years if its shareholders are individuals and annual turnover does not exceed specified thresholds. France does not apply a US-style check-the-box election. Hybrid mismatch elections must comply with EU ATAD 1 and ATAD 2 instruments transposed into the French General Tax Code, notably regarding reverse hybrid and branch mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.2%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for services for established French small businesses under the franchise en base; however, for non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in France, there is no statutory registration threshold and a VAT registration is mandatory (subject to reverse charge mechanisms for B2B services).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to exports of movable goods physically transferred or transported out of the European Union by the seller, the buyer, or a third party acting on their behalf, provided the goods leave EU territory and are not subject to installation or assembly tests altering their nature. For offensive and defensive testing laboratory services, zero-rating applies to services directly related to exported goods, while cross-border B2B testing services rendered to a VAT-registered non-French client are generally outside French VAT scope under reverse charge or taxed in the customer’s jurisdiction. The lab must retain documentary evidence of the contractual link, the destination, and the actual export of the tested goods within the required proof period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / EAD) with MRN
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial Invoice and test report tied to the exact exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/FX settlement proof demonstrating payment in foreign currency

France imposes a progressive mandatory e-invoicing regime for B2B transactions from September 2026, requiring issuance and transmission through registered dematerialization platforms (PDPs) or the public Portal, with e-reporting of cross-border and B2C transaction data to the DGFiP via API. B2G invoices must be submitted to Chorus Pro. For retail cash operations, certified cash register software (logiciel de caisse) per Article 286 CGI must resist falsification; no real-time fiscal stamping is currently applied to general B2B VAT invoices.

Fraudulent or inadequately documented export zero-rating is subject to retrospective VAT reassessment (rappels), a 40% penalty for substantial inaccuracies or bad faith (or 80% in cases of concealed activity or abusive manoeuvres), and late-payment interest of 0.20% per month. Criminal exposure includes fines up to EUR 37,500 and imprisonment for tax fraud; the appointed tax representative is jointly and severally liable for VAT, interest, and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible with both capital and current account operations liberalised under the EU Treaty and the French Monetary and Financial Code. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and liquidation proceeds are freely transferable to non-residents, subject only to domestic withholding tax obligations and statistical balance-of-payments reporting. There is no exchange control permission or surrender requirement for ordinary inbound or outbound trade and investment flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no statutory timeline for conversion into local currency. Export receipts may be held in foreign currency or euro indefinitely on the books of a French bank, must be collected through a regulated banking channel, and must be reported as part of the monthly or quarterly balance-of-payments declaration filed with the Banque de France under the 'Problematic' statistical regime.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France / Direction Générale du Trésor

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High viability for a low-risk professional testing laboratory, but subject to strict legal limitations. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) may support payment initiation and FX conversion services only if they are authorised in the European Economic Area and pass the French client review. They cannot operate as a deposit-taking credit institution, cannot provide the capital escrow guarantee expected by counterparties, and are not acceptable as the sole banking relation for the corporate entity. A French Tier-1 bank account or a bank account in a correspondent French clearing participant remains mandatory for statutory treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are executed via standard SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer using the company's French IBAN. Recommended purpose codes include S301 for equity contribution, S010 for professional fees, and S018 for testing and analysis services. Verification must include targeted financial sanctions screening against French, EU, and UN lists, confirmation of the absence of a false positive on the beneficial owner, checks of the congruence between the payment reference and the signed testing service agreement, and collection of a certified copy of the VAT registration certificate for cross-border service receipts.Outbound trade settlement uses SEPA Credit Transfer within the SEPA framework, SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA jurisdictions, and commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT707) for battery sample or calibration equipment procurement. The French bank must be granted a standing settlement instruction to convert invoices denominated in USD or CNY into euro, and all outbound wires must include the France's balance-of-payments reporting code to avoid regulatory rejection.Outright FX forwards for currencies freely traded in the European market, primarily USD, GBP, and CNYNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or low-liquidity emerging-market currencies when bilateral deliverability is not availableCross-currency interest rate swaps for material multi-year testing contracts with significant deferred settlement riskPlain vanilla European FX options for protecting a defined future cash-flow window without margin accounting complexity

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax (Article 119 bis 2 CGI) on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders outside the EU/EEA or not covered by an applicable treaty; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if the parent holds more than 10% of capital for at least two years.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal years ending 31 December, the return is due by the second working day after 1 May of the following year. For any other fiscal year end, the return is due within three months of the closing date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400,000,000 (French Local File and Master File obligation threshold: annual group turnover or gross assets exceeding €400 million; CbCR threshold €750 million group consolidated revenue)

Economic substance scope: ESR (Economic Substance Regulations) are a UAE-specific instrument and are not directly applicable in France. France applies substance-over-form through the general anti-abuse rule in Article L.64 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales (LPF), complemented by BEPS/civil law jurisprudence. For a French SARL engaged as a dangerous-goods testing laboratory, treaty benefits require actual personnel, premises, and decision-making in France. The absence of these factors gives the tax authorities power to recharacterize the SARL as a shell and attribute profits to a shareholder's country.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude fiscale

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI, fraud is punishable by up to five years' imprisonment (seven years when carried out through an intermediary established outside the EU) and a fine of €500,000 (or, if greater, 75% of the evaded tax). Tax penalties: 80% of the unpaid tax plus 0.20% per month late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: The managing director and de facto directors can be pursued under Article L.267 LPF for payment of the company's tax debts where they have been actively involved in fraudulent activities, with personal bank accounts frozen, travel bans, and imprisonment upon criminal conviction.
Artificial Profit Shifting and Transfer Pricing Abuses

Statutory sanction: Under Article L.13 AA LPF and Article 57 CGI, the DGFiP can re-determine arm's-length profit and impose a penalty of 100% of the redistributed profits if the enterprise cannot substantiate reasonableness (or 50% if failure to respond to a demand for TP documentation). Abuse-of-right penalty may reach 80% under Article 1729 CGI.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP documentation or act as intermediaries in a circular scheme can be held jointly liable for the tax assessed and exposed to a commercial court's 'action en comblement de passif' for managerial fault.
Shell Company / Sham Substance / Abus de Droit

Statutory sanction: The DGFiP may declare the arrangement an 'abus de droit' under Article L.64 LPF, disregard the scheme, and restore the true taxable base; the taxpayer faces an 80% anti-abuse penalty and a reopening of the statute of limitations to six years; CIR claims may be denied with interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly establishes a sham arrangement is personally liable for the reinstated taxes through abuse-of-right procedure; travel bans and director disqualification can be imposed in conjunction with criminal prosecution for organised fraud.
AML/CTF and Export-Control Related Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Violation of Articles L.561-2 and L.562-1 CMF can result in administrative sanctions of up to €4 million or 10% of consolidated turnover for the entity; criminal money laundering (Article 324-1 Penal Code) carries up to ten years' imprisonment and €750,000 fine, with 50% additional confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly encourage or facilitate such activities face personal criminal liability, civil restitution for unjust enrichment, a travel ban pending prosecution, and a prohibition on managing commercial companies for five years or more.
