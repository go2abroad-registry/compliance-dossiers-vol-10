Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

INPI – Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), transmitting to the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce territorialement compétent for the registered office (in Paris: Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce de Paris), which maintains the RCS file and issues the K-bis extract.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title II, Chapter VII, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); general formation rules under Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10; implementing decrees R.227-1 to R.227-3; incorporation formalities governed by the unified 'Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises' regime introduced by the 2021 French single-window reform.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic 'Déclaration de constitution de société' (replacing the historical M0 form) submitted via the Guichet unique, together with the fully executed Statuts (Articles of Association) and all statutorily annexed certificates.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital: the amount is freely fixed in the Statuts (Eur 1,000 is commonly adopted for commercial credibility). Cash contributions must be paid up to at least 50% of nominal value before registration, with the balance due within five years. Subscription funds must be deposited in a blocked account with a French bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and are released only against the RCS registration certificate (K-bis).

Funding & unlock path: Prior to operation the company must secure: (i) building legality certification against the local urban-planning code (production of the building permit or prior-notification attestation to the Mairie); (ii) fire-safety conformity with the Règlement de sécurité contre l'incendie and the Labor Code, covering emergency exits, fire extinguishers, alarm systems and storage of flammable inks and foils in a dedicated flammable-liquid cabinet; (iii) a workplace health-and-safety clearance comprising the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUER) and the roll-out of occupational-health medical surveillance for the President and all employees; and (iv) where visitors have access, compliance of the premises with applicable ERP fire-safety classifications. No separate civil-defense license is mandated for this activity.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital: the amount is freely fixed in the Statuts (Eur 1,000 is commonly adopted for commercial credibility). Cash contributions must be paid up to at least 50% of nominal value before registration, with the balance due within five years. Subscription funds must be deposited in a blocked account with a French bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and are released only against the RCS registration certificate (K-bis).

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Préfecture du département (ICPE declaration or registration under Code de l'environnement) supported by DREAL (Direction Régionale de l'Environnement, de l'Aménagement et du Logement); occupational safety and workplace control exercised by DREETS/DRIEETS (Direction Régionale de l'Économie, de l'Emploi, du Travail et des Solidarités).

Category: No statutory operating or manufacturing permit is required for the printing and publishing of collectible trading cards under French law; a regulated environmental filing (ICPE declaration or registration under Rubrique 2450 of the nomenclature des installations classées) applies only if the installation's organic-solvent consumption from inks, varnishes, adhesives or cleaning agents reaches the regulatory thresholds.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique – Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); official portals are impots.gouv.fr and guichet-unique.entreprises.gouv.fr (INPI).

E-government filing portal

An SAS formed under French law is first registered through the INPI guichet unique, which generates the SIREN/SIRET identifiers and the APE/NAF code at the Registre National des Entreprises. The company then applies to its territorial SIE on form 1603-SD before undertaking taxable transactions in order to obtain the French intra-Community VAT number. A foreign entity without a French establishment that makes taxable supplies in France registers directly with DGFIP/SIE using the same form 1603-SD; a non-EU-established taxable person generally must appoint an accredited French tax representative (représentant fiscal) under CGI Article 289-A. The registration application must include constitutional documents, proof of foreign establishment, a power of attorney and a description of the intended French economic activity. An EU seller may instead use the One Stop Shop for qualifying distance sales of goods, but any taxable activity requiring a localized French VAT identity requires the 1603-SD procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a French-resident SAS is a separate taxable person subject by default to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on its profits; no pass-through default applies.

Available election forms

France does not have a US-style check-the-box election. Relevant elections for an SAS and French groups include: (i) election for fiscal integration (régime d'intégration fiscale) by a 95%-owned French group; (ii) option for a single VAT taxable person grouping (assujettissement unique) for closely linked entities; (iii) application to DGFIP for a different accounting/fiscal year end; and (iv) the parent-subsidiary regime (régime mère-fille) for dividend exemption where applicable. Hybrid and reverse-hybrid outcomes are governed by mandatory anti-hybrid rules implementing ATAD; there is no elective hybrid mismatch check-the-box item, and reportable cross-border arrangements must be disclosed under DAC6.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable to a French-established SAS: VAT registration and filing are compulsory from inception and from the first taxable transaction. For non-established EU sellers of goods to French consumers, the EU-wide distance-selling threshold of EUR 10,000 applies; taxable persons already established in France cannot use that threshold. Non-EU sellers must register before making any taxable French supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of collectible trading cards to a destination outside the EU is exempt from French VAT with credit for input VAT (effectively 0%) under CGI Articles 262-I-1° and 269, provided the goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU by or on behalf of the seller and the seller holds documentary proof of export. Directly connected services, such as packing, handling and shipping of the exported goods, may also be zero-rated under Article 262-I-2°. Services supplied to non-EU customers are outside the French VAT base under B2B place-of-supply rules (Article 259) when the customer is outside the EU. Intra-EU supplies of goods are not zero-rated but are exempt under Article 262-ter if the customer provides a valid EU VAT ID and the supplier files a recapitulative statement; e-commerce distance sales may fall under OSS/IOSS where applicable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation/DAU) lodged through the DELTA-X/UX customs system with the export accompanying document
  • External transport evidence (Bill of Lading, CMR, Air Waybill, or postal/express consignment note showing dispatch outside the EU)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103/bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the export proceeds
  • Proof of non-EU arrival/clearance where available (port release, customs entry, or signed proof of delivery copy)

France is implementing mandatory structured e-invoicing and e-reporting. From 1 September 2026 all established taxable persons must be able to receive electronic invoices and large/medium enterprises must issue them; from 1 September 2027 all businesses, including SMEs/micro-enterprises, must issue electronic invoices and transmit transaction and payment data. Invoices must be issued in an EN 16931-conformant structured format (Factur-X, UBL or CII) and exchanged through a certified dematerialization partner (PDP) or the DGFIP public portal (PPF) in the 4-corner model. Transaction and payment data are reported to DGFIP through the PPF/PDP API. France does not presently operate a real-time inline fiscal stamp or clearance engine; no digital stamping is required.

Export zero-rating claims are closely scrutinised by DGFIP during a vérification de comptabilité. If the exporter fails to produce the required customs, transport and payment evidence, or if the goods are diverted within the EU, the VAT becomes lawfully due and is clawed back with statutory late-payment interest and the 40% bad-faith penalty (manquement délibéré, CGI Article 1729) or the 80% penalty for fraudulent concealment (activité occulte), in addition to denial or reversal of input VAT credits and refunds. Criminal tax evasion under CGI Article 1741 can be prosecuted, carrying imprisonment up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000; legal representatives and directors may be jointly liable in cases of serious misconduct in managing the SAS.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is part of the euro area and applies European capital movement rules. Current account and capital account transactions are broadly free of exchange controls for both residents and non-residents. The euro is fully convertible. Profit remittance requires no prior administrative authorization; dividends paid by a French SAS may be subject to withholding tax under national law (30% default) unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty or exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when paid to a qualifying EU parent. Outbound direct investment is unrestricted for EU counterparties; investment by non-EU parties in the collectible card printing sector (ISIC 3240) is not in the restricted sectors list and only requires ex post reporting to the Banque de France for statistical purposes. No exchange control approval is necessary for imports or exports of goods or for service payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not maintain mandatory export proceeds surrender or forced conversion timelines. Export receipts, whether in euros or foreign currencies, may be retained in commercial foreign currency accounts, converted freely at market rates, or repatriated without an obligatory deadline. However, for balance-of-payments statistics, French resident companies must declare direct investment and significant cross-border flows (including share capital and loans exceeding certain thresholds) to the Banque de France. The Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier) requires truthful economic declarations for cross-border transfers, and funds from exports must be booked in accordance with standard invoice settlement timelines between related parties.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (Treasury) under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; cross-border statistical reporting and balance-of-payments registration are administered by the Banque de France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI (electronic money institution) accounts are legally viable in France when the provider is licensed by the ACPR or passporting under PSD2/EMD2 into France. Such accounts can support operational payments, collections, and FX conversion, and connect to SEPA, SWIFT, and card rails. However, EMIs are not suitable for the mandatory share-capital escrow account required before formation of a SAS, which must be held with a bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations. EMIs also typically cannot issue documentary credits (letters of credit) or provide committed credit facilities. Feasibility for transaction banking: Medium-High for collectible-card publishing collections and supplier payouts; Low for full treasury/liquidity clearing and trade finance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial SAS share capital, funds must be deposited in a French bank blocked account (compte bloqué) or with a notary/share issue escrow. The transfer is initiated from the shareholder's bank by SWIFT MT103 using purpose code 'G51' or equivalent equity-related code (e.g., outward direct investment by the sender's jurisdiction); for payments from within the EU, SEPA Transfer with regulation reference 'QUOS' may be used. French exchange control law does not require pre-authorization for non-resident equity investment in this hard-goods printing sector. After incorporation, the bank releases the credited amount to the business operating account on presentation of the registration certificate (Kbis). For ongoing capital contributions and loans, the routing should be via direct bank credit transfer with clear remittance narrative referencing the share capital or shareholder loan agreement; Banque de France balance-of-payments declarations (procedural 'DPU 1500' or the 'ex post' reporting) are required for direct investment flows above applicable thresholds or as otherwise instructed.Trade settlements to international suppliers and service providers are executed via SEPA for euro-denominated payments within the EEA, and via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border/foreign-currency payments. For large raw-material purchases or machinery imports from Asia, France-incorporated entities commonly use documentary collection or irrevocable letters of credit (commercial LC, MT700 issue/MT707 amendment, MT710/MT720 confirmations) issued through the corporate bank, particularly when the supplier requires title documents, bill of lading, packing list, commercial invoice, certificate of origin, and customs transit confirmation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard CIT; 15% reduced rate on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit per full fiscal period for eligible SMEs with annual turnover below EUR 10 million

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% under domestic law (Article 119 bis CGI) on gross dividends paid by a French SAS to non-resident corporate shareholders; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the EU/EEA parent meets the ≥10% holding and two-year holding period; treaty rates commonly 0%, 5% or 15% depending on percentage of capital held.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For fiscal year ending 31 December, filing due by 3 May of the following year (administrative practice); for any other closing date, the return must be lodged within three months of the fiscal year-end. Quarterly CIT advance payments are due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation applies where the French entity belongs to a group whose consolidated annual turnover or gross assets exceed EUR 400 million (Article L.13 AA LPF). Members of such a group must prepare and file a Master File and a French Local File. Entities with fewer than 250 employees and either turnover not exceeding EUR 50 million or total assets not exceeding EUR 43 million are ordinarily exempt from formal documentation obligations unless transacting with non-cooperative jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substance requirements are embedded in the French General Anti-Abuse Rule (abus de droit, Article L.64 LPF), the 'acte anormal de gestion' doctrine, and the OECD BEPS minimum standards. A French SAS must evidence real local substance: actual premises, employees, local management and business decisions. A pure letterbox company or an intermediary with no economic function is exposed to recharacterisation, profit adjustment, and anti-avoidance penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: 40% to 80% penalty on the underpaid tax plus 0.20% monthly late interest; criminal conviction under Article 1741 CGI carries imprisonment up to five years and a fine up to EUR 500,000, increased to seven years and EUR 750,000 in aggravated circumstances.

Director personal liability: A director may be held personally liable for the company's tax debts where fraudulent intent is established; the court may impose a professional ban, a prohibition on managing a French company, travel bans (interdiction du territoire français), and seizure of personal assets.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: The French tax administration may disregard the artificial entity, recharacterise the transactions, reassess income at the level of the real taxpayer, and impose an 80% penalty for abuse of law in cases of fraud or sham, together with late interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur 'action en comblement du passif' under Article L.651-2 of the Commercial Code if the shell's insolvency results from their mismanagement; criminal liability for tax evasion may also be asserted personally, with potential imprisonment and travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Fine of EUR 30,000 for non-communication of the documentation, plus a surcharge of 5% of the undocumented transactions; reassessment of taxable profits at arm's length and an additional 40% penalty for bad-faith withholding of documents.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the distorted pricing may be jointly liable for the corporate tax arrears and penalties in cases of intentional fault; criminal prosecution for tax evasion may lead to personal fines and disqualification from management.
Beneficial Ownership / AML Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine for the company and criminal fines for the president or manager; failure to declare beneficial owners can trigger a fine of up to EUR 15,000 and imprisonment of up to six months; anti-money laundering violations may attract additional penalties under the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failure to rectify the beneficial ownership register; in severe money laundering cases, travel bans and imprisonment up to five years are possible, and the court may order the company to be wound up or the director to be barred from managing any French business.
