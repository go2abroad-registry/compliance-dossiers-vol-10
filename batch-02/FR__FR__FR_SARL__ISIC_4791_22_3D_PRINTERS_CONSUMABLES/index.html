Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the impots.gouv.fr professional portal. The Institut national de la statistique et des études économiques (INSEE) assigns the SIREN after company formation; the SIE assigns or confirms the French VAT number.

E-government filing portal

Form the SARL through the formalities portal operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), which transmits the registration data to the relevant authorities. INSEE assigns a SIREN and establishment-level SIRET. Register the company with the competent SIE and request a VAT number before making transactions for which VAT registration is required; the number is generally FR plus a two-digit key and the SIREN. A foreign company without a French establishment must apply to the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) for VAT registration when required. A non-EU-established applicant may be required to appoint a French fiscal representative, subject to applicable statutory exceptions and mutual-assistance arrangements. Obtain an EORI number separately from customs when required for customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). An eligible SARL may elect the temporary partnership-taxation regime under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code, generally for up to five fiscal years, if statutory conditions are met. A qualifying family SARL may elect income-tax treatment under Article 239 bis AA, subject to continuing eligibility. France has no US-style check-the-box election. Hybrid-mismatch rules apply under French law and EU implementation; they do not provide a general classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 annual turnover for domestic supplies of goods under the French small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base), subject to statutory rules and applicable current-year thresholds; EUR 37,500 applies to services. These are exemption thresholds, not a general VAT-registration safe harbor. A business may opt for VAT, and a non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in France generally must register from the first relevant transaction. Cross-border distance sales and EU-wide small-business rules have separate conditions and thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the EU may be exempt with the right to deduct input VAT, provided the statutory export conditions are met and the seller can substantiate that the goods left the EU. Intra-EU supplies are exempt with right to deduct only where the applicable conditions, including customer VAT status, transport evidence, and reporting, are satisfied. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer or payment is offshore: place-of-supply rules determine whether French VAT applies, and the recipient's status and service type matter.

  • Export customs declaration and MRN, together with customs confirmation of exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and sales order identifying the goods, buyer, destination, and VAT treatment
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, carrier receipt, or other reliable transport evidence
  • Proof of payment or accounting records where relevant; no inward-remittance certificate is generally required under French law

France is not generally a real-time invoice-clearance jurisdiction. Domestic B2B e-invoicing and transaction/payment e-reporting are being phased in: all in-scope businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance begins on that date for large and intermediate-sized companies and on 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises. In-scope invoices must use the prescribed formats and transmission channels through an approved private platform (plateforme agréée); the DGFiP public portal provides directory and data functions. Consumer and cross-border transactions are generally subject to e-reporting rather than domestic B2B e-invoicing. No general digital tax-stamping or pre-clearance requirement applies to ordinary retail invoices.

France imposes no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The exporter must retain evidence supporting export exemption and input-tax deduction. If export or other exemption conditions are not substantiated, the DGFiP may assess output VAT, interest for late payment, and applicable penalties. Deliberate evasion or fraudulent documentation can trigger aggravated tax penalties and, where statutory elements are met, criminal proceedings. The specific assessment, penalty, and limitation period depend on the facts and applicable provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full convertibility for both capital and current account transactions within the EU single market framework; no general exchange control restrictions on euro-denominated flows. Profit remittances by a French SARL to non-resident shareholders are freely permitted subject to (i) corporate income tax of 25% on realised profits, (ii) dividend withholding tax at 25% or reduced treaty rate (e.g. 15% under FR-China DTA, 0% if EU parent directive applies), and (iii) the absence of thin capitalisation or anti-abuse recharacterisation under article 212 of the French General Tax Code (CGI). Payment incidental to current transactions is unrestricted; however, the non-resident exporter must obtain a SIREN/SIRET business identification number and a valid bank account in the SEPA zone to receive proceeds via the standard SEPA Credit Transfer or SWIFT MT103. Any payment from a French bank account requires a purpose code or narrative enabling the French bank to satisfy anti-money laundering Article L561-2 of the Monetary and Financial Code. No administrative authorisation is required for routine trade settlement below EUR 150,000; cross-border flows above EUR 10,000 trigger automatic reporting to DG Tresor for balance-of-payments statistics but do not constitute control or require prior approval unless involving sanctioned jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose export proceeds surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios. Export receipts may be freely retained in EUR or a foreign currency account; nevertheless, French resident companies are obliged to declare cross-border payments exceeding EUR 10,000 to the Banque de France via the 'Rapport de Flux' electronic filing for balance-of-payments purposes within 20 business days of the transaction month-end. The SARL may designate an authorised intermediary bank to execute this declaration. No forced conversion to EUR and no deferred receipt period are applied; invoicing is permitted in any commercial currency, but the functional currency of accounts remains EUR for statutory corporate income tax filing.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) and Ministere de l'Economie, des Finances et de la Relance (Tresor Public), with DG Tresor serving as the Foreign Exchange Administration authority under EU Capital Movements Directive and French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monetaire et financier)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for day-to-day treasury operations; Medium for principal banking relationship. French Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL) may open accounts with EU-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the PSD2 passporting regime, including entities such as Wise Europe SA, Ebury Partners SAS, Qonto SAS (licensed as établissement de paiement), and Revolut Ltd (passported into France). Such accounts are permissible for liquidity management, inbound capital receipt from foreign shareholders, and e-commerce trade settlement. However, EMIs do not possess a full banking licence and therefore cannot provide credit lines, letters of credit, or commercial documentary collections. For the receipt of proceeds from Stripe and Shopify Payments, a SEPA account at an EMI is sufficient, but the treasury blueprint recommends a Tier 1 bank anchor account to manage regulatory risks and to secure future factoring or trade finance facilities. French AML/KYC imposes a full identification of the beneficial owner and the physical address of the registered office; EMIs may accept remote onboarding via video identification, but the bank account name must exactly match the legal entity name of the French SARL.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant processing of the initial shareholder capital subscription and top-up working capital contributions to the French SARL should be executed via SWIFT MT103 with mandatory purpose codes. Recommended message narrative: '/REC/CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION SHARE SUBSCRIPTION SARL FRANCE/ISO COV:TDWP'. Use purpose code (i) 'CIRCLE' or the EU Balance of Payments classification under code 'N99' for capital transfers, or (ii) 'S010' when sourced from direct investment. The remitting bank must reference the French corporate identifier Kbis, VAT number FRXXXXXXXXX, and the bank account IBAN of the SARL. For any inbound remittance exceeding EUR 150,000 the French receiving bank will file the balanced-payments declaration (Rapport de Flux) to DG Tresor; the sending entity must transfer only after the notarial deed or board resolution approving the capital increase has been recorded at the Greffe. Where the parent is outside the EU, the inbound capital is exempt from French withholding tax if recorded as share capital in the category of capital increases; verification of anti-money laundering requires an affidavit of source of funds and a copy of the parent's bank debit advice. The clearing rail prioritises the EUR SEPA Express Instant for amounts below EUR 100,000 (TARGET Instant Payment Settlement) or the SWIFT standard for larger wires.Outbound trade disbursements for purchase of 3D printer consumables (resin, filaments, spare parts) should settle through (i) SWIFT MT103 direct credit for commercial invoices above EUR 50,000, (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Business-to-Business Direct Debit for intra-EU suppliers, and (iii) Commercial Letter of Credit (Sight MT700) for Asia-based manufacturers if payment in advance risk is unacceptable. An ordinary EUR-denominated MT103 from the French SARL's BNP Paribas account to a Chinese supplier via Bank of China is the standard, with the instruction note 'TRADE SETTLEMENT-3D PRINTER CONSUMABLES-PURCHASE INVOICE NO.XXXX-UNRESTRICTED' and the mandatory purpose code 'G000' (general trade). No French exchange control approval is required for imports; however, the importer must keep the commercial invoice, the AWB/bill of lading, and the customs clearance declaration (Import One Stop Shop if applicable for EU B2C flows) for 10 years. For high-value commercial LC, the beneficiary presents documents at the advising bank in the seller's country, which triggers the reimbursement through the SWIFT MT754/MT756 cycle. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High for day-to-day treasury operations; Medium for principal banking relationship. French Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL) may open accounts with EU-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the PSD2 passporting regime, including entities such as Wise Europe SA, Ebury Partners SAS, Qonto SAS (licensed as établissement de paiement), and Revolut Ltd (passported into France). Such accounts are permissible for liquidity management, inbound capital receipt from foreign shareholders, and e-commerce trade settlement. However, EMIs do not possess a full banking licence and therefore cannot provide credit lines, letters of credit, or commercial documentary collections. For the receipt of proceeds from Stripe and Shopify Payments, a SEPA account at an EMI is sufficient, but the treasury blueprint recommends a Tier 1 bank anchor account to manage regulatory risks and to secure future factoring or trade finance facilities. French AML/KYC imposes a full identification of the beneficial owner and the physical address of the registered office; EMIs may accept remote onboarding via video identification, but the bank account name must exactly match the legal entity name of the French SARL.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

French domestic law applies a 25% withholding tax to dividends paid to a non-resident corporate shareholder and 30% for a non-resident individual, unless reduced or eliminated by an applicable double tax treaty. Under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive the French withholding tax is eliminated where the parent holds directly at least 10% of the SARL's capital and has held that interest for 24 months. The standard rate may be increased to 75% where the shareholder is established in a non-cooperative jurisdiction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For any accounting year ending on 31 December, the 2065-SD annual return must be filed no later than the second working day after 1 May of the following year. For an accounting year ending on any other date, the return is due within 3 months of the end of the accounting period. Instalments are due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December during the fiscal year; the final balance is due with the annual return.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Advance instalments for the current fiscal year are due 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December. The final settlement of the balance is due at the time of the annual 2065-SD return: second working day after 1 May for 31 December year-ends, otherwise 3 months after year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million annual turnover or balance-sheet assets threshold. A French SARL is required to prepare the full Local File and Master File where the SARL or the parent group exceeds EUR 400 million in turnover or assets, or where the SARL is controlled, directly or indirectly, by a non-resident enterprise exceeding that threshold. Even below the threshold, the SARL must maintain transfer-pricing evidence in its accounting records sufficient to refute a DGFiP recharacterisation of cross-border transactions under Article 57 CGI.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code comparable to UAE or Caribbean regimes. Equivalent substance requirements arise from the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD), the OECD BEPS Action 6 treaty anti-abuse rules, the French abuse-of-law doctrine under Article L64 LPF, and Article 155-A CGI anti-avoidance provisions. The SARL must demonstrate genuine commercial activity, physical or e-commerce operational presence, actual decision-making in France, adequate employees and commercial risk-bearing. A French SARL used as a pure conduit that invoices foreign sales, retains no operational substance, and remits profits to an artificial offshore parent will be treated as a shell, the DTA benefit will be denied, and the managing director may be personally liable.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Concealment of Turnover

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 80% of the evaded tax for fraudulent manoeuvres, plus 0.20% monthly late-payment interest. Criminal tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI is punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 500,000, increased to 7 years and EUR 2,000,000 where committed as an organised gang. Tax authorities may seize bank accounts and assets via conservatory attachment.

Director personal liability: The gérant (managing director) and any de facto manager who knowingly signs false returns can be criminally prosecuted, barred from managing a commercial company for up to 10 years, and ordered to pay fines personally. Where the SARL is insolvent and mismanagement caused the inability to settle tax debts, the commercial court may order the director to contribute to the company's liabilities under the action en comblement de passif.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The DGFiP may invoke the abuse-of-law procedure (Article L64 LPF), disregard the SARL's corporate separateness, recharacterise the profits as belonging to the ultimate non-resident beneficiary, and assess additional corporate tax, 25-30% withholding tax, 80% fraud penalty where bad faith is proved, and late-payment interest. The court may also refuse treaty or Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and shareholders participating in the artificial arrangement may be held jointly and severally liable for the tax and penalties. Directors can face criminal conviction for laundering the proceeds of tax evasion; personal asset confiscation and travel bans may be imposed pending criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under Article 57 CGI the DGFiP can reassess profits by reference to the arm's-length standard and impose a 40% penalty for intentional breach or 80% for abusive manoeuvres, plus monthly late-payment interest. Failure to produce transfer-pricing documentation may result in a specific fine and adverse evidentiary presumption, shifting the burden of proof to the SARL.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign statements containing artificial transfer prices may be personally assessed for penalties and interest if they acted as de facto managers. In a criminal tax-fraud case, the court may prohibit the director from exercising commercial functions and order confiscation of personal assets.
Money Laundering and AML Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Money laundering (Article 324-1 Code pénal) is punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 375,000; habitual laundering carries 10 years' imprisonment and a EUR 750,000 fine. The court may also order temporary closure of the business and forfeiture of seized assets.

Director personal liability: The gérant is criminally liable if the SARL is used as a vehicle for laundering proceeds of tax fraud. A director who fails to prevent or knowingly facilitates such activity may be sentenced personally, removed from corporate management, and made liable for asset restitution. Even at a Low_Risk_Standard AML tier, proper customer due diligence and transparent payment-rail records are mandatory.
