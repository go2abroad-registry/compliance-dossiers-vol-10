Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry) maintaining the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), accessible exclusively through the INPI-operated Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises portal.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), SARL provisions at Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 et seq.; single-window filing mechanism under Articles L.123-31 to L.123-35-4 and Decree No. 2021-1488; tax classification under Articles 206 and 239 bis AA of the French General Tax Code (impôt sur les sociétés).

Formation / constitutional filings

Single electronic formalities dossier ('dossier de création d'entreprise') filed via formalites.entreprises.service-public.fr, comprising the signed French-language 'statuts' (Articles of Association) and all statutory attachments; legal personality is conferred by the entry in the RCS (immatriculation) evidenced by the Extrait Kbis.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SARL; the Articles fix the capital freely, commonly EUR 10,000-15,000. All cash contributions must be fully paid on subscription (Art. L.223-7 Code de commerce) and deposited in a blocked escrow account at a French credit institution or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; funds are released only after RCS registration. In-kind contributions must be delivered in full at signing and, where their aggregate value exceeds EUR 30,000 or more than half of the capital, must be valued by a commissaire aux apports.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique; primary taxpayer portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; unified company-registration gateway (Guichet Unique): formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated SARL, the legal representative files the single integrated incorporation dossier via the Guichet Unique (formulaire Cerfa n°11679), which triggers automatic issuance by INSEE and DGFiP of: (1) SIREN/SIRET identification numbers, (2) the intra-Community VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire), and (3) the non-consolidated corporate income tax (IS) account number. Foreign entities without a French permanent establishment that must self-assess French VAT on domestic or OSS supplies must file a simplified VAT registration form (Cerfa n°15970) with the competent SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises); if established outside the EU, they must appoint a fiscal representative who is jointly and severally liable for the VAT (Article 289-A CGI). Standard DGFiP processing time is 1 to 2 weeks for EU applicants and up to 4 weeks for non-EU applicants.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a standard commercial SARL is by default within the scope of corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on its worldwide profits subject to French treaty override, with a territorial limitation for foreign-source business income where the company has no French-linked permanent establishment (Article 209-I CGI). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a standard commercial SARL is by default within the scope of corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on its worldwide profits subject to French treaty override, with a territorial limitation for foreign-source business income where the company has no French-linked permanent establishment (Article 209-I CGI). The entity remains separately subject to VAT as an established taxable person regardless of IS filing elections.

Available election forms

(a) Pass-through option: a qualifying close-held SARL whose equity is held exclusively by natural persons and whose turnover is not excessive may elect under Article 239 bis AA CGI to be taxed as an income-tax transparent company (option pour le régime des sociétés de personnes) for a maximum of five accounting periods, with reversal to IS automatic thereafter; (b) Tax consolidation: a 95%+ French group may elect to apply the French fiscal-integration regime (Articles 223 A to 223 U CGI) to aggregate results; (c) Hybrid mismatch alignment: France has implemented ATAD 1 via Articles 212 bis and 212 ter CGI; an entity with hybrid features must report and align deductions with the 2016/1164/EU directives, and a 'PE mismatch' declaration is statutorily required under Article 209 CGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic franchise en base (small-business exemption): VAT registration is optional below EUR 91,900 of annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods and EUR 36,800 for supplies of services (including virtual event ticketing); registration becomes mandatory above these thresholds. For intra-EU distance sales of electronic/digital services to private consumers, the OSS scheme triggers at EUR 10,000 of EU-wide cross-border digital revenue; the SARL may nevertheless voluntary register at any turnover level to enable input-VAT deduction, which is strongly recommended in an export-oriented metaverse business.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(i) Physical goods (e.g., merchandising ancillary to the concert) exported outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Article 262-I-1 CGI if shipped and evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration; (ii) Virtual-event services and digital ticket tokenization: the supply is OUTSIDE French VAT (effectively 0%) when provided to a business customer established outside France or outside the EU (place-of-supply rule of Articles 259-1 and 259-1-2° CGI, with reverse charge for EU business customers holding a valid intra-Community VAT number) and when provided to a final consumer whose residence is outside the EU (Article 259-1-3° CGI); (iii) B2C supplies to private consumers established in other EU Member States are NOT French zero-rated but are taxable in the customer's Member State via the OSS scheme at the destination rate, which the SARL must charge and remit through OSS, or be deemed supplied by the platform under the 2021 e-commerce VAT package if sale occurs through an electronic interface.

  • For physical exports: accepted EU Customs Export Declaration (DELTA/EUA clearance), Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, commercial invoice and packing list, transport contract (CMR/IATA/courier), and incoterms-based proof of export completion.
  • For virtual services and tokenized tickets: signed service contract or platform terms evidencing place of supply of the metaverse concert, validated intra-Community VAT number (VIES check) or foreign registration certificate of the B2B customer, KYC record of the B2C customer's residence country, proof of payment via SWIFT/SEPA or on-chain USDC transaction record with its crypto-asset exchange reference, and the OSS declaration substantiation file if the destination-state rate is applied.
  • Intercompany evidence (if a non-resident group entity is involved): intercompany agreement plus transfer-pricing documentation demonstrating the economic substance and remuneration of the French SARL in relation to the virtual-event hosting services.

Yes — France has mandated nationwide structured e-invoicing (Factur-X/UBL/CII) and e-reporting under Articles 289 bis and 232 nonies CGI, with clearance through the public portal PPF (Portail Public de Facturation) or a licensed PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire). Phased rollout: from 1 September 2026 all enterprises must be able to receive French B2B e-invoices and large/mid-size enterprises must issue e-invoices; from 1 September 2027 small enterprises must also issue. Daily e-reporting of all B2C digital-service transactions (including tokenized ticket sales and metaverse access paid by USDC) is required through the PPF/PDP API, including transaction-level data, counterparty identification and payment-evidence hashes; failure to transmit triggers penalty under Article 1737 CGI. Stripe and stablecoin rails must therefore produce structured, API-transmissible transaction records that match the OSS or e-reporting file format.

French tax law contains no separate 180-day statutory FX-repatriation clock; the 180-day benchmark reflects ordinary commercial credit terms and prudent management of EUR/USDC treasury. However, misuse of the 0% rate constitutes misdeclaration exposing the SARL to DGFiP reassessment within the standard three-year control period (six years in case of fraud) under Article L.176 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales, plus late-payment interest of 0.20% per month; a deliberate omission triggers a 40% penalty (Article 1729 CGI), while abusive schemes, organised tax fraud or a fictitious export qualify for the 80% penalty with possible criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI (up to five years imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine). For cross-border digital services, failure to register and remit under OSS, or applying French 0% to intra-EU B2C sales of virtual concerts, will additionally trigger destination-State penalties and the suspension of the SARL's intra-Community VAT number by DGFiP.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a euro-area Member State, maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account operations. The euro is freely transferable and no exchange-control approval is required for profit remittances from a French SARL to foreign shareholders. Remittances are subject only to statutory tax withholding (if any under double-tax treaties), anti-money-laundering / know-your-customer (AML/KYC) documentation, and statistical balance-of-payments reporting obligations to Banque de France. There are no capital repatriation authorisations for ordinary dividends or liquidation proceeds, though banks may request evidence of the underlying corporate decision and the legality of the funds source.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in domestic or foreign bank accounts and are not required to convert foreign currency into EUR within any timeline. Under Article R. 152-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code, cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 50,000 (or the applicable carrier threshold) must be reported for balance-of-payments statistics. There is no statutory conversion ratio and no minimum repatriation percentage for virtual-concert ticket sales or digital event licences.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France acting within the French Ministry of Economy and Finance's exchange-control framework; balance-of-payments reporting is supervised by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) pursuant to the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for daily treasury, platform payouts, and EUR/SEPA settlement; Medium for inbound foreign-equity escrow and capital injection. French and EEA Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed under the EU Payment Services Directive 2 (PSD2) may provide cross-border payment accounts without a full banking licence. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot take deposits or grant credit; they are permitted solely for issuing electronic money and executing payment transactions. Escrow of share capital contributions is generally restricted to credit institutions because of capital-protection requirements under French company law. Cryptocurrency-fiat gateways (e.g. USDC on-exchange conversions) are not treated as banking services and trigger additional AML/CTF controls under the AMF and TRACFIN reporting rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity contributions from the foreign parent to the French SARL must be executed via SWIFT MT103 or a SEPA transfer with the purpose code designated as 'Equity – Direct Investment' under the IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). The payment reference must state 'Apport en numéraire / augmentation de capital' and be supported by the notarial/corporate resolution and a signed capital-subscription certificate. The receiving bank must provide an incoming-transfer record for the Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration (EC3 form) when the amount exceeds EUR 50,000. For shareholder current-account loans, a written loan agreement, arm's-length interest terms, and the French tax reporting form (Creditors and Loans) must be supplied. Wire transfer verification requires dual matching of the sender's IBAN/BIC, the beneficiary French IBAN, and the MT103 field 70 remittance information. Non-euro inbound transfers must be routed through a foreign-exchange conversion at a licensed credit institution. Cryptocurrency (USDC) is not legal tender and is not accepted as a direct capital injection; if treasury funding is converted from USDC, it must first be liquidated to EUR via a licensed crypto-to-fiat exchange and then transmitted to the French bank with a complete source-of-funds audit trail.For operational disbursements and trade settlement from the French SARL: EUR payments within the EEA use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT), with clearing through TARGET2. Non-EEA trade disbursements use SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary details (BIC, IBAN/account number and clearing codes). For virtual-concert event settlements and platform payouts, Stripe Connected Account distributions are routed in EUR/SEPA to the CDD-verified event provider accounts. Commercial letters of credit (L/C) are performed under UCP 600 for physical or digital event merchandise suppliers, with issuance by the French buyer's bank and confirmed by the beneficiary's bank. Outbound amounts must be supported by the underlying contracts, invoices, and any applicable French customs or export-control documentation. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard rate for fiscal years 2022 onwards; reduced rate of 15% applies to the first €42,500 of taxable profit for eligible SMEs with turnover ≤ €10 million)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% (standard withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The corporate income tax return must be filed within 3 months of the fiscal year-end (or 15 days after the 3-month period if the fiscal year ends on December 31, i.e., by May 15 for calendar-year taxpayers). For fiscal years ending other than December 31, the deadline is the 3rd month following the close of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million (annual turnover or total assets) for the entity or the group; entities below this threshold are subject to simplified documentation requirements (e.g., a basic transfer pricing statement) if they have related-party transactions exceeding EUR 50 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE, but the concept of substance is embedded in anti-abuse rules (e.g., the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) under Article L.64 of the French Tax Procedure Code) and the 'substance over form' doctrine. For treaty benefits, the OECD's Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the Multilateral Instrument (MLI) applies, requiring that the main purpose of an arrangement is not to obtain treaty benefits. A French SARL must demonstrate real economic activity (e.g., employees, office, decision-making) to avoid challenges under GAAR and to benefit from treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: up to €500,000 fine and 5 years' imprisonment (increased to €3 million and 7 years for aggravated fraud). Administrative penalties: 80% of the tax evaded, plus interest at 0.20% per month. Publication of the sanction may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the tax debt if they have committed fraud or mismanagement. Civil restitution of the evaded tax, travel bans, and potential imprisonment (up to 5 years) are possible. The corporate veil can be pierced if the SARL is used as a sham to evade taxes.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) and application of the GAAR, leading to recharacterization of transactions and additional tax assessments. Penalties of 80% for abuse of law (abus de droit) and potential criminal charges for tax fraud if intent is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the additional tax and penalties if they knowingly participated in the sham arrangement. They may face civil liability for damages to the state and potential criminal sanctions (imprisonment up to 5 years).
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 5% of annual turnover for non-compliance with AML obligations (under the French Monetary and Financial Code). Criminal penalties for money laundering: up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine (increased for aggravated cases).

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for AML failures if they were aware of or negligently ignored the obligations. They may face criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment. The corporate veil may be pierced if the entity is used as a vehicle for money laundering.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profits by the tax authorities, plus interest and penalties (up to 40% for lack of good faith, 80% for abuse). Documentation penalties: €15,000 per missing document or 5% of the adjusted amount.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the additional tax and penalties if they signed the tax return or were involved in the transfer pricing decisions. In cases of fraud, criminal liability may arise.
