Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a SARL under Article L.223-2 of the Code de commerce; articles may fix a higher threshold. If cash capital is used, the funds must be deposited and blocked in a French banking account, with a notary, or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the blockage certificate must be kept until RCS registration; in-kind contributions must be fully paid up and valued in the articles.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone Member State; the euro (EUR) is domestic legal tender and is fully and freely convertible. Current-account convertibility for advisory retainer fees and lobbying service fees is unrestricted for EU/EEA counterparties. For non-EU/EEA counterparties, settlement of current-account operations is free from prior authorisation provided payment is channelled through an ACPR-licensed authorised intermediary and is covered by balance-of-payments reporting. Capital-account convertibility is equally preserved, but the restored French exchange-control regime (Finance Act 2020, Decree No. 2020-1549, CMF Articles L.151-1 to L.151-4) imposes a prior-authorisation threshold of EUR 1,500,000 per beneficiary/counterparty cluster per calendar year for defined 'financial relations' with non-EU/EEA states, including loans, guarantees, deposits, securities issues and foreign-exchange operations. Intra-EU/EEA capital movements are protected under the TFEU freedom-of-capital and cleared through TARGET2. Profit remittance for a French SARL is not a hard currency hurdle: dividends are paid from after-corporate-tax profits (IS at French standard rate 25%) and, when paid to non-resident shareholders, are subject to the French 25% dividend withholding tax (or 29.7% individual levy where applicable) unless reduced by a double-tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% at qualifying holding thresholds). No foreign-exchange approval attaches to dividend distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Historically French export-surrender obligations were abolished with the adoption of the euro; no mandatory conversion ratio or surrender requirement exists for export proceeds or service-export income. A French SARL may lawfully receive retainers from non-resident clients in EUR or in any freely convertible foreign currency, maintain non-EUR denominated accounts, and deploy those funds without any forced conversion quota. The binding statutory constraints are: (i) all inward proceeds must be received through an authorised intermediary bank so that Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical reporting is effected (individual transaction reporting at or above EUR 50,000 per operation, plus the monthly/quarterly direct-reporting aggregation thresholds applicable to enterprises); (ii) cross-border services exchanges (including government-affairs advisory) must be declared under the French 'Déclaration d'Échanges de Services' (DES) programme when the annual value per non-EU partner country exceeds the applicable exemption floor (currently EUR 250,000 per calendar year, as adjusted by customs order); and (iii) proceeds originating from jurisdictions subject to enhanced vigilance or sanctions will trigger additional AML/CFT controls and, where required, a declaration to TRACFIN. No percentage of export/service proceeds must be surrendered or converted into EUR.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty for exchange-control authorisations under Articles L.151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier – CMF), and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the prudential supervisor and AML/CFT licensing authority for banks and electronic-money institutions operating in France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury rail; Medium only as a secondary collection or disbursement channel. Legal constraints: any electronic-money institution (EMI) serving a French-resident corporate client must be authorised in France by the ACPR under CMF Articles L.525-1 et seq., or must passport into France from another EU/EEA state under the revised E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC, as amended by PSD2). Post-Brexit UK-chartered EMIs and US/offshore fintechs hold no standalone right to operate in France and cannot be used by a French SARL for settlement of non-EU receipts without an ACPR-licensed banking partner, because the French authorised-intermediary rule requires cross-border current-account settlements to pass through an authorised credit institution. The ISIC 7020 lobbying scope is classified as 'Medium_Risk_Enhanced' under FATF-based French typologies, so most automated-KYC EMIs decline onboarding; an EU-passported EMI with a full corporate UBO dossier and a French correspondent bank may be acceptable for low-value retainer collections, but the principal treasury account should remain with a Tier-1 French commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital injection from non-resident shareholders: (i) open an operational EUR account with an ACPR-licensed French bank or, for cash capital increases/changes, a blocked 'compte bloqué' with the bank, Caisse des Dépôts, or a notary until the RCS registration is updated; (ii) instruct the sending bank to issue a SWIFT MT103 with beneficiary FR_SARL, the French IBAN, the bank BIC, and a clear economic reference such as 'INVESTISSEMENT DIRECT / APPORT EN CAPITAL' to allow Banque de France classification as direct-investment capital; (iii) supply the bank with the board/shareholder resolution approving the capital increase, the updated certified articles, source-of-funds attestation and the French INSEE/Kbis dossier; (iv) for non-EU injections exceeding EUR 1,500,000 in aggregate in one calendar year, obtain the French Treasury prior authorisation before crediting the second tranche; and (v) where the remitting jurisdiction is on the FATF or EU denial-of-authorisation list, complete enhanced due diligence and independently verify the economic rationale before acceptance. The receiving bank automatically performs balance-of-payments declaration to Banque de France; the SARL must preserve the banking reference so that tax and audit records match the declared purpose code.For outbound trade disbursements related to retainer pass-through fees, international advocacy payments and subcontractor advisers, the primary clearing rails are: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant for EUR-denominated payments within the EU/EEA; (ii) SWIFT MT103 (with GPI tracking) for non-EU wire settlements, carrying an ISO 20022 purpose-code classification for professional advisory services and the French BIC/IBAN identification; and (iii) UCP600-compliant Commercial Letters of Credit only where the underlying contract expressly requires documentary settlement for trade-policy advisory milestones, although in practice a clean payment against signed retainer and invoice is the prevailing instrument. Where a Bank Payment Obligation (BPO) is proposed, it is legally permissible but commercially marginal for ISIC 7020 services. All outbound settlements must be executed by an authorised French intermediary, must not breach EU sanctions/embargo rules, and must contain a full payment-reference chain so that operations above EUR 50,000 per beneficiary per transaction are automatically reported to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics.Deliverable FX forwards on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF and EUR/CNY, tenor-matched to contracted retainer receivables or supplier payables and documented with a real underlying exposure.FX swaps (changes de devises / swaps cambistes) for temporary conversion and reconversion of non-EUR retainers or disbursement floats through the ACPR-licensed treasury booking bank.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCS/CRS) under an ISDA master agreement to hedge intra-group shareholder loans or acquisition financings carrying EUR/USD and reference-rate exposure.Vanilla European-style FX options (calls and puts) on major convertible pairs, purchased solely for downside protection on forecast advisory-currency income and confirmed under a standard FX master agreement.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for EUR-based underlyings in France because the euro is freely convertible and fully deliverable; a French resident treasury cannot book NDFs for EUR unless the counterparty currency is itself restricted, a situation not presented by this SARL.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) for standard taxable profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate recipient, subject to domestic exemptions, European Union rules and applicable tax treaties. A 12.8% rate generally applies to distributions paid to non-resident individual taxpayers, subject to treaty and domestic-law provisions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on a date other than 31 December, filing is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. For a 31 December year-end, filing is generally due on the second business day after 1 May of the following year. The French tax administration may publish the applicable electronic filing date for the relevant year. Corporate income tax installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, with a final balance generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month after year-end; a 31 December year-end generally has a 15 May balance deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million threshold for full French transfer pricing documentation generally applies where the French entity's annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets reach at least EUR 400 million, where it belongs to a group meeting that threshold, or where it is controlled by or controls an entity meeting the statutory conditions. A simplified annual transfer pricing declaration generally applies from EUR 50 million of turnover or gross assets, subject to additional statutory conditions. Local File and Master File content must be available in the required French or accepted language and produced within the statutory response period.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a standalone economic substance regime equivalent to the substance regulations of certain international financial centres. Substance remains relevant under the French general anti-abuse rule, abuse-of-law doctrine, beneficial ownership analysis, controlled foreign company rules, permanent establishment principles, interest limitation rules, EU anti-hybrid rules and treaty principal-purpose tests. A French SARL conducting government-affairs advisory and lobbying work should maintain French operational substance proportionate to its functions, including genuine personnel or decision-making capacity, premises or operational infrastructure, client files, engagement evidence, documented board decisions, banking control and commercially defensible remuneration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Accounting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, late-payment interest and penalties; serious tax fraud may lead to criminal fines and imprisonment, including aggravated tax fraud penalties that can reach seven years' imprisonment and EUR 3 million for individuals, subject to statutory conditions and judicial findings.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur criminal liability, civil liability for intentional misconduct, tax penalties and liability for fraudulent or seriously negligent conduct contributing to tax non-payment. Travel restrictions or asset seizures may occur through judicial or enforcement procedures. Corporate veil piercing is exceptional and is not automatic merely because the company is insolvent.
False Transfer Pricing and Profit Diversion

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax reassessment, withholding tax exposure, interest, penalties for deliberate understatements or abuse of rights, and specific sanctions for missing or materially deficient transfer pricing documentation. A French tax authority adjustment may also trigger corresponding foreign tax disputes.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they knowingly approve sham arrangements, fraudulent records or abusive distributions. Ordinary commercial transfer pricing errors do not by themselves pierce the corporate veil.
Unlawful Lobbying and HATVP Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative publication, compliance measures and criminal sanctions may apply depending on the breach. Certain failures to comply with HATVP obligations can result in imprisonment and fines under the French transparency legislation; the precise sanction depends on the prohibited conduct and status of the actor.

Director personal liability: Responsible managers and individual lobbyists may be prosecuted personally. A director is not automatically liable for every employee breach, but authorization, instruction, concealment or failure to implement reasonable controls can support personal civil or criminal exposure.
Bribery, Influence Peddling and Public-Sector Corruption

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution may result in substantial fines, imprisonment, confiscation, exclusion from public contracts and corporate penalties. French anti-corruption law applies to conduct connected with France and may have extraterritorial reach in specified circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, employees and intermediaries can be personally prosecuted. Corporate liability may coexist with individual liability; proceeds and assets may be frozen or confiscated. Travel restrictions can be imposed by judicial order where legally justified.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Supervisory, administrative and criminal measures may include fines, professional restrictions, confiscation, tax reassessment and imprisonment for money laundering or terrorist-financing conduct. Suspicious activity reporting obligations apply where the entity falls within a regulated or legally designated category; contractual and bank compliance obligations may apply more broadly.

Director personal liability: Managers and compliance officers may be liable for knowingly participating in laundering, facilitating prohibited payments or deliberately ignoring red flags. Individual sanctions may include imprisonment, fines, confiscation and judicial travel restrictions.
Sanctions, Export-Control and Restricted-Party Breach

Statutory sanction: Asset freezes, transaction prohibitions, administrative or criminal fines, confiscation and imprisonment may apply. Banking relationships may be terminated and the company may be excluded from public or regulated engagements.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees who authorize or knowingly facilitate circumvention may face personal criminal liability. Internal delegation does not eliminate responsibility where senior management knew or should have known of the prohibited transaction.
Improper Dividend, Capital or Related-Party Distribution

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, corporate and shareholder claims, tax reassessment, withholding tax exposure, management liability and possible criminal consequences for unlawful dividend distribution or fraudulent insolvency conduct.

Director personal liability: Managers may be required to restore improperly distributed amounts and compensate creditors or the company. Personal exposure is heightened where accounts were knowingly false, distributions were knowingly unlawful or insolvency was aggravated by management misconduct.
Permanent Establishment and Unreported French Activity

Statutory sanction: French corporate income tax, payroll and VAT assessments, withholding tax, interest, penalties and possible double-tax treaty disputes. The tax authority may recharacterize the arrangement based on actual functions and authority rather than contractual labels.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for deliberate concealment or fraudulent records. Failure to register or account for a French taxable presence may also generate civil, administrative and employment-law consequences, but ordinary classification disputes do not automatically create personal liability.
