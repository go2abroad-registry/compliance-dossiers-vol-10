Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies to a French SARL; the capital is freely fixed in the statuts. Under Article L.223-7 of the Code de commerce, each cash contribution must be paid up to at least one-fifth at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. The funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account or with a notary and evidenced by an attestation de dépôt de fonds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a euro-area member, operates full capital and current account convertibility under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements; no general exchange control restrictions apply to EUR transactions. Profit remittances by an SARL are freely permitted after corporate tax and legal reserve obligations, subject to standard anti-money laundering and beneficial-ownership verification by payment channels. Cross-border payments are generally unrestricted, but French resident entities must comply with monthly or quarterly balance-of-payments reporting to Banque de France where aggregate cross-border flows exceed EUR 50 million per annum; smaller flows are exempt from systematic granular reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory requirement exists in France to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or to convert export receipts into EUR within a mandatory timeline. Exporters retain free disposition of foreign-currency proceeds. France has had no obligatory repatriation or conversion requirement since the full liberalisation of exchange controls; entities need only maintain supporting documentation for tax, customs, and balance-of-payments statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (central bank) acting with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking supervision and the Direction Générale du Trésor / Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances for foreign exchange regulatory policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Cross-border digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions licensed in the European Economic Area may legally operate in France under the EU payment services passport. Viability is Medium-High for ancillary payout and foreign-exchange services, such as Wise Business and specialised FX providers, but mainstream merchant treasury is still best concentrated in a licensed French commercial bank to secure merchant-of-record settlement flows, French VAT registration, and credible bank references. Legal constraints require the EMI to be registered with the ACPR or passported into France, funds to be safeguarded, client money to be segregated, and full KYC/KYB of the SARL to be performed before account opening.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (for fiscal years opening on or after 1 January 2022); reduced 15% rate applies to the first €42,000 of taxable profit for SMEs meeting the strict statutory definition (turnover < €10 million and shareholding owned at least 75% by individuals).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under domestic law (Article 119 bis 2 CGI) on dividends paid by a French SARL to non-resident corporate shareholders. A reduced 0% rate applies to EU/EEA parent companies holding ≥ 5% of the distributable share capital for at least 2 years (Parent-Subsidiary Directive). For non-resident individuals, a 12.8% withholding applies under the flat-tax regime, plus French social levies where mandated. Treaty relief can reduce the rate (commonly 5% or 15%).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File are compulsory where the French entity's annual turnover or gross book assets exceed €400 million, or where it belongs to a tax-integrated group exceeding €400 million; a simplified transfer pricing documentation duty applies from €50 million (turnover or total balance sheet). In all cases, the French administration may request TP documentation below these thresholds if there is a serious presumption of profit shifting, particularly in digital/social-commerce flows.

Economic substance scope: France applies economic-substance principles through the general anti-abuse provision (abus de droit, Article L.64 LPF) and the MLI Principal Purpose Test. A French SARL used to stream TikTok Shop revenues must demonstrate real and credible French substance: local staff, offices, decision-making on stock/customers/marketing, and actual commercial risk. If characterised as a letterbox or conduit company, DGFiP may ignore the SARL, reallocate income to the foreign parent, deny treaty benefits, impose an 80% penalty and transfer the liability to the foreign beneficial owner. The SARL must therefore keep payroll records, lease contracts, board minutes and logistics documents showing operational control in France.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Under-reporting of Social Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI: criminal fine up to €500,000 (increased to €2,000,000 and 7 years' imprisonment for aggravated fraud); in all cases, 80% penalty on the evaded tax plus 0.20% per month late interest, mandatory publication of the judgment, loss of good standing, non-deductibility of the fine.

Director personal liability: The gérant of the SARL faces personal criminal prosecution, civil restitution of the evaded taxes, travel restrictions (interdiction du territoire français), bank account freezing, surrender of passport, and disqualification from managing French companies. Article L.267 LPF allows the tax administration to collect corporate tax debts personally from the gérant where the gérant's intentional misconduct caused the non-payment.
Burden of Proof / Fake Substance (Shell Company)

Statutory sanction: Article L.64 LPF abuse-of-law procedure: recharacterization of the transactions, reintegration of all shifted profits, application of the 80% penalty (when abus de droit is proven), suspension of statute-of-limitation periods, and additional withholding taxes on deemed dividends/royalties; in criminal cases the 80% penalty may be augmented by Article 1741 sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally subject to the full set of tax liabilities under Article L.266/L.267 LPF, criminal fines, imprisonment up to 5 years, prohibition from commercial activity, and travel bans. In insolvency proceedings, the gérant may be ordered to bear the company's tax deficit as part of the faute-de-gestion action.
AML / Fraudulent Use of Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Monetary and Financial Code: corporate fine up to €1,000,000 for money laundering; seizure of funds held in the payment accounts up to the amount of the illegal proceeds; automatic reporting to Tracfin; withdrawal of PSP access; publication of AML sanctions; and administrative penalties by the Auditeur de la conformité.

Director personal liability: Directors of the SARL are criminally liable for laundering, receiving up to 5 years' imprisonment and fines up to €375,000, plus civil restitution, freezing of personal assets, passport seizure and travel bans. The company's compliance officer (or the default gérant) may additionally face a management ban from the AMF/ACP.
Withholding Tax Evasion at Source

Statutory sanction: The payor (SARL) is required to pay the entire unreported withholding tax; the amount is increased by an 80% penalty for intentional non-collection and monthly late interest. The administration may attach the SARL's assets and payment rails, including TikTok Shop payouts, for the tax debt.

Director personal liability: Article L.267 LPF: the gérant is personally liable for the unpaid withholding when it is proven that the gérant either collected or directed the payment without withholding tax and that the SARL cannot satisfy the claim. Criminal prosecution for fraud may lead to travel bans, civil recovery of the withheld amounts, and economic disqualification.
False Accounting and Non-Intrusive Digital Filing Errors

Statutory sanction: 10% late filing penalty; 40% if relevant to a formal demand; 80% for fraudulent omission; late interest at 0.20% per month; €150 per missing annexe; criminal fine under Article 1743 CGI up to €500,000 with imprisonment up to 5 years if false accounting is proven.

Director personal liability: The gérant may be held jointly and severally liable for the penalties and tax under the faute-de-gestion doctrine; a travel ban and prohibition on commercial management can be imposed; in case of insolvency, Article L.651-2 of the Commercial Code requires the gérant to repay the company's tax liabilities out of personal assets where gross negligence caused the shortfall.
