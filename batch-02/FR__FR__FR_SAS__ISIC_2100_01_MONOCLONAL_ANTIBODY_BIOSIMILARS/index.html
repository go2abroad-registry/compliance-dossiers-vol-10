Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully liberalized FX regime under EU law. Capital account transactions (equity investments, loans) are convertible without prior approval. Current account transactions including profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are fully convertible with no surrender obligations. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to tax compliance, beneficial ownership declarations, and AML verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the central bank. Exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds offshore or convert to Euro at prevailing market rates. No statutory conversion ratios apply; repatriation timeline is flexible but all transactions require commercial documentation and reporting if exceeding €10,000.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border operations via Electronic Money Institutions are feasible under French Monetary and Financial Code and PSD2 licensing. Legal constraints include mandatory capital requirements, ongoing AML/KYC obligations, and restrictions on proprietary activities. Overall viability rating: Medium with full regulatory compliance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. A qualifying small enterprise may access a 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to French ownership, turnover, capital and fully paid-in capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to exemptions and treaty relief. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption, implemented in French law, may reduce withholding to 0% where the parent satisfies qualifying legal-form, tax-subject-to-tax, minimum ownership, holding-period and anti-abuse conditions. French resident individual recipients are generally subject to a 12.8% income-tax withholding component, in addition to social levies where applicable.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and corporate tax balance are generally due within three months after the fiscal year-end. For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the statutory filing deadline is generally the second business day after 1 May of the following year. Electronic payment of installments is generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, subject to statutory exceptions and first-year rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

VAT deadlines depend on the chosen regime, taxable turnover and tax authority instructions. Monthly VAT returns are generally due during the following month; annual simplified-regime returns and installment forms follow the statutory timetable shown in the professional tax account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer-pricing documentation under Article L13 AA generally applies where French turnover or gross assets is at least EUR 150 million, or where the company controls, is controlled by, or belongs to a group involving an entity meeting the statutory threshold. Simplified documentation may apply below that threshold where the French entity engages in material controlled transactions, including transactions meeting the applicable EUR 50 million statutory threshold. A country-by-country report generally applies to qualifying multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Intercompany transactions remain arm's-length and supportable even below documentation thresholds.

Economic substance scope: France has no single standalone economic-substance-regulation regime equivalent to certain jurisdiction-specific ESR statutes. Substance is nevertheless enforced through the abuse-of-law doctrine, principal-purpose and treaty anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled-foreign-company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation, general anti-abuse principles and transfer-pricing provisions. The SAS should maintain French decision-making, qualified personnel, suitable premises, manufacturing and quality systems, risk control, board records, financial capacity and genuine commercial functions proportionate to its claimed profits. A French company that is contractually characterized as a routine manufacturer but bears no operational risks must not retain residual entrepreneurial returns without factual support.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent corporate tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest and penalties generally up to 80% in the most serious cases. Tax fraud may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated cases carrying imprisonment of up to 7 years and a fine of up to EUR 3 million or twice the proceeds, subject to applicable law.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur criminal liability, civil tax liability for fraudulent or repeated non-compliance, asset-preservation measures and management bans. Courts may impose travel restrictions or detention-related measures where justified in criminal proceedings; imprisonment is not automatic.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and undocumented related-party dealings

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length profit, interest and penalties. Missing or materially incomplete documentation may trigger a specific penalty generally equal to the higher of EUR 50,000 or 0.5% of the transactions covered by the missing documentation, subject to statutory rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for deliberate misstatements, fraudulent accounting, insolvency-related misconduct and tax-fraud participation. Corporate protection does not shield personal criminal conduct.
Dividend, royalty and interest withholding failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, late-payment interest, tax penalties and possible denial of treaty relief. Repeated or fraudulent withholding violations may create criminal exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may be pursued for intentional withholding failures, fraudulent distributions and breaches of tax-payment duties. Personal liability depends on conduct, fault and insolvency circumstances.
Shell company, treaty shopping and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or statutory benefits, recharacterization of transactions, corporate tax reassessment, withholding-tax recovery, abuse-of-law penalties and possible criminal tax-fraud proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face criminal liability for participation in fraudulent arrangements, asset confiscation, management bans and civil claims for losses.
VAT and customs fraud

Statutory sanction: Customs duties, import VAT, VAT reassessment, interest, penalties, seizure, loss of customs authorizations and potential criminal prosecution for aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors, customs representatives and responsible officers may face personal criminal liability, fines, confiscation and professional or management restrictions.
Pharmaceutical manufacturing and product-compliance breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative suspension or withdrawal of authorization, product recall, seizure, fines, civil product liability and criminal sanctions under French public-health law. Tax deductions and R&D-credit claims may also be challenged where expenditure is unlawful or unsupported.

Director personal liability: Directors and qualified persons may face criminal prosecution, professional sanctions, civil damages and, in serious cases, imprisonment.
Anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing breach

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines, transaction restrictions, confiscation and criminal sanctions for money laundering or terrorist financing. Money laundering may carry imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 375,000, with aggravated cases carrying higher penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally prosecuted for knowing participation, facilitation or deliberate failure to maintain mandatory controls. Assets may be frozen or confiscated.
Accounting falsification and unlawful distributions

Statutory sanction: Account restatement, tax reassessment, repayment of unlawful distributions, fines, director disqualification and criminal sanctions under French company law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unlawful distributions, misuse of corporate assets, presentation of misleading accounts, insolvency misconduct and creditor losses.
Research and development tax-credit abuse

Statutory sanction: Repayment of the tax credit, interest, penalties, denial of future claims and possible audit referral. Deliberate fabrication may constitute tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and technical signatories may face civil and criminal exposure for knowingly false claims, fabricated records or coordinated fraud.
Beneficial-ownership, sanctions and export-control breach

Statutory sanction: Transaction blocking, asset freezing, administrative fines, confiscation, loss of licenses and criminal prosecution under sanctions, customs and export-control laws.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance personnel may face personal fines, imprisonment, confiscation and management prohibitions for knowing or reckless violations.
