Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS; capital may legally be set at EUR 1. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash capital must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, which issues the deposit certificate. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed at incorporation and may require a commissaire aux apports report, subject to statutory exemptions.

Funding & unlock path: Before opening the site, verify building conformity, fire compartments, escape routes, alarm and suppression systems, emergency access, hazardous-material storage, and electrical safety with the mairie, SDIS, and applicable building-control professionals. The employer must complete the DUERP occupational-risk assessment, provide workplace training and personal protective equipment, arrange occupational-health monitoring, and comply with Labour Code requirements. Public-access areas may require ERP safety and accessibility authorization.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — operating under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; official portal: impots.gouv.fr; online corporate and VAT registration platform: Guichet Unique administered by INPI (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); customs and export control authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), portal douane.gouv.fr (DELTA-X and CDE systems).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the SAS through the INPI Guichet Unique, filing the statuts, the beneficial-owner declaration (RBE) and the appointment of the président; a minimum capital is not legally required for the SAS (optionally fixed in the statuts or by a capital increase). Step 2 — The Guichet Unique transmits the registration to the INSEE (SIRENE) and to the DGFiP; the entity receives the SIREN/SIRET identifiers and is automatically enrolled for corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés) with its local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Step 3 — Upon issuance of the RCS extract (greffe du tribunal de commerce), the SAS applies for its French intra-community VAT number (format FR + 13 digits) with the SIE; the number is activated and validated through the EU VIES exchange system. Step 4 — For a foreign entity that does not incorporate a French subsidiary but has a taxable French permanent establishment, a direct Form No. 3707-SD (Cerfa 12373) application must be lodged with the local SIE before the first taxable transaction, and the appointment of an accredited VAT fiscal representative may be required for non-EU principals under article 289-A of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI). Step 5 — The completed tax identity package must include the RCS certificate, the VAT number confirmation letter, the SIRET data record, and confirmation of the registered office and bank account details to be used on all future invoices and customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The SAS is statutorily opaque and subject to corporate income tax at entity level under article 206 CGI; there is no check-the-box or partnership-taxation regime for an SAS. Available statutory elections include: (i) tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under articles 223 A to 223 U CGI where the parent holds at least 95% of the SAS capital; (ii) formation of a French VAT group (assujetti unique) under article 1693 ter CGI for closely related established taxable persons; (iii) election of the reduced 15% IS rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for PME enterprises under article 219 CGI, with the balance taxed at 25%; (iv) option for the EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) non-Union and Union schemes under articles 293 A to 293 F CGI for distance sales of solar inverters to final consumers within the European Union; (v) anti-hybrid and interest limitation elections and documentation safe harbours under articles 212 bis and 223 quinquies B CGI; and (vi) the option under article 262 ter CGI to zero-rate intra-EU supplies of inverters only upon receiving and verifying the customer's valid intra-community VAT number and lodging the recapitulative statement (EC Sales List).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 — a French SAS carrying on the manufacture of solar inverters is subject to compulsory VAT registration from its first taxable supply, with no turnover threshold; by way of parallel benchmark, the franchise en base de TVA (small-business exemption) under article 293 B CGI exempts enterprises with annual turnover below EUR 85,000 (sales of goods) or EUR 37,500 (services) in the prior calendar year, but this exemption is not practically available for a manufacturer realizing intra-EU acquisitions, export refunds or B2B invoicing to EU customers; for a non-established foreign seller making distance sales to French consumers, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 distance-selling registration threshold applies under the OSS regime (article 293 A CGI), below which VAT remains chargeable in the seller's home member state and above which French VAT becomes due.

France has enacted the mandatory e-invoicing/e-reporting reform (Loi de finances pour 2020, implementing decrees and BOI-TVA-DECL-30-10-30) based on a Y-model with no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping as in Latin American jurisdictions. B2B invoices must be issued in a structured data format (Factur-X, UBL or CII national subset) and transmitted electronically through either a certified Partenaire de Dématérialisation (PDP) or the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); e-reporting of transaction data (B2C and cross-border flows) to the DGFiP is separate. Phased entry into force: from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and ETI (midcaps), and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises, including French taxable persons with annual turnover below the SME thresholds; mandated API interoperability requires the ERP invoicing module to generate the structured invoice, transmit the payload to the chosen PDP and synchronise the e-reporting datasets, while invoices intended for export zero-rating must retain the customs declaration references (MRN) and the export proof documents within the digital archiving chain; import and export clearances of inverters are processed through the DELTA/DEB and CDE/DES interfaces of the DGDDI, and there is no tax stamping requirement.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France has no general foreign-exchange controls or distinction imposing routine exchange restrictions between current-account and capital-account transactions. The euro is freely convertible, and a French SAS may generally receive, hold, convert, and remit funds domestically or cross-border, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing rules, tax requirements, bank controls, and applicable EU or French investment-screening rules. Dividends and other profit distributions may be paid abroad after corporate-law authorization, availability of distributable profits, withholding-tax analysis, and any applicable treaty or EU-law conditions. Interest, royalties, service fees, and loan repayments require genuine underlying transactions, appropriate documentation, transfer-pricing support where relevant, and tax withholding analysis. Banks may require evidence of source of funds, beneficial ownership, business purpose, and tax status. France does not generally require prior central-bank approval for ordinary commercial payments or capital transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds to France by a specified deadline or to convert a prescribed percentage into euros. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign currency or in an overseas account, subject to tax, accounting, sanctions, contractual, and bank-reporting requirements. Banks may request invoices, customs or transport documentation, and payment-purpose information. Separate statistical reporting obligations may apply to certain cross-border transactions or entities under Banque de France rules; applicability and thresholds should be checked for the entity and reporting period.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France's national central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. The Direction générale du Trésor administers France's foreign-investment screening regime; it is not the foreign-exchange regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine payments and foreign-currency collections; low as the sole banking arrangement during incorporation or for complex trade finance. An electronic money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts and cross-border transfers if duly authorized in France or passported into France under EU rules. Electronic-money balances are safeguarded under applicable rules but are not bank deposits covered by the French deposit-guarantee scheme. Confirm the provider's authorization, safeguarding arrangements, permitted services, account ownership, currencies, transfer limits, and support for SWIFT, letters of credit, guarantees, and tax or capital-deposit requirements. Offshore status alone does not establish authorization to serve French customers.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard rate for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2022). Reduced rate of 15% applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) with turnover below EUR 10 million and fully paid-up capital held 75% by individuals or qualifying entities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% standard withholding tax on dividends distributed to non-residents (Article 119 bis CGI). However, a reduced rate of 12.8% applies to dividends paid to individuals resident in France; for non-residents, the 30% withholding applies unless reduced by an applicable DTA. Note: The French domestic rate for non-resident dividends is 30% (prélèvement forfaitaire obligatoire for individuals, but for legal persons the standard withholding is 30% unless treaty relief). EU parent-subsidiary directive may exempt dividends to qualifying EU parent companies (25% holding, two years).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally within three months following the fiscal year-end. For a 31 December year-end, filing deadline is the second working day following 30 April of the following year (typically early May). Payment of the balance of IS is due by 15 May for calendar-year taxpayers. Advance installments are due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File required for French entities that are part of a group with consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 50 million and that are subject to TP documentation requirements (Article 223 quinquies B CGI). Local File required for French entities with annual turnover or gross balance sheet exceeding EUR 400 million, or belonging to a group with such thresholds, and for entities with transactions with related parties exceeding EUR 100,000 per transaction or EUR 1 million aggregate per year (thresholds may vary; the statutory threshold for mandatory TP documentation is EUR 400 million turnover for the Local File, and the Master File threshold is EUR 50 million). However, the French Tax Code requires TP documentation for entities that are dependent or controlling and that have related-party transactions exceeding EUR 100,000 per transaction or EUR 1 million aggregate. In practice, the documentation obligation is triggered at EUR 400 million turnover for the full Local File, but the tax authority may request TP documentation for smaller entities on audit.

Economic substance scope: France applies a general anti-abuse rule (abus de droit, Article L64 LPF) and specific anti-abuse provisions. Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as such do not exist; however, substance is assessed under the abus de droit doctrine and the concept of 'acte anormal de gestion' (abnormal management act). For foreign entities, France may apply Article 209 B CGI (controlled foreign company rules) if the entity is subject to a privileged tax regime and predominantly passive income. French TP rules require that intercompany transactions comply with the arm's length principle under Article 57 CGI. For SAS entities, no specific statutory economic substance requirement, but the tax authority examines actual decision-making, risk control, and functions performed. The EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) has been transposed, including interest limitation rules, CFC rules, and exit taxation. Beneficial ownership and PPT (Principal Purpose Test) apply under DTAs following MLI.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 40% or 80% of unpaid tax; criminal penalty of EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment for tax fraud (Article 1741 CGI). Aggravated fraud (e.g., use of offshore accounts) may result in EUR 2,000,000 fine and 7 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives (président, directeurs) may be held personally liable for civil restitution of tax debts in case of fraud or mismanagement (Article 1745 CGI; action en comblement de passif). Criminal conviction may include imprisonment and a ban on managing companies (faillite personnelle). Travel bans may be imposed for unpaid tax debts exceeding EUR 50,000 under certain conditions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of EUR 10,000 per year for failure to provide documentation; increased to EUR 50,000 in case of repeat offense. Additional reassessment of taxable income and 40% penalty for insufficient documentation if the taxpayer fails to justify pricing. Under Article 57 CGI, the tax authority may adjust taxable income to arm's length, with penalties of 40% or 80% for intentional mispricing.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they participated in the non-compliant pricing or failed to implement compliance procedures. Criminal liability for tax fraud may apply if the mispricing is intentional.
AML / KYC Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of turnover for serious breaches; criminal penalties of up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine for money laundering (Article 324-1 Code pénal). The ACPR may impose sanctions including withdrawal of license.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fines and imprisonment for AML violations. They may also face professional bans and disgorgement of profits.
Shell Company / Abusive Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Rejection of treaty benefits, reassessment of profits, and penalties of 80% for abuse of law (abus de droit). The entity may be requalified as a French tax resident or its income reallocated. Penalties under Article 1729 CGI (80%) and Article 1759 (aggravated).

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax debts and penalties, and may face criminal prosecution for tax fraud and money laundering. Travel bans and asset freezes may apply.
Failure to File Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Surcharges of 10%, 20%, 40%, or 80% depending on delay and whether a formal notice has been issued. Late payment interest of 0.20% per month. In case of concealed activity, 80% penalty and additional criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the tax and penalties if the failure is due to their fault, especially in case of liquidation or insolvency. They may also face criminal penalties for fraudulent non-filing.
