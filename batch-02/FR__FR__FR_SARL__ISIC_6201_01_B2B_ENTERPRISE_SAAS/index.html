Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for SARL under French law; the articles of association freely determine the capital. Cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Cash funds must be deposited in a French bank account, with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or with a notary until registration. In-kind contributions must be fully paid-up at subscription and require a Commissaire aux Apports review if valued above €30,000 or where statutory valuation rules apply.

Funding & unlock path: For the office occupier: valid fire safety certificate for any Établissement Recevant des Salariés (ERP type R) under the Labor Code; structural load confirmation for server racks or heavy equipment; electrical installation conformity certificate if a French electrical system is modified; compliance with the French Labor Code workplace hygiene, lighting, ventilation, and emergency exit requirements.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official portal impots.gouv.fr. Company formation and registration data are filed through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI); the Institut national de la statistique et des études économiques (INSEE) allocates the SIREN and SIRET identifiers.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate and register the SARL through the INPI Guichet unique. 2. Obtain the SIREN identifier for the legal entity and the SIRET identifier for each registered establishment from INSEE. 3. Open the entity's professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr and identify the competent SIE. 4. Submit the VAT registration request, normally using the applicable French VAT registration form and supporting corporate, business-activity, establishment, bank-account, and representative documentation. 5. The DGFiP issues or confirms the French VAT number in the format FR plus an administrative two-digit key and the entity's nine-digit SIREN. 6. Non-established entities making taxable supplies in France must register where required; a fiscal representative may be required for non-EU businesses, subject to applicable mutual-assistance rules. 7. Validate EU VAT numbers through the VIES system where intra-EU transactions are conducted. A French entity may have a SIREN without being VAT-registered if it qualifies for the French VAT exemption regime or has no taxable VAT activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). There is no US-style check-the-box regime. Subject to statutory eligibility conditions, a SARL may elect taxation at shareholder level under the income tax regime: a qualifying newly established company may generally elect for a limited period of up to five fiscal years, while a qualifying family SARL may elect for income tax treatment without the ordinary five-year limitation. Elections are formal, generally irrevocable or subject to statutory withdrawal rules, and require timely notification to the relevant tax authority. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implementing ATAD 2 may deny deductions, impose inclusion, or neutralize mismatches; they do not constitute a freely available entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no general turnover threshold that permits a non-established taxable person to ignore French VAT registration when it makes taxable supplies requiring registration in France. For French-established businesses, the domestic franchise en base de TVA exemption for services generally applies below the statutory annual turnover threshold of EUR 37,500, with a tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250 under applicable rules and annual legislative adjustments. A B2B SaaS supply to a business customer established in another EU Member State is generally subject to customer-accounted reverse charge and may require EU recapitulative reporting rather than French output VAT. The EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold is relevant primarily to certain cross-border B2C electronic services and is separate from French domestic registration thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from France or the EU to a destination outside the EU may be invoiced without French VAT if the supplier can prove export and customs exit in accordance with French and EU VAT rules. The supplier must retain the export declaration, the electronic customs exit result or equivalent exit evidence, commercial invoices, transport records, and transaction-level accounting support. B2B SaaS and other electronically supplied services are generally not treated as exported goods. A service supplied to a business customer established outside France is normally taxed according to the place-of-supply rule and may be outside the scope of French VAT or subject to reverse charge, rather than technically being a 0% export supply. The supplier must substantiate the customer's business status, establishment outside France, contractual supply location, and actual use where relevant. French VAT refunds require valid invoices, customs evidence where applicable, accounting records, and proof that input VAT relates to activities giving a right of deduction.

  • Electronic export declaration and customs Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Electronic confirmation of exit from the EU customs territory or equivalent customs exit evidence
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and destination
  • Packing list and transport document, such as bill of lading, airway bill, or CMR consignment note, where applicable
  • Contract, purchase order, and accounting records linking the goods to the export transaction
  • Proof of delivery or destination evidence where required by the customs or tax audit
  • For services: customer VAT or business-identification evidence, contract, customer-establishment evidence, service description, place-of-supply analysis, and reverse-charge wording where applicable
  • Banking or payment records where relevant to commercial substantiation; an inward-remittance certificate is not generally a French statutory export-VAT document

France is implementing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction reporting. From 1 September 2026, all French-established businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; issuance obligations apply from 1 September 2026 to large enterprises and intermediate-sized enterprises and from 1 September 2027 to small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises, subject to statutory transitional changes. Invoices must generally be transmitted through an accredited private platform or other authorized platform using prescribed structured formats and data fields. France uses a platform-based transmission and e-reporting model rather than universal real-time invoice clearance. E-reporting applies principally to qualifying transactions with non-established customers and certain payment data. Public-sector invoices continue to use the Chorus Pro framework. Digital archiving, invoice integrity, authenticity, readability, and audit-trail requirements apply.

France has no general foreign-exchange repatriation requirement or universal 180-day deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption or deduction may be denied or clawed back if customs exit, customer status, destination, transport, or transaction records are insufficient. Reassessed VAT may include late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month, subject to statutory rules, together with penalties commonly ranging from 10% for specified filing or payment failures to 40% for deliberate non-compliance and 80% for fraudulent maneuvers or abuse of rights. False documents, fraudulent VAT claims, or organized VAT fraud may trigger criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment under the French Tax Code and Penal Code. Customs violations may generate customs duties, VAT reassessment, fines, seizure, and loss of customs authorizations. The applicable assessment period and penalty depend on the nature of the error, concealment, fraud, and relevant limitation rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions under Articles 63–65 TFEU and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Articles L151-1 et seq.). No prior exchange-control license is required for profit remittances, dividend distributions, franchise royalties, or ordinary trade flows. Cross-border transfers to non-EEA destinations are subject only to statistical declaration obligations to the Banque de France and, for inbound direct investments capitalizing a French SARL, a mandatory ex-post or prior declaration under the FDI screening regime if the activity touches a sensitized sector (ordinary B2B SaaS activity is not sensitized). For outbound investments or capital account movements exceeding €1,000,000, DG Trésor reporting applies. SaaS revenue may be invoiced in foreign currencies, but statutory financial statements must be maintained in euros.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender deadline, mandatory conversion ratio, or obligation to repatriate foreign currency to a French concentrator account. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domiciled bank accounts established in France, provided each transaction or periodic aggregation exceeds the €750,000 monthly balance-of-payments reporting threshold and the corresponding declaration is filed with the Banque de France. Receipts may be freely converted, offset against foreign-currency payables, or used for treasury operations without central-bank authorization.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) as central bank; Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty as the competent foreign exchange administrator and balance-of-payments reporting authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for payment routing, low feasibility as a sole banking relationship. French-approved or passporting Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, and Qonto provide remote onboarding, multi-currency IBANs, and lower-cost cross-border execution. They are authorized under PSD2 and may receive third-party SaaS subscription payments in multiple currencies. However, an EMI account is not a deposit account; funds are safeguarded, not covered by the French statutory deposit-guarantee scheme. A capital contribution to a new SARL is safer through a full-service conventional bank. For operational use, combining a tier-1 bank for treasury and borrowing with an EMI for cross-border payables is viable and legally compliant.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident shareholder or UBO of a French SARL

Root cause: Non-resident shareholder or UBO of a French SARL

Defense evidence: Original or certified extract from the foreign company registry; current international passport and proof of residential address for the natural-person UBO; audited financial statements or tax return substantiating the source of wealth; a funding-chain confirmation letter from the client's existing bank; a board resolution explicitly endorsing the French entity's capital contribution; and a sworn statement that the contributed funds are unencumbered and derived from lawful commercial activity.
Cross-border SaaS invoice trail with high incoming transaction velocity

Root cause: Cross-border SaaS invoice trail with high incoming transaction velocity

Defense evidence: Signed master subscription agreements, standard terms-of-service linked to a published website, purchase orders from distinct enterprise customers, treasury-consolidated payment reconciliation report, and a copy of the VAT registration certificate proving a French taxable personality. In addition, a company presentation package explaining the low-risk recurring-revenue nature of the software-as-a-service model and a commitment to provide transaction-level explanations on AML request.
Receipts from jurisdictions under enhanced-AML scrutiny or high-risk money-laundering jurisdictions

Root cause: Receipts from jurisdictions under enhanced-AML scrutiny or high-risk money-laundering jurisdictions

Defense evidence: A jurisdiction-specific risk assessment prepared by the MLRO or external advisory firm; commercial contracts involving local distributors data-centre providers or resellers; proof of economic substance in the counterparts (registered address, business license); and confirmation that each transaction is settled through an already-KYC cleared correspondent bank. For particularly challenged jurisdictions, a six-month telemetric analysis of user behaviour and revenue cycle supports the legitimate-end-activity defence.
Unexplained quick switch of the treasury account or concentration of recurring subscription payments

Root cause: Unexplained quick switch of the treasury account or concentration of recurring subscription payments

Defense evidence: A bank-switching certificate issued by the previous account-holder within the French banking system (demande de mobilité bancaire), audited annual accounts, historical cash-flow waterfall, and the external auditor's comfort letter acknowledging revenue-recognition policies in accordance with IFRS 15. This bundle neutralises the red flag by demonstrating predictable commercial use.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a capital contribution from a non-French shareholder, the standard compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's retained bank account to the SARL's tier-1 Paris-based euro operating account. The wire must include a structured reference identifying the investor, the corporate name (FR SARL), and the transaction purpose code 'S13 / Direct Investment Capital Contribution'. A Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR) shall be recorded. The bank will require a copy of the executed board/partner resolution showing the authorised subscription, a statement of the amount and currency of issuance of capital, and evidence of the beneficial-owner identification. If the inbound amount corresponds to registered capital, contribution must occur no later than the statutory deadline and be verified by the managing director. For amounts exceeding the €750,000 threshold, a balance-of-payments declaration to Banque de France and a mandatory FDI declaration to DG Trésor via its dedicated portal are required; the declarations are ex-post except where the shareholder participates in actions listed in Article R151-3 of the Monetary and Financial Code, in which case prior authorization is necessary.Cross-border disbursements for trade creditors, cloud-infrastructure providers, overseas distributors, and intercompany service fees shall be executed via secure clearing rails as follows: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) in euro for EEA counterparties; (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA euro and foreign-currency payments, including required structured beneficiary identifiers and invoice references; (3) ISO 20022 MX pain.001 for batch enterprise payments when integrated with a treasury workstation; (4) commercial letters of credit or standby letters of credit only where a contractual guarantee is mandatory, typically for procurement of hardware from Asia; (5) first-party open-loop corporate cards (Virtual Business Credit Cards) for small-value digital-advertising and software-license payments; and (6) EMI rails such as Wise Business for rapid multi-currency settlement where the counterparty is not a sanctioned entity and the beneficial recipient is identifiably the contracted supplier. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard rate of impôt sur les sociétés (IS) applicable since 1 January 2022; reduced 15% rate applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying small businesses with fiscal-year revenue not exceeding EUR 10 million and fully paid-up capital.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months after the fiscal year-end (Article 223 CGI); e-filing extends the deadline by 15 days. For a 31 December year-end, the filing deadline is 3 May (paper) or 17 May (electronic); for other fiscal year-ends, the deadline is the end of the third month following the close (electronic filers obtain a 15-day extension).
