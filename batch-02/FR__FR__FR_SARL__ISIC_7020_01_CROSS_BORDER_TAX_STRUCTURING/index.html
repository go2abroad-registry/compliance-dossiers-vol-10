Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Conditionally accepted: a SARL must maintain a registered office in France (Article L.123-10 Code de commerce). Domiciliation through a state-approved business domiciliation company under Article L.123-11-2 of the Code de commerce is permissible and is a standard market practice for tax-consulting firms. However, a purely nominal virtual office with no identifiable local management, no physical file retention, and no decision-making presence substantially increases the risk of adverse characterization under the French abuse of law doctrine (abus de droit, Article L.64 of the Livre des procédures fiscales) and under OECD BEPS substance requirements for transfer pricing and tax advisory activities.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a founding member of the euro area and applies no general currency convertibility restrictions under Article 63 TFEU on free movement of capital. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible between EUR and foreign currencies through authorised resident credit institutions. There are no exchange control licences, no prior approval requirements for profit remittances, and no substantive statutory hurdle to dividend distributions, shareholder loan repayments, or trade payments. Practical impediments are limited to regulatory-compliance duties: French banks must transmit balance-of-payments (BOP) statistics to Banque de France for cross-border flows, verify economic purpose under AML/KYC rules, and apply sanctions and embargo screening. Profit remittance by a French SARL is consequently constrained not by FX restriction but by corporate-law solvency tests, shareholder approval formalities, and French dividend withholding tax / parent-subsidiary directive eligibility where the recipient is an EU resident corporate shareholder.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. French and EU rules impose no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. The mandatory conversion rate is therefore 0%, and exporters may invoice in EUR or any freely convertible foreign currency, hold the proceeds in an account abroad or in France, and use them without approval. The only reporting obligation is indirect: the resident French bank records cross-border receipts in the balance-of-payments data collection of Banque de France for statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for external financial relations and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking supervision and payment services authorisation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High for a French or EU/EEA regulated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) holding an ACPR authorisation or using PSD2 passporting into France, provided it offers French IBAN-bearing operational accounts and can support cross-border wires. Medium for a pan-EU EMI with no French physical branch, because transaction limits and clearing-rail access may be narrower and capital-deposit escrow services are rarely available. Low / not viable for an offshore digital EMI outside the EU/EEA: it cannot lawfully provide payment services to a French SARL, has no access to French payment schemes, does not hold a Banque de France settlement account, and creates disproportionate AML/CTF and sanctions risk.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory as the stated commercial purpose

Root cause: Tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory as the stated commercial purpose

Defense evidence: A signed engagement letter defining boutique tax consultancy (ISIC 7020), evidence of registered statutory licence or regulated professional status (e.g. avocat or expert-comptable subject to an Ordre), professional indemnity insurance certificate, CVs and credentials of the directors, and a written statement confirming no tax evasion, no shell activity, and full substance in France.
Non-resident directors or shareholders controlling a French SARL

Root cause: Non-resident directors or shareholders controlling a French SARL

Defense evidence: Passports and proof of residence of all directors and 25% beneficial owners, tax residence certificates, local police/regulatory background checks, corporate extract or shareholder register, source-of-wealth documentation for each material shareholder, and a clear explanation of the legitimate tax structuring role of any non-French entity.
Layered or offshore holding chain without transparent economic rationale

Root cause: Layered or offshore holding chain without transparent economic rationale

Defense evidence: A full organisational chart identifying every ultimate beneficial owner under the French UBO register obligation (Décret n° 2014-1284), corroborating commercial agreements, audited or certified financial statements for intermediate holding entities, and transfer pricing documentation prepared under OECD and French tax rules (article L.13 AA of the French Tax Code).
High expected cross-border receipts and repeated FX or treasury transactions

Root cause: High expected cross-border receipts and repeated FX or treasury transactions

Defense evidence: A treasury policy, annual budget and cash-flow forecast, underlying client contracts and invoices, documented purpose for each major inbound/outbound transaction, confirmation that all transactions relate to bona fide advisory services, and a commitment to provide periodic transaction justifications on request to the French bank.
Initial capital injection from a foreign shareholder raising source-of-funds doubts

Root cause: Initial capital injection from a foreign shareholder raising source-of-funds doubts

Defense evidence: A notarised or certified shareholder resolution approving the capital contribution, bank statements from the remitting shareholder showing a lawful origin of funds, evidence of the deposit of cash with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the legal share-capital subscription certificate issued for the SARL.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require the foreign shareholder to transmit the capital injection by SWIFT MT103 through a reputable overseas bank to the French SARL's account at a Tier-1 French bank. The beneficiary account must be stated using the French IBAN (FR76 with the appropriate BIC), the transaction must include a transparent purpose code such as ISO 20022 purpose 'INTR' (intra-group capital transaction) or an equivalent corporate treasury code, and the remittance information must state 'Capital increase under French Commercial Code / souscription et libération du capital'. The French bank will verify the subscriber, screen against sanctions and PEP lists, confirm the consistency of the amount with the capital resolution, and issue or recognise the 'certificat de dépôt des fonds' required by the incorporation process. For amounts constituting foreign direct investment, the resident bank reports the BOP category to Banque de France; no prior administrative authorisation is required, but the French SARL must retain the board/shareholder resolution, subscriber identification records, and bank certificate to evidence clean source of funds.For EUR disbursements within the EEA, the SARL should use SEPA Credit Transfer, settled through TARGET2, with the counterparty's IBAN and invoice reference. For non-EEA or multi-currency disbursements, the SARL should use SWIFT MT103 with a UETR tracking reference, clearly stating the purpose code (e.g. 'TRAD' for trade payable or the relevant ISO 20022 code) and the invoice number, with charges arranged as 'SHA' or 'OUR' depending on vendor requirements. For larger trade settlements in goods-related transactions, a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the French bank by SWIFT MT700/MT707 under UCP 600 is a recognised clearing rail, complemented by documentary collections or bank payment undertakings; the SARL's treasury should maintain a sufficient EUR and foreign-currency liquidity buffer to cover settlement date mismatches and fees.Deliverable FX outright forwards (EUR vs foreign currency) with a credit institution or investment firmNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or low-liquidity currencies where physical delivery is impracticalFX swaps (FXS) combining spot and forward legsCross-currency swaps (CCIRS) for asset-liability alignment on cross-border investmentsEUR/foreign-currency options (call/spread and put/spread) with documented underlying commercial exposureCombinations of the above executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or French master derivative documentation, subject to EMIR clearing, margin and trade-reporting obligations
