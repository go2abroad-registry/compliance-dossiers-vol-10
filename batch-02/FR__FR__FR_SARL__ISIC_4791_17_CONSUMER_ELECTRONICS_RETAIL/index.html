Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) held at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce de Paris, receiving filings through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL), L.210-1 et seq. (general company provisions), L.123-1 and L.123-7 (RCS registration); implementing Décret n° 2021-300 and Law n° 2023-171 on the Guichet Unique formalities system; Code monétaire et financier provisions governing blocked capital deposits.

Formation / constitutional filings

Official statutory incorporation form (standard business-creation questionnaire, historically referenced as M0) filed electronically through the Guichet Unique, attaching the signed statuts, attestation de dépôt des fonds, attestation de parution in a legal gazette, identity documents, and beneficial-owner declaration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for a French SARL (Loi Pacte 2019 reform); however at least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation and the amounts blocked in a dedicated escrow bank account (attestation de dépôt des fonds) until RCS registration, with the remaining 80% payable within five years. A practical paid-up capital of €1,000 is recommended to meet payment-processor (Stripe/PayPal) underwriting expectations for online consumer-electronics retail.

Funding & unlock path: Compliance with the French Labour Code (Code du travail) for the gérant and all staff, including mandatory occupational-health monitoring and workplace-risk evaluation; baseline fire safety measures (periodic extinguisher maintenance, electrical installation conformity certificate, emergency exits and signage) are required for any back-office or logistics space; establishments open to the public require a fire-safety commission clearance only if retail premises admit customers, which is not expected in a pure e-commerce model; for headcounts above 50 or designated ICPE sites, DREETS/CARSAT attestation and safety inspections are required.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for a French SARL (Loi Pacte 2019 reform); however at least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation and the amounts blocked in a dedicated escrow bank account (attestation de dépôt des fonds) until RCS registration, with the remaining 80% payable within five years. A practical paid-up capital of €1,000 is recommended to meet payment-processor (Stripe/PayPal) underwriting expectations for online consumer-electronics retail.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED: The founding members, pursuant to Articles L.223-1 through L.223-43 of the French Commercial Code, hereby (1) adopt the articles of association; (2) subscribe the full share capital and acknowledge deposit of cash contributions in the escrow account; (3) adopt the registered office at 12 Rue de la Paix, 75002 Paris; (4) appoint Mr. Jean-Pierre Laurent as sole gérant; (5) ratify the legal gazette notice; and (6) authorize the gérant to file the formation dossiers with the INPI Guichet Unique, obtain the RCS K-bis extract, and complete all tax and social registrations. This resolution takes effect immediately.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Ministère de la Transition Écologique via ADEME (Agence de la Transition Écologique) and the DREETS/SPE regional inspectorate; no dedicated industrial operating license applies to ISIC 4791 online retail, but statutory extended-producer-responsibility (EPR) registration for electrical and electronic equipment is mandatory through the SYDEREP national registry.

Category: No physical industrial license is required for registered online retail of personal electronics and smart gadgets; the relevant mandatory registration is the annual EPR/WEEE scheme enrollment with an approved French eco-organism, supplemented, where any warehouse premises are used, by the ICPE déclaration/enregistrement regime (nomenclature 1510 or 2511) under Articles L.511-1 et seq. of the French Environmental Code.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official impots.gouv.fr portal. Corporate formalities, including registration of a French SARL, are filed through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is incorporated and assigned a SIREN and SIRET through the INPI Guichet unique. The company then activates its professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr and communicates with its competent SIE. The French VAT identification number is issued by the DGFiP and generally has the format FR followed by a two-character control key and the nine-digit SIREN. The company must register for VAT before commencing taxable activities where registration is mandatory, or where it elects to waive the franchise en base de TVA. A non-established foreign entity with French VAT obligations applies to the DGFiP non-resident tax service or the relevant tax office, depending on its establishment and transaction profile. EU distance sales to French consumers may instead be reported through the Union One Stop Shop (OSS), subject to the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold and applicable OSS conditions. Imports may require an EORI number and customs registration through French customs authorities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying SARL may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu, IR), generally subject to statutory conditions including size, age, ownership, and operating-activity requirements; the election is time-limited and must be filed within the statutory deadline. A family SARL may elect IR treatment without the same five-year limitation where the statutory family-ownership and activity conditions are satisfied. France has no United States-style check-the-box election. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the relevant EU ATAD provisions and can deny deductions, impose inclusion, or otherwise neutralize mismatches involving hybrid entities, instruments, or payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic supplies of goods, the principal franchise en base threshold is generally EUR 85,000 of annual turnover, with a tolerance threshold generally of EUR 93,500, subject to the applicable tax year, statutory indexing, and legislative changes. Consumer electronics retail sales are ordinarily taxable at the standard 20% rate. The EU-wide threshold for intra-EU distance sales of goods and certain electronically supplied services is EUR 10,000, calculated under EU rules; above that threshold, destination-country VAT generally applies, subject to use of OSS. A business may voluntarily opt out of the franchise en base and register for VAT below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT where the seller can substantiate exportation and the statutory conditions are met. The primary evidence is the electronic customs export declaration together with confirmation that the goods exited the EU customs territory. Intra-EU supplies to a VAT-identified customer may qualify for the intra-Community exemption where the customer provides a valid VAT number, the goods are transported to another Member State, and the supplier retains prescribed transport and commercial evidence. B2B services supplied to a customer established outside France are generally taxed according to the customer-location rules and may be outside the scope of French VAT rather than technically zero-rated. B2C distance sales and electronically supplied services generally follow destination-based EU VAT rules and may require OSS reporting. Export VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices, customs evidence, accounting records, and proof that the claimant incurred deductible French VAT.

  • Electronic customs export declaration, including the export accompanying reference or equivalent customs reference
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent evidence that the goods exited the European Union
  • Commercial invoice identifying the seller, purchaser, goods, value, and delivery terms
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, airway bill, consignment note, courier record, or carrier delivery evidence
  • Packing list and, where relevant, import documentation or delivery confirmation in the destination country
  • Proof of the purchaser's identity and delivery address
  • For intra-EU exempt supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number and transport evidence
  • Inward remittance evidence is not generally a mandatory French VAT zero-rating document; payment records may nevertheless support the commercial and audit trail

France does not generally operate a universal real-time invoice-clearance system. French VAT invoices must contain the statutory invoice particulars and be issued and retained in a compliant electronic or paper form. The national electronic invoicing reform requires all businesses established in France to be capable of receiving electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Mandatory issuance and related e-reporting are phased: large enterprises and tax groups from 1 September 2026, and small and medium-sized enterprises, including most SARLs, from 1 September 2027, subject to legislative or regulatory amendments. Invoices will generally pass through an approved private platform (plateforme agréée), with public-platform interoperability and structured data reporting. B2C invoices are not generally subject to the B2B electronic-invoicing issuance mandate, although transaction and payment-data reporting may apply in specified cases. French certified cash-register software requirements may apply where the retailer receives payments from non-business customers and is subject to VAT, subject to statutory exemptions.

France has no general 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement for export VAT treatment. The seller must retain reliable evidence that the goods left the EU and must correct or repay VAT where exemption conditions are not met. The DGFiP may reassess unpaid VAT, interest for late payment, and tax penalties. Depending on the conduct and circumstances, penalties may include a 5% penalty for certain late or omitted VAT payments, a 40% surcharge for deliberate failure, and an 80% surcharge for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, in addition to statutory late-payment interest. False customs declarations may trigger customs duties, VAT recovery, customs penalties, seizure, and criminal liability. Input VAT refunds or deductions unsupported by valid invoices or export evidence may be denied or clawed back during audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and generally has full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no general French exchange controls, mandatory conversion of foreign-currency receipts, or restrictions on repatriation of dividends, interest, loan principal, or liquidation proceeds, subject to corporate-law approvals, distributable-profit rules, withholding-tax analysis, transfer-pricing requirements, anti-money-laundering controls, sanctions, and applicable European Union restrictive measures. Cross-border payments must be supported by commercially credible documentation and may be subject to bank transaction monitoring. Payments involving sanctioned jurisdictions, sanctioned persons, unusual third-party beneficiaries, opaque ownership, or unexplained source of funds may be delayed, rejected, or reported. Cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more into or out of the European Union require customs declaration by the person carrying the funds. Certain foreign direct investments in sensitive French sectors may require prior filing or approval; ordinary online consumer-electronics retail is generally outside the principal strategic-sector authorization regime unless the business involves controlled technology, critical infrastructure, or another protected activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no general mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or deposited with a French bank. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be received through payment institutions, subject to contractual bank terms, accounting records, VAT and customs documentation, sanctions screening, and accurate financial reporting. For intra-European Union trade, SEPA transfers and euro settlement are generally available. For extra-European Union exports, the company should retain customs declarations, commercial invoices, transport evidence, payment records, and reconciliation between receivables and bank receipts. Any applicable statistical reporting, balance-of-payments reporting, or bank information request must be satisfied when imposed by the relevant authority or financial institution.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange and payment-services supervision also involves the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution and the Autorité des marchés financiers, as applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for an EEA-authorized electronic money institution or payment institution that is authorized to provide services in France through passporting or a French establishment and can perform French-language corporate KYC. Low for a non-EEA offshore digital EMI without an EU authorization, passport, French branch, or compliant correspondent arrangement. An EMI account is not a bank deposit account; customer funds must be safeguarded under the applicable payment-services regime and may not receive ordinary deposit-guarantee protection. The SARL should verify the provider's authorization in the European Banking Authority or relevant national register, permitted services, safeguarding model, settlement limits, chargeback rules, supported currencies, beneficiary-country restrictions, and ability to issue reliable account statements. A fintech wallet should not be used as the sole treasury account for capital formation, payroll, tax payments, or material supplier concentration without confirming legal and operational suitability.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident shareholder or gérant, particularly where the individual has no French tax residence or operating footprint

Root cause: Non-resident shareholder or gérant, particularly where the individual has no French tax residence or operating footprint

Defense evidence: Certified passport or national identity document; proof of residential address; French company registration extract or draft incorporation documents; proof of registered office; appointment and powers of the gérant; business plan; expected countries, currencies, volumes, and counterparties; tax-residence information; explanation of French commercial substance; and documented source of wealth and source of funds.
Unclear or layered beneficial ownership, nominee shareholders, trusts, bearer-like arrangements, or ownership through multiple jurisdictions

Root cause: Unclear or layered beneficial ownership, nominee shareholders, trusts, bearer-like arrangements, or ownership through multiple jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Current ownership chart to the natural-person ultimate beneficial owners; constitutional documents for every intermediate entity; registers of shareholders and directors; certified corporate extracts; shareholder agreements; control analysis; UBO declarations; identification documents for all controlling persons; and written explanation of each holding entity's commercial purpose.
Inbound capital, shareholder loans, or working-capital funding from an unrelated foreign account

Root cause: Inbound capital, shareholder loans, or working-capital funding from an unrelated foreign account

Defense evidence: Executed capital subscription or loan agreement; board or shareholder approval; remitter-to-owner relationship evidence; audited or management accounts of the remitter where relevant; bank statements; sale-of-business or investment documents; tax filings or wealth evidence; sanctions screening; payment instruction showing the exact corporate purpose; and reconciliation of the incoming amount to the capitalization or loan ledger.
Consumer-electronics e-commerce with high transaction counts, card acquiring, refunds, chargebacks, marketplace activity, or cross-border sales

Root cause: Consumer-electronics e-commerce with high transaction counts, card acquiring, refunds, chargebacks, marketplace activity, or cross-border sales

Defense evidence: Website and terms of sale; product catalogue; supplier invoices; logistics and warehouse agreements; payment-service-provider contracts; chargeback and refund policy; expected monthly transaction profile; customer geography; customs and import records; VAT registrations and filings; proof of product authenticity and warranty arrangements; and documented fraud-prevention controls.
Payments involving high-risk, sanctioned, embargoed, or weakly regulated jurisdictions, or counterparties with adverse media

Defense evidence: Counterparty KYC file; sanctions and politically exposed person screening results; end-use and end-customer statement; commercial contract; shipping and customs documentation; beneficial-ownership confirmation; legal review of applicable European Union and United Nations sanctions; and documented escalation and approval procedure.
Mismatch between declared business activity, projected turnover, payment flows, website content, and tax or customs profile

Root cause: Mismatch between declared business activity, projected turnover, payment flows, website content, and tax or customs profile

Defense evidence: Updated business plan; 12-month cash-flow forecast; management accounts; customer and supplier concentration analysis; marketplace statements; VAT and customs registrations; inventory records; contracts; explanation of seasonal or promotional peaks; and written confirmation of the expected source and destination of funds.
Incomplete French corporate records or failure to demonstrate that the SARL is duly incorporated and authorized to operate the account

Root cause: Incomplete French corporate records or failure to demonstrate that the SARL is duly incorporated and authorized to operate the account

Defense evidence: Kbis extract; articles of association; certificate of capital deposit or incorporation documents; registered-office evidence; beneficial-owner filing evidence; gérant appointment; tax identification details; board or shareholder banking resolution; specimen signatures; and powers of attorney for each account operator.
Requests for third-party payments, personal-account settlement, rapid pass-through transfers, or unexplained cash withdrawals

Root cause: Requests for third-party payments, personal-account settlement, rapid pass-through transfers, or unexplained cash withdrawals

Defense evidence: Written treasury policy; approved payment matrix; contract and invoice linking each payer and beneficiary; explanation of agency or marketplace arrangements; segregation of company and personal funds; reconciliation schedule; transaction-monitoring records; and documented prohibition or escalation process for non-standard settlement instructions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For paid-in SARL capital, obtain the bank's incorporation account before registration, remit funds from an account held in the subscriber's legal name, use a clear payment reference such as 'SARL capital contribution - [company name] - [shareholder name]', and provide the subscription decision, draft articles, identity and ownership documents, source-of-funds evidence, and remitting-bank details. The bank issues a certificate of deposit after satisfactory verification; the funds are released to the operational account after incorporation and delivery of the Kbis or equivalent registration evidence. For shareholder loans or additional paid-in funding, use a written loan or contribution agreement, corporate approval, transfer-pricing and interest analysis where relevant, and a payment reference identifying the legal basis. EUR payments should normally use SEPA Credit Transfer where available. Non-EUR or non-SEPA payments should use SWIFT with complete originator, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, purpose, and invoice or agreement references. The company should reconcile the bank receipt to its general ledger, capital or loan account, and supporting corporate approvals. Do not route capital through personal accounts, unrelated third parties, anonymous wallets, or unsupported offshore intermediaries.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro suppliers within the SEPA area, subject to bank limits and fraud controls. Use SWIFT MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent for non-SEPA or foreign-currency payments, with complete beneficiary legal name, address, account or IBAN, BIC, intermediary-bank data, payment purpose, invoice number, and allocation of bank charges. Commercial letters of credit may be issued or advised through a French bank under UCP 600, with documentary requirements aligned to the purchase contract, Incoterms, transport document, insurance, inspection, and customs responsibilities. Documentary collections under URC 522 may be used where appropriate. For consumer-electronics imports, retain purchase orders, commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading or air waybills, customs declarations, tariff classification, origin evidence, import VAT records, and sanctions or export-control screening. Apply dual-approval controls, beneficiary-change call-backs, segregation of duties, payment-limit thresholds, and three-way matching between purchase order, receipt, and invoice. Pay only the contractual supplier or a documented and bank-approved financing or logistics intermediary.Deliverable FX forwards for documented forecast or committed EUR foreign-currency payables and receivables, subject to bank credit approval, suitability assessment, treasury policy, confirmation controls, and accounting treatment.FX swaps for short-term liquidity management and currency conversion, subject to counterparty documentation, maturity controls, collateral or credit limits, and transaction reporting requirements where applicable.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies subject to convertibility or settlement restrictions, where offered by an appropriately authorized counterparty and used for a genuine underlying exposure; settlement is generally by net cash difference rather than physical delivery.FX options or option structures may be used only after assessing premium, liquidity, downside risk, suitability, and whether the structure creates disproportionate or speculative exposure.Treasury documentation should normally include an ISDA Master Agreement or equivalent bank documentation, an LEI where required, confirmations, exposure registers, hedge designations, valuation records, collateral terms, EMIR and MiFID II classification analysis, and evidence that derivatives hedge identifiable commercial exposures rather than constitute unauthorized speculative activity.
