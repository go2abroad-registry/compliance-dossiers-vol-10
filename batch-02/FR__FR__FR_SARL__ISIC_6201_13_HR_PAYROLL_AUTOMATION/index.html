Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum legal capital; capital is fixed in the statutes. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation and deposited in a blocked escrow account with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; in-kind contributions require a commissaire aux apports report if the value exceeds EUR 30,000 or represents more than 50% of the capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) is required for any office premises open to employees or customers; a structural load inspection is not needed for standard commercial leases, but workplace electrical and ventilation certificates are mandatory under the French Labor Code (Articles R.4222-1 and R.4227-1 et seq.); occupational clearance is evidenced by a formal health and safety risk assessment (document unique) maintained in the workplace.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum legal capital; capital is fixed in the statutes. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and paid up at incorporation and deposited in a blocked escrow account with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; in-kind contributions require a commissaire aux apports report if the value exceeds EUR 30,000 or represents more than 50% of the capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French General Directorate of Public Finances, Ministry of Economy and Finance; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; company registration portal: guichet-entreprises.fr

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, incorporation is filed online through the Guichet Unique (formalités entreprises); INSEE then issues SIREN and SIRET numbers, and DGFiP automatically allocates a French intra-community VAT number (FR + key + SIREN) to established taxable persons. A foreign entity with no French establishment deriving taxable supplies from France must apply for a French VAT number by filing Form CA16 with the DGFiP (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) and, where applicable, use the non-Union OSS or Union OSS scheme for distance sales and certain B2C services.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a French SARL is the legal taxpayer for French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on its worldwide profits when tax resident in France; non-resident entities remain taxable in France only to the extent of a French permanent establishment or stabilized French-source income. — For a French SARL, incorporation is filed online through the Guichet Unique (formalités entreprises); INSEE then issues SIREN and SIRET numbers, and DGFiP automatically allocates a French intra-community VAT number (FR + key + SIREN) to established taxable persons. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a French SARL is the legal taxpayer for French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on its worldwide profits when tax resident in France; non-resident entities remain taxable in France only to the extent of a French permanent establishment or stabilized French-source income.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under French law. A SARL may in limited cases opt for pass-through taxation as a société de personnes, notably a SARL de famille (CGI Art. 239) when all partners are within the same family, or, for certain eligible small enterprises, under Article 239 bis AA for a five-year corporate income tax option; elections are filed with the DGFiP and are subject to statutory conditions. Hybrid mismatch recategorizations under EU ATAD are mandatory and automatic, not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For businesses established in France, the small-business exemption/toleration threshold is EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services; foreign entities making taxable French supplies must register without any local turnover franche, except that intra-EU cross-border distance sales of goods benefit from an annual EUR 10,000 OSS threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of goods from France to a destination outside the EU is zero-rated at 0% VAT if the supplier retains sufficient documentary proof of actual export and the goods are removed from France within the statutory timeline; certain services directly connected to exported goods, and services supplied B2B to non-EU recipients under the French place-of-supply rules, may also qualify for zero-rating or placement outside the scope of French VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / DAU) with movement reference number
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note
  • Commercial invoice and customs value evidence
  • Proof of receipt of consideration / inward remittance certificate or bank swift
  • Export register with commodity code, exporter, consignee, destination and dates

Mandatory structured e-invoicing (Factur-X, UBL, or CII) and e-reporting to the DGFiP are being phased in from 1 September 2026 through 2027 via the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); invoices must be transmitted through an immatriculated dematerialization platform or the PPF API. France does not use pre-clearance digital stamping; instead, e-reporting data is transmitted near-real-time and subjected to automated audit risk-scoring algorithms.

The DGFiP may retrospectively reverse export zero-rating within the standard three-year assessment period, extended to six years for hidden activities or offshore havens; if export documentation is missing, misleading or false, the VAT is clawed back at 20% plus monthly late-payment interest, and a 40% bad-faith penalty or 80% abusive-practice penalty may be applied; severe fraud and customs misdeclaration may attract criminal fines up to the value of the goods and imprisonment.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in France for both current account and capital account transactions under TFEU Articles 63-66. No exchange-control authorization, currency quota, or mandatory conversion applies to software export receipts, client invoicing, inbound equity injections, intra-group loans, or profit remittances. Dividends and other profit distributions to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted from an FX perspective, but banks require standard AML/KYC evidence, a tax residence certificate, current SARL Kbis, corporate authorization, and, where treaty relief is claimed, appropriate withholding-tax documentation. Existing limitations are limited to EU/UN financial sanctions, asset freezes, and temporary safeguard measures under Article 66 TFEU.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation, or conversion requirement for export proceeds. There is no mandatory timeline and no minimum conversion ratio; the effective required conversion ratio is 0%. Exporters may keep foreign currency abroad for legitimate treasury needs. French resident credit institutions are required to capture cross-border transactions for the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical returns; for typical SMEs this reporting is performed via the bank rather than by the exporting company itself.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor for external financial relations and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking and AML/CFT supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions passporting into France under PSD2 through an ACPR notification; Low/Nil for offshore digital EMIs based outside the EEA or relying on a revoked UK passport. An offshore EMI cannot legally maintain a French SARL payroll account or hold client funds because it lacks authorization under Article L.521-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code. An EU-licensed EMI may be used as an ancillary non-settlement rail for high-speed, low-value SEPA contractor payouts, but the master payroll account and payroll-fund custody must remain at an ACPR-licensed credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For EUR capital contributions from an EU/EEA shareholder, use a SEPA Credit Transfer in ISO 20022 pain.001 with the bank-specific purpose code for 'capital increase / dépôt de fonds' or, where the bank accepts it, Category Purpose INTC for intra-company payments. For USD, GBP, or non-EEA inbound funding, use SWIFT MT103 or MT103 COV where intermediary banks are involved. The remittance information must explicitly state 'FR_SARL capital injection / share subscription'; it must not describe the payment as goods, services, or fees. Delivery documents include notarised subscription minutes for capital increases, updated UBO registration, current extrait Kbis, shareholder resolution, source-of-funds file, and, if applicable, a shareholder loan agreement with documented arm's-length terms. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate; 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for eligible SMEs

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under domestic law (CGI Article 119 bis); reduced to 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (≥10% holding for two years) and to treaty rates (e.g., 0-15%) under applicable DTA; 75% if paid to a non-cooperative jurisdiction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal years ending 31 December, the return is due by midnight on the date published by the administration in May of the following year (typically 3 May for online filers); for other fiscal year-ends, within three months of the fiscal year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Frame 14 - Résultat fiscal (taxable income) Enter the net taxable income after adjustment of accounting profit (add non-deductible expenses, deduct profits/losses of operations allocated to France). If a foreign company carries on a business in France through a permanent establishment, include only the PE's attributable income and expenses; losses of the foreign head office may not be set off against French PE profits. Late filing: 10% surcharge (spontaneous correction) or 40% after formal demand (80% for illegal activity); monthly late-payment interest of 0.20%; additional €10,000 fine for non-electronic filing; parallel criminal penalties in cases of fraud.
Frame 15 - Déficits ordinaires reportables (ordinary deficit carryforward) Report prior-year deficits used against current-year taxable profits, respecting the annual cap of €1,000,000 plus 50% of taxable profit exceeding €1,000,000. Non-resident companies can offset deficits incurred within the French PE, but losses from their home country cannot be used in France. Late filing: 10% surcharge (spontaneous correction) or 40% after formal demand (80% for illegal activity); monthly late-payment interest of 0.20%; additional €10,000 fine for non-electronic filing; parallel criminal penalties in cases of fraud.
Box 16 - Crédits d'impôt (tax credits) Enter tax credits such as Crédit Impôt Recherche, foreign tax credits taken under a DTA, and other recoverable credits. Attach the TRC and detail supporting the credit; non-residents may claim refunds on form 2065 if a French PE exists; taxes withheld at source on French income may be credited. Late filing: 10% surcharge (spontaneous correction) or 40% after formal demand (80% for illegal activity); monthly late-payment interest of 0.20%; additional €10,000 fine for non-electronic filing; parallel criminal penalties in cases of fraud.
Box 17 - Transactions with related entities Report intercompany transactions with related entities, including cost-sharing arrangements and IP services, required for transfer-pricing assessment. Non-resident parent with French subsidiary or PE must ensure that Master File/Local File documentation is available to the French tax authorities on demand. Late filing: 10% surcharge (spontaneous correction) or 40% after formal demand (80% for illegal activity); monthly late-payment interest of 0.20%; additional €10,000 fine for non-electronic filing; parallel criminal penalties in cases of fraud.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The obligation to prepare and file transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) applies where the annual consolidated group revenue or total assets exceed EUR 400,000,000; smaller entities must nevertheless provide basic documentation on request.

Economic substance scope: France applies the OECD arm's-length principle and a substance-over-form approach; a discrete Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) statute is not in force, but an entity lacking business substance, employees, premises, and local management is exposed to recharacterization under the abuse-of-law doctrine, ATAD CFC rules, and permanent establishment attribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full avoidance amount plus 0.20% monthly interest; penalty of 40% of additional tax after a formal demand (80% in cases of concealed activity); criminal fine of €15,000 or €30,000 and imprisonment of up to five years (CGI Article 1741).

Director personal liability: The manager (gérant) and participants may be held jointly and severally liable; courts may lift the corporate veil, issue travel bans, freeze assets, and disqualify the director from managing companies.
Transfer pricing profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment restating French taxable profits under Article 57 CGI; 0.20% monthly interest; additional 40% penalty for deliberate inaccuracies; failure to produce TP documentation triggers a penalty equal to 5% of the reassessed amount (minimum €10,000) under CGI Article 1735.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer-pricing documents face personal liability for resulting tax liabilities and fines; severe cases may attract criminal sanctions for fiscal fraud.
Shell company / abuse of law

Statutory sanction: Invocation of the fraudulent abuse-of-law procedure re-characterizes the arrangement and imposes an 80% penalty (or 40% if not fraudulent); the entity's registration may be cancelled and unpaid taxes become immediately due.

Director personal liability: Managers who set up or use a shell SARL may be declared jointly liable for understated corporate taxes; civil restitution petitions, asset attachments, and travel restrictions may be ordered; anti-money-laundering charges can also be brought.
AML / beneficial-ownership non-disclosure

Statutory sanction: Fine up to €375,000 and imprisonment of five years for AML non-disclosure under the French Monetary and Financial Code; the RCS may order rectification and administrative injunctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners can be held personally liable for fines, compelled to surrender passports, and charged with money-laundering offenses if funds are laundered through programmed payroll payments; professional bans may be imposed.
