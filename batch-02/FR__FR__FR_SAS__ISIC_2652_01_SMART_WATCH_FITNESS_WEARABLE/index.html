Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The capital is fixed in the articles of association; cash subscriptions must be deposited into a blocked bank account in France or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the company is registered. In-kind contributions must be valued by a commissaire aux apports unless all shareholders waive appointing an auditor. Funds are released only after successful registration and issuance of the Kbis extract.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the impots.gouv.fr portal and the Online TIN (Numéro de TVA Intracommunautaire) validation system; corporate income tax filings are made through the DGFiP professional online portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a French SAS must file statutory incorporation documents with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the Guichet Unique (formalités.entreprises.gouv.fr). Upon completion, the entity receives its SIREN and SIRET numbers and the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) issues a French intra-community VAT number enabling VAT registration and subsequent EU-wide transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a French SAS must file statutory incorporation documents with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the Guichet Unique (formalités.entreprises.gouv.fr). Upon completion, the entity receives its SIREN and SIRET numbers and the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) issues a French intra-community VAT number enabling VAT registration and subsequent EU-wide transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No general check-the-box regime is available in France. The SAS is per se subject to French corporate income tax, but an irrevocable option may be filed to be taxed as a société de personnes (partnership) for five years; conversely, certain partnership forms may elect corporate income taxation. Hybrid mismatch rules under the French transposition of ATAD apply to deny deductions or impose recharacterisation where asymmetric tax treatment arises across jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 annual taxable turnover for most supply-of-goods activities (EUR 37,500 for certain services), with the same threshold generally applying to foreign suppliers conducting distance sales of goods into France; reduced thresholds may apply for supplies of e-services under the OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A taxable person may apply a 0% VAT rate on the export of goods outside the EU when the goods are physically dispatched or transported to a non-EU destination, with the supplier retaining proof of export; services rendered directly to overseas customers and certain intangible supplies linked to exported goods may be zero-rated, provided the effective benefit or use is outside the EU and the general conditions of Article 260 undecies and CGI Article 262 are met.

Mandatory e-invoicing for domestic business-to-business transactions is phased in from 1 September 2026 for all entities (full deployment by 1 September 2027), requiring issuance through registered PDP platforms connected to the DGFiP's Chorus Pro clearance infrastructure. The existing real-time reporting for B2C transactions via the Fractional VAT Payment scheme utilises Chorus Pro and requires data transmission upon invoicing, with fiscal data clearing via APIs and digital stamping to authenticate and report transactions.

False or abusive export zero-rating claims trigger full VAT clawback with interest at the statutory rate, a fine of up to 80% of the tax evaded in bad-faith cases, and potential criminal penalties of up to EUR 750,000 per manager and five years' imprisonment for tax fraud under Article 1741 of the French General Tax Code; administrative penalties include a 40% negligence penalty or 80% intentional misconduct penalty, and officers may be held jointly liable under Article L.267 of the French Tax Procedure Book.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in France: current-account and capital-account transactions are in principle unrestricted, with no FX licence, allocation, prior approval or repatriation ceiling for trade, profit, dividend or royalty remittances. France is bound by the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements and by EU Treaty freedoms. Residual frictions are limited to (i) the prior-authorisation regime for non-EU direct investments in sensitive or FDI-listed sectors (art. L151-3-2 CMF covering defence, cybersecurity, critical infrastructure and certain sensitive technologies; consumer-wearable electronics manufacturing is not listed); (ii) statistical reporting to Banque de France for selected cross-border financial flows; and (iii) the EUR 10,000 cash/bearer-instrument declaration at border crossings. Profit remittance is subject only to standard corporate formalities (audited accounts, board resolution, recipient tax-residence certificate) and separately to the applicable withholding-tax treaty regime; it is not an exchange-control hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory repatriation or forced conversion ratio applies to French exporters. A French SAS may keep export receipts in EUR or in foreign-currency accounts ('comptes en devises') at any EU/EEA bank without central-bank approval. Remaining obligations are transparency-type: (i) monthly/periodic customs statistical declarations ('Declaration d'Echanges de Biens' / DEB) for extra-EU and intra-EU trade via the French Customs administration; (ii) a prior customs declaration for physical cash or bearer instruments equal to or exceeding EUR 10,000 transported across the border; and (iii) full traceability of export proceeds to underlying invoices and customers for AML/KYT purposes. No forced conversion timeline is imposed at any stage.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French central bank; Eurosystem/ESCB member) as monetary, financial-stability and balance-of-payments regulator, jointly with the Direction Generale du Tresor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy and Finance), which administers foreign-exchange control ('relations financieres avec l'etranger') and direct-investment screening under Code Monetaire et Financier (CMF) Articles L151-1 to L151-4. Banque de France operates the TARGET2 settlement interface and collects statutory balance-of-payments statistics from resident companies; no separate exchange-control commission exists in France for ordinary corporate flows.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat rate for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2022 (ordinary corporate income tax, French: impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Reduced rate of 15% applies under conditions to taxable profits up to EUR 42,000 for enterprises with annual turnover below EUR 10 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on gross French-source dividends distributed by the French SAS to non-resident shareholders unless an EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption or a Double Tax Agreement reduction applies. For treaty-eligible shareholders, the rate is reduced, commonly to 0%, 5%, or 15% depending on the residence state and ownership threshold.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual income tax return must be filed no later than the second business day after 1 May of the year following the close of the fiscal year. For fiscal years ending on 31 December, the deadline is typically 2 May or the first business day thereafter if 1 May falls on a non-business day. Non-calendar fiscal years must file by the second business day after 1 May following the end of the fiscal year; a specific deadline is determined by the tax administration. Tax payments are made through four quarterly instalments, with the balance due by the statutory filing deadline.

Box 2059-A: Transfer Pricing Annexe Annual transfer pricing documentation annexe (Formulaire 2059-A) required for companies exceeding EUR 400 million in turnover and for entities controlled by a foreign group; includes a summary of related-party transactions, methods applied, and functional analysis. The non-resident parent must ensure that the French SAS discloses all transactions with related enterprises established outside France, including intercompany purchase of components, toll manufacturing contracts, IP licenses, management fees, and financial transactions; provide the group allocation of profits and the arm's length policy. Late filing of the corporate income tax return triggers a penalty equal to 10% of the tax due if the return is filed within 30 days of the deadline, increased to 40% if filed after 30 days. Interest for late payment (intérêt de retard) accrues at the statutory monthly rate of 0.20% (annualised 2.69% for 2024) on the unpaid tax. Non-filing, incomplete filing, or fraudulent return carries a 80% tax penalty if the administration establishes deliberate evasion, plus a minimum EUR 1,000 bank charges penalty. For manifestly incorrect or omitted declarations, a 40% penalty applies. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The French transfer pricing documentation (fichier documentation) must be prepared and filed by companies exceeding EUR 50 million in annual revenue (CA HT, consolidated turnover) where the entity is controlled by or controls foreign enterprises, or where the entity is a French resident enterprise with related-party transactions exceeding EUR 400,000 in total per year; in all cases the full Master File and Local File must be established in French or English and be submitted upon request. The simplified transfer pricing annexe (Formulaire 2059-A) is mandatory for enterprises with annual revenue (excluding tax) of at least EUR 400 million. For a smartwatch manufacturing SAS, documentation thresholds are assessed separately; even below the EUR 400 million threshold, the company must maintain contemporaneous documentation for controlled transactions involving the import of components, assembly labour, IP financing, and royalties.

Economic substance scope: France applies the Economic Substance Doctrine under general anti-abuse provisions (Article 102 of the French Tax Code, l'abus de droit) and into force through the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) and the recent DAC6/Ministerial Regulations (loi anti-abus). For the wearable electronics sector, the doctrine requires the French SAS to demonstrate real managerial and operational substance in France: actual hiring of technical and commercial personnel, physical presence of production support and after-sales functions, control over inventory and logistics, commercial decision-making autonomy, and financial substance (bank accounts, treasury management, invoicing and collection). A French SAS that acts as a pure shell or flow-through entity, whose board decisions are effectively made by a foreign parent, and whose assets, functions, and risks are not aligned with reported income, can be disregarded for tax purposes, resulting in reallocation of profits to the non-resident enterprise and full recharacterisation of transactions. The French tax administration also applies the 'substance over form' rule and can invoke the abuse of law procedure (retour à la lettre de la loi) to recharacterise artificial arrangements; mandatory disclosure rules require cross-border arrangements containing hallmarks connected to economic substance to be reported to the French tax authorities. For entities engaging in intercompany distribution of smartwatches, the French administration requires a functional and risk analysis (FAR) demonstrating that the SAS performs the core marketing, warranty, credit risk, and regulatory compliance activities and that it retains a commensurate profit under OECD TP principles.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 (up to EUR 2,000,000 in case of aggravated circumstances) and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalty under Article 1729 of the CGI equal to 80% of the tax understated where bad faith intent is proven. Automatic publication of administrative sanctions under the 'Name and Shame' scheme, and loss of certain tax advantages and public procurement rights.

Director personal liability: The managing director and de facto directors of the SAS are jointly and severally liable for tax debts if they have participated in the fraud. Under Article L.267 of the Livre des procédures fiscales, a president or general manager who committed fraud or who was grossly negligent in the management of the corporation may be personally liable for all tax penalties and interest. Directors face a travel ban from leaving French territory (interdiction de quitter le territoire) during tax enforcement procedures, an asset freeze order (saisie conservatoire), and criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years for tax fraud. The corporate veil is pierced where the entity is deemed fictitious or the transactions are considered sham; directors lose the limitation liability shield of the SAS.
Shell Company or Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The French tax administration may recharacterise the entire structure under Article 1651 of the CGI and the OECD Model Treaty Article on Limitation on Benefits, denying treaty benefits and imposing the 25% withholding on deemed distributions, a 15% withholding on deemed royalties, and reallocating profits to the parent; penalties of 80% for concealed activity and interest at 0.20% per month. Under EU law, the anti-abuse Directive (2016/1164) and OECD BEPS Action 6, the entity may be denied any DTA relief. The corporate entity may also be struck off the French Trade and Companies Register if the French tax authorities or the court determine that the registered head office is fictitious; administrative dissolution of the entity is possible under Article L.235-3 of the Companies Code.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly causing the creation of a shell company can face personal obligations for corporate taxes under the judicial liquidation procedure (action en complément de passif) and may be declared personally bankrupt (faillite personnelle) for mixing corporate assets with personal assets, misusing corporate property, or continuing a sham business. The court may order a temporary prohibition on managing any enterprise and a travel ban; the president of the SAS may be regarded as the sole decision-making person and bear full liability for the full amount of unpaid taxes plus damages to creditors.
Money Laundering and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: France classifies the manufacturing of electronic equipment as part of the non-financial sector subject to AML obligations only if the entity acts as a DNFBP (professionnel de la vente de biens, or auctioneer/casino businesses); however, if the entity is used for laundering the proceeds of tax evasion, the sanction is a criminal fine of up to EUR 750,000 (10% of the laundered principal) and imprisonment up to 10 years under Articles 324-1 to 324-6 of the French Criminal Code. Administrative fines under SARFIG (Service à competence nationale) can reach EUR 5 million or twice the value of the misused assets. French TRACFIN (financial intelligence) will receive reports, and repeat offenders face enhanced penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in laundering or fail to comply with binding AML obligations (KYC, beneficial ownership identification, systematic reporting to TRACFIN for suspicious transactions) are criminally liable; the corporate veil is pierced for wilful blindness. Directors face imprisonment, fines, lifelong prohibition from managing any French company, and freezing of personal assets located in France. With the entry into force of the French Fifth AML Directive transposition (Code monétaire et financier), the inability to prove an honest source of the funds may lead to a presumption of guilt.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Related-Party Pricing Adjustment

Statutory sanction: Penalty under Article 1735 ter B of the CGI: EUR 15,000 per fiscal year for absence or non-submission of transfer pricing documentation, plus a further EUR 5,000 per undisclosed related enterprise or per incomplete documentation. If documentation is submitted within more than 30 days, the penalty increases to EUR 20,000. If the tax administration adjusts the taxable base, the 40% penalty for lack of good faith applies, and the burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer; the tax adjustment is also subject to interest. For enterprises with revenue above EUR 400 million that fail to file the simplified declaration, the penalty is EUR 150,000 additionally.

Director personal liability: The president and other directors of the SAS are personally responsible for ensuring the documentation obligation is fulfilled. If the failure to document is coupled with intentional misrepresentation of intercompany pricing, the directors risk criminal tax fraud charges; the corporate veil can be lifted by a commercial court in the event of gross negligence leading to the company's insolvency (action en responsabilité pour insuffisance d'actif). Where the transfer pricing adjustment results in a criminal conviction, the director may be banned from managing any French company for 5 years and be ordered to restitute the tax savings to the treasury.
Profit Repatriation and Dividend Distribution Without Withholding Compliance

Statutory sanction: The French tax administration can reassess the company for the unpaid withholding (retenue à la source), impose a 40% penalty for failure to withhold, plus late interest at 0.20% per month. The company is denied any deduction for the fees or royalties that were deemed concealed distributions. In addition, if the shareholder is a low-tax jurisdiction resident and the anti-abuse rule applies, the withholding rate rises to 75% (Article 119 quater 2 bis) and the recipient is denied all treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved or executed a distribution without compliance may be jointly liable for the withholding tax debt under the general tax liability provisions and may face the 'responsabilité solidaire' of the tax procedure; if the distribution is legally disguised as a sham, the president and financial director will be personally liable for the entire amount of the unpaid tax and the 40% fraud penalty, and may receive a travel ban under enforcement proceedings.
