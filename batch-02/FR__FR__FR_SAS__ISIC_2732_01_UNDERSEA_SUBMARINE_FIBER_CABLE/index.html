Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent commercial court clerk (greffe du tribunal de commerce) maintains the Trade and Companies Register (Registre du commerce et des sociétés, RCS). The incorporation application is submitted through the national company formalities portal operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L. 210-1 et seq. (commercial companies) and L. 227-1 to L. 227-20 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); applicable provisions of the Civil Code and the Monetary and Financial Code also apply.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company-formation formalité (formerly the M0 declaration), submitted through the Guichet unique, supported by the signed articles of association and required attachments; registration results in RCS registration and issuance of a SIREN number and Kbis extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to a SAS; capital must be fixed in the articles. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription, with the balance payable within five years of RCS registration. Cash contributions are deposited with a bank, notary, or other legally authorized depositary before registration; no general statutory escrow-account requirement applies. Contributions in kind are fully contributed at formation and may require a commissaire aux apports, subject to statutory exceptions.

Funding & unlock path: Before operation, obtain applicable planning permission and building authorization, comply with the French Construction and Housing Code and fire-safety rules applicable to the building’s use, and complete required fire-protection measures and inspections. Structural design and floor loading must be certified as appropriate for machinery and storage. The employer must complete workplace risk assessment (DUERP), implement occupational health and safety measures, arrange required occupational health services, and satisfy labor-inspection and worker-safety obligations. Additional fire-service or public-access-building approvals apply if the premises fall within the relevant classification.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies to a SAS; capital must be fixed in the articles. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription, with the balance payable within five years of RCS registration. Cash contributions are deposited with a bank, notary, or other legally authorized depositary before registration; no general statutory escrow-account requirement applies. Contributions in kind are fully contributed at formation and may require a commissaire aux apports, subject to statutory exceptions.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The undersigned President and directors of [Company Name], a société par actions simplifiée in formation, resolve as follows: (1) to acknowledge and approve the subscriptions set out in the executed articles of association and confirm that the required initial cash contributions have been deposited with the designated depositary; (2) to adopt [full address] as the registered office; (3) to appoint [name] as President and [name(s), if applicable] as Directeur Général, on the terms stated in the articles or appointment instruments; and (4) to authorize the President to complete registration formalities, open the company’s operating bank account, and obtain all registrations and permits required for its activities. These resolutions take effect upon signature.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

No general industrial licensing board or sector-wide manufacturing permit applies solely because the company manufactures submarine fiber-optic cables. The prefect of the department where the facility is located is the competent authority for applicable ICPE procedures, supported by the DREAL (Direction régionale de l’environnement, de l’aménagement et du logement); workplace and construction requirements are administered by the relevant labor, planning, and building authorities.

Category: No universal operating or manufacturing license. Depending on site activities and thresholds, the facility may require an ICPE declaration, registration, or environmental authorization under the Environmental Code, together with applicable planning and building permits.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy and Finance, 10 rue du Centre, Noisy-le-Grand; corporate portal: impots.gouv.fr; single-window incorporation e-filing at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique administered by INPI); customs and export clearance authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via the DELTA-X platform (douane.gouv.fr); non-resident VAT registration through the Service des impôts des entreprises non-résidentes (DENI, Paris).

E-government filing portal

At incorporation, the Guichet Unique transmits the SAS registration to INSEE, which issues SIREN, SIRET and the APE/NAF code 27.32Z; within 5 to 10 business days the DGFiP allocates the French intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN. Existing SAS entities file modifications via the Guichet Unique or form 3726 at the competent SIE. A foreign company acting through a French fixed establishment without a legal seat files under Article 286 quater CGI at the DENI to obtain a SIRET and a French VAT number within 4 to 6 weeks. Before the first customs export declaration, the manufacturer must also obtain an EORI number (starting FR) from the DGDDI.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SAS is mandatorily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) under Article 206-1 CGI at 25% (reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profits for SMEs); no pass-through option exists. Under Article 209-I CGI territoriality, profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment are generally excluded unless the worldwide-consolidation option is exercised; the SAS manufacturing profits are taxable in France. — At incorporation, the Guichet Unique transmits the SAS registration to INSEE, which issues SIREN, SIRET and the APE/NAF code 27.32Z; within 5 to 10 business days the DGFiP allocates the French intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SAS is mandatorily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) under Article 206-1 CGI at 25% (reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profits for SMEs); no pass-through option exists. Under Article 209-I CGI territoriality, profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment are generally excluded unless the worldwide-consolidation option is exercised; the SAS manufacturing profits are taxable in France.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in French law; SAS tax identity is fixed and non- electable. Available statutory elections: (1) fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for a group head holding at least 95% of French subsidiaries; (2) the Article 209 A CGI option to include foreign branch income on a worldwide basis (irrevocable 5-year term; the 'bénéfice mondial' variant requires ministerial authorization); (3) waiver of the VAT franchise under Article 293 F CGI; (4) choice between the régime réel normal and régime simplifié d'imposition for filing; and (5) mandatory hybrid-mismatch disclosure under Article 212 bis II CGI and DAC6 (form 2258-SD), due within 45 days of any hybrid arrangement — no opt-out election is granted; penalties reach EUR 1,500 per missing arrangement plus 5% of the value of relevant transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA: EUR 91,900 annual turnover for principal supplies of goods (2025 barème, revalued annually) and EUR 36,800 for services; for this subsea-cable export manufacturer, VAT identification is mandatory from the first transaction because exports are exempt-with-credit: the SAS must hold a VAT number to zero-rate export invoices, to recover input VAT (annual input credits exceeding EUR 760 refundable via form 3519-SD), and to report intra-EU dispatches; registration is also required where intra-EU acquisitions exceed EUR 10,000 per calendar year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Definitive export of armored subsea fiber cables out of EU customs territory qualifies for 0% VAT (exemption-with-credit) under Article 262-I-1° CGI where the seller proves physical dispatch outside the EU and transfer of ownership; the invoice must mention 'Exonération TVA – article 262-I-1° du CGI' and be backed by the registered customs export declaration. Services directly connected to the export, including offshore cable laying, engineering and commissioning outside the EU, are exempt under Article 262-I-2° CGI; B2B services supplied to non-EU telecom operators are outside French VAT under Article 259-1° CGI. Deliveries to another EU Member State (cross-border cable landings) rely on the intra-Community exemption under Article 262 ter CGI, conditional on the customer's valid EU VAT number (VIES verification). Supplies delivered and installed within French territory, including French territorial waters, remain taxable at 20%.

  • Customs Export Declaration — DAU (Document Administratif Unique) / SAD lodged via DELTA-X with exit confirmation stamp
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or multimodal) or Air Waybill naming the non-EU consignee and final cable destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit confirmation or government Letter of Credit drawdown record evidencing receipt of export proceeds on the French account
  • Commercial invoice (original) stating Article 262-I-1° CGI export VAT exemption, Incoterms and full shipment particulars
  • Packing list and transport insurance certificate corroborating the export movement
  • EORI number (FR) and, where applicable, REX (Registered Exporter) number for preferential origin declarations
  • Non-EU customer's purchase order and delivery acceptance protocol evidencing transfer of title and risk

France mandates nationwide B2B e-invoicing from 1 September 2026: all VAT-taxable persons must receive e-invoices and large/mid-sized enterprises must issue them; issuance becomes compulsory for SMEs and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be transmitted via a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) in Factur-X (hybrid PDF/XML), UBL or CII structured formats, each carrying the SIREN-derived unique invoice identifier and both parties' VAT numbers; transactional e-reporting to the DGFiP occurs within one business day of the invoice event, covering sales, purchases and payment data; there is no pre-issuance fiscal clearance stamp or per-invoice digital duty regime, but transmission integrity and the platform audit trail constitute the mandatory VAT evidence, with a EUR 15 fixed penalty per non-compliant invoice under Article 1737 CGI.

Export transactions are a declared priority of the DGFiP fiscal control programme; the DGDDI export declaration file is automatically cross-checked against VAT returns, and any missing customs or banking proof triggers re-taxation of the export at 20% with monthly late-payment interest (0.20% per month in 2025, Article 1727 CGI) and penalties of 40% (gross negligence) or 80% (fraud) under Article 1729 CGI. Non-repatriation of proceeds within 180 days breaches Article L112-4 of the Code monétaire et financier and the DGDDI exchange-control rules, exposing the SAS to civil sanctions of 1x to 2x the un-repatriated amount and suspension of export authorizations. Criminal exposure includes Article 1741 CGI tax fraud (up to EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years' imprisonment) and customs smuggling penalties under Articles 414-2 and 415 of the Code des douanes (confiscation of the cable shipment plus a fine of 1x to 2x its value). The standard three-year VAT reassessment window under Article L176 LPF extends to ten years for hidden activities or organized fraudulent export schemes; input VAT refunds on export activity remain claimable monthly or quarterly via form 3519-SD.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a founding Eurozone member, maintains full convertibility for both current and capital accounts; profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation face no regulatory hurdles for qualifying French SAS entities meeting standard tax and reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds; French entities must declare all export revenues under VAT and corporate tax regimes, with proceeds freely repatriable subject to standard AML/KYC verification and tax filing requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France
