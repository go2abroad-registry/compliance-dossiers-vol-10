Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Relance; taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; business-registry and tax-initiation portal via the INPI Guichet Unique (formalités@inpi.fr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity needing a French tax identification number must file an RCS company registration (for an established permanent establishment or branch) through the Guichet Unique, or use the non-resident VAT registration procedure with the DGFiP Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) handling non-established suppliers. Where the foreign entity has no PE and only makes taxable supplies in France, it must register as a non-established taxable person for TVA purposes and may be required to appoint an accredited tax representative (représentant fiscal) if it is established outside the EU and receives B2C supplies requiring French VAT collection. The DGFiP issues the SIREN/SIRET and the individual TVA intra-community identification number (FR + 11 digits).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity needing a French tax identification number must file an RCS company registration (for an established permanent establishment or branch) through the Guichet Unique, or use the non-resident VAT registration procedure with the DGFiP Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) handling non-established suppliers. Where the foreign entity has no PE and only makes taxable supplies in France, it must register as a non-established taxable person for TVA purposes and may be required to appoint an accredited tax representative (représentant fiscal) if it is established outside the EU and receives B2C supplies requiring French VAT collection. The DGFiP issues the SIREN/SIRET and the individual TVA intra-community identification number (FR + 11 digits).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

There is no generic check-the-box election in France. A SARL is by default subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at entity level. Under CGI Article 239 bis AA, a SARL can elect for fiscal transparency (taxation in the hands of individual shareholders) if all shareholders are natural persons and no corporate member remains; the election is made by notification before the due date of the first profit-making year and is irrevocable for five years. For cross-border hybrid mismatch treatment, France applies the ATAD anti-hybrid rules (CGI Art. 212 bis, 209 B and 209 C) and no separate hybrid mismatch election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic established SARL: no mandatory VAT registration below the franchise thresholds of EUR 85,000 annual turnover for goods and EUR 37,500 for services; once those thresholds are exceeded, VAT registration is mandatory. Non-established foreign supplier: no de minimis threshold; any taxable supply in France triggers a French VAT registration obligation unless a special scheme (OSS/IOSS) applies or the supply is reverse-charged to a French business customer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU qualify for the VAT zero rate (exonération) under CGI Article 262 ter I, provided the goods physically leave the EU within the statutory timeframe and the exporter retains proof of exit. Digital services, including AI video synthesis and virtual avatar generation SaaS, supplied to non-EU customers are outside French VAT where the customer is established or has his usual residence outside the EU (B2B place-of-supply rule under CGI Art. 259, and B2C electronic services under the destination principle). For B2C digital services to EU consumers, French VAT may apply unless OSS is elected.

  • Customs Export Declaration certified by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (SAD via DELTA)
  • Commercial invoice with explicit zero-rating reference
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill or other international transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Proof of payment or inward remittance from the non-EU buyer
  • Intra-community sales list and EU exporter summary report (where applicable) for goods moving under the Union Customs Code

France is implementing mandatory structured electronic invoicing for all domestic B2B transactions under the 2022 Finance Law reform, with phased applicability beginning 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized enterprises and 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises. Invoices must flow through a registered dematerialization partner (PDP) or the public invoicing portal (Portail Public de Facturation), with structured data transmitted to the DGFiP. B2C transactions require e-reporting of transaction data to the DGFiP. There is no transactional real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system; compliance is based on periodic VAT returns and e-invoicing data analytics.

Where documentation is missing or the customs export proof is not obtained within the statutory period, the DGFiP reassesses output VAT at the standard or reduced rates applicable at the date of supply, and charges late-payment interest (intérêt de retard). In case of deliberate intent to evade or conceal tax, the penalty is 40% of the understated tax (mauvaise foi); where fraud is established or where the taxpayer has engaged in abusive practices, the penalty rises to 80% (manœuvres frauduleuses). Criminal prosecution for tax fraud (fraude fiscale) can impose imprisonment up to five years and a fine up to EUR 375,000, plus additional judicial penalties and publication of sanctions. Clawback periods follow the general French limitation rules of three years for annual VAT assessments, extending to six or ten years in cases of fraud or serious cross-border non-disclosure.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member with full and unrestricted euro convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under Articles 63 through 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and Council Directive 88/361/EEC. There are no exchange-control approval requirements for the SARL to export AI-avatar video-generation SaaS subscriptions, receive USD licensor or distribution fees, or remit dividends to non-resident shareholders. The only statutory friction points are tax-related, not FX-related: distribution of profits to non-resident individual shareholders is subject to the 30% flat withholding rate (prélèvement forfaitaire unique, PFU) absent treaty relief, while corporate shareholders resident in the EU/EEA may benefit from the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU) where the 10% participation condition is met. Additionally, the SARL must comply with balance-of-payments statistical reporting obligations to the Banque de France for direct investment flows and with EU sanctions screening under Regulation (EU) 269/2014, Regulation (EU) 833/2014, and subsequent restrictive-measure packages.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion ratio exists in France for export proceeds or SaaS subscription receipts. The SARL may lawfully retain, hold, or freely convert USD and other foreign currency proceeds through French banks or the interbank FX market without prior Banque de France authorization. The only compulsory obligations are (i) EU cash-control declaration under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 for physical movements of cash or bearer instruments exceeding EUR 10,000, and (ii) cross-border balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Banque de France for transactions exceeding EUR 1,000,000 per operation or periodic consolidated filings where applicable. Invoicing in USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency is permitted; no conversion quota applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) is the central bank with authority over balance-of-payments statistics, cash management, and the right-to-an-account mechanism; prudential oversight of credit institutions and payment institutions is delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); foreign-exchange control policy is administered by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) within the framework of EU Treaty provisions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the SARL's equity injection, the first step is opening a blocked capital escrow account at a French bank or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, then executing a SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank with remittance information clearly stating 'Apport en numéraire – souscription au capital social – SARL [legal name]'. No FX authorization is required for EUR or USD wires; a non-EU shareholder sending EUR must include its own institution's compliance documentation such as source of funds and authorization from the capital-receiving country. Under France's foreign direct investment screening regime (art. L.151-3 CMF), prior authorization from the French Treasury is required only if a non-EU/ETA investor acquires control of, or obtains 25% of voting rights in, an entity active in strategic sectors including AI and robotics; otherwise, a qualifying inbound direct investment must be reported to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics. The gérant must provide source-of-wealth declarations, the depository bank's attestation that the funds are blocked pending registration, and the draft articles of association.SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) are the primary clearing rails for all EU/EEA supplier and refund disbursements; non-EU vendor payments, cloud-computing capacity purchases, and subcontractor fees are executed via SWIFT MT103 with structured remittance data (invoice number, purchase-order reference, and purpose text). Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are legally available and accepted by all tier-1 French banks but are commercially unsuitable for recurring AI-avatar SaaS licensing; they should be reserved for one-off high-value hardware or perpetual-license transactions.
