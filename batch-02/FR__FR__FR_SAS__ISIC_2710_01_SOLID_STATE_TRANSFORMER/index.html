Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by French law for an SAS; the capital is fixed freely in the articles (e.g., EUR 1,000). Cash contributions must be deposited by the subscribers in a blocked trust account with a bank, notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and remain escrowed until the RCS registration extract (Kbis) is obtained, after which the funds are released to the Président on behalf of the company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for industrial premises from the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) and the local safety commission; structural load verification and electrical conformity certificate (Consuel) from an approved technical inspection body; workplace clearance opened with the DREETS (labor inspectorate) and CARSAT covering high-voltage and machinery safety, ergonomics, ventilation and fire-exit compliance before commercial production begins.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — official portal: impots.gouv.fr (professional area: Portail Fiscal des Professionnels). Company formalities run through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. VAT identification is issued by the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); customs export formalities are administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) at pro.douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS and for foreign entities establishing a French operation: (1) file the incorporation or branch-registration file with the Guichet Unique to obtain the SIREN/SIRET; (2) lodge the statutory business commencement (début d'activité) declaration with the SIE; (3) the DGFiP then issues the definitive intra-community VAT number (FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN), normally within 8-10 working days; (4) non-EU-established persons liable for VAT in France must additionally appoint a jointly and severally liable fiscal representative under Art. 289 A CGI; EU suppliers may cover distance sales through the OSS/IOSS without a French fixed establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SAS and for foreign entities establishing a French operation: (1) file the incorporation or branch-registration file with the Guichet Unique to obtain the SIREN/SIRET; (2) lodge the statutory business commencement (début d'activité) declaration with the SIE; (3) the DGFiP then issues the definitive intra-community VAT number (FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN), normally within 8-10 working days; (4) non-EU-established persons liable for VAT in France must additionally appoint a jointly and severally liable fiscal representative under Art. 289 A CGI; EU suppliers may cover distance sales through the OSS/IOSS without a French fixed establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box regime. Statutory elections relevant to a SAS: (i) option for French fiscal integration (intégration fiscale, Art. 223 A CGI) for a 95%-owned French group; (ii) election, for newly created SMEs, to be taxed as a pass-through on French income tax for up to five fiscal years (Art. 239 bis AB CGI); (iii) no elective hybrid-mismatch designations — ATAD2 anti-hybrid provisions (Arts. 209, 212 bis, 212 ter CGI) apply compulsorily. The SAS therefore defaults to full entity-level corporate income tax (IS).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for goods supplies (the rule governing an ISIC 2710 manufacturer) and EUR 37,500 for services; the higher tolerance (seuil majoré) is EUR 93,500 for goods and EUR 41,000 for services. Foreign distance sellers established outside the EU are taxable on their first euro of French B2C sales; EU-established sellers use the EUR 10,000 intra-EU OSS threshold. French registration becomes compulsory once turnover exceeds EUR 85,000 of goods in the preceding calendar year (rolling test).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Outbound exports of goods from French customs territory to destinations outside the EU are definitively exempt with full right to input VAT deduction (exportation exonérée, Art. 262-I-1 CGI); intra-EU dispatches to a buyer identified for VAT in another Member State are exempt under Art. 262 ter-1 CGI. Ancillary services (transport, loading, expert supervision) follow the export exemption. Offshore B2B services (e.g., engineering or embedded software supporting solid-state transformers) are zero-rated where the customer is a taxable person established outside the EU (place-of-supply rule, Art. 259 A CGI). Eligibility requires physical exit of the goods within the statutory 90-day (3-month) window and full evidentiary support of the transport movement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation / DAU-SAD)
  • Commercial invoice (facture commerciale)
  • Bill of Lading (connaissement) or equivalent transport document (CMR road consignment note, air waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation de change) from the authorized bank evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • For intra-EU dispatches: buyer's EU VAT ID and the EC Sales List / Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens (DES)

France implements the EU e-invoicing reform (facturation électronique) through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) interconnected with certified partner dematerialization platforms (PDPs). There is no Italian-style fiscal-stamping clearance: the PPF validates the mandatory 45-field DGFiP data schema and the transmission pipeline. B2B domestic invoices must be issued as structured e-invoices (Factur-X, UBL, or CII) and exchanged through a PDP; cross-border B2B, export, and B2C supplies are covered by mandatory e-reporting in near real time. The phased timeline starts 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and mid-caps, and 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-entities.

DGFiP audits export zero-rating strictly. Missing or falsified export evidence triggers re-levy of VAT on the seller (Art. 272 CGI), a 40% negligence penalty or 80% bad-faith/fraud penalty (Art. 1729 CGI), and late-payment interest of 0.20% per month (Art. 1727 CGI); the non-EU fiscal representative is jointly liable. Tax-fraud conviction under Art. 1741 CGI carries up to EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years' imprisonment. The VAT recovery period is 3 years (LPF Art. L. 169), extended to 6 years for concealed activity and up to 10 years for cross-border fraud; customs penalties (Code des douanes Arts. 414-423) may add up to twice the evaded duties with seizure of goods and retrospective clawback.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). France imposes no exchange-control authorization, no conversion quota, and no profit-repatriation license. Capital account entries are free except for (i) inbound foreign direct investment in restricted sectors requiring prior authorization under CMF Article L151-3 and Decree No. 2019-1590 — power transformer manufacturing (ISIC 2710) is not a listed sensitive sector, although a preliminary DG Trésor screening questionnaire is prudent when the ultimate parent is non-EU; and (ii) balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Banque de France for every cross-border flow of EUR 750,000 or more per transaction (aggregated by calendar month). Profit remittance: dividends paid by a French SAS to a non-resident corporate shareholder are subject to 25% domestic withholding tax, reduced to 0% for EU/EEA corporate shareholders meeting the 2-year holding condition under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU), and reduced to 0%-15% under applicable double-tax treaties (e.g., 15% under the France-China treaty at a 10% threshold; 0% under France-US treaty at an 80% ownership and 2-year holding threshold). There are no exchange-control hurdles to dividend transfer; only standard tax filings (Form 2561-B, or the crediting institution withholds and files 2777-SD for the 25% rate) are required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may freely hold export receipts in euro or in foreign-currency accounts. No forced repatriation deadline exists beyond commercial credit terms. Compliance obligations are purely statistical: export receipts of EUR 750,000 or more (per transaction or cumulative monthly) must be reported to Banque de France through the authorized intermediary bank, with the exporter supplying the transaction motive code (nomenclature des motifs monétaires et financiers), contract reference, and underlying invoice. Exports of ISIC 2710/HS 8504 products are zero-rated for VAT (exigibilité after delivery); the exporter must retain proof of export (bill of lading, customs exit validation in the DELTA customs system) to sustain the zero-rating upon audit.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Paris) exercises monetary authority and balance-of-payments statistics; banking prudential licensing and AML supervision are delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); foreign-exchange control regulations are codified in Articles L151-1 to L153-8 of the French Code monétaire et financier (CMF), administered jointly by Banque de France and the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) for investment screening.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a secondary liquidity and disbursement account; Low as the primary trade-finance or escrow institution. Qonto, Wise Business, and Shine are authorized by ACPR as payment institutions or electronic-money institutions under PSD2, offer French IBANs, and permit fully remote onboarding within 48-72 hours; they clear SEPA CT and can execute SWIFT MT103 wires through partner banks (e.g., BNP, Société Générale) at capped volumes. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot grant credit, cannot open or confirm documentary letters of credit in-house (LCs must be routed via the EMI's licensed banking partner), may reject funds from regulated industries without a banking license, and are subject to stricter transaction-velocity limits that are unsuitable for large-ticket transformer payments (typically EUR 100k-500k per wire). Recommendation: maintain an EMI account for administrative/disbursement agility while anchoring liquidity and documentary credits at a Tier-1 bank that provides a full SWIFT and LC platform.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% (French standard corporate income tax / impôt sur les sociétés); an additional 3.3% social contribution applies to corporate income tax exceeding EUR 763,000, producing a maximum headline burden of approximately 25.825% for larger companies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on gross dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders, reduced to 0% where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive applies (10% or greater shareholding, two-year holding attachment, substance and non-abuse conditions) and further reduced to 5% or 15% under many French income tax treaties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A French Local File and Master File are mandatory where the French entity or the consolidated group exceeds total balance-sheet assets or gross turnover of EUR 400 million. A Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for ultimate parent groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. The franchised heavy electrical equipment manufacturer must maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation in French or English if within the scope.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations, but anti-abuse rules, the abuse-of-right doctrine, CFC rules and beneficial-ownership clauses in treaties are vigorously applied. A French SAS must maintain actual management, engineering, inventory and decision-making functions in France; a hollow shell or letterbox entity using the French treaty network for conduit structures will be disregarded and reassessed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (fraude fiscale) and deliberate false declaration

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties of 40% to 80% on the additional tax, monthly late-payment interest of 0.20%, and automatic tax reassessment. Criminal conviction can result in imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to EUR 75,000, increased to EUR 750,000 when the proceeds of the fraud are laundered. The company may additionally lose government contracts and be excluded from tax settlement procedures.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false returns are exposed to criminal prosecution as co-perpetrators. The corporate veil may be pierced via the action en comblement de passif where fraudulent management caused the company to be unable to pay taxes; civil restitution, travel restrictions, judicial supervision and a ban on managing any French business for up to 15 years may be imposed.
Abuse of rights and artificial shell company arrangement

Statutory sanction: Application of the doctrine d'abus de droit under Article L64 of the Livre des procédures fiscales, yielding 80% penalty on additional tax plus interest. The DGFiP may recharacterise transactions, disregard the shell entity and reassess the beneficial owner. Failure to maintain valid substance can also trigger penalisation under the anti-money-laundering regime for corporate registrants.

Director personal liability: Directors of the abusive structure risk personal liability because the entity has no economic reality; courts may order personal payment of corporate tax and penalties, and the directors may be condemned for misuse of corporate assets (abus de biens sociaux). Judicial management bans and, in cross-border fraud cases, international travel bans apply.
Transfer pricing documentation and intercompany charging failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty can reach 5% of the amount of the transfer-pricing adjustment, plus possible fixed penalties for failure to respond to a documentation request. The tax administration may increase French taxable income by the mispriced amount, apply interest and the standard 40% bad-faith penalty, and impose reporting fines for CbC breaches.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length pricing may bear personal civil liability for the company's tax damage. Directors may also be jointly liable for the added tax where the billing structure is found to be a sham. In liquidation, the action en comblement de passif can reach directors who failed to put in place required transfer-pricing governance.
AML and Tracfin compliance red flags concerning payment rails

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of EUR 375,000, increased in organised-crime circumstances. Administrative penalties may include withdrawal of licences, freezing of accounts and asset confiscation. The French Financial Intelligence Unit may also impose penalties for non-reporting by an accountable professional.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly participating in laundering, or failing to prevent laundering when in control, may be prosecuted personally; personal fines and imprisonment can be imposed separately from corporate penalties. Directors' assets can be provisionally seized, they may receive a professional ban and travel bans are frequently imposed pending investigation.
