Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) — portal: impots.gouv.fr; business registration single portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (operated by INPI); customs and export data interface: douane.gouv.fr (DELTA/DX)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in France that carries out a taxable French economic activity must file Form No. 13967*01 (EU/EEA/UK resident) or Form No. 13968*01 (non-EU resident) with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). The filing must include apostilled constitutional documents of the foreign entity, certified French translations, proof of address or appointment of a French fiscal representative, and a statement of intended activities. DGFIP then issues a SIREN/SIRET number for any permanent establishment or direct taxable nexus and, if VAT-liable transactions are present, a French VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire, format FR+key+SIREN).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the entity level under Articles 206 et seq. CGI, irrespective of the ISIC 7310 service activity; the territorial principle limits French taxation to French-source profits. — A foreign entity not established in France that carries out a taxable French economic activity must file Form No. 13967*01 (EU/EEA/UK resident) or Form No. 13968*01 (non-EU resident) with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the entity level under Articles 206 et seq. CGI, irrespective of the ISIC 7310 service activity; the territorial principle limits French taxation to French-source profits.

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box regime; classification follows the CGI legal form. Available elections: (i) option for income-tax treatment under Article 239 bis A CGI for qualifying small SARLs; (ii) option for VAT grouping as a single taxable person (assujetti unique) under Article 256 C bis CGI; (iii) election for French fiscal integration under Article 223 A CGI for 95%-owned subsidiaries; (iv) hybrid-mismatch neutralization elections under Articles 212 bis et seq. CGI transposing ATAD 2 (payee/granting jurisdiction additional-tax disallowance elections).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA under Article 293 B CGI: €36,800 annual turnover for service activities (€91,900 for goods activities); however, non-established suppliers making taxable supplies in France are mandatorily registrable without any threshold, and cross-border B2C electronic services may be declared via the OSS/IOSS regime. For a French SARL engaged in ISIC 7310 advertising/ASO services, registration is mandatory upon exceeding the €36,800 service threshold, and B2B services to French VAT-registered clients are reverse-charged.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to: (i) exports of goods dispatched or transported outside the EU with proof of export (Article 262 I 6° CGI); (ii) services the place of supply of which is outside France, notably B2B services supplied to a non-EU established recipient under Article 259-1 CGI and B2C digital/intangible services supplied to non-EU consumers under Article 259 D CGI. SEO, ASO and localization services fall under the general B2B place-of-supply rule; when the customer is established outside the EU the supply is outside the scope of French VAT, with input VAT recovery strictly limited to French taxable activities.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation via DELTA/DX under the Union Customs Code, with MRN and exit summary)
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading or Air Waybill) evidencing physical departure from France/EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment evidence (FRX attestation) demonstrating receipt of export proceeds within the statutory period

Mandatory structured e-invoicing reform: all B2B and B2C transactions must be transmitted via a DGFIP-registered PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) or the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF) with simultaneous e-reporting of transaction data; B2G e-invoicing via Chorus Pro is already mandatory. Large and mid-size companies must comply by 1 September 2026, SMEs and micro-enterprises by 1 September 2027. No fiscal stamping/pre-clearance model applies; instead, immutable structured Factur-X/INVOIC syntax is transmitted in near-real time through certified APIs, with full audit trail.

DGFIP may reassess VAT within the 6-year statutory period (10 years in cases of fraud/occult activity) and apply penalties of up to 80% for gross negligence or fraudulent zero-rating claims under Articles 1728, 1729 and 1731 CGI, plus delay interest of 0.20% per month under Article 1727 CGI. Fabricated export documentation engages criminal customs liability under Article 414 Code des douanes, including confiscation and fines up to twice the value of the goods, and automatic denial of VAT refund claims for fictitious exports. Claims are cross-matched against the customs ECS liquidation system and DELTA export records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full euro convertibility for current account transactions, including cross-border service charges and profit remittances, subject to standard transaction documentation. Capital account transactions are also freely convertible within the EU, while non-EU direct investments require post-transaction statistical declaration to Banque de France (DSI/DRP forms) when amounts exceed €15,000. Profit remittances by a SARL are treated as current account payments and require only a valid corporate resolution and normal accounting documentation. There is no prior exchange control authorization for genuine commercial or investment flows. For non-EU resident shareholders, dividend distribution is subject to the parent–subsidiary directive (if eligible at 5% or more shareholding) or the applicable double tax treaty; the remittance itself faces no convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the state in France. All export receipts may be retained in a domestic or foreign foreign-currency account. For statistical balance of payments reporting, cross-border receipts exceeding €15,000 (or the lower €1,500 if reporting to Banque de France per general regulation on monetary and financial code provisions) must be reported by the bank on behalf of the company on a monthly basis through the 'Entreprises' reporting channel (BOF-SAIP). Companies are not required to convert export proceeds into euros, but for operational liquidity and hedge accounting a natural conversion may be performed. The standard statutory timeline for declaring the transaction to Banque de France is within one working day of the transaction for the reporting bank, with the company having no direct filing obligation but providing supporting documents upon request. Any non-EU invoicing above €12,500 or cumulative €125,000 requires an invoice-related movement declaration field and can be handled electronically by the bank.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, Directorate General of Foreign Trade (Direction Générale du Trésor) and the Ministry of the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; the Eurosystem (European Central Bank) for monetary policy and the ECB regulation on statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High viability for inbound fee collection and low-value trade settlement, but subject to important legal constraints. Wise (TransferWise Ltd, EMI licensed in Belgium and passported to France) can be opened remotely with a French SIREN, proof of identity, and UBO declaration; it provides a French IBAN (FR76) and EU SEPA direct debit capabilities. Payoneer (Payoneer Europe Ltd, EMI in Ireland) strongly supports marketplaces and freelance B2B receipts in multiple currencies, with onshore USD balances and settlement to the French SARL operating account. Qonto and Shine (backed by Société Générale) are fully licensed as payment institutions (or agents) and act as regulated EMIs registered with the ACPR; they provide domestic and cross-border SEPA payments without a full banking license. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI cannot extend credit, overdraft, or foreign exchange forwards without a credit institution licence, so forward hedging must be provided by a bank or registered broker; (ii) trust-account balances may not be protected by the French deposit guarantee (FGDR), only by safeguarding requirements under the E-Money Directive; (iii) for premiums above €10,000 per month, the entity should supplement EMI rails with a bank account to maintain corporate treasury stability and audit alignment. The recommendation is a 'hybrid' setup: primary Tier-1 bank for FX and clearing, plus Wise/Payoneer for low-cost disbursement and local unattached market fees. An offshore EMI not registered in France (e.g., a non-EU payment provider) is not permitted to hold a French corporate account and may not provide a local IBAN; such an entity would only be a receivable settlement tool using payout to bank accounts, and under PSD2 requires the DevOps agent to verify AML compliance.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Foreign (non-French) ownership of the SARL, especially when ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) is resident outside the EEA or in a high-risk third country (as defined by the FATF list).

Root cause: Foreign (non-French) ownership of the SARL, especially when ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) is resident outside the EEA or in a high-risk third country (as defined by the FATF list).

Defense evidence: Provide a certified copy of passport; a company excerpt from the Registry of Commerce (extrait Kbis); a legalised certificate from a local notary or embassy confirming identity; a signed UBO declaration (Déclaration de Bénéficiaires Effectifs) submitted to the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce; audited or notarised funding source evidence (bank reference letter from the foreign branch, salary slips, tax returns or cryptocurrency-related provenance letter if relevant); and a business plan articulating the app growth agency's revenue model, client contracts, and forecast cash flows.
High transaction velocity and cross-border third-party settlement flows (e.g., receiving fees from app stores, foreign publishers, and paying subcontractors worldwide) that resemble payment intermediation or money service business activity.

Root cause: High transaction velocity and cross-border third-party settlement flows (e.g., receiving fees from app stores, foreign publishers, and paying subcontractors worldwide) that resemble payment intermediation or money service business activity.

Defense evidence: Provide a drafted service agreement with each major counterparty and an AML self-assessment report showing that the Taiwan company does not collect, hold or transfer external money on behalf of third parties, and that each transaction is pre-approved by the client with clear commercial purpose. Present a detailed KYC file of the top 10 counterparties (legal name, registered address, tax ID) and an outline of the company's internal sanctions screening process using a reputable provider (e.g., Dow Jones, World-Check) to demonstrate that no PEP/high-risk jurisdiction relationships are activated without enhanced due diligence.
Rapid deposit surges or immediate outbound payments (layering pattern) from a newly opened corporate account within the first 90 days, especially when combined with invoices to tax havens or jurisdictions not aligned with FATF standards.

Root cause: Rapid deposit surges or immediate outbound payments (layering pattern) from a newly opened corporate account within the first 90 days, especially when combined with invoices to tax havens or jurisdictions not aligned with FATF standards.

Defense evidence: Maintain a treasury historical ledger (monthly) with the invoice reference, bank purpose code, corresponding proof of contract, and a description of the good/service (e.g., App Store Optimization, ASO, SEO, mobile advertising) for each transaction. Provide a board resolution authorising the payment pattern; prior bank statements from the company's previous banking relationship showing the same business activity; and a letter from the company's registered accountant (Expert-Comptable) confirming that the flows are consistent with the SARL's declared revenue and tax filings.
Unclear origin of funds when subscribing to capital, particularly if the shareholder contributes euros converted from a foreign currency in amounts greater than €15,000.

Root cause: Unclear origin of funds when subscribing to capital, particularly if the shareholder contributes euros converted from a foreign currency in amounts greater than €15,000.

Repeated disbursements to offshore advertising platforms or to digital asset exchanges (for billing/invoicing purposes) that may be viewed as round-tripping to evade tax or capital controls.

Root cause: Repeated disbursements to offshore advertising platforms or to digital asset exchanges (for billing/invoicing purposes) that may be viewed as round-tripping to evade tax or capital controls.

Defense evidence: Obtain an export/import invoice with a clear purchase order; keep a complete file of the supply agreement with the ad network (e.g., Meta, Google, TikTok) or media buying platform; a breakdown of user acquisition costs by campaign and period; an independent audit conducted by a French certified accountant confirming that the transactions are at arm's length and correspond to the actual performance marketing services rendered.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant injection for the French SARL may be made via international wire transfer using SWIFT MT103 (for EUR credit to a French IBAN) or SEPA InST for EU-origin transactions. The reference field must contain the ISIN of the company (SIREN/SIRET) and the purpose code 'D5 – Direct investment in the capital of a French company' or 'O1 – Other investment' depending on the non-resident ownership structure. For non-resident owner injection above €15,000, the shareholder must complete and provide to the bank a 'Declaration Cerfa 15555*01' (participation in the financing of a French company) within 20 business days of the payment. The bank will automatically file the 'Déclaration Statistique et Financière' with the Banque de France using the following standard BOP code: 'BF3102 – Foreign investment in the creation or expansion of a commercial enterprise'. A copy of the corporate resolution (PV d'AG) increasing the share capital must be provided. If the capital is paid into a provisional account before registration, the funds must be blocked until the Kbis is issued; after the Kbis, the bank reclassifies the funds as paid-up capital. The routing must be the France-Based bank's SWIFT BIC: e.g., BNPAFRPP, SOGE FRPP, CRLYFRPP (Crédit Agricole). For hedge accounting, foreign currency excess cash should be repatriated at month-end to avoid uncovered positional FX risk; for daily liquidity above EUR 50,000, use a money market sweep or overnight deposit line (DAV, Genie) at the main bank.The primary clearing rail to Luxembourg or non-EU counterparties is the SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer, with the mandatory purpose code 'Service charges' (code 22) for consultancy/marketing fees. For payments inside the EU below €100,000, SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) are permissible and cheapest; however for cross-border non-EU disbursements above USD limits, MT103 should be used via the bank's international gateway. Documentary instruments include Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) and at-sight documentary collection, although given the SME service business, a bank transfer with proforma invoice is customary. For payments to subcontractors via Wise Business, the rail is an EEA internal transfer to Wise's European IBAN (e.g., BG24... for France), from which Wise executes an Agent's MT103 in local currency. For FX conversions for trade settlement, the bank provides treasury execution via a spot transaction (T+2) and can support same-day value with a 'S.T.P. FX' service. The recommended clearing structure: (i) all non-EUR fees are collected in original currency by a multi-currency sweep account (EUR/GBP/USD), (ii) conversion is done via bank forward or FX Forward contract for the top 70% of forecast receivables, (iii) euro payments issued via SEPA for EU counterparts, and MT103 for non-EU service providers. Invoices must include a VAT clause if billed to French clients, and for B2B cross-border services allow the reverse-charge mechanism. All outbound instructions require the beneficiary's IBAN (if shudder) or SWIFT/ABA code coupled with a mandatory 14-digit reference matching the trade invoice.Foreign Exchange Forward (Outright EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) with tenors from 1 week to 12 months, requiring underlying commercial receivable/payable exposure and an executed ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the French bank's standard treasury terms.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) for currencies without free convertibility (e.g., Egyptian pound, Argentine peso, Nigerian naira, offshore RMB) but strictly forbidden with all EU residents under Regulation (EC) 974/98 without the trade being for a documented export/import exposure; in practice, French 'crédit documentaire' banks allow NDFs for hedging EMEA trade flows only through an authorized broker.Currency Swaps (FX Swap) for converting temporary surplus EUR into foreign currency for a specified period, with an agreement to reverse at spot or forward at maturity; allows the company to fund local branch payroll or market-specific ad campaigns without using a deposit.European-Style EUR/USD Options (call/put) through an OTC dealer (e.g., BNP Paribas, Société Générale) for hedging budgeted transactional exposure, requiring a credit line approval by the bank's global markets desk.SEPA Forex Contracts via Fintech API (e.g., Ebury, Wise Embedded) which are bilateral over-the-counter contracts not exchange-traded, permissible under Marcy in France if the intermediary is registered with the ACPR or an exempted authorised foreign entity under the European passport, and only used to mitigate commercial FX risk, not for speculation.

An offshore EMI not registered in France (e.g., a non-EU payment provider) is not permitted to hold a French corporate account and may not provide a local IBAN; such an entity would only be a receivable settlement tool using payout to bank accounts, and under PSD2 requires the DevOps agent to verify AML compliance.
