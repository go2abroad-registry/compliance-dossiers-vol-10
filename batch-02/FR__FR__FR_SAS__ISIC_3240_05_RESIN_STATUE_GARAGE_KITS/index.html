Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP); online business tax services and account management are provided through impots.gouv.fr. Company formation and allocation of the SIREN/SIRET identifiers are processed through the INPI-operated Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, with registration data transmitted to the relevant authorities.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is formed through the Guichet unique. Following registration in the Registre national des entreprises and, where applicable, the Registre du commerce et des sociétés, INSEE assigns a SIREN and establishment-level SIRET. The competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) handles corporate-tax and VAT administration. A French VAT identification number is generally issued or activated by the tax administration and consists of FR, a two-character control key, and the SIREN. A foreign taxable business without a French establishment applies to the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères for VAT registration, providing evidence of legal existence, business activity, identity, and the relevant French taxable transactions. A non-EU business generally must appoint an accredited French fiscal representative unless an applicable legal arrangement provides otherwise. VAT registration may be required before commencing taxable transactions; registration obligations can arise without domestic turnover where cross-border or other taxable transactions require it.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). An eligible closely held SAS may elect temporary taxation under the personal income-tax regime under statutory conditions, including limits concerning age, workforce, turnover or balance-sheet size, ownership, and the nature of its activity; the election is time-limited and is not a general check-the-box election. A qualifying family-owned company conducting an eligible commercial activity may, subject to statutory conditions, elect the partnership-like regime. France has no US-style check-the-box classification regime. Hybrid mismatch rules under French law implementing EU anti-hybrid provisions can deny deductions or require income inclusion in specified cases; they are anti-avoidance rules, not elective entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France has no general turnover threshold that exempts a taxable business from VAT registration once it is required to account for VAT. The domestic small-business franchise en base thresholds are generally EUR 85,000 for sales of goods and specified similar activities and EUR 37,500 for services, with higher tolerance thresholds generally EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250 respectively. These thresholds and their application are subject to legislative changes and transaction-specific rules. Cross-border supplies, acquisitions, imports, or an election for ordinary VAT treatment may require registration irrespective of these thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union may be exempt with a right to deduct input VAT, effectively applying a 0% rate, where statutory conditions are met and the supplier can prove that the goods left the EU. The supplier must retain reliable customs evidence linking the export to the sale. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: their VAT treatment depends principally on place-of-supply rules, customer status, and any applicable special rules. For many B2B services supplied to a business established outside France, the place of supply is outside France and the transaction is outside the scope of French VAT, subject to evidence and invoicing requirements.

  • Export customs declaration and customs exit confirmation, including the export accompanying document reference or equivalent electronic exit evidence
  • Commercial invoice, order or contract, and records identifying the goods and connecting the supply to the export declaration
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, CMR, courier evidence, or other transport documentation appropriate to the shipment
  • Proof of delivery or receipt by the customer where available
  • Payment and accounting records supporting the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory prerequisite for export VAT exemption
  • For services treated as supplied outside France, evidence of the customer's business status and establishment, contract, and records substantiating the nature and performance of the service

France is implementing mandatory domestic electronic invoicing and transaction e-reporting. From 1 September 2026, all businesses within scope must be able to receive electronic invoices; issuance obligations apply from that date to large and intermediate-sized businesses and are scheduled from 1 September 2027 for small and micro businesses. In-scope invoices are to be transmitted through an approved private platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire) or the public portal where available, with required data reported to the tax administration. This is not a general real-time invoice-clearance or digital-stamping system. E-reporting applies to specified transactions not covered by domestic e-invoicing, including certain cross-border and business-to-consumer transactions. Applicable scope, exemptions, technical standards, and implementation dates should be verified against current legislation and DGFiP guidance.

France imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. The supplier must substantiate export exemption and retain supporting records for tax audit. If export or place-of-supply conditions are not proved, the administration may assess output VAT, late-payment interest, and penalties. Depending on the facts, penalties may include 10% for specified filing or payment defaults, 40% for deliberate failure, or 80% for fraudulent conduct or specified abusive practices; the applicable statutory basis and rate depend on the violation. Input VAT deductions or refunds linked to unsupported exempt exports may be denied or recovered. Fraudulent documents or conduct may also trigger criminal proceedings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains a fully liberalized FX regime under the Eurozone framework. Capital account transactions are convertible without prior authorization for corporate entities. Current account convertibility is unrestricted, permitting free repatriation of profits, dividends, and royalties to shareholders. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal for EU-resident SAS entities, subject only to standard corporate tax filings, beneficial ownership declarations, and anti-money laundering reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to a designated bank is required. Exporters may retain receipts in foreign currency accounts. However, all FX transactions exceeding €10,000 must be reported to the Banque de France via the Système d'Endettement et de Déclarations de Transactions (SEDI) for statistical purposes. No statutory conversion ratios or surrender obligations apply to legitimate trade receipts.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions face significant legal constraints under French and EU AML directives (AMLD5/6). Local banking relationships are mandatory for cross-border operations, with strict KYC and transaction reporting requirements limiting viability of purely offshore EMIs.
