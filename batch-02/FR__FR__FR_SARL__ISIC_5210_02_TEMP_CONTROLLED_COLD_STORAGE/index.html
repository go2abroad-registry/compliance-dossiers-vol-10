Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via impots.gouv.fr; corporate and VAT formalities filed through the National Business Formalities Portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); customs clearance administered by Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via Pro.Douane DELTA.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity engaged in taxable supplies in France must register for French corporate income tax (Société à Responsabilité Limitée subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés) and VAT by filing the standard incorporation/formalities dossier unless it only requires VAT identification. Non-EU entities must appoint an accredited tax representative in France in accordance with CGI Article 289-A. The local SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) allocates the SIREN/SIRET numbers and the intra-Community French VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire), which is also required for customs declarations and electronic invoicing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in France. A small SARL meeting the statutory conditions may elect fiscal transparency under CGI Article 239 bis AA, thereby being taxed under the income tax (IR) partnership regime rather than corporate income tax. A qualifying SARL may also elect for group fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under CGI Article 223-A. Hybrid mismatch elections are restricted by ATAD/anti-hybrid provisions codified in CGI Articles 202 quater and 205 and by administrative doctrine; aggressive hybrid-electivity is disallowed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Foreign entities have no de minimis VAT registration threshold: any taxable supply of goods or services physically performed in France, including temperature-controlled warehousing, triggers immediate French VAT registration. Domestic micro-businesses may invoke the franchise en base de TVA (€91,900 for goods / €36,800 for services annual turnover), but this relief is practically irrelevant for cold-chain B2B warehousing contracts and is not available to non-established suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under CGI Article 262-I to goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union, provided the customs export declaration is accepted by DGDDI and definitive proof of exit from EU territory is held. Temperature-controlled storage and related handling services may qualify for exemption under CGI Article 262-II-2° where the service is directly and exclusively connected to a completed export of the goods, even if performed in a French customs warehouse or free zone. Services performed for goods remaining in France or moving intra-EU are not zero-rated; absent the direct-export link, warehousing is localized where the goods are physically located and attracts French VAT at 20%, subject to B2B place-of-supply reverse-charge rules only where the statutory localization rules so provide.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU) electronically lodged via DELTA/DDN with DGDDI and accepted for export
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport instrument evidencing physical departure of goods from EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export stock release order cross-referencing the DAU reference
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MF payment credit evidence matching the export invoice value and confirming the foreign buyer's payment
  • Bonded warehouse or free-zone inventory movement records, storage tickets, and customs warehouse permits evidencing the physical presence and exit of the exact goods
  • Delivery acceptance certificate or destination customs clearance proof issued by the importing country (where obtainable)

France mandates progressive B2B electronic invoicing under the anticompliance reform: all companies must be able to receive structured e-invoices from September 1, 2026, with issuance obligations phased to large/mid-size undertakings from September 2026 and extended to SMEs/micro-businesses from September 2027. Invoices must be transmitted through certified Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs) or the public portal Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) in national structured formats (Factur-X 1.0.07 / CII / UBL 2.1) under the Y-model, whereby transactional and e-reporting data flow to DGFiP without real-time fiscal stamping. Authenticity of origin, integrity of content, and readability are compulsory; electronic signatures or secure EDI exchanges are accepted evidencing audit trails.

DGFiP may reopen and reassess VAT periods for the standard 3-year reprise window, extended to 10 years where the activity was concealed or fraudulent documentation is established; unsupported export zero-rating entries are clawed back in full with per-month late payment interest (intérêts de retard, currently 0.20% per month). Penalties escalate as follows: 40% for deliberate inaccuracies under CGI Article 1729-a; 80% for fraudulent maneuvers, abuse of right, or use of fabricated export documents under CGI Article 1729-b; specific fines up to €1,500 for failure to produce required documentation. Criminal prosecution under CGI Article 1741 for export VAT fraud may result in imprisonment up to five years and a fine up to €75,000, increased to €150,000 in aggravated circumstances, alongside permanent disqualification of the managing director.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France offers full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no foreign-exchange controls, no prior administrative authorisation for inbound capital injections, no quantitative limits on outbound profit remittances, and no restriction on the currency denomination of payments. Practical hurdles for profit remittances by the SARL are limited to: (i) French corporate-law distribution rules (Code de commerce Articles L232-10 to L232-12) requiring the statutory legal reserve of 5% of annual net profit up to 10% of share capital to be withheld and prohibiting distributions when net equity is reduced below half of corporate capital; (ii) the 25% withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders, which may be reduced to 0% where the recipient parent satisfies the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive holding requirements (at least 10% interest, two-year holding) or a double-tax-treaty rate otherwise applies; and (iii) for individual non-resident shareholders, the flat prélèvement forfaitaire unique of 12.8% plus social levies, with repatriation subject to treaty allocation. All foreign-exchange transactions with third-country residents must be reported to the Banque de France for statistical and balance-of-payments purposes when they exceed the regulatory de minimis threshold, and direct investment flows are covered by the mandatory direct-investment declaration regime. No exchange-rate gain or loss can be classified as non-resident or blocked.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation exists in France. A French entity conducting refrigerated logistics (ISIC 5210 / NAICS 493120) is not required to convert, repatriate, or surrender export proceeds or payments for services rendered to non-residents; there is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export invoices may be denominated in EUR or any freely convertible currency and may be credited to foreign-currency accounts held at any euro-area bank, or paid through SWIFT to foreign accounts, without central-bank authorisation. The sole remaining obligation is the Banque de France statistical declaration of payments received from non-residents above the applicable reporting threshold under the balance-of-payments instructions, together with the standard VAT and customs reporting (intra-EU reverse-charge reporting under Article 283-1 of the Code général des impôts; EU customs monthly declarations for goods movements).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the competent national authority for foreign-exchange oversight, balance-of-payments statistics and the administration of economic transaction declarations under the Code monétaire et financier; prudential supervision of banks and payment institutions is exercised jointly by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the French supervisory authority and the European Central Bank (ECB) within the Single Supervisory Mechanism; the eurozone clearing infrastructure (TARGET2, T2S, and SEPA clearing) is operated through the Eurosystem with French books settled via Banque de France systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rated Medium-to-High for ancillary treasury uses but strictly Inadmissible as the principal or sole banking relationship. An EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) passporting into France under PSD2 (e.g., a fintech with an ACPR or EEA-home-state authorisation) is feasible as a collection and disbursement rail for low-risk, non-deposit operations: receiving EUR/non-EUR revenue from pharmaceutical logistics clients, issuing local IBANs for client wire settlement, and executing outbound SWIFT MT103 payments. However, an EMI is not a credit institution under French banking law, and therefore cannot (i) hold the statutory capital-blocked escrow deposit required for SARL formation, (ii) grant overdrafts, credit lines, or factoring, or (iii) provide documentary letters of credit or FX hedging under banking safe-harbour rules. Given the target payment rails (ACH/Direct and Bank Wire) and the highly regulated perishing-pharmaceutical storage scope, the treasury blueprint mandates a tier-1 ACPR-regulated bank as the master treasury account holder, with at most one licensed EMI used as a segregated receivable-collection sub-rail holding less than 5% of aggregate treasury float, with full UBO passporting documentation and agreements in French governed by the Code monétaire et financier.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SARL's injection protocol uses a dedicated statutory escrow sub-account at a licensed French credit institution for the incorporation phase (blocked funds), followed by release to the operational current account after RCS registration. EUR-denominated injections from EEA shareholders are executed via SEPA Credit Transfer with the ISO 20022 reference field carrying the exact corporate purpose code 'EQUI' (equity contribution) or 'ADVA' (shareholder advance 'compte courant d'associé'); third-country contributions and amounts above materiality use SWIFT MT103 (SWIFT GPI) with the same purpose-code discipline and the beneficiary IBAN of the blocked sub-account, remitter bank details, and the SARL's RCS registration reference. The bank issues an 'attestation de dépôt des fonds' (capital-blocking certificate) only after full receipt and value-in; funds are then released against the RCS registration certificate (Kbis) and the correctly filed Articles of Association. For USD/GBP contributions, a standing spot-conversion mandate or an outright forward purchase of EUR hedges the conversion at the receiving bank; all capital movements exceeding the Banque de France reporting threshold are declared on the direct-investment form and the underlying purpose-code evidence is retained for anti-money-laundering inspection for at least five years. No inward wire may be credited to the operational account without passing the sanctions-maintenance and beneficiary-consistency screen at the compliance level.Domestic and euro-area payables (freight, energy, salaries, warehouse utilities, maintenance of refrigeration units) are settled via SEPA Instant Credit or SEPA Credit Transfer with a complete Unstructured Remittance Identifier linking invoice number and warehouse reference. Non-EEA trade settlements (refrigerated trailer equipment, IT software, international suppliers, customs broking fees) are routed through SWIFT MT103 using European balance-of-payments purpose labels (e.g., codes for goods purchase '101' or general services 'commercial services') and screened against SWIFT sanctions lists. For capital expenditure above EUR 25,000 and for pharmaceutical inbound cargo adjudication, an irrevocable documentary commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) opened by the French bank with the supplier as beneficiary is admitted, with transport documents, CMR, customs entry, and signed proof-of-delivery forming the presentation set; a confirmed L/C by a top-tier European bank is added in case of third-country suppliers with a sovereign or bank risk. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate under Article 219 of the French General Tax Code (CGI). A reduced 15% rate applies to the first EUR 38,120 of annual taxable profit for SMEs whose turnover does not exceed EUR 10,000,000 and whose share capital is fully paid up and held at least 75% by individuals.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Article 187 of the French General Tax Code for dividend distributions to non-resident corporate shareholders outside the EU/EEA; 12.8% withholding plus 17.2% social levies (total 30% flat tax) apply to dividends paid to non-resident individuals. No French withholding tax applies to EU/EEA parent companies holding at least 10% of the SARL's capital for at least two years under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive implementation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal years ending 31 December, the annual return must be filed by the second working day after 1 May of the following year. For off-calendar fiscal years, the return is due no later than the end of the third month after the closing of the fiscal year, or one month following the expiry of the statutory information period where applicable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 — a French SARL is required to prepare and file the Local File when its gross annual turnover or total assets exceed EUR 400,000,000, or when the group to which it belongs has consolidated turnover/total assets above that threshold and the French entity is a significant participant. The Country-by-Country Report obligation arises when the consolidated group turnover is at least EUR 750,000,000. Even below those thresholds, all related-party cross-border transactions must satisfy the arm's-length principle and contemporaneous documentation must be retained upon tax audit request.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply the Gulf-regime Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as a standalone statute. However, substantive presence is required through the French anti-abuse doctrine (abus de droit) under Article L.64 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales, the OECD BEPS PE attribution rules, and the EU ATAD general anti-abuse rule. A cold-storage SARL must maintain genuine premises in France, employed personnel, functioning refrigeration and monitoring equipment, local management decisions, and real exposure to operational risk. Absent economic substance, the tax administration may recharacterise the SARL as a sham, disregard its legal form, and tax its income at the level of the foreign owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / dissimulation de recettes

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI, aggravated tax evasion is punishable by up to 7 years' imprisonment and a criminal fine of EUR 1,000,000, increaseable to twice the sum evaded. Companies face a corporate fine multiplied by five under Article 131-38 of the Criminal Code. The Tax Administration may simultaneously impose a 40% penalty for bad faith or 80% for organised sham arrangements, plus 0.30% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: The gérant (and, where applicable, the president) can be sentenced to imprisonment and personally fined. French case law allows the fiscal authority to seek joint and several civil liability (action en insuffisance d'actif) against directors who caused or aggravated a deficiency in corporate assets. Travel bans (interdiction de quitter le territoire), asset freezing, and professional disqualification for up to 10 years are common ancillary orders.
Shell company / artificial arrangement / abus de droit

Statutory sanction: The Tax Administration may recharacterise the arrangement under Article L.64 LPF and assess corporate income tax, social contributions and VAT in the hands of the true economic owner. The monetary penalty is 80% of the reassessed amount for abusive arrangements with an artificial purpose, or 40% without that intent, plus late payment interest. Criminal courts may also apply a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and 5 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be treated as the actual taxable persons (la personne réellement bénéficiaire) and held personally liable for the 80% penalty. Courts may pierce the corporate veil based on fictive company evidence, leaving the gérant jointly liable for all corporate tax debts. Directors may be barred from managing any company in France for up to 15 years and subjected to a travel ban while criminal proceedings are active.
Anti-Money-Laundering (AML) / failure to report to Tracfin

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Article 324-1 of the French Criminal Code attracts 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 375,000. Aggravated laundering (habitual, organised, or using professional activity) carries 10 years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine. Company-level AML compliance failures under the Code monétaire et financier are subject to administrative sanctions of up to EUR 5,000,000 for non-credit institutions or, when AGEC is involved, fines matching 10% of turnover.

Director personal liability: A gérant who knowingly participates in laundering can be convicted as the principal offender, resulting in imprisonment, a substantial fine, and civil liability for repatriating laundered assets. The court may order confiscation of personal assets, travel bans, and prohibition of any professional activity during or after the sentence. Directors are held to the managerial AML duty; failure to establish and enforce a compliance programme gives rise to personal administrative sanctions by Tracfin and the competent supervisory body.
