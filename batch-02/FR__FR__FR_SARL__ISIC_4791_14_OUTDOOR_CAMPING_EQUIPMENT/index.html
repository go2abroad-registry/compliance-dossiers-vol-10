Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital; the shareholders freely determine the amount (customarily €1–€100). Where capital is €10,000 or less, the entire amount must be paid up at subscription; where it exceeds €10,000, at least 20% must be paid at formation and the balance within five years. Funds must be deposited in a blocked, interest-bearing account at a French credit institution or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and are released only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the local SDIS fire authority for the administrative and storage premises; compliance with the French Labour Code (Articles R.4227-1 et seq.) covering means of escape, extinguishers, employee information, and evacuation drills. Structural load inspection certificates are required if racked storage exceeds standard floor ratings. No ERP (public access building) clearance is triggered where the facility is not open to the public; a simple workplace opening declaration is sufficient. Third-party logistics must hold their own valid ICPE status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS). A SARL meeting the statutory conditions may elect for income tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, or IR), including the family SARL regime for qualifying family-owned companies. A newly formed SARL may, subject to ownership, size, age, activity, and shareholder requirements, elect temporary IR treatment for up to five financial years. France has no general United States-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions, include income, or neutralize arrangements producing deduction-without-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the French domestic VAT franchise regime applicable to ordinary sales of goods and commercial activities, the principal annual turnover threshold is EUR 85,000, subject to the statutory prior-year and current-year threshold tests and applicable indexed thresholds. Exceeding the relevant threshold removes the business from the franchise regime and may require VAT registration. The lower threshold applicable to services is generally EUR 37,500 and is not ordinarily relevant to the stated outdoor equipment retail activity. The franchise regime does not eliminate VAT obligations for imports, certain intra-Community transactions, or transactions subject to special rules. Cross-border B2C distance sales within the EU are generally subject to destination-country VAT once the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold is exceeded, unless OSS is used or another statutory rule applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT with the right to deduct input VAT, provided the exporter can substantiate the export and the customs exit from the EU. The supplier must retain a valid commercial invoice, identify the customer and goods, use the correct customs procedure, and obtain reliable electronic evidence of exit, normally through the customs export declaration and exit results. Intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-identified customer in another EU Member State are generally exempt with the right to deduct when the transport, customer VAT identification, and recapitulative reporting conditions are satisfied; they are not exports outside the EU. Services supplied to a business established outside France are generally governed by place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of French VAT rather than technically zero-rated. B2C services and services connected with goods require separate place-of-supply analysis. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, business-use evidence, and compliance with the relevant domestic, EU refund, or non-EU refund procedure.

  • French customs export declaration and export accompanying document or equivalent electronic customs record
  • MRN (Movement Reference Number) and electronic confirmation of exit from the European Union
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, VAT treatment, and delivery terms
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, air waybill, consignment note, or courier export record as applicable
  • Proof of delivery or transport to the non-EU destination where required to corroborate customs evidence
  • Customs broker mandate and customs power of attorney where a representative files the export declaration
  • Import customs or destination-country entry evidence where available
  • Accounting records linking the export declaration, invoice, inventory movement, payment, and VAT return
  • Bank payment evidence where commercially available; an inward remittance certificate is not generally a mandatory French VAT export document
  • Customer VAT identification and transport evidence for qualifying intra-Community supplies, where the transaction is within the EU rather than an export outside the EU

France does not currently operate a universal real-time pre-clearance system for all private-sector invoices. French domestic B2B e-invoicing is being introduced in phases: all French businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; large enterprises and medium-sized enterprises must issue them from 1 September 2026; and small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises must issue them from 1 September 2027, subject to statutory implementation rules and possible regulatory adjustments. Transmission is expected through the public platform or an accredited private platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire, or PDP). E-reporting obligations will apply to specified cross-border and B2C transactions. Invoices must satisfy French requirements for authenticity, integrity, legibility, mandatory content, and electronic preservation. General digital stamping is not required. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. VAT returns and payment are filed electronically through the DGFiP systems, with filing frequency determined by the applicable VAT regime and turnover.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds applicable to this business. Export VAT exemption is subject to retrospective assessment if customs exit or transaction conditions are not substantiated. The administration may reassess unpaid VAT, deny input VAT recovery, charge late-payment interest, and impose tax penalties. A 10% penalty may generally apply for late payment or certain filing failures, with higher penalties for deliberate non-compliance, including 40% for deliberate failure and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, subject to the facts and statutory safeguards. False invoices, concealment, or organized VAT fraud may create criminal exposure in addition to civil tax liabilities. Interest and penalties may apply from the original due date, and clawback may cover all affected reporting periods within the applicable limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is a fully convertible currency for both current and capital account transactions; France has no exchange-control licensing regime for ordinary trade, dividends, loan servicing, or capital repatriation. Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union guarantees free movement of capital, including profit remittances by non-resident shareholders of a French SARL, subject only to standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) compliance and, where applicable, dividend withholding tax relief under a double-taxation treaty. Balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the Banque de France applies only for declared cross-border financial relations above the statutory threshold (as adjusted by the Banque de France, generally EUR 1,000,000 of annual external flows); no prior administrative authorization or capital repatriation license is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exist under French law. Export receipts in EUR or foreign currency may be retained in ordinary operational accounts or dedicated foreign-currency accounts (comptes en devises) at ACPR-licensed banks. The only mandatory obligations are (i) EU/AML controls on cash transactions above EUR 10,000 and suspicious-transaction reporting, (ii) Banque de France balance-of-payments declarations for financial relations with non-residents above the reporting threshold, and (iii) full compliance with EU/UN restrictive measures and sanctions screening of counterparties. Conversion of export receipts into EUR is permitted at market rates without prior approval.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the Eurosystem and in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB); prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment service providers is delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution), and external finance and financial sanctions policy is administered via the Direction Générale du Trésor under the Code monétaire et financier.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) that passport into France under PSD2 (for example a French-licensed EMI or a passported continental EMI with an ACPR-registered branch or agent), but Low for offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA (e.g., United Kingdom after Brexit, United States, Singapore or Hong Kong). Legal constraints: an offshore non-EEA EMI cannot lawfully provide payment accounts or e-money services to a French SARL without obtaining a French EMI licence, implementing an EEA passporting arrangement, or operating through a French-authorised payment institution agent. The Code monétaire et financier requires payment services to be rendered exclusively by licensed or passported institutions, and French banks retain the primary relationship for K-bis verification, UBO filings, credit facilities and balance-of-payments declaration. Recommended structure: main treasury account at a Tier-1 bank supplemented by an EEA-licensed EMI for high-frequency marketplace payouts and online card settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for shareholder capital: for EEA/EUR shareholders use SEPA Credit Transfer displaying the ISO 20022 ExternalPurpose code 'CAPC' (capital payment); for non-EEA shareholders use SWIFT MT103 with mandatory UETR (gpi) and a remittance reference containing the Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose code for a direct investment/capital contribution, plus the text 'Capital Contribution FR SARL ISIC 4791'. The receiving bank will verify the K-bis extract, UBO declaration, articles of association, and the blocked-capital attestation if applicable. Aggregate inbound/outbound flows above the annual reporting threshold require Banque de France RF declarations; no prior exchange-control approval is required, but sanctions screening and source-of-funds verification apply. Capital increases must be recorded at the RCS/INPI and, where the contribution is in kind, verified by a commissaire aux apports.Outbound clearing rails: for EEA suppliers, SEPA Credit Transfers (TARGET2-SCT) with structured remittance references and trade-related purpose codes; for non-EEA suppliers, SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV under the gpi standard with UETR and ISO 20022 ExternalPurpose codes 'TRAD' (trade) or 'PURC'/'GOOD' indicating goods purchase; for high-value or first-time supplier relationships, an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit (payable at sight, UCP600) confirmed by a French/EEA bank, covering HS Chapter 94 camping/outdoor goods, with commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, and SAD customs declaration aligned to the SARL's NAF/APE code 4791Z. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) that passport into France under PSD2 (for example a French-licensed EMI or a passported continental EMI with an ACPR-registered branch or agent), but Low for offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA (e.g., United Kingdom after Brexit, United States, Singapore or Hong Kong). Legal constraints: an offshore non-EEA EMI cannot lawfully provide payment accounts or e-money services to a French SARL without obtaining a French EMI licence, implementing an EEA passporting arrangement, or operating through a French-authorised payment institution agent. The Code monétaire et financier requires payment services to be rendered exclusively by licensed or passported institutions, and French banks retain the primary relationship for K-bis verification, UBO filings, credit facilities and balance-of-payments declaration. Recommended structure: main treasury account at a Tier-1 bank supplemented by an EEA-licensed EMI for high-frequency marketplace payouts and online card settlement.
