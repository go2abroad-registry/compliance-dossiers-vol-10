Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The articles must state the capital amount. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must generally be fully paid at incorporation. Cash contributions are deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations account pending registration; the deposit certificate is then used for release of funds. The stated USD fee is an approximate conversion of the standard RCS registration fee of approximately EUR 37.45 and excludes beneficial-owner filing, legal-notice publication, professional fees, and any sectoral charges.

Comply with the French Labour Code, occupational-risk assessment obligations, chemical-agent exposure controls, electrical and machinery safety, ventilation, personal protective equipment, workplace medical arrangements, and mandatory declarations or inspections for regulated pressure, lifting, and fire-protection equipment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SAS is subject to corporate income tax at 25% standard rate; no pass-through). Territorial scope: worldwide income for established entities, with exemptions for foreign PE income under tax treaties. No tax-exempt zone.

Available election forms

Not applicable. France does not recognise check-the-box elections; a SAS is invariably a separate taxable person. Hybrid mismatch elections may only arise as disclosures under Article 205 A CGI and DAC6 for cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 for goods (franchise en base de TVA, Art. 293 B CGI); reduced to EUR 35,000 for services; mandatory registration irrespective of threshold for B2B supplies where reverse charge is not applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Article 262-I-1 CGI, provided exporter holds explicit proof of physical removal and retains all transport/commercial documentation. Offshore services to non-EU clients may also be zero-rated under Article 259 A CGI if benefited by a non-EU person.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Summary Declaration - EXS / DAM) through GUN/Delta
  • Bill of Lading / CMR or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from France/EU
  • Commercial invoice and export client contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing payment from non-French buyer

Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoice clearance from 1 September 2026 (reception) and 1 September 2027 (issue) via Chorus Pro or certified Dematerialization Platform (PDP); real-time transaction reporting to DGFiP applies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a member of the euro area, applies the EU free movement of capital under TFEU Articles 63-66. Euro is freely convertible without any general capital controls; both resident and non-resident commercial transactions are unrestricted. Current account payments (trade in goods/services, royalties, interest) are fully convertible and unblocked. Capital account remittances (equity investments, loan repayments, intellectual property disposals) are also freely convertible, but may require statistical reporting to the Banque de France for amounts exceeding €15,000 per transaction. Profit remittances by a French SAS (dividend distribution) are permitted without prior FX license, subject to corporate distribution formalities, tax withholding treaties (if any) and anti-money-laundering verification at the executing bank. No legislative hurdle beyond standard compliance and beneficial ownership declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender, conversion or repatriation timeline for export proceeds in France. Exporters may retain and freely use euro or foreign currency receipts across the EU. For balance-of-payments statistics, French resident exporters must report any cross-border transaction with a non-resident counterparty (including export proceeds) to the Banque de France if the nominal amount exceeds €15,000 (this is an information duty, not a surrender or conversion obligation). Export receipts denominated in freely convertible currencies (USD, GBP, JPY) may be held, but accounting closure and tax rules under French GAAP require that foreign currency receivables be valued at balance-sheet closing rates, with any unrealized yet certain exchange gains taxed on an annual basis unless specific hedge accounting is applied.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) together with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) exercises authority over currency and monetary operations; the Foreign Exchange Administration is delegated through the Ministry of the Economy and Finance's ACPR (Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority) for financial institution compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

France permits authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto and Treezor (Arkéa-founded) to operate as regulated payment and electronic money institutions under the ACPR/EBA passport regime. Cross-border offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Platform, Revolut Business, Multitude Bank) are viable for euro-denominated collections and low-value operational payments, but they do not substitute for a full-service corporate bank when large bilateral trade settlements, documentary letters of credit (L/Cs) or sophisticated FX hedging are required. Legal constraints include: (a) offshore EMIs cannot provide fully-fledged commercial lending trade finance without a banking license; (b) their ability to intermediate for L/Cs and cross-border guarantees is limited; (c) certain offshore EMIs (outside EU) may need to comply with EU AML directives, but often have limited proof of French physical presence; (d) capital contributions from non-EU shareholders are more easily settled through a tier-1 French bank with a correspondent banking network.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound euro capital contributions (shareholder loans) to the French SAS must be routed through the main corporate EEA-zone bank account (SEPA or SWIFT) with purpose code 'F' or 'S710' (capital injection). For non-euro (USD/GBP) injections, a correspondent bank wire is used with an explicit beneficiary account of the SAS and dual documentation: (i) the SWIFT MT103 covers all fees; (ii) the underlying statutory share subscription document ('acte de souscription') is attached for compliance review. The transfer must be reported to the Banque de France within 20 business days after the calendar month if the total exceeds €15,000. The bank will complete the 'LISBI' declaration requirement (direct investment report) on the treasury side. No withholding tax, stamp duty or intra-France FX control is required; only CRS/FATCA information on the ultimate beneficial owner is needed. For share premium exceeding nominal capital, the SAS must file a notarized intellectual property or cash contribution report with the Registrar of Commerce.For trade settlements related to sodium-ion battery cell manufacturing (e.g., cathode material imports, separator films, electrolyte shipments), the recommended rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 (payment at time of order) for standard raw materials, with a balanced purpose code 'License and Royalty' or 'Trade Finance Line' depending on the underlying invoice; (b) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C - URR 725 / UCP 600) for long-lead equipment and high-value electrolytic components. The L/C should be advised through a French confirmatory bank (BNP or SocGen), with documents including the transport bill, insurance certificates, certificate of origin, and customs agent endorsed compliant with the EU customs code. For EU-based suppliers, SEPA Credit Transfers are normally sufficient, but for above €50,000, additional KYC evidence of the beneficiary's trade registration must be retained.
