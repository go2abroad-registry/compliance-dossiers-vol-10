Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registry of the Commercial Court) territorially competent; filings are intermediated by the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (INSEE online portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr)

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title III, Articles L. 223-1 to L. 223-43 (SARL regime), supplemented by Articles L. 210-1 to L. 210-10 (general company provisions), R. 223-1 et seq., and R. 123-1 et seq. (Register of Commerce and Companies / RCS rules)

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de constitution d'une société (M0 electronic declaration) filed through the Guichet Unique, together with the signed articles of association (statuts), the bank capital-deposit certificate, the legal-publication certificate, and the beneficial-owner declaration; RCS enrolment is evidenced by the official Extrait Kbis

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital is imposed for an SARL (a €1 or higher amount may be chosen), but the entire stated capital must be subscribed. Where the stated capital exceeds €300, at least 1/5 of each cash contribution must be paid up at incorporation and the balance must be called within five years (Art. L. 223-7 C. com.). The paid-up funds must be escrowed in a blocked account held by a French bank, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary until the company obtains its Kbis (Art. L. 223-8 C. com.).

Funding & unlock path: Offices must pass the fire-safety compliance set out in the French Code de l'incendie (smoke detectors, fire extinguishers, emergency exits, evacuation signage, and an annual inspection certificate issued by a certified fire-safety body, renewed per ERP standards where the premises are open to the public); occupational health compliance requires compulsory preparation of the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUER), display of first-aid and evacuation instructions, maintenance of the workplace-safety register, and access to the occupational health service (médecine du travail) under Articles L. 4121-1 et seq. and R. 4227-1 et seq. of the Code du travail; electrical installations must be certified conforming by an approved body (Consuel attestation). Premises accessibility for disabled persons (PMR) must be demonstrated under Law 2005-102 and the Construction and Housing Code; structural stability of floors (e.g. > 250 kg/m² for server rooms) must be attested by the lessor's structural engineer or by a civil-defence inspection where the lease requires it.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital is imposed for an SARL (a €1 or higher amount may be chosen), but the entire stated capital must be subscribed. Where the stated capital exceeds €300, at least 1/5 of each cash contribution must be paid up at incorporation and the balance must be called within five years (Art. L. 223-7 C. com.). The paid-up funds must be escrowed in a blocked account held by a French bank, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary until the company obtains its Kbis (Art. L. 223-8 C. com.).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); official portal: impots.gouv.fr; non-resident VAT registration handled by Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), Paris.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL receives SIREN/SIRET and VAT intra-community identification automatically upon company incorporation via INPI and the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS). A foreign entity without a French establishment must file form 16216-SD (Demande d'immatriculation) with the SIEE to obtain a French VAT number, submitting proof of foreign registration, authenticating statutory documents, and a local fiscal representative where required; processing typically 2–4 weeks; online submission via impots.gouv.fr or the Guichet Entreprises.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A French SARL receives SIREN/SIRET and VAT intra-community identification automatically upon company incorporation via INPI and the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS). A foreign entity without a French establishment must file form 16216-SD (Demande d'immatriculation) with the SIEE to obtain a French VAT number, submitting proof of foreign registration, authenticating statutory documents, and a local fiscal representative where required; processing typically 2–4 weeks; online submission via impots.gouv.fr or the Guichet Entreprises.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies EU free movement of capital; the euro is a fully convertible currency. Current-account convertibility is unrestricted; capital-account transactions are liberalised, with only statistical reporting obligations. Dividend repatriation from a French SARL is permitted following approval of the statutory accounts at the annual general meeting and verification of distributable profit. Practical hurdles are the documentary evidence required by the remitting bank to prove the distribution is lawful, and non-resident dividend withholding tax under domestic law (potentially up to 25% for corporate shareholders, or the 30% flat levy for individuals), reduced by available tax treaties and the parent-subsidiary directive exemption where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no minimum conversion ratio and no statutory repatriation timeline for export receipts. Exporters retain proceeds in foreign currency or convert to euro at their discretion. However, balance-of-payments statistical declarations to Banque de France apply to transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 per month, and the receiving bank must report the transaction. No conversion ratios are applicable under the French Foreign Exchange Regulation (Code monétaire et financier).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (within the Eurosystem; the French authority for balance-of-payments reporting, monetary policy execution and financial stability supervision, utilising the framework of the European Central Bank)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability rating: High for a French-domiciled SARL with a verified gérant. French fintech EMI providers (e.g., Qonto, Anytime) offer efficient corporate onboarding and multi-currency IBANs for cross-border SaaS operations, subject to full ACPR licensing and the EMIR's compliance with Low_Risk_Standard. A cross-border EMI authorised under the EEA passporting regime may provide services in France if it holds a valid e-money or payment institution licence from another EU/EEA regulator and complies with ACPR oversight. Legal constraints include the prohibition on EMIs granting credit, mandatory segregation of client funds, and the requirement that the ultimate beneficial ownership structure be completely transparent; if health-related payment data is processed, the institution must also comply with the French Health Data Hosting (HDS) framework where applicable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loans to the French SARL must be wired via SWIFT MT103 or intra-EU SEPA transfer, using the French payment purpose code 'C120' (apport en capital) or appropriate code for capital contributions. For non-EU transfers, conversion to EUR is done at the receiving bank. The remitting bank must attach the French SIREN/SIRET, an extract of the trade register (extrait Kbis), minutes of the shareholders/manager resolution, the capital deposit certificate, and a clear narrative identifying the shareholder and the purpose. If the contribution exceeds EUR 12,500, the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting obligation rests with the bank; the company should retain all supporting documentation for internal review.The principal clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 and SEPA instant payments for salary, supplier and intercompany disbursements. For tangible medical device or any ancillary goods-related settlements, a commercial letter of credit (documentary credit) may be established through French banks; however, for a pure EMR SaaS company, bank transfers via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA CT are preferred. All non-EUR payments are cleared via Paris correspondent banks with FX conversion at the mandated bank rate. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard rate; reduced 15% on first €38,120 of profit for SMEs with turnover ≤ €10 million)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% (standard rate for non-resident shareholders; reduced to 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if holding ≥10% for 2 years; treaty rates may apply)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal year ending December 31, the return must be filed by May 3 of the following year (online filing mandatory for companies with turnover > €50,000; paper filing deadline May 15).

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Résultat fiscal (taxable profit or loss before adjustments) Non-resident companies must report French-source income and may be required to appoint a tax representative in France. Late filing: 5% of tax due plus 0.5% per month of delay (capped at 10%). Late payment: 0.2% per month of unpaid tax. Additional penalties for fraudulent filing (up to 80% of tax due).
Box 2 Bénéfice imposable (taxable income after deductions and reliefs) Apply treaty provisions and ensure proper allocation of income to French permanent establishment if applicable. Late filing: 5% of tax due plus 0.5% per month of delay (capped at 10%). Late payment: 0.2% per month of unpaid tax. Additional penalties for fraudulent filing (up to 80% of tax due).
Box 3 Déficit reportable (tax loss carryforward) Losses from French operations may be carried forward indefinitely, subject to annual limitation rules. Late filing: 5% of tax due plus 0.5% per month of delay (capped at 10%). Late payment: 0.2% per month of unpaid tax. Additional penalties for fraudulent filing (up to 80% of tax due).
Box 4 Crédits d'impôts (tax credits, e.g., CIR, CII) Non-residents may claim credits only if they have a permanent establishment in France. Late filing: 5% of tax due plus 0.5% per month of delay (capped at 10%). Late payment: 0.2% per month of unpaid tax. Additional penalties for fraudulent filing (up to 80% of tax due).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File required if group consolidated revenue exceeds €400 million. For smaller entities, basic TP documentation (including a functional analysis and arm's length justification) must be maintained.

Economic substance scope: France applies general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) and substance-over-form principles. While no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist, the French tax administration scrutinizes entities lacking real economic activity, employees, and premises. A SARL must demonstrate genuine business purpose and substance to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (Article 1741 CGI). Civil penalties: 40% to 80% of understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they participated in fraudulent acts (action en comblement de passif). Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, denial of treaty benefits, and imposition of 40% penalty for lack of substance (Article 155-A CGI).

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they knowingly facilitated the artificial arrangement. Criminal charges for tax fraud may apply.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 5% of annual turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine (Articles 324-1 et seq. Penal Code).

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment and fines, if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent money laundering. Civil liability for damages to third parties.
