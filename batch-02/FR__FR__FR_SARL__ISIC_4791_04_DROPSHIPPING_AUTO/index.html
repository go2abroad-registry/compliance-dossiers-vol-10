Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce — Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), with mandatory electronic filing via the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises operated by INPI.

Governing statute

Code de commerce (French Commercial Code) — Articles L. 223-1 to L. 223-43 (SARL), L. 210-2 (share capital), L. 223-7 (payment-up and escrow of share capital), R. 223-1 et seq., and R. 123-103 (RCS registration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'une société commerciale (single electronic dossier) submitted on the Guichet Unique portal, accompanied by the Statuts (Articles of Association) and statutory exhibits.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 3 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SARL; the capital is freely fixed in the Articles. Where cash contributions are made, at least 20% of the cash capital must be paid up before filing and deposited into a blocked escrow account (séquestre) held by an authorised bank, a notary or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the balance must be paid up within five years (Article L. 223-7 C. com.).

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - French Public Finances Directorate; official portal: impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a French SARL must first obtain a SIREN number via the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE) or the online Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), followed by a SIRET establishment code. For VAT purposes, the entity must apply to the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) having territorial jurisdiction to obtain a French VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire). Non-resident entities without a French establishment carrying out taxable transactions in France may register directly with the DGFiP's foreign business unit (Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères). The application requires the foreign entity's statutory documents, proof of identity of legal representatives, and a description of the French taxable activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the entity level. Under Articles 206 and 239 of the French General Tax Code (Code général des impôts), certain closely-held SARLs whose shares are not listed and whose shareholders are individuals may elect, for a maximum period of five fiscal years, to be treated as transparent partnerships (régime des sociétés de personnes) subject to income tax at shareholder level. This election is filed with the competent tax authority and is irrevocable for the five-year period. Hybrid mismatch elections are limited and generally not available for domestic French entities; France applies the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) hybrid mismatch rules in domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for foreign suppliers; anyone making taxable supplies of goods or services in France in the course of business must register for French VAT regardless of turnover. For distance sales of goods imported from other EU member states, an annual threshold of €10,000 applies for cross-border EU distance sales; beyond this, French VAT registration or OSS (One Stop Shop) registration is required. For non-EU suppliers, registration is required immediately upon the first taxable supply with no de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to exports of goods physically shipped outside the European Union (Article 262 CGI) and to certain services directly related to exports such as transport. To claim zero-rating, the seller must prove that the goods were physically dispatched or transported outside the EU under the conditions of Article 262-0 CGI. The purchaser must be an economic operator, or the goods must be removed from EU customs territory. Evidence of removal is generally established within three months from the invoice date; the three-month deadline can be extended to one year upon prior request to the tax administration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / Single Administrative Document - DAU),
  • Bill of Lading (Connaissement) or Air Waybill / CMR (international transport document),
  • Commercial invoice with mention of export nature, value and destination,
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / proof of payment from the foreign purchaser, where applicable, demonstrating actual receipt of consideration in euros or convertible currency, and
  • Proof of physical departure from EU customs territory (e.g., customs export stamp, electronic departure confirmation, or carrier certificate of shipment).

France mandates electronic invoicing (e-invoicing) for B2B transactions between taxable persons established in France starting 1 September 2026, with mandatory e-reporting of transaction data to the DGFiP through the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation) or a certified private dematerialization platform (PDP). Invoicing data must be transmitted in structured electronic format (Factur-X). For B2C cross-border supplies, e-reporting is required. The system does not currently require real-time fiscal stamping or cryptographic seals, but data transmission is mandatory for all transactions subject to French VAT. For automated dropshipping platforms, integrated API-based reporting will be required to synchronize invoicing and transaction data with the prescribed dematerialization platform.

Export zero-rating is subject to stringent documentary scrutiny by the DGFiP. Failure to produce credible proof of export within the statutory deadline results in the reversal of the 0% rate and assessment of the standard VAT rate (20%) on the transaction along with interest for late payment (intérêts de retard). In cases of fictitious exports, fraudulent invoicing, or deliberate dissimulation, the tax administration may impose an 80% penalty for bad faith (manquement délibéré) or 100% for abusive manipulation or participation in a fraudulent scheme. Criminal sanctions may include fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment for the legal representatives under tax fraud provisions. Retrospective clawback can extend to all previously zero-rated exports of the same pattern within the statute of limitations period (generally three years, extended to six years for undisclosed facts).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is a fully convertible currency under the EU acquis and French law. Both current and capital accounts are liberalized with no prior authorization required for most transactions. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without restriction, subject only to standard beneficial ownership disclosure and post-transaction tax reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds is required under French law. Companies may retain foreign currency in offshore accounts. All export transactions must comply with French VAT (TVA) rules, be recorded in corporate accounts, and declared if exceeding reporting thresholds for customs or tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France
