Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SARL (may be fixed as low as EUR 1); however, the entire share capital must be fully subscribed and each cash contribution must be deposited in a blocked bank account opened in the company's name, released only upon issuance of the RCS registration extract. In-kind contributions above EUR 30,000 or exceeding 50% of the share capital require a statutory capital auditor (commissaire aux apports); cash contributions of any amount exceeding EUR 1,000 require the blocked-account attestation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP); central taxpayer portal 'impots.gouv.fr'; business filings and registrations through the Guichet Unique (INPI) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; corporate tax returns filed via 'Espace Professionnel' / e-services GEDI.

E-government filing portal

A newly formed SARL obtains its legal identity and tax identification through the Guichet Unique operated by INPI. After RCS (Registre du commerce et des sociétés) registration, DGFIP automatically assigns a SIREN (9-digit national company identifier), a SIRET workplace identifier, and a French intra-Community VAT number (format FR[two-digit key] + SIREN) for VAT-registered activities. A foreign non-EU company engaged directly in French taxable activities must apply proactively to the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) of its place of business or to the non-resident tax office (Service des impôts des particuliers / non-résidents) using Form 1447? or the dedicated Article 289 B registration procedure, submitting constitutional documents, proof of permanent establishment/economic activity in France, and confirmation of the appointed French tax representative/representative fiscal if no permanent establishment. The application must be made before the first taxable supply in France.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election under French law. A standard commercial SARL is by default subject to French corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). The CGI provides a limited transparency election for eligible 'SARL de famille' under Article 239 bis AA CGI, allowing taxation at the level of individual partners in place of entity-level IS when the statutory family-shareholding conditions are met. Contrariwise, an EURL (SARL with a single natural-person member) is treated as a transparent societé de personnes unless it opts irreversibly into IS under Article 206.4 CGI. For cross-border scenarios, France applies the ATAD anti-hybrid rules (Articles 212 bis, 212 ter CGI) which deny deductions or recharacterize payments where another jurisdiction treats an SARL as transparent or applies a different entity classification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France grants full currency convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions, in accordance with Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the EU free movement of capital regime. The euro is fully convertible; there are no surrender or repatriation impediments. Profit remittances by a French SARL to non-resident shareholders are permitted without exchange-control authorisation, subject to corporate law conditions: existence of distributable profit, compliance with legal reserve requirements, and shareholder approval. The practical hurdles are documentary and statistical, not regulatory: the cross-border transfer must be reported to the Banque de France via the intermediary credit institution, using the appropriate balance-of-payments purpose code and including tax-residence documentation of the beneficial owner. Withholding-tax exposure on dividends or interest is separate from FX-control and must be assessed under domestic and treaty rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no fixed deadline for repatriating export receipts. Export proceeds may be held in foreign currency, converted at the corporate's discretion, or used directly to settle foreign-currency import obligations. However, cross-border transactions involving French resident entities must be declared for balance-of-payments statistical purposes if the amount exceeds the applicable regulatory threshold (typically EUR 10,000 or the per-transaction reporting threshold), through the authorised intermediary bank. The underlying export contracts, customs declarations (DEB), and commercial invoices must be maintained to substantiate the purpose and lawful origin of the funds.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) — monetary authority and responsible for balance-of-payments statistics; exchange-control and foreign-investment policy is coordinated with the Direction Générale du Trésor; banking and e-money licensing and supervision are delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High for the operational treasury and low for the statutory capital escrow function. French-regulated electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, and licensed neobanks) are viable for daily euro and multi-currency accounts, receiving platform payouts from Uber Eats and DoorDash, and executing inbound and outbound SEPA payments. These EMIs operate under the ACPR e-money licence or as registered payment institutions under PSD2, and they are permitted to provide payment accounts and payment services but are not full credit institutions; they cannot offer the official attestation de dépôt des fonds required for the establishment of a SARL capital. Offshore EMIs that lack an EU or ACPR-recognised licence cannot lawfully provide payment services to a French SARL under the mandatory EU passporting or equivalence requirements; using an unlicensed offshore EMI would expose the company to banking-license, AML/CFT, and wire-transfer-decision enforcement risk. Therefore, the capital contribution must be held in a Tier-1 commercial bank, while the foreign-expansion payout accounts may be opened at an ACPR-regulated EMI for operational efficiency.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from a non-resident shareholder to the French SARL must be executed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV for interbank pass-through) from the shareholder's bank, with the remittance information field explicitly stating 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / DÉPÔT DU CAPITAL SOCIAL' and referencing the SARL's corporate name and the statutory manager. The receiving French bank will file the cross-border transaction declaration with the Banque de France using the harmonised purpose code for direct investment or capital injection (e.g., code 900/EDP in Banque de France nomenclature) and will verify that the funds originate from a permissible source under AML/CFT. Before the SARL registration, the funds must be deposited in a blocked capital-escrow account with a French credit institution or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations or a notary; the bank issues a 'certificat de dépôt des fonds' that is submitted to the RCS to complete incorporation. After registration, the funds are released to the operational account. The transfer must be supported by a shareholder resolution approving the capital injection, proof of shareholder identity, and a long-term lending agreement if the injection is shareholder debt rather than equity.For trade and procurement settlements, the SARL may use the SWIFT MT103 for direct euro or foreign-currency payments, which is the most common rail for paying ingredient suppliers and equipment vendors. SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) is the efficient rail for euro-denominated payments to EU counterparties, while SWIFT MT103 remains necessary for non-EEA wires. For high-value procurement, a commercial irrevocable documentary letter of credit can be established by the French bank under the ICC UCP600 and settled through SWIFT MT754 and MT799 reimbursement messages. Cash-in-advance and open-account settlement are supported by the same MT103 infrastructure. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 – a French SARL is subject to mandatory transfer pricing documentation (local file, master file) if its annual turnover or total balance-sheet assets exceed EUR 50 million or it belongs to a group whose French subsidiary meets these thresholds. Below that threshold, the documentation must still be prepared and provided upon the tax administration's specific request if material cross-border transactions exist, based on the level of controlled international transactions.

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing register. The substance of a French SARL is established through GAAR article L64 of the LPF and the anti-abuse rule under CGI art. 205 A. A ghost-kitchen SARL must evidence real physical kitchen premises, employed delivery-coordination staff, platform contracts with actual external meal-service operators, and bookkeeping that supports the arm's-length nature of all fees paid or received. Without such substance the tax administration may disregard artificial arrangements and recharacterise transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under CGI art. 1741, fine up to EUR 75,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; aggravation by organized group raises the penalty to EUR 200,000 and 7 years. The tax administration concurrently demands 80% of the evaded tax as a punitive assessment plus late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors as natural persons incur joint and several civil liability for the evaded tax and penalties; in aggravated cases travel bans, freezing of personal bank accounts, and imprisonment are imposed.
Artificial Arrangement / Abuse of Law (Abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: Under LPF art. L64, the tax administration disregards the artificial act and reassesses tax as if it had not occurred, plus a penalty of 80% if the taxpayer cannot prove good faith; otherwise the penalty is 40%. Monthly late-payment interest accrues at 0.2%.

Director personal liability: Directors having signed the underlying fraudulent contracts are personally liable for the increased assessment and any unpaid corporate tax; where the abuse amounts to fraud, criminal liability under art. 1741 attaches with imprisonment.
Money Laundering / AML Infraction

Statutory sanction: French Monetary and Financial Code arts. L.574-1 et seq. punish laundering of proceeds of aggravated tax fraud with up to 10 years’ imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 750,000; the privilege of conducting commercial activities may be suspended and the corporate entity dissolved.

Director personal liability: Directors who organise or direct the scheme face criminal fines multiplied by the corporate fine framework (up to EUR 1,000,000 for natural persons), permanent prohibition from managing a company, and asset seizure.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: CGI art. 1735-B imposes a fine equal to 5% of the amount of the undocumented transfers, capped at EUR 50,000 per financial year, in addition to a tax reassessment under domestic arm's-length rules and mandatory interest.

Director personal liability: If the incomplete documentation results in a mis-assessment, directors may be held personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax under la responsabilité solidaire; fraudulent intent invokes the 5-year/€75,000 criminal penalty of art. 1741 and a potential travel ban during the criminal investigation.
