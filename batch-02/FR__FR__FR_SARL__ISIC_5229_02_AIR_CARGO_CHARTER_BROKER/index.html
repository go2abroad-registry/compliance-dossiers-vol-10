Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for a SARL; the capital is freely fixed in the Articles of Association. The subscribed capital must be fully subscribed, and at least one-fifth (20%) must be paid up on incorporation (Art. L.223-7), with the balance payable within five years. Funds must be deposited with a French credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the depositary issues an escrow certificate of deposit valid until the Kbis registration, upon which the funds are released. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), French General Directorate of Public Finance, under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty. Primary portal: https://www.impots.gouv.fr; business registration single-window portal (Guichet Unique) operated by INPI: https://procedures.inpi.fr; customs and export control authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

For a newly formed French SARL: (i) file incorporation with the Guichet Unique electronic portal to obtain SIREN (9-digit INSEE identification number) and SIRET (establishment code); (ii) upon SIREN issuance, the DGFiP automatically allocates the French intra-Community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) with format FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN where the entity files an activity declaration (déclaration d'existence) with the competent SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises); (iii) foreign non-established entities performing taxable transactions in France must register directly with the DGFiP Non-Resident Unit (Unité de Gestion des Impositions des Non-Résidents, 10 rue du Centre, Noisy-le-Grand) using form No. 3826-SD (VAT) or via the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) in the Member State of establishment for distance sales of goods; (iv) documentary package for non-resident registration: certified articles of association, certificate of foreign tax residence, power of attorney for the appointed fiscal representative (for non-EU principals), director's passport and proof of address, and bank statements evidencing the operating account in France or the client country; (v) the SARL must also file the annual beneficial-owner declaration (RBE - Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs) and obtain an EORI number (GBC-000-XXXXX) for customs operations through the DGDDI portal SOPRANO Pro.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope: A French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés - IS) at the standard rate of 25%, with a reduced 15% rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs (turnover below €10 million); resident entities are taxed on worldwide profits, while foreign companies are taxed in France exclusively on profits attributable to a French permanent establishment (établissement stable); dividends received benefit from the 95% participation-exemption (régime mère-fille) subject to a 5% taxable fraction, and profits repatriated by a foreign parent are subject to a 30% withholding tax on distributions absent treaty relief. Air cargo charter brokerage revenue is treated as taxable service income in France unless the place-of-supply rules (Articles 259 to 259 D CGI) assign the taxable event outside France.

Available election forms

France has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) Option for partnership taxation under Article 239 bis AA CGI enabling a SARL to be taxed transparently at the shareholder level (Impôt sur le Revenu) for a maximum duration of five fiscal years where the company is dedicated to a defined activity (e.g., specialized brokerage) and all qualifying shareholders consent; (ii) fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI allowing a French parent holding at least 95% of the SARL to consolidate the SARL's results in a group tax return — optional but binding for five years; (iii) option under Article 219 CGI for small and medium companies to retain the 15% reduced IS rate; (iv) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Article 256 C III CGI for closely linked French entities; (v) hybrid mismatch declarations under Article 212 bis CGI and DAC-6 (Directive 2011/16/EU as amended) requiring disclosure of hybrid instruments and reverse-hybrid arrangements; elections are filed with the competent SIE by the tax return filing deadline (generally 30 April of the following year via EFI/EDI platform).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Established French SARL: the 'franchise en base de TVA' (small-business VAT exemption) applies only below €91,900 annual turnover for goods and €36,800 for services (2025 thresholds, with reduced ceilings of €39,100 and €18,900 after two consecutive years above the standard threshold); once the threshold is exceeded, full VAT registration is mandatory from the first transaction of the exceeding month. Non-established foreign entities: NO registration threshold applies — any taxable B2B or B2C transaction performed in France triggers immediate VAT registration, although B2B services supplied by a non-resident broker to a French VAT-registered client are effectively shifted to the client via the reverse-charge (autoliquidation) mechanism under Article 283-2 CGI; EU-established sellers may use the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) regime for distance sales when their EU-wide turnover is below the €10,000 electronic-commerce micro-threshold; air cargo charter brokerage services are B2B in nature and thus subject to reverse charge where the customer is a taxable person established in France, while services to non-EU clients fall outside French VAT under the place-of-supply rule of Article 259 B CGI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération avec droit à déduction / TVA au taux 0%) is available under: (i) Article 262-1 CGI for goods physically exported outside the European Union provided dispatch occurs within 90 days of supply and proof of exit is retained; (ii) Article 262-2 CGI for ancillary and brokering services directly connected to exported goods, including air cargo transport, charter brokerage, loading, handling, and freight-forwarding services rendered to the exporter or to the carrier of the exported goods; (iii) Article 259 B CGI provides that B2B services supplied by the French SARL broker to a client established in a non-EU country are outside the territorial scope of French VAT (i.e., deemed zero-rate), irrespective of where the goods travel, provided the recipient is a taxable person acting as such and the intermediary service is legally distinct from the underlying agreement; (iv) intra-Community supply of goods to EU clients may also qualify for exemption under Article 262-1-1 CGI subject to Greek Union sales-list filing; the broker must retain contractual evidence (charter party agreement, agency or brokerage contract, air waybill) demonstrating the direct link between the brokerage service and the physical export movement, and must verify the customer's non-EU VAT status through reliable commercial databases.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / Déclaration Administrative Unique) lodged electronically via the DELTA-X / ECS module of SOPRANO Pro, bearing the customs office master reference and export date
  • Air Waybill (LTA - Lettre de Transport Aérien) or Master Bill of Lading evidencing the cargo movement from France to a non-EU destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Charter Party or Brokerage Contract stating the place of supply, service description, and the non-EU status of the counterparty
  • Proof of Physical Exit and Delivery (destination customs entry proof, consignee delivery receipt, carrier cargo manifest, or port/airport authority stamped documentation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bordereau de rapatriement) — bank SWIFT credit confirmation or transfer receipt evidencing settlement from the non-French counterparty within the commercial credit period
  • Packing List and Cargo Manifest / Master House Manifest certification where applicable

France is deploying a mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting framework under Articles 289 bis CGI and Decree No. 2022-1299: (i) from 1 September 2026 all VAT-registered businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices in the standard formats (Factur-X hybrid PDF/XML, UBL, or CII), and large and mid-size companies (grandes et moyennes entreprises) must issue them; (ii) from 1 September 2027 all small and micro entities (PME/TPE) must issue e-invoices and transmit transaction data through a certified PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) connected to the public clearing portal PPF (Portail Public de Facturation); (iii) clearance is near-real-time — transaction data must be transmitted to the PPF within 1 working day of invoice issuance, including e-reporting data for B2C sales, intra-Community supplies, and export transactions; (iv) digital stamping and integrity requirements mandate a qualified electronic signature (cachet de l'opérateur) or a secure business-data exchange network; (v) non-compliance penalties under Article 1731 ter CGI: €15 per non-compliant invoice (capped at €15,000 per calendar year), and 5% of the transaction amount (50% for fraud) for failure to transmit e-reporting data. The SARL cargo charter broker must ensure its invoicing platform is Factur-X compliant and connected to an authorized PDP before the 1 September 2026 obligation date.

DGFiP and DGDDI jointly verify export zero-rating claims through the Enquête Export internal control program and customs post-clearance audits. Failure to produce the statutory export proof within the required window triggers: (i) VAT reassessment at the standard 20% rate plus intérêts de retard at 0.2% per month under Article 1727 CGI (2.4% per annum); (ii) a 5% late-payment surcharge if assessed tax remains unpaid after 30 days; (iii) a 40% penalty for bad-faith understatement (mauvaise foi) and 80% penalty for fraudulent maneuvers (manœuvre frauduleuse) under Article 1729 CGI; (iv) customs sanctions under Articles 414 to 423 of the Code des Douanes, including fines from one to three times the tax evaded, confiscation of the goods or transport equipment, and imprisonment up to 5 years in cases of organized fraud; (v) retrospective clawback extends 3 fiscal years (6 years in the presence of fraud or undeclared activity, and up to 10 years for non-registered operations); (vi) the SARL and its gérant are jointly and severally liable for assessed duties; denial of zero-rating also severs related input VAT recovery and may trigger the interest charge and publication of corrective tax returns (fondations).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full capital account convertibility and current account convertibility under EU regulations. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted, subject solely to standard corporate tax compliance, beneficial ownership declarations, and anti-money laundering verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies. Air cargo charter receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts without conversion. There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio; proceeds may be used freely for operational purposes after standard documentation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax rate (impôt sur les sociétés).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. The rate may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable double tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. A qualifying EU parent may generally obtain a 0% rate where ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, beneficial ownership, anti-abuse, and documentary conditions are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on a date other than 31 December, the return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. For a 31 December year-end, the electronic return is generally due on the second business day following 1 May of the following year, subject to the annual DGFiP filing calendar. Corporate income tax instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, or by 15 May for a 31 December year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million is the principal threshold for mandatory comprehensive transfer pricing documentation, generally applying where the French entity or French tax group reaches the statutory turnover or gross-assets threshold, or where the entity is owned or controlled by an entity meeting the threshold. The documentation generally comprises a Master File and Local File and must be available to the French tax authority upon request. A separate annual simplified transfer pricing declaration on Form 2257-SD is generally triggered at EUR 50 million turnover or gross assets, or by specified ownership, control, or transaction conditions.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a separate general Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless essential under the French general anti-abuse doctrine, abuse-of-law rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, treaty anti-abuse provisions, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, hybrid mismatch rules, and permanent-establishment principles. A French SARL should maintain French commercial rationale, decision-making, personnel or appropriately controlled outsourced functions, premises or operational capacity, bank and accounting records, risk control, and contemporaneous evidence that the air-cargo brokerage activity is actually performed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, surcharges up to 80% in specified fraudulent or abusive cases, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that may include imprisonment and substantial fines. Aggravated tax fraud can attract imprisonment of up to seven years and a fine of up to EUR 3 million for individuals, subject to statutory conditions and judicial determination.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur criminal liability, civil liability to the company or creditors, management bans, and tax-liability proceedings where fraudulent or seriously negligent conduct causes non-payment. Imprisonment is not automatic. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax sanction but may arise from judicial measures or criminal proceedings.
Shell company, treaty abuse, and beneficial-ownership failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or EU relief, withholding tax assessment, interest, 40% or 80% penalties where applicable, general anti-abuse or abuse-of-law adjustments, and possible criminal exposure where false declarations or fraudulent structures are established.

Director personal liability: Directors approving sham structures or false certificates may face personal criminal and civil exposure, management restrictions, and liability for resulting tax losses where statutory conditions for personal liability are met.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment, denial of deductions, withholding or recharacterization of payments, documentation penalties, late-payment interest, and tax surcharges. The documentation penalty may be based on the statutory minimum or a percentage of the transactions covered by missing or incomplete documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for deliberate profit diversion, insolvency-related liability, and criminal liability if documentation is knowingly falsified or the arrangement forms part of tax fraud.
VAT and invoice fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, recovery of improperly refunded VAT, late-payment interest, penalties potentially reaching 40% or 80%, invoice penalties, and criminal prosecution for organized or fraudulent conduct.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally liable in cases of fraudulent VAT conduct, deliberate participation, or serious management fault, in addition to criminal sanctions and possible management bans.
Customs, sanctions, export-control, and aviation-security breach

Statutory sanction: Customs duties, VAT, seizure, administrative sanctions, loss of authorizations, transaction prohibitions, substantial fines, and criminal penalties under French, EU customs, sanctions, export-control, and aviation-security law.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, imprisonment where applicable, professional disqualification, and civil claims. Travel bans may be imposed only through applicable judicial or administrative measures.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing failure

Statutory sanction: Regulatory enforcement, reporting obligations to TRACFIN where applicable, administrative fines, criminal money-laundering penalties, confiscation, and suspension or loss of professional authorizations.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for laundering, complicity, failure to prevent or report qualifying conduct, or deliberate compliance failures. Imprisonment, fines, confiscation, and management bans may apply; travel restrictions depend on judicial orders.
Unlicensed or improperly operated freight-forwarding and transport-intermediary activity

Statutory sanction: Administrative orders, fines, prohibition from operating, withdrawal of registration or authorization, contract and insurance consequences, and criminal sanctions where the underlying conduct constitutes an offense.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for knowingly operating without required authorization, misleading customers or authorities, or causing safety, customs, or financial harm. Civil damages, management bans, and criminal penalties may follow.
Corporate insolvency and asset diversion

Statutory sanction: Clawback of transactions, insolvency proceedings, extension of insolvency liability, bankruptcy-related sanctions, fines, and potential imprisonment for fraudulent bankruptcy or asset concealment.

Director personal liability: Managers may be ordered to contribute to company liabilities, prohibited from managing companies, and prosecuted for mismanagement or fraudulent bankruptcy. Civil restitution and confiscation may apply. Travel bans are possible only where ordered under applicable judicial procedures.
