Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a French SARL; the articles may fix the share capital freely. Each part sociale must be fully paid-up at subscription. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked bank escrow account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and may be released to the managers only after RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a Commissaire aux apports valuation report where one contribution exceeds €30,000 or exceeds half of the share capital.

Funding & unlock path: SOLAS-certified Fire Safety System Certificate and International Load Line Certificate; structural load line inspections and hull surveys by an approved classification society; ISPS Ship Security Certificate; occupational health and safety clearance by French maritime labor inspection authorities; workplace risk assessment, fire safety control measures and structural load clearance for the corporate registered office or any shore facility occupied by the company.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – French Public Finance Directorate; business portal: impots.gouv.fr and Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) for e-invoicing

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first establish a French fixed base or permanent establishment (or incorporate/register an existing SARL through the Guichet Unique). The SARL obtains its SIREN (national business identifier) and SIRET (establishment number) from the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the INPI single window. For VAT purposes, the entity must apply to the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for a French VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) using Form M0 or the online e-procédure; foreign taxable persons that carry out taxable transactions in France may also request direct VAT registration under Article 286 ter of the French General Tax Code (CGI), with supporting evidence of legal existence (certificate of incorporation, registered office, proof of economic activity in France). The application must specify the nature of the activity and estimated French turnover; a valid French VAT number is normally issued within 2 to 4 weeks after receipt of a complete file. Separate registrations may be required for payroll (URSSAF) and customs (EORI number) where goods movements occur.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law does not provide a check-the-box regime. A SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). However, certain small SARLs may elect, under Article 239 bis AA CGI, to be taxed as a partnership (régime des sociétés de personnes) on a five-year renewable basis if the members are natural persons and the company meets specified size thresholds. VAT grouping (assujetti unique) is available under Article 256 C CGI with effect from 1 January 2023 for French-established entities and certain non-established entities, allowing combined filing. No hybrid mismatch 'check-the-box' election exists; hybrid mismatch disallowances are implemented under ATAD (Articles 212 bis A and following CGI) and may require notification of mismatch arrangements to the DGFiP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For French-established small businesses, the VAT exemption threshold is EUR 85,000 for principal activities involving sale of goods and EUR 37,500 for principal service activities (Article 293 B CGI). However, the place of supply for a RoRo maritime transport service between France and a third country is generally outside France under Article 259 B CGI, and therefore a foreign carrier providing such services may not need French VAT registration. French VAT registration is mandatory for any business established in France making taxable supplies, without a turnover threshold, and for foreign providers using a French VAT registration for supplies of services where the place of supply is France under B2B or B2C rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to the export of goods outside the European Union under Article 262-0 CGI, and to international transport services involving transportation of goods between a point in France or another EU Member State and a point outside the EU, and to maritime transport services directly linked to those exports/imports (Article 262-1 CGI). For a RoRo vessel carrying vehicles between France and a non-EU port, the ocean freight and related transport services are zero-rated where the transport is direct and is evidenced as cross-border services. The supply must be made to a customer established outside the EU, or the transport must physically commence or terminate at a non-EU port. Services physically performed wholly outside the EU are outside the scope of French VAT. The taxpayer must hold documentary evidence of the international character of the voyage and, if goods are co-loaded, the customs export or import declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation via DELTA/SWIFT) – required where vehicles or goods are physically exported; for a service-only RoRo voyage, a commercial invoice and voyage service contract serve in lieu
  • Bill of Lading (Connaissement / B/L) – issued for vehicles carried by sea; must indicate loading port and discharge port, vessel name, consignee, and cargo description
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank SWIFT MT103 / receipt of payment from the foreign charterer or freight payer) – substantiates receipt of the international transport consideration for the zero-rating claim

France is phasing in mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting. Under the Finance Act 2023, businesses established in France must issue invoices electronically via the PPF (Portail Public de Facturation) or a certified Private Dematerialization Platform (PDP) from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small and medium-sized enterprises. Structured e-invoicing formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII) and transaction data (e-reporting) are transmitted to the DGFiP through the PPF or PDP APIs in near real time. There is no digital stamping or VAT fiscalization requirement for ordinary invoices; however, for customs and export goods, the export declaration must be submitted electronically through the DELTA system and validated by French Customs.

The French tax administration can retrospectively deny zero-rating if the documentary evidence is inadequate, misfiled, or if the transport is not genuinely international. Under Articles 1728 and 1729 CGI, a deliberate VAT under-payment or misclassification attracts a penalty of 40% (serious inaccuracy) or 80% (deliberate fraud), plus late-payment interest of 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI). Customs offences relating to false export declarations or falsified transport documents are sanctioned under Articles 414-2 and 415-1 of the French Customs Code and may include confiscation of goods, vessel sanctions, criminal fines up to twice the value of the subject goods, and imprisonment. Clawbacks may extend to recovered VAT credits plus penalties and may jeopardize the VAT registration status of the operator.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the Code monétaire et financier (CMF), Articles L151-1 et seq.; no administrative authorization is required for routine trade, service, or financial cross-border flows. Practical hurdles are limited to: (i) mandatory Banque de France balance-of-payments (couverture statistique) reporting for every cross-border payment equal to or exceeding EUR 750,000 and for all foreign direct investments regardless of amount; (ii) EU and French foreign-investment screening under Regulation (EU) 2019/452 and CMF filed reporting requirements, which may apply only where the SARL controls critical infrastructure, including certain port/maritime critical assets — deep-sea RoRo car-carrier vessel operation is not per se screened but French-registered vessel-owning assets may trigger notification; (iii) profit remittance to foreign shareholders is unrestricted once tax compliance is evidenced through the issuance of a tax clearance certificate (nihil obstat) by the French tax administration; no other FX or transfer approvals apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion obligation. The SARL may retain 100% of its USD freight income, demurrage, ancillary RoRo handling fees, and charter-hire proceeds in foreign-currency accounts with any authorized credit institution indefinitely. There is no prescribed timeline for conversion into EUR and no minimum conversion ratio; the treasury may voluntarily maintain USD balances to match USD-denominated liabilities or hedge into EUR. The sole compliance duty is statistical: the resident bank reports all cross-border flows of EUR 750,000 or more per transaction to the Banque de France, and invoices denominated in USD are freely permitted under French law.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the Eurosystem, jointly overseeing exchange-control statistics and balance-of-payments reporting with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury); prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment service providers is delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-to-Low as primary treasury and High as a sub-operational wallet. French and EU-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Wise Business, Revolut Business, and Bunq are payment institutions under the Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366 and CMF Book V; they cannot lawfully take deposits, issue letters of credit, provide documentary trade finance, or hold a capital-escrow mandate because a capital increase deposit requires a licensed credit institution (établissement de crédit) or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations. Legal constraints also include safeguarded-account segregation rather than commercial deposit protection, inability to provide MT103-backed correspondent clearing for large USD flows, and practical ACPR-driven friction where the aggregate monthly throughput exceeds the EUR 750,000 balance-of-payments reporting threshold. Recommended use: port disbursement cards, crew expense cards, and small-value SEPA direct debits only; the capital escrow and all LC operations must remain with Tier-1 credit institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) open a blocked capital escrow account (compte bloqué) at a Tier-1 French credit institution acting as depository; (2) the shareholder effects payment via SWIFT MT103 with the following content — Field 50K (ordering institution) must show the shareholder's identity, Field 59 (beneficiary) must show the SARL French IBAN and SIRET/SIREN, Field 70 in narrative must state 'AUGMENTATION DE CAPITAL SARL [name]' and reference the extraordinary general meeting date; French banks map this to Banque de France purpose code '6.26' (direct investment / equity); (3) if the shareholder is a non-EU person or entity, file the French Treasury foreign direct investment declaration (CERFA 14959*03) with the Direction Générale du Trésor and the Banque de France within 20 days of the transaction; intra-EU shareholders benefit from the EU-investor exemption from prior notification; (4) the bank will release escrow funds into the operational account only upon delivery of the notarized capital-increase minutes, the subscription certificate (acte de souscription), updated K-bis, and, if required by law or the bank, a statutory auditor's report on the capital increase. Aggregate transfers with a value equal to or above EUR 750,000 are automatically declared by the French bank to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes.Clearing hierarchy for operational and trade disbursements: (i) SWIFT MT103 (single-charge 'OUR' structure preferred) is used for charter-hire, bunker-supplier payments, port disbursements, stevedoring, crew agency fees, and broker commissions, with the related invoice number and contract reference in Field 70; (ii) Irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by the French Tier-1 bank and payable under UCP 600 are the mandated rail for shipyard installments, vessel acquisition deposits under memorandum of agreement, and any major equipment supply; for demand guarantees and refund guarantees URDG 758 applies; (iii) in EUR, TARGET2 transfers are available through the French bank as the primary rail for high-value payments, while SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant are used for domestic payroll and low-value disbursements; (iv) in USD, the preferred correspondent rails are CHIPS and Fedwire via the bank's New York branch (BNP Paribas New York or Société Générale New York), with instructions to debit in USD only and all charges applied to benefiter; (v) every disbursement equal to or exceeding EUR 750,000 triggers a Banque de France statistical balance-of-payments declaration, filed by the French bank; the SARL's treasury must provide documentary support (commercial invoice, booking note, bill of lading, charter-party excerpt) to each payment instruction.FX Forward (EUR/USD outright) with tenors up to 12 months, entered into exclusively with an ACPR-licensed credit institution and documented under an ISDA or French master agreement; no margin required for interbank relationship lines.FX Swap (short-dated swap cambiste) for liquidity and maturity-mismatch management between USD freight receipts and EUR operating expenses, including spot-next and tom-next rolling swaps where operational.Zero-Cost Collar structured as a European-style option pair (long call / short put) on EUR/USD matched to the forecast cargo-flow schedule, permitted only with board approval and set within conservative treasury policy limits.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCIRS) to convert USD-denominated ship-finance term-loan liabilities into EUR or to synthetically match long-term USD charter income with EUR balance-sheet exposure; hedging eligibility requires that a directly related USD debt or firm freight contract exists.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium-to-Low as primary treasury and High as a sub-operational wallet. French and EU-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Wise Business, Revolut Business, and Bunq are payment institutions under the Payment Services Directive (EU) 2015/2366 and CMF Book V; they cannot lawfully take deposits, issue letters of credit, provide documentary trade finance, or hold a capital-escrow mandate because a capital increase deposit requires a licensed credit institution (établissement de crédit) or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations. Legal constraints also include safeguarded-account segregation rather than commercial deposit protection, inability to provide MT103-backed correspondent clearing for large USD flows, and practical ACPR-driven friction where the aggregate monthly throughput exceeds the EUR 750,000 balance-of-payments reporting threshold. Recommended use: port disbursement cards, crew expense cards, and small-value SEPA direct debits only; the capital escrow and all LC operations must remain with Tier-1 credit institutions.

Tax Evasion
