Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) via impots.gouv.fr; customs and export VAT supervision: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities incorporating or establishing a French SARL must file legal and tax registration through the 'Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises' (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) to obtain SIREN/SIRET and the KBis company extract. The DGFIP then allocates the French intra-community VAT number (FR + 2-digit checksum + 9-digit SIREN). Non-established foreign taxable persons making supplies in France must use the DGFIP non-resident registration procedure (Form 12103/Cerfa n° 12103*7) and may be required to appoint a tax representative if they have no EU establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities incorporating or establishing a French SARL must file legal and tax registration through the 'Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises' (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) to obtain SIREN/SIRET and the KBis company extract. The DGFIP then allocates the French intra-community VAT number (FR + 2-digit checksum + 9-digit SIREN). Non-established foreign taxable persons making supplies in France must use the DGFIP non-resident registration procedure (Form 12103/Cerfa n° 12103*7) and may be required to appoint a tax representative if they have no EU establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box regime. A SARL is subject by default to entity-level corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) under CGI Article 206; a qualifying 'family SARL' may elect pass-through individual income tax under CGI Article 239. Applicable group elections include 'intégration fiscale' (tax consolidation) for French companies operating through a 95%+ French subsidiary chain. Hybrid mismatch outcomes must be reported on the statutory hybrid declaration and are subject to ATAD disallowance rules rather than elective treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 34,400 for predominantly service activities (including digital and distance services); EUR 85,000 for goods sales under CGI Article 293 B — annual turnover thresholds for the 'franchise en base de TVA'.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical exports of goods outside the EU, the supply is zero-rated under CGI Article 262(I) if title and risk pass to the non-resident buyer and the goods are physically shipped outside the EU within the prescribed evidentiary period (usually 90 days from supply). For services, hyper-casual mobile game advertising monetization is generally assessed under the B2B place-of-supply rule based on the customer's establishment; non-EU B2B customers keep the service outside French VAT, while EU B2C digital services may trigger the OSS scheme. The taxable person must retain commercial, transport, and banking proof of the actual cross-border movement or the foreign customer's establishment.

  • Customs export declaration (Déclaration d'exportation) filed electronically via DELTA/GUN, bearing an export declaration number
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or CMR road consignment note indicating France/European Community as departure origin and non-EU destination
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT/transfer confirmation evidencing collection of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sales contract
  • Proof of physical exit from EU customs territory (exit summary statement, export accompaniment document/ELBA, or destination customs record)

Electronic invoicing is mandatory for B2B supplies on a phased basis: from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for SMEs, receivable through certified Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDP) using Factur-X, UBL, or CII formats; invoice data is transmitted via PDP APIs to DGFIP for e-reporting. France does not currently impose transaction-level fiscal stamping or pre-clearance continuous controls, but e-reporting of B2B and B2C transaction data is required, and public-sector invoices already use the Chorus Pro portal. Non-EU suppliers using digital platforms may have compliance obligations under the OSS/IOSS schemes.

The DGFIP rigorously examines export evidence; if zero-rating is disallowed, the tax administration reassesses output VAT with intérêt de retard (0.20% per month), a 10% late-payment inclusion, a 40% deliberate-omission penalty (manquement délibéré), or an 80% penalty for fraudulent maneuvering/abus de droit. Criminal prosecution under CGI Article 1741 may include imprisonment and fines of up to EUR 500,000 or 500% of evaded tax. Customs authorities may impose confiscation and additional monetary penalties under the French Customs Code where false export declarations are used, with a three-year assessment limitation extended to ten years for tax fraud, and potential joint and several liability of the legal representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible in both current and capital accounts under EU directives 88/361/EEC and Articles 63-65 TFEU; the French FX-control regime imposes no licensing or exchange-control approval on trade, profit remittance, or debt service. Capital and current movements are freely executed through an authorised resident bank, but must satisfy financial information reporting thresholds. In practice, profit repatriation by a French SARL requires annual accounts approval, a CEO/manager remittance resolution, tax residency and beneficial ownership certification for treaty relief, and a positive solvency test under French corporate law; the standard French dividend tax of 30% (12.8% income tax plus 17.2% social levies) applies absent EU parent-subsidiary or treaty reduction. No French FX authorisation is required for dividend remittance; the only hurdle is compliance with bank documentary and anti-money-laundering expectations. Non-resident shareholders may hold 100% of the SARL, and currency conversion at the time of market payment is carried out by the receiving/remitting bank at freely negotiated market rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio for export or commercial proceeds in France. Exporters are not required to convert foreign currency receipts into euro, nor are they obliged to repatriate them into France within a specified number of days, provided the funds are held in an account in a compliant financial institution. However, French economic statistics require all cross-border settlements exceeding EUR 50,000 (or cash transactions above EUR 10,000) to be reported by the resident intermediary bank to Banque de France using standard balance-of-payments transaction codes. For a hyper-casual gaming publisher, export proceeds consist of ad-network payouts and digital goods sales; the company must preserve traffic, sales and transaction-level settlement reports by country of residence of the payer, alongside VAT evidence, to substantiate export treatment and ensure accurate purpose-code classification.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French central bank and balance-of-payments statistical authority), acting jointly with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) under the Code Monétaire et Financier and the Haut Conseil de Stabilité Financière.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Core treasury viability is Moderate-to-High for operational disbursements: a French-licensed or EU-passported Electronic Money Institution (EMI) under PSD2 may hold payment accounts, execute SEPA/SWIFT payment initiation, and deliver multi-currency FX conversion. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot provide an escrow guarantee, negotiate commercial letters of credit, or offer deposit-like lending services without an additional credit-institution licence; the French banking monopoly under Articles L511-1 et seq. of the Code Monétaire et Financier prevents non-banks from taking deposits from the public. Accordingly, EMIs such as Qonto, Shine, N26 and Lydia are best deployed as operational rails for inbound ad-network payouts and outbound traffic-acquisition settlements, whereas a full-service tier-1 bank should remain the sole custodian of subscribed capital and statutory escrow funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Subscribed capital and shareholder loans must arrive by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT for euro transfers initiated by a French/EU bank) to the SARL's opening account held with a French licensed bank. The instructing bank must include the full business name of the company, the purpose of the transaction, and the Banque de France balance-of-payments investment code 'A07' for direct investment abroad into France, with a narrative reference such as 'Apport en capital souscrit - création de la SARL FR_SARL' whenever the recipient is a French company. Where the subscriber is an EU parent, the transfer may use payout code 'S604' or the equivalent statutory reporting code for a parent-subsidiary contribution. The receiving bank will require three complete supporting records: (i) the signed board/shareholders' resolution approving the capital increase, (ii) a banker's confirmation of the account number and the capital-escrow nature of the account, and (iii) a certificate of beneficial ownership confirming the immediate and ultimate holding chain. No prior French administrative approval is needed; the obligation is a post-event statistical declaration by the bank to Banque de France for any capital operation exceeding EUR 50,000.Outbound clearing rails for a hyper-casual game company follow the nature of the counterparty. For European suppliers and sub-publishers, SEPA Credit Transfers are standard. For non-EEA service providers in digital advertising, cloud analytics, or SDK licensing, settlement runs over SWIFT MT103 with the correct IETF/ISO 20022 purpose code (e.g. 'B108' for data-processing and hosting services, 'R001' for licence fees, 'N400' for advertisement and marketing services). Implemented rails: (1) a payment factory through the tier-1 bank aggregating settlement lines with an API/EBICS channel and reconcilement against invoices; (2) SWIFT MT103 for USD/EUR/GBP high-value disbursements; (3) commercial letters of credit are accessible but, for digital services, typically replaced by a guarantee or an escrow account tied to campaign margin deposits; if the company imports physical merchandise or in-game physical loyalty goods exceeding EUR 50,000, a documentary L/C issued against shipping documents is the preferred low-risk instrument. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% for distributions by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable tax treaties. A qualifying EU parent company may obtain exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary regime, generally requiring qualifying legal form, subject-to-tax status, minimum participation, holding-period, and anti-abuse conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150,000,000 of annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets, or equivalent group-control conditions, generally triggers the French comprehensive transfer-pricing documentation regime, including Local File and Master File requirements. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups meeting the EUR 750,000,000 consolidated-revenue threshold. Below the main threshold, the arm's-length rule still applies and the taxpayer must provide sufficient information on request.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a single standalone economic-substance regulation equivalent to certain offshore ESR regimes. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the arm's-length principle, general anti-abuse doctrine, beneficial-ownership analysis, EU anti-abuse rules, interest limitation rules, controlled-foreign-company rules, withholding-tax relief, and the principal-purpose test in treaties. A French SARL should maintain genuine French management, decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises where commercially appropriate, accounting records, bank functions, and documented functions, assets, and risks. A French company that merely receives advertising-platform proceeds and remits them to a foreign group without performing or evidencing substantive functions presents significant recharacterization and treaty-benefit risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Reporting

Statutory sanction: Administrative tax reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties generally up to 40% for deliberate conduct and 80% for fraudulent maneuvers, and potential criminal tax-fraud penalties of up to five years' imprisonment and EUR 500,000, with higher sanctions in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Managers may face criminal prosecution, civil liability for losses caused by misconduct, management bans, and personal tax liability in cases of fraudulent or repeated obstruction under French tax law. A corporate veil is not pierced automatically. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered by a court in relevant criminal proceedings.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustments under the French arm's-length principle, corresponding secondary tax consequences, interest, penalties, withholding-tax exposure, and possible documentation penalties. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation may result in a penalty generally linked to the reassessed amount and subject to statutory minimums and maximums.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for approving knowingly unsupported arrangements and may face criminal exposure if the structure forms part of fraudulent tax evasion. Personal liability is fact-dependent and is not created solely by an ordinary transfer-pricing adjustment.
Withholding-Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid withholding tax against the French payer, late-payment interest, surcharges, possible denial of treaty relief, and penalties for inaccurate or omitted reporting. The payer may remain liable even where the foreign recipient received the gross amount.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for deliberate or grossly negligent failure to withhold and criminal exposure where withholding-tax fraud is intentional. Personal assessment is not automatic and generally requires statutory conditions or serious misconduct.
Artificial Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or domestic exemptions, application of anti-abuse rules, tax reassessment, withholding-tax recovery, interest, penalties, and possible criminal investigation where false documentation or fraudulent intent is involved.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed to civil claims, management bans, and criminal sanctions for intentional fraud or false declarations. A shell-company finding alone does not automatically create imprisonment or travel restrictions.
Beneficial-Ownership and Corporate Register Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Potential criminal penalties, registry measures, refusal or suspension of corporate formalities, AML escalation, and enhanced scrutiny by financial institutions and payment providers. Penalties depend on the specific omission and applicable French commercial and AML provisions.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and relevant beneficial owner may face personal criminal and civil exposure for knowingly false or incomplete declarations. Management bans may be ordered in serious cases.
Money Laundering and Proceeds Concealment

Statutory sanction: Money laundering may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of EUR 375,000, with aggravated forms carrying higher imprisonment and fines, including confiscation and asset-freezing measures.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and employees who knowingly participate may face personal criminal prosecution, confiscation, management bans, and court-ordered restrictions. Travel bans may be imposed by judicial order but are not automatic.
Accounting and Corporate-Records Offences

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, accounting penalties, rejection of deductions, civil claims, and criminal sanctions for presentation or publication of false accounts or other applicable corporate offences.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur civil liability for losses, criminal liability for knowingly false accounts, and management disqualification. Personal exposure depends on knowledge, participation, and causation.
Improper Distributions and Insolvency Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, director liability for insufficient assets in applicable insolvency proceedings, clawback of suspect-period transactions, fines, management bans, and possible criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Managers may be ordered to contribute to company liabilities for management faults, prohibited from managing companies, and prosecuted for misuse of corporate assets or bankruptcy-related offences. Imprisonment is fact-specific and not automatic.
VAT and Digital-Services Misclassification

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties, joint-and-several collection exposure in specified cases, and possible criminal proceedings for deliberate VAT fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where they deliberately organized or participated in VAT fraud or caused an insolvency through serious misconduct. Ordinary classification errors generally produce company-level tax liability rather than automatic personal liability.
