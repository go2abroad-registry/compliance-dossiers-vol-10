Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital (Article L.223-2); the Articles may lawfully fix capital at €1. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at subscription and 100% of in-kind contributions must be fully transferred before registration (Article L.223-7). Funds must be deposited in a dedicated blocked escrow bank account and certified by an attestation de dépôt des fonds.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification under the Code de la construction et de l'habitation for tertiary workplaces: functional fire extinguishers, evacuation plan, emergency lighting, smoke detection, and fire-resistant furnishing materials; if the premises receive visiting cast or third parties as studios or screening areas, the site must be declared as an ERP of the 5th category to the local Mairie and pass the consultative safety visit. Occupational clearance under the Code du travail: registration of employees with URSSAF, subscription with a Service de Prévention et de Santé au Travail for medical surveillance, and establishment of the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP) covering work on screens, acoustic stress, and filming-site movement risks.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE); official online portals include impots.gouv.fr, the professional tax account (Espace professionnel), and the business formalities portal at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. French customs matters are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI), principally through douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). Upon registration, the entity receives an identification number (SIREN), an establishment number (SIRET), and an activity code (APE/NAF). The company must activate its professional tax account with the DGFiP and register for corporate income tax, payroll taxes where applicable, and VAT where required. The French VAT identification number is generally formatted as FR followed by a two-digit control key and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the appropriate non-resident or business tax service and submits incorporation documents, constitutional documents, proof of business activity, identity and authority documents for representatives, French bank or commercial evidence where requested, and evidence of the French taxable transactions. A non-EU business generally must appoint a French fiscal representative for VAT purposes unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement or statutory exemption applies. An EU-established business generally does not need a French fiscal representative but must complete French VAT registration procedures. For EU cross-border goods transactions, an EORI number is obtained from French customs where required. Digital platform sales through Apple App Store, Google Play, or payment processors do not by themselves eliminate the company's responsibility to determine its French and EU VAT obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS). An eligible SARL may elect temporarily for income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, or IR), generally for up to five fiscal years, subject to statutory conditions concerning ownership, size, age, activity, and shareholder composition; the election is not a general check-the-box election. A qualifying family SARL may elect for IR treatment without the ordinary five-year limitation, subject to applicable conditions. There is no general US-style check-the-box regime in France. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implement the EU ATAD framework and may deny deductions, include income, or restrict relief; they do not create a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France has no single universal VAT registration threshold. The domestic franchise en base de TVA may apply to eligible small businesses below the applicable annual turnover limits. For services and non-commercial activities, the principal threshold is generally EUR 37,500, with a tolerance threshold generally of EUR 41,250; for trading, sales, and accommodation activities, the principal threshold is generally EUR 85,000, with a tolerance threshold generally of EUR 93,500. Thresholds are subject to statutory indexing, legislative amendments, activity classification, and special regimes. Businesses making intra-EU acquisitions, imports, exports, reverse-charge transactions, electronically supplied services, or transactions requiring a VAT invoice may require VAT identification irrespective of domestic franchise thresholds. A business exceeding the relevant threshold or voluntarily opting into VAT must register and comply from the applicable effective date.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

French VAT law generally treats qualifying exports of goods from France to a destination outside the EU as VAT-exempt with a right of deduction, rather than technically as a domestic 0% rate. The supplier must establish that the goods left the EU customs territory and that the transaction satisfies the export conditions. Evidence normally includes the export customs declaration, the electronic exit result or equivalent customs proof, transport documentation, commercial invoices, and a consistent audit trail linking the goods, customer, carrier, customs declaration, and payment. Intra-EU supplies of goods may be exempt where the customer has a valid VAT number in another Member State, the goods are dispatched to that Member State, and the supplier retains transport and VAT-number evidence. Services are not automatically export zero-rated. B2B services are generally located where the business customer is established and may be outside French VAT, often under customer reverse charge. B2C electronically supplied services are generally taxable in the customer's jurisdiction; EU consumer transactions may be reported through OSS, while non-EU consumer supplies require analysis under the destination country's rules. Production, licensing, streaming, advertising, and platform-distribution revenue must be classified separately because place-of-supply and exemption outcomes differ.

  • Export customs declaration or electronic export record containing the Movement Reference Number (MRN), where applicable
  • Electronic customs exit confirmation or other legally acceptable proof that the goods exited the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and contract or order documentation identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and destination
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, courier record, or carrier delivery documentation
  • Packing list and, where relevant, export licences, certificates of origin, or product-specific regulatory documents
  • Supplier's French VAT identification number and, where relevant, EORI number
  • Payment and accounting records linking the export transaction to the customs and transport documents; an inward remittance certificate is not a generally mandatory French export-VAT document but may be relevant as supplementary commercial evidence or under the law of the destination country

France has mandatory electronic invoicing for transactions involving French-established taxable businesses and the public sector under the applicable public-procurement and Chorus Pro framework. The private-sector rollout is scheduled in phases: all businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance obligations generally apply to large and intermediate businesses from 1 September 2026 and to small and medium-sized businesses and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027, subject to legislative and regulatory implementation. Invoices will generally be transmitted through an approved private electronic invoicing platform or the public portal designated by law. France is adopting structured invoice data and separate electronic reporting of relevant transaction and payment data; this is not a universal real-time pre-clearance model comparable to Italy's SDI for every domestic invoice. Digital invoice integrity, authenticity, readability, retention, VAT invoice fields, and audit-trail requirements remain mandatory. App-store statements, payment-processor reports, and platform settlement records should be reconciled with the company's VAT books and supporting transaction data.

France does not generally impose a universal 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline as a condition for export VAT exemption or refund. Export proceeds should nevertheless be traceable through accounting, banking, customs, and contractual records. If the taxpayer cannot substantiate export conditions, the DGFiP may deny the exemption or refund, reassess output VAT, recover improperly deducted input VAT, and charge late-payment interest. Tax penalties may include a 10% late-filing or late-payment surcharge, a 40% penalty for deliberate failure, and an 80% penalty for fraudulent manoeuvres or certain abusive conduct, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards. Customs violations may trigger duty and tax recovery, customs penalties, confiscation, and potentially criminal liability. Deliberate VAT fraud may also lead to criminal prosecution. Records should generally be retained for the applicable statutory audit period, commonly at least six years for VAT documentation, with longer retention where commercial, customs, accounting, or litigation rules require it.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility. France operates a liberalized exchange-control regime compliant with EU Treaty obligations (TFEU Articles 63-65 on free movement of capital) and OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. Both current-account flows (royalties, service fees, profits) and capital-account flows (equity injections, shareholder loans, loan repayments, sale of shares) are freely convertible without prior exchange-control approval. No specific exchange rate regime restrictions apply; the euro trades freely. Profit remittance by a French SARL is subject only to standard corporate formalities (shareholder resolution and distributable-reserve tests under French commercial law) and applicable anti-money-laundering documentation; no exchange control tax or levy is imposed. France applies no spin-off specific restrictions on distribution of dividends to non-resident shareholders; any withholding taxes are governed solely by domestic tax law and the applicable tax treaty (e.g., France-China DTA). Transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 moving across the border (including inbound capital contributions) are reportable to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics; this is a post-transaction statistical filing, not a restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France has no mandatory export proceeds surrender or repatriation obligation for merchandise or services exporters. The SARL may retain export proceeds in foreign currency onshore or offshore at its discretion; no forced conversion into EUR, no statutory timeline for bringing proceeds onshore, and no mandatory conversion ratio exists. The only ancillary condition is the balance-of-payments statistical declaration to Banque de France (via the bank or direct filing) for any single cross-border transaction equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500, and the annual 'liaison financière' direct-investment survey where a foreign shareholder holds 10% or more of the SARL's capital or voting rights. These are informational duties, not surrender or exchange-approval requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with ultimate monetary authority vested in the European Central Bank (ECB) for euro-area policy. Prudential supervision and licensing of credit institutions is delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), which operates under the Banque de France's umbrella. All euro payment infrastructure (TARGET2, Eurosystem) is accessible to the SARL through its French settlement bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for operational use but low sufficiency as the sole or primary account. ACPR-licensed French electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Manager.one, and Shine offer efficient onboarding (fully remote, 24-hour approvals) and are suitable as collection/disbursement workhorses, including multi-currency pockets. Non-French EU EMIs (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Pliant) operate under passporting and can open accounts for a French SARL; their viability is High for treasury operations (low-cost FX, virtual cards) but Medium on compliance confidence because certain EMI accounts are geoblocked for App Store merchant registration and some French tax authorities (and counterparties) prefer an account held with a credit institution rather than with an EMI under the French 'droit au compte' regime. Legal constraint: for the statutory capital deposit attestation required to incorporate or increase the capital of a SARL, only a bank established in France (credit institution), a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations can issue the blocking certificate; an EMI cannot. EMIs must not be relied upon for the capital escrow account. Additionally, the SARL benefits from the French statutory right to a bank account (Code monétaire et financier, Arts. L312-1 et seq.): if two banks refuse onboarding, Banque de France designates a bank; this right is exercised through ACPR, and it materially guarantees the SARL's access to payment services regardless of the EMI debate.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for operational use but low sufficiency as the sole or primary account. ACPR-licensed French electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Manager.one, and Shine offer efficient onboarding (fully remote, 24-hour approvals) and are suitable as collection/disbursement workhorses, including multi-currency pockets. Non-French EU EMIs (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Pliant) operate under passporting and can open accounts for a French SARL; their viability is High for treasury operations (low-cost FX, virtual cards) but Medium on compliance confidence because certain EMI accounts are geoblocked for App Store merchant registration and some French tax authorities (and counterparties) prefer an account held with a credit institution rather than with an EMI under the French 'droit au compte' regime. Legal constraint: for the statutory capital deposit attestation required to incorporate or increase the capital of a SARL, only a bank established in France (credit institution), a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations can issue the blocking certificate; an EMI cannot. EMIs must not be relied upon for the capital escrow account. Additionally, the SARL benefits from the French statutory right to a bank account (Code monétaire et financier, Arts. L312-1 et seq.): if two banks refuse onboarding, Banque de France designates a bank; this right is exercised through ACPR, and it materially guarantees the SARL's access to payment services regardless of the EMI debate.
