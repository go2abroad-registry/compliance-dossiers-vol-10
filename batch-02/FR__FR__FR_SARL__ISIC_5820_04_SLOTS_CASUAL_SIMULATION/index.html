Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum paid-up capital for a French SARL. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and paid up to at least 20% at incorporation, the balance within five years; the paid-up cash must be deposited with a bank or notary in a blocked account/escrow pending RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a valuation report by a statutory auditor (commissaire aux apports) unless exempt.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office must comply with fire-safety requirements of the Code du travail and, if open to the public, the Code de la construction et de l'habitation (ERP). A simple administrative office is generally not subject to a formal civil-defence permit; the employer must provide first-aid equipment, accessible emergency exits, and electrical safety checks.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum paid-up capital for a French SARL. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and paid up to at least 20% at incorporation, the balance within five years; the paid-up cash must be deposited with a bank or notary in a blocked account/escrow pending RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a valuation report by a statutory auditor (commissaire aux apports) unless exempt.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances; digital portal: www.impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Foreign shareholders form the SARL via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); the statutory tax ID (SIREN) is issued by INSEE and the intra-community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) is subsequently assigned by the DGFiP. A non-established foreign entity making taxable supplies in France must submit Form 5595 (demande d'immatriculation à la TVA) to the Service des Impôts des Entreprises de l'Étranger; non-EU entities must appoint a tax representative approved by the DGFiP. Applicants must provide proof of legal existence, ultimate beneficial ownership details, and expected French turnover.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (SARL subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés — IS — at 15% reduced rate on first EUR 42,500 of taxable income and 25% standard rate; no automatic pass-through at member level) — Foreign shareholders form the SARL via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); the statutory tax ID (SIREN) is issued by INSEE and the intra-community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) is subsequently assigned by the DGFiP. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SARL subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés — IS — at 15% reduced rate on first EUR 42,500 of taxable income and 25% standard rate; no automatic pass-through at member level)

Available election forms

France does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime. The principal elections available are: (1) option for income-tax transparency for eligible family SARLs under CGI art. 239 bis AA; (2) election to form a single VAT entity (assujetti unique) under CGI art. 256 C for closely linked French-established persons; (3) ATAD hybrid-mismatch elections or adjustments are mandatory/non-elective and are addressed via the anti-hybrid rules in the French finance acts.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 91,900 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for services (statutory franchise en base under CGI art. 293 B, re-assessed annually, for established small businesses). Non-established foreign persons have no registration threshold and must register for French VAT before making any taxable supply not covered by OSS/IOSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods to destinations outside the EU qualify for 0% French VAT under CGI art. 262-I, provided the goods are physically transported out of France/EU under a customs export declaration and the exporter retains transport and commercial evidence. Ancillary transport, loading and freight services directly linked to the export are also zero-rated. For social-casino/digital gaming services: B2C supplies to customers outside the EU are outside the scope of French VAT (CGI art. 258); B2C supplies to EU customers are generally subject to French VAT unless simplified OSS/IOSS applies; B2B digital services are taxable at the customer's place of establishment and are normally reverse-charged.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU — Déclaration d'Exportation / Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading or transport document evidencing physical departure (B/L, CMR, AWB) with date and route
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer proof showing export proceeds received and repatriated to the French bank
  • Commercial invoice issued to the foreign buyer
  • Packing list and additional documents required by the customs procedure

France is introducing mandatory structured e-invoicing via the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) and accredited Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs). B2B invoices must be transmitted as Factur-X, UBL or CII XML files over certified API pipelines, with B2C transaction data e-reported to the DGFiP. The implementation begins September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises and extends to small businesses/self-employed by September 2027. There is no real-time government issuance or digital stamping; DGFiP performs continuous verification of transactional data, and platform payment data may be accessed via digital audit APIs.

In the absence of valid export proof, the DGFiP reassesses the transaction at the standard 20% VAT rate, with monthly late interest (intérêt de retard of 0.30% per month) and penalties of 40% for deliberate bad faith or 80% for organised fraud (manœuvres frauduleuses). Customs authorities may also seize and confiscate exported goods and impose a fine of up to twice the value of the goods for false export declarations. The standard reassessment window is three years, extended to ten years in cases of tax fraud; claimed input VAT credits associated with the exported goods are subject to retrospective clawback.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. France does not maintain capital controls, prior-exchange licenses, or profit-remittance authorization procedures for ordinary commercial operations. Dividend and profit remittances by a French SARL are lawful without central-bank approval, provided that: (i) a valid shareholders' distribution resolution exists; (ii) the statutory dividend withholding tax and social levies are settled (25% default rate, reduced under income tax treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive); and (iii) the transfer is executed through an authorized credit institution established in the EEA and recorded in the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistics. Capital-account transactions are also unrestricted, except that inbound direct investment in sensitive sectors (defence, cybersecurity, critical infrastructure, gambling-related state monopolies) is screened under Article L151-3 CMF; software publishing and social-casino operations are outside this list and require no prior authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds; the effective conversion ratio is 0%. There is no statutory repatriation deadline: proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts opened with an authorized credit institution in the EEA. For statistical purposes, a resident French company must report cross-border settlements to the Banque de France when an individual transaction equals or exceeds EUR 12,500, and must retain documentary evidence of payment receipt for six years pursuant to Article L123-22 of the Code de commerce. The reserve emergency powers of the Minister of the Economy under Article L151-4 CMF could theoretically reintroduce exchange controls during a financial security crisis, but these powers are dormant and not currently exercised.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks, together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) as co-administrator of France's foreign financial relations regime under Articles L151-1 to L165-1 and R151-1 to R165-1 of the Code monétaire et financier (CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High operational feasibility, medium-to-low suitability as primary capital-escrow custodian. ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions such as Qonto, Shine, Mangopay, and Treezor offer fast remote onboarding, SEPA and SWIFT connectivity, virtual IBANs, and competitive foreign-exchange pricing. However, their EMI licences prohibit credit intermediation and their compliance policies frequently re-flag social-casino/gaming flows under sectoral reputational rules. They are optimally used for payroll, VAT, marketing, and disbursement sub-accounts rather than for shareholder capital escrow or large trade settlements requiring full balance-sheet banking relationships.
