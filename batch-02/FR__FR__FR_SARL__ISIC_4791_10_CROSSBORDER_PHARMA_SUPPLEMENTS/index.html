Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered-office department, operating the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), with mandatory electronic filing via the single portal formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 et seq. (SARL formation and governance); Articles R.123-1 et seq. (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés); Directive (EU) 2017/1132 as transposed by Decree No. 2021-1554 (cross-border registration framework).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts de constitution (Articles of Association) executed as a private or notarial deed, accompanied by the standardized CERFA 13912*07 / M0 incorporation declaration (Demande d'immatriculation) filed through the Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital applies to a French SARL (minimum_capital_requirement: false). Under Article L.223-7 of the Commercial Code, at least 20% of subscribed cash capital must be paid into a blocked escrow deposit account at incorporation, with the balance payable within five (5) years; the deposit must be certified by a bank-issued attestation de dépôt des fonds, released only upon production of the RCS registration certificate (K-bis).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French tax authority; online portal impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities without a French permanent establishment must register for French VAT by filing Form 3550 (or online through the non-resident taxpayer service at impots.gouv.fr) with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); the applicant is assigned an individual tax identification number (SIREN) and a French VAT number (FR followed by a two-digit key and SIREN). A French SARL obtains its SIREN automatically upon incorporation and its VAT number is derived from SIREN; the TIN is the SIREN.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities without a French permanent establishment must register for French VAT by filing Form 3550 (or online through the non-resident taxpayer service at impots.gouv.fr) with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); the applicant is assigned an individual tax identification number (SIREN) and a French VAT number (FR followed by a two-digit key and SIREN). A French SARL obtains its SIREN automatically upon incorporation and its VAT number is derived from SIREN; the TIN is the SIREN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A multi-member SARL is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (IS). An EURL (single-member SARL) may elect to be taxed as a corporation (IS) instead of being transparent in the hands of the individual member; civil companies may elect partnership taxation under Article 1655 bis CGI. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD impose mandatory disallowance, with no elective opt-in or opt-out election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established SARL, the VAT registration threshold under the franchise en base is €91,900 for supplies of goods and €36,300 for service supplies; mandatory VAT registration applies above these thresholds, and no threshold applies to non-established foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically delivered to destinations outside the European Union are eligible for VAT zero-rating under Article 262-1 CGI, provided the exporter retains proof of export. Intra-EU supplies of goods to taxable dealers in other member states may also be zero-rated under Article 262-0 CGI, subject to the customer's valid EU VAT identification number and inclusion in the recapitulative statement (EC Sales List). Services physically and effectively related to exports qualify for zero-rating under Article 259 A CGI. For an online retail business selling dietary supplements, zero-rating applies when title is obtained by a non-EU customer and the goods are transported out of the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU), Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate, Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and transport documentation evidencing physical movement outside the EU

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate exists in France. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing will be phased in from September 2026, requiring electronic invoices (Factur-X or EDI) transmitted through the French Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) using certified dematerialisation platforms. B2C e-commerce and cross-border supplies are subject to OSS/IOSS reporting but not real-time transaction clearance.

Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to VAT reassessment, late interest (intérêts de retard), and the 80% bad-faith penalty under Article 1729 CGI (or 40% for substantial undervaluation), plus potential criminal fines under Articles 1741 and 1742 CGI. In addition, the DGFiP routinely scrutinises export documentation nil-value or missing transport evidence, and a retrospective clawback of improperly zero-rated VAT may extend up to the normal statutory limitation period (six years, or ten years in cases of bad faith under Article L.176 of the Livre des procédures fiscales).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully convertible euro regime with unrestricted capital and current account convertibility. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without FX surrender obligations, subject only to standard corporate dividend withholding tax (12.8% plus social charges) and beneficial ownership declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion ratio applies to euro-denominated or intra-EU transactions. For non-eurozone exports, French SARLs must file a customs declaration (DAU) and may retain or freely convert foreign exchange proceeds. Large transactions above €100,000 require tax authority reporting within 30 days via the Impôts framework.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Central Bank of France) and Direction Générale du Trésor (DGT), Ministry of Economy and Finance
