Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), attached to the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique; taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate registration portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique); the dedicated non-resident corporate filing office is the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), 10 rue du Centre, 93465 Noisy-le-Grand Cedex; customs (export/EORI) authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the SARL via the Guichet Unique portal with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce to obtain the INSEE-issued SIREN (9 digits), SIRET (14 digits) and the APE/NAF code (ISIC 5223 maps to French NAF 52.23Z, services auxiliaires des transports aériens). A classic SARL has no minimum capital requirement; non-resident shareholders are permitted. Step 2: within 15 days of legal incorporation, file the statement of existence and constitutional documents (liasse fiscale) with the territorial SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) to open the corporate tax file and obtain the internal tax account number. Step 3: obtain VAT registration: the established French SARL files Cerfa form 14463*01 with the SIE (or with the SIEE when the company is foreign-controlled) to receive the intra-Community VAT identification number (FR + 11 characters) and, issued by DGDDI, the EORI number (FR + SIREN) used for customs declarations. Step 4: open a French bank account and document the banking domiciliation required under the exchange-control regime. Step 5: file recurring returns: yearly corporate income tax (Cerfa 2065-SD with annexes 2050-2065) and VAT return (Cerfa 3310-CA3) under the real regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope (the French-resident SARL is a capital company taxed as a separate taxpayer on profits of enterprises operated in France under Article 209-I CGI; profits attributable to foreign branches are de facto exempt under the French territoriality principle, with recapture of losses; the SARL is not a pass-through entity and is not situated in a tax-exempt zone).

Available election forms

Available elections: (1) SARL de famille option for partnership-like transparency under Article 239 bis AA CGI, restricted to close-family shareholders, making the SARL transparent for income tax purposes; (2) French fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI, elected by a French parent holding at least 95% of subsidiaries, consolidating group taxable results for periods of five years; (3) option to waive the franchise en base de TVA threshold and enter the normal VAT regime under Article 293 F CGI; (4) option to form a VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 256 C bis CGI for entities with financial, economic and organisational links; (5) OSS/IOSS elections for intra-EU distance sales and digital services through the single French VAT portal, and (6) the Article 137 option already exercised by France which grants the right to deduct input VAT on the Article 262-II-1° aircraft-international exemption. France does not operate a check-the-box system; hybrid mismatch outcomes are corrected unilaterally under the ATAD 2 provisions (Articles 212 bis and 223 quinquies CGI) through double-deduction, deduction/non-inclusion, branch and hybrid-transfer adjustment mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The domestic small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base) under Article 293 B CGI applies to a French-established SARL: for sales of goods, EUR 91,900 basic threshold and EUR 101,000 closing threshold; for supplies of services (including dry leasing of aircraft engines, treated as a supply of services), EUR 36,800 basic threshold and EUR 40,800 closing threshold (Loi de finances 2023 values). The franchise does not shield non-established foreign suppliers, nor does it apply when the taxpayer opts to be taxed. For intra-EU distance sales and telecommunications/broadcasting/e-services, a EUR 10,000 per-calendar-year OSS threshold applies. Because the SARL is French-established, it must register for VAT regardless of turnover whenever it performs taxable activities requiring a VAT number to claim zero rating or input VAT deduction, or once turnover exceeds the franchise thresholds.

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing (facturation électronique) for domestic B2B transactions, introduced by Loi de Finances 2020 (Article 153) and Décret n° 2022-1299 of 7 October 2022. From 1 September 2026 all taxpayers must be able to receive e-invoices and large and mid-size companies must issue them; from 1 September 2027 all companies, including small and micro entities, must issue e-invoices. Invoices must use structured formats (Factur-X, UBL 2.1 or CII, conforming to EN 16931) and be transmitted through a certified Partner Dematerialisation Platform (PDP) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF). France applies a 'Y'-type model: no pre-clearance or fiscal stamping by the tax authority, but continuous e-reporting (transaction and payment data) to DGFiP. Authentication and integrity are assured by qualified electronic signature or seal. Exports outside the EU remain governed by the DGDDI digital customs environment (DELTA-G / DES-D2023 and the DUA), and intra-EU dispatches require Intrastat/DEB filings when value thresholds are met.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full currency convertibility as a Eurozone member. Current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted under Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier). There are no prior administrative authorisations for profit remittance, dividend distributions, or cross-border capital movements, subject to standard banking compliance, tax clearance for large payments under Article L.561-15-1 of the code, and balance-of-payments statistical reporting thresholds administered by Banque de France.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement applies in France or in the Eurozone. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency in French-registered banks indefinitely, and the full amount may be converted freely. For aircraft engine dry-lease companies, lease rental inflows originating from non-resident lessees are treated as ordinary export of services for balance-of-payments purposes; Banque de France requires direct statistical declaration for any transaction exceeding €50,000 or its equivalent when the counterparty is outside the Eurozone.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (as the French National Central Bank within the Eurosystem), operating under the prudential and regulatory authority of the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) and in coordination with the European Central Bank and European Banking Authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is LOW for primary treasury operations and MODERATE only as a supplementary collection channel. A French-registered EMI (Établissement de Paiement under the PSD2 / French Monetary and Financial Code) can legally open payment accounts and execute cross-border collections, but cannot grant credit, hold client funds as a bank, or provide sophisticated trade-finance instruments such as commercial LCs. A non-EEA offshore digital EMI (e.g., Volt, or other third-country wallets) is legally unacceptable for the principal corporate treasury function of an aircraft engine lessor, as it will typically not satisfy aerodynamic engine title security, escrow segregation, or Banque de France statistical reporting requirements. It may be used exclusively for small incidental collections pending migration to bank rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection into the French SARL must be effected by wire transfer (SWIFT). For direct investment from an EEA parent, use the standard correspondent-bank routing through TARGET2. For third-country shareholder investment, an MT103 through a long-established clearing bank is required. The purpose code must align with the French balance-of-payments nomenclature (Banque de France 'enquête IMU-OF / catégorie A39'): classification as 'Investissements directs – Capitaux propres' (equity injection / increasing financial participation). If the injection is a shareholder loan, use code 'Crédits commerciaux et opérations assimilées – Prêts de sociétés non-résidentes' or the standard 'Autres investissements – Prêts'. Supporting verification documents are (i) executive board minutes authorising the capital increase, (ii) notarised deed and publication in a French legal gazette, (iii) anti-money-laundering declaration if injected amount exceeds €10,000, (iv) Bank of France receipt of follow-up statistical declaration of capital relationship (liaison financière) if the net liability to non-residents exceeds €250,000, and (v) bank-to-bank verification of the originating account holder.Clearing rails for trade disbursements under aircraft engine dry leasing: (i) SWIFT MT103 payments for lease return costs, maintenance transfers (labor and non-labor components), insurance premiums, and individual engine disposition payments; (ii) documentary Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) issued under UCP600 for the purchase or re-purchase of engines / spare parts, with documents required as aircraft registry release, certificate-of-airworthiness, and title/encumbrance certification; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfers for cross-border EUR transactions to EEA vendors used for French aviation suppliers; (iv) high-value multi-currency netting to a global liquidity sweeper via EUR/USD contractor; and (v) automated clearing-house (ACH/SEPA B2B) to regular captive vendors. All payments above €50,000 to non-EEA counterparties require contingent report to Banque de France for external-payment statistics; over €1,000,000 or high-risk jurisdiction payments also require documentary evidence of underlying trade contract, a signed invoice, and the engine ESN in the exception notice to the payment gateway.Cash-forward outright exchange forwards on EUR/USD and EUR/GBP to lock-in lease rental conversion rates for a defined engine lease periodEuro-future and non-deliverable-forwards (NDF) on USD, as needed for emerging-market lessees denominating rents in USD but settling in EUR line to the SARL's account, expressly permitted for hedge-only operationsCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) converting floating USD funding into fixed EUR liability when the engine acquisition is financed through Eurobond or bank debtEuropean-style premium-adjustable FX options (EUR call / USD put) for contingent hedging of lease residual valueFX swaps (tom/next and outright settled) to manage liquidity between EUR and foreign-currency transit accounts without speculationCurrency swaps with Banque de France-approved credit institutions for long-dated structural exposures (third-country leases extending beyond two years)

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard corporate income tax rate applicable from 1 January 2022)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% for dividends distributed to non-resident legal entities under Article 119 bis 2 of the French General Tax Code (CGI). For non-resident individual shareholders, the withholding tax rate is 12.8%, with an additional 17.2% social levies in certain cross-border cases.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50 million annual net turnover (excluding VAT) or total balance-sheet assets of EUR 50 million triggers the Local File obligation for the French SARL. Master File is mandatory when the French entity belongs to a group with consolidated annual group turnover exceeding EUR 750 million. Country-by-Country Report filing threshold is EUR 750 million group revenue (or equivalent in EUR).

Economic substance scope: France does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations as a lex specialis; instead, economic substance is governed by general anti-abuse rules and treaty principles. The SARL must demonstrate de facto management and control in France (Article 209 CGI) to maintain French tax residency. For treaty benefits, the Principal Purpose Test denies relief when the arrangement lacks genuine business purpose, and the doctrine of 'abus de droit' (LPF Article L64) permits the tax administration to disregard artificial transactions lacking legitimate substance. Specifically for aircraft engine dry leasing, sufficient physical presence, custodian or maintenance contracts, local staff, and decision-making in France are required to avoid a finding of a shell company or sucursale abroad. The French patent box regime also requires IFRS-defined IP ownership and active R&D in France for the 5% effective rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EUR 1.5 million; aggravated fraud (committed by an organised gang) increases the fine to EUR 3 million and imprisonment from 7 to 10 years. The tax authority adds an evasion penalty of 80% of the evaded tax (Article 1729-1 CGI), plus late payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally imprisoned, subject to travel bans (interdiction de quitter le territoire), and personally liable for civil restitution of unpaid taxes and penalties; a management ban and confiscation of assets are also possible.
Abuse of Law / Artificial Arrangement (Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: If the tax authority successfully invokes LPF Article L64, the transaction is recharacterized, resulting in a 80% penalty applied to the resulting tax adjustment, plus 0.2% monthly late-payment interest; criminal prosecution for fraud may also follow.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held civilly liable for the tax deficiency under the shadow-management doctrine, and criminal penalties include imprisonment up to 3 years and fines of up to EUR 750,000; prohibition from conducting a commercial activity for 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine of EUR 10,000 for non-filing of CbCR (Article 1729 E CGI); for TP documentation failure, a fine of up to 5% of the amount of the reassessed profit after tax adjustment, or up to 5% of the omitted taxable base, plus 0.2% monthly interest. Profits may be reallocated to France from the related party.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil procedures for the adjustment; in cases where documents are falsified or used to hide unrepatriated profits, exposure exists for fraud and personal liability for fines and interest.
Anti-Money Laundering / AML Violations (LCB-FT)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine under the Monetary and Financial Code (CMF) of up to EUR 3 million for entities and EUR 750,000 for individuals; judicial criminal fine for money laundering up to EUR 5 million and imprisonment of 5 years. The ACPR may additionally suspend or withdraw the SARL's authorisation to conduct aviation asset leasing.

Director personal liability: Directors personally face criminal imprisonment up to 5 years, fines, prohibition from employment, and confiscation of assets; civil liability covers damages and restitution of illegal flows. Travel bans may be ordered to prevent cross-border concealment.
