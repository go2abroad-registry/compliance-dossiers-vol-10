Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (commercial court registry) exercising territorial jurisdiction over the registered office, operating the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); filings are made electronically through the national one-stop shop, the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises, managed by INPI.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce): Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL formation, capital and management); Articles L.123-1 et seq. and R.123-1 et seq. (RCS registration); Articles L.210-1 to L.210-3 (legal form, corporate purpose and publicity); implementing regulatory regime: Décret n°67-236 of 23 March 1967, Articles R.223-1 et seq.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (articles of association) accompanied by the electronic enterprise-creation declaration (successor to the former M0/CRE form) filed on the Guichet Unique portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 5 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital for a SARL (Article L.223-2 C. com.); the statutes freely fix the capital, typically EUR 1 to EUR 1,000. Cash contributions must be paid up at subscription to at least 50% of their nominal value, with the balance payable within five years (Article L.223-7 C. com.). The amounts must be deposited in a blocked account with a commercial bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and are released only upon issuance of the K-bis extract. In-kind contributions require either a notarial deed or a statutory auditor's valuation report (commissaire aux apports).

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — official portal www.impots.gouv.fr; legal entity and VAT registration coordinated through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities: (1) appoint a legal representative or establish a French registered branch; (2) file incorporation/registration via the Guichet Unique to obtain SIREN/SIRET numbers; (3) complete form P0 or SIRENE declaration for VAT purposes with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); (4) receive the intra-Community VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) upon evidence of taxable activity or first transaction in France; (5) register for payroll, Corporate Income Tax (IS) file, and e-filing credentials. Non-EU entities must appoint an individual tax representative accredited by DGFiP for VAT compliance unless an EU parent or fixed establishment is present.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities: (1) appoint a legal representative or establish a French registered branch; (2) file incorporation/registration via the Guichet Unique to obtain SIREN/SIRET numbers; (3) complete form P0 or SIRENE declaration for VAT purposes with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); (4) receive the intra-Community VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) upon evidence of taxable activity or first transaction in France; (5) register for payroll, Corporate Income Tax (IS) file, and e-filing credentials. Non-EU entities must appoint an individual tax representative accredited by DGFiP for VAT compliance unless an EU parent or fixed establishment is present.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Default SARL taxation is Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at the standard 25% headline rate. Statutory elections: (a) option for income tax pass-through (IR) under CGI Art. 239 bis A for small SARLs with ≤50 employees and ≤€10 million annual turnover, irrevocable for 5 fiscal years; (b) SARL de famille (family SARL) election enabling IR treatment on jointly-held shares; (c) hybrid mismatch rules under Art. 212 bis CGI disallow deductions for arrangements resulting in double non-taxation or double deduction within the scope of ATAD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Foreign entities: no registration threshold — any taxable supply made in France or intra-Community acquisition exceeding €10,000 triggers obligation to apply French VAT. Domestic micro-businesses may benefit from the franchise en base de TVA exemption: €36,800 (services) and €91,900 (goods) turnover thresholds per calendar year, above which normal VAT registration applies. Distance-selling of goods to French consumers via marketplaces or own channels is now covered by the EU-wide €10,000 OSS threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under CGI Art. 262-I-1° provided the exporter retains definitive proof of removal from EU territory within 90 days. Intra-Community supplies to a customer holding a valid EU VAT ID qualify for 0% under Art. 262 ter if the EC Sales List is filed and transport evidence is kept. B2B services (executive search, headhunting) supplied to a business customer established outside the EU are outside the French VAT territorial scope under Art. 259-1°; B2B services supplied to EU customers outside France follow the reverse-charge mechanism. Services benefiting from effective use and enjoyment in France remain taxable at 20% and cannot be zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Détail / DAU-ENS)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document evidencing removal from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice and client purchase order
  • Proof of payment and bank transfer (Inward Remittance Certificate where applicable)
  • EC Sales List (recapitulative statement) for intra-Union supplies
  • Contract evidencing the offshore establishment and business purpose of the recipient for services

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing for all domestic B2B transactions is phased in from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and taxable persons, extending to all businesses thereafter. Invoices must be transmitted via the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or through certified Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires (PDP) using structured formats Factur-X, UBL, or CII. Real-time e-reporting of cross-border transactions and B2C data is transmitted automatically to DGFiP; fiscal clearance is electronic via monthly VAT returns in e-File (EDI-TVA) with no physical stamp, but missing e-invoicing/e-reporting triggers an administrative fine of €15 per invoice and refusal of input VAT deduction.

France abolishes mandatory FX repatriation deadlines under the Code Monétaire et Financier (Art. L152-2); the 180-day benchmark is applied as internal cash-flow and transfer-pricing defensibility guidance. In tax audits (vérification de comptabilité), unsupported zero-rating claims are recharacterized as domestic taxable supplies, triggering 20% VAT reassessment plus late interest under Art. 1727 CGI. Deliberate documentary omissions carry a 40% bad-faith penalty (Art. 1729-a); fraudulent schemes, falsified export proof, or shell-arrangement abuse of right carry an 80% penalty (Art. 1729-b) and possible criminal prosecution under Art. 1741 for tax fraud, with fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years. Art. 1740 ter additionally provides a 50% majoration for use of a foreign entity to conceal French tax liability.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax. A qualifying small company may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, provided its annual turnover does not exceed EUR 10 million, its share capital is fully paid, and at least 75% of the share capital is held by individuals or qualifying entities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The standard French withholding tax on dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate recipient is generally 25%, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable tax treaties. Dividends paid to a qualifying EU or EEA parent may be exempt under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive if the statutory ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. A French resident corporate parent generally receives dividends under the participation-exemption regime, with a taxable share of expenses and charges, typically 5% of the dividend and potentially 1% for qualifying tax-consolidated or equivalent group situations.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the electronic annual return is generally due by the second business day after 1 May of the following year. For a non-calendar year, the return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The balance of corporate income tax is generally due by the fifteenth day of the fourth month following the year-end; calendar-year companies generally pay the balance by 15 May.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Four installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December. A final balance payment is generally due by the fifteenth day of the fourth month following the financial year-end, subject to specific calendar and exceptional-date rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Corporate income tax follows the financial-year deadlines stated above. Payroll withholding and social declarations generally follow monthly or quarterly deadlines determined by the applicable employer regime. VAT filing frequency depends on the applicable real-tax regime.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150,000,000 turnover or gross assets, tested under the French statutory rules, generally triggers the full transfer-pricing documentation obligation, including Local File and Master File requirements, for the French entity or relevant group. The threshold can also be relevant where the entity is controlled by or controls an entity meeting the statutory conditions. A transfer-pricing declaration may apply at a lower or separately defined threshold, including the EUR 50,000,000 threshold used for specified reporting obligations under the French rules.

Economic substance scope: France has no single general Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the dedicated ESR regimes of certain other jurisdictions. Nevertheless, French tax law requires genuine commercial substance and permits reassessment under the abuse-of-law doctrine, principal-purpose-test concepts, beneficial-ownership rules, permanent-establishment rules, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest-limitation rules, and general anti-abuse provisions. A French SARL should maintain French decision-making, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational capacity, real premises or documented operating arrangements, contemporaneous records, commercially justified contracts, and arm's-length remuneration. A headhunting agency with no genuine French activity, no personnel or operating capacity, circular invoices, or a conduit function may face denial of deductions, withholding-tax reassessment, permanent-establishment recharacterization, and anti-abuse penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, 0.20% monthly late-payment interest, surcharges of up to 80%, and criminal tax-fraud exposure. Ordinary tax fraud may be punished by up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine; aggravated circumstances can increase exposure to up to seven years' imprisonment and a EUR 3,000,000 fine, subject to statutory conditions and judicial determination.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur criminal liability for personal participation, complicity, or intentional concealment. Courts may order confiscation, professional disqualification, civil tax liability, and restitution. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be imposed by judicial order. The SARL does not shield a manager from liability for intentional fraud or personal misconduct.
Shell company, abuse of rights, or artificial profit diversion

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization under the French abuse-of-law or anti-abuse rules, denial of deductions, application of withholding tax, permanent-establishment assessment, recovery of unpaid tax, and penalties that can reach 80% where the statutory abuse or fraudulent conditions are met.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for deliberate mismanagement, contribution to tax liabilities in insolvency proceedings, criminal liability where fraud is proven, management bans, and confiscation. Travel bans or imprisonment require a specific judicial basis and are not automatic consequences of a tax reassessment.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: French tax authorities may adjust taxable profit under the arm's-length principle, deny deductions, reclassify payments, impose withholding tax, and apply documentation-related penalties. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation can result in a penalty generally calculated by reference to the higher of a statutory minimum and a percentage of reassessed profits or controlled transactions, subject to applicable caps and current law.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally exposed for deliberate misrepresentation, obstruction, or fraudulent arrangements. Ordinary documentation deficiencies normally produce corporate tax consequences rather than automatic personal imprisonment, but serious intentional conduct can trigger criminal tax-fraud proceedings.
Anti-money laundering, terrorist-financing, and suspicious payment conduct

Statutory sanction: French anti-money-laundering offences can carry imprisonment and fines, with penalties varying by offence, proceeds, aggravating circumstances, and whether the conduct constitutes laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions evasion, or participation in an organized group. Assets may be frozen or confiscated, and the company may face supervisory, banking, and regulatory consequences.

Director personal liability: Managers and employees may be prosecuted personally for knowing participation, facilitation, or failure to implement required controls where a regulated obligation applies. Imprisonment, fines, confiscation, management bans, and judicial travel restrictions require the relevant offence and judicial process; they are not automatic for an ordinary compliance deficiency.
Undeclared employment, illegal labor supply, or false self-employment

Statutory sanction: Recovery of payroll taxes and social contributions, penalties, exclusion from public benefits, administrative closure measures, and criminal sanctions. Illegal concealed employment can expose an individual to up to three years' imprisonment and a EUR 45,000 fine, with increased penalties in aggravated circumstances; legal-person penalties and ancillary sanctions may also apply.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally prosecuted where they organized or knowingly tolerated concealed employment. Potential consequences include criminal fines, imprisonment, management bans, civil claims for employee entitlements, social-security recovery, and confiscation. A travel ban is possible only through judicial measures.
Bribery, corruption, or improper commercial influence

Statutory sanction: French corruption offences may result in imprisonment, substantial fines, confiscation, exclusion from public procurement, and corporate compliance orders. Exact sanctions depend on active or passive bribery, public or private status, cross-border conduct, and aggravating factors.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees may be personally liable for authorization, solicitation, facilitation, or concealment. The corporate veil does not protect personal perpetrators. Courts may impose disqualification, confiscation, restitution, and judicial travel restrictions in addition to imprisonment and fines.
Failure to withhold or report cross-border payments

Statutory sanction: The French SARL may be assessed for the unpaid withholding tax, interest, late-payment surcharges, filing penalties, and potentially fraud-related penalties where the omission was intentional. Treaty relief may be denied retroactively.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur personal liability for deliberate withholding-tax evasion, fraudulent certification, or intentional failure to remit collected tax. Ordinary administrative error generally remains a corporate liability, but serious misconduct can support civil liability, criminal prosecution, management bans, confiscation, or judicial travel restrictions.
