Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France adopts the euro, which enjoys full convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. No foreign exchange licensing is required for non-resident companies engaging in capital movements, and no prior central bank authorization applies to profit remittances or repatriation of invested capital. Restrictions are limited to national security FDI screening in sensitive sectors and periodic reporting for balance-of-payments statistics. Profit remittance is generally tax-transparent under the parent-subsidiary directive, subject to beneficial ownership proof and anti-tax-avoidance verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion of foreign currency earned by French residents. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts. All cross-border settlements, including export proceeds, must be reported to the Banque de France via the resident intermediary bank for balance-of-payments statistics. Non-EU foreign currency balances are subject to annual customs declarations and may be required for corporate tax audits; however, no timeline for repatriation is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Recovery

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low — French licensed EMI/fintech providers (e.g., Qonto, Shine) generally do not accept blank-check acquisition vehicles or SPAC structures due to their inherent money-laundering risk profile. Escrow funds from public subscriptions must be placed in prudentially regulated credit institutions under the AMF (Autorité des Marchés Financiers) rules; EMIs lack the authority to hold such escrow deposits. Limited cross-border utility exists for low-volume operational expenses, but not for the core treasury and clearing functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from foreign sponsors or IPO subscriptions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with clear purpose codes classified as 'direct investments' or 'capital increase' under the Banque de France’s balance-of-payments nomenclature. Each wire must include the SPAC’s legal name, the purpose reference (e.g., 'Subscriptions in escrow'), and the individual investor’s identifier. The receiving French bank will require a copy of the escrow agreement and a certificate from a notary or accountant confirming the share issue. All movements above EUR 1,000 are automatically captured in the French balance-of-payments reporting system via the intermediary bank.Outbound disbursements for the SPAC’s acquisition of a target company are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for cash payments directly to target shareholders’ accounts. If a commercial letter of credit is used, it would apply only to post-acquisition trade operations, not to the equity acquisition itself. For acquisition disbursements, the bank will require the notarial transfer deed, board resolutions, AMF statement if triggered, and tax clearance certificates (attestation de régularité fiscale) to release funds. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A reduced 15% rate may apply to qualifying small and medium-sized companies on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to statutory shareholding, turnover and capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident, subject to domestic exemptions, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, and an applicable tax treaty. A qualifying EU or EEA corporate parent may generally obtain a 0% rate if minimum ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, legal-form and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. Treaty rates commonly range from 0% to 15%, but the applicable bilateral treaty controls.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the annual return is generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the following year, subject to the officially published filing calendar and any standard electronic filing tolerance. For a financial year ending on another date, filing is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The final corporate income tax balance is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, or by 15 May for a 31 December year-end, subject to applicable electronic-payment rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Withholding tax is generally remitted when the payment is made or made available, with the related declaration and payment due within the statutory period applicable to Form 2777-SD.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million. A French entity generally falls within the full transfer pricing documentation regime where its annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets reach EUR 400 million, where it belongs to a group meeting the threshold, or where specified controlled-transaction conditions apply. A Local File and Master File based on OECD concepts are required for qualifying taxpayers. A simplified transfer pricing declaration and supporting documentation may apply below the full threshold. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to the French reporting entity of an international group meeting the EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations applicable to every holding or financing company. Substance is nevertheless tested through transfer pricing, beneficial ownership, anti-abuse and principal-purpose rules, controlled foreign company provisions, interest limitation, hybrid mismatch rules, management-and-control principles and the French abuse-of-law doctrine. A French SARL acting as a SPAC acquisition vehicle should maintain genuine French governance, documented board decision-making, appropriate personnel or outsourced functions under effective French supervision, French accounting and banking records, commercial rationale, risk control and evidence that financing, acquisition and advisory activities actually occur as reported.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, statutory interest, surcharges generally ranging from 40% to 80%, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that may include imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated cases potentially reaching 7 years and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory aggravating conditions.

Director personal liability: Managers and directors who personally participate in fraudulent conduct may face criminal prosecution, civil liability, tax recovery actions and professional disqualification. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be imposed by a court in appropriate criminal proceedings. Corporate limited liability does not protect intentional personal wrongdoing.
Failure to withhold or remit cross-border withholding tax

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, late-payment interest, late filing and payment surcharges, potential gross-up liability, and increased penalties where the omission is deliberate or fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability where deliberate non-payment, gross negligence or fraudulent organization is established. A director may also face criminal exposure for falsification, concealment or participation in tax fraud.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary tax adjustment, denial of deductions, withholding tax exposure, interest, penalties, deemed-distribution treatment and possible documentation penalties. Inadequate transfer pricing documentation may permit a penalty generally equal to the greater of the statutory minimum and a percentage of the adjusted transactions or tax reassessment, subject to the applicable rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may be pursued for intentional misrepresentation, abuse of corporate assets or fraudulent tax conduct. Mere failure to achieve a preferred transfer pricing outcome does not automatically create personal liability.
Shell company and abuse of law

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or directive relief, recharacterization of payments, corporate income tax reassessment, withholding tax, abuse-of-law surcharge potentially up to 80%, and invalidation of tax-neutral restructuring treatment where statutory conditions are not met.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims, contribution actions in insolvency, management bans and criminal liability if they knowingly implement sham arrangements. The corporate veil is not automatically pierced merely because the company is thinly staffed.
Corporate asset misuse and unlawful profit extraction

Statutory sanction: Repayment of misused assets, cancellation of unlawful distributions, tax reassessment, civil damages and, where applicable, criminal penalties for misuse of company assets or distribution of fictitious dividends.

Director personal liability: SARL managers can be personally liable for management faults and unlawful distributions. Criminal sanctions may include imprisonment and fines under French company-law provisions, with additional bans on management. Travel bans are not automatic.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist financing

Statutory sanction: Regulatory enforcement, administrative fines, supervisory measures, freezing or blocking of funds, criminal money-laundering penalties and confiscation. Serious money laundering may carry imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 375,000, with aggravated cases carrying higher sanctions.

Director personal liability: Managers and compliance officers may face criminal liability for knowing participation, concealment or negligent failure where legally relevant. Courts may order asset confiscation, management bans and restrictions on movement in criminal proceedings.
Sanctions, export-control and restricted-party breach

Statutory sanction: Asset freezes, payment blocking, administrative and criminal fines, imprisonment, confiscation and regulatory restrictions under applicable French and EU sanctions legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors and authorized signatories may be personally prosecuted where they authorized, facilitated or knowingly ignored a prohibited transaction. Corporate authorization does not eliminate personal criminal exposure.
False accounting and financial reporting

Statutory sanction: Restatement, tax reassessment, civil damages, regulatory sanctions and criminal penalties for false accounts or dissemination of misleading information. Consequences may include fines, imprisonment and director disqualification depending on the offense.

Director personal liability: Managers and directors approving knowingly false accounts may be personally liable to the company, creditors and investors and may face criminal prosecution. Directors' and officers' insurance cannot generally cover deliberate fraud.
Unlawful distribution or failure to preserve capital

Statutory sanction: Repayment of distributions, civil liability, corporate-law penalties and potential criminal liability for fictitious dividends or related misconduct.

Director personal liability: SARL managers may be jointly or personally liable for unlawful distributions and resulting creditor loss. Management bans and criminal sanctions may apply in aggravated cases.
Destruction or withholding of tax and accounting records

Statutory sanction: Assessment based on available information, rejection of deductions, adverse evidentiary presumptions, procedural penalties, obstruction penalties and increased tax surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for obstruction, document destruction or fraudulent concealment. Authorities may seek judicial measures and criminal sanctions where intentional obstruction is established.
