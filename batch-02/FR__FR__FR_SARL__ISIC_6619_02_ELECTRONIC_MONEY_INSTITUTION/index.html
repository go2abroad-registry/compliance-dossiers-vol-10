Competent corporate registry

Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), transmitting to the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce in whose district the registered office is located (e.g., Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce de Paris) for final registration on the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book I, Title II, Article L.123-1 et seq. (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés); Book II, Title III, Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL structure and management); Article L.223-7 (deposit of cash share capital); Article L.210-2 (lawful business purpose); complemented by French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier), Book V, Articles L.525-1, L.526-1 to L.526-8 and Article L.526-4 (mandatory EUR 350,000 initial capital for electronic money institutions).

Formation / constitutional filings

Dossier de création d'entreprise (single telemetric file) comprising the Statuts (Articles of Association), the M0 / formalité de création declaration, legal publication certificate, bank's blocked-fund certificate, proof of office address and beneficial-owner declaration; the issued instrument is the Extrait K-bis.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital under Code de commerce Article L.223-2 for a SARL; however, under Code monétaire et financier Article L.526-4, the EMI must have a statutorily paid-up initial capital of at least EUR 350,000 entirely subscribed and deposited in a blocked escrow-style cash account opened with a credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; funds are irrevocably released only after RCS registration (K-bis) and ACPR licence conditions are met.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety compliance certificate for the office under the ERP (Établissement Recevant du Public) regime issued by the Commission de Sécurité before public occupancy; structural load clearance for server racks and vaults delivered by an approved contrôleur technique; electrical conformity attestation (attestation de conformité) for the premises; evacuation plans and fire extinguisher/sprinkler certification; occupational hazard assessment (DUERP) filed with DREETS and registration in the company's social documentation; mandatory annual preventive-medicine visit and workplace conditions clearance under Code du Travail Articles L.4121-1 to L.4121-5.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital under Code de commerce Article L.223-2 for a SARL; however, under Code monétaire et financier Article L.526-4, the EMI must have a statutorily paid-up initial capital of at least EUR 350,000 entirely subscribed and deposited in a blocked escrow-style cash account opened with a credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; funds are irrevocably released only after RCS registration (K-bis) and ACPR licence conditions are met.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); online portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate formalities portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (guichet unique)

E-government filing portal

For the SARL established in France, incorporation via the ONE-INPI guichet unique automatically assigns SIREN/SIRET (INSEE) and the intra-Community VAT ID (FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN), kept in the DGFiP taxpayer account. The legal representative of the newly formed SARL files the first DGFiP registration (liquidation initiale) and must complete the 'Déclaration de création d'entreprise' with a functional bank account; the VAT identification is issued electronically in the EDI dossier. Foreign-owned SARLs need a lawful French address for the registered office, a managing director with a valid domicile, and the publication of a 'statuts' registration in the Journal d'Annonces Légales; the DGFiP will issue the SIRET and the FR TVA intracommunautaire number within about 1 to 4 weeks. For pre-incorporation VAT purposes, the manager may use the provisional registration service of a chartered accountant (expert-comptable) through the 'service des impots des entreprises non établies' (SI NE) at Noisy-le-Grand.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; French-resident SARL is subject to corporate income tax ('Impôt sur les Sociétés', IS) on worldwide profits (CGI Art. 209 I); territorial scope is expanded for resident companies, unlike branch taxation for non-residents. New/existing eligible SMEs may apply the 15% reduced IS rate on the first €42,500 of profits, provided turnover < €10M; financial services of an EMI are French-source and therefore fully within French taxing domain. Tax classification excludes CFC treatment within the EU for a French tax-resident subsidiary? Actually the EMI is a French resident — out of scope.

Available election forms

SARL family option (CGI Art. 239. AA) allows a 'SARL de famille' to elect transparent partnership taxation, but a licensed EMI can only qualify if all shareholders (joint holders) are family members; this election is available at the DGFiP within the first 3 months of the tax year and is a dual risk. Group tax consolidation ('intégration fiscale', CGI Art. 223 A) may be elected by a French parent with 95% voting rights. Hybrid mismatch disallowed: France applies ATAD 1 & 2 (CGI Art. 212 bis) denying deductions for payments that derive from double non-taxation arrangements, including hybrid financial instruments and reverse hybrid mismatches; no check-the-box equivalent exists. For VAT, the 'assujetti unique' group option (CGI Art. 256 C IV) allows the EMI to join a VAT group for simplified filing of exempt financial services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Legal registration is mandatory for any taxable person established in France (CGI Art. 286), so there is no economic threshold; a micro-business 'franchise en base de TVA' (exemption) applies below EUR 91,900 (2025) for goods sales, but this measure is not accessible to financial services carrying the exemption of Art. 261 C. For non-established EU businesses supplying electronic, telecom, broadcasting services, France applies the EU EUR 10,000 annual EU-distance-sale threshold that does not relieve from registration, because France allows the customer-location rule; the EMI's VAT identification is mandatory ab initio due to the exempt financial services it renders.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods physically exported from the EU, the French exporter applies 0% on the invoice (exonération, Art. 262 I CGI), with covert right to deduct input VAT. Services: as an EMI, the issuance of virtual IBANs and digital wallet reserve management are intra-Community and export services covered by the Article 261 C exemption (exempt without recovery) if the customer is outside France; the French place-of-supply rules (Art. 259 A. 259 B) bring B2B services to the customer's establishment and do not produce a zero-rating entitlement. For pure export of digital payment software/data hosting to a non-EU business, the services remain exempt and input VAT is blocked. The 0% scheme for exports is therefore limited to tangible goods; for an EMI without goods, zero-rating is only available for exported software licensing where software qualifies as assimilated goods — not relevant for an electronic money institution.

  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (currency movement record on French exchange balance)
  • Commercial invoice and exporting sales contract

Mandatory national e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions is phased: from 1 Sept 2026 all large and mid-sized companies must issue and receive e-invoices; from 1 Sept 2027 SMEs must issue and 1 Sept 2028 for all remaining companies to receive. Invoices must be transmitted through the state public portal (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF) or a registered 'Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire' (PDP) in structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII), with systematic 'e-reporting' of transaction data to DGFiP. There is no real-time per-invoice fiscal clearance in standard sectors; however, the DGFiP receives transaction datasets via the PDP network at high frequency within 30 business days after the reporting period and carries out machine audits of the FEC (Fichier des écritures comptables) requirement. The EMI is also subject to the separate 3DS/PSD2 reporting and is captured by the DGFiP for CA3/VAT emissions regardless of the exempt status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility. France is a euro-area Member State applying Articles 63–66 TFEU on free movement of capital and Code monétaire et financier (CMF) Book I, Title V on financial relations with foreign states. There is no exchange-control authorization regime, no dual-rate system, no current-account versus capital-account convertibility distinction, and no profit-remittance convertibility hurdle. The only frictions are non-FX: EU autonomous sanctions screening, AML/CFT obligations (CMF Book V), ACPR safeguarding and segregation requirements for e-money float (CMF Article L526-14 et seq.), and inbound foreign-direct-investment authorization for non-EU investors under Décret n°2019-1590/Regulation (EU) 2019/452 where sensitive sectors are touched. Dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are subject solely to French withholding tax rules (12.8% PFU for individuals, 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders, treaty relief otherwise) — a tax matter, not a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. French law imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A French EMI or SARL may freely hold, transfer and convert export proceeds invoiced in EUR or foreign currency. Residual obligations are statistical and bank-mediated: resident credit institutions and payment/e-money institutions must report cross-border direct investment, deposit, loan, securities and money-market flows to Banque de France under CMF Article R152-1 and the ECB balance-of-payments reporting framework (Regulation (EC) No 184/2005), using EU Bop6 classification codes. No FX conversion or repatriation requirement attaches to these reports.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), operating with the Direction Générale du Trésor for foreign-investment screening and with the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) as the prudential and AML/CFT supervisor for e-money institutions; the European Central Bank governs euro-area monetary operations and T2/TARGET settlement finality.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High with strict legal constraints. A French-licensed EMI may lawfully use EEA-regulated fintech and EMI correspondents as technical issuers of virtual IBANs or custodians of segregated safeguarding accounts under Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2) and Directive 2009/110/EC (EMD2), but the French SARL must remain the supervised entity responsible for end-client due diligence and safeguarding. Offshore digital EMIs established in the UK post-Brexit no longer benefit from EU passporting and would require a separate ACPR licence or French agent registration to onboard French clients, making them unviable as primary treasury counterparties. EEA EMIs (e.g., Lithuanian or German licensed e-money/payment institutions) can be used as sub-processors or white-label IBAN platforms only where an ACPR-approved outsourcing agreement and clear contractual liability cascade exist. In practice, the French EMI should hold its master operational and safeguarding accounts with a Tier-1 French or EU credit institution and use foreign digital EMIs solely for ancillary IBAN distribution, not for reserve custody.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium-High with strict legal constraints. A French-licensed EMI may lawfully use EEA-regulated fintech and EMI correspondents as technical issuers of virtual IBANs or custodians of segregated safeguarding accounts under Directive (EU) 2015/2366 (PSD2) and Directive 2009/110/EC (EMD2), but the French SARL must remain the supervised entity responsible for end-client due diligence and safeguarding. Offshore digital EMIs established in the UK post-Brexit no longer benefit from EU passporting and would require a separate ACPR licence or French agent registration to onboard French clients, making them unviable as primary treasury counterparties. EEA EMIs (e.g., Lithuanian or German licensed e-money/payment institutions) can be used as sub-processors or white-label IBAN platforms only where an ACPR-approved outsourcing agreement and clear contractual liability cascade exist. In practice, the French EMI should hold its master operational and safeguarding accounts with a Tier-1 French or EU credit institution and use foreign digital EMIs solely for ancillary IBAN distribution, not for reserve custody.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate for financial years opened on or after 1 January 2023; an additional 3.3% social surcharge applies to large taxpayers with turnover above €7,630,000 and current tax liability exceeding €63,000, yielding an all-in headline peak of 25.825%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory withholding tax on dividend distributions by French-resident companies to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (which grants a 0% rate where the parent company holds at least 10% of the French subsidiary's capital continuously for at least two years).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal years ending on 31 December, the annual return must be filed by the first business day following 1 May of the next year; for off-calendar fiscal years, it must be filed within 60 days after the fiscal year end. Certain filers using EDI receive a one-month extension for the balance sheet annexes.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Instalments are due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December of the current financial year; if a statutory date falls on a non-business day, the due date shifts to the next business day. A balance top-up is filed with the annual return under 2065-SD.
