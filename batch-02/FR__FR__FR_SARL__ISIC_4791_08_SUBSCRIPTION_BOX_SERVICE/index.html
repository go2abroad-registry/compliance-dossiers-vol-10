Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital; however, at least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation, with the balance due within 5 years. Capital may be as low as €1, but must be fully subscribed and deposited in a blocked bank account until registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for commercial premises (Code du travail, Articles R.4216-1 et seq.) – obtain a fire safety certificate from the local fire department (SDIS) for premises open to the public or with high occupancy. Structural load inspections for warehouse areas per building codes. Occupational health and safety clearance from the regional health insurance fund (CARSAT) for workplace compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital; however, at least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation, with the balance due within 5 years. Capital may be as low as €1, but must be fully subscribed and deposited in a blocked bank account until registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no general French exchange controls, restrictions on repatriation of lawful profits, or mandatory use of domestic currency for ordinary commercial payments. Dividend, interest, royalty, and intercompany-payment remittances remain subject to corporate approvals, available distributable profits, withholding-tax analysis, transfer-pricing support, beneficial-owner verification, sanctions screening, and applicable EU and French anti-abuse rules. Cross-border cash movements and certain transactions may trigger statistical, tax, customs, or anti-money-laundering reporting. French resident entities must retain accounting, invoice, contractual, and payment evidence supporting cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or deposited with a French bank. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or received through foreign banks, subject to accounting, tax, customs, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, and payment-services requirements. Exporters must maintain evidence supporting the commercial basis, value, counterparties, and settlement of transactions. Cash and equivalent cross-border movements are subject to applicable EU and French declaration rules, including declaration requirements for physical cash of EUR 10,000 or more when entering or leaving the European Union.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France; foreign-exchange policy is principally governed by the European Union and euro-area framework, including the European Central Bank, with French anti-money-laundering supervision involving the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution and the financial intelligence unit TRACFIN.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for operational collections and payments, but not a complete substitute for a French bank account. A licensed European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro payment accounts, SEPA transfers, card acquiring, and subscription-payment functionality under its licence and passporting or local authorisation framework. It may not provide bank deposits or credit unless separately authorised, and safeguarding is not equivalent to deposit-guarantee protection. Provider risk appetite may restrict newly incorporated entities, non-resident owners, high refund volumes, marketplace-like activity, rapid cross-border flows, or physical-goods delivery risks. Offshore digital EMIs outside the European Economic Area cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to French customers without an applicable EU authorisation, passport, establishment, or other lawful basis. Stripe Billing and comparable processors should be connected to a verified French or EEA business account and supported by clear terms, refund, chargeback, consumer-protection, and transaction-monitoring procedures.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident or multi-layered ownership, nominee arrangements, or inability to identify the ultimate beneficial owner

Root cause: Non-resident or multi-layered ownership, nominee arrangements, or inability to identify the ultimate beneficial owner

Defense evidence: Current Kbis extract or equivalent registration evidence; articles of association; ownership chart to each natural-person beneficial owner; passports or national identity documents; proof of residential address; beneficial-owner declaration; shareholder register; certified powers of attorney; and written explanation of the ownership and control chain.
Unclear source of capital, unusually large initial funding, or capital funded by a third party

Root cause: Unclear source of capital, unusually large initial funding, or capital funded by a third party

Defense evidence: Bank statements; audited or management accounts; tax returns where appropriate; sale agreements, dividend records, loan agreements, or investment agreements; capital contribution resolutions; sender-to-recipient relationship evidence; source-of-wealth narrative; and confirmation that funds originate from an account held by the subscriber or a documented financing party.
Newly incorporated company without demonstrated commercial activity or French operating substance

Root cause: Newly incorporated company without demonstrated commercial activity or French operating substance

Defense evidence: Business plan; product catalogue; supplier and logistics contracts; warehouse or fulfilment arrangements; website and customer terms; projected transaction volumes; marketing evidence; director-management profile; office or registered-address agreement; invoices or purchase orders; tax registrations; and a 12-month cash-flow forecast.
Subscription-commerce exposure involving recurring card payments, refunds, chargebacks, consumer complaints, or delivery failures

Root cause: Subscription-commerce exposure involving recurring card payments, refunds, chargebacks, consumer complaints, or delivery failures

Defense evidence: Payment-flow map; processor agreement; chargeback and refund policy; customer-consent and recurring-payment evidence; delivery and returns procedures; carrier contracts; inventory controls; complaint-management process; fraud-monitoring rules; historic portfolio metrics where available; and reconciliation procedures between orders, settlements, refunds, and bank receipts.
High-risk jurisdictions, sanctioned parties, politically exposed persons, adverse media, or unexplained cross-border payment corridors

Root cause: High-risk jurisdictions, sanctioned parties, politically exposed persons, adverse media, or unexplained cross-border payment corridors

Defense evidence: Counterparty register; sanctions and PEP screening records; customer and supplier due-diligence files; country-risk assessment; transaction rationale; end-use and end-customer evidence; beneficial-owner confirmations; compliance officer approval; and enhanced-monitoring plan. Transactions involving sanctioned persons or prohibited jurisdictions must not proceed.
Mismatch between declared activity and payment flows, including marketplace characteristics, third-party collections, or large cash activity

Root cause: Mismatch between declared activity and payment flows, including marketplace characteristics, third-party collections, or large cash activity

Defense evidence: Detailed business-model memorandum; contractual allocation of merchant-of-record responsibility; payment-flow diagrams; customer and supplier agreements; sales ledgers; tax and VAT treatment; expected monthly transaction profile; explanation of all third-party payments; and formal prohibition or control of cash-intensive activity.
Incomplete corporate records, expired identification documents, inconsistent registered addresses, or unauthorised account operators

Root cause: Incomplete corporate records, expired identification documents, inconsistent registered addresses, or unauthorised account operators

Defense evidence: Recent corporate registry extract; current articles and resolutions; valid identification documents; address evidence; specimen signatures; director appointment evidence; board or shareholder mandate; authorised-user list; and certified translations where documents are not in an accepted language.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route share capital through a regulated bank or payment institution directly from the subscriber or documented financing entity into the SARL capital-deposit account, using a payment reference such as share capital contribution or capital increase and identifying the company, subscriber, amount, currency, and corporate resolution. The bank should receive the formation documents, draft or executed articles, shareholder and beneficial-owner evidence, source-of-funds documentation, and capital subscription records before releasing funds or issuing the deposit certificate. After registration, transfer or release funds to the operational account only under the bank's procedure. For shareholder loans or intercompany funding, use a written loan agreement, arm's-length terms, repayment schedule, transfer-pricing support where relevant, and source-of-funds evidence. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT for non-SEPA or foreign-currency payments. Reconcile incoming funds to the general ledger, preserve bank payment messages, and assess any applicable Banque de France statistical reporting, French tax reporting, foreign-account reporting, and direct-investment reporting obligations.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro suppliers within the SEPA scheme, subject to bank limits and fraud controls. Use SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly documented by an MT103 or its ISO 20022 successor equivalent, for non-SEPA or foreign-currency supplier payments. Commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, open-account transfers, and supplier prepayments may be used according to bank policy, underlying contracts, sanctions controls, customs documentation, and trade-finance eligibility. For subscription-box merchandise, retain purchase orders, commercial invoices, packing lists, transport documents, customs declarations, import VAT and duty records, proof of delivery, supplier due diligence, and three-way reconciliation between purchase order, goods receipt, and invoice. Do not split payments to avoid screening or reporting, and ensure payment references accurately identify the invoice and commercial purpose.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with an authorised bank or eligible regulated investment firm, supported by an identified commercial exposure, documented treasury policy, counterparty limits, maturity controls, and accounting treatment.Non-deliverable forwards may be used for currencies or jurisdictions where delivery is restricted, subject to counterparty authorisation, applicable EU financial-markets rules, suitability and appropriateness requirements, collateral terms, sanctions screening, and a genuine underlying exposure.Currency swaps and cross-currency swaps may be used for documented recurring or longer-term foreign-currency cash flows, subject to ISDA or equivalent documentation, netting and collateral terms, counterparty credit limits, valuation controls, and regulatory reporting.Plain-vanilla foreign-exchange options or collars may be used for documented exposure management, subject to premium approval, risk disclosure, valuation, accounting, and prohibition on speculative dealing outside the approved treasury mandate.Spot foreign-exchange conversions through regulated banks or payment institutions are permissible for operational settlements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File mandatory when group turnover or consolidated assets exceed EUR 400 million; Country-by-Country Reporting at EUR 750 million group revenue; lower thresholds apply when tax administration formally requests

Economic substance scope: France has no offshore-style ESR for onshore SARL; but anti-abuse and GAAR rules (including beneficial ownership doctrine) require real management, staff, and substance for DTA access; shell companies without substance may be recharacterized and lose treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude fiscale

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty per Article 1741 CGI: up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 75,000 fine; civil tax penalties up to 80% plus late interest; seizure and search powers exercised by DGFiP

Director personal liability: Gérant/manager may be personally prosecuted for fraudulent evasion; civil veil-piercing occurs on proof of fictitious or artificial company; travel bans, asset seizure, and disqualification from management.
