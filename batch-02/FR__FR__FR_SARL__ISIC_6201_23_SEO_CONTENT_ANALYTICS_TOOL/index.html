Competent corporate registry

Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS) held by the Greffe du tribunal de commerce; filing is submitted through the Guichet unique operated by Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Code de commerce (Commercial Code), particularly Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL), Article L.223-7 (share capital), Article L.223-18 (managers), and R.123-30 et seq. (RCS registration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Immatriculation au RCS via the official Guichet unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), accompanied by the signed Articles of Association (statuts), capital deposit certificate, legal notice of publication, and manager declarations.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital; one euro is permissible. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and deposited into a blocked French escrow bank account until RCS registration is completed.

Funding & unlock path: For ordinary office premises, comply with the Code du travail health-and-safety rules: maintain a Document unique d'évaluation des risques, verify electrical installation compliance, provide emergency evacuation arrangements, and, if the premises receive the public, obtain the applicable fire-safety clearance from the commission de sécurité (ERP classification).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via impots.gouv.fr, with VAT registration procedure administered by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in France must appoint a fiscal representative and file a simplified VAT registration application under Article 286 ter of the French General Tax Code (CGI) with the competent SIE. The entity must provide authenticated constitutional documents, proof of foreign registration, a power of attorney appointing the fiscal representative, and evidence of taxable transactions in France. Upon approval, the DGFiP issues a French intra-community VAT number (FRxxxxxxxxx) and a SIRET business identifier, where applicable. For direct establishment loss or deemed establishment of a SARL, the entity completes a taxable activity declaration through the CFE to obtain an individual SIREN/SIRET and associated corporate tax dossier.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity not established in France must appoint a fiscal representative and file a simplified VAT registration application under Article 286 ter of the French General Tax Code (CGI) with the competent SIE. The entity must provide authenticated constitutional documents, proof of foreign registration, a power of attorney appointing the fiscal representative, and evidence of taxable transactions in France. Upon approval, the DGFiP issues a French intra-community VAT number (FRxxxxxxxxx) and a SIRET business identifier, where applicable. For direct establishment loss or deemed establishment of a SARL, the entity completes a taxable activity declaration through the CFE to obtain an individual SIREN/SIRET and associated corporate tax dossier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under French law. A SARL may, however, opt under Article 239 bis AA CGI to be treated as taxable in the hands of its partners (pass-through/partnership treatment) for French income tax purposes in narrowly defined circumstances, chiefly when capital is held by individuals, unless exercised within the fist fiscal year. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are implemented through Article 212 bis CGI and relevant administrative doctrine, requiring an election to align tax characterization across jurisdictions; a separate branch exemption election under Article 209 (V) CGI may apply for foreign permanent establishments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For non-established foreign suppliers there is no general VAT registration threshold; registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction in France unless the transaction is covered by the OSS reverse-charge regime. For established businesses enjoying the franchise en base de TVA, the French general threshold is EUR 85,800 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for enumerated services; digital and SaaS services, including SEO and content analytics tools, are excluded from the franchise exemption for foreign suppliers and are always taxable in France when supplied to a French customer, subject to the B2B place-of-supply rule and reverse charge mechanism. Intra-EU distance sales of services to French non-taxable persons trigger a EUR 10,000 pan-European distance sales threshold for the OSS, after which French VAT is due at the 20% standard rate or 10%/2.1% reduced rates as applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods qualify for 0% French VAT under Article 262 CGI when the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the EU by or on behalf of the seller, evidenced by the customs export declaration. For SaaS software and SEO content analytics services, the underlying supply is treated as an electronically supplied service; where the customer is a business established outside the EU, the supply falls outside the scope of French VAT under Article 259 A CGI and no French VAT is chargeable. The supplier must retain evidence of the customer's business status, country of establishment, and usage data to support the territoriality exemption; intra-EU B2B supplies are zero-rated via reverse charge with a valid EU VAT number. Domestic services physically performed in France remain taxable at standard rate unless a specific exemption applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / DEB)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial invoice expressly indicating zero-rated export or intra-EU reverse charge nature
  • Proof of non-EU business customer status and establishment (VAT registration certificate or equivalent corporate evidence)
  • Service contract or technical deliverable logs evidencing offshore delivery of the digital service

France is transitioning to mandatory structured electronic invoicing and e-reporting scheduled for September 2026 for large taxpayers and from September 2027 for SMEs, modeled under the PPF (Portail Public de Facturation). The regime requires issuance of compliant e-invoices in approved formats (Factur-X, UBL 2.1, or CII) and real-time transaction data reporting to the DGFiP. There is no continuous fiscal stamping or clearance engine equivalent to the Italian Sistema di Interscambio, but the PPF will serve as the central exchange and dynamic audit hub; every invoice and transaction report will be visible to the tax administration for automated VAT verification and cross-checking against customs and banking data.

The DGFiP may apply a 40% negligence penalty under Article 1728 CGI, increased to 80% for willful misconduct or organized concealment under Articles 1729 and 1729-0 CGI, together with late-payment interest of 0.2% per month under Article 1727 CGI. Retrospective VAT reassessments may extend to the statutory limitation periods: the standard 3-year period, extended to 10 years for undeclared taxable activity. Where export zero-rating is claimed without adequate documentation or where the exporter fails to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days absent valid cause, the tax authority reverses the 0% rate, reassesses output tax at 20%, imposes penalties on the net amount due, and disqualifies related input VAT credits under Article 271 CGI. Criminal sanctions may additionally apply for tax evasion under Articles 1741 and 1743 CGI, including imprisonment and fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility for the euro within the EU/EEA pursuant to Articles 63-66 TFEU, Directive 88/361/EEC and Articles L151-1 et seq. of the Code Monétaire et Financier. Non-residents may freely hold shares in a French SARL, subscribe capital, receive dividends, settle SaaS licence fees and repatriate liquidation proceeds without any exchange-control approval. The only legal impediments are transactional documentation requirements: proof of the underlying commercial invoice or corporate resolution, and, for dividends, a French distribution attestation and, unless an exemption applies (EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU or a double-tax-treaty rate reduction), payment of the withholding tax under Article 119-2 CGI (generally 25%, reduced to 15% or lower under treaties). No prior authorisation is required for inward foreign direct investment in SEO/MarTech analytics software, which falls outside the sensitive-sector strategic investment regime of Article L151-3 CMF. During sanctions screening, payments to Russia, Belarus or other embargoed jurisdictions will be blocked, and banks may require a declaration of the true beneficial owner before executing any unusually sized inbound or outbound remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio: export receipts for SaaS subscriptions or IT services may be retained in foreign currency in French financial institutions (comptes en devises) and need not be repatriated or converted into euros within any prescribed deadline. The only regulatory duty is statistical: each payment between a French resident and a non-resident must be captured in the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting framework. Where the flow is executed through a French intermediary bank, the bank automatically reports the transaction; where the SARL invoices via a foreign acquiring platform or a non-French EMI and receives funds from abroad, the SARL itself must file via the OECD/OFI portal when the cumulative monthly flow or a single transaction exceeds EUR 10,000. In practical terms, Stripe and Paddle settle euro funds to the SARL's French bank account under a merchant-acquiring arrangement, so the external position is recorded automatically, and there is no conversion timeline because the SARL's functional and statutory currency is the euro.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) — central bank and statutory administrator of the French balance-of-payments statistical regime; treasury and exchange-control policy resides with the Direction Générale du Trésor of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial Sovereignty; prudential licensing and AML supervision are exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for the low-risk SaaS profile, but only for EEA-licensed electronic money institutions holding an ACPR licence or a valid EU/EEA passport, e.g., Qonto, Shine (Société Générale group), Finom, Manaos, Revolut Business and N26 Business. These EMIs onboard a remote French SARL in 24-72 hours using digital identity verification, provide a French or SEPA IBAN, issuing debit cards and direct payout integration along the Stripe/Paddle rails, at a typical monthly cost of EUR 30-100. Legal constraint: an offshore EMI without an EU passport and without a French registered establishment cannot lawfully execute the SARL's treasury operations without breaching the French payment-services monopoly (Articles L521-1 to L525-8 CMF); using an unlicensed non-EEA provider would expose the company to AML/CFT compliance failure and reputational risk, and would not be recognised by French tax authorities for R&D tax credit (CIR) refund workflows or by auditors. Even licensed EMIs lack Tier-1 credit lines, ISDA/EMIR derivative capability and the ability to issue a negative bank confirmation letter for audit purposes; the recommended blueprint is therefore a Franco-EMI hybrid: Qonto or Revolut Business as a rapid-disbursement secondary rail, with BNP Paribas or Société Générale retained as the primary clearing, credit and hedging bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital subscriptions from non-resident founders are executed by SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pain.001) with purpose code CFI 'Capital contribution/Investment' and remittance information referencing the share subscription agreement and apport deed. Euro funds arrive via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant; USD, GBP and CHF funds are converted by the receiving French bank at the interbank rate plus an all-in spread of 0.2-0.5% and immediately credited to the capital depository account. The receiving bank performs EU/OFAC sanctions screening and automatically records the flow for the Banque de France balance-of-payments file. Before the SARL is registered, the entire capital contribution must remain blocked on a dedicated capital-deposit account (or at the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations); the bank issues a certificat de dépôt des fonds required for the immatriculation at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce. Shareholder loans (comptes courants d'associés) are routed with purpose code CLIN 'Intercompany loan' and must be supported by a dated deed stating maturity, arm's-length interest (using the quarterly TME-type reference in force) and subordination; without a formal deed the bank may treat the inflow as a capital contribution, with adverse tax consequences for the shareholder. Cash deposits above EUR 10,000 are subject to mandatory declaration under Article R561-2 CMF and are strongly discouraged for a MarTech SaaS entity.Recurring SaaS settlements are primarily cleared via SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) within the EEA, SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) for sub-10-second payment of invoices and contractor fees, and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA counterparties in USD, GBP and CHF. Stripe and Paddle operate as merchant acquirers and settle net subscription proceeds to the SARL's EUR account on a T+2 to T+7 schedule via the card rails of Visa/Mastercard; the SARL then makes internal treasury disbursements to SEPA business accounts. A commercial letter of credit under UCP 600 (MT700), with confirmation where required, is available for high-value physical transactions such as colocation hardware or data-centre equipment, but is inappropriate for recurring licence fees. Dividend payments and capital redemptions to foreign shareholders use MT103 or SEPA with purpose code DIVI, accompanied by a resolution of the gérant approving the distribution, an état de répartition evidencing available distributable reserves, and the French withholding declaration (Form 2561/2777-D) so that the ordering and beneficiary banks can verify application of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or the relevant tax treaty rate. Payment aggregation inflows should be matched monthly against the SARL's VAT filing and liasse fiscale so that the bank sees a consistent merchant-acquiring narrative.Outright FX forward contracts (spot-forwards with fixed or option dates up to 24 months) under the Convention Cadre FBF (French Banking Federation Master Agreement) for EUR/USD and EUR/GBP revenue generated through US and UK-dominated payment platforms; no mandatory margining applies for short corporate tenors.FX swaps (swaps de change) permitting temporary conversion of USD subscription receipts into EUR for working capital needs while preserving the ability to reconvert at a pre-agreed future date.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) documented under ISDA 2002 with a Credit Support Annex, used where the SARL has assumed a USD or GBP-denominated term loan or a financed data-centre contract; subject to EMIR collateral posting.Vanilla FX option strategies (EUR call/USD put and EUR put/USD call) acquired from a licensed French bank or EU investment firm to hedge the SaaS forward revenue pipeline, with premia expensed in the commercial accounts.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted strictly for invoicing in soft-currency markets (e.g., INR, CNY, TRY) where an onshore forward market is inaccessible, provided the hedge is backed by a genuine commercial subscription contract and executed with a French licensed bank under EMIR trade reporting.FX spot contracts for daily treasury netting at market rate without derivative documentation; executed on the bank's electronic platform with agreed dealing limits.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for the low-risk SaaS profile, but only for EEA-licensed electronic money institutions holding an ACPR licence or a valid EU/EEA passport, e.g., Qonto, Shine (Société Générale group), Finom, Manaos, Revolut Business and N26 Business. These EMIs onboard a remote French SARL in 24-72 hours using digital identity verification, provide a French or SEPA IBAN, issuing debit cards and direct payout integration along the Stripe/Paddle rails, at a typical monthly cost of EUR 30-100. Legal constraint: an offshore EMI without an EU passport and without a French registered establishment cannot lawfully execute the SARL's treasury operations without breaching the French payment-services monopoly (Articles L521-1 to L525-8 CMF); using an unlicensed non-EEA provider would expose the company to AML/CFT compliance failure and reputational risk, and would not be recognised by French tax authorities for R&D tax credit (CIR) refund workflows or by auditors. Even licensed EMIs lack Tier-1 credit lines, ISDA/EMIR derivative capability and the ability to issue a negative bank confirmation letter for audit purposes; the recommended blueprint is therefore a Franco-EMI hybrid: Qonto or Revolut Business as a rapid-disbursement secondary rail, with BNP Paribas or Société Générale retained as the primary clearing, credit and hedging bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory for a French entity with gross revenue above €50 million; Master File: mandatory for groups with consolidated turnover exceeding €750 million. Below these thresholds, simultaneous documentation (form n° 2257) must still be produced upon request where intercompany transactions exceed €400,000 in royalties/charges.

Economic substance scope: France enforces substance via the 'abus de droit' (art. L64 of the Book of Tax Procedures), the beneficial-ownership clauses of DTAs, and the permanent establishment definition in Article 209. A SARL claiming treaty protection must show commercial activity, own premises, qualified staff, and independent decision-making. The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of offshore jurisdictions are not applicable, but their substance criteria are analogous to the OECD BEPS approach.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude Fiscale

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI, fine of up to €500,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; aggravated fraud (use of fictitious accounts, non-cooperative jurisdiction) carries a fine of up to €2,000,000 and 7 years imprisonment. The corporate entity is also liable to pay penalties equal to 40% or 80% of the tax evaded; the tax authority may order the immediate posting of a surety bond.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly orchestrate evasion are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax debts after a finding of faute détachable (unseverable fault). Personal assets may be seized, travel bans and exit prohibitions can be imposed, and extradition may be sought.
Transfer Pricing Violation / Lack of Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 57 CGI and Article L13 B LPF, the tax authority may recharacterize profits and add back the difference, plus a 5% documentation penalty on the reassessed amount (minimum €25,000). For deliberate price-shifting with a sham arrangement, the penalty rises to 80%.

Director personal liability: If the offense is committed for the fraudulent benefit of a related foreign entity, fiduciary liability applies; the director may be disqualified from managing French companies for up to 10 years and personally assessed for unpaid tax.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Application of Article L64 du LPF (abus de droit): the entity is disregarded, the tax interplay is recharacterised, and an 80% penalty is applied to the additional tax. Migrant scores are confiscated; distribution to the foreign shareholder may become subject to a 30% withholding with no treaty protection.

Director personal liability: Directors may be sued by the corporate creditor (including the tax authority) for artificially stripping assets; directors may face a judicial order to replenish capital or personal payout of outstanding tax, plus a ban on holding management posts.
Money Laundering / AML Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Under the French Monetary and Financial Code (art. 324-1 et seq.), laundering is punishable by 5 years imprisonment and a €350,000 fine (aggravated by 10 years and €750,000 in noncooperative jurisdictions). The authorities may confiscate the company's assets and freeze payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable if they commit the offence or allow it through manifestly negligent oversight. Directorial travel bans and bank account restrictions may be imposed; in serious cases, citizens may be extradited.
