Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy and Finance; professional portal: impots.gouv.fr; company registration via Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; VAT/TIN allocation by DGFiP (Service des Impôts des Entreprises, SIE) with cross-border non-resident desk at SIEE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères, Noisy-le-Grand).

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the SARL defaults to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) on worldwide profits under Article 206-1 CGI regardless of non-resident ownership: standard 25% rate; reduced 15% on the first €42,500 of profit for SMEs with turnover under €10m and ≥75% individual ownership. France is a unitary national system with no sub-federal income tax; the remittance activity is resident in France by place of effective management (Article 209 CGI). Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable to mainland France; overseas DOM/TOM special zones are out of scope. Pass-through treatment is not automatic and requires the 5-year statutory election described below.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box; the relevant French statutory elections are: (1) Article 239 bis AA CGI – small SARLs (≤5 individual partners, qualifying industrial/commercial/artisanal/liberal activities) may elect pass-through taxation (régime des sociétés de personnes) for up to 5 years; revocation triggers immediate IS entry with reserve recapture. (2) Article 260 C CGI – option to tax (option pour l'imposition à la TVA) the otherwise VAT-exempt financial transactions, permitting recovery of input VAT on remittance operational costs where the agency is in a net input-VAT position. (3) Article 239 quater CGI – defunct 5-year start-up option for IR on newly formed SARLs. (4) No hybrid-mismatch election exists under French CFC rules; the EU ATAD/Pillar Two (15% minimum top-up under Directive EU/2022/2523) binds only groups with consolidated revenue exceeding €750m, which is inapplicable to this micro-remittance SARL. (5) If the SARL is held at ≥95% by a French PE, the parent may elect tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA thresholds (Article 293 B CGI, adjusted by Loi de finances 2025): €110,000 for goods and €45,000 for services. A remittance agency performing exempt financial transactions must nevertheless be registered for VAT and file returns from its first euro of fees because the exemption under Article 261 C is a substantive exemption, not the small-business franchise; the entity is therefore subject to compulsory monthly CA3 filing of the régime réel normal irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The remittance service is not a zero-rated goods export; French VAT treatment is achieved by territoriality and exemption: (i) B2B fees invoiced to foreign established counterparties are outside the scope of French VAT under the place-of-supply rule of Article 259-1 CGI (service deemed performed at the recipient's establishment), resulting in 0% French VAT where the counterparty is outside France; (ii) B2C cross-border money-transfer fees are VAT-exempt (not zero-rated) under Article 261 C 1° a) CGI; (iii) any incidental physical export of tangible goods (e.g., POS hardware, cash logistics equipment to a non-EU branch) qualifies for 0% VAT under Article 262-I CGI provided the goods leave the EU within 90 days of dispatch when the exporter does not arrange transport; proof documents must be retained for 6 years.

  • DAU – Document Administratif Unique electronic export declaration (DELTA-X / PVI system) where goods are physically exported
  • Bill of Lading (connaissement) or Airway Bill (lettre de transport aérien) evidencing outbound EU movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation de rapatriement de devises) – bank confirmation that export proceeds were credited to the SARL's French bank account
  • Kbis extract and foreign customer's TIN/VAT registration certificate to support B2B place-of-supply status
  • Executed remittance mandate or correspondent agreement with foreign banks/agents plus SWIFT MT103/MT202 messages evidencing the cross-border settlement flow

Continuous transaction control (CTC) without real-time fiscal clearance: (1) From 1 September 2026, all French VAT-registered businesses must be able to receive B2B structured e-invoices; large and mid-sized enterprises (GE/ETI) must issue e-invoices through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP). (2) From 1 September 2027, small and micro enterprises (including this SARL) fall under the issuance obligation. (3) Mandatory formats: Factur-X (hybrid PDF/XML), UBL 2.1 and CII (EN 16931). (4) E-reporting: B2B transaction data, B2C sales data and payment status data must be transmitted to DGFiP electronically via PPF/PDP within two working days of the invoice or payment event; the French CTC is post-transaction with an extensive electronic audit trail, not a pre-clearance digital stamping API. (5) VAT-exempt remittance fee invoices must still be issued electronically and mention the exemption reference 'TVA non applicable – Article 261 C du CGI'.

DGFiP and DGDDI joint audit controls apply; the statutory reprise period (délai de reprise) is 3 years for VAT and IS (Article L169 LPF), extended to 6 years on concealed activity or fraudulent manoeuvres (Article L176 LPF). Reassessed VAT and export-connected clawbacks carry 0.20% monthly late interest (Article 1727 CGI); bad-faith inaccuracies attract a 40% penalty, 80% for abusive/artificial arrangements or active concealment (Article 1729 CGI); e-invoicing and e-reporting failure is fined €15 per invoice and €250 per transaction (Article 1737 CGI). Criminal liability: tax fraud (fraude fiscale) carries a €500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment, increased to €3m and 7 years for organised-gang fraud (Article 1741 CGI). Retrospective clawback of input VAT (reverse-charge at 20%) is imposed if the exemption or zero-rating claim is overturned; the SARL's manager and any signatory are jointly liable for the deficiency, interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in France across both current and capital accounts. France maintains no exchange-control restrictions on current account transactions, foreign direct investment, or profit/dividend remittances. Capital movements are unrestricted within the EU and with most third countries, subject only to statistical balance-of-payments declarations to Banque de France. Profit remittance by a SARL to non-resident shareholders is lawful through an ordinary dividend distribution approved by shareholders, with the principal compliance hurdles being documentary (tax clearance status, annual-accounts approval in the shareholders' meeting, and dividend payment protocols) and treaty-based withholding tax liabilities rather than regulatory exchange controls. All settlements are freely denominated in euros or any counterpart currency at market-determined rates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio for any category of exports, including remittance service revenues and trade-related receivables. Because the euro is the domestic legal tender and freely floatable, exporting entities may retain and manage foreign-currency proceeds offshore without conversion obligation. However, cross-border cash or bearer instruments in excess of EUR 10,000 must be declared at French customs, and all resident payment institutions must submit balance-of-payments reporting filings to Banque de France through the French Direct Investment and International Transactions Reporting System, including monthly corridor volume and counterpart country data.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French central bank), in conjunction with the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) for licensing and prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment service providers, the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for balance-of-payments reporting, and the European Central Bank (ECB) for common euro-area monetary policy. France applies fully the EU freedom of capital movements enshrined in Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. EU-domiciled Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) with a valid passport into France through the ACPR are operationally viable as segregated clearing counterparts for the SARL's remittance float, provided they maintain an ACPR-licensed e-money account and comply with safeguarding obligations under the PSD2. Offshore non-EU digital EMIs without a French or EU branch are rated Low: the ACPR and Banque de France require an EU-registered legal entity, a physical registered office, a local compliance officer, and internal audit arrangements in France; purely offshore EMIs cannot lawfully operate in the French remittance market.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscriptions into the SARL capital escrow account must originate from the ultimate investor's bank account and be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 with the EU/Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose code S302 (Direct Investment – Equity Capital). The receiving French bank requires the following verification bundle: apostilled and legalised subscription deed, updated Kbis extract, beneficial-owner declaration, board resolution approving the capital increase/call, source-of-funds certificate from the remitting bank, and the SARL's tax identification number imprinted in the wire narration. Funds must be credited to the segregated Capital Escrow Account, quarantined until the notary or registered agent confirms legal receipt and the Banque de France external direct investment declaration is filed where applicable.Outbound remittance disbursements clear through three rails: (A) intra-Eurozone beneficiary payments use SEPA Instant Credit Transfer or SEPA Core clearing at T+0/T+1; (B) non-euro international payments use SWIFT MT103 correspondent-bank settlement with strict purpose codes and dual-sided reconciliation; (C) high-volume African and South-East Asian cash-pickup corridors use licensed local payment aggregator sub-wallets and mobile-money API clearing pools (e.g. Orange Money, Wave, MTN MoMo, Wari) linked to the SARL's French operational account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2022

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax on dividends paid by a French SARL to a non-resident corporate shareholder; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when a 10% or greater direct participation is held for at least two years; individual non-residents bear a 30% single flat rate (12.8% income tax + 17.2% social levies) with possible treaty reduction

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal year ending 31 December, the return is due by the second working day after 1 May. For a non-calendar fiscal year-end, the return is due within 60 days following the close of the financial year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000: mandatory Local File/Master File is triggered when the French group or entity reaches annual tax-exclusive turnover exceeding EUR 400 million or holds controlled foreign entities meeting that asset threshold; an administrative guideline also expects simplified TP documentation from entities with annual turnover between EUR 50 million and EUR 400 million, especially in high-risk remittance flows; documentation may also be requested by the tax authority in audit even below threshold

Economic substance scope: France does not administer a separate Economic Substances Regulations (ESR) code like common-law financial centres; however, doctrine and anti-abuse instruments impose substantive French activity demonstration for tax residence, permanent establishment creation, treaty entitlement (MLI Principal Purpose Test), and the 'abus de droit' doctrine. A remittance agency SARL must retain evidence of board meetings in France, employees, premises, settlement and clearing decisions, and arm's-length pricing of intra-group SWIFT/clearing fees

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Fraud (Fraude Fiscale) / False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI: 5 years' imprisonment and EUR 75,000 fine; where organized or aggravated circumstances apply, up to 7 years and EUR 100,000 fine. Additional 40% penalty for bad faith or 80% for abuse of law under Articles 1729 to 1730 CGI, plus late-payment interest

Director personal liability: Directors who personally participated in or profited from fiscal evasion can be prosecuted for fraud; the court may impose a temporary or permanent ban from directing a commercial company, restriction to travel abroad (interdiction de sortie du territoire), confiscation of assets, and they can be held jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax debts under Article L267 LPF where they committed irregularity
Shell / Artificial Arrangement (Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: The French tax administration may invoke abuse of law under Article L64 LPF; the company is liable for a re-determined tax increase, plus interest and penalties of 40% or 80% according to dominant intention or deliberate manoeuvring

Director personal liability: Directors liable in civil and criminal turf when the arrangement was designed, signed and executed for personal tax/AML evasion; court may order restitution, proportional financial sanction, and disqualification from managing a French company
Unauthorized Payment Institution / AML Infringement

Statutory sanction: Unlawful provision of payment services is a delict under Article L521-2 CMF: imprisonment up to 3 years and fine up to EUR 375,000; may also trigger withdrawal of the SARL's registered commercial status in severe cases

Director personal liability: The managing partner (gérant) and effective managers are personally exposed to criminal sanctions for wilful supervision failure, civil liability for victims' funds, and no professional travel or account transfer allowances during the proceeding; court can impose a ban on managing a French company for up to 10 years
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Adjustment by French tax administration under Article 57 CGI; recapture of artificial profit into the French tax base, 40% penalty for deliberate misconduct or 80% for abuse; separate fixed fine of EUR 10,000 for failure to produce TP documentation when requested under Article L13 AA LPF

Director personal liability: Directors may be held financially liable for added corporate tax and penalties when they signed related-party agreements without transfer pricing documentation, or knowingly set non-arm's-length rates stripping French profits; civil action may extend to seizure of personal assets
DTA Misuse and False Residence Certificate

Statutory sanction: Nullity of the tax relief claim; retroactive withholding levy at 25% or 30% with interest and sanctions; criminal indictment under Article 1741 CGI and Article 433-19 Code pénal for use of a false official document

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is automatic for shell liability; court can order the managing partner to repay all mis-statutory distributions plus 80% penalty; travel ban applied when French treasury liability exceeds EUR 10,000
