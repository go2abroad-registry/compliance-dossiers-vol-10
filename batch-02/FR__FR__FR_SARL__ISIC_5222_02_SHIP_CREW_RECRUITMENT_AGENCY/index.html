Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for a French SARL; the articles may fix the capital from EUR 1 upwards. All cash contributions must be paid up in full at incorporation and blocked on a dedicated escrow or deposit account (compte bloqué) with a credit institution, Caisse des Dépôts or a notary; funds are released to the company only upon RCS registration (Code de commerce, Article L.223-7).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: stamp issued by the Commission de sécurité or a fire safety attestation for tertiary premises accessible by employees or the public; structure: attestation of building regulatory compliance where the office undergoes fit-out or change of use; occupational health: validated DUERP (Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels), registration with workplace health services, and documented training for crew travel safety and emergency procedures

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for a French SARL; the articles may fix the capital from EUR 1 upwards. All cash contributions must be paid up in full at incorporation and blocked on a dedicated escrow or deposit account (compte bloqué) with a credit institution, Caisse des Dépôts or a notary; funds are released to the company only upon RCS registration (Code de commerce, Article L.223-7).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – impots.gouv.fr; corporate entity reporting via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must file a registration notice with the DGFiP's Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for the entity's registered office or permanent establishment in France. The application requires corporate constitutional documents, proof of the foreign parent company's existence, a French address for the establishment or a designated fiscal representative for non-residents, and a description of the planned French business activities. Upon approval, the SIE issues a French tax identification number (numéro d'identification), which is distinct from the SIREN/SIRET numbers, and, where applicable, the company is also registered for VAT and homologated for the French VAT identification number (TVA intracommunautaire) via the EU VAT Information Exchange System.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must file a registration notice with the DGFiP's Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for the entity's registered office or permanent establishment in France. The application requires corporate constitutional documents, proof of the foreign parent company's existence, a French address for the establishment or a designated fiscal representative for non-residents, and a description of the planned French business activities. Upon approval, the SIE issues a French tax identification number (numéro d'identification), which is distinct from the SIREN/SIRET numbers, and, where applicable, the company is also registered for VAT and homologated for the French VAT identification number (TVA intracommunautaire) via the EU VAT Information Exchange System.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

SARL is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by default; it may elect for fiscal transparency as a société de personnes under Article 239 bis AA of the French General Tax Code (CGI) for eligible small/medium enterprises meeting shareholder and turnover criteria. Group taxation (intégration fiscale) is available under Articles 223 A et seq. CGI for 95%-owned French subsidiaries; VAT grouping election under Article 267 bis CGI is available for French-established entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-established suppliers of B2B services; VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply in France. For domestic micro-businesses, the franchise en base threshold is EUR 91,900 (goods) / EUR 36,800 (services), but this does not apply to foreign entities supplying ship crew recruitment services. For distance selling of goods by EU suppliers, the French threshold is EUR 35,000, and EUR 10,000 for cross-border B2C electronic services; ship crew recruitment is a B2B service subject to reverse charge unless the customer is a French-established shipowner, in which case the foreign supplier must register and charge French VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports of goods outside the EU applies under Article 262 CGI, requiring evidence that goods left French and EU territory within mandated time limits. For services, including crew recruitment, zero-rating may be claimed where the place of supply is outside France or where the service is directly linked to transportation of goods/persons or related to tangible movable property that is genuinely exported outside the EU by the supplier's customer. For ship crew recruitment to a non-EU shipowner or for services supplied to vessels engaged in international navigation, the supply can be zero-rated if the service recipient is established outside France and receives the benefit for the vessel's commercial use outside the EU. Documentary proof of the customer's non-EU establishment and the vessel's operating routes is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/EXS) transmitted via DELTA-X
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), air waybill, CMR, or other transport document evidencing shipment outside France/EU
  • Commercial invoice, signed crew placement contract, inward remittance certificate or bank transfer proof of payment

France mandates structured electronic invoicing for all VAT-registered businesses in phases commencing 1 September 2026 (and from 1 September 2027 for large enterprises). Invoices must be issued in a structured XML or EDI format, transmitted through a registered Dematerialisation Partner Platform (PDP), and reported to the DGFiP. Real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping are not currently in force; however, mandatory e-reporting of transaction data (e-reporting) to the tax authority will be implemented, and pre-existing anti-fraud data transmission requirements apply to certain high-risk supplies.

Fraudulent or unjustified zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to immediate VAT reassessment, denial of the deduction/refund, plus a 40% penalty for deliberate misstatement, 80% penalty for abuse of rights or failure to file returns, and 100% penalty for manoeuvres frauduleuses, together with late interest at 0.20% per month on unpaid VAT. Criminal sanctions may reach up to EUR 500,000 fines and 5 years' imprisonment for tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI. The tax authority may withdraw VAT registration retroactively and claw back all zero-rated supplies with a mandatory minimum penalty regime.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a regime of full current account convertibility for trade, services, and profit remittances. Capital account transactions require prior declaration to the Banque de France for amounts exceeding €100,000 per year. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted following corporate tax clearance and beneficial ownership verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies under current French FX regulations. All foreign exchange transactions must be reported to the Banque de France via the Système Economique de Déclaration des Opérations de Change (SEDOC) if exceeding reporting thresholds. Export receipts from maritime services require prompt repatriation supported by commercial invoices and shipping documents for tax and AML compliance.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

EMI cross-border operations in France are feasible under ACPR oversight but subject to strict passporting requirements and capital adequacy rules. Offshore digital EMIs face legal constraints due to the need for local licensing or equivalent compliance for payment services.
