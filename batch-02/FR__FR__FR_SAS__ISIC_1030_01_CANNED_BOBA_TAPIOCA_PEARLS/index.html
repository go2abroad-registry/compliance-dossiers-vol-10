Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent commercial registry is the Register of Commerce and Companies maintained by the competent commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce), with filings submitted through the national Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 to R.227-2 concerning the société par actions simplifiée; Articles L.123-1 et seq. concerning the Register of Commerce and Companies; Articles L.123-33 et seq. concerning mandatory electronic business formalities.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of incorporation of a legal entity submitted through the Guichet unique, supported by the executed SAS articles of association, beneficial-owner declaration, and required evidence of registered office and capital deposit.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 35 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; EUR 1 is legally sufficient, subject to adequate capitalization. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation and the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations, which issues the capital-deposit certificate. The stated USD amount is an indicative conversion of the standard commercial-registry filing charge and excludes the variable legal-notice publication cost, beneficial-owner filing charge, translations, notarization, and professional fees.

Funding & unlock path: Before operation, obtain the required fire-safety review or approval from the competent fire and rescue service (Service départemental d'incendie et de secours, SDIS) where applicable, particularly for premises open to the public or subject to building and fire-safety controls. Complete electrical, machinery, pressure-equipment, ventilation, emergency-exit, and structural-load inspections required by the building permit, insurance, ICPE conditions, or occupational-safety rules. Comply with the French Labour Code through risk assessment, a documented DUERP, machine guarding, chemical-risk controls, occupational medical arrangements, training, emergency procedures, and workplace hygiene controls.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; EUR 1 is legally sufficient, subject to adequate capitalization. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation and the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations, which issues the capital-deposit certificate. The stated USD amount is an indicative conversion of the standard commercial-registry filing charge and excludes the variable legal-notice publication cost, beneficial-owner filing charge, translations, notarization, and professional fees.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), acting through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); business registration filings are made via the INPI Guichet unique (https://procedures.inpi.fr) and the tax portal is https://www.impots.gouv.fr. The customs administration (Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects - DGDDI) separately processes export clearance through DELTA.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident entity with no French permanent establishment: appoint an accredited tax representative in France under Article 289 A CGI, then file the application for a French VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) with the competent SIE; no SIREN is needed if the non-resident has no French establishment. For a French fixed establishment or a French SAS: register the entity with the INPI Guichet unique to obtain SIREN/SIRET and simultaneous INSEE codes, then the SIE allocates the French VAT number; the registration requires notarized corporate documents, identity proof for management, a power of attorney, and evidence of the appointed tax representative where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a non-resident entity with no French permanent establishment: appoint an accredited tax representative in France under Article 289 A CGI, then file the application for a French VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) with the competent SIE; no SIREN is needed if the non-resident has no French establishment. For a French fixed establishment or a French SAS: register the entity with the INPI Guichet unique to obtain SIREN/SIRET and simultaneous INSEE codes, then the SIE allocates the French VAT number; the registration requires notarized corporate documents, identity proof for management, a power of attorney, and evidence of the appointed tax representative where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

SAS status is mandatorily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at entity level; no check-the-box or general pass-through election exists. Available statutory elections include: group fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for French companies under common 95% ownership; VAT group treatment for closely-linked persons under Article 1693 ter CGI; the option for tax consolidation of German/Italian subsidiaries via a French parent (Glaner/Jay-Pension); and the application of hybrid mismatch allocation rules under Articles 209, 212 bis, 212 bis A CGI with no elective hybrid default.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for non-established foreign taxable persons: a foreign entity making its first taxable supply of goods or services in France must register for VAT before or at the time of that supply. The domestic small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA) of EUR 85,000 for sales of goods and EUR 37,500 for services generally does not apply to non-EU-established businesses. An intra-Community distance-selling threshold of EUR 35,000 is only relevant for EU-established suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies under Article 262(I)(1) CGI to supplies of tangible goods (including aseptic canned tapioca pearls) that are physically exported outside the EU by the supplier or by a third party on the supplier's behalf; the supplier must demonstrate that the goods were removed from French territory within the statutory time limits and that the specific goods were exported unaltered. For processing or offshore services connected to goods, 0% may apply when the customer belongs (établissement) outside the EU and the services directly relate to exported/imported goods; compliance with Article 259-D CGI (services related to goods) must be evidenced.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU) with customs office exit confirmation via DELTA
  • Bill of Lading or other transport document (CMR, airway bill) evidencing physical exit from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice issued at 0% VAT with purpose of export
  • Packing list and product specification sheet for canned/tubed tapioca pearls
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence showing receipt of export proceeds
  • Proof of direct transport/container tracking and warehouse/terminal handling records
  • Export licenses and processing/quality certificates required for food products (including sanitary certificates if requested by importing country)

France imposes mandatory e-invoicing on domestic B2B supplies and e-reporting of B2C transactions under the 2026-2027 reform. As of 1 September 2026 all companies must accept electronic invoices, and from 1 September 2027 all companies must issue e-invoices using the Factur-X or UBL 2.1 format. Invoices are cleared via the national Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or through accredited Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs) connected via secure API; no digital stamp or real-time fiscal clearance by the tax authority is applied. Export declarations must be filed electronically through the DGDDI DELTA system using the SAD/DAU format.

The French tax administration may deny zero-rating where the export evidence is absent or contradictory; the export VAT exemption is then reversed and the standard 20% VAT is assessed on the invoice amount, together with late-payment interest (0.20% per month). Penalties range from 40% to 80% for negligence, bad-faith (mauvaise foi) or fraudulent concealment; in serious customs fraud cases, criminal penalties under the Code des Douanes may apply, including imprisonment of up to three years and fines of up to EUR 50,000, plus confiscation of goods and vehicles. The ordinary reassessment period is three years after the filing year, extended to ten years in cases of serious tax fraud or hidden tax activity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has full current-account convertibility. There is no general French exchange-control approval for receiving export proceeds, paying suppliers, repatriating profits, or converting euros into foreign currencies. Capital movements are generally unrestricted, subject to AML and counter-terrorist-financing controls, EU and French sanctions, beneficial-owner transparency, anti-market-abuse rules, payment-services requirements, and applicable foreign-direct-investment screening. A non-resident shareholder may generally fund the SAS by equity, shareholder loan, or other permitted financing without a foreign-exchange permit. Profit distributions require distributable profits, approved accounts, corporate approvals, solvency compliance, and appropriate withholding-tax analysis. French domestic withholding on dividends to non-residents may apply, subject to treaty relief, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, qualifying exemption conditions, beneficial-ownership requirements, anti-abuse rules, and required documentation. Banks may request corporate, tax, source-of-funds, and transaction-purpose evidence before releasing or converting funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may ordinarily be held in euro or foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank terms, accounting records, sanctions screening, tax compliance, customs documentation, and anti-money-laundering monitoring. Goods exports must be supported by appropriate customs and commercial documentation, including the relevant export declaration and evidence required for VAT treatment. Banks may impose operational deadlines or request proof of shipment, delivery, invoice settlement, and economic purpose; these are contractual or compliance requirements rather than a general French FX-surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. Foreign-exchange activity is also governed by directly applicable European Union financial-services, AML, sanctions, payment-services, and market-infrastructure rules, together with French banking law administered by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution and the Autorité des marchés financiers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational payments and collections, but low as a sole treasury bank for a manufacturing SAS requiring capital deposits, documentary letters of credit, extensive trade finance, cash-credit facilities, and sophisticated FX hedging. An electronic-money institution or payment institution established and licensed in France or another European Economic Area state may provide permitted services under PSD2 or the Electronic Money Directive, subject to authorization, passporting, safeguarding, AML controls, transaction limits, and product scope. A non-EEA offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services into France merely through an online interface unless it has an appropriate French or EEA authorization, passport, branch, or authorized agent arrangement. EMI safeguarded funds are not equivalent to deposits and generally do not provide the full bank deposit, credit, cash-management, documentary-credit, or capital-deposit functionality required by the SAS. The company should retain a regulated French or EEA bank as primary treasury institution and use a compliant fintech only as a documented secondary rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity funding into the bank-designated SAS capital-deposit account before incorporation or before a registered capital increase. Use a regulated bank-to-bank transfer, preferably SWIFT MT103 with a unique end-to-end transaction reference and complete ordering-customer, beneficiary, and ultimate-funder information. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for operational payments and collections, but low as a sole treasury bank for a manufacturing SAS requiring capital deposits, documentary letters of credit, extensive trade finance, cash-credit facilities, and sophisticated FX hedging. An electronic-money institution or payment institution established and licensed in France or another European Economic Area state may provide permitted services under PSD2 or the Electronic Money Directive, subject to authorization, passporting, safeguarding, AML controls, transaction limits, and product scope. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax, plus a 3.3% social contribution on corporate income tax for companies meeting the statutory turnover and tax-liability thresholds, producing an effective marginal rate of approximately 25.83%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic French withholding tax rate on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders is generally 25%, subject to exemptions and tax-treaty reduction. Dividends paid by a French subsidiary to a qualifying EU parent may be exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary regime, generally requiring the minimum participation, holding-period, subject-to-tax and anti-abuse conditions. French resident individual shareholders are generally subject to a separate 12.8% income-tax withholding mechanism, plus social contributions, which is not the ordinary non-resident corporate rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the annual return is generally due by the second business day after 1 May of the following year. For other year-ends, the return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end. Electronic filing and payment are mandatory for SAS entities.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December. The fourth installment may be subject to a special percentage of estimated annual liability for larger companies. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month after year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million. A French entity is generally subject to full transfer-pricing documentation obligations where its gross annual turnover or gross assets are at least EUR 400 million, or where it belongs to a group meeting the relevant statutory threshold, or where it is controlled by or controls an entity meeting the statutory conditions. The documentation consists principally of a Master File and Local File. Entities below the threshold remain subject to the arm's-length principle and may have simplified documentation and information obligations.

Economic substance scope: France has no single general economic-substance regulation equivalent to a standalone offshore ESR statute. Substance is enforced through the arm's-length principle, permanent-establishment rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, general anti-abuse rules, abuse-of-law doctrine, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, hybrid-mismatch rules, principal-purpose testing under treaties and EU anti-avoidance measures. The SAS should maintain French operational substance proportionate to its manufacturing activity, including premises, personnel, production assets, decision-making, inventory control, quality systems, commercial risk and locally supported accounting records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, 40% penalty for deliberate non-compliance or 80% penalty for fraudulent maneuvers. Tax fraud may result in imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated cases potentially reaching 7 years' imprisonment and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, civil liability for personal misconduct, contribution claims and tax-payment liability where they organize or knowingly participate in fraudulent conduct. Courts may impose professional prohibitions and confiscation. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from judicial measures; imprisonment is possible after conviction.
Accounting fraud and destruction of evidence

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment and penalties, rejection of accounts, estimated assessment, criminal prosecution and possible imprisonment and fines under French commercial and criminal law. Obstruction or failure to provide records may produce additional administrative penalties.

Director personal liability: The president, directors and de facto managers may face criminal prosecution, management bans, civil damages and personal tax-liability actions where their intentional or grossly negligent conduct caused non-payment.
Transfer-pricing abuse and profit diversion

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to the French taxable profit, denial of deductions, withholding-tax exposure, late-payment interest, documentation penalties and potential 40% or 80% fraud penalties where intentional manipulation is established.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for deliberate misrepresentation, false records or participation in tax fraud; ordinary commercial exposure does not automatically pierce the SAS liability shield.
Shell company, sham substance or abusive treaty access

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, permanent-establishment findings, tax reassessment, withholding tax, abuse-of-law penalties and possible criminal fraud sanctions if deception is intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable where they knowingly operate a sham structure, misstate beneficial ownership or use the company to conceal unlawful conduct. A shell-company label alone does not automatically create personal liability or criminality.
VAT and customs fraud

Statutory sanction: Recovery of import duties and VAT, customs penalties, VAT fraud penalties, seizure or forfeiture of goods, suspension of customs facilities and criminal prosecution for serious fraud.

Director personal liability: Managers may face criminal liability and civil claims for knowing participation. Customs and tax authorities may pursue personal liability in cases of fraud, asset dissipation or deliberate obstruction.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing controls

Statutory sanction: Transaction blocking, reporting to Tracfin where applicable, administrative sanctions, confiscation, criminal money-laundering penalties and imprisonment. Banks may terminate accounts or suspend payments.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly facilitate laundering may face criminal prosecution, imprisonment, fines, confiscation and professional bans. Travel restrictions may be ordered by judicial authorities but are not automatic.
Unlawful profit repatriation and withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of withholding tax from the payer, interest, penalties, denial of deductions, treaty-benefit denial and potential shareholder-level reassessment.

Director personal liability: The SAS president and responsible officers may face civil liability for intentional or grossly negligent failure to withhold and remit tax; criminal liability requires proof of a qualifying offense or fraudulent conduct.
Food, product-safety and licensed-manufacturing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative injunctions, product withdrawal or recall, suspension or closure, fines and criminal penalties for unsafe or misleading products. Tax consequences may include denial of deductions and audit risk where records are unreliable.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal or civil liability for knowingly placing unsafe or misleading food products on the market, particularly where injury, fraud or deliberate concealment is involved.
