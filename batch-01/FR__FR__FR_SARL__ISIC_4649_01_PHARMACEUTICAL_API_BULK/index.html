Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SARL has no statutory minimum capital (may be as low as one euro). Under Article L.223-7 of the Commercial Code, cash contributions must be paid up to at least one-fifth at incorporation and the balance within five years. The subscribed funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account maintained by a French credit institution or a notary until the certificate of registration (Kbis) is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance issued after passage before the departmental consultative commission for safety and accessibility (CCDSA) with a favourable opinion of the fire brigade for premises exceeding 300 m2; structural stability inspection confirming load-bearing capacity of storage racks; occupational health clearance by the occupational health service with verification of workplace layout under Articles R.4214-1 et seq. of the Labour Code; periodic electrical installation inspection in accordance with NF C 15-100 with a three-year renewal cycle.

Paid-in capital rule: SARL has no statutory minimum capital (may be as low as one euro). Under Article L.223-7 of the Commercial Code, cash contributions must be paid up to at least one-fifth at incorporation and the balance within five years. The subscribed funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account maintained by a French credit institution or a notary until the certificate of registration (Kbis) is issued.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), operating under the Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances; official portal: impots.gouv.fr (professional access via 'Gérer mon entreprise'). Customs fiscal duties, export controls, and export valuation are administered separately by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI); official portal: douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned SARL: file incorporation through the single French business formalities desk (Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises, accessible at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). The dossier generates the SIREN/SIRET identification numbers. The business court (Tribunal de Commerce / Greffe) issues the RCS extract, and the DGFiP automatically assigns the competent corporate tax office (Service des Impôts des Entreprises, SIE). The SARL must then submit VAT registration via the DGFiP form 3310 (option or compulsory registration) through impots.gouv.fr to obtain the French TVA number and, where applicable, the intra-Community VAT number. For customs export transactions, the company must additionally obtain an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number from the DGDDI. The SARL is required to designate a day-to-day legal representative resident in France; where the shareholder is outside the EU, the DGFiP may require a statutory fiscal representative for indirect tax formalities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL is a capital company taxed by default at the level of the entity under the impôt sur les sociétés (IS) at the standard rate of 25% on worldwide profits (Article 206-1 CGI). Pass-through treatment is not automatic and is available only narrowly: a 'SARL de famille' satisfying Article 239 bis AA CGI, or a newly created small SARL meeting Article 239 bis AB CGI, may elect income-tax transparency in the hands of shareholders. For a wholesale distribution business dealing in pharmaceutical APIs, the practical and dominant default remains entity-level taxation. — Foreign-owned SARL: file incorporation through the single French business formalities desk (Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises, accessible at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL is a capital company taxed by default at the level of the entity under the impôt sur les sociétés (IS) at the standard rate of 25% on worldwide profits (Article 206-1 CGI). Pass-through treatment is not automatic and is available only narrowly: a 'SARL de famille' satisfying Article 239 bis AA CGI, or a newly created small SARL meeting Article 239 bis AB CGI, may elect income-tax transparency in the hands of shareholders. For a wholesale distribution business dealing in pharmaceutical APIs, the practical and dominant default remains entity-level taxation.

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory. Available elections include: (i) the election under Article 239 bis AA/AB CGI for a qualifying SARL to be taxed like a partnership rather than at corporate level; (ii) the option for a VAT normal regime by a small operator benefiting from the franchise en base de TVA; (iii) the election to form a French group VAT entity under Article 267 CGI for closely controlled French subsidiaries; (iv) the election for fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) for French subsidiaries held directly or indirectly at 95% or more, allowing intra-group loss offset and transfer of tax attributes. Hybrid mismatch treatment under ATAD is mandatory and cannot be opted out; no elections are available to override anti-hybrid or exit-tax rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France applies the EU small business exemption ('franchise en base de TVA'). For a wholesale trader of tangible goods such as pharmaceutical APIs, the standard VAT registration threshold is EUR 85,000 annual taxable turnover, with an upper tolerance of EUR 94,300 before registration becomes compulsory. Service activities use thresholds of EUR 37,500 and EUR 41,300 respectively. A French-established SARL conducting normal corporate accounting and wishing to recover input VAT has an unconditional right to elect for VAT registration below these thresholds. For intra-Community distance sales of goods, the harmonized EU threshold of EUR 10,000 per calendar year applies. A non-established foreign supplier has no turnover threshold: any taxable supply of goods or services in France requires a French VAT registration and a designated tax representative where the supplier is established outside the EU.

France has adopted a mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting framework under Article 289 bis CGI. As currently legislated, French taxable persons must issue and receive structured electronic invoices and transmit transaction data to the DGFiP via the Public Invoice Platform (Portail Public de Facturation – PPF) or a certified private dematerialization platform (PDP): from 1 September 2026 for large and medium enterprises (grandes entreprises et ETI), and from 1 September 2027 for small enterprises and micro-enterprises. The mandatory formats are Factur-X (EN 16931), UBL, and CII. The system does not use Italian-style real-time fiscal stamping; integrity and legibility guarantees are required, and structured invoice data must be transmissible via interoperable APIs (Y1/Y2 flows) to the administration. For pharmaceutical API transactions, the invoice must contain the customs tariff code (CN), GTIN/product nomenclature where relevant, and the correct Incoterms to avoid mismatches between DGFiP e-reporting and DGDDI export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully and freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions, subject only to the general safeguards permitted under Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. France imposes no prior FX authorisation or administrative quota for trade settlement, profit remittances, or capital injections on the capital account. Dividend remittance by a French SARL to a non-resident parent is unrestricted from an FX standpoint, though it remains subject to French corporate income tax, applicable withholding tax treaties, and CRS/FATCA reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion rule applies to export receipts in France. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign currency proceeds in authorised bank accounts within the EEA and are free to use them for working-capital or settlement purposes. Each cross-border payment is automatically captured by the resident credit institution for balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the Banque de France; physical export declarations must be lodged through the French Customs DELTA/DX system. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (within the Eurosystem and ESCB); external financial relations are administered by the Direction Générale du Trésor under the Code monétaire et financier; prudential supervision and AML enforcement are within the remit of the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: low-to-medium. An Electronic Money Institution (EMI), whether French-licensed by the ACPR or passporting under PSD2, may provide payment accounts and money remittance but cannot lawfully take deposits, extend credit, issue documentary letters of credit, or guarantee trade instruments in France. For a high-risk pharmaceutical API wholesale business, EMIs offer practical one-click multi-currency collection but fail to meet the full settlement rails required for Swift LC operations and therefore should be considered a secondary or ancillary payment channel rather than a primary treasury banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections must be wired to the French SARL’s blocked capital account through SEPA/TARGET2 if in EUR, or via SWIFT MT103 with cover through MT202 for USD/GBP/CNH. Use ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI (equity contribution) where provided; for shareholder intra-group loans use INTC. The receiving bank will quarantine the proceeds in a non-interest-bearing compte bloqué until the notarised deed of incorporation and the KYC defensive file are complete; the capital is thereafter released for operational use. No FX authorisation is required; however, the bank must submit a balance-of-payments statistical declaration to the Banque de France for any cross-border transaction, and foreign direct investment notifications to the Direction Générale du Trésor must be assessed where the pharma wholesale business touches sensitive-activity sectors or where sovereign rules impose prior authorisation thresholds.Operational payments to API suppliers are executed by SWIFT MT103 for cross-currency funds, with correspondent clearing through major money-centre banks (e.g., Fedwire/CHIPS for USD, CIPS/offshore RMB for CNY, TARGET2 for intra-EUR payments). For bulk-API purchase contracts, the primary clearing rail is a commercial letter of credit under UCP 600: the French bank issues an MT700 through the SWIFT network to a nominated correspondent bank, against presentation of compliant shipping and insurance documents. Trade settlements should be routed exclusively via the French corporate bank account and not through cryptocurrency platforms or services that obscure the payment purpose. For export operations, payments can be collected using an MT103 inbound message with purpose code TRAD or INTC and documentary evidence retained for customs and tax audits.Spot foreign exchange transactions against the ECB euro reference rate for immediate conversion of export receipts or supplier payments.Outright forward exchange contracts (change à terme) with maturities up to 24 months, used to lock currency conversion on future USD, GBP, or CNY trade flows.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or non-deliverable currencies such as mainland CNH or INR indices where physical settlement is not available, provided the underlying trade exposure is marketable and documented.Cross-currency swaps for term-mismatch hedging of FX cash flows on long-term API supply arrangements or intercompany financing.European-style FX options and zero-cost collars (collar mechanisms) to cap unfavourable FX movement while preserving upside on favourable rate changes, exclusively for balance-sheet buyers and with no speculative leverage.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard rate; reduced rate of 15% applies to first €42,500 of profit for companies with annual turnover below €50 million)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) is mandatory for companies with annual turnover or total assets exceeding €400 million. For smaller companies, a simplified documentation (Form 2257) is required if cross-border related-party transactions exceed €150,000.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but substance is critical for tax residency and anti-abuse rules. The General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) and the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under tax treaties require genuine economic activity, including premises, employees, and decision-making in France. A shell company without substance risks denial of treaty benefits and recharacterization of transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (Article 1741 CGI). Civil penalties: 40% to 80% of the evaded tax, plus interest at 0.2% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they participated in fraudulent acts. Travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition may apply. In severe cases, imprisonment up to 5 years and a ban on managing companies.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 25% withholding tax on deemed dividends. Administrative penalties up to 100% of the tax understated (Article 1729 CGI).

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for the company's tax debts if they knowingly facilitated the sham structure. Travel bans and restrictions on leaving France may be imposed during tax investigations.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 5% of annual turnover (Monetary and Financial Code). Criminal penalties for money laundering: up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine (Article 324-1 Penal Code).

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent money laundering. Professional bans and confiscation of assets may apply.
