Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for a SARL under Article L.223-2. If capital is fixed, cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% at subscription, with the balance payable within five years. The full cash capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the RCS registration certificate (extrait K-bis) is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and prevention clearance must be obtained from the departmental fire service (SDIS) and the local planning authority; structural load inspection is required for cleanrooms, autoclaves, water purification systems, and heavy technical equipment, typically attested by a bureau de contrôle; workplace clearance requires CARSAT registration, a risk assessment document (DUERP), occupational health approval, and compliance with accessibility and emergency exit requirements for sterile manufacturing areas.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for a SARL under Article L.223-2. If capital is fixed, cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% at subscription, with the balance payable within five years. The full cash capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the RCS registration certificate (extrait K-bis) is issued.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully liberalised current-account and capital-account convertibility regime under the Code monétaire et financier and EU Treaty free movement of capital. Profit remittance is unrestricted for EU/EEA investors; distributions to non-EU shareholders are subject to domestic withholding tax (generally 25-30%, reduced or exempt under applicable double-tax treaties) after approval of the annual financial statements. No special foreign-investment approval is required for share capital injections, although extra-EU inbound direct investments may trigger a post-closing prior declaration to DG Trésor under the foreign-investment screening regime. Dividend and interest repatriation is legally unimpeded once bank KYC, tax-residence evidence and treaty-relief forms are in place.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory currency surrender or mandatory conversion applies in France: export proceeds may be freely retained and recycled in EUR or foreign currency. However, all cross-border transfers involving non-EU counterparties must be executed through an authorised intermediary credit institution and declared for monetary and balance-of-payments statistical purposes to Banque de France when the transaction threshold applies. Export documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs export declaration via DELTA G) must be archived to evidence the underlying trade flow, particularly for ex-works EU sales of sterile ophthalmic pharmaceuticals.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France); external financial relations and exchange-control oversight delegated to the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; banking supervision by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational subsidiaries. ACPR-licensed or EEA-passported electronic money institutions/fintechs (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Treezor) can open operational sub-accounts quickly and provide payment cards and SEPA rails, but they cannot host capital escrow or statutory share-capital deposits, lack full trade-finance/LC issuance capability, and are generally unsuitable for large SWIFT/LC flows. Legal constraint: such providers are not credit institutions and cannot perform documentary-credit functions; some French Tier-1 banks reject beneficiary relationships with unlicensed crypto/EMI structures, so a dedicated commercial bank account remains mandatory for escrow and treasury.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2022; large taxpayers remain subject to the 3.3% social surcharge on IS where annual IS liability exceeds EUR 763,000, and to the 15% reduced rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable income for eligible SMEs with turnover below EUR 10 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months of the fiscal year end; for fiscal years ending 31 December the deadline is extended to the first business day after 30 April (in practice 3 May); for off-calendar year ends the return is due no later than the last day of the third month following the fiscal year close.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Instalments due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December of the relevant fiscal year (for calendar-year taxpayers; off-calendar years use the 15th day of the 4th, 7th, 10th, and 13th months, or the 20th day where the administration has granted a dispensation); final settlement is paid at the same time as the annual return.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 01 / company identification (case AV) SARL corporate identity, SIREN/SIRET number, registered office, fiscal year opening and closing dates, and the identity of the legal representative; also confirms the applicable reduced IS rate threshold for SMEs. The French-resident SARL must be designated as the taxpayer; non-resident shareholders have no direct filing right or obligation; the return must include the annex listing non-resident beneficiaries and, where applicable, the managers' fees paid abroad subject to withholding tax under art. 182 A CGI. Late filing triggers a 10% surcharge when no prior demand is issued, 40% after formal notice, and 80% in cases of deliberately false or incomplete filings (art. 1728 CGI); unpaid tax also incurs monthly interest at 0.20% (2.4% annual, art. 1727 CGI), a 5% late payment penalty where payment is not received by the due date, and automatic loss of certain tax credit offsets until the default is regularized.
Line 100 / résultat fiscal (after restatements) Taxable result computed from statutory net profit with add-backs of non-deductible expenses, depreciation recapture rules, and the integration of foreign exchange and transfer pricing adjustments; carryforward losses are offset here under the annual cap. Royalties or service recharge payments to non-resident group companies must be supported by prior transfer pricing documentation and, for cross-border flows falling below EUR 50,000 of related-party turnover, the simplified annex; otherwise the administration may recharacterize the deduction. Line 230 / impôt sur les sociétés dû Computation of final IS due: 15% on the first EUR 42,500 and 25% on the balance for SMEs; cancellation of CIR and CII tax credits; any advance payments made quarterly are deducted, and the remaining balance is paid with the filing. Non-resident shareholders claiming treaty benefits on dividends out of retained earnings must attach proof of effective beneficiary status; the IS field itself is payable by the French entity, not by the foreign parent; early withholding or quarterly payment obligations remain unaffected. Late filing triggers a 10% surcharge when no prior demand is issued, 40% after formal notice, and 80% in cases of deliberately false or incomplete filings (art. 1728 CGI); unpaid tax also incurs monthly interest at 0.20% (2.4% annual, art. 1727 CGI), a 5% late payment penalty where payment is not received by the due date, and automatic loss of certain tax credit offsets until the default is regularized.
Payment voucher fields (amount payable) Each instalment equals 25% of the estimated annual IS; taxpayers collect the amounts payable as a percentage of the tax base estimated at the beginning of the year and may reduce instalments if the final IS proves lower. Funds must be wired in EUR from the French-domiciled SARL treasury to the DGFiP central account; invoicing or payment routing from a non-resident parent or affiliate is not permitted and will be treated as a deficiency subject to recovery. Missed instalments attract a 5% penalty on the amount not paid plus the monthly 0.20% late interest (art. 1731 and 1727 CGI); the administration issues an automatic collection notice and the taxpayer loses any right to reduce future instalments without prior approval.
