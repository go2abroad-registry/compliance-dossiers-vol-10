Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for a SARL under Article L.223-2 (capital may be €1); cash subscriptions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account with a bank depositary or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released only upon registration; cash contributions are payable at the minimum legal fraction (20%) at incorporation and the balance within five years.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate delivered by the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) after technical control of extinguishing systems, evacuation routes and fire compartments; building structural load verifications by a licensed technical controller (contrôleur technique) for heavy CNC machines; and a workplace clearance from the Inspection du Travail covering machine guarding, chip evacuation, coolant mist filtration, vibration, noise exposure at/under 85 dB(A) and emergency stop systems on all five-axis machining centers.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for a SARL under Article L.223-2 (capital may be €1); cash subscriptions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account with a bank depositary or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released only upon registration; cash contributions are payable at the minimum legal fraction (20%) at incorporation and the balance within five years.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), including the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) or Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE), through impots.gouv.fr and its professional taxpayer portal. Customs registrations and export declarations are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI), principally through the DELTA IE customs system.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate or register the SARL through the French business formalities single window operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), obtaining the SIREN and establishment-level SIRET identifiers. 2. Register for French corporate income tax, VAT, payroll taxes, and other applicable taxes with the competent SIE through the DGFiP professional portal. 3. Obtain the French VAT identification number, normally formatted as FR followed by a two-digit key and the nine-digit SIREN. 4. A non-established foreign entity making taxable supplies in France must generally obtain a French VAT number before or from its first taxable transaction, unless the transaction is fully covered by a reverse-charge mechanism or another statutory exemption. 5. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS). A qualifying family SARL may elect taxation under the French personal income tax regime without the ordinary five-year limitation, subject to statutory eligibility requirements. A qualifying SARL formed for less than five years may elect personal income tax treatment for a maximum five-year period, subject to conditions concerning size, ownership, management, activity, and non-listing. The election is made with the competent tax authority within the statutory period and is generally irrevocable for the applicable period. France has no general United States-style check-the-box election for SARLs and no elective hybrid-mismatch classification regime. Anti-hybrid rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance framework may deny deductions or impose income inclusions where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business eligible for the VAT exemption based on the franchise en base, the principal 2025 annual turnover thresholds are EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods, accommodation, and comparable activities and EUR 37,500 for services, with higher tolerance thresholds of EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250 respectively. These thresholds are subject to legislative change and detailed activity rules. A foreign business not established in France generally has no usable domestic small-business threshold for French taxable supplies and must register from its first taxable transaction, unless a reverse charge, special scheme, or other exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT where the supplier, the supplier's agent, or an eligible intermediary dispatches or transports the goods outside the EU and can substantiate the physical exit from the EU. The supplier must retain the export declaration, Movement Reference Number (MRN), customs exit confirmation, commercial invoice, transport evidence, packing records, and proof linking the goods to the transaction. Goods supplied for export by a customer or its agent may qualify only where the statutory conditions and documentary evidence are satisfied. Services are not automatically zero-rated. Business-to-business services generally follow the customer-location rule and may be outside French VAT with reverse-charge wording, while specific services connected with French immovable property, admission to events, transport, and other statutory categories follow special place-of-supply rules. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, business-use evidence, and compliance with the applicable French or EU refund procedure. Failure to prove export normally converts the transaction into a taxable French supply.

  • French or EU customs export declaration submitted through the applicable customs system, normally DELTA IE
  • Customs Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs proof of exit from the European Union
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and VAT treatment
  • Packing list and product-identification records, including serial numbers where relevant for CNC machinery
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or other independent transport evidence
  • Sales contract, purchase order, delivery terms, and customer correspondence linking the shipment to the export
  • Evidence of payment or receivable settlement where relevant to audit substantiation
  • End-user certificate, export-control authorization, dual-use authorization, or sanctions-screening records where the CNC equipment or destination triggers French or EU export-control requirements
  • Inward remittance certificate: not a general French VAT requirement; banking evidence may be retained as supplementary commercial substantiation but does not replace customs proof of exit

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting in phases. All businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Large and intermediate-sized companies must issue domestic B2B electronic invoices and submit required transaction data from 1 September 2026; small and medium-sized enterprises must generally issue such invoices from 1 September 2027. The final statutory framework uses registered private platforms (plateformes de dématérialisation partenaires, or PDPs) and the public invoicing portal infrastructure, rather than a universal current real-time government clearance model. B2C and cross-border transactions generally fall within electronic reporting rules rather than domestic B2B e-invoice issuance, subject to implementing rules. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. Invoices must contain mandatory French and EU VAT data, including supplier and customer identification, VAT numbers where applicable, taxable basis, rate, amount, exemption or reverse-charge wording, currency, and invoice sequencing.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export VAT zero-rating. Export proceeds are subject to ordinary French and EU banking, anti-money-laundering, sanctions, customs, and accounting requirements, and payment evidence may be requested during an audit. If the exporter cannot prove EU exit or otherwise fails the exemption conditions, the French tax authority may assess the unpaid VAT retrospectively, together with late-payment interest and applicable penalties. A 40% surcharge may apply for deliberate non-compliance, and an 80% surcharge may apply for fraudulent conduct or abusive manoeuvres, subject to statutory defenses and procedural rules. Interest is generally charged on late-paid tax at the statutory monthly rate applicable for the relevant period. False invoices, fabricated export evidence, customs fraud, or intentional tax fraud may also trigger criminal prosecution, including imprisonment and substantial fines; aggravated tax fraud can carry penalties of up to seven years' imprisonment and EUR 3 million, subject to the facts and applicable statutory provisions. Customs violations may independently generate customs duties, import VAT, fines, seizure, loss of authorizations, and export-control sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and under IMF Article VIII obligations implemented through the Eurosystem. No prior exchange-control authorization or administrative permit is required for a French SARL to receive, convert, or remit EUR or foreign currency. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are lawful, subject to the standard 30% French final withholding tax (or reduced treaty withholding where the shareholder provides a tax-residence certificate), filing of Form 2569, and AML source-of-funds documentation. The only practical hurdles are banking-side EDD, DG Trésor statistical reporting, and the obligation for French resident entities to declare any foreign accounts annually. There are no capital-repatriation quotas, import-payment licences, or convertibility restrictions affecting the EUR functional currency of the SARL.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no timeline for repatriating export receipts. A resident exporter may retain collections in EUR or hold permitted foreign-currency accounts indefinitely. Applicable obligations are: (i) annual declarations to DG Trésor under the French balance-of-payments reporting regime for all cross-border settlements exceeding the monitoring threshold; (ii) EU Border Cash Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2018/1672) declarations for cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more; and (iii) documentary retention of the customs export declaration (SAD/DAU), commercial invoice, transport documents, and end-user certificate to evidence the underlying trade for bank compliance and FX reporting.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (national central bank and Eurosystem member) with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as banking supervisor, the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) as balance-of-payments and foreign-exchange information authority, and the European Central Bank (ECB) for Eurosystem monetary and payments oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, with material legal constraints. French-regulated EMIs and fintechs (e.g., Qonto and Shine, licensed under the PSD2 transposition and Directive 2009/110/EC with ACPR supervision) can open operational EUR accounts and provide payment rails; however, most systematically reject or severely restrict clients exposed to the high-risk manufacturing tier (ISIC 2822, CNC machine tools) where title documents, documentary credits, and end-user certificates are required. Offshore non-EEA digital EMIs (notably UK entities post-Brexit) no longer benefit from EU passporting and cannot lawfully provide an IBAN-based business account to a French SARL without a licensed French branch; they are unviable for capital escrow or trade clearing. Recommended structure is a domestic Tier-1 bank as the treasury anchor and, at most, a French-licensed EMI for minor operating expenses; prohibited reliance should be placed on any unlicensed or regime-shopping fintech for cross-border trade settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections and shareholder current-account advances must arrive via compliant regulated wires referencing the corporate act that authorized the funds. For non-EU originators: SWIFT MT103 through a correspondent bank with the SARL as final beneficiary, using the EPC purpose code 'INVC' (investment in capital) for equity or 'QUAS' (quasi-equity shareholder current account) for avances en compte courant, and ISO 20022 remittance information stating 'apport en capital social' or 'augmentation de capital'. For EU originators: TARGET2 / SEPA Credit Transfer with the same purpose codes. USD and CNY inflows may be received in standard correspondent systems (CHIPS, Fedwire, CIPS) and held in separate currency accounts before conversion, but the receiving bank requires the originator bank's KYC file, the notarized shareholder resolution increasing capital or approving the advance, the publication and registration evidence of the capital increase, an anti-fraud source-of-funds declaration, and confirmation that no sanctioned counterparty appears in the payment chain; funds must remain traceable for AML/CFT record-keeping under the French Monetary and Financial Code.Export disbursements for CNC milling centers should use two controlled clearing rails. First, clean payments: SWIFT MT103/MT202 against a validated commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, and customs SAD/DAU, settled through TARGET2 or the destination-country RTGS/OCR system; no ACH, instant-payment, or crypto rails are acceptable for trade settlements. Second, documentary trade: irrevocable, confirmed Letters of Credit issued by SWIFT MT700 under UCP600 and, where applicable, with the End-User Certificate as a condition of negotiation; presentation must include the commercial invoice, full-set bills of lading or CMR, packing list, insurance certificate, export declaration, inspection certificate, and any import-permit documentation required by the buyer's jurisdiction. CE-marking and EU export-control evidence accompany the documents. Drawings are made at the confirming bank, and funds are credited in EUR or the agreed settlement currency only upon strict document compliance; discrepancies are resolved through the treasury back-office before re-presentation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium, with material legal constraints. French-regulated EMIs and fintechs (e.g., Qonto and Shine, licensed under the PSD2 transposition and Directive 2009/110/EC with ACPR supervision) can open operational EUR accounts and provide payment rails; however, most systematically reject or severely restrict clients exposed to the high-risk manufacturing tier (ISIC 2822, CNC machine tools) where title documents, documentary credits, and end-user certificates are required. Offshore non-EEA digital EMIs (notably UK entities post-Brexit) no longer benefit from EU passporting and cannot lawfully provide an IBAN-based business account to a French SARL without a licensed French branch; they are unviable for capital escrow or trade clearing. Recommended structure is a domestic Tier-1 bank as the treasury anchor and, at most, a French-licensed EMI for minor operating expenses; prohibited reliance should be placed on any unlicensed or regime-shopping fintech for cross-border trade settlements.
