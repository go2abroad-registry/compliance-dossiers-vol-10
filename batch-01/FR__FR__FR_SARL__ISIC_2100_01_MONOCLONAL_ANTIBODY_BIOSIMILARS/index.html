Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

French law imposes no statutory minimum share capital for an SARL; the capital is freely fixed in the articles. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked account opened in the SARL's name with a licensed French bank, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary. At least one-fifth of the cash contributions must be paid up before filing, and the balance may be paid up in one or more instalments within five years of RCS registration; the deposit must remain blocked until the K-bis certificate is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification from the Departmental Fire and Rescue Service (SDIS) and the Préfecture's commission de sécurité; structural-load compliance certificate from an approved design office; workplace clearance from DREETS under the Code du travail; occupational-health surveillance by a workplace physician for staff handling biologics; ICPE inspection confirms both environmental and occupational compliance before production start-up.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French national tax authority. Main portals: impots.gouv.fr (corporate tax and VAT returns), formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet unique for company incorporation and registrations) and pro.douane.gouv.fr / DELTA (customs declaration system for export VAT evidence). Non-resident taxpayers without a French establishment are handled by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), Paris.

E-government filing portal

French-incorporated SARL: file incorporation formalities through the Guichet unique portal; INSEE automatically allocates SIREN and SIRET identifiers; DGFiP allocates the French intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN. Foreign entity without a permanent establishment: apply to the SIEE on Form SD 8471 (if established in the EU) or SD 8473 (if established outside the EU), enclosing certified articles of association, certificate of incorporation, proof of fiscal domicile, and a power of attorney in favour of the appointed tax representative (a fiscal representative is compulsory for non-EU established entities); the SIEE then grants a French intra-Community VAT number and activates the impots.gouv.fr professional account.

The SARL does not benefit from automatic pass-through taxation; a French branch of a foreign company is equally taxed at entity level on its French-source profits. — French-incorporated SARL: file incorporation formalities through the Guichet unique portal; INSEE automatically allocates SIREN and SIRET identifiers; DGFiP allocates the French intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — The SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés) at a 25% standard rate, with a reduced rate of 15% on the first €42,500 of annual taxable profit for qualifying SMEs (turnover below €10 million). The SARL does not benefit from automatic pass-through taxation; a French branch of a foreign company is equally taxed at entity level on its French-source profits.

Available election forms

(1) An SARL whose shareholders are exclusively natural persons may elect for partnership-type transparency under Article 239 bis AA CGI for a five-year period; (2) a single-member EURL with an individual shareholder may likewise opt to be taxed in the personal income tax regime; (3) corporate groups holding at least 95% of a French subsidiary may elect tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI; (4) ATAD 1/2 hybrid mismatch rules codified in Articles 229 to 239 quinquies CGI restrict hybrid instruments and hybrid permanent establishment mismatches; no US-style check-the-box election exists under French law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established foreign suppliers: nil threshold — VAT registration is mandatory as of the first taxable supply of goods or services in France, subject to reverse-charge relief for certain B2B imported services under Article 283 CGI where the French customer self-assesses VAT. Resident SMEs may use the franchise en base de TVA up to €93,500 per year for sales of goods and €37,500 per year for services (2025 thresholds); the franchise is not available to an SARL that has opted for IS or to foreign suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Article 262-I CGI provided the goods are physically transported out of France and the exporter holds documentary evidence; intra-EU dispatches of goods to a taxable customer are exempt under Article 262 ter CGI where the purchaser's intra-Community VAT number is validated, the DES/EC sales list is filed, and transport to another Member State is proven. Services physically performed on goods to be exported are zero-rated under Article 262-1° CGI; B2B services supplied to non-French entrepreneurs are outside French VAT under Article 259-1° CGI if the customer is established abroad. Biosimilar monoclonal antibody production for export accordingly benefits from 0% VAT treatment on cross-border supplies, while French domestic sales remain taxable at 20%.

  • Customs export declaration (EX-E) lodged through the DELTA system and the DPE/DEB summary declaration
  • Transport evidence: original Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB) or CMR consignment note evidencing physical dispatch from France
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, packing list and inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank credit evidence
  • For intra-EU dispatch: the customer's validated intra-Community VAT ID and the DES (Déclaration d'Échange de Biens) record

Mandatory structured e-invoicing and e-reporting are phased in through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) and certified dematerialisation platforms (PDPs). From 1 September 2026, all VAT-registered businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices, and large and mid-size enterprises must issue structured e-invoices (Factur-X, UBL, CII) and transmit e-reporting of transaction and payment data. From 1 September 2027, small and micro businesses, including a typical SARL, must issue fully structured e-invoices. France does not operate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; e-reporting must be transmitted to DGFiP within 10 days of invoice issuance through the PPF or a certified PDP.

DGFiP and French Customs cross-check export declarations against VAT returns, the EC Sales List/DES and bank payment flows. If zero-rating is disallowed, VAT at 20% is clawed back under Article 1788 A CGI, together with late-payment interest at 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI) and penalties of 40% for insufficient declarations or 80% for deliberate misstatement or abuse of rights (Articles 1729-1730 CGI). False customs declarations connected with exports may trigger criminal sanctions under Articles 414-415 of the French Customs Code, including fines up to twice the VAT amount in issue and confiscation of the goods. Export substantiation documents must be retained for six years, and refunds already credited can be retrospectively reassessed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully and freely convertible. France has liberalised both current-account and capital-account payments since Law No. 88-1201 of 23 December 1988; dividend and profit remittances, shareholder loan repayments, interest, royalties and trade settlements are not subject to FX licensing or convertibility ceilings. Residual operational hurdles are AML/KYC documentation, EU and national sanctions screening, FATCA/CRS reporting, and, for a bio-health manufacturer acquiring critical infrastructure exposure, prior foreign-investment approval under Article L.151-3 paragraph IV CMF. An SARL may remit dividends without FX permission once the annual accounts are approved, subject to corporate-law solvency tests and applicable dividend withholding rules; no treasury drain results from currency-transfer restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds in France. Exporters may retain receivables in any currency, maintain foreign-currency accounts domestically or abroad, and convert at market rates without restriction. The only residual duty is statistical: cross-border payments connected with exports and receivables must be captured in the Banque de France balance-of-payments data collection system, either automatically through the resident payment bank or via direct monthly declaration when amounts exceed the applicable notification threshold. No repatriation deadline or conversion period applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty); banking conduct and AML/CFT supervision by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) under the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury infrastructure; at best a secondary disbursement channel. An offshore digital EMI that is not authorised in an EEA jurisdiction may not lawfully provide payment accounts to French residents on a passporting basis, and UK EMIs lost passporting rights after Brexit. EEA-licensed EMIs are supervised but typically lack SWIFT correspondent banking, MT103 trade-wire capability, documentary credit issuance and Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting features. Given the High_Risk_Special_License classification of biosimilar manufacturing, French Tier-1 banks will not extend full AML reliance to an EMI-only setup; a regulated Tier-1 or Tier-2 bank mandate is mandatory for structural liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or shareholder-loan transfers are executed by SWIFT MT103 in EUR settled on the T2/TARGET2 RTGS platform, with USD legs via the French bank's New York correspondent using CHIPS or Fedwire. Three mandatory compliance layers apply: first, purpose field 70 states the Banque de France nature-of-transaction code for direct investment or shareholder financing (capital increase or long-term shareholder loan) so that balance-of-payments classification is correct; second, the receiving bank verifies the notarised declaration of subscription and payment, the amended statuts, the executed shareholder-loan agreement where applicable, the SARL's Legal Entity Identifier, and UBO screening of the remitting shareholder; third, if the non-EEA shareholder would acquire direct or indirect control of a French biohealth-sector entity, prior authorisation from the Direction Générale du Trésor under Article L.151-3 paragraph IV CMF must be obtained before the transfer is cleared. Funds land in the dedicated blocked or capital-escrow account and are released to the operating account only after issuance of the banker's capital certificate and confirmation that the manufacturing licence gate is satisfied.Outbound treasury settlements use SWIFT MT103 in EUR via T2/RTGS or in USD via correspondent clearing for consumables, equipment, licensing and service fees; irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP600 or eUCP opened for bioreactor raw-material imports and biosimilar export deliveries, with presentation of GMP certificates, certificates of analysis, proforma invoices and transport documents; documentary collection on D/P and D/A terms for low-risk recurring counterparties; and approved supply-chain finance facilities for longer-tenor suppliers. Every outbound instruction above the notification threshold feeds the company's Banque de France balance-of-payments monthly declaration executed through the resident bank, and each payment is checked against the pre-approved beneficiary list and the group sanctions-screening engine before final release.Deliverable FX forward contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNY) hedging binding commercial and capital-expenditure exposuresCross-currency interest rate swaps (EUR/USD basis) on shareholder loans and equipment financing to eliminate currency mismatchesFX swaps (spot/forward combinations) for temporary conversion of foreign-currency export receivables into operational EUR liquidityNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to sales or procurement ledger currencies with restricted convertibility or illiquid onshore interbank markets

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for primary treasury infrastructure; at best a secondary disbursement channel. An offshore digital EMI that is not authorised in an EEA jurisdiction may not lawfully provide payment accounts to French residents on a passporting basis, and UK EMIs lost passporting rights after Brexit. EEA-licensed EMIs are supervised but typically lack SWIFT correspondent banking, MT103 trade-wire capability, documentary credit issuance and Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting features. Given the High_Risk_Special_License classification of biosimilar manufacturing, French Tier-1 banks will not extend full AML reliance to an EMI-only setup; a regulated Tier-1 or Tier-2 bank mandate is mandatory for structural liquidity.
