Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for a French SARL. The capital is freely fixed in the articles of association; any cash contribution must be fully paid up and deposited on an escrow-type blocked account with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the RCS certificate is issued, evidenced by an attestation de dépôt de fonds.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies EU rules on free movement of capital and payments and has no general exchange-control approval requirement for ordinary commercial or capital transfers. The euro is freely convertible. Cross-border transfers remain subject to bank AML, sanctions, source-of-funds, tax, and transaction-purpose checks. Profit distributions to non-resident shareholders are generally permitted after applicable corporate-law approvals and French withholding-tax, treaty, and reporting analysis; dividend payments are not automatically tax-free. Interest, royalties, service fees, and intercompany payments require evidence of genuine transactions, transfer-pricing support where applicable, and withholding-tax review. Banks may request contracts, invoices, customs and excise records, accounts, and beneficial-owner information before processing payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign-currency export receipts. No general deadline requires export receipts to be converted into euros. The company must retain adequate accounting and transaction records, comply with applicable customs, VAT, excise, and statistical reporting obligations, and respond to bank or authority requests. Reporting obligations may apply to particular cross-border transactions or payment-service providers; confirm applicability with the bank and French advisers for the transaction profile.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, within the Eurosystem and under the monetary policy framework of the European Central Bank. French customs and tax authorities, including the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI) and the Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), administer relevant trade, excise, and tax requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro collections and payments, subject to the provider's French or EEA authorization, permitted services, risk appetite, and account terms. An electronic-money account is not a substitute for a French bank account where a bank account or capital-deposit certificate is required for incorporation. Offshore or non-EEA digital EMIs may face correspondent-bank, safeguarding, sanctions, and onboarding limitations and may not support bonded-trade documentation, commercial letters of credit, or complex foreign-currency services. Verify the provider's regulatory status and permitted cross-border services before use.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident or layered ownership, nominee arrangements, or difficulty identifying the ultimate beneficial owners.

Root cause: Non-resident or layered ownership, nominee arrangements, or difficulty identifying the ultimate beneficial owners.

Defense evidence: Current corporate registry extracts and constitutional documents for each ownership layer; certified ownership chart through natural-person beneficial owners; passports and proof of residential address; beneficial-owner register information; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds evidence; and board or shareholder authorizations.
Unclear capital funding, third-party deposits, transfers from unrelated entities, or funds originating in a higher-risk jurisdiction.

Root cause: Unclear capital funding, third-party deposits, transfers from unrelated entities, or funds originating in a higher-risk jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Capital subscription and shareholder resolutions; bank statements tracing funds to the originating account and beneficial owner; audited financial statements, tax returns, sale or dividend records supporting source of funds; loan agreements where relevant; and an explanation of the commercial rationale and payment chain.
Alcohol, bonded-warehouse, excise, or customs exposure without demonstrated licensing, tax controls, or traceable inventory movements.

Root cause: Alcohol, bonded-warehouse, excise, or customs exposure without demonstrated licensing, tax controls, or traceable inventory movements.

Defense evidence: Relevant French registrations and authorizations, including applicable excise and customs registrations; warehouse and logistics agreements; evidence of any required excise-suspension or movement-system arrangements; supplier and customer contracts; invoices; product and origin records; customs declarations; inventory reconciliation; and documented procedures for VAT, excise, and bonded transfers.
High-value or unusual payments, cash-intensive activity, transactions inconsistent with the stated wholesale model, or payments involving sanctioned parties or restricted destinations.

Root cause: High-value or unusual payments, cash-intensive activity, transactions inconsistent with the stated wholesale model, or payments involving sanctioned parties or restricted destinations.

Defense evidence: Transaction-specific purchase orders, invoices, transport and delivery documents, customs and excise records, counterparty due diligence, sanctions-screening results, payment instructions, and a reconciliation linking each payment to the underlying shipment and accounting entry. Maintain a written policy prohibiting unlawful cash payments and transactions with prohibited parties.
Material differences between the stated business activity, expected turnover, transaction geographies, account flows, and public or corporate records.

Root cause: Material differences between the stated business activity, expected turnover, transaction geographies, account flows, and public or corporate records.

Defense evidence: Business plan and cash-flow forecast; current management accounts; tax and VAT filings where available; supplier and customer profile; expected currency and jurisdiction matrix; warehouse and shipping arrangements; and a written explanation of seasonal, inventory-financing, or exceptional transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Fund the SARL from an account held by the shareholder or another documented, legally authorized funding source. Before incorporation, coordinate the capital deposit with the selected bank or other eligible depositary and obtain the capital-deposit certificate for registration. After registration, transfer operating funds to the SARL's corporate account, using SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments and SWIFT for non-SEPA or other-currency transfers. Use accurate payment references identifying the capital contribution, shareholder, and company; do not use an invented or generic regulatory purpose code. Provide subscription documents, shareholder resolutions, bank-origin evidence, and source-of-funds documentation. Record any shareholder loan separately from equity and document its terms. Reconcile each payment to the purchase contract, invoice, beneficiary, shipment, customs declaration, and applicable excise or bonded-warehouse records. Confirm beneficiary-account details independently, allocate bank charges expressly, and screen counterparties and banks for sanctions and payment restrictions.
