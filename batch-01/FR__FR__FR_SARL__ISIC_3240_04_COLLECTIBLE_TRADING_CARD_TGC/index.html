Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), with registration in the Registre National des Entreprises and, for commercial activities, the competent local Registre du commerce et des sociétés.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L223-1 to L223-43 and R223-1 to R223-36 governing SARLs; Articles L123-1 et seq. governing the Registre National des Entreprises.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of creation of a legal entity through the Guichet unique, accompanied by signed SARL articles of association and supporting documents; the historical M0 form is no longer the standard filing instrument.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to a SARL. Capital is freely determined in the articles. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must be fully transferred at incorporation. Funds may be deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations and released after registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) — portal: impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Register the SARL with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce via the online Registre National des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); obtain the K-bis extraction, SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) identification codes; the DGFIP automatically allocates a French VAT number (format FR + 2-digit key + SIREN) upon tax registration; request an intra-community VAT number and EORI number for customs via the DGFIP's Service des Impôts des Entreprises.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Register the SARL with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce via the online Registre National des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); obtain the K-bis extraction, SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) identification codes; the DGFIP automatically allocates a French VAT number (format FR + 2-digit key + SIREN) upon tax registration; request an intra-community VAT number and EORI number for customs via the DGFIP's Service des Impôts des Entreprises.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Not a check-the-box jurisdiction. Available elections: (a) option for SARLs meeting CGI Art. 239 bis AA conditions to be taxed quasi-transparently under personal income tax (IR); (b) VAT group registration (assujetti résultant d'un groupement) under CGI Art. 257 C; (c) corporate income tax consolidation (régime d'intégration fiscale) under CGI Art. 223 A for French wholly-owned subsidiaries; (d) DAC6/MDR disclosure for hybrid mismatch arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established foreign suppliers: EUR 0 threshold (VAT registration required from the first taxable transaction unless the Import One-Stop-Shop applies for consignment imports ≤ EUR 150). French-established SMEs: annual turnover exemption threshold of EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services (CGI Art. 293 B). Intra-EC distance sales threshold: EUR 10,000 for supplies to French consumers if the supplier is EU-established.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU third countries are zero-rated (exonération à l'export) under CGI Art. 262 II, provided the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by the seller or on its behalf within one year (three months for means of transport). Supplies of services directly related to exported goods (including printing and production of collectible cards for export) qualify for 0% when the services are linked to tangible property placed under external transit or customs-union regime. Intra-Community supplies to a VAT-registered EU customer are taxed at 0% subject to valid French and customer VAT IDs and EC sales list filing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Échanges de Marchandises via DELTA / DAE)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill issued to the French exporter
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent bank payment confirmation)
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list referencing the DAE and destination incoterms

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting will be phased in from 1 September 2026 (large and mid-sized enterprises) and 1 September 2027 (small enterprises). The DGFIP operates the Chorus Pro public portal; e-invoices must use structured XML formats (Factur-X, UBL or CII) and be transmitted via certified Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires using API or direct EDI. No digital fiscal stamp or real-time transaction clearance; periodic e-reporting of transaction data to DGFIP.

DGFIP may claim late interest at 0.20% per month plus penalties of 40% (mauvaise foi) or 80% (manoeuvre frauduleuse) where export evidence is insufficient or fraudulent. In aggravated tax fraud cases criminal penalties can reach EUR 750,000 fine and ten years imprisonment. Export zero-rating may be retrospectively clawed back from the first transaction in the reassessment period; the ordinary reassessment statute is six years, extended to ten years for unreported foreign activity.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full capital and current account convertibility applies to EUR as the domestic currency within the eurozone; France imposes no general exchange control authorization for inbound equity or outbound trade payments. Direct investment and cross-border financial transfers are unrestricted but must comply with statistical reporting to Banque de France (balance-of-payments declarations such as the 'Déclaration de flux' for transactions exceeding €15,000 per calendar month or as otherwise threshold-triggered). Profit remittance via dividends is freely convertible and transferable, subject to corporate income tax withholding (28% standard rate, reduced to 0-15% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or applicable double tax treaty) and to anti-artificial-avoidance rules. No administrative pre-approval is required for FX conversion, but banks require full underlying documentation for source-of-funds verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement applies in France; there is no mandatory period for converting export receipts into EUR and no prescribed conversion ratio. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency bank accounts subject to Banque de France statistical declaration obligations for balance-of-payments reporting when aggregate flows exceed the regulatory threshold (€15,000 per month). For a French SARL active in collectible trading card exports, receipts denominated in USD or GBP can remain offshore or in EUR-denominated accounts without forced conversion, though corporate treasury policy should match currency exposure with permitted hedging instruments.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting within the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism; prudential oversight delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); foreign exchange and balance-of-payments administration governed by the French Monetary and Financial Code and EU Capital Movement Directives.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. An EU-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI), including a French entity licensed by the ACPR or an EU entity passporting into France under PSD2, can open accounts and issue IBANs without local banking branch presence; such EMIs are viable for receivables collection from global card buyers and for low-value disbursements. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EU are not permitted to provide payment accounts or clearing services to a French SARL without a passport or an agent/licensing arrangement in France; use of such providers creates AML/CTF and regulatory compliance risk and should be avoided for core treasury operations. All EMIs must perform Beneficial Ownership (UBO) declarations and may restrict settlement in high-risk jurisdictions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) or foreign holding company in the ownership chain, especially in low- or non-cooperative tax jurisdictions

Root cause: Non-resident ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) or foreign holding company in the ownership chain, especially in low- or non-cooperative tax jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Extrait K-bis (less than 3 months old), certified articles of association (statuts), full UBO declaration (Déclaration de bénéficiaires effectifs) filed with the Registre du Commerce, organizational chart of the ownership group, certified passports of UBOs, sworn source-of-wealth documentation (bank statements, audited financial statements, tax returns) covering the capital contribution, and a written group structure rationale letter.
High-volume or high-value cross-border wire traffic in the first 6–12 months inconsistent with projected business volume or target market geography

Root cause: High-volume or high-value cross-border wire traffic in the first 6–12 months inconsistent with projected business volume or target market geography

Defense evidence: Commercial contracts with distributors and marketplaces, platform payout statements (e.g., Stripe, Amazon), proforma invoices and purchase orders, logistics and customs documentation, carrier waybills, and a treasury narrative explaining trade flows; all documents must be consistent with the NAF/APE code declared at registration.
Business description involving holographic printing, security features, or collectible intellectual property that appears unusual for the bank's standard merchant risk taxonomy

Root cause: Business description involving holographic printing, security features, or collectible intellectual property that appears unusual for the bank's standard merchant risk taxonomy

Defense evidence: Business plan detailing manufacturing and sourcing partners, proofs of intellectual property ownership or licensing agreements (trademark certificates, licensing contracts), samples of registered trading card product lines, website and marketplace storefront evidence, and confirmation that no special statutory license is required (with legal opinion if requested).
Rapid onboarding of multiple signatories or frequent subsequent addition of mandate holders (dual-control access requests)

Root cause: Rapid onboarding of multiple signatories or frequent subsequent addition of mandate holders (dual-control access requests)

Defense evidence: Notarized board or shareholder resolutions approving signatory appointments, certified copies of identity documents of all mandate holders, updated extrait K-bis, and a signed internal authorization matrix documenting segregation of duties and maximum transaction limits.
Use of offshore digital EMI or intermediary payment processors creating an opaque settlement trail before funds reach the French bank

Root cause: Use of offshore digital EMI or intermediary payment processors creating an opaque settlement trail before funds reach the French bank

Defense evidence: Regulatory status evidence of the EMI (licence number and regulatory register), the contractual master service agreement with the payment processor, complete transaction-level reporting/ledger exports, and a written undertaking to settle exclusively through the French corporate bank account after the onboarding phase.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant injection of equity capital into the French SARL is executed through SWIFT MT103 with Sender-to-Reference purpose code indicating 'Capital contribution – constitution or increase of capital' (OECD purpose category E001/E002; French balance-of-payments code '221' for direct investment); the remitting bank must be a corresponding bank of the receiving French bank, and the beneficiary account should be a specifically designated Capital Escrow Account pending legal completion of the share capital increase. Verification includes the notarized minutes of the shareholder resolution, the updated statuts, the deposit certificate (attestation de dépôt de fonds) from the escrow bank or notary, and the UBO declaration. Inbound transfers from non-EU jurisdictions exceeding €50,000 are systematically escalated for source-of-funds review; funds should originate from the corporate account of the direct shareholder and be supported by audited financial statements or dividend certificates to demonstrate legitimate provenance. No FX approval is required for EUR-denominated injections, but USD or other converted currencies must be swapped under transparent execution with no obscuring conversion.Outbound disbursements for procurement of printing materials, manufacturing services, and third-party production use SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for open-account supplier payments and MT202/MT202COV for interbank cover payments; large purchase orders or new supplier relationships should be supported by a Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700 series) to mitigate counterparty risk, particularly for holographic film and security substrate suppliers. Intra-EU payments in EUR execute via the SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) scheme settled through TARGET2, providing same-day clearing; non-EUR trade payments (USD, GBP, CNY) settle via correspondent banking through CHIPS/Fedwire, with daylight overdraft and FX processing determined by the French corporate bank's conversion desk. For recurring royalty or licensing disbursements to non-resident rights holders, execute MT103 with the appropriate purpose code (e.g., 'IP royalties') and retain withholding tax certificates (attestation de précompte) and double-tax-treaty relief forms (formulaires Cerfa 5000-series) in the treasury file to ensure clean auditability.Deliverable FX Forward (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNY) — executed with the same-tier French corporate bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex, available only with documented underlying trade exposure.FX Swap (swap de change) for short-term liquidity conversion between EUR and export proceeds currencies, permitted for treasury cash management.Cross-Currency Swap (fixed-to-fixed or floating-to-fixed) for hedging multi-year non-EUR monetary liabilities or receivables.Vanilla European FX Options (EUR/USD puts/calls) allowed for hedging of forecasted export revenues, subject to the bank's derivatives eligibility and suitability assessment.Flexible Forward (window forward) — permitted as a variation of the deliverable forward, allowing partial monthly drawings against a single trade flow.

Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EU are not permitted to provide payment accounts or clearing services to a French SARL without a passport or an agent/licensing arrangement in France; use of such providers creates AML/CTF and regulatory compliance risk and should be avoided for core treasury operations.
