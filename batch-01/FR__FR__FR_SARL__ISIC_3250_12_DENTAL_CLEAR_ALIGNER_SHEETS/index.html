Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP); online tax services and filings are administered through impots.gouv.fr. Business formation is filed through the INPI-operated Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises.

E-government filing portal

Register the SARL through the Guichet unique. The registration process results in assignment of a SIREN and SIRET and entry in the Registre national des entreprises; the company receives its French VAT number when registered for VAT by the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). A foreign company without a French establishment applies to the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). A non-EU business generally appoints a French fiscal representative unless an applicable legal exemption or mutual-assistance arrangement applies. Confirm the VAT number through the EU VIES system before cross-border use.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Eligible SARLs may elect income-tax transparency (impôt sur le revenu, IR) for a limited period, generally up to five financial years, subject to statutory conditions. A family-owned SARL conducting an eligible activity may elect IR without that five-year limit while the conditions continue to be met. France has no U.S.-style check-the-box election; the IR election is not automatic and must be made under prescribed procedures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts a French-established business from VAT registration when it conducts taxable transactions outside the small-business franchise regime. The franchise en base de TVA may apply below the applicable annual thresholds; the general thresholds are EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for services, subject to statutory tolerance thresholds and current-year legislative changes. A business must assess its activity and applicable rules rather than assume that manufacturing or mixed supplies qualify for the goods threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the EU may be exempt with credit (effectively 0% VAT) when statutory conditions are met and the supplier retains evidence that the goods left the EU. Intra-EU supplies of goods may be exempt when the customer is VAT-identified in another Member State, the goods are transported there, and reporting and documentary conditions are satisfied. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore; place-of-supply rules, customer status, service type, and any applicable exclusions determine treatment.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the goods, parties, VAT treatment, and transaction
  • Export customs declaration and MRN
  • Customs confirmation of exit from the EU, such as the electronic exit result or IE599 message where available
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR, or carrier delivery records
  • Purchase order, payment evidence, and correspondence supporting the transaction and destination
  • For qualifying intra-EU supplies: customer VAT-number validation, transport evidence, and required recapitulative statement reporting

France does not generally require real-time tax-authority clearance or digital stamping of domestic VAT invoices. The domestic e-invoicing and e-reporting reform is being phased in: businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance and transaction-reporting obligations apply according to the statutory timetable and business size, with the final phase scheduled for 1 September 2027. In-scope invoices must use the prescribed formats and approved platforms or the public invoicing portal, as applicable. Verify implementation dates and platform rules before launch.

The supplier must substantiate the export or other exemption and retain records for the applicable statutory period. If the conditions or evidence are insufficient, the tax authority may reassess VAT, interest, and applicable penalties. Intentional fraud or false documentation may attract enhanced tax penalties and criminal sanctions. France imposes no general 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds; the specified deadline is therefore not applicable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a regime of full capital and current account convertibility for the Euro. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are unrestricted following standard corporate tax compliance, beneficial ownership registration under the French Transparency Act, and AML monitoring. No capital controls or material FX transfer hurdles apply to intra-EU or OECD transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the central bank. Export receipts must be declared to customs authorities (Douane) within one month of shipment. Companies may retain and utilize proceeds freely after declaration, subject only to standard AML transaction monitoring and reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. French EMI licensing by ACPR permits cross-border electronic money services within the EEA but imposes strict capital, liquidity, reserve, and AML obligations. Offshore digital EMIs face regulatory barriers for non-EEA activities and require full local compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) is mandatory for companies with annual revenue or total assets exceeding €400 million; simplified documentation applies to SMEs with revenue below €400 million but still requires a basic TP file if cross-border transactions exceed €50,000 per category.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the general anti-abuse rule (Article 64 of the French Tax Code) and the doctrine of 'substance over form' apply. The French tax administration may challenge arrangements lacking economic substance, particularly for shell companies or conduit entities. The 'abuse of law' procedure (abus de droit) can recharacterize transactions if the sole purpose is tax avoidance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty of up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (increased to 7 years for aggravated circumstances); civil penalty of 80% of the evaded tax plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they have committed fraud or mismanagement; travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed; in severe cases, imprisonment up to 5 years
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions under the abuse of law doctrine; denial of treaty benefits; imposition of 80% penalty on recharacterized amounts; potential criminal charges for tax fraud

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly liable for the company's tax debts if the company is deemed a sham; personal assets may be seized; travel bans and prohibition from managing companies may be imposed
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income by the tax authority; 40% penalty for lack of documentation or inaccurate documentation; interest on underpaid tax; potential criminal liability for fraudulent transfer pricing

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's length transactions may be held personally liable for the resulting tax adjustments and penalties; in cases of fraud, criminal prosecution may follow
AML / Financial Irregularities

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering; administrative penalties for non-compliance with French AML regulations (e.g., failure to report suspicious transactions)

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable for AML violations; personal fines and imprisonment; travel bans and seizure of personal assets; disqualification from managing companies
