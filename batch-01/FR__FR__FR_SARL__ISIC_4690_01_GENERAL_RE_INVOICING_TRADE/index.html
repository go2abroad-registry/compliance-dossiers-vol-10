Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) kept by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the competent commercial court for the registered office; filing is made electronically through the INPI national single window at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 governing the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); Articles R.223-1 et seq. and R.123-30 et seq. on RCS registration formalities; Decree No. 2009-1085 of 19 August 2009 on business formalities; Order of 28 October 2021 establishing the single business window (guichet unique).

Formation / constitutional filings

The unified business-creation declaration (M0 form) combined with the signed statutory articles (statuts) and the supporting deposit and publication certificates, filed through the guichet unique.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital applies to a French SARL under Article L.223-2 of the Code de commerce; the articles may set the capital at €1 or higher, with €1,000 recommended for this re-invoicing hub. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and at least 20% of each cash portion must be paid up on incorporation, with the balance to be liberated within five years. All cash funds are deposited in a blocked bank account, with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations or a notary, and are released to the Manager only upon presentation of the Kbis registration extract.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique — taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; company registration/registry portal: guichet-entreprises.fr / infogreffe.fr; French VAT is administered by DGFiP via the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE), with large taxpayers serviced by the Direction des grandes entreprises (DGE); customs and export duties are administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) through the Guichet Unique du Dédouanement (GUN) / operational import-export platform; the national e-invoicing infrastructure is the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF), interoperable with certified Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires (PDPs).

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the SARL through guichet-entreprises.fr; obtain INSEE SIREN and SIRET numbers, the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés) extract (Kbis) and the NAF Rev. 2 activity code matching the ISIC 4690 re-invoicing scope, i.e., 46.90B (other non-specialised wholesale trade). 2. Register the TIN/TVA number: file the commencement of taxable activity declaration (M0/M1) and apply for the intra-Community VAT identification number (numéro d'identification TVA intracommunautaire) in the format FR + 11 digits from the competent DGFiP service; the application is generally processed within one month and the number is evidenced by the 'extrait d'immatriculation TVA'. 3. For a re-invoicing hub buying from non-established suppliers, French VAT applies by reverse charge (art. 283-1 CGI) so the French SARL itself is the taxable debtor and must be VAT-registered from the first taxable transaction; there is no registration threshold for foreign suppliers selling into France unless they elect the importer-of-record or franchise regime. 4. Obtain a customs EORI number (format FR + SIREN) from DGDDI, free of charge, through the Guichet Unique du Dédouanement, to lodge export declarations and customs transit formalities. 5. Register as principal for the e-invoicing reform: subscribe to a certified PDP or use the PPF, and complete technical onboarding before the applicable issuance/reception deadlines (1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises; 1 September 2027 for SMEs/VSEs). 6. Where the SARL acts for foreign principals under a commissionaire or re-invoicing model, appoint responsible officers and maintain SIC (Système d'Information Comptable) and treasury files evidencing that commercial risk, inventory risk and invoicing authority are genuinely held by the SARL; the DGFiP may recharacterise the hub as an artificial interposition if no substance is demonstrated. 7. Foreign traders without French establishment that wish to use the SARL as a strategic re-invoicing vehicle must not merely 'pass through' invoices; they must appoint a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) approved by DGFiP only where the foreign principal itself incurs French VAT liabilities; the SARL's own French VAT number suffices for its independent resell operations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — An ordinary commercial SARL incorporated under French law is by default a taxable person subject to l'impôt sur les sociétés (corporate income tax) under art. 206-1 CGI on its worldwide profits derived from French business activity, unless the profits are attributable to a foreign permanent establishment, in which case France's territorial limitation in treaty/domestic law applies only to the foreign PE (art. 209-I CGI). The SARL is also a normal VAT taxable person without the publicity or pass-through attributes of a partnership; it is fully liable for French VAT on its taxable supplies, imports and intra-Community acquisitions, and can recover input VAT in accordance with art. 271 CGI. Where the SARL acts for foreign principals under a commissionaire or re-invoicing model, appoint responsible officers and maintain SIC (Système d'Information Comptable) and treasury files evidencing that commercial risk, inventory risk and invoicing authority are genuinely held by the SARL; the DGFiP may recharacterise the hub as an artificial interposition if no substance is demonstrated. 7. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — An ordinary commercial SARL incorporated under French law is by default a taxable person subject to l'impôt sur les sociétés (corporate income tax) under art. 206-1 CGI on its worldwide profits derived from French business activity, unless the profits are attributable to a foreign permanent establishment, in which case France's territorial limitation in treaty/domestic law applies only to the foreign PE (art. 209-I CGI). The SARL is also a normal VAT taxable person without the publicity or pass-through attributes of a partnership; it is fully liable for French VAT on its taxable supplies, imports and intra-Community acquisitions, and can recover input VAT in accordance with art. 271 CGI. For ISIC 4690 re-invoicing operations, the SARL is the legal principal in the chain and its taxable margin (resale price minus cost of goods) is subject to French entity-level taxation.

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. The available statutory elections and transparency mechanisms for a foreign-owned French SARL engaged in general re-invoicing trade are: (1) family SARL income-tax option under art. 239 bis AA CGI, restricted to qualified family-held companies and therefore generally unavailable for an arms-length commercial re-invoicing hub; (2) tax consolidation election (régime d'intégration fiscale) under art. 223 A CGI for a French group holding at least 95 percent of the SARL's share capital and voting rights, allowing the group to pool French taxable results and offsets; (3) ordinary IS regime versus the reduced IS regime for small and mid-size enterprises when statutory turnover and ownership conditions are met; (4) VAT filing elections, including monthly versus quarterly declarations and the exporter's election for monthly VAT credit refunds (remboursement du crédit de TVA); (5) OSS (Union/Non-Union One Stop Shop) and IOSS elections for distance sales and for imported consignments valued at EUR 150 or less; (6) anti-hybrid and mismatch treatment: France transposed ATAD and ATAD2 into art. 209 and 212 bis CGI, so no elective 'check-the-box' carve-out is recognised; a French SARL cannot be disregarded merely because a foreign affiliate treats it as transparent, and any mismatch that produces double non-taxation or a deduction without corresponding inclusion is neutralised by the French administrative authority; the taxpayer may, however, elect to be treated as a transparent entity only in jurisdictions whose domestic law so provides, and must disclose such mismatches in the annual tax return (file 2257) to avoid the 80 percent abuse-of-right penalty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France applies the franchise en base de TVA (small-business exemption) at EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for supplies of services, with tolerance (reduced) thresholds of EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,000 respectively; the exemption is mandatory below those ceilings and cannot be waived, but it does not apply to imports, intra-Community acquisitions, or to taxable persons conducting export, triangulation or distance-selling operations. A wholesale re-invoicing and export hub under ISIC 4690 is therefore required to register for VAT from its first taxable transaction even if its turnover is below the franchise ceilings, because the franchise is excluded for cross-border supplies, goods held on consignment and services connected to exports; the hub should register with the SIE immediately upon incorporation and maintain VAT registration irrespective of the turnover thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

1. Exports of goods outside the European Union (art. 262-I-1° CGI): the supply is entitled to a 0 percent VAT rate if the French seller transfers ownership of the goods, physically dispatches them from French territory (or from a French customs warehouse) to a destination outside the EU, and holds evidence of the export declaration (DAU/EX1) and actual physical exit from the EU within three months from the date of supply; failure to evidence physical exit within that window triggers a retroactive 20 percent VAT assessment. 2. Intra-EU deliveries (art. 262 ter CGI): the supply is exempt with 0 percent output VAT where the customer provides a valid TVA intra-Community identification number and the French hub files the recapitulative statement reporting the sale. 3. Services directly connected to exported goods (art. 262-II CGI), including transport, handling, inspection, insurance and ancillary commission services rendered to a non-EU principal, qualify for the export exemption where the goods are actually exported outside the EU. 4. For pure offshore re-invoicing and intermediary commissions: where the French SARL renders services to a foreign entrepreneur and the place of supply is outside France under art. 259-4 CGI (B2B main rule, customer-established basis), no French VAT is chargeable (outside scope/0 percent equivalent); the hub must maintain a business attestation from the foreign customer confirming its VAT status and business location. 5. Low-value imported consignments (≤ EUR 150) may be sold through the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) without French import VAT, followed by an exempt onward supply; alternative structuring (normal import clearance then domestic sale) triggers import VAT and standard French treatment. 6. A zero-rating claim cannot be based on mere re-invoicing, paper transfers, or triangular invoice flows if the goods never leave the EU or the French hub does not legally act as principal; diversion, re-importation or abandonment of the goods destroys the exemption and invokes the penalty regime.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation / DAU with EX-status) lodged in the Guichet Unique du Dédouanement and endorsed by DGDDI, with the MRU/out-turn reference
  • Bill of Lading (Connaissement maritime) or equivalent transport document (Air Waybill / CMR road consignment note) evidencing dispatch from France to a destination outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign payment evidence — SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wire confirmation, or a bank attestation showing receipt of export proceeds in the French hub's account within the contractual payment window
  • Commercial invoice, underlying purchase order and sales contract establishing legal ownership transfer from the SARL to the foreign customer
  • Transport and insurance documentation (packing list, weight note, container tracking, insurance certificates) confirming the physical movement of goods out of the European Union
  • For intra-EU supplies: recapitulative statement (État récapitulatif de TVA) and the customer's validated TVA intra-Community number
  • Customs or licensed-agent mandates (power of attorney, forwarding instructions, EX1 follow-up certificates) if export clearance is performed by a third-party broker
  • IOSS/OSS registration references and import VAT evidence when low-value consignments are routed through the Import One-Stop-Shop
  • Intercompany documentation for related-party re-invoicing, including transfer pricing documentation for commission rates and margin allocation to support the economic substance of the French hub

Mandatory e-invoicing under the French facturation électronique reform (loi de finances 2024/2025; art. 289 CGI): every French-established taxable person must be able to receive structured electronic invoices through a certified PDP or the PPF from 1 September 2026; issuance is phased, with large and mid-size enterprises required to issue via the PPF/PDP from 1 September 2026 and SMEs/VSEs from 1 September 2027. The regime applies to all domestic B2B transactions and to B2C/business-outside-France transactions through e-reporting of transaction data; there is no per-invoice real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping, but each PDP must transmit invoice data to the PPF within one working day, and the PPF maintains a national invoice directory and data monitoring facility. Foreign suppliers subject to French VAT (including OSS/IOSS registrants) must comply with equivalent structured-data reporting for their French supplies. For a re-invoicing hub, the e-invoice must carry the mandatory fields: French TVA number, customer's TVA number (where B2B), VAT breakdown at 20 percent or 0 percent with the appropriate exemption code, and, for exports, the customs declaration references and transport evidence.

French export zero-rating is subject to joint audit scrutiny by DGFiP (VAT audit) and DGDDI (customs control). If the export declaration, bill of lading, payment evidence or exit proof is missing, inaccurate or fraudulent, the 20 percent VAT is retroactively clawed back, plus interest at 0.20 percent per month (intérêt de retard) under art. 1727 CGI. In cases of bad faith or deliberate concealment, the 40 percent penalty for mauvaise foi applies under art. 1729 CGI; for fraud or abuse of right the penalty rises to 80 percent, and the auditor may apply the fiscal abuse procedure under art. L.64 LPF to disregard artificial re-invoicing arrangements. Customs offences (code des douanes) carry confiscation of goods and a fine ranging from one to five times the tax evaded; criminal tax evasion under art. 1741 CGI exposes the natural-person directors to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, and the SARL itself is subject to civil recovery, denial of export VAT refunds (remboursement du crédit de TVA), forfeiture of the monthly-refund election, publication of sanctions and de-registration from the PPF/PDP ecosystem. The general DGFiP assessment window is three years (art. L.169 LPF), extended to ten years for concealed or fraudulent activity (art. L.176 LPF), and the bank and treasury records (SWIFT confirmations, IBAN statements, FX contracts) supporting the export proceeds must be retained for at least ten years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has no general foreign-exchange control regime. Current-account payments, including imports, exports, services, interest, dividends, and ordinary commercial settlements, are generally freely transferable. Capital movements, shareholder funding, intercompany loans, acquisitions, and profit distributions are also generally permitted, subject to anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions, tax, corporate-law, beneficial-ownership, and statistical-reporting requirements. A SARL may remit legally distributable profits, interest, and loan principal to non-resident owners or lenders, provided accounts are approved, corporate approvals are documented, withholding-tax and transfer-pricing rules are satisfied, and the bank receives an adequate source-of-funds and purpose file. Certain foreign investments and acquisitions involving sensitive French sectors may require prior notification or authorization under French foreign-investment-control rules. Cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more across the EU external border require customs declaration, and banks may request supporting documentation for unusual cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, convert them into euros, or repatriate them within a prescribed period. Export receipts may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be netted through approved banking arrangements, subject to contractual, accounting, tax, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering controls. Exporters must retain commercial invoices, contracts, transport and customs evidence, payment records, and VAT documentation. Banks may request export evidence before processing or releasing funds, and Banque de France statistical or balance-of-payments reporting may apply to designated transactions or reporting populations. No universal mandatory conversion ratio or statutory export-collection deadline applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, including its Foreign Exchange and Balance of Payments reporting functions; prudential and payment-services supervision is principally conducted by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, while customs and trade-flow controls are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection, SEPA transfers, multi-currency wallets, and routine supplier payments; Low as the sole banking architecture for a trade re-invoicing hub requiring capital deposit, documentary trade finance, letters of credit, substantial foreign-currency turnover, or access to cash-management credit. An EU-authorized electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide regulated payment services under its authorization and passporting rights, but it does not provide a bank deposit in the legal sense, may be required to safeguard customer funds rather than maintain them as deposits, and may not offer credit, deposit-taking, documentary letters of credit, or certain treasury products without separate authorization. Offshore digital EMIs without EU authorization, passporting, or an appropriately licensed EU agent should not be used for core operating receipts or material cross-border settlement. The SARL must complete the EMI's legal-entity, UBO, source-of-funds, transaction-profile, sanctions, and expected-volume checks.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Foreign ownership chain is opaque, contains nominee shareholders, uses trusts or layered holding companies, or lacks a clearly identifiable natural-person UBO.

Root cause: Foreign ownership chain is opaque, contains nominee shareholders, uses trusts or layered holding companies, or lacks a clearly identifiable natural-person UBO.

Defense evidence: Current French corporate extract from the Registre national des entreprises, articles of association, shareholder register, complete ownership chart to natural-person UBOs, certificates of incorporation for foreign entities, registers of directors and shareholders, passports and address evidence for UBOs and directors, trust or nominee documentation where applicable, and signed beneficial-ownership certification.
Business model is described as re-invoicing, intermediary trading, or offshore commerce without sufficient commercial substance or clear value creation.

Root cause: Business model is described as re-invoicing, intermediary trading, or offshore commerce without sufficient commercial substance or clear value creation.

Defense evidence: Detailed business memorandum, product and supplier descriptions, customer and supplier contracts, purchase orders, commercial invoices, logistics and title-transfer model, Incoterms, customs procedures, margin policy, agency or principal analysis, organizational chart, staffing and office evidence, website and marketing materials, and a 12-month transaction forecast by country, currency, counterparty, and payment type.
Expected flows involve high-value imports or exports, rapid pass-through of funds, third-party payments, unusual payment corridors, or transactions inconsistent with the declared business profile.

Root cause: Expected flows involve high-value imports or exports, rapid pass-through of funds, third-party payments, unusual payment corridors, or transactions inconsistent with the declared business profile.

Defense evidence: Board-approved treasury and payment policy, counterparty master file, invoice-to-payment reconciliation process, purchase orders, bills of lading, customs declarations, certificates of origin, proof of delivery, bank statements, source-and-use-of-funds schedule, transaction-monitoring controls, exception-approval matrix, and documented prohibition of unrelated third-party payments.
Exposure to sanctioned countries, sanctioned persons, dual-use goods, restricted commodities, or counterparties in high-risk jurisdictions.

Root cause: Exposure to sanctioned countries, sanctioned persons, dual-use goods, restricted commodities, or counterparties in high-risk jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Sanctions-screening policy covering EU, UN, French, UK, and relevant U.S. restrictions, counterparty screening records, end-user and end-use certificates, product classification, export-control review, denied-party controls, shipment-route analysis, licenses or authorizations, and written escalation procedures.
Insufficient evidence for capital injection, shareholder loans, dividend payments, interest, or other cross-border related-party transfers.

Root cause: Insufficient evidence for capital injection, shareholder loans, dividend payments, interest, or other cross-border related-party transfers.

Defense evidence: Shareholder resolutions, capital subscription documents, bank capital-deposit certificate, executed loan agreement, loan pricing and maturity terms, beneficial-owner source-of-wealth and source-of-funds evidence, repayment schedule, transfer-pricing support, board approval, French accounting entries, tax analysis, and proof of withholding-tax compliance where applicable.
Tax-residency, permanent-establishment, VAT, customs, or transfer-pricing inconsistencies between the payment activity and the declared French operations.

Root cause: Tax-residency, permanent-establishment, VAT, customs, or transfer-pricing inconsistencies between the payment activity and the declared French operations.

Defense evidence: French tax registration, VAT number, customs EORI registration, tax-residency certificates, VAT and customs process map, intercompany agreements, transfer-pricing policy, master and local files where applicable, permanent-establishment analysis, management-and-control evidence, and filings or correspondence with relevant authorities.
Incomplete identity records, expired passports, inconsistent addresses, non-certified translations, or inability of signatories to explain the business and payment flows.

Root cause: Incomplete identity records, expired passports, inconsistent addresses, non-certified translations, or inability of signatories to explain the business and payment flows.

Defense evidence: Current identity documents, recent address evidence, specimen signatures, powers of attorney, certified French or English translations, corporate authorization matrix, signatory interview preparation file, and a concise transaction-flow presentation matching the onboarding questionnaire.
Adverse media, politically exposed person status, criminal or regulatory history, or unexplained wealth indicators involving a UBO, director, or key counterparty.

Root cause: Adverse media, politically exposed person status, criminal or regulatory history, or unexplained wealth indicators involving a UBO, director, or key counterparty.

Defense evidence: Independent adverse-media review, legal and regulatory explanations, court or settlement documents, PEP declaration, enhanced source-of-wealth file, source-of-funds evidence, compliance-office approval, risk-mitigation plan, and senior-management sign-off where required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For SARL formation, subscribe the capital into a French bank's restricted capital-deposit account before registration, using a transfer from an account held by the subscriber or documented investor. The bank should receive the draft articles of association, subscription schedule, identity and UBO documents, corporate approvals, source-of-funds evidence, and intended-use statement. After registration, provide the Kbis extract and use the bank's certificate to release the funds to the operational account. For subsequent equity injections, route funds through SEPA Credit Transfer in euros or SWIFT cross-border payment in the investment currency, clearly identifying the French SARL, investor, amount, currency, and purpose as equity contribution or shareholder loan. France does not impose a universal FX purpose-code system; banks use internal payment-purpose fields and may request a capital subscription agreement, loan agreement, board resolution, and accounting treatment. Verify beneficiary name and IBAN, intermediary-bank requirements, sanctions screening, foreign-investment-control exposure, tax consequences, and any applicable Banque de France balance-of-payments or statistical reporting. Do not use unexplained third-party remitters or cash funding.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for euro-area suppliers where appropriate, and SWIFT customer credit transfer for non-SEPA or foreign-currency settlements. MT103 is the traditional SWIFT customer-payment format; banks are migrating to ISO 20022 correspondent-payment messages, so the current bank-specific format must be confirmed. For documentary trade, use a commercial letter of credit under UCP 600, with documentary presentation through the issuing, advising, confirming, and nominated banks. Require invoices, transport documents, insurance documents where applicable, packing lists, certificates of origin, customs data, sanctions and export-control checks, and segregation of duties. Reconcile each payment to an approved purchase order, invoice, shipment, and counterparty record. For open-account trade, establish counterparty limits, payment terms, dual approval, beneficiary-change call-back controls, and trade-document retention. Confirm that any bank guarantee, standby letter of credit, documentary collection, or supply-chain-finance product is within the bank's mandate and supported by underlying commercial documentation.Deliverable FX forwards for forecast or committed receivables and payables, subject to documented hedging purpose, counterparty approval, suitability assessment, ISDA or local master documentation, collateral terms where applicable, and compliance with EU derivatives rules.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies that are non-convertible or subject to local settlement restrictions, provided the product is legally available, the settlement index and fixing source are documented, the counterparty is properly authorized, and the SARL understands cash-settlement and basis risk.FX swaps for short-term liquidity and currency-mismatch management, subject to documented maturities, counterparty limits, pricing controls, and applicable EMIR reporting, risk-mitigation, and clearing requirements.Plain-vanilla FX options and collars for asymmetric protection, only after suitability, premium, valuation, accounting, and termination-risk review; complex or leveraged structures should be prohibited unless separately approved by the board and treasury policy.EUR foreign-currency accounts, natural hedging through matching currency receipts and payments, and contractual currency clauses as non-derivative risk-management tools. Hedging must be linked to identifiable exposures, independently confirmed, periodically marked to market, and recorded under the applicable French accounting framework or adopted IFRS policy.
