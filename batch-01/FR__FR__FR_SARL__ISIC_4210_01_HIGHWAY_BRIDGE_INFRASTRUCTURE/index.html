Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; the Articles of Association may set the capital from €1 upward. The full share capital must be subscribed, and each share must be allocated by name. At least 20% of cash contributions must be effectively paid-up at incorporation unless full payment is stipulated in the Articles, with the balance paid within five years; cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked bank or notary account, released only upon RCS registration, while in-kind contributions are fully paid-up and verified by a commissaire aux apports where required.

Funding & unlock path: Construction site clearance requires: (i) fire safety certification from SDIS for site offices and worker accommodation; (ii) structural-load inspection certificate issued by a licensed bureau de contrôle for temporary works, falsework, and lifting plans; (iii) worker occupational-health validation under the Code du travail, including the Plan Particulier de Sécurité et de Protection de la Santé (PPSPS) approved by the SPS coordinator; and (iv) inspection certificates for cranes and lifting equipment under Decree No. 2009-483 and electrical conformity for temporary installations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French tax administration; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; business registration via Guichet Unique: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a French SARL must file a single incorporation application through the Guichet Unique with certified articles of association, proof of legal existence from home country, and due-diligence KYC documentation. Upon completion, INSEE assigns the SIREN (enterprise identifier), the company is registered with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), and the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) allocates the definitive French tax ID (tax identification number) and, where applicable, an intra-community VAT number (FRxxxxxxxxx). Foreign companies without French establishment undertaking taxable supplies in France must submit a Form 10870 to the SIE to obtain a French VAT identification number before commencing taxable operations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a French SARL must file a single incorporation application through the Guichet Unique with certified articles of association, proof of legal existence from home country, and due-diligence KYC documentation. Upon completion, INSEE assigns the SIREN (enterprise identifier), the company is registered with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), and the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) allocates the definitive French tax ID (tax identification number) and, where applicable, an intra-community VAT number (FRxxxxxxxxx). Foreign companies without French establishment undertaking taxable supplies in France must submit a Form 10870 to the SIE to obtain a French VAT identification number before commencing taxable operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is by default subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level. Statutory options include: (1) election for income tax as a pass-through under CGI article 239bis AA (family SARL) or article 239bis AB (young small SARL), subject to strict turnover and shareholder conditions; (2) option for French group tax consolidation under CGI article 223A for corporate groups; (3) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under CGI article 256C(2) for closely linked French entities. France does not maintain a 'check-the-box' regime; hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are statutorily binding.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For French-established small businesses, the VAT franchise (small business exemption) applies up to EUR 85,000 annual turnover for goods and EUR 37,500 for services (2024/2025 statutory thresholds). Foreign-established entities supplying goods or services in France have no de minimis threshold and are required to register for French VAT immediately upon commencing any taxable supply, subject to the EU EUR 10,000 cross-border distance-selling threshold for final consumers if not otherwise subject to tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods to destinations outside the European Union are zero-rated under CGI article 262(1) provided the exporter proves dispatch/transport out of France to a third-country destination. Intra-Community supplies to an EU VAT-registered customer are zero-rated under article 262-1. Services (including construction and infrastructure engineering services) are zero-rated when the place of supply is outside France under the place-of-supply rules in articles 258 to 259-0 (e.g., services relating to immovable property located in a foreign country). For construction works on a bridge physically located in France, French VAT at 20% applies; zero-rating is restricted to the professional export documentation proving overseas delivery or the foreign place of supply.

Mandatory e-invoicing for domestic French B2B transactions will apply from 1 September 2026 (large and mid-size entities) and 1 September 2027 (small enterprises), through certified dematerialisation platforms (PDP) interoperating with the Public Invoice Portal (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF). French tax law requires exporters to retain full supporting evidence for six years. Fraudulent or unfounded zero-rating claims are reclassified as domestic taxable supplies with VAT assessed at 20%, plus late payment interest (intérêt de retard) at 0.0024% per month and penalties: 10% for mere filing errors, 40% for intentional misstatement (manquement délibéré), and 80% in cases of abus de droit/fraud. Criminal fines under CGI article 1741 may reach EUR 500,000 with imprisonment. Retrospective clawback can cover a full six-year statutory limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in France for both current and capital account transactions, subject to EU-level free-movement of capital provisions. France maintains no general exchange-control authorisation for ordinary commercial trade, investment, or profit remittance. Capital account transfers, including direct capital injections, debt financing, and intra-group swings, are unrestricted for OECD/EU counterparties but must be reported to Banque de France for statistical and national-security screening under French Decree No. 2020-1675 (foreign investment in sensitive sectors). Profit remittance and dividend distribution in euros or foreign currency are unrestricted; however, dividends paid to non-residents are subject to French withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty, and repatriation may require an attestation of ownership and tax residency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose any mandatory sale, conversion, or surrender of export proceeds into euros. Exporters are permitted to retain foreign currency in designated accounts with authorised banks. All cross-border transfers exceeding EUR 12,500 must be declared to the DGDDI/Banque de France via the monthly or quarterly balance-of-payments reporting regime, including the underlying export contract reference and currency conversion details. For public-works export contracts, no specific conversion ratio is mandated; however, foreign currency receipts used to settle external subcontractors may be netted provided full transaction documentation is maintained.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as national central bank, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank (ECB); foreign exchange policy and monitoring delegated to the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) and the French Customs and Indirect Taxation Administration (DGDDI) for balance-of-payments reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. French-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) and pan-EU payment institutions are generally unable to provide the full treasury infrastructure required for an infrastructure EPC contractor, including documentary letter of credit issuance, standby credit lines, or the IBAN-level multi-currency trust structures demanded by sovereign bond-holding employers. Legal constraints include the prohibition on EMI taking deposits or granting credit in France under the French Monetary and Financial Code (CMF), and the requirement for EMI flows to sit on a licensed credit institution's balance sheet; thus, an EMI account cannot serve as the primary escrow or clearing vehicle for high-value bridge EPC payments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% standard Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) rate for fiscal years opened on or after 1 January 2022; reduced 15.0% rate applies to the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit for small enterprises meeting the turnover ceiling of EUR 10 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed by a French SARL to non-resident shareholders under Article 119 bis-2 CGI; reduced to 0% when the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive applies (minimum 10% direct shareholding held at least 2 years) and to treaty rates of 0% to 15% for residents of treaty jurisdictions; a 12.8% flat tax (PFU) applies to dividends distributed to non-resident individuals, plus 17.2% social levies unless waived under treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form 2065-SD and the full statutory financial statements must be filed within 3 months of the fiscal year-end (Article 223-1 of the CGI). For a calendar year-end SARL (31 December), the electronic filing deadline is 15 May of the following year; for non-calendar fiscal years, the deadline is the 3rd month following the closing date. Paper filings, where exceptionally authorized, are due by 3 May.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File (documentation pénalement exigible) under Article L.13 AA of the Book of Tax Procedures when the French entity's annual turnover or total assets exceed EUR 400 million. A simplified transfer pricing annex (Form 2257-SD) is mandatory when the annual turnover exceeds EUR 50 million. Country-by-Country reporting (déclaration pays par pays, Form 2258-SD) applies to French groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Infrastructure EPC contractors below the EUR 400 million threshold must still maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing evidence if they conclude cross-border intragroup transactions (management fees, guarantees, equipment leasing, engineering services).

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute as in the UAE, but enforces a substantive economic substance doctrine through: (i) the judicial concept of abus de droit (Article L.64 of the Book of Tax Procedures), under which a SARL that is artificially interposed without genuine commercial, industrial, or management substance is disregarded and reattributed to its beneficial owner; (ii) the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) of Article 209 CGI for artificial chains of transactions; (iii) the OECD/G20 Principal Purpose Test incorporated into French DTAs and the MLI; and (iv) the EU ATAD anti-hybrid and controlled-foreign-company rules. For an infrastructure EPC contractor, substance is demonstrated by local staff, engineering and project-management premises, contract negotiation authority, risk assumption, and a physical bank account receiving sovereign letter-of-credit proceeds. Absence of these indicia exposes the SARL to reclassification as a shell and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI: imprisonment up to 5 years, a fine up to EUR 500,000 (doubled to EUR 1,000,000 when aggravated), and the automatic 80% penalty increase of the evaded tax plus late interest at 0.20% per month; corporate entities incur the criminal fine multiplied by a factor of 5 (EUR 2,500,000). The court may order the publication of the sanction in official media and a ban on receiving public procurement contracts for up to 5 years — fatal for an infrastructure contractor relying on sovereign tenders.

Director personal liability: The gérant (managing director) is criminally liable as the author of the offense and may be personally fined and imprisoned; the tax authority may seek, under Article L.266 of the Book of Tax Procedures, joint and several civil liability of the director for the corporate tax debt when the fraud results from serious misconduct (agissements frauduleux); civil courts may order material seizure (saisie pénale) of personal assets, an exit/ travel ban (interdiction de quitter le territoire), and a 10-year prohibition on managing a commercial company (Article L.653-8 of the Commercial Code).
Late Filing / Omission of Tax Return (Défaut et Retard de Déclaration)

Statutory sanction: Automatic 10% tax increase when the return is filed late with tax paid on time; 40% increase when the tax is also unpaid; 80% increase for fraudulent maneuvers; monthly late interest of 0.20%; the tax authority additionally proceeds to an automatic re-assessment (taxation d'office) and rejects the carryforward of prior-year losses.

Director personal liability: The gérant may be held personally liable for the full corporate tax debt if the failure to file is coupled with irregularities rendering the corporation unable to pay (insuffisance d'actif) — Article L.651-2 of the Commercial Code; bank account freezes, travel restrictions, and a personal fine of up to EUR 15,000 can be imposed for persistent non-compliance.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation (Prix de Transfert)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 57 CGI, the tax authority reallocates the shifted profits to the French entity and reassesses the IS, the 3.3% social surcharge, and corresponding VAT; a 5% annual penalty on the reallocated profit applies when no acceptable documentation is produced; if the manipulation is deemed abusive, the 80% penalty increase and Article 1741 criminal sanctions may be triggered.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the intragroup contracts consolidating unadjusted transfer prices risk personal civil liability under Article L.223-22 of the Commercial Code for damages, joint liability for the reassessed tax if the structure was artificial, and criminal prosecution in cases of fictitious services; the French anti-fraud unit (DNEF) may reference the director to the public prosecutor.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may invoke abus de droit (Article L.64 LPF) and disregard the legal fiction: the SARL's profits are reattributed to the non-resident beneficiary, exposing the shareholder to a 80% penalty on the concealed tax (in the case of an exclusively fiscal purpose) and a personal administrative fine of EUR 10,000 to EUR 20,000; treaty benefits are denied under the Principal Purpose Test; the entity is struck off the commercial register (radiation) and its bank accounts are closed.

Director personal liability: The nominal director of an empty shell SARL incurs personal liability for the unpaid corporate tax under Article L.266 LPF, criminal exposure for abuse of company assets (abus de biens sociaux) under Article L.241-3 of the Commercial Code with imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of EUR 375,000, a travel ban, and a commercial management ban; the court may order the piercing of the corporate veil and attachment of the director's personal and foreign assets.
Anti-Money Laundering and Payment Integrity Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the French Monetary and Financial Code (L.561 et seq.), the SARL faces an administrative fine up to EUR 5,000,000 and a temporary suspension of its activity; financial intermediaries may be ordered to terminate banking relations; criminal penalties for laundering of aggravated tax fraud carry up to 10 years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 in fines for natural persons.

Director personal liability: The gérant is the designated AML compliance officer of the SARL and is personally liable for failing to establish a transaction monitoring framework; sanctions include a personal fine up to EUR 375,000, a permanent ban from directing a company, confiscation of proceeds, an exit ban, and extradition exposure where foreign bank accounts were used; mandatory reporting of suspicious transactions to Tracfin is a non-delegable personal duty of the director.
