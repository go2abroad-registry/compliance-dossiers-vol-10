Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
France is an EU Member State and a euro-area member, and the euro is fully convertible. Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union guarantees free movement of capital and transfers between Member States and third countries, subject only to non-discriminatory public-policy derogations and EU financial sanctions. France abolished its prior authorisation exchange-control system on 29 December 1989 and has not reinstated administrative FX controls. Both capital-account and current-account transactions are free of any licensing requirement, and profit remittances by a French SARL to its non-resident shareholders are freely payable without any currency conversion gate. Residual obligations are statistical and documentary only: all cross-border financial flows must be capable of transparent identification through the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting framework, and all payments must be screened against EU, UN, OFAC and French national financial sanctions lists.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
France operates no mandatory export-proceeds surrender regime. Export receipts derived from the distribution of commercial aircraft rotables and avionics surplus may be retained indefinitely in USD or any other freely convertible currency, converted into EUR, or applied directly to permitted FX hedging instruments. The sole compulsory layer is statistical reporting to Banque de France: a French resident company whose cross-border financial transactions exceed the regulatory aggregate threshold of EUR 1,000,000 over twelve consecutive months must file the monthly Liaison Financière (balance-of-payments declaration) through its resident bank or the Banque de France direct collection channel. In addition, all export movements of goods must be reflected in the customs declaration of exchanges of goods (Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens) submitted through the DELTA system. There is no conversion ratio, no mandatory conversion timeline and no certificate of validation of the receipt of export proceeds; exporters may maintain foreign-currency accounts indefinitely and repatriate funds at their own commercial discretion.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) - National central bank of the French Republic; monetary policy and foreign exchange reserve functions are exercised within the European System of Central Banks under the authority of the European Central Bank; prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment institutions is delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), an institution attached to Banque de France.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a complementary payment rail; LOW-MEDIUM as the primary escrow vehicle. ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Treezor, Mangopay, Lemonway, Qonto, Lydia/Sumeria) can legally open multi-currency IBANs and execute SWIFT MT103 transfers through partner banks under the PSD2 e-money passport. However, an EMI is prohibited from granting credit, issuing bank guarantees, or opening documentary credits, all of which are core instruments in the aviation surplus spare-parts escrow and settlement cycle. Additionally, the e-money safeguarding obligation under Article L525-4 of the French Monetary and Financial Code means that funds held by an EMI are not held as a classic bank deposit and do not legally constitute a true escrow account. Most institutional aviation counterparties and escrow instruction letters therefore require a full credit institution (bank) rather than an EMI as the escrow agent. EMIs remain workable for low-value disbursements, contractor payments and quick vendor settlements, but the main inbound aviation escrow and trade-finance accounts should be established with a Tier-1 French commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound capital injections from the non-resident parent will be credited through the French bank's receiving account centre using SWIFT MT103 in EUR or USD with the UETR (Unique End-to-end Transaction Reference) included for wire tracking. The French receiving institution expects the following compliant wire protocol: (i) tag 50K/F identifying the real originator account and complete legal name of the parent entity; (ii) tag 57D naming the receiving French bank and its SWIFT BIC (e.g., BNPAFRPP for BNP Paribas, SOGEFRPP for Société Générale, CRAGFRPP for Crédit Agricole); (iii) tag 59E containing the French SARL's full registered name and French IBAN (FR76...) prefix; (iv) tag 70 showing a purpose narrative from the Banque de France external transactions purpose matrix, preferably 'APPORT EN CAPITAL / COMPTE COURANT ASSOCIE' or its numeric counterpart '210 - INVESTISSEMENT DIRECT' for equity contributions and shareholder current-account advances respectively. There is no prior-exchange-control authorisation requirement for this inbound flow, and the French foreign-investment screening regime (IOSE/decree framework) does not apply to a wholesale distributor of non-sensitive commercial aircraft spare parts. However, the transaction triggers the monthly Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration where cross-border flows exceed the published threshold, so the SARL should instruct the bank to identify the credit as 'apport en capital' and not as 'loan proceedings' in order to maintain clean financial statements and avoid unintended transfer-pricing documentation. The bank will verify that the originator is not a sanctioned entity and will request a copy of the parent's board resolution and source-of-funds attestation for the specific injection before crediting.Outbound disbursements for the settlement of surplus commercial aircraft parts and avionics will run through three clearing rails. (i) USD-denominated trade payments to overseas MRO shops, teardown centres or airlines will be routed by SWIFT MT103 (with fixed UETR and the proper beneficiary account identifiers) through the French bank's New York correspondent account, settling over CHIPS or Fedwire; for escrow-backed transactions, funds will be released from the capital escrow account only upon joint instruction evidencing delivery and verification of the EASA Form 1 / FAA 8130-3 documents. (ii) Document-backed high-value settlements above approximately USD 1,000,000 will be undertaken via a Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary credit governed by UCP 600) opened by or in favour of the French SARL, with presentation of the commercial invoice, air waybill/bill of lading, proof of conformity and any required certificates of origin at the nominated French bank's trade-services desk; a standby letter of credit may be used to guarantee advance payments. (iii) EUR-denominated payments within the European Union will be executed as SEPA Credit Transfers settled through TARGET2, and euro payments outside the EU will use standard SWIFT MT103 messages. For every export disbursement, the bank will require correlation with the underlying commercial contract and with the French customs declaration, and the SARL must maintain its DELTA export filings to ensure the export payment matches the declared value of the goods. Aviation escrow deposits remain the dominant rail for buyer-paid purchase prices and will be governed by a bank-issued escrow agreement specifying release conditions, interest-accreted proceeds and joint instruction protocols.FX forward contracts (change à terme) for EUR/USD and EUR/GBP with tenors of up to 13 months, executed OTC bilaterally under French law and documented under an ISDA Master Agreement, used to hedge firm USD receivable and payable streams in the rotables tradeNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for partially convertible or restricted settlement currencies (for example EUR/CNH, EUR/INR, EUR/BRL) were there is a real underlying commercial exposure; the NDF is cash-settled in EUR or USD and documented under ISDA 2002 with the relevant local-market annexCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD) exchanging fixed or floating interest and principal notional amounts, permitted where the SARL has a durable USD liability or receivable schedule and subject to EMIR margining and trade-reporting obligations for a non-financial counterpartyFX swaps (swap de change) combining a spot EUR/USD transaction with a forward re-exchange, used to manage periodic liquidity mismatches between inbound escrow wires in USD and outbound disbursements in EURPlain-vanilla EUR/USD foreign-exchange options, consisting of purchased calls and puts (but not naked option writing), employed to hedge uncertain order-flow timing in the aviation aftermarket while preserving upside flexibility

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
25% corporate income tax on taxable profits. A qualifying SARL may benefit from a 15% reduced rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to the applicable capital ownership and turnover conditions.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
For dividends and similar distributions paid by a French SARL to a non-resident corporate shareholder, the domestic withholding tax rate is generally 25%, subject to exemptions and treaty relief. A qualifying EU parent may obtain exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, generally requiring the required minimum participation, holding-period, subject-to-tax, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse conditions. Domestic withholding tax does not generally apply to dividends paid to a French corporate shareholder subject to the relevant participation exemption conditions, although a taxable share of expenses may apply.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: The principal French full transfer-pricing documentation threshold is EUR 150 million of annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets for the French entity, or specified group and ownership conditions that bring the entity within the statutory scope. The annual transfer-pricing declaration generally applies from a EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold or equivalent statutory group conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Thresholds and filing mechanics must be tested for the relevant fiscal year.

      
Economic substance scope: France does not operate a single standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless assessed through transfer-pricing arm's-length requirements, the abuse-of-law and principal-purpose doctrines, beneficial-ownership standards, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation, EU anti-abuse provisions, and general deductibility requirements. The SARL should maintain French decision-making capacity, personnel or outsourced operational capability, premises or logistical arrangements appropriate to its trading activity, inventory and quality-control records, customer and supplier functions, bank-account control, board and management records, and evidence that intra-group services are actually received. A French entity used only as an invoicing conduit is a material audit risk.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax evasion and fraudulent accounting
        
Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, tax surcharges of up to 80% in aggravated cases, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that can generally reach five years' imprisonment and EUR 500,000 for individuals, with higher penalties for aggravated cases. Corporate criminal fines can be substantially higher under the French legal-entity fine multiplier rules, together with confiscation and publication measures.

        
Director personal liability: Managers may incur criminal liability for their own participation and civil liability for intentional misconduct. A manager may also be declared personally liable for tax debt under Article L267 of the French Tax Procedures Code where repeated or serious management misconduct makes collection impossible. Imprisonment, professional disqualification, and asset confiscation may apply. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal proceedings.

      

      
      

        VAT carousel fraud and false intra-Community transactions
        
Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, recovery of improperly claimed credits, 40% or 80% tax surcharges depending on intent, late-payment interest, and potential criminal prosecution for tax fraud, forgery, or use of false documents.

        
Director personal liability: The gérant may face criminal prosecution where aware of or complicit in the scheme, personal civil liability for direct wrongdoing, and management bans. Directors should not approve VAT treatment without transport, customs, customer-status, and delivery evidence.

      

      
      

        Customs fraud and export-control violations
        
Statutory sanction: Customs debt, seizure or forfeiture, administrative penalties, withdrawal of customs authorizations, and potential criminal sanctions under the French Customs Code and export-control rules. Sanctions depend on the goods, destination, intent, value, and whether the conduct is organized or repeated.

        
Director personal liability: The gérant and responsible officers may be personally prosecuted where they ordered, authorized, or knowingly tolerated the conduct. Confiscation, professional prohibitions, and custodial penalties may apply. A travel ban is case-specific and not an automatic consequence.

      

      
      

        Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing failure
        
Statutory sanction: Money laundering can generally attract up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with aggravated cases reaching ten years and EUR 750,000. Legal entities may face multiplied fines, confiscation, dissolution or other statutory measures. Separate regulatory sanctions may apply to regulated intermediaries.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and managers may be criminally liable for knowing participation, facilitation, or concealment. They may face confiscation, management prohibitions, and imprisonment. Reporting duties depend on the entity's regulated status, but all businesses should conduct sanctions, beneficial-owner, counterparty, and source-of-funds controls.

      

      
      

        Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit shifting
        
Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, withholding-tax exposure, interest limitation, anti-abuse adjustments, penalties for failure to provide documentation, and potential 40% or 80% surcharges where deliberate or fraudulent conduct is established.

        
Director personal liability: Ordinary pricing disagreement does not automatically create personal criminal liability. Personal exposure increases where directors knowingly approve fictitious services, false invoices, concealment, or fraudulent records. Serious misconduct can support tax-director liability and management disqualification.

      

      
      

        Undisclosed beneficial ownership and corporate transparency failure
        
Statutory sanction: Court orders, fines, registry restrictions, and potential criminal sanctions for false declarations or related concealment. The company may be unable to complete banking, customs, or payment processes until corrected.

        
Director personal liability: The gérant is responsible for accurate corporate filings and may face personal sanctions for intentional false statements or concealment. Management bans and criminal liability may apply where the filing failure forms part of a fraud or money-laundering scheme.

      

      
      

        Illegal distributions and unlawful depletion of capital
        
Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, tax reassessment, potential criminal penalties for unlawful distributions, and civil claims by the company, creditors, or insolvency practitioner.

        
Director personal liability: The gérant may be required to restore assets, compensate creditors, and bear insolvency-related liability where management misconduct contributed to insufficient assets. Criminal liability is possible for knowingly approving unlawful distributions.

      

      
      

        Insolvency and wrongful trading
        
Statutory sanction: Court-ordered liability for all or part of the insufficient assets, bankruptcy-related penalties, clawback of transactions, management bans, and possible criminal prosecution for fraudulent bankruptcy.

        
Director personal liability: The gérant may face personal civil liability, interdiction de gérer, confiscation, and imprisonment in aggravated or fraudulent cases. Travel restrictions are judicial and fact-dependent, not automatic.

      

      
      

        Aviation certification, traceability, and product-safety misconduct
        
Statutory sanction: Product recalls, contract damages, regulatory enforcement, loss of aviation approvals, customs and export-control consequences, and criminal sanctions for forgery, fraud, endangerment, or involuntary injury where applicable.

        
Director personal liability: Managers and responsible technical personnel may be personally liable for intentional falsification or knowing distribution of unsafe parts. Civil claims can include restitution, damages, recall costs, and creditor claims; imprisonment is possible where criminal elements are proven.

      

      
      

        Late filing and statutory recordkeeping
        
Statutory sanction: Fixed penalties, tax surcharges, late-payment interest, estimated assessment, loss of procedural protections, and audit escalation. Repeated non-compliance may support a finding of deliberate or fraudulent conduct.

        
Director personal liability: The gérant generally bears the company's compliance governance responsibility. Personal liability is not automatic for an ordinary late filing but may arise from deliberate concealment, repeated serious misconduct, insolvency-related misconduct, or conduct preventing tax collection.
