Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI); company-name and trademark searches may also be conducted through INPI's official databases.

Name reservation / lock period

France does not generally provide a statutory 30-day corporate-name reservation period through the company register. Check availability and conflicting rights before filing; a database search is not a guarantee of registrability or freedom to use. If a filing is rejected or a formal deficiency is notified, use the Guichet unique procedure to correct or supplement the filing within the stated deadline. For a substantive refusal, obtain the written decision and grounds, then pursue the review or judicial remedy identified in that decision and applicable procedural rules; there is no single name-rejection appeal route or universal deadline. Trademark objections and infringement disputes follow separate procedures.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministry of Economy and Finance; primary professional portal is www.impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel). Company law formalities and the initial SIREN/SIRET issuance are channelled through the national single window operated by INPI at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Non-resident VAT registration is handled by the SIE Non-Résidents (Service des Impôts des Entreprises – Non-Résidents); customs export processing, EORI issuance and export fiscal controls are administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI). Domestic VAT returns, the public e-invoicing portal (Portail Public de Facturation – PPF) and FEC audit data collection are all DGFiP-operated.

E-government filing portal

A French-established SARL receives its SIREN (9-digit national identifier) automatically upon INPI incorporation and is then assigned an intra-Community VAT number by the DGFiP, usually within 10 business days, in the format FR + 2 control digits + 9-digit SIREN; the number is validated via the EU VIES system. A foreign EPC contractor established in another EU member state may register directly for French VAT by filing the official non-resident registration application (Cerfa 13962) with the competent SIE without appointing a French intermediary, and must also obtain a French EORI from the DGDDI before any customs clearance. A non-EU EPC contractor performing works in France must mandatorily appoint a DGFiP-approved fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) under Article 289 A of the French General Tax Code (CGI); the representative files the Cerfa 13961 application with the SIE Non-Résidents, is jointly and severally liable for the foreign principal's French VAT, and once the file is complete the French VAT number and EORI are issued within approximately two weeks. Registration must in all cases precede the first taxable transaction or physical customs operation in France, and a worksite in France creating a permanent establishment will additionally require a French branch SIRET through the INPI single window.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) at the standard rate of 25%, with no flow-through treatment; profits are computed, declared and taxed at entity level (Form 2065 series). France applies a territoriality principle under Article 209-I CGI under which IS is levied only on profits attributable to operations carried on in France or to French permanent establishments; foreign permanent-establishment income is excluded where the foreign jurisdiction has a relevant tax treaty and the company proves the allocation. The SARL is not a tax-exempt zone vehicle and no general exemption applies to an electromechanical-data-center EPC business; however, the reduced 15% IS rate may apply to the first €42,500 of annual profit for qualifying SMEs. — A French-established SARL receives its SIREN (9-digit national identifier) automatically upon INPI incorporation and is then assigned an intra-Community VAT number by the DGFiP, usually within 10 business days, in the format FR + 2 control digits + 9-digit SIREN; the number is validated via the EU VIES system. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) at the standard rate of 25%, with no flow-through treatment; profits are computed, declared and taxed at entity level (Form 2065 series). France applies a territoriality principle under Article 209-I CGI under which IS is levied only on profits attributable to operations carried on in France or to French permanent establishments; foreign permanent-establishment income is excluded where the foreign jurisdiction has a relevant tax treaty and the company proves the allocation. The SARL is not a tax-exempt zone vehicle and no general exemption applies to an electromechanical-data-center EPC business; however, the reduced 15% IS rate may apply to the first €42,500 of annual profit for qualifying SMEs.

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box election, and classification is determined by law. Available statutory mechanisms include: (1) the option for the transparent personal-income-tax (IR) regime under Article 239 bis AA CGI for SARLs with fewer than 50 employees and turnover not exceeding €10 million, provided predominantly individual shareholders hold the capital, with profits taxed in the partners' hands instead of entity-level IS; (2) the option for French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI available to a French parent holding at least 95% of the SARL, whether for a domestic or foreign-owned chain, subject to the 5% participation tolerance; (3) procedural elections for monthly VAT returns versus the annual scheme; (4) the option to waive the franchise en base exemption so as to be voluntarily VAT-registered; and (5) in the cross-border context, the application of France's ATAD hybrid-mismatch provisions, which override entity classification where a payment is deductible in the payor state and not taxed in France, denying French deductions or imposing French tax under the 2021 Finance Act rules. No separate statutory hybrid-mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established SARL there is no registration threshold above the first taxable euro; the only applicable limit is the franchise en base de TVA small-business exemption: €85,000 of annual turnover for goods and €37,500 for services (with tolerance ceilings of €91,900 and €41,000 respectively before compulsory registration, as fixed by the 2025 Finance Act and subsequent decrees). An EPC contractor supplying construction and installation work is treated under the services threshold. For a non-established foreign entity, no turnover threshold applies: VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable transaction carried out in France, and construction services related to a French immovable property create a French VAT nexus regardless of the invoiced amount.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 262-I CGI for goods physically exported outside the EU by the SARL or for its account, provided the export customs declaration has been accepted and proof of the effective departure of the goods is obtained within 90 days from dispatch; intra-EU dispatches to a taxable person holding a valid EU VAT number are zero-rated under Article 262-ter CGI. For services, including engineering, design and remote supervisory services supplied to a non-French taxable customer, French VAT is not chargeable where the B2B place-of-supply rule places the supply outside France under Article 259 A CGI and the service is not connected to French immovable property. Services connected with a hyperscale data center situated in France — construction, mechanical and electrical installation, cooling-system works and related assembly — are treated as services connected with immovable property and remain subject to French VAT at 20% even if invoiced to a foreign head contractor; the reverse-charge mechanism applies only in the limited cases where a non-established supplier renders works to a French taxable customer. An exporter habitually in a permanent net credit position may apply for monthly refunds of French input VAT (Form 3519-SD) by presenting the same export evidence bundle.

  • Customs export declaration / Single Administrative Document (DAU) lodged electronically in the DELTA/ECS system and correlated with the VAT receivable
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill, CMR consignment note or CIM railway document) evidencing physical dispatch and exit from EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list referencing the customs declaration number, export order and destination consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange statement proving repatriation of export proceeds within the 180-day deadline
  • Works and equipment inventory certificate for the EPC project segregating exportable goods from local French construction services

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing is being phased in under the 2023 Finance Law. From 1 September 2026 all French VAT payers must be able to receive structured e-invoices (Factur-X, UBL or CII), and large and mid-sized enterprises must issue them and transmit transaction data; from 1 September 2027 small and micro enterprises, including most SARLs, must also issue e-invoices. Issuance is effected through a certified Partner Dematerialisation Platform (PDP) connected by API to the DGFiP's public platform (PPF); each structured invoice, together with payment-status data (Y and Z codes), is transmitted in near-real-time, so the PPF acts as a central fiscal clearance repository. There is no physical or digital stamp, but the DGFiP performs automated data validation and matching, while the taxpayer must archive native structured files for 10 years in a format compatible with the FEC accounting-file audit standard. B2C sales and certain international transactions are covered by e-reporting obligations.

During a DGFiP or DGDDI audit, the exporter must substantiate every zero-rated invoice, usually within 30 days of the formal request; failure to produce the full export-proof bundle leads to immediate reclassification as domestic taxable supplies, with the uncollected VAT clawed back together with Article 1727 late-payment interest at 0.20% per month. Penalties escalate: 40% for inaccuracies in bad faith, 80% for manoeuvres frauduleuses, and a 100% fine for fictitious invoicing under Article 1737 CGI. Where fraud is established, criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI exposes the SARL and its de jure and de facto managers to a fine of up to €500,000 and five years' imprisonment (seven years and €3,000,000 in aggravated money-laundering cases), together with ancillary sanctions such as loss of tax rights, publication of the judgment and court-ordered compliance supervision. The DGFiP may also hold the appointed VAT fiscal representative jointly and severally liable for the clawed-back VAT, penalties and interest on behalf of the foreign principal.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible, and France has no general exchange-control approval regime for ordinary current-account payments or cross-border capital transfers. Payments for goods, services, dividends, interest, loans, capital contributions, and disposals may generally be made through authorized payment providers, subject to AML/CFT checks, sanctions, tax rules, company-law requirements, and applicable statistical reporting. A SARL may remit lawful, properly approved and documented distributable profits to a non-resident shareholder without a general FX permit. Before a dividend payment, confirm distributable reserves, annual accounts and shareholder approval, withholding-tax treatment, beneficial-owner and treaty documentation, and any required tax filings. Interest, royalties, service charges, and other related-party payments require support for contractual substance, transfer-pricing compliance, and applicable withholding tax. Certain foreign investments in France and investments by French residents abroad are subject to separate reporting or, in sensitive sectors, screening rules; these are not ordinary currency-convertibility restrictions. Banks may request contracts, invoices, corporate approvals, source-of-funds evidence, and tax documents, and may delay or reject transactions that fail controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated and no mandatory conversion ratio requiring exporters to surrender or convert foreign-currency receipts into euros. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to accounting, tax, banking, sanctions, and applicable balance-of-payments reporting requirements. The company should retain export contracts, customs declarations where applicable, invoices, transport or delivery evidence, and bank advices to substantiate receipts. Specific Banque de France statistical reporting obligations may apply to cross-border transactions or positions depending on the reporting perimeter and thresholds in force; the company should confirm the applicable current instructions with its bank or Banque de France rather than assume that all transactions are exempt.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France’s national central bank and the competent authority for specified balance-of-payments and external-financial-statistics reporting. France applies the European Union’s common framework for capital movements and payments, including Regulation (EC) No 1889/2005 as amended and Regulation (EU) 2019/518. The European Central Bank is responsible for euro-area monetary policy. Banque de France reporting requirements and bank requests for transaction information do not constitute general exchange-control approval.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary collections and payments, subject to the provider’s permitted services, country coverage, safeguarding arrangements, and onboarding approval. An EU electronic money institution or payment institution may provide services under its authorization and applicable passporting framework, but it is not a substitute for a deposit-taking bank account or a bank-issued letter of credit, guarantee, or other regulated credit product. An offshore non-EU EMI has no automatic right to provide regulated services in France; verify its authorization, French/EU permissions, safeguarding and insolvency treatment, sanctions controls, and ability to provide named corporate accounts and required payment rails. The SARL should maintain a French or other EEA bank relationship for capital deposits, core operating liquidity, statutory payments, and trade-finance requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity subscriptions and shareholder loans from an account held in the investor’s or lender’s verified name to the SARL’s capital depositary or operational bank account, using an originating institution subject to appropriate AML/CFT controls. Identify the remittance accurately as a share-capital subscription, share premium, or shareholder loan; do not label a capital contribution as trade proceeds. Provide the subscription or loan agreement, shareholder or board resolutions, ownership and beneficial-owner evidence, source-of-funds documents, and the bank’s requested formation documents. Obtain the capital deposit certificate for formation where applicable and retain the SWIFT payment message or SEPA transfer record, credit advice, and accounting reconciliation. There is no universal French FX purpose-code system for ordinary commercial payments; use accurate payment references and any purpose or regulatory codes required by the sending bank, receiving bank, payment scheme, or statistical reporting instruction. Screen the remitter and intermediary banks for sanctions and ensure that payment data is complete and consistent with the supporting documents.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT cross-border credit transfers, commonly evidenced by an MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for other currencies or corridors. For EPC equipment imports and project procurement, settle against the contract, purchase order, invoice, delivery or milestone evidence, and approved payment schedule. A documentary commercial letter of credit may be arranged through a bank subject to credit approval, collateral, documentation rules, and the terms agreed with the beneficiary; specify applicable documentary-credit rules, normally UCP 600 where agreed, and verify presentation requirements and expiry dates. Consider demand guarantees or standby letters of credit for advance-payment, performance, or retention obligations where commercially appropriate and bank-approved. Maintain segregation of duties, dual authorization, beneficiary verification, invoice and contract matching, sanctions screening, and controls against payment-detail substitution fraud. Reconcile bank statements, FX conversions, fees, and project cost allocations promptly.Deliverable FX forwards for identifiable forecast or contracted exposures, subject to bank onboarding, suitability or appropriateness requirements where applicable, credit limits, collateral terms, and documented hedge purpose.Non-deliverable forwards where offered and legally available for the relevant currency pair and counterparty, subject to product eligibility, applicable conduct and reporting rules, and assessment of basis and settlement risk.Currency swaps, including FX swaps for short-term liquidity management and cross-currency swaps for longer-term exposures, subject to counterparty credit approval, collateral arrangements, valuation controls, and applicable derivatives documentation.FX options or collars, if offered and approved, with documented exposure limits, premium or contingent-liability controls, and clear authorization; avoid speculative positions unrelated to the SARL’s underlying business exposures.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for ancillary collections and payments, subject to the provider’s permitted services, country coverage, safeguarding arrangements, and onboarding approval. An EU electronic money institution or payment institution may provide services under its authorization and applicable passporting framework, but it is not a substitute for a deposit-taking bank account or a bank-issued letter of credit, guarantee, or other regulated credit product. An offshore non-EU EMI has no automatic right to provide regulated services in France; verify its authorization, French/EU permissions, safeguarding and insolvency treatment, sanctions controls, and ability to provide named corporate accounts and required payment rails. The SARL should maintain a French or other EEA bank relationship for capital deposits, core operating liquidity, statutory payments, and trade-finance requirements.
