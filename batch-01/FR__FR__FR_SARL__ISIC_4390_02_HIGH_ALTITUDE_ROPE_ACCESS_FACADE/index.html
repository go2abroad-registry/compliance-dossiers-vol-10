Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate e-filing and registration gateway: pro.impots.gouv.fr; single business formalities portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique Electronique).

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is incorporated through the Guichet Unique Electronique; the competent Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce transmits the application to INSEE, which issues the national identifier SIREN and establishment identifier SIRET. The local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) automatically assigns the intra-Community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) usually within 2 weeks of registration, based on Article 286 of the CGI. A foreign entity without a French fixed establishment that carries out taxable transactions in France must apply directly to the DGFiP/SIE for a French VAT number; non-EU applicants must appoint an accredited fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) unless a specific derogation applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box exists in France. Available elections: (1) Option for Impôt sur le Revenu (IR) under Article 239 bis AA CGI for an eligible PME (fewer than 50 employees and turnover not exceeding EUR 10M), valid for 5 years, including the SARL de famille regime under Article 239 bis AA/AB CGI; (2) default corporate income tax (IS) applies absent the IR option; (3) group VAT single taxpayer election ('assujetti unique') under Article 256 C CGI for closely linked entities; (4) option for the franchise en base de TVA small-business exemption where turnover thresholds are met; (5) ATAD 2 hybrid-mismatch election/notification obligations under Articles 212 bis CGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold: a French-established SARL exercising any taxable activity must be VAT-registered from the first euro. The small-business exemption ('franchise en base de TVA') applies to established taxpayers below EUR 85,000 (goods) and EUR 37,500 (services) for 2025, but this is an exemption, not a registration threshold. For non-established EU/foreign suppliers of B2C distance sales of goods, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies (OSS special scheme); for B2B supply of works on immovable property located in France (rope-access façade repairs), registration or reverse charge applies from the first euro without threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the European Union are taxable at 0% (exonération) under Article 262 I CGI provided the goods physically leave the EU and the exporter retains proof of export. However, the core commercial scope — rope-access structural glass replacement and façade works on supertall skyscrapers located in France — constitutes a supply of services connected with immovable property situated in France under Article 259 A 1° CGI; therefore the place of supply is France and the services are subject to French TVA at the standard rate of 20% (or, where contractually eligible, the 10% reduced rate for certain renovation works on residential premises older than two years). These domestic immovable-property services do not qualify as zero-rated exports. For other services rendered to foreign clients where the place of supply is outside France under Articles 259, 259 A to 259 D CGI (e.g., B2B general rule: customer established outside France and outside the EU), the supply falls outside the scope of French VAT, and corresponding input VAT recovery follows ordinary refund procedures.

  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill / CMR road consignment note proving physical exit from EU
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds

France is implementing a phased mandatory e-invoicing reform: all established taxable persons must receive structured e-invoices from 1 September 2026 and issue them from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for ETIs and SMEs/PMEs. Transmission must occur via the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) using structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII) compliant with EN 16931 / Peppol BIS 3.0. The reform also imposes mandatory e-reporting (données de transactions et de paiements) to DGFiP. France does not operate real-time pre-clearance digital stamping; issuance is transmitted after the transaction with periodic settlement, but the fiscal data transmission obligation is mandatory and automated.

The tax administration may verify export substantiation within the standard 3-year review period (6 years for fraud/aggravated situations) under Article L. 176 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales. Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger retroactive VAT reassessment, monthly late-payment interest of 0.20% (intérêt de retard), a 40% negligence penalty under Article 1728 CGI, or an 80% bad-faith penalty under Article 1729 CGI. Criminal exposure under Article 1741 CGI for tax/misdeclaration fraud includes imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine up to EUR 45,000 (aggravated organized fraud: up to 5 years and EUR 100,000), in addition to corporate criminal sanctions. Export and VAT records must be retained for 10 years under Article L. 102 B of the LPF.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a fully liberalised Eurozone jurisdiction: both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible, with no exchange-control licensing, no repatriation authorisation, and no quantitative limits. A French SARL may lawfully hold foreign-currency accounts and remit dividends or profits to non-resident shareholders without FX approval. The residual compliance burden is purely documentary: (i) every cross-border payment through the French banking system must carry a Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose code (direct investment, construction service, or goods classification); (ii) any transfer of funds to or from France exceeding EUR 10,000 triggers the Article L152-1 Code monétaire et financier declaration obligation (CERFA n°14008 filed with DGDDI for transported cash or evidenced via the bank for wire transfers); (iii) profit-remittance dossiers should contain audited financial statements, filed tax returns, and withholding-tax certificates. No capital controls target the rope-access facade construction sector; convertibility friction is effectively zero, but documentary artefacts must be pre-agreed with the bank to avoid settlement delay.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France abolished all export-proceeds surrender requirements as part of full exchange-control liberalisation. A French SARL is free to collect export receivables in EUR or any convertible currency, retain them abroad, hold foreign-currency accounts at EEA-licensed banks, and convert at market rates without central-bank approval. The sole mandatory duty is statistical: export transactions must be tagged with the correct Banque de France balance-of-payments code (100-series for goods; 200-series for services; direct-investment category where works abroad constitute a permanent establishment), with periodic electronic reporting to the Banque de France balance-of-payments service. No conversion ratio applies to export proceeds; a bank confirmation evidencing the underlying export operation is sufficient for customs and statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the Eurozone national central bank, balance-of-payments statistics authority, and administrator of the cross-border fund-transfer declaration framework under the Code monétaire et financier; ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) as the French banking licensor and AML-CFT prudential supervisor; European Central Bank (ECB) as supreme monetary authority of the eurozone; Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) as the enforcement authority for cross-border fund-transfer declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High for operational euro rails; Low for capital escrow, documentary LCs, supply-chain trade finance, and FX hedging. Legal constraints: an offshore non-EEA digital EMI cannot lawfully provide payment services to a French SARL or hold client funds for it absent an ACPR licence or a valid EEA passport; only ACPR- or EEA-authorised EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Wise Business, Revolut Business) may onboard French corporate clients, and only for payment and e-money activity. Such EMIs may never hold capital-contribution escrow funds (fonds de séquestre), may not issue documentary letters of credit, and may not structure ISDA/EMIR-cleared FX derivatives. The treasury blueprint therefore designates the French Tier-1 bank as the sole clearing, escrow, LC, and hedging node, with EMIs deployed only as supplementary rails for same-day SEPA disbursements and low-value progress payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection is routed via SWIFT MT103 (or, for euro-origin flows, SEPA Credit Transfer) from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the SARL's EUR IBAN at the French Tier-1 bank. The payment purpose field must state the capital character, e.g., 'Apport en capital / Capital contribution – FR_SARL'; the receiving bank must classify the flow under the Banque de France balance-of-payments direct-investment category (equity-capital contribution), and where the portal mandates an ISO 20022 purpose code, 'CAPC' (Capital Contribution) must be inserted so the credit is booked as equity rather than trade or treasury flow. The compliance verification pack comprises the notarised contribution act, shareholder resolution, the transferring shareholder's bank statements evidencing the debit cascade, and the receiving bank's source-of-funds validation for any inbound transfer exceeding EUR 10,000. For USD/CHF injections, the French bank may credit a foreign-currency account and convert via spot at market rate; no exchange-control authorisation is required, but the direct-investment BOP tag and the Article L152-1 declaration duty remain mandatory.Primary clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant via EBA CLEARING / TARGET2 for eurozone subcontractors, glazing suppliers, and progress-ACH milestone invoices; (ii) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers with MT202 cover for USD, CHF, GBP, and CNH trade settlements through the French bank's correspondent network; (iii) irrevocable commercial documentary Letters of Credit (UCP 600) and standby LCs (ISP98) issued by the French Tier-1 bank for import of structural glass, aluminium facade units, and high-altitude access equipment, with the required document set of commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or AWB, CMR, certificate of origin, and insurance certificate; (iv) for US-facing vendors, disbursements route via Fedwire or CHIPS through the correspondent tier, using US ACH only where the vendor maintains a US clearing-member account; (v) demand guarantees and bid bonds issued via the SWIFT network to support international facade tenders. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 (French entity or group annual revenue or gross assets) for mandatory Master File and Local File; EU CBCR obligation at EUR 750,000,000 group consolidated turnover. Below the mandatory threshold, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must still be maintained and produced within 30 days of a French tax authority request.

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations statute; however, the abuse-of-law doctrine under Article L.64 LPF and the EU ATAD substance requirements deny treaty benefits, interest deductions and royalty exemptions to entities lacking real economic substance. A rope-access facade SARL must demonstrate French operational direction, local employees, owned or leased equipment, active decision-making and effective business functions in France; a mailbox SARL with a foreign nominee manager and no French activity can be recharacterised as a sham and subjected to 80% penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Profit Suppression

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI imposes up to five years imprisonment and a EUR 750,000 fine, increased to EUR 2 million in aggravated cases; administrative fines of 40% to 80% of the recharacterized base plus 0.20% monthly late interest are assessed simultaneously.

Director personal liability: The gérant may be personally prosecuted for fraud, receive a travel ban and management prohibition, be ordered to pay the tax debt and interest under the action for corporate deficit contribution, and suffer seizure of personal assets.
Shell Company and Artificial Profit Absorbing Structure

Statutory sanction: French anti-abuse procedure permits recharacterization of transactions, disallowance of deductions, recapture of the entire tax advantage, and a penalty of up to 80% in cases of sham or fictitious contracts. Judicial dissolution may be sought where the SARL is found to be a mere shell harming creditors.

Director personal liability: The de jure gérant and the shadow director are jointly liable for the resulting tax debt and interest; they may be disqualified from managing a business for up to 15 years, subjected to travel bans, and held civilly liable in liquidation proceedings.
Non-Filing, False Filing and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Relevant sanctions: 10% to 80% increase of assessed tax, 0.20% monthly interest, automatic assessment where no return is filed, EUR 1,500 fine per undisclosed foreign bank account (EUR 10,000 for non-cooperative jurisdictions), and criminal penalties for fraud.

Director personal liability: Persistent false filings may constitute fraudulent bankruptcy or criminal fraud; directors risk civil attachment on personal assets, personal disqualification, prohibition from holding a commercial passport, and imprisonment where intentional concealment is proven.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation Between Related Undertakings

Statutory sanction: The French tax administration may adjust profits using an arm's length method, recharacterise undocumented payments as distributions, impose a 40% bad-faith or 80% fraud penalty, levy EUR 10,000 documentation penalties, and charge 0.20% monthly interest on the extra tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign artificial transfer pricing contracts or allow fraudulent documentation are exposed to civil liability for the tax and penalties, and to criminal sanctions as co-authors of fraud if they personally controlled the scheme.
Abusive Distribution, Thin-Capitalization and Disguised Deemed Dividends

Statutory sanction: The administration may deny interest deductibility under the French thin-capitalization rules, recharacterize excessive interest or royalties as deemed dividends under Article 109-1 CGI, and apply the 25% withholding tax plus 40% penalties and 0.20% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Gérants who approve abusive distributions in bad faith are jointly liable for missed withholding tax and social debts; in insolvency proceedings the court may order contribution to liabilities, issue a personal bankruptcy order, and impose a management ban of up to 15 years.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: French AML law imposes criminal fines up to EUR 5 million, imprisonment up to five years, and ACPR administrative sanctions including licence withdrawal and asset freezes; independent administrative fines may reach 10% of annual turnover.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face automatic criminal liability for laundering the proceeds of tax fraud; travel bans and seizure of assets are common, and directors may be held jointly for restitution of illicit transfers plus monetary penalties.
