Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum: the SARL may be incorporated with a single euro or any higher amount. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and paid up to at least one-fifth (20%) at incorporation, with the remaining balance payable within five years. The subscribed cash capital must be placed in escrow in a blocked account with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the Kbis registration extract is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises open to the public must obtain a fire safety review by the Commission de sécurité under ERP 5th category rules; any warehouse holding cotton bales requires a structural load inspection by an approved contrôleur technique and a firefighting-access certification; the employer must file the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques (DUER), organize periodic medical visits with the médecin du travail, and post the emergency and fire instructions in the workplace before staff are occupied.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum: the SARL may be incorporated with a single euro or any higher amount. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and paid up to at least one-fifth (20%) at incorporation, with the remaining balance payable within five years. The subscribed cash capital must be placed in escrow in a blocked account with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the Kbis registration extract is issued.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate registration portal: guichet-entreprises.fr / INPI.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident companies must first appoint a tax representative in France unless they are EU/EEA residents, then file the standard French company or branch registration application (e.g., P0/P2 forms or via the Guichet Unique), obtain a SIREN/SIRET number from INSEE and a comex (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) from DGFiP. For direct VAT registration without a permanent establishment, a Form CA/CO and appointment of a fiscal representative may be required for non-EU entities. Issuance typically takes 2–6 weeks; the tax ID is used for all indirect tax, wage tax, and corporate income tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — SARL is a French limited-liability company subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on worldwide income in principle, with territoriality limitations for activities performed abroad; profits from raw cotton export brokerage conducted through a French permanent establishment are taxable at the standard 25% rate.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (1) option for VAT group (assujetti unique) within the meaning of Article 11 of the VAT Directive; (2) election for the French fiscal integration regime (intégration fiscale) for a 95%-owned group; (3) option for tax consolidation within the EU under certain conditions; (4) statement of hybrid mismatch treatment under the ATAD/ATAD2 transposition (BOI-IS-GPE-20-60); (5) for eligible small businesses, the option for the normal VAT regime rather than the franchise de TVA. For SARLs, a check-the-box-style election is not available under French law; tax classification is statutory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA: EUR 91,900 for deliveries of goods (2025) and EUR 36,760 for services; however, a taxable person making intra-Community acquisitions in France exceeding EUR 10,000 per calendar year must register without threshold, and foreign businesses making taxable supplies in France from abroad have no de minimis threshold — any export transaction does not trigger registration if the seller is not established in France, but the French importer must self-account for VAT via reverse charge on intra-Community acquisitions; a French-established SARL exporting ginned cotton is required to register for VAT on the basis of its French incorporation alone (no turnover threshold applies).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of ginned raw cotton from France to a destination outside the European Union are eligible for the 0% VAT rate under Article 262-I of the French General Tax Code (CGI) when three cumulative conditions are met: (1) the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by the seller or a third party for the customer within 12 months of the sale (oral or written evidence must be available within a reasonable period not exceeding 90 days); (2) the exporter is the taxable person that actually transfers the ownership of the goods to a non-EU buyer, and the goods are destined for a non-EU customer; (3) the exporter holds robust documentary proof of export, including a valid customs export declaration (DAU/DELTA-G), transport evidence (B/L, CMR, air waybill), and evidence of the customer's non-EU establishment. The same zero-rating applies to certain services directly connected with exported goods and to intra-Community supplies (Article 262-III) subject to the buyer's valid VAT number and declaration in the VIES. Offshore supplies of services to non-EU business clients are zero-rated under Article 259-B and Article 262 as long as the place of taxation is outside the EU and the relevant proof of the client's status and effective use is documented.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en douane — DAU / DELTA-G)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or other transport document evidencing departure from the EU (CMR, air waybill, combined transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a French authorised bank confirming receipt of the export proceeds within the regulatory window

France mandates a phased interoperability regime: from 1 September 2026 all French taxable persons must receive e-invoices in a structured format for B2B domestic and intra-Community supplies; from 1 September 2027 invoicing for small enterprises becomes mandatory. The clearance architecture requires transmission through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified dématérialisation partenaire (PDP) using EN 16931 formats. There is no per-transaction fiscal stamp; e-reporting (e-reporting) of cross-border B2B and B2C transactions is required, and export declarations must be submitted via France's DELTA-G or DESI customs clearance systems, with data cross-checked against e-reporting flows.

Fraudulent claims of export zero-rating expose the exporter to: reassessment of the full 20% French VAT plus statutory delay interest (0.2% per month); application of the 40% penalty for substantial underpayment (Manquement délibéré) or 80% penalty if the claim is considered an abusive practice or dissimulation (manœuvre frauduleuse); possible criminal tax fraud sanctions under Article 1741 of the CGI consisting of a fine up to EUR 500,000 and 5 years' imprisonment (increased to EUR 1,000,000 and 7 years for organised fraud). The DGFiP and French anti-fraud units (Service National de Douane) may retrospectively audit the past 6 years (extendable to 10 years for fraud against the Treasury) and jointly subject exporters to fiscal and penal clawback proceedings, including seizure and attachment of assets under the Livre des procédures fiscales.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to EU and French sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax-reporting, customs, and statistical-reporting requirements. A French SARL may receive foreign capital, pay foreign suppliers, repatriate profits, service shareholder loans, and convert euros into other currencies without a general exchange-control approval. Profit remittance should be supported by approved financial statements, distributable-profit calculations, shareholder resolutions, withholding-tax analysis where applicable, and evidence that the payment is not a disguised capital distribution or prohibited value transfer. Banks may require enhanced due diligence for non-EU shareholders, politically exposed persons, sanctioned or high-risk countries, unusual shareholder loans, circular flows, and payments inconsistent with the company's registered activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no universal statutory deadline requiring conversion of foreign-currency export receipts. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's discretion, subject to contractual bank terms, accounting in euros, VAT and customs records, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, and applicable statistical or balance-of-payments reporting. Export evidence, commercial invoices, transport documents, customs declarations, and payment references should be retained for the applicable French accounting and tax record-retention period. Banks may impose internal documentary deadlines before releasing or converting funds, but those are contractual compliance requirements rather than general French exchange controls.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the oversight of the European Central Bank; French financial-intelligence and anti-money-laundering matters are also administered by TRACFIN and relevant French authorities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary treasury and payment functions, but not a complete substitute for a French relationship bank. A licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution established in the European Economic Area may provide payment accounts, SEPA payments, foreign-exchange execution, and safeguarded customer funds under its applicable authorization and passporting rights. Customer funds held by an EMI are safeguarded and are not equivalent to deposits protected by the French deposit-guarantee regime. An offshore non-EEA EMI generally cannot provide regulated EU payment services into France without an appropriate French or EEA authorization, establishment, or permitted cross-border arrangement. Fintech providers may restrict cotton-origin countries, trade corridors, correspondent banks, cash deposits, documentary credits, high-value transfers, and third-party payments. The SARL should retain a French or EU bank capable of issuing or advising commercial letters of credit, handling documentary collections, supporting customs-related evidence, and maintaining stable correspondent-bank access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For subscribed SARL capital, open a French capital deposit account in the company's formation name, provide draft articles and subscription documentation, and remit funds from an account held by the subscribing shareholder or investor. The payment reference should identify the company, capital subscription, shareholder, and share-issuance purpose. The bank should verify remitter identity, ownership of the remitting account, source of funds, sanctions status, and consistency with the subscription documents. After receipt of the certificate of incorporation or Kbis extract, request the bank's capital-release process and transfer the funds to the operational account. Shareholder loans, additional paid-in capital, and other funding should be routed under separately documented agreements and clearly identified payment references. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the applicable SEPA perimeter and SWIFT cross-border transfer for non-SEPA currencies or corridors. Apply the bank's requested ISO 20022 purpose information or legacy payment-purpose field; France does not impose a general statutory capital-import approval for this activity, but statistical, tax, sanctions, and AML reporting may apply.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments and SWIFT cross-border transfers for non-SEPA currencies. A SWIFT MT103 is a legacy customer-credit-transfer format increasingly replaced by ISO 20022 pacs.008 messaging; the remitting bank determines the applicable message format. For documentary trade, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit advised or confirmed by a suitable bank, with invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, inspection certificate, insurance document, bill of lading, and other agreed documents.
