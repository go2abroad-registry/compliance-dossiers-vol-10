Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full member of the euro area, and the euro is freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no restrictive foreign exchange controls applicable to residents or non-residents. Profit remittances by a SARL to non-resident shareholders are permitted without prior authorisation, subject to withholding tax coordination under tax treaties, proof of corporate existence, and compliance with anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-terrorist financing (CTF) obligations. Capital account flows, including inbound equity injections and outbound dividends, are unrestricted. However, transaction-level statistical reporting to the Banque de France is mandatory for cross-border transfers exceeding €750,000 per transaction, and legal entities must report direct investment flows to the French Treasury for balance-of-payments and direct investment monitoring purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline for export proceeds surrender and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in France. The euro being the domestic legal tender, exporters may invoice and receive settlement in foreign currency, hold the proceeds abroad, and convert at will. However, for commercial and statistical prudence, cross-border export receipts above €750,000 per transaction must be declared to the Banque de France via the standard reporting form for balance-of-payments purposes. French resident exporting entities must maintain full supporting documentation for a period of six years to satisfy customs and tax audit requirements. No obligation exists to channel export proceeds through a domestic French bank account.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (national central bank within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank) and the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the economic policy framework of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for PSD2-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) incorporated in the EEA and passporting into France; Low for offshore or third-country digital EMIs operating outside the European Economic Area. Under the Second Payment Services Directive (PSD2), an EMI must be chartered in an EU member state and passported to France to offer cross-border payment and e-money services. Non-EEA fintech entities cannot open local bank accounts or provide payment services without obtaining a French licence from the ACPR (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution). Even with ACPR licensing, the practical viability for clearing large trade disbursements is constrained by limits on holding client funds and the absence of fully-fledged credit services. For a medium-risk orthopedic implant manufacturer with Letters of Credit and SWIFT wiring needs, a tier-1 bank remains the legally safest and operationally robust clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols for inbound capital injection into the French SARL: execute a commercial-purpose SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank cover) with a valid purpose code matching the capital account category, typically 'Investment in capital in a resident company' under the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting classification. Accompanying verification must include the executed capital subscription agreement, notarised board minutes, representation of the new corporate shareholder (certified attestation), the bank's own UBO and negative-list screening confirmation, and prior declaration of direct investment to the French Treasury if the investor is from a non-EU jurisdiction or the investment surpasses statutory thresholds. All inbound transfers must clearly identify the originator and beneficiary exact legal name and account number. The receiving bank must be instructed to credit the funds to a dedicated 'Capital Escrow Account' or a newly opened operational account with a note 'apport en capital' to prevent commingling and facilitate seamless statutory publication of capital increase.Trade settlements for outbound disbursements are executed via: (1) SWIFT MT103 for individual trade payments to suppliers and distributors with correct purpose codes (e.g., 'Prepayment of imports' or 'Payments for imports' under the French current account coding), including corresponding invoice and customs documents; (2) Documentary and Standby Letters of Credit (MT700, MT707) for structured purchase contracts, especially where the counterparty requires a bank undertaking before dispatch; and (3) MT202 COV for interbank wire cover where an MT103 knock-off is not possible. The SARL may use bank-executed commercial LC arrangements with the issuing French bank for inbound raw-material purchases and outbound finished-goods sales, provided the documentary bundle strictly conforms to the LC terms (invoice, bill of lading, packing list, certificate of origin). For tax and FX audit integrity, all outbound disbursements must be authorised by dual signatory protocols and must reference the applicable business relationship contract. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.000% standard rate under CGI Art. 219 (fiscal years opening on or after 1 January 2022); top effective rate 25.825% inclusive of the 3.3% social contribution surcharge levied on CIT exceeding EUR 763,000 for companies with turnover above EUR 7,630,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax on distributions paid by a French company to non-resident corporate shareholders (CGI Art. 119 bis 2), where the distribution exceeds the share-capital basis exemption; distributions to non-resident individuals are subject to the 12.8% flat tax (prélèvement forfaitaire unique) plus, for French-resident recipients, social levies. The French payer files form n° 2756 on the SEPA date of the distribution and pays the withholding to the Direction Générale des Finances Publiques.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return must be filed by the second working day following 1 May (ordinarily 3 May). For a fiscal year not aligned with the calendar year, the deadline is the second working day following the expiry of three months and fifteen days after the fiscal year-end. The final CIT balance is payable on the filing deadline; advance payments follow the quarterly schedule.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 (consolidated group turnover or balance-sheet total) triggers the mandatory Master File, French Local File, and annual transfer-pricing declaration (form n° 2257-SD) under CGI Art. 223 quinquies B. Below this threshold, the SARL is not exempt from demonstrating arm's-length pricing on demand: it must retain contemporaneous comparability analysis, intercompany agreements, budgets, and pricing formulas for each controlled transaction, and the absence of documentation will shift the burden of proof to the taxpayer upon audit.

Economic substance scope: France does not administer a formal Economic Substance Regulations filing (UAE-style ESR), but the substance-over-form doctrine, the abuse-of-law regime (LPF Art. L.64), the anti-artificial-arrangement doctrine (CGI Art. 155 A for services, Art. 57 for transfer pricing), and the BEPS anti-abuse rules (ATAD interest limitation, CFC rules under CGI Art. 209 B for profits routed through low-tax subsidiaries) require the SARL to demonstrate real local substance: a registered office, operational premises, employees performing engineering and manufacturing functions, board meetings held in France, and a commercial rationale for the central functions (R&D, quality certification of titanium orthopedic implants, sterilization, logistics). A letterbox SARL with management and decision-making abroad triggers recharacterization, recomputation of French taxable income, 40%-80% penalties, and denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Five years' imprisonment and EUR 500,000 fine under CGI Art. 1741; aggravated cross-border or organized fraud attracts seven years' imprisonment and EUR 3,000,000 fine, together with confiscation of assets, publication of the decision, and a ten-year professional ban.

Director personal liability: The gérant is jointly and severally liable under LPF Art. L.267 for tax, interest, and penalties where the fraudulent management caused the corporate tax debt; criminal courts may order restitution, asset freezing, travel bans (interdiction de quitter le territoire), and imprisonment of the gérant for intentional participation.
Late Filing and Concealed Profit

Statutory sanction: 10% penalty for late filing, 40% when filed after formal notice, 80% for intentional concealment plus 0.2% monthly late-payment interest (CGI Art. 1728); hidden income is recharacterized with a 40% penalty under Art. 1729 for bad faith and 80% for manoeuvres frauduleuses.

Director personal liability: Directors who engineered the concealment face personal liability for the company's tax arrears (LPF Art. L.267), disqualification from commercial management, and criminal sanctions for falsified accounting records.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Reassessment by the DGFiP under CGI Art. 57 with add-back of the non-deductible excess; 40% penalty for lack of bona fide pricing evidence, 80% for artificiality; interest at 0.2% monthly; applicability of the exchange-of-information and MAP procedures where double taxation arises.

Director personal liability: The gérant who signed the intercompany agreements and the CIT return may be personally assessed for the reassessed tax where the pricing arrangement was designed to evade French tax; criminal exposure under Art. 1741 if documents were falsified.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (MLI); recharacterization under LPF Art. L.64 abus de droit with an 80% penalty on the tax benefit; refusal of the reduced rate and reinstatement of 25% withholding plus interest; possible joint liability of the parent.

Director personal liability: Directors face corrective reassessment personally for abus de droit when they personally engineered the artificial chain; in fraudulent schemes, imprisonment and fines under Art. 1741, and interdiction de gérer (management ban) for up to fifteen years.
AML / Beneficial Ownership and Payment Compliance

Statutory sanction: Failure to file the RBE declaration: six months' imprisonment and EUR 15,000 fine for the gérant, EUR 75,000 for the company (Monetary and Financial Code Arts. L.561-2 ff., L.562-5 ff.); AML administrative sanctions up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of turnover by the ACPR; banking penalties on the prohibited payment rails.

Director personal liability: The gérant is personally liable for RBE omissions; money-laundering complicity involving commercial funds attracts ten years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine (Penal Code Art. 324-1 ff.), confiscation of the titanium implant inventory and proceeds, and travel restrictions.
