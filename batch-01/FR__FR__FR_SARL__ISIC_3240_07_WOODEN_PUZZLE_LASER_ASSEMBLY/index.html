Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique, France; internet portal: impots.gouv.fr; company registration and statutory formalities via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; customs and export-side authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), portal: douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Standard operating procedure for a French-established SARL: (1) File the incorporation dossier through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) under Ordinance No. 2021-1389 of 27 October 2021 (in force since 1 January 2023); INSEE issues the 9-digit SIREN and 14-digit SIRET identifiers within approximately 3 to 5 working days and automatically transmits the file to DGFiP. (2) The statutory declaration of existence triggers VAT identification; DGFiP issues the intra-Community VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire) in the format FR + 2-digit key + 11-digit SIREN, under Article 286-I of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI); the number appears on the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) extract and is verifiable on the EU VIES database. (3) For business activities falling below the Article 293 B CGI small-business franchise thresholds, the SARL may claim the franchise, but any direct or marketplace-mediated sale of goods normally justifies full VAT registration; the franchise is waivable at the taxpayer's option. (4) Register with DGDDI to obtain the French EORI number (format FR + SIREN), mandatory for any customs export declaration. (5) For distance sales of goods to consumers in other EU Member States, enroll in the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) module through the DGFiP professional online portal. Non-resident companies not established in France are required instead to appoint a tax representative (représentant fiscal) accredited by DGFiP; this requirement does not apply to the resident FR_SARL.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; the following statutory elections exist: (a) under Article 239 bis AA CGI, a newly created SARL/EURL satisfying SME criteria (not more than 50 employees and turnover or balance sheet total not exceeding €10 million) may opt, within the first 3 months of its first fiscal year, for income-tax transparency (régime des sociétés de personnes) for a maximum of 5 fiscal years, after which it reverts mandatorily to corporate income tax (IS); (b) under Article 8 CGI, a SARL de famille may elect income-tax transparency at shareholder level; (c) under Article 239 CGI, partnerships may opt for corporate income tax; (d) EU anti-hybrid and reverse-hybrid rules (Directives (EU) 2016/1164 and 2017/952) apply mandatorily and cannot be circumvented by elective classification; (e) under Article 209 I CGI, the territoriality principle allows exclusion of foreign permanent-establishment profits from the French IS base, while Article 209 quinquies CGI permits an election for worldwide taxation. The default regime for a standard commercial SARL remains entity-level French IS at the standard rate of 25%, with the reduced 15% rate applicable to the first €42,500 of annual taxable profit for small businesses, plus the local territorial economic contribution (CET: CFE + CVAE) depending on the plant and turnover profile.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no minimum turnover threshold for mandatory VAT registration of a French-established SARL; any taxable person carrying out an economic activity in France must file a declaration of existence (Article 286-I CGI). The small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA, Article 293 B CGI) applies up to €85,000 per calendar year for sales of goods and €37,500 for supplies of services, with upper override thresholds of €93,500 (goods) and €41,000 (services); however, the franchise is not applicable to intra-Community distance sales declared through the OSS and is, in commercial practice, incompatible with marketplace-mediated consumer sales where the platform or payment facilitator (Amazon Pay, Stripe) requires evidence of VAT registration. A fully registered SARL selling manufactured wooden puzzles under ISIC 3240 / NAF 32.40Z will therefore collect French VAT at 20% on domestic B2C sales and will be required to file periodic CA3 VAT returns.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union are zero-rated under Article 262-I-1° CGI provided the removal is physically accomplished and the exporter holds evidential proof prior to the VAT return deadline and upon audit request; services directly connected to exported goods (freight, handling, customs representation) are exempt under Article 262-I-2° CGI. For intra-Community supplies (B2B), the exemption under Article 262-I-3° CGI requires the customer's valid VAT identification number and the submission of EC Sales Lists; the supplier must dispatch the goods to another Member State. Distance sales of goods to EU final consumers do not qualify for export zero-rating: they are taxable in the Member State of arrival, with the intra-EU distance sales threshold of €10,000 per year and the optional OSS scheme applying. Where Amazon operates as marketplace or FBA provider, the platform may be treated as deemed supplier (Article 283 CGI implementing Article 14a of Directive 2006/112/EC); genuine third-country exports require actual customs exit and the exporter retains the burden of proof. Input VAT on costs attributable to genuine exports remains recoverable under Article 271 CGI and no refund procedure is required for the resident exporter.

  • Customs export declaration (Déclaration d'exportation DAU / Single Administrative Document) validated by DGDDI with a valid MRN (Movement Reference Number) evidencing the definitive exit from EU customs territory
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading (B/L) for sea freight, CMR waybill for road freight, or Air Waybill (LTA IATA) showing the routing to the third-country destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the CN8/HS commodity code, weight, value, and beneficiary of the export
  • Inward remittance certificate (attestation bancaire de rapatriement des devises / SWIFT MT103 credit advice) demonstrating effective collection of export proceeds in the seller's bank account
  • EORI certificate and VAT registration extract to align the customs declarant with the DGFiP tax file
  • Intrastat DEB (déclaration d'échange de biens) for intra-EU dispatches where the annual dispatch threshold (€460,000 as of 2025) is exceeded
  • Retained export dossier (preuve d'exportation) with all underlying transport and insurance documents kept for at least 6 years for audit purposes

Mandatory e-invoicing (B2B domestic) and e-reporting (B2C and cross-border) under the French Finance Act 2023 and the implementing decrees of 2024: from 1 September 2026 all large and mid-sized enterprises must issue and receive structured electronic invoices (EN 16931 formats: Factur-X, UBL, CII) through DGFiP-registered dematerialization platforms (PDP) or partner dematerialization platforms (PPD); from 1 September 2027 the mandate extends to small and micro enterprises, including the FR_SARL tier. B2C transactions settled through Amazon Pay or Stripe are subject to e-reporting: transaction data must be transmitted by the accredited PDP to DGFiP no later than the next working day after issuance. France does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance system with digital stamping in the style of the Italian SDI; instead, the DGFiP relies on structured data exchange through registered platforms, with open REST/SOAP APIs and the mandatory DSR (Données de facturation et de transaction) data model. The administration will progressively pre-fill VAT returns from the transmitted e-invoice and e-reporting data while the periodic CA3/CA12 filing obligation remains in force.

French customs and tax administrations apply strict documentary scrutiny to export zero-rating. Missing, inconsistent, or retrospectively invalid export proof triggers reassessment of the 20% VAT on the export price, together with late-payment interest (intérêt de retard) at 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI) and penalty surcharges: 40% for bad faith (mauvaise foi, Article 1729-b CGI) or 80% for fraudulent maneuvers (manœuvres frauduleuses, Article 1729-c CGI). Goods covered by a false export declaration may be seized under the Customs Code (Articles 414 and 423), with fines proportional to the value and potential confiscation. The ordinary reassessment window is three fiscal years from the filing date, extended to six years for fraud or undisclosed activity; deliberate evasion is prosecutable under Article 1741 CGI. In practice, DGFiP and DGDDI examiners verify that export proceeds are collected within a 180-day benchmark and that payment evidence matches the declared foreign counterparty; the absence of FX repatriation proof is a recurring trigger for clawback at 20% plus 40% penalties. For commerce flowing through Amazon Pay or Stripe, the audit file must reconcile payment-facilitator settlement reports with the corresponding export declarations, delivery proofs, and the underlying customer orders.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies the complete free movement of capital regime under Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), transposed into Articles L151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier - CMF). The euro is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions; there is no general foreign-exchange restriction, no surrender obligation and no executive authorisation requirement for ordinary inbound equity or shareholder debt. Non-EU foreign direct investment (FDI) is screened only in sensitive sectors (defence, security, dual-use, critical technologies and critical infrastructure) under Decree No. 2019-1590 as amended by Decree No. 2021-1404; laser-cutting of wooden educational puzzles (ISIC 3240) is outside that perimeter. Profit remittance by the SARL to non-resident shareholders is lawful without licence; dividend distributions are subject to French withholding tax (25% for non-resident corporate shareholders; 30% flat tax for natural persons comprising 12.8% income tax plus 17.2% social levies), reduced to 0% for eligible EU/EEA corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, and to applicable tax-treaty rates for US, UK, Chinese or other non-EU shareholders. Practical remittance hurdles are documentary: the paying bank requires a French tax-residence certificate for the SARL and an up-to-date beneficial-owner declaration before any dividend wire is released.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, repatriation deadline or mandatory conversion ratio for French export proceeds. Exporters freely retain EUR or foreign currency abroad or in local accounts; the only obligations are statistical reporting of cross-border flows by the intermediary bank under CMF Articles R154-1 et seq. and the EU cash-control declaration for cash movements across EU borders exceeding EUR 10,000. Transfers to or from States listed by France as non-cooperative jurisdictions (ETNC) trigger prior declaration or Treasury authorisation under CMF Articles L151-3 and L511-41-5 and will be rejected by compliant banks absent clearance. Export deliveries and intra-EU dispatches benefit from VAT zero-rating under Article 262-I of the French General Tax Code (CGI); the SARL must maintain customs and transport evidence (CN23, EX-A/EX-T, bills of lading, delivery receipts) to substantiate the zero rate and to answer any bank query on the underlying trade purpose.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); it exercises balance-of-payments statistics collection, foreign-exchange data oversight and macroprudential supervision jointly with the ACPR (Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority) and the French General Directorate of the Treasury (Direction générale du Trésor) for capital-movement authorisations and foreign-investment screening.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Free-flow routing applies: EU/EEA shareholders transfer EUR via SEPA Credit Transfer with purpose code EQUI or LOAN, requiring no French clearance; the receiving bank files the balance-of-payments declaration to Banque de France automatically. Non-EU shareholders route via SWIFT MT103 through a correspondent chain (CITI or JPMorgan London/New York) into the SARL's EUR IBAN, using purpose code S131 for FDI exceeding 10% of voting rights or S231 for long-term shareholder loans; the beneficiary bank performs automatic BoP statistical reporting. For USD-denominated injections, conversion to EUR is executed at the receiving French bank's spot rate on value date; filed field 72 must include the narrative 'INVEST/S131' together with the capital subscription resolution (procès-verbal), share register and transfer certificate. Transfers originating from an ETNC jurisdiction require prior authorisation under CMF Articles L151-3 and L511-41-5 and are otherwise refused.
