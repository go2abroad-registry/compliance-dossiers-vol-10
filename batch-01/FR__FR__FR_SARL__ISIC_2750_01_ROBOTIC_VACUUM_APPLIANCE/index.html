Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital is required; the capital may be EUR 1 or more. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations, which issues the capital-deposit certificate. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed at incorporation unless the statutory valuation rules permit otherwise.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupation, obtain applicable municipal planning and premises approvals, maintain fire-safety systems and emergency access, and complete fire-risk review with the local Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours where required. Structural floors, racks, lifting equipment, and production installations must be suitable for their intended loads and inspected under applicable French rules. The employer must prepare the required single occupational-risk assessment document, implement workplace safety measures, arrange statutory inspections for regulated equipment, and comply with Labour Code requirements; no general pre-opening occupational licence applies.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital is required; the capital may be EUR 1 or more. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The competent authority is the French Directorate-General of Public Finance (Direction générale des finances publiques, DGFiP), acting through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). The principal online portal is impots.gouv.fr. Company formation and registration data are filed through the INPI Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, which allocates or confirms the SIREN and SIRET identifiers. Foreign businesses without a French establishment generally deal with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE), subject to the applicable transaction profile.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL obtains its SIREN, SIRET and APE activity code through the INPI Guichet unique registration process. The DGFiP then assigns the corporate income tax account and VAT account. The French VAT identification number generally consists of the country prefix FR, a two-character key and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity without a French establishment must submit the applicable registration request to the SIEE before undertaking transactions requiring French VAT registration, including relevant domestic supplies, imports, intra-Community acquisitions or taxable services. The application should include constitutional documents, foreign tax registration evidence, proof of business activity, identity and authority documents for representatives, French business or customs details where applicable, bank information where requested, and transaction-flow documentation. Customs operations additionally require an EORI number obtained through French customs or the EU customs registration process. VAT registration does not replace EORI registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Subject to statutory conditions, an SARL may elect temporary taxation under the income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu, IR), generally for up to five financial years, if it satisfies the qualifying ownership, size, age and activity requirements. A qualifying family SARL may elect IR taxation without the same five-year limitation, subject to the family-company conditions. France has no United States-style general check-the-box election for SARLs. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework may deny deductions or impose inclusion where an arrangement produces a deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcome.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For businesses eligible for the French VAT exemption based on the franchise en base regime, the principal 2025 threshold for supplies of goods is EUR 85,000, with a higher tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500 subject to statutory conditions. A business exceeding the applicable threshold, making transactions excluded from the franchise, importing goods, or undertaking intra-Community transactions may require VAT registration irrespective of the domestic franchise threshold. The EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold applies to specified intra-Community distance sales and certain electronically supplied, telecommunications, broadcasting and electronic services, rather than serving as a general French domestic registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT where the exporter is able to prove exportation and exit from the EU customs territory, normally through the customs export declaration and electronic exit confirmation. The exporter must retain a consistent audit trail linking the invoice, goods, transport documents, customs declaration and exit evidence. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for VAT exemption rather than domestic export zero-rating when the customer has a valid VAT identification number in another EU Member State, the goods are dispatched to that Member State, and the supplier satisfies reporting and documentary requirements. Services are generally outside the scope of French VAT or exempt under the applicable place-of-supply rule when supplied to a business established outside France or outside the EU, subject to customer-status, service-type and use-and-enjoyment requirements. Export VAT refunds require valid invoices, customs and transport evidence, proper VAT returns, and absence of prohibited or unsupported input VAT.

  • French or EU customs export declaration containing the applicable export procedure and Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Electronic confirmation of exit from the EU customs territory or equivalent customs export evidence
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, customer, goods, value, Incoterms and VAT treatment
  • Packing list and product or serial-number records where relevant for robotic appliances
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note or other transport document
  • Purchase order, sales contract and proof linking the shipment to the export invoice
  • Import or customs documentation in the destination country where available
  • Proof of payment or other commercial settlement evidence where required to corroborate the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory prerequisite because France has no general foreign-exchange repatriation control

France is introducing mandatory electronic invoicing and transaction reporting through accredited private electronic invoicing platforms (plateformes de dématérialisation partenaires, PDPs). From 1 September 2026, all French-established businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; issuance obligations begin on 1 September 2026 for large and intermediate-sized enterprises and on 1 September 2027 for small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises. Transaction reporting (e-reporting) will apply to specified business-to-consumer and cross-border transactions. France does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance requirement for ordinary domestic B2B transactions. Invoices must use the mandated structured data formats and be transmitted through the permitted platform architecture. Public-sector invoices are generally submitted through Chorus Pro. Fiscal stamping is not generally required for ordinary French VAT invoices.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption or refund may be reassessed if customs exit, transport, customer status, invoice linkage or transaction reality is not substantiated. Reassessed VAT may include statutory late-payment interest, generally 0.20% per month, and penalties of 10% for late or deficient payment, 40% for deliberate failure, or 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abusive conduct, subject to the facts and procedural rules. Fraudulent VAT claims can also result in criminal tax-fraud proceedings, with imprisonment and fines that may be increased for aggravated conduct. Unsupported input VAT refunds may be recovered, and improperly exempted output VAT may be assessed retrospectively with interest and penalties. Customs duties, import VAT and customs penalties may additionally apply where export or customs declarations are inaccurate, incomplete or fraudulent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under the EU Treaty framework with full convertibility for the euro. Current account payments and invisible transactions are unrestricted within the EU/EEA and with the majority of OECD and treaty partners. Capital account operations are generally liberalized, and profit remittances, dividend distributions, and intercompany capital repatriations by a French SARL to non-resident shareholders are freely executable, subject only to tax withholding where applicable and transaction-level AML scrutiny. There is no prior licensing regime for standard trade financing or investment flows involving compliant jurisdictions; however, specific prior authorizations apply to non-cooperative jurisdictions, sanctioned entities, and sensitive defense-related sectors. All material cross-border capital flows of €300,000 or above trigger an ex-post statistical declaration to the Banque de France as part of corporate balance-of-payments reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose mandatory export proceeds surrender or statutory conversion requirements. Exporters may retain receivables in foreign currency or euro without forced repatriation timelines or minimum conversion ratios. The obligations are limited to statistical reporting of foreign receivables/payables to the Banque de France under the balance-of-payments regime and, where applicable, to the customs declaration obligation for physical cross-border cash or negotiable instruments exceeding €10,000. All export receipts may be held in foreign-currency accounts at an authorized bank or converted on the interbank market at commercial rates, subject to internal treasury policy and information required for sanctions screening.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (Treasury Directorate) as the competent French authorities for external financial transactions, balance-of-payments statistics, and capital movement compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

European digital EMI/neobank platforms registered with the ACPR under the EU e-money passport are feasible for routine euro operational flows and small payment transactions, rating Medium for day-to-day treasury settlements. Viability as the sole corporate treasury vehicle is Low because these institutions cannot legally provide documentary trade credit, commercial letters of credit, or foreign-exchange forward lines, and may terminate non-resident relationships without immediate escalation. They are also generally unsuitable for holding committed capital escrow balances where the onboarding bank must provide a domestic banking license and systemic protection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally, the corporate income tax return (Form 2065-SD and associated schedules) must be filed no later than the second working day after 1 May following the close of the fiscal year, i.e., typically by 3 May for calendar-year taxpayers. For fiscal years ending on a date other than 31 December, the deadline is the second working day after 30 April of the year following the close of the fiscal year.
