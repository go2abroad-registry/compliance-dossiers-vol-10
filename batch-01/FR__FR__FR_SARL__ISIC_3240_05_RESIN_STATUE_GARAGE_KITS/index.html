Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum for SARL; capital is freely set in the Articles of Association. Cash contributions (apports en numéraire) must be fully subscribed and paid up as to at least 20% at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Undisbursed funds must be deposited into a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released only upon presentation of the RCS K-bis extract.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: 5th-category establishment notice to the mayor, evacuation signage, registered fire extinguishers and an annual fire drill log; electrical installation inspected and certified by an approved body within the last five years; structural load verification of resin storage racks. Urban-planning clearance: verification that the workshop premises comply with the local Plan Local d'Urbanisme craft zone rules prior to lease signing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); online portal: impots.gouv.fr; non-resident VAT registration unit: Service des Impôts des Entreprises Non-Résidents (SIE Non-Résidents).

E-government filing portal

1) Form the French SARL through Guichet des Formalités (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) to obtain the SIREN identification number and the SIRET establishment number. 2) File for corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés) status with the competent SIE. 3) For VAT, use Form 1065 (Cerfa n° 15787) to register with the SIE; a non-established foreign company must register directly with SIE Non-Résidents before making taxable supplies in France, and a non-EU third-country supplier must appoint an accredited fiscal representative in France. An EU taxable person making intra-Community distance sales above EUR 10,000 must register for French VAT either through the OSS system or via SIE Non-Résidents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a SARL is a taxable company subject to French corporate income tax at a 25% standard rate on taxable profits; it is not fiscally transparent by default. Territorial scope: a resident SARL is taxable on profits derived from businesses operated in France, with profits attributable to a foreign permanent establishment generally sheltered under French territoriality. Non-resident companies are taxable only on French-source profits if they operate through a French permanent establishment. No Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment applies to this entity.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in the French system. France does not permit a unilateral hybrid-mismatch election. The only material transparency election for a SARL is Article 239 bis AA of the CGI, under which a family SARL whose shareholders are exclusively natural persons in the direct line, or siblings, may opt for taxation as a partnership (régime des sociétés de personnes) instead of corporate income tax. An EURL with a single individual shareholder is taxed at the shareholder's income-tax level unless it opts for corporate income tax. Hybrid entity outcomes are governed by France's ATAD transposition rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for non-established foreign suppliers; for an established French SARL selling goods, the franchise exemption applies until annual taxable turnover reaches EUR 85,000 (main threshold) or EUR 93,500 (marginal tolerance), after which full VAT registration is mandatory. For services the threshold is EUR 37,500 (EUR 41,000 tolerance). Intra-Community acquisitions of goods by a VAT-registered SARL also require monitoring above EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating applies to exports of resin statues outside the European Union where the seller proves that the goods were physically sent from French customs territory to a non-EU destination and the export customs declaration was accepted by French Customs. Intra-EU supplies of goods to a VAT-registered business in another Member State are exempt (not zero-rated) upon the buyer's valid EU VAT number, subject to Intrastat reporting. Incoterms must support transfer of risk to the foreign buyer. Input VAT directly attributable to export transactions may be recovered provided the export documentary file satisfies Articles 262-263 CGI.

  • Customs export declaration (Déclaration en détail via the DELTA/GUN system) formally accepted by French Customs and showing the non-EU destination
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment from France to the non-EU consignee
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with Incoterms and value of the exported resin statues
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment evidence showing the export proceeds were actually received and repatriated to France
  • Export contract or purchase order linking the buyer, invoice, shipment, and payment

France mandates B2B electronic invoicing under the Facturation électronique reform applicable from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized enterprises and 1 September 2027 for smaller businesses. Invoices must be transmitted through a certified PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) using structured data APIs or through the public portal choral.finances.gouv.fr. There is no real-time fiscal stamping or pre-clearance of each individual invoice; instead, structured e-invoice data and e-reporting data are submitted electronically, and DGFiP performs digital audits. Export invoices must include all statutory French invoice mentions and supporting data retained for at least 6 years.

French Customs and DGFiP require production of the complete export file on request. If export zero-rating is unsupported or if the invoice is considered materially false, the VAT not priced out is assessed at 20%, with statutory late-payment interest and penalties: a 50% surtax under Article 1729 CGI for deliberate misstatement or concealed operation, 80% for abusive behavior, fraud by use of false invoices, or opposition to tax control. The standard retroactive reassessment period is three calendar years, extended to ten years for fully concealed activities. Criminal prosecution for export VAT fraud can lead to fines up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to seven years. Export zero-rating may be retrospectively clawed back and penalty interest charged if foreign exchange repatriation is not completed within 180 days or documentation is incomplete.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate shareholder are generally subject to 25% withholding tax, subject to treaty relief and statutory exemptions. A qualifying EU parent may be exempt under the Parent-Subsidiary regime, generally requiring a qualifying holding of at least 10% for at least two years, subject to anti-abuse and other conditions. Different domestic rules apply to individual recipients; the recipient's legal status must be verified.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

File within three months after the fiscal year-end. For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the statutory date is the second working day following 1 May; the tax administration generally allows an additional 15 calendar days for electronic filing. Pay the IS balance by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a 31 December year-end, generally 15 May. Quarterly instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December for a calendar-year company.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File documentation generally applies to French entities or French permanent establishments whose annual turnover or gross assets are at least EUR 400 million, and to specified entities in groups meeting the statutory ownership or control conditions where a qualifying group entity reaches that threshold. A separate annual summary declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies at a lower threshold of EUR 50 million in annual turnover or gross assets, subject to statutory group and control conditions. Related-party transactions must in all cases comply with the arm's-length principle, and the tax administration may require supporting evidence.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a general Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore regimes. French tax law nevertheless applies transfer-pricing rules, anti-abuse and sham-transaction doctrines, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled-foreign-company rules, and substance-based analysis in relevant contexts. A French SARL should maintain decision-making capacity, personnel or outsourced operational resources appropriate to its activities, accurate accounts, commercial evidence, and support for its pricing and financing arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest, and penalties that may reach 80% in specified cases. Criminal tax fraud may result in up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine; aggravated cases may carry up to seven years' imprisonment and a EUR 3 million fine, subject to statutory conditions and possible higher proportional penalties.

Director personal liability: Managers may face personal criminal liability where they personally participate in, direct, or knowingly facilitate fraud. Civil liability or recovery against a manager may arise under specific statutory conditions, including serious and repeated tax breaches. Imprisonment and travel restrictions are not automatic consequences of ordinary tax non-compliance.
Transfer-pricing misstatement or inadequate documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may adjust taxable profit, impose interest and applicable tax penalties, and apply documentation-related fines, including the statutory minimum or transaction-based penalty where required documentation is not produced.

Director personal liability: Directors may be exposed to civil or criminal liability where their own conduct constitutes fraud, falsification, or a separately sanctionable breach. An ordinary transfer-pricing adjustment does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Undeclared or improperly relieved outbound withholding tax

Statutory sanction: The French payer may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, interest, and applicable penalties. Treaty or directive relief may be denied where beneficial ownership, anti-abuse, holding, or documentation requirements are not met.

Director personal liability: Company-level liability is primary. Personal liability may arise only under specific statutory conditions or where a director knowingly participates in fraud or other unlawful conduct.
Shell, sham, or artificial arrangement

Statutory sanction: The administration may disregard the arrangement under applicable anti-abuse or sham-transaction rules, deny the tax benefit, reassess tax, and impose interest and enhanced penalties where statutory conditions are met.

Director personal liability: Personal sanctions require a legal basis and facts establishing individual participation, fraud, or a specific statutory liability. Corporate status alone does not create automatic personal liability.
Corporate and accounting record failures

Statutory sanction: Possible accounting adjustments, estimated assessments, denial of unsupported deductions, tax penalties, and separate accounting or commercial-law sanctions where applicable.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally liable for their own unlawful conduct or under specific statutory provisions. Civil, criminal, or travel restrictions are not automatic and require applicable legal grounds and procedures.
