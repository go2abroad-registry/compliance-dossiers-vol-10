Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital is imposed on an ordinary SARL (Article L.223-2, Code de commerce); the articles freely set the amount. Cash contributions are subscribed and at least one-fifth of each in-cash share's nominal value must be paid before filing (Article L.223-7). The corresponding funds must be deposited in a French bank blocked account (compte bloqué) opened on behalf of the company in formation, or covered by a joint-and-several bank guarantee; funds are released only after issuance of the Kbis. In-kind contributions exceeding €30,000 or more than 50% of capital require a commissaire aux apports report unless the partners unanimously waive it.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique; portals: impots.gouv.fr and formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Customs and export proof matters are handled by Direction Générale des Douanes et des Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident foreign entity supplying taxable goods or services in France: (1) confirm the French VAT place-of-supply nexus; (2) if established outside the European Union, appoint an accredited VAT fiscal representative unless an exemption or special regime applies; (3) file Form Si 1558-SD with DGFiP, attaching the foreign trade registry extract, constitutional/deed documents, director's identity documents, power of attorney, and evidence of the French nexus; (4) DGFiP validates the application and issues a French intra-community VAT number (format FR plus key and 11 digits). For the French-resident SARL: registration is effected via the single business formalities portal (Guichet Unique), which transmits the M0 filing to INSEE; INSEE issues the SIREN/SIRET identifiers; DGFiP then registers the entity for VAT and issues the intra-community VAT number automatically or upon application where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the SARL is a limited-liability opaque entity default-assessed to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés). Territorial scope: profits generated by the French SARL's French activities are within the French corporate tax net; foreign permanent-establishment income may be relieved under tax treaties and French territoriality rules. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable to this wholesale distribution activity.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists under French law. A French SARL is generally not eligible for pass-through taxation; narrow statutory exceptions allow certain family or qualifying small SARLs to elect for income tax (IR) treatment under CGI Art. 239 bis AA. For VAT, an entity may elect to form a single taxable person (assujetti unique/VAT group) under the conditions set by French VAT laws and EU VAT Directive. France applies the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) hybrid mismatch rules; there is no elective mechanism to reclassify an opaque SARL as a hybrid or reverse-hybrid entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business, the small-business VAT franchise exemption applies to sales of goods when annual turnover is below EUR 91,900 (CGI Art. 293 B), with threshold ceiling rules. For a non-established foreign supplier, there is generally no monetary registration threshold: the first taxable French supply triggers VAT registration unless a reverse-charge, OSS, or customs import VAT mechanism applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods from France to a destination outside the European Union qualifies for the 0% export exemption under CGI Art. 262(1) if the seller proves physical removal of the goods from EU customs territory and the transfer of ownership to the foreign purchaser. The zero rating also applies to intra-EU supplies of goods under CGI Art. 262 ter where the buyer is VAT-registered in another Member State and gives a valid intra-community VAT ID, the goods are transported out of France, and the seller files the recapitulative statement (DEB) and relevant Box 20 of the VAT return. Services may be outside French VAT where the place of supply is located offshore under the B2B general rule of CGI Art. 259; export or offshore-service claims require business records evidencing the service recipient's establishment and the actual benefit of the service outside France.

  • French export customs declaration (DAU/DEX) lodged through DELTA-X or ECUS, endorsed or electronically confirmed by French customs as proof of exit
  • Commercial invoice and packing list evidencing seller, buyer, goods description, quantity, unit price, Incoterms and destination
  • Transport document evidencing shipment from France to the third country or other EU Member State: Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, Air Waybill, or rail/combined-transport document
  • Proof of arrival or receipt in the destination foreign market (e.g., destination customs entry, signed POD) where available
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement record when required by a Letter of Credit, trade finance bank, or French exchange-control documentation; this is a banking/commercial document rather than a statutory French VAT zero-rating precondition

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting is being phased in under French legislation. From 1 September 2026, all French-established businesses must be able to receive e-invoices; large and mid-sized suppliers must issue via a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the Public Invoicing Portal (PPF/Chorus Pro), using Factur-X, UBL, or CII structured formats. Small and micro suppliers follow from 1 September 2027. There is no traditional digital stamping or continuous real-time fiscal clearance; instead, invoice and transaction data are reported electronically to DGFiP through the PPF/PDP ecosystem, enabling automated cross-validation with VAT returns and customs declarations. API connectivity and registered electronic identification are required for PDPs and large issuers.

French customs and tax authorities closely audit export files. If a zero-rated export is not substantiated, DGFiP may retrospectively impose French VAT at the standard 20% rate, recover input VAT credits previously granted, and apply statutory late interest plus penalties: 40% for intentional bad faith and up to 80% for fraudulent behavior or proven tax evasion under CGI Art. 1729. Customs may also levy fixed fines and proportional penalties where the export declaration is false or the goods are diverted from their declared destination. The ordinary statute of limitations is six years; in aggravated international tax fraud or anti-money-laundering connected cases, the recovery period may extend to ten years. Repeated or systematic failure to prove exports can trigger criminal tax fraud prosecution and disqualification for VAT refund status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies the euro-area fully liberalized capital and current account regime. Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) prohibit all restrictions on capital movements and payments between Member States and with third countries, subject only to EU safeguard measures, sanctions, and prudential requirements. Under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, Book I, Title V) resident legal entities may freely receive foreign direct investment, subscribe equity capital, open foreign-currency accounts, and remit dividends, royalties, interest, and trade proceeds without prior exchange-control authorization. The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Residual profit-remittance hurdles are limited to: (i) withholding tax on outbound dividends (0% for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders under Directive 2011/96/EU and 5%–15% treaty rates for other treaty shareholders; 30% statutory rate for non-treaty foreign individuals or companies absent treaty relief); (ii) EU/UN financial-sanctions screening (no business with designated persons in Russia, Belarus, Iran, Syria, North Korea, or Crimea); (iii) mandatory balance-of-payments statistical filings with Banque de France for any cross-border remittance of EUR 50,000 or more; (iv) French banking AML/KYC verification of the ultimate beneficial owners at 25% or above, including the 'Registre des bénéficiaires effectifs' filing; and (v) compliance with EU export-control and food-safety certification for the tallow-based hotpot base product. No foreign-exchange licensing, no required FX conversion, no surrender of export proceeds, and no central bank permission is required for the SARL's cross-border treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline exists under French law. The SARL may retain export receipts in EUR or in freely convertible foreign currency, onshore or offshore, with no obligation to convert into euros. The only binding obligations are: (i) statistical declaration to Banque de France of any cross-border payment equal to or exceeding EUR 50,000, filed by the SARL's authorized intermediary bank on the 'déclaration de règlement' balance-of-payments schedule; (ii) maintenance of full trade documentation (signed commercial invoice, export customs declaration (DAU/ECS), bill of lading/airway bill, and proof of receipt) to evidence the underlying transaction for VAT and customs audit; (iii) arm's-length pricing and transfer-pricing documentation for intercompany export sales; and (iv) EU/UN sanctions screening of all receiving counterparties. French banks typically request a 'good-end' confirmation of export proceeds within 7 business days of the maturity date, but this is a credit-policy convention, not a legal surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the central bank of France and the national authority for balance-of-payments statistics, cross-border financial transaction reporting, and foreign-exchange surveillance within the Eurosystem; coordinated with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for exchange-control policy and with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for prudential banking supervision and AML/CFT enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for operational multi-currency treasury and cross-border settlement, but functionally restricted. A FinTech EMI (e-money institution) may lawfully hold and process the SARL's operational receipts provided it holds a European Economic Area (EEA) payment/e-money license under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and is either authorized by the French ACPR or benefits from passporting rights into France. UK-only licensed EMIs lost EEA passporting post-Brexit and must operate through an EU/EEA subsidiary or agent. The SARL can use EMI rails (e.g., virtual IBANs, API treasury, multi-currency wallets, and FX conversion at live interbank rates) for operational settlements below EUR 50,000 and for supplier disbursements to China/Asia using the EMI's correspondent cloud. However, EMIs are legally prohibited from receiving deposits, granting credit, or issuing letters of credit and bank guarantees (French Monetary and Financial Code L526-1 et seq.); therefore the EMI is NOT viable for (i) the capital escrow deposit at incorporation, (ii) commercial LC issuance, or (iii) standby guarantees. The bank-account hierarchy should keep at least one Tier-1 credit institution account for capital and trade-finance instruments, with the EMI as a secondary treasury rail. Legal constraints: (a) the EMI must segregate client funds in safeguarding accounts; (b) the SarL's bank must obtain the EMI's authorization details for KYC; (c) French banks may impose the entity's own 'vetting' on EMIs whose BICs are on high-risk correspondent lists; and (d) any payment between the EMI and the SARL's Tier-1 account above EUR 50,000 is subject to Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the capital injection as a SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's own bank account to the SARL's temporary blocked capital-deposit account (compte de dépôt des fonds) opened at BNP Paribas or Société Générale before corporate registration. Protocol: (1) Currency and denomination: EUR only, with the sending bank converting investor currency at its spot rate. (2) Field 50K/52A: must show the actual shareholder as ordering customer; third-party remitters are not acceptable. (3) Field 59: the SARL's full legal name and IBAN. (4) Remittance information: 'Apport en capital — constitution de SARL [legal name]' with reference to the dated shareholders' resolution. (5) ISO 20022 purpose code: 'CAPC' (Capital Increase/Decrease) with a supplementary bank instruction code 'CORT' where applicable. (6) For French balance-of-payments statistics, the bank maps the transaction to Banque de France 'motif de règlement' category 'Investissement direct — apport en capital' and files the declaration automatically when the amount exceeds EUR 50,000. (7) The bank issues a 'certificat de dépôt des fonds' to the remitter, enabling the SARL's notary to complete the incorporation filing with the French company register. (8) Upon issuance of the Kbis extract, the escrow balance is released to the SARL's operational current account; the bank will require a copy of the Kbis and the updated RCS attestation as release condition. (9) For amounts above EUR 100,000, the bank also requests the shareholder's audited financial statements or bank reference to satisfy source-of-funds documentation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for operational multi-currency treasury and cross-border settlement, but functionally restricted. A FinTech EMI (e-money institution) may lawfully hold and process the SARL's operational receipts provided it holds a European Economic Area (EEA) payment/e-money license under PSD2 (Directive (EU) 2015/2366) and is either authorized by the French ACPR or benefits from passporting rights into France. UK-only licensed EMIs lost EEA passporting post-Brexit and must operate through an EU/EEA subsidiary or agent. The SARL can use EMI rails (e.g., virtual IBANs, API treasury, multi-currency wallets, and FX conversion at live interbank rates) for operational settlements below EUR 50,000 and for supplier disbursements to China/Asia using the EMI's correspondent cloud. However, EMIs are legally prohibited from receiving deposits, granting credit, or issuing letters of credit and bank guarantees (French Monetary and Financial Code L526-1 et seq.); therefore the EMI is NOT viable for (i) the capital escrow deposit at incorporation, (ii) commercial LC issuance, or (iii) standby guarantees. The bank-account hierarchy should keep at least one Tier-1 credit institution account for capital and trade-finance instruments, with the EMI as a secondary treasury rail. Legal constraints: (a) the EMI must segregate client funds in safeguarding accounts; (b) the SarL's bank must obtain the EMI's authorization details for KYC; (c) French banks may impose the entity's own 'vetting' on EMIs whose BICs are on high-risk correspondent lists; and (d) any payment between the EMI and the SARL's Tier-1 account above EUR 50,000 is subject to Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration.
