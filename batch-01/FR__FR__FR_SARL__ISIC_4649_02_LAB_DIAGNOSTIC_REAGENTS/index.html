Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to a SARL. Cash contributions must be paid up at least 20% upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Funds must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations, and the deposit certificate must accompany the filing. Contributions in kind must be fully transferred upon issuance unless otherwise structured under applicable law.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain building and fire-safety approval where construction or change of use requires it, maintain emergency access, fire detection, extinguishing systems, evacuation plans, and chemical storage controls, and complete any required inspection by the municipal authority or fire and rescue service (SDIS). Verify structural floor loading and racking capacity before installation. Apply the French Labour Code occupational-risk rules, including risk assessment, safety data sheets, employee training, personal protective equipment, exposure controls, and occupational-health arrangements. For an establishment open to the public, ERP fire and accessibility requirements may also apply.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to a SARL. Cash contributions must be paid up at least 20% upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Funds must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations, and the deposit certificate must accompany the filing. Contributions in kind must be fully transferred upon issuance unless otherwise structured under applicable law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the French Ministry of Economy and Finance. Resident businesses, including SARLs, are administered by the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); foreign non-established suppliers are handled by the dedicated non-resident business unit (Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères, Noisy-le-Grand). Official online portal: impots.gouv.fr (professional space). Customs matters are administered separately by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) through the DELTA customs clearance system.

E-government filing portal

Domestic SARL: incorporation is effected through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), which registers the company with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) and triggers INSEE issuance of the SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) identifiers; the local SIE then derives the intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN and opens the VAT account. Non-established foreign entity without a French branch: submit to the DGFiP non-resident unit a French VAT identification application accompanied by (i) certified articles of association or commercial registry extract with official French translation, (ii) proof of the foreign VAT or company number, (iii) identity documents of the legal representative, (iv) a power of attorney appointing a tax representative or sole contact, and (v) a brief description of intended French supplies; the unit assigns the FR + 11-digit VAT number. If the foreign entity has a French permanent establishment, it must also register through the Guichet Unique, thereby obtaining SIREN/SIRET and full IS/VAT obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: a commercial trading SARL is subject by default to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) at the 25% standard rate, with a 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying small enterprises. Pass-through eligibility is exceptional and limited to family-owned SARLs (SARL de famille) that validly opt for income-tax (IR) treatment at shareholder level. Territorial scope follows the French territoriality principle (CGI art. 209-I): IS is charged only on profits attributable to business operations carried on in France; profits of a foreign permanent establishment are excluded from the French tax base under qualifying conditions, and a foreign corporation without a French establishment is not chargeable to IS. Tax-exempt zone: none applicable — wholesale distribution of IVD reagents in France is not situated in a free zone and enjoys no general tax holiday; narrowly targeted incentive regimes (e.g., JEI status, patent box, territorial aid) are not relied upon here. — Domestic SARL: incorporation is effected through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), which registers the company with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) and triggers INSEE issuance of the SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) identifiers; the local SIE then derives the intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN and opens the VAT account. Non-established foreign entity without a French branch: submit to the DGFiP non-resident unit a French VAT identification application accompanied by (i) certified articles of association or commercial registry extract with official French translation, (ii) proof of the foreign VAT or company number, (iii) identity documents of the legal representative, (iv) a power of attorney appointing a tax representative or sole contact, and (v) a brief description of intended French supplies; the unit assigns the FR + 11-digit VAT number. If the foreign entity has a French permanent establishment, it must also register through the Guichet Unique, thereby obtaining SIREN/SIRET and full IS/VAT obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a commercial trading SARL is subject by default to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) at the 25% standard rate, with a 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying small enterprises. Pass-through eligibility is exceptional and limited to family-owned SARLs (SARL de famille) that validly opt for income-tax (IR) treatment at shareholder level. Territorial scope follows the French territoriality principle (CGI art. 209-I): IS is charged only on profits attributable to business operations carried on in France; profits of a foreign permanent establishment are excluded from the French tax base under qualifying conditions, and a foreign corporation without a French establishment is not chargeable to IS. Tax-exempt zone: none applicable — wholesale distribution of IVD reagents in France is not situated in a free zone and enjoys no general tax holiday; narrowly targeted incentive regimes (e.g., JEI status, patent box, territorial aid) are not relied upon here.

Available election forms

Available elections under French law: (i) family-SARL transparency election (CGI art. 239 AA) converting the SARL to income-tax (IR) treatment with taxation at the shareholder level; (ii) VAT accounting options — election to use the actual invoice basis, the simplified actual basis (régime simplifié d'imposition), or the payments (cash) basis where the statutory turnover thresholds are met; (iii) fiscal integration election (régime d'intégration fiscale) where the SARL is at least 95%-owned by a French parent and the group satisfies the consolidation conditions; (iv) no US-style check-the-box regime — a French SARL cannot elect arbitrary entity classification; hybrid mismatch outcomes are neutralized by ATAD 2 rules implemented through CGI art. 205 et seq., with no elective anti-hybrid instrument available to taxpayers.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Effectively no monetary registration threshold for taxable economic activity: any entity making taxable supplies with a place of supply in France is required to register and charge VAT, except where the client is liable under the reverse charge. Resident micro-businesses may benefit from the domestic small-business exemption (franchise en base, CGI art. 293 B) up to €91,900 p.a. for supplies of goods and €36,800 p.a. for services, with tolerance ceilings of €101,000 and €39,100 respectively; below these ceilings the exempt business issues invoices without VAT and is not registered for VAT. The franchise exemption is not available to non-established foreign suppliers. For EU sellers making intra-EU distance sales of goods, the pan-EU €10,000 registration threshold applies in the destination Member State; non-EU sellers of goods valued at or below €150 may use the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% (exempt with full input VAT credit) rate is available for: (i) definitive export of goods dispatched or transported by the vendor or a third party on its behalf to a destination outside the European Union (CGI art. 262-I-1°), provided the vendor holds complete and coherent documentary proof of physical exit; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered customer in another EU Member State (CGI art. 262-I-2°), reflected in the EC Sales List and supported by a valid EU customer VAT number; and (iii) certain services directly connected with exported goods, such as transport, handling and freight, supplied by the exporter as principal (CGI art. 262-II). Intra-EU B2B services follow the general place-of-supply rule, which shifts taxation to the customer's Member State. Domestic French sales of IVD reagents are generally taxable at the 20% standard rate; the 0% rate is reserved for genuine exports and intra-Community supplies satisfying the evidentiary conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration — the completed electronic export declaration (document administratif unique / EAD) filed through the DELTA system, showing the exporter EORI number, goods description, value, destination and customs office of departure, together with a dated proof of exit matching the declaration
  • Transport documentation — ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note or rail waybill evidencing dispatch from France to the non-EU destination under terms consistent with the sale contract
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list identifying the buyer, goods, quantities, unit values, export contract terms and destination country
  • Inward remittance / FX settlement certificate — bank SWIFT credit advice or certificate of exchange (attestation de change) proving collection of the foreign-currency proceeds by a French intermediary within the statutory repatriation period

France is transitioning to mandatory structured B2B e-invoicing: from 1 September 2026, large and medium-sized enterprises must issue e-invoices; from 1 September 2027, small and micro-enterprises must do the same. All e-invoices must be transmitted through a DGFiP-registered dematerialisation platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire — PDP) or the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation — PPF), using structured formats such as Factur-X, UBL or CII. Invoice data and cross-border transaction data (e-reporting) are transmitted to DGFiP within one working day of the relevant transaction in machine-readable form. France does not apply real-time fiscal stamping or continuous control; however, the 2016 anti-fraud law requires certified cash-register software with tamper-proof audit trails for retail sales, and all VAT returns (CA3) are filed electronically via impots.gouv.fr or EDI/API channels.

Export zero-rating is a high-risk audit area in the IVD distribution sector. The DGFiP requires the full documentation bundle to be produced on demand; where the export cannot be substantiated, the zero rate is withdrawn and output VAT is reassessed at 20%, the input VAT originally credited may be reversed where fraud in the invoice chain is shown, and the taxpayer is charged late-payment interest (intérêts de retard) at 0.20% per month plus penalties: 40% of the reassessed tax for deliberate misconduct (manquement délibéré) and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of right. Fabricated customs or transport documents also expose the exporter to DGDDI customs sanctions, including confiscation of the goods and pecuniary sanctions of one to three times the evaded sums, as well as criminal liability. Under EU VAT fraud doctrine, a supplier who knew or should have known of an export fraud may be jointly liable for the unpaid VAT. The ordinary VAT reassessment window is three years (art. L. 169 LPF), extended in cases of omission, concealed activity or fraud.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full liberalization of both capital and current account transactions for euro-area residents and non-resident counterparties. There are no exchange-control authorizations for ordinary trade, direct investment, portfolio investment, or profit remittances. The euro is fully convertible at market rates onshore and offshore; profit and dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is subject only to standard withholding tax (default 30% for non-treaty residents, reducible under an applicable double-tax treaty) rather than convertibility or capital-control restrictions. The only administrative reporting burden is the monthly and quarterly balance-of-payments declaration to the Banque de France for cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline for export receipts. EUR and foreign-currency export proceeds may be retained in export proceeds accounts (comptes en devises) at ACPR-licensed banks, used directly to settle trade payables, or converted at prevailing market rates at the exporter's discretion. The only obligation is statistical in nature: the exporter must report the underlying transaction through the bank to the Banque de France balance-of-payments surveillance system for values above the EUR 10,000 threshold.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) acting as the national central bank and balance-of-payments statistical authority; prudential supervision of credit institutions and licensed financial intermediaries is vested in the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) holding residual competence over exchange-control policy within the framework of the European Union free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (whether UK-authorised, Lithuanian, Irish, or German-licensed) may operate in France only under an ACPR authorisation or an EU/EEA passport issued under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the E-Money Directive (EMD2). Feasibility rating for this SARL is Medium-Low for primary treasury operations: such EMIs are legally acceptable for inbound collections and small outbound supplier payments, but are not acceptable as the sole banking counterparty for capital escrow, LC confirmations, or cross-border trade settlement. Non-EEA offshore EMIs without a passport cannot lawfully provide payment accounts to a French-domiciled SARL and represent an AML-completion risk; a domestic Tier-1 bank must remain the primary clearing node.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound EUR capital contributions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA remitters) to the dedicated capital escrow account held at a Tier-1 French bank, with the beneficiary reference stating 'Apport en capital / SARL [name]'. The required balance-of-payments purpose code is the direct-investment code (code 'K' for equity capital under the Banque de France CODEP classification), and transactions above EUR 10,000 must be included in the monthly BOP declaration. The bank will pre-clear the credit only after receiving the K-bis, the UBO filing, the subscription minutes, and the equity contribution agreement to satisfy AML source-of-funds checks; upon release of the escrow block, the funds are credited to the operational account and the entity must file the capital-increase formality with the commercial registry within the statutory delay.EU and EEA supplier settlements may be executed through SEPA Credit Transfers at near-zero latency and cost. Non-EU supplier payments, especially for ELISA reagents sourced from China, the United States, or the UK, must be cleared via SWIFT MT103 with a trade-related purpose code (code 'T' or a valid commercial-services sub-code) and, where a supplier demands transport risk mitigation, via irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued by the SARL's French bank and advised/confirmed under ICC UCP 600. For recurring reagent replenishment, a bank-guaranteed standby LC or an open-account TT facility paired with trade credit insurance is the recommended clearing configuration. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs (whether UK-authorised, Lithuanian, Irish, or German-licensed) may operate in France only under an ACPR authorisation or an EU/EEA passport issued under the revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the E-Money Directive (EMD2). Feasibility rating for this SARL is Medium-Low for primary treasury operations: such EMIs are legally acceptable for inbound collections and small outbound supplier payments, but are not acceptable as the sole banking counterparty for capital escrow, LC confirmations, or cross-border trade settlement. Non-EEA offshore EMIs without a passport cannot lawfully provide payment accounts to a French-domiciled SARL and represent an AML-completion risk; a domestic Tier-1 bank must remain the primary clearing node.
