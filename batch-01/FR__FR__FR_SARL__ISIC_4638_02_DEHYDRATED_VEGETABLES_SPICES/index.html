Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). The principal portal is impots.gouv.fr. Business formation and registration data are filed through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), while INSEE issues the SIREN and SIRET identifiers.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is formed through the Guichet unique managed by INPI. The filing includes constitutional documents, registered office evidence, beneficial-owner information, management identification, business activity, and, where applicable, establishment and customs information. Following registration, INSEE issues the SIREN and establishment-specific SIRET. The competent SIE assigns the French intra-Community VAT number, generally formatted as FR plus a two-character control key and the nine-digit SIREN. The entity must activate its professional account on impots.gouv.fr and file the applicable VAT registration or option forms. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères for a French VAT number when it carries out transactions for which France has taxing jurisdiction. A non-EU entity generally must appoint an accredited French fiscal representative unless an applicable statutory exemption applies; EU-established businesses are generally not required to appoint one. VAT registration does not by itself create a French permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by default. A family SARL meeting statutory conditions may elect income-tax treatment, and a qualifying newly formed SARL may elect temporary income-tax treatment for up to five financial years, subject to eligibility, shareholder, turnover, employee, and activity conditions. The election is not a United States-style check-the-box election and is subject to notification and timing requirements. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implement the EU ATAD framework and may deny deductions, include income, or impose other corrective treatment for hybrid mismatches; they do not provide a general elective hybrid classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France does not provide a single universal VAT-registration threshold for all taxable businesses. The French VAT franchise regime generally permits eligible established businesses to remain outside VAT collection while annual domestic turnover does not exceed approximately EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and specified commercial activities or EUR 37,500 for services, subject to statutory annual threshold and tolerance rules. Businesses exceeding the applicable limits, opting for VAT, importing goods, making taxable intra-Community transactions, or carrying out transactions requiring immediate registration may need VAT registration regardless of those franchise thresholds. The EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold is relevant principally to certain intra-Community distance sales and electronically supplied, telecommunications, and broadcasting services, not to ordinary domestic wholesale exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT under Article 262 of the French Tax Code if the goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU and the exporter can prove both the export transaction and exit from the EU customs territory. The exporter must be identified for VAT purposes where required and retain a customs export declaration, exit confirmation, commercial invoice, transport evidence, and transaction records. Intra-Community supplies of goods may be exempt from French VAT where the customer is VAT-identified in another EU Member State, the goods are transported from France to that Member State, and the supplier retains the required VAT-number and transport evidence. Services supplied to a business customer established outside France are generally taxed according to place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of French VAT rather than technically zero-rated; specific exceptions apply. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, import documents, customs records, and evidence that the costs relate to taxable business activities.

  • Electronic export customs declaration filed through the applicable French customs system, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs release and exit results, such as the electronic export exit confirmation or equivalent proof of exit
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, consignee, goods, value, delivery terms, and VAT treatment
  • Packing list and product description, including quantities, weights, and commodity classification
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent transport document
  • Purchase order, sales contract, delivery evidence, and customer account records
  • Proof of transport and destination outside the European Union
  • Export permits, sanitary or phytosanitary documents, certificates of origin, and product-specific documents where applicable to dehydrated vegetables or spices
  • Evidence of payment or bank settlement where commercially available; an inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory prerequisite for export VAT exemption
  • For intra-Community supplies rather than third-country exports, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number and evidence of transport to the other Member State

France does not currently operate universal real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory digital stamping for all private-sector invoices. The e-invoicing reform requires all French-established businesses to be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Mandatory issuance begins on 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized enterprises and on 1 September 2027 for small and micro-enterprises, subject to legally applicable implementation rules. Electronic invoices must use an approved platform or public platform architecture and structured or hybrid formats. Transaction and payment data reporting (e-reporting) will apply to specified business-to-consumer, cross-border, and payment transactions. Invoices to French public-sector customers are handled through Chorus Pro. Export invoices generally remain outside the domestic B2B e-invoicing scope where the transaction is outside the French domestic reporting perimeter, but customs and VAT evidence must be retained.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption may be reassessed if customs exit evidence, transport proof, customer identification, invoices, or transaction records are missing, inconsistent, or unreliable. The reassessed VAT may include late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month, together with tax penalties. Common penalty levels include 10% for late or omitted filing, 40% for deliberate non-compliance, and up to 80% for fraudulent activity or abusive concealment, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards. Customs breaches may additionally trigger duty and import-VAT assessments, customs penalties, seizure, or forfeiture. Serious intentional tax fraud may result in criminal prosecution and imprisonment or fines for responsible individuals, with separate or increased sanctions potentially applicable to legal entities. Refunds or input-VAT credits obtained on the basis of fraudulent or unsupported export claims may be clawed back with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area Member State with full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-65 TFEU, Council Directive 88/361/EEC, and the French Code monétaire et financier. The euro is freely convertible without surrender rules, FX licensing, or profit-remittance quotas. A French SARL may invoice in EUR or any foreign currency and may transfer dividends, repayment of shareholder loans, and trade payments freely through authorized banking channels. The only practical hurdles for profit remittance to non-resident shareholders are standard AML/KYC due diligence, the obligation to prove distributable profits per Article L232-11 of the French Code de commerce, and, where applicable, proof of tax-residence for withholding-tax treaty relief; no FX law obstacle exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in original currency, held in any foreign-currency account, converted to EUR, or used to settle external payables without authorization. The relevant obligations are: (i) statistical reporting to the Banque de France, via the receiving/sending bank, for every cross-border payment exceeding €12,500 (BOP reporting), classified under merchandise trade and direct investment categories; (ii) preservation of underlying export documentation (commercial invoices, packing lists, customs declarations, transport documents) for ten years under commercial law; (iii) compliance with EU/VAT rules on physical export declaration (EORI registration and DTI/SAD customs filings); there is no fixed timeline or surrender ratio.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), in statistical cooperation with the Direction Générale du Trésor (General Directorate of the Treasury, French Ministry of the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty); prudential and AML supervision delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution). France has no separate foreign-exchange control administration; exchange-control authority is exercised through the Banque de France balance-of-payments (BOP) regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM. Cross-border digital EMIs (e-money institutions authorized in Luxembourg, Ireland, Lithuania, or other EU states and validly passported through the ACPR under PSD2) can reliably support the SARL's operating treasury — collection of export proceeds, TT/Wire supplier disbursements, multicurrency account balances, and basic FX conversion. Legal constraints: (1) the French banking monopoly in Article L511-1 of the Code monétaire et financier reserves deposit-taking and credit operations to licensed credit institutions, so an EMI cannot grant credit or issue commercial letters of credit; (2) under Articles L526-1 to L526-34, the EMI must validly hold a French or passported EU license and an ACPR-registered agent arrangement to execute French payments; (3) if the EMI issues a non-French IBAN that is not accepted by French public administrations, treasury friction may arise; (4) LCs under UCP600 are impossible through a pure EMI, requiring a Tier-1 bank or a licensed bank partner. Overall: adequate for TT/Wire clearing, inadequate as sole trade-finance rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscriptions or shareholder advances must be remitted to the SARL's French operational account: for EUR flows, use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) with the IBAN and BIC of the French bank; for non-EUR flows, use SWIFT MT103 with Field 50K (ordering customer), Field 59 (beneficiary SARL, BIC/IBAN), and Field 70 Remittance Information clearly stating 'Apport en capital — augmentation de capital — SARL [Name]' or 'Compte courant d'associé' for shareholder loans. France applies no exchange-control prior approval; the only regulatory layer is the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical declaration, executed by the beneficiary bank for any cross-border credit above €12,500, classified under BOP direct-investment categories. The receiving bank will demand: the capital-increase deed (décision de l'associé unique/procès-verbal), a deposit certificate from the escrow bank or notary, the acte de dépôt de fonds, updated Kbis, RBE confirmation, source-of-funds bank statements of the subscribing shareholder, and identity documents of the legal representatives. All funds must flow through the same banking channel as the declared UBO structure.Outbound trade settlements should be executed through the following clearing rails: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) for EUR payments to EU suppliers using IBAN/BIC with Field 70 invoice references; (2) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer for non-EUR suppliers, quoting the commercial invoice number, contract reference, and INCOTERMS in Field 70, with the SARL as ordering party and any necessary correspondent-bank instructions in Field 72B; (3) irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit (Commercial LCs) governed by ICC UCP600, issued or received through French banks and transmitted by SWIFT MT700, with confirmation through correspondents in the seller's jurisdiction; (4) standby LCs and documentary collection (DP/DA) as supplements. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: MEDIUM. Cross-border digital EMIs (e-money institutions authorized in Luxembourg, Ireland, Lithuania, or other EU states and validly passported through the ACPR under PSD2) can reliably support the SARL's operating treasury — collection of export proceeds, TT/Wire supplier disbursements, multicurrency account balances, and basic FX conversion. Legal constraints: (1) the French banking monopoly in Article L511-1 of the Code monétaire et financier reserves deposit-taking and credit operations to licensed credit institutions, so an EMI cannot grant credit or issue commercial letters of credit; (2) under Articles L526-1 to L526-34, the EMI must validly hold a French or passported EU license and an ACPR-registered agent arrangement to execute French payments; (3) if the EMI issues a non-French IBAN that is not accepted by French public administrations, treasury friction may arise; (4) LCs under UCP600 are impossible through a pure EMI, requiring a Tier-1 bank or a licensed bank partner. Overall: adequate for TT/Wire clearing, inadequate as sole trade-finance rail.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard French corporate income tax rate, CGI art. 219, applicable from 1 January 2022); reduced rate of 15% applies to the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs (turnover below EUR 10 million and fully paid-up capital); a 3.3% social surcharge applies only where the annual income tax exceeds EUR 763,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic French withholding tax (CGI art. 119 bis) on dividend distributions to non-French corporate shareholders that do not qualify for the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; 30% flat withholding for distributions to non-resident individuals (12.8% income tax plus 17.2% social levies); 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the EU/EEA corporate shareholder holds directly at least 5% of the SARL's capital for two consecutive years; under the France-China DTA the rate may be reduced to 10% for a corporate shareholder holding at least 25% of the share capital, subject to beneficial ownership evidence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of up to five years under CGI art. 1741; increased to EUR 3,000,000 and seven years for organised fraud; civil re-assessment plus 0.20% monthly interest, with 40% penalty for deliberate concealment and 80% penalty for fraud or abuse of law (CGI art. 1729 and 1729-0 A).

Director personal liability: The gérant and signatories may be held jointly and severally liable for the SARL's unpaid corporate income tax, VAT and social contributions under CGI art. L266 LPF where they organised the concealment; courts may impose an interdiction de gérer, an interdiction du territoire français, attachment of personal assets, and a ban on managing any French company.
Shell Company / Fictitious Entity

Statutory sanction: Invocation of the abuse-of-law procedure (CGI art. L64 LPF) permits the tax administration to disregard the artificial entity, recharacterize the contracts and reattribute the export income to the truly controlling foreign party; an 80% penalty applies where the artificiality is deliberate (CGI art. 1729-0 A); the Registry may also strike the company off the RCS.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain a fictitious structure are jointly liable for all taxes evaded (CGI art. AML and Financial Crime
