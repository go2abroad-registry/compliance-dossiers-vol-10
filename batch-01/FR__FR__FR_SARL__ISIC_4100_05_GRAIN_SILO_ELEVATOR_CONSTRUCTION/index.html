Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

French Directorate-General of Public Finances (Direction générale des finances publiques, DGFiP), administered through the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official tax portal impots.gouv.fr. Corporate formation and business-registration filings are submitted through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is formed through the INPI Guichet unique, which transmits the formation filing to the competent registries and public authorities. Following registration, the entity receives a SIREN number for the legal entity, a SIRET number for each establishment, and an intracommunity VAT number beginning with FR. The DGFiP assigns or confirms the corporate tax account and VAT obligations through the competent SIE. The company must provide its articles of association, registration extract, registered-office evidence, directors' and shareholders' information, business activity description, bank details where requested, and beneficial-owner information. A foreign entity carrying on taxable activities in France must obtain a French SIREN/SIRET and VAT registration through the INPI process or the competent foreign-entity tax service, generally the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères, depending on the activity and filing circumstances. An EU-established business may use the OSS regime for eligible cross-border B2C supplies but must obtain French VAT registration where French domestic VAT obligations, local establishments, imports, or non-OSS transactions require it. VAT identification can be verified through the EU VIES system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). An SARL satisfying statutory conditions may elect for partnership-style income taxation (impôt sur le revenu, IR), including an election available to certain newly formed companies for a limited period and a family-SARL election where all shareholders are qualifying family members and the statutory conditions are met. The election is made with the competent tax authority and is subject to eligibility, timing, and revocation rules. France has no general US-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing ATAD II can deny deductions, impose inclusion, or otherwise neutralize arrangements involving hybrid entities or instruments and related-party or structured transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no universal VAT registration threshold for all taxable businesses. Taxable supplies in France generally create VAT obligations unless an exemption applies. The domestic French franchise en base de TVA may apply to eligible small businesses below the applicable statutory turnover thresholds, generally EUR 25,000 of prior-year turnover and EUR 27,500 of current-year turnover for the general activities threshold under the current framework, subject to activity-specific thresholds, legislative changes, and transitional rules. A business exceeding the applicable threshold, opting for ordinary VAT treatment, importing goods, making transactions requiring a VAT number, or otherwise falling outside the franchise must register and comply with VAT obligations. Construction activities may also trigger sector-specific invoicing, reverse-charge, and subcontracting rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT under Article 262 I of the French Tax Code where the supplier, an authorized intermediary, or the customer completes the export formalities and the goods physically leave the EU customs territory. The supplier must retain reliable evidence linking the commercial invoice, goods, export declaration, and exit confirmation. Intra-Community supplies of goods may be exempt where the customer is identified for VAT in another EU Member State, the goods are transported from France to that Member State, and the supplier holds the required VAT numbers and transport evidence. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore. B2B services are generally located at the customer's establishment under the EU place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of French VAT, while specific services, including services connected with French immovable property, remain taxable in France. Construction and installation services connected with immovable property must be analyzed according to the place-of-supply rules and may not qualify as exports.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, contractual terms, and VAT treatment
  • Electronic export declaration accepted by French customs, including the Movement Reference Number or equivalent customs reference
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent evidence that the goods exited the European Union customs territory
  • Transport document, such as a bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or carrier delivery record
  • Packing list and, where applicable, proof of loading and delivery
  • Purchase order, contract, and payment evidence linking the shipment to the transaction
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number and transport evidence
  • For VAT refunds, VAT invoices, import VAT documents, accounting records, and proof that the claimant incurred the VAT in the course of taxable business activities
  • An inward-remittance certificate is not a general French statutory requirement for claiming export VAT exemption; banking evidence may nevertheless support the commercial substance and audit trail.

France is introducing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and electronic transaction-data reporting through approved private platforms or the public platform designated under the reform. All French businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Issuing obligations apply from 1 September 2026 to large companies and in-scope intermediate-sized enterprises and from 1 September 2027 to small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises, subject to the legally applicable timetable. France generally operates a post-transaction reporting model rather than universal real-time invoice clearance. Cross-border B2B transactions and B2C transactions may require electronic reporting of transaction data and, where relevant, payment data. Invoices must satisfy French VAT content, authenticity, integrity, and retention requirements. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. Import and export customs declarations are submitted electronically through the French customs systems, principally DELTA IE and related customs services.

France has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. Export VAT exemption or deduction may be denied or clawed back if the supplier cannot prove physical export, valid customer status, transport, customs exit, or the taxable-business connection. The DGFiP may assess unpaid VAT, late-payment interest, and tax penalties. The standard penalty for an intentional breach may be 40 percent of the reassessed tax, while fraudulent conduct may attract an 80 percent penalty, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards. Failure to produce records can result in rejection of the exemption, estimated assessment, or reversal of input VAT recovery. False customs declarations may also trigger customs recovery, customs penalties, seizure measures, and potentially criminal liability. Repeated or deliberate VAT fraud may result in criminal prosecution and additional financial penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of the euro on both current and capital account by virtue of French law and Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union; no foreign exchange control authorization is required for inbound capital injection, trade settlement, profit distribution, or cross-border borrowing. Profit remittance by a French SARL is structurally unencumbered at the FX level; the only practical constraints are corporate law (répartition of distributable profits) and the applicable dividend withholding tax (25% statutory, up to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or treaty exemption).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts under the French Code monétaire et financier; since the 1990 liberalization, exporters may freely retain proceeds in foreign currency or convert to euros. The sole compliance duty is statistical: the importing/exporting bank must include transactions in the balance-of-payments report to Banque de France (using BPM6-compliant purpose codes) for threshold-exceeding operations.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as national central bank; foreign exchange and balance-of-payments regulatory competence is shared with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) under the Ministry of Economy, Finance, and Recovery.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-low for complete treasury orchestration: ACPR-licensed and EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under PSD2 can provide efficient SEPA/instant cross-border payment rail, multi-currency IBANs, and expense management, and are viable as a secondary operational payment layer. However, EMIs in France are legally barred from deposit-taking, credit intermediation, and issuing documentary credits; they cannot independently issue commercial letters of credit, guarantees, or URDG instruments, and settlement through an EMI still requires a partner credit institution for SWIFT MT103 correspondent clearing and for capital-escrow certification. For the grain-silo EPC model (milestone wires and LCs), a fully licensed credit institution remains mandatory as primary treasury counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injections into the SARL must be executed by SWIFT MT103 wire to a French-domiciled blocked or operational account with clear remittance purpose 'RE: Capital Increase — SARL [Name]' and the French BOP/transmission purpose code corresponding to direct investment capital (P801/P802 under BPM6 classification). The funds are frozen in a depository (bank or notary) until the RCS registration or publication of the modification is completed; the bank issues a deposit certificate (attestation de dépôt des fonds) required for the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce filing. For incoming EUR from EU parents, routing over TARGET2/SEPA (SCT) is frictionless; for USD and non-EUR funds, the bank converts or credits a foreign-currency working account as designated, with no prior FX authorization. Verification pack: shareholder resolution, appraisal report for in-kind contributions if any, signed capital increase deed (sous seing privé), and K-bis update after registration.Trade settlements for the silo elevator project are routed primarily over (i) SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for subcontractor payments, aeration-tower equipment suppliers, and freight/shipping charges; (ii) irrevocable commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) governed by ICC rules, with deferred or at-sight payment structured upon certificate of provisional acceptance, enabling counterparties to finance the lead time; and (iii) demand guarantees/standby LCs (URDG 758) for performance and advance-payment security. Euro-denominated disbursements settle instantly via SEPA Credit Transfer/SEPA Instant on the TARGET2 platform of Banque de France; US-dollar disbursements are cleared on the correspondent banking network of the Paris-based clearing bank (BIC correspondent/CHIPS). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% generally applies to dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate shareholder, subject to applicable treaty relief, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions, and anti-abuse rules. French resident individual recipients are generally subject to a 12.8% income-tax withholding, with social contributions handled under separate rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and tax package are generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. For financial years ending on 31 December, the filing deadline is generally the second business day after 1 May of the following year. Corporate income tax instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following the financial year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million. French Local File and Master File obligations generally apply where the French entity or the relevant French tax group exceeds the statutory turnover or gross-assets threshold, or where the related foreign enterprise meets the corresponding conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone regime named Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore ESR regimes. Substance is nevertheless relevant under French transfer-pricing rules, the beneficial-ownership requirement, the general anti-abuse doctrine, the principal-purpose test in treaties, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, and EU anti-tax-avoidance measures. A French SARL should maintain genuine French decision-making, qualified personnel, premises or operational capacity appropriate to its construction activity, commercial risk control, contract management, technical supervision, and contemporaneous evidence of services received or supplied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, 40% or 80% tax penalties depending on conduct, late-payment interest, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that may include imprisonment for up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000; aggravated cases may reach seven years and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur criminal liability, personal fines, management bans, civil liability for deliberate or serious tax misconduct, and liability under French tax recovery provisions. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from judicial measures or an outstanding criminal proceeding.
Transfer-Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to arm's-length profit, late-payment interest, and documentation penalties that may be calculated by reference to the transaction amount or the reassessed adjustment, subject to statutory minimums and limits. Severe cases may also generate a 40% or 80% penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for deliberate mismanagement, criminal exposure where documents are falsified or fraud is established, and personal liability for tax recovery where statutory conditions for management fault are met.
Fictitious Services and Shell-Company Conduct

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, withholding-tax reassessment, anti-abuse adjustment, penalties of up to 80% in aggravated cases, potential fraud, forgery, fraudulent insolvency, or money-laundering prosecution, and possible corporate dissolution or management prohibition.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be personally prosecuted for participation, complicity, concealment, or money laundering; sanctions can include imprisonment, fines, asset confiscation, management bans, and civil restitution. A shell company is not unlawful solely because it is foreign or low-substance; the offense arises from its use for unlawful or abusive conduct.
Undeclared Dividend, Interest, or Royalty Payments

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax, late-payment interest, penalties, potential denial of deductions, and joint or payer liability where French withholding obligations were not correctly performed.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for deliberate withholding failures, fraudulent documentation, or participation in concealment. Civil and criminal consequences depend on intent and the amount involved.
VAT and Construction Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties generally ranging from 10% to 80%, invoice penalties, suspension of VAT numbers in serious cases, and criminal prosecution for organized or fraudulent schemes.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally liable for intentional VAT fraud, false invoicing, or serious management fault and may face imprisonment, fines, confiscation, and management restrictions.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist-Financing Controls

Statutory sanction: Regulatory, administrative, and criminal consequences may include substantial fines, transaction restrictions, professional sanctions, asset freezing, and money-laundering imprisonment of up to five years and fines of up to EUR 375,000; aggravated forms may carry higher penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally liable for knowing participation, deliberate blindness, or concealment. Asset confiscation, management prohibition, and judicial travel restrictions may apply; travel bans are not automatic.
Beneficial-Ownership and Corporate-Registry Failure

Statutory sanction: Court orders, fines, registry restrictions, disclosure requirements, and possible criminal penalties for false declarations or concealment. Related tax, money-laundering, and fraud sanctions may apply independently.

Director personal liability: The legal representative may incur personal criminal liability for false or incomplete filings and may face fines, imprisonment where applicable, management bans, and civil damages.
Accounting, Books, and False-Document Offenses

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, accounting and tax penalties, rejection of accounts, and criminal sanctions for forgery, use of forged documents, fraudulent accounting, or obstruction.

Director personal liability: Directors, finance officers, and participating advisers may face personal criminal prosecution, imprisonment, fines, confiscation, management bans, and civil claims by the company, creditors, or tax authority.
Construction-Contract and Profit-Recognition Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Restatement of taxable income, tax penalties, late-payment interest, audit adjustments across multiple years, and possible fraud or false-accounting prosecution where manipulation is intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for intentional misstatement, breach of management duties, creditor harm, and tax fraud. Professional advisers may also face liability where they knowingly participated.
Abusive Debt, Thin Capitalization, and Hybrid Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Interest deduction limitation, recharacterization of debt as equity or dividend, withholding-tax reassessment, anti-hybrid denial, transfer-pricing adjustment, and penalties for abusive or fraudulent arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability and, where concealment or falsification is proven, criminal tax-fraud or money-laundering liability.
Sanctions, Export-Control, and Prohibited-Payment Risk

Statutory sanction: Payment blocking, asset freezing, administrative fines, contract invalidation, procurement exclusion, and criminal sanctions under applicable French, European Union, and international sanctions regimes.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, confiscation, and professional or management bans. Ignorance is not a defense where mandatory screening and escalation controls were deliberately avoided.
Failure to Preserve Tax and Transfer-Pricing Evidence

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions, adverse inference during audit, transfer-pricing adjustment, documentation penalties, withholding reassessment, and late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for gross negligence or deliberate destruction of records and criminal exposure if evidence was falsified, concealed, or destroyed to obstruct an audit.
