Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS) via the INPI Guichet Unique portal at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL); general company provisions L.210-1 to L.210-10; register formalities under L.123-1 to L.123-17 and R.123-101 et seq.; Guichet Unique teleprocedure per Law PACTE 2019-486.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital; the statutes may fix the share capital at EUR 1 (Article L.223-2). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through impots.gouv.fr and the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). French business formation and registration filings are submitted through the INPI-operated Guichet unique; the resulting registration data are transmitted to the relevant authorities.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered through the INPI Guichet unique and receives a SIREN and SIRET after registration in the Registre national des entreprises. The competent SIE assigns or confirms the French intracommunity VAT number, generally in the format FR plus a two-digit key and the SIREN. The company must activate its professional account on impots.gouv.fr for tax filings and payments. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in France without a French establishment applies to the DGFiP Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères for VAT registration before or upon commencing taxable transactions. There is generally no turnover threshold for a non-established taxable person. A non-EU/EEA-established applicant may need to appoint an accredited French fiscal representative, subject to applicable legal exceptions; an EU-established applicant generally does not.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Eligible SARLs may elect temporary partnership taxation under the conditions and time limits in the French Tax Code, generally for up to five fiscal years. A SARL formed by members of the same family may, if statutory conditions are met, elect partnership taxation without that five-year limit. A SARL may also elect the special partnership-tax treatment available to a qualifying SARL de famille. France does not have a US-style check-the-box regime. Classification and treaty treatment depend on French law and the entity's legal characteristics; hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or require income inclusion in specified cases.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to a non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in France; VAT registration is generally required from the first taxable transaction. For eligible French-established small businesses, the domestic VAT exemption (franchise en base) is subject to statutory turnover thresholds and conditions, which vary by activity and applicable tax year; it does not generally exempt a non-established supplier from registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the EU may be exempt from French VAT with a right to deduct input VAT when statutory conditions are met and the supplier can substantiate that the goods left the EU. Intra-EU supplies are subject to separate exemption rules, including customer VAT-number and transport-evidence requirements. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: place-of-supply rules determine whether French VAT applies, and specific exemptions may apply to qualifying services directly connected with exports or international transport. Marine dredging and seawall works require transaction-specific analysis, including the location of immovable-property-related services, customer status, and any applicable domestic reverse-charge rules.

  • Export customs declaration with the applicable export MRN and electronic confirmation of exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and contract identifying the goods, customer, destination, and applicable VAT exemption basis
  • Bill of lading, sea waybill, or other carrier transport document, together with delivery and transport records
  • Customer order, proof of payment, and accounting records reconciling the transaction and export proceeds
  • For services, contract, work records, location evidence, customer VAT-status evidence where relevant, and documentation supporting the applicable place-of-supply or exemption rule
  • An inward remittance certificate is not a general French VAT zero-rating requirement

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions between VAT-taxable persons established in France and electronic transaction reporting for specified transactions outside that invoicing scope. From 1 September 2026, all in-scope businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; issuance obligations begin on that date for large and intermediate-sized businesses and on 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-businesses. In-scope invoices must use an approved platform or the public invoicing portal under the applicable rules and structured formats. France does not generally require real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of every invoice. Cross-border transactions, consumer transactions, and other reportable operations may be subject to e-reporting rather than domestic e-invoicing.

France imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. Export exemption depends on satisfying the applicable legal conditions and retaining reliable evidence. Unsupported or incorrectly exempted supplies may be assessed for VAT, late-payment interest, and tax penalties; intentional or fraudulent conduct can attract aggravated penalties and, where applicable, criminal sanctions. Input VAT refunds may be denied or recovered, and assessments may generally be reopened within the applicable statutory limitation period, subject to extensions or exceptions for fraud and other specified circumstances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member with fully liberalised capital and current-account convertibility under Articles 63 to 66 TFEU, Directive 88/361/EEC and the French Monetary and Financial Code. The euro is freely convertible into other currencies without exchange-control approval. Current-account payments covering trade invoices, subcontractor fees, interest, royalties and profit distributions can be settled without a French foreign-exchange licence. Profit remittance hurdles are indirect rather than prohibitive: the French SARL must demonstrate real business substance, maintain audited accounts, meet withholding tax and tax-treaty requirements on distributions to non-resident shareholders, and ensure no EU/CFSP sanctions screening failure arises. The receiving French bank is required to report reportable cross-border transactions to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics, but this is a declaratory regime, not a convertibility restriction. For capital-account operations such as shareholder capital increases, subordinated loans and equity repatriation, no prior exchange-control authorisation exists; the transaction must be documented with corporate resolutions, financing agreements and a lawful economic purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

French law imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender, no statutory remittance timeline and no conversion ratio. A French exporter may retain foreign-currency receivables in a foreign-currency account, convert them to euro, or use them for permitted treasury settlement. The only legal obligations are the balance-of-payments declaration submitted by the resident bank to Banque de France for reportable transactions, and the exporter's obligation to maintain commercial documentation sufficient to identify the export, the counterparty and the payment flow. No drachma-era or euro-era surrender rule remains in force. In practice, for clean audit and banking compliance, export proceeds should be credited within normal agreed trade terms, evidenced by customs declarations, invoices, and settlement advice.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in its capacity as the Eurosystem national central bank, with foreign-exchange policy and balance-of-payments reporting functions exercised under the French Monetary and Financial Code. The Direction Générale du Trésor exercises State Treasury oversight, while the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) supervises credit institutions and anti-money-laundering obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary treasury banking rail and capital escrow provider; Medium as an ancillary payment-processing rail. Legal constraints are definitive under French law: an offshore digital EMI that is not established or passported into France under Directive 2009/110/EC and the PSD2 framework cannot lawfully hold funds, open a treasury account or settle cross-border payments for a French SARL. Any payment institution, electronic money institution or crypto-asset service provider must be licensed or passported into the French market and registered with the ACPR and/or ORIAS. Even an EEA-licensed EMI may only provide payment services and cannot provide deposit accounts, credit facilities, FX treasury limits or commercial cash escrow as a substitute for a licensed credit institution. Therefore, the SARL should use an EMI only as a supplemental disbursement rail for low-value operational payments, never as the treasury account holder for capital or port-authority milestone funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a capital increase or intercompany shareholder loan to a French SARL, the compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR remittances or a SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments from within the EEA. The beneficiary must be the SARL itself, in the bank account titled to the SARL's French SIREN number. The payment text must include the exact corporate transaction reference, the upstream contract or resolution number, and a brief purpose, for example 'shareholder capital increase under French Company Law' or 'intercompany loan agreement dated [date]'. The French resident bank will ask for the underlying corporate deed and shareholder approval. For reportable amounts the bank will classify the operation under the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting scheme and may require a 'motif' code corresponding to capital transaction or financing operation. The receiving bank must also screen the transfer against EU and UN sanctions lists. No French exchange-control permit is required. For a contribution to share capital, the statutory corporate procedure must be completed before settlement is accepted; for a loan, a fully executed loan agreement, interest rate evidence and repayment schedule must be lodged with the bank file.Outbound treasury disbursements for marine dredging procurement, subcontract labour, equipment charters and port dues should be cleared on the following rails: SEPA Credit Transfer for direct EUR payments to beneficiaries inside the SEPA zone; SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA or non-EUR payments to international suppliers; documentary letters of credit issued under UCP 600 for the purchase of capital marine equipment or through suppliers requiring payment against documents; and standby letters of credit or dematerialised bank guarantees for performance bonds payable to the port authority or main contractor. For high-value EUR clearing inside the Eurosystem, TARGET2 or its successor T2 service provides immediate final settlement. Outbound payment instructions must carry the underlying contract number, invoice reference, the exact beneficiary bank BIC and IBAN where available, and a truthful payment purpose code consistent with the French balance-of-payments classifications. 25% standard corporate income tax on all French-resident companies from fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2022.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

12.8% for non-resident individual recipients; 30% for non-resident corporate shareholders; 75% if the beneficiary is established in a non-cooperative State. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may provide a 0% rate for an EU parent holding at least 25% of the capital for 24 months, and most French DTAs reduce the source-state rate to 0%, 5%, 10% or 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within three months of fiscal year-end; a 31 December year-end uses the statutory 3 May deadline or the next working day when 1 May falls on a weekend.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000: mandatory Local File and Master File preparation for a French group entity when the French entity or the group to which it belongs has annual net turnover or balance-sheet total above €400,000,000 and is at least 50% controlled by or exercises control over a non-French related party.

Economic substance scope: France applies the OECD-aligned substance doctrine through the abuse-of-rights rule under Article L.64 of the French Tax Procedure Code, ATAD CFC rules, the Principal Purpose Test under DTAs and the MLI. For an ISIC 4290 maritime dredging SARL, economic substance must be evidenced by a real French head office, locally employed administrative and marine engineering personnel, dredging vessels and equipment owned or chartered by the French company, project procurement decisions executed from France, and bank accounts held in the French entity's name.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent income-tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 40% or 80% of evaded tax plus late-payment interest; criminal conviction under Article 1741 CGI may result in up to five years imprisonment and a fine of €75,000, together with confiscation of vessels and equipment and prohibition from managing commercial companies.

Director personal liability: Directors who organise or endorse false filings may be jointly and severally liable for tax, penalties and interest. Criminal courts may order imprisonment, travel bans, asset freezing, disqualification from corporate management and mandatory restitution to the French State.
Transfer pricing mispricing and artificial intercompany arrangements

Statutory sanction: DGFiP recharacterisation to arm's length, additional taxable income, 2.4% annual late-payment interest and a 40% penalty for inadequate documentation. In fraudulent cases the penalty increases to 80% and the file may be referred to the national anti-fraud committee.

Director personal liability: Managing directors who knowingly sign sham transfer-pricing documentation can be held personally liable for the reassessed deficits and penalties, and may face criminal liability for abuse of law or fraudulent misrepresentation.
Shell company / treaty-shopping structure

Statutory sanction: Application of France's specific anti-abuse rule and the MLI Principal Purpose Test may deny treaty benefits, reclassify the entity as non-resident, impose 80% penalty and disallow carryforward losses. The tax authority may also disregard the legal entity and reassess the actual foreign beneficiary directly, with fines and interest.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and shadow directors can be pursued personally under the abuse-of-rights doctrine. If the corporate veil is pierced, personal assets may be seized and directors may be subject to travel bans, reputational sanctions and criminal prosecution for organised tax fraud.
AML violations and corruption in public procurement

Statutory sanction: Money laundering and corruption offences carry imprisonment of up to ten years and fines up to €1,000,000 for natural persons; a SARL can be fined up to €5,000,000 and face the confiscation of corporate vessels, equipment, bank balances and contract rights.

Director personal liability: Directors may be imprisoned, prohibited from managing commercial companies, subjected to travel restrictions and ordered to pay restitution to the injured public authority. Criminal forfeiture extends to personal assets where the court determines personal benefit from laundered public funds.
