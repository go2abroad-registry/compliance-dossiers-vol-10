Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

In France, commercial domiciliation (virtual office / société de domiciliation) is permissible for administrative functions for a maximum cumulative duration of five years under Articles L.123-11 and R.123-4 of the French Commercial Code. However, for an ISIC 3250 manufacturer of critical care invasive mechanical ventilators, virtual office use is not permissible as the principal place of business because the company must maintain physical facilities, production equipment, quality-control systems, and regulatory documentation opening as required under French health law and the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR 2017/745). The ANSM requires a declared physical manufacturing site in France; thus, full commercial substance with an actual office, registered laboratory premises, and ventilators storage area is mandatory from incorporation.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), administered by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered-office department; electronic filing through the Guichet unique des entreprises operated by INPI (formalities.entreprise.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 through L.223-43 and R.223-1 et seq. (SARL provisions); Regulation (EU) 2017/745 on medical devices applies to the business scope post-registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form M0 – Déclaration de création d'une société commerciale, with annexed certified Articles of Association (statuts), deposit certificate, and proof of legal publication; registration culminates in the issuance of the official Kbis extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SARL (Art. L.223-2, Code de commerce); capital amount is freely fixed in the Articles of Association. At least 50% of cash contributions (apports en numéraire) must be fully paid up at subscription and the balance within five years. All cash capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, releasable only after RCS registration. In-kind contributions are valued in the Articles; a contributions auditor (commissaire aux apports) is mandatory where any in-kind contribution exceeds €7,500 or the aggregate in-kind value exceeds half of the share capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection and certification by SDIS (Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours) or the Commission de Sécurité where the premises are open to staff/public; structural load inspection certificate for cleanroom equipment and elevated storage; occupational clearance under the Code du travail comprising emergency evacuation plans, ventilation, periodic electrical verification, and mandatory employee medical surveillance; ISO 14644 cleanroom classification compliance for aseptic assembly if the ventilator patient interface demands controlled conditions.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for an SARL (Art. L.223-2, Code de commerce); capital amount is freely fixed in the Articles of Association. At least 50% of cash contributions (apports en numéraire) must be fully paid up at subscription and the balance within five years. All cash capital must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, releasable only after RCS registration. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under a fully convertible currency regime for the euro. Capital account transactions are generally permitted without prior approval for resident companies. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are straightforward, subject to corporate tax compliance and beneficial ownership declarations. Hurdles are limited to standard KYC and source of funds verification for large amounts exceeding €100,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the Banque de France is required under French law. However, banks must verify the legitimacy of export receipts through supporting documentation such as commercial invoices, packing lists, and certificates of origin. Proceeds should ideally be collected within 180 days of shipment and recorded appropriately for tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasible for authorized fintechs under PSD2 and French EMI regulations, but offshore digital EMIs face legal constraints including potential authorization requirements and compliance with French data protection laws (GDPR).

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate recipient, subject to exemptions and treaty relief. Qualifying EU or EEA parent companies may obtain an exemption under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring a qualifying participation, minimum holding period and anti-abuse compliance. Individual recipients may be subject to different domestic rates.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the annual return is generally due on the second business day following 1 May of the following year. For a financial year ending on a date other than 31 December, the return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The filing calendar published by the French tax administration applies where the statutory date is adjusted. Corporate income tax installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, with the final balance generally due by the fifteenth day of the fourth month following year-end, or by 15 May for a 31 December year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Installments are generally payable by 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December. The balance is generally payable by the fifteenth day of the fourth month following year-end, subject to the special deadline for a 31 December year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Master File and Local File documentation is generally mandatory where the French entity or French tax group has annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, or where it is controlled by or controls an entity meeting that threshold, subject to statutory group and filing conditions. An annual transfer pricing declaration generally applies at lower thresholds, commonly where turnover or gross assets reach EUR 50 million or where controlled transactions exceed specified category thresholds, including the EUR 100,000 category threshold. Documentation should nevertheless be maintained below the formal threshold where material related-party transactions exist.

Economic substance scope: France has no single standalone economic substance regulation equivalent to the United Arab Emirates ESR regime. Substance is enforced through transfer pricing, permanent establishment, controlled foreign company, interest limitation, beneficial ownership, anti-abuse and principal-purpose rules. The French SARL should maintain genuine French functions, qualified personnel, premises or operational capacity appropriate to its activity, decision-making evidence, inventory and manufacturing records, customer and supplier management, and commercially justified intragroup arrangements. A French entity lacking operational substance may face denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization, transfer pricing adjustments, permanent establishment findings, withholding tax exposure and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, tax interest, conduct-based penalties generally up to 80%, and criminal tax fraud penalties that may include imprisonment for up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000. Aggravated circumstances may increase exposure to up to 7 years' imprisonment and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Managers and directors who personally participate in fraud may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, imprisonment and civil liability. Tax collection may be extended to a director whose serious and repeated management misconduct makes collection impossible. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax-fraud sanction but may arise from judicial measures or other proceedings.
Transfer pricing manipulation and hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Addition of the excessive amount to French taxable income, potential withholding tax on recharacterized distributions, 40% or 80% penalties depending on conduct, late-payment interest, and possible documentation penalties. The French tax administration may apply a French taxable profit adjustment under the arm's-length principle.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability where they knowingly approve fictitious or abusive transactions, participate in fraud or cause insolvency. Ordinary transfer pricing adjustment does not automatically pierce limited liability.
Undeclared permanent establishment or disguised French activity

Statutory sanction: Retroactive taxation of attributable French profits, corporate income tax, VAT, payroll and social charges, withholding obligations, interest and penalties. Deliberate concealment may constitute tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for fraudulent concealment, payroll violations or insolvency-related misconduct. Civil veil piercing is exceptional and requires serious personal misconduct or statutory grounds.
Fraudulent insolvency and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: Court-ordered clawback, extension of insolvency proceedings, director liability for all or part of the insufficiency of assets, management bans and possible criminal bankruptcy sanctions, including imprisonment and fines under the French Commercial Code.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution, contribution to liabilities, disqualification from management and criminal prosecution. A travel ban is not automatic but judicial control or other criminal measures may restrict travel.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist financing

Statutory sanction: Suspicious transactions may be reportable to TRACFIN where the entity is within an obliged-profession category. Criminal money laundering may carry up to 5 years' imprisonment and EUR 375,000, with aggravated forms carrying higher penalties. Assets may be seized or confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly facilitate laundering may be prosecuted personally, fined, imprisoned and subject to asset confiscation. Corporate limited liability does not protect intentional criminal participants.
Medical-device regulatory and customs misclassification

Statutory sanction: Product withdrawal, recall, prohibition of sale, customs detention, administrative fines, loss of market access and civil damages. False customs declarations may generate customs duties, penalties and criminal exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability for knowingly placing unsafe or non-compliant medical devices on the market, particularly where injury or death results. Civil claims may include compensation and restitution; travel bans are not automatic.
Dividend distribution in breach of capital-maintenance rules

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unlawfully paid dividends, shareholder and director liability, accounting remediation and potential criminal sanctions for fictitious dividends or false corporate accounts.

Director personal liability: Managers approving unlawful distributions may face civil liability, management restrictions and criminal prosecution. Personal restitution may be ordered; limited liability does not protect directors from their own statutory breach.
False accounting and corporate-record falsification

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, accounting penalties, civil damages, criminal penalties for false accounts or related offenses, and possible prohibition from managing a company.

Director personal liability: The director or manager who orders, approves or knowingly tolerates false records may be personally prosecuted and may face fines, imprisonment, restitution and management disqualification.
