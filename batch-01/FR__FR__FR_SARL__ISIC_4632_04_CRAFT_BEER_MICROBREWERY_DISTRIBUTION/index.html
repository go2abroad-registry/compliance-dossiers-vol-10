Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry) having territorial jurisdiction over the registered office; all applications must be filed electronically through the single national portal formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 through L.223-56 and R.223-1 through R.223-29 (formation, share capital, management of a Société à Responsabilité Limitée); Civil Code Articles 1835 to 1844-17 (general contract-of-partnership rules); Decree No. 67-237 of 23 March 1967 on commercial companies; applicable sections of the Environmental Code (ICPE regime) and Public Health Code (alcohol wholesale licensing).

Formation / constitutional filings

Formulaire M0 – Demande unique d'immatriculation (Single Registration Application) filed together with the signed articles of association (Statuts), the capital deposit certificate, and the certificate of publication of the legal notice in a Journal d'Annonces Légales.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 16 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital (a SARL may be formed with as little as €1). If the subscribed cash capital is €5,000 or less, it must be fully paid up before filing and held in a blocked escrow account with a French bank or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; if it exceeds €5,000, at least 20% must be released on incorporation and the balance within five years. In-kind contributions must be fully transferred at formation, and a contribution auditor (commissaire aux apports) is required unless all shareholders unanimously waive the appointment. Escrowed funds are released to the Manager only upon presentation of the K-bis registration extract.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is fully integrated into the euro area and maintains unrestricted convertibility for both current and capital account operations under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. No prior exchange-control authorization is required for trade settlements, dividends, shareholder loans, or equity injections. The principal practical hurdle is tax-related rather than FX: outbound profit remittances are subject to a 25% domestic withholding tax, reducible to 0% for qualifying EEA corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or to reduced rates under applicable double tax treaties. Non-EU inbound direct investment may trigger prior screening approval under French FDI regime (Decree 2019-1590) in sensitive sectors, but this is not a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no fixed repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Exporters may lawfully maintain receivables in foreign currency and manage Forex balances through French bank accounts. For financial statements and corporate income tax purposes, export proceeds must be translated into euro at the applicable Banque de France or accounting reference rate. Reporting obligations are indirect: Intrastat dispatch declarations for intra-EU beer/wine deliveries above the annual threshold, and Single Administrative Documents plus DGDDI customs declarations for third-country exports. The corporate treasury should nevertheless keep export proceeds segregated and traceable to satisfy banking AML and transfer-pricing documentation expectations.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) acting as the integrated prudential and AML/CFT supervisor for credit institutions and payment/e-money institutions. There is no separate foreign exchange control administration in France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. An EU-licensed Electronic Money Institution passported into France can provide remote onboarding, euro IBANs, FX conversion, payment initiation, and virtual accounts; this is workable as a contingency rail. An offshore, non-EEA digital EMI has Low feasibility because it would require an ACPR e-money licence or French branch under Article L.525-6 of the Code monétaire et financier, and French banks will generally reject transfers from non-licensed providers. EMIs cannot hold funds as deposits, cannot grant credit, and are not adequate for capital escrow, commercial letters of credit, or excise duty payment mechanisms. The primary legal constraint is that PSD2 payment services must be provided through an EU-authorised or French-passported institution only.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident shareholder or foreign UBO obscurity in the SARL ownership chain.

Root cause: Non-resident shareholder or foreign UBO obscurity in the SARL ownership chain.

Defense evidence: Certified corporate extract (extrait K-bis), full shareholder register, up-to-date beneficial owner declaration under the French Trust and Beneficial Ownership Register, passports and residence permits of all natural-person shareholders, tax residence certificates from the countries of residence, source of wealth and source of funds statements, notarized contribution certificates, and a corporate structure chart showing the entire ownership chain to ultimate natural persons.
Newly formed SARL with no audited financial history and no French trading footprint.

Root cause: Newly formed SARL with no audited financial history and no French trading footprint.

Defense evidence: Business plan for the craft beer distribution undertaking, lease or proof of warehouse and cold-chain facility, French customs and excise registration (Certificat d'accises / DGDDI), distributor letters of intent, supplier agreements with breweries, net worth evidence from the contributing shareholders, personal financial references, and a projected six-month cash-flow statement.
Unclear commercial purpose or mismatch between declared ISIC 4632 activity and payment traffic.

Root cause: Unclear commercial purpose or mismatch between declared ISIC 4632 activity and payment traffic.

Defense evidence: Copy of the statuts, KBIS showing 'commerce de gros de boissons', valid CI number (SIREN/SIRET), DGDDI licence for alcoholic beverage wholesaling, actual invoices and transport documents for hazy IPA kegs, Incoterms contracts, insurance certificates for excise/customs guarantees, and an explanation of anticipated FX flows to and from non-French distributors.
High-value inbound capital injection into an intermediation company triggering source-of-funds review.

Root cause: High-value inbound capital injection into an intermediation company triggering source-of-funds review.

Defense evidence: Notarized minutes of the capital increase, depository certificate of funds, bank statements of the investing entity proving prior lawful accumulation, audited financial statements or solicitors' escrow letter, signed shareholder loan agreement if funds come as current account advances, and a sanctions and adverse-media screening report prepared by a regulated third-party provider.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use a SWIFT MT103 for non-euro inbound transfers or a SEPA Credit Transfer in euro, credited to the French beneficiary bank account (BIC/IBAN) of the SARL. The payment MUST include the correct Banque de France payment purpose code to avoid automatic hold: use category C100 for direct investment equity subscription, or an intercompany loan category for a shareholder current-account advance (e.g. compte courant d'associé). The remitting entity must be exactly the same legal person as the shareholder named in the capital increase minutes; third-party payments are unacceptable. Supporting documents should include the French FDI clearance decision, if applicable, the deposit certificate, and a legal cover letter identifying the purpose code, underlying transaction, and ultimate beneficial owner. Funds must be booked to a dedicated capital escrow account before activation of operating accounts.For distributor payments inside the EEA, execute euro SEPA Credit Transfers (or SEPA Instant for urgent transfers) directly to European IBANs. For outside the EEA, clear trade settlements through SWIFT MT103 with full invoice and customs references; for large keg shipments on documentary terms, use an irrevocable, confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit issued through the French relationship bank. Cold-chain logistics, duty exactions, and DGDDI excise deferments should be handled through the bank's trade finance module, with Incoterms carefully matched to transfer of title. Local direct debit rails can be used for recurring subscriptions from French bars/restaurants, but the primary cross-border rail remains SWIFT/SEPA with MT103 and optional electronic bank document matching.Spot FX transactions in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CHF executed under a standard treasury line with same-day or T+2 settlement.Plain-vanilla FX forward contracts with maturities up to 12 months backed by underlying export or import invoices, ideally executed on an ISDA or French bank master agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) but only in currencies without a deliverable euro market and only where the corporate has a demonstrable real exposure; NDFs are not permitted for speculative use.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD or EUR/GBP) used to hedge long-term shareholder loans or acquisition financing, subject to internal hedge documentation.Purchased European-style FX options (zero-premium collars and vanilla calls/puts) to provide protection without exposing the SARL to margin calls; barrier and exotic options should be avoided for a distribution company.
