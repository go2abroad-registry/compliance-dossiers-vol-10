Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL has no statutory minimum capital. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed, and at least 20% of each cash contribution must be paid up before RCS registration; the balance must be paid within five years. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up at subscription. Cash funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account or with a notary until the Kbis is obtained.

Funding & unlock path: A pure trading office requires no fire-safety or civil-defense permit. If a showroom, stockroom or warehouse is used, obtain an SDIS fire-safety inspection and opinion, a structural load certificate from an approved technical control office, CARSAT occupational health and safety clearance, electrical installation conformity, and, if the premises are open to the public, an ERP authorization from the local Mairie.

Paid-in capital rule: The SARL has no statutory minimum capital. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed, and at least 20% of each cash contribution must be paid up before RCS registration; the balance must be paid within five years. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up at subscription. Cash funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account or with a notary until the Kbis is obtained.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Category: Not applicable — French law does not require a separate operating license for the wholesale liquidation of branded overstock apparel. A general commercial activity is covered by the company's RCS registration and Kbis.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; digital portal: impots.gouv.fr; single company registration window: Guichet Unique Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); customs service for export clearance: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) with DELTA/NPE electronic declarations; EU VAT number verification: VIES (ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies).

E-government filing portal

(1) For a French SARL owned by a foreign parent, incorporate through the Guichet Unique Entreprises portal by filing the statutes (statuts), manager identity documents, registered office proof and the M0 form; (2) INSEE automatically assigns SIREN/SIRET identifiers and the DGFiP registers the company for VAT, allocating the intra-Community TVA number (format FR + two-digit key + nine-digit SIREN); (3) obtain an EORI number (FR + SIREN) from DGDDI free of charge prior to lodging customs export declarations; (4) open a French bank account, which is also needed to evidence inward remittances on export files; (5) the foreign parent acting directly in France without a permanent establishment must appoint a DGFiP-approved fiscal representative for VAT (art. 289 A CGI) and use the same representative to obtain a non-resident TVA number; (6) a domestic SARL must file the first corporate income tax return (liasse fiscale 2065) within three months of incorporation and file monthly or quarterly CA3 VAT returns unless opting for the small-business franchise (in which case no VAT number activity is declared). The Kbis extract (RCS at the commercial court) is the official company existence and tax registry proof.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (default): the French SARL is automatically subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the 25% headline rate (15% reduced rate applies on the first EUR 42,500 of annual profit for SMEs with turnover below EUR 10 million). Pass_Through_Eligible: small SARLs meeting the statutory size tests may elect for five years (renewable) to be taxed as partnerships under art. 239 bis AA CGI, with profits taxable at associate level (impôt sur le revenu). Territorial_Scope: IS is limited to profits attributable to French establishments; a French parent SARL can validly hold non-resident subsidiaries without current taxation of undistributed offshore profits unless the French territoriality election is waived. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable - no general tax-exempt special economic zone regime covers wholesale garment liquidation activity.

Available election forms

(i) Check-the-box is not a French concept: entity classification follows the legal form and available tax options; (ii) five-year pass-through option for small SARLs (art. 239 bis AA CGI) upon filing a notice with the competent DGFiP service; (iii) tax consolidation (intégration fiscale, art. 223 A CGI) available to a French head company holding at least 95% of French subsidiaries, including the SARL, enabling single taxable result and loss offset within the group; (iv) parent-subsidiary (mère-fille) regime under arts. 145 and 216 CGI at 95% shareholding, neutralising 95% of dividend flows; (v) VAT grouping (assujetti unique, art. 256 C CGI) allowing French related entities to file a single VAT return; (vi) OSS/IOSS one-stop-shop elections for distance sales of goods and imports; (vii) hybrid mismatch rules of art. 212 bis CGI (ATAD transposition) apply automatically and no elective avoidance mechanism is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 - France applies no turnover threshold for VAT registration: every person making taxable supplies is required to be registered from the first transaction (art. 256 CGI). A domestic de minimis exemption (franchise en base de TVA, art. 293 B CGI) may apply up to EUR 91,900 annual turnover for sales of goods (EUR 101,000 tolerance threshold), but it is unavailable to foreign non-established traders and is unattractive for an export-oriented off-price wholesale model because it forfeits input VAT deduction; a waiver option exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union qualify for 0% VAT under art. 262-I-1° CGI where: (i) the goods are sold 'en l'état' (in their original state, without processing in France); (ii) they are physically dispatched or transported from France to a non-EU destination by the seller, on behalf of the seller, or by a purchaser not established in France; and (iii) the goods leave the EU within three months of the sale. The 0% rate also applies to intra-Community supplies (art. 262 ter CGI) provided the EU buyer is VAT-registered, its TVA number is validated on VIES, the goods are transported to another member state, and the seller files the EC Sales List (DEB). Off-price wholesale liquidation of branded overstock garments meets the export exemption if standard customs evidence is kept, including where the non-EU buyer arranges collection from a French or EU consolidation point. Zero-rating is denied if the agreement retains processing/value-added in France or if delivery is completed to an EU-based reseller before ultimate export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / ExE) lodged electronically via DELTA G/EXP or NPE, stating garment HS codes (e.g., 6104, 6204) and export value;
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR consignment note (road) or Air Waybill (air) naming the French SARL as consignor/exporter, with destination outside the EU;
  • Commercial invoice and packing list under Incoterms 2020, stating the price, quantity and liquidation discount;
  • PLDA / EX1 proof of exit (Preuve de la Livraison en Dehors de l'Union) or transporter's goods-leaving certificate confirming physical departure from the EU;
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of sales proceeds from the non-resident buyer;
  • For intra-EU sales: VIES validation printout of the customer TVA number and the filed EC Sales List (DEB) acknowledgement;
  • Liquidation/distribution agreement or purchase order documenting retention of title and transfer of risk at export.

Mandatory structured e-invoicing and e-reporting under the 2020 Finance Act (art. 153) are phased in as follows: from 1 September 2026 all VAT-registered persons must receive structured electronic invoices and large and mid-size enterprises must issue them; from 1 September 2027 all businesses, including SMEs and SARLs, must issue electronic invoices. Invoices flow through DGFiP-accredited Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); transaction data (e-reporting) covering B2B sales and B2C payment data must be transmitted to the administration within one business day of issuance. The French model is transmission-based digital clearance with schema validation, not a pre-authorisation stamping system (contrast with Italy's SDI); mandatory formats are Factur-X 2.0 (hybrid), UBL 2.1 and CII via the PEPPOL exchange network, with an electronic signature/seal or secure business flow required. Retail cash-register software must comply with NF 525 anti-fraud certification (art. 88 CGI, Loi 2015-1785).

Export zero-rating is routinely audited in the wholesale sector. Where evidence is missing, inconsistent with customs data, or the goods do not physically leave the EU within three months, the DGFiP may (i) reassess output VAT for the full limitation period (six years, art. L102 B Livre des Procédures Fiscales); (ii) apply de retard interest of 0.55% per month (art. 1727 CGI); (iii) impose a 40% penalty for deliberate misdeclaration (manquement délibéré, art. 1729 b CGI) or 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres (art. 1729-1 CGI); and (iv) pursue criminal sanctions for tax evasion of up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine (art. 1741 CGI). Customs authorities may separately impose penalties of one to three times the evaded duties and confiscation under Code des Douanes arts. 410-414. Liquidation markdowns on branded stock must be arm's-length and documented (art. L13 B LPF) to resist recharacterisation of zero-rated export prices; export VAT refunds are claimed on the periodic CA3 return and are subject to prior audit where the credit exceeds the declared turnover.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully liberalised euro-based currency regime. The euro is freely convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange-control licensing requirements for trade settlements, inward direct investments, or outward profit remittances by a French SARL. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are subject only to standard corporate tax, income-distribution withholding, and tax-treaty provisions; there is no foreign-exchange approval or conversion obstacle. Certain statistical and direct-investment declaration duties with Banque de France remain, and anti-money laundering documentation must be maintained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation applies in France. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency, converted at market rate, or immediately deployed for working capital. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation, and the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. Resident exporters are nevertheless subject to the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical reporting regime for cross-border flows, with periodic declarations required for transactions in excess of regulatory thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting under the monetary and financial policy framework of the European Central Bank, with foreign-exchange policy support from the Direction Générale du Trésor at the Ministry for the Economy and Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

European Economic Area digital EMI licences can generally be passportised into France and provide practical onboarding for a low-risk apparel-liquidation SARL, though tier-1 relationship banking remains preferable for LC issuance and sizeable capital flows. Non-EEA offshore EMIs are not permissible as principal payment service providers for French treasury operations; they may act as ancillary technology providers only when reception or processing of customer funds remains with a licensed French or passported EEA institution. Overall feasibility is medium-high for EEA-regulated EMIs and low for offshore/non-EEA entities.

Defense evidence: Client care policy, complete receivables ageing ledger, formal assignment agreements under French Civil Code Articles 1689-1690, notices to debtors, legal opinions on merchantability of damaged or unsold stock, and board minutes authorising the debt-recovery channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from a non-resident shareholder are processed by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer using the SARL's French IBAN. The wire instruction must explicitly indicate the capital nature of the transfer in Field 70/Remittance Information, with supporting shareholder resolution, an engagement of subscription or share transfer agreement, and proof of identity/source of funds. For direct investment purposes, the transaction must be declared to Banque de France under the direct investment notification regime when foreign ownership thresholds are crossed. Verification requires confirmation of the debit from the shareholder's bank, matching EUR settlement, and anti-money laundering screening before funds are credited to the operational account.Outbound settlements to overseas garment suppliers use SWIFT MT103 for open account payments; trade settlements may also be executed through commercial letters of credit issued under SWIFT MT700 and reconciled by MT202/MT210 reimbursements. For EEA suppliers SEPA SCT or SEPA Instant Payment can be used. Documentary collections (cash against documents / documents against acceptance) under URC522 are available when LC costs are disproportionate. Funds are cleared across TARGET2 for euro RTGS, CORE SEPA for standard euro batch payments, and the SWIFT gpi network for non-euro cross-border wires.Plain vanilla physically-settled FX forwards on EUR/USD and EUR/CNY, supported by documented underlying purchase orders and invoices, executed with a licensed French or EU banking counterparty under an ISDA 2002 master agreement and French clearing or credit-support annex.Non-deliverable forwards permitted only for hedging currencies subject to exchange-control restrictions, such as certain emerging-market currencies where physical delivery is not legally possible; these must remain strictly linked to the underlying apparel import/export exposure and cannot be used for speculative positions.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps structuring long-term treasury exposure, used solely for asset-liability matching in EUR/USD and EUR/GBP pairs, subject to board approval, capital treatment under EU financial regulation, and counterparty credit-limit policy.Cost-effective FX swaps and forwards only to roll short-dated net trade positions; hedging transactions must satisfy MiFID II scope, French professional eligibility criteria, and the SARL's governance mandate.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: European Economic Area digital EMI licences can generally be passportised into France and provide practical onboarding for a low-risk apparel-liquidation SARL, though tier-1 relationship banking remains preferable for LC issuance and sizeable capital flows. Non-EEA offshore EMIs are not permissible as principal payment service providers for French treasury operations; they may act as ancillary technology providers only when reception or processing of customer funds remains with a licensed French or passported EEA institution. Overall feasibility is medium-high for EEA-regulated EMIs and low for offshore/non-EEA entities.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2022.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Article 119 bis of the French Income Tax Code for distributions to non-resident corporate shareholders; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds at least 10% of the French entity for at least two years; 30% single flat withholding combined with social levies for non-resident individual shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory French transfer pricing documentation (dossier de prix de transfert) applies when the French entity or the multinational group exceeds either an annual turnover or total balance-sheet threshold of EUR 400 million. Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more. The simplified transfer pricing annex (Form 2257) must accompany the annual return when these thresholds are met.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a Luxembourg- or UAE-style formal Economic Substance Regulations regime, but applies strong substantive-analogue doctrines: abuse of law under Article L.64 of the French Tax Procedures Book, sham transaction analysis, and the principal-purpose test for treaty benefits. An off-price apparel liquidation SARL must maintain real local decision-making, inventory and warehouse capacity, employees, bank accounts, accounting records, and commercial risk exposure; otherwise, treaty relief and tax deductions may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 500,000 and five years’ imprisonment under Article 1741 of the French Income Tax Code; aggravated cases involving organised activity raise the fine to EUR 750,000 and the sentence to seven years. Tax assessment penalties of 40% for bad faith and 80% for fraudulent conduct apply, plus monthly 0.2% late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: The gérant or managing director may be held personally liable for French corporate tax debts under Article L.267 of the French Tax Procedures Book in cases of repeated serious irregularities or fraud. Criminal proceedings may lead to ancillary orders including travel bans, passport seizure, confiscation, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Abuse-of-law penalty of 80% of the understated tax under Article L.64 of the French Tax Procedures Book and Article 1729 of the French Income Tax Code; transactions may be disregarded, deductions disallowed, and the repatriated profit recharacterised as a distribution subject to 25% withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately create artificial shell structures can be joined to the tax liability as authors of the abuse, face civil restitution of the evaded tax, and be exposed to criminal sanctions if fraud is established.
Money Laundering and Undisclosed Cash Flow

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under the French Monetary and Financial Code carries a fine of EUR 375,000 and five years’ imprisonment; aggravated laundering increases the fine to EUR 750,000 and the term to seven years. France’s financial intelligence unit (Tracfin) may order freezing of assets and reporting obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who facilitate or fail to prevent suspicious transactions face criminal liability, disqualification from corporate management, confiscation of assets, and operative travel bans where the penal court finds a flight risk.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm’s-Length Margins

Statutory sanction: The French tax authorities may reallocate profits under Article 57 of the French Income Tax Code, impose a 40% penalty for deliberate documentation failure, apply a minimum documentation fine of EUR 10,000, and charge 0.2% monthly late-payment interest from the original filing date.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs false transfer pricing documents or directs the execution of artificial pricing arrangements may be treated as an accomplice to tax fraud, with personal liability for restitution, criminal fines, and imprisonment in serious cases.
