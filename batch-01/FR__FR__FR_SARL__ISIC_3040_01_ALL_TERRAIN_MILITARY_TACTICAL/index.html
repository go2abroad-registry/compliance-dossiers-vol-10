Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) of the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Recovery, with the electronic filing portal at impots.gouv.fr and business registration portal at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; VAT operational matters are handled by the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and interregional DGFiP VAT units.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must complete statutory business formation or branch registration via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), obtaining the SIREN registration number and SIRET establishment number. The SIREN serves as the French tax identification number. For VAT purposes, the DGFiP assigns a French intra-Community VAT number (FR followed by the INSEE key and SIREN) once the business registration file is transmitted. A foreign company not established in France and not required to register through the Guichet Unique must file Form FI? or Form 5466? alternative online application for direct VAT registration with the competent DGFiP VAT service. If the foreign entity has no permanent establishment in France, it must generally appoint an individual tax representative approved by DGFiP who is jointly and severally liable for French tax, or use the non-EU simplified distance selling registration procedure where applicable. Bank account powers of attorney, confirmation of legal existence, local address or representative mandate, and certified translation of constitutional documents are required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the SARL is by default liable to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) under Article 206 of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI). The territoriality scope for IS covers the worldwide profits of French-established SARLs, subject to tax treaties on non-French source profits through neutralization or permanent establishment analysis; profits of foreign branches are assessed in France if not allocated to a foreign PE, with tax credits available. Under narrow statutory conditions, a family SARL may elect partial taxation at the partner level as a transparent partnership.

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box regime comparable to the US IRS. Identified statutory election mechanisms include: (i) the option for a SARL whose shares are continuously held by individuals and which qualifies as a 'family SARL' to be taxed under Article 239 quater CGI on the income tax scale of the partners, subject to revocation within a time limit; (ii) the option under Article 239 nonies for certain services enterprises to be treated similarly; (iii) the election under Article 206-2 to renounce corporate income tax for collective civil entities?; and refusal of extension to hybrid entities. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD are transposed through Articles 212-1 bis? and 212-1 ter? of the CGI, requiring classification consistency between France and the foreign jurisdiction; mismatches may give rise to mandatory denial of deductions or inclusion of with-in cash amounts. Any election concerning transparent treatment must be filed with the local SIE within the tax return deadline for the relevant fiscal year and is binding until formally abandoned.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is full legal tender in France; current-account payments are freely convertible for EU/EEA counterparties and generally liberalised for non-EU counterparties under Articles L151-1 to L151-5 of the Code monétaire et financier. Capital-account movements are fully liberalised within the EU, but a non-EU foreign direct investment into an ISIC 3040 armored tactical vehicle manufacturer triggers Decree No. 2019-1590 and the EU FDI screening Regulation; prior Ministerial approval is required before any non-EU investor acquires a controlling stake or provides related funding. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are legally unrestricted after the SARL's annual financial statements are approved and French withholding-tax/dividend formalities have been completed; the practical obstacles are not exchange-conversion limits but the bank's sanctions screening of beneficial owners seated in non-cooperative jurisdictions. France does not distinguish between soft and hard currencies in bank practice, but credit institutions may decline payments to or from countries on FATF/EU blacklists unless a derogation is pre-approved by the French Ministry of Economy.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no percentage surrender or forced conversion obligation on export proceeds denominated in EUR; the mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0%. For export receivables in a foreign currency, the SARL must collect the receivable within 30 days after the contractual due date, route collection exclusively through an ACPR-licensed French credit institution, and promptly convert or offset the foreign-currency balance into EUR unless a genuine matching FX position exists for trade debt. Every cross-border transfer exceeding EUR 10,000 must be reported monthly to the Banque de France by the French intermediary; late repatriation beyond 30 days must be individually declared and may trigger administrative fines under Articles L161-1 and R161-1 et seq. of the Code monétaire et financier.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France; the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) exercises banking licensing and AML supervision, while the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) administers exchange control, FDI and defense-sector screening, and financial sanctions in the French Republic.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for principal treasury use and Medium only for incidental operating expenses. French law permits an ACPR-licensed electronic money institution or payment institution to provide payment accounts; however, offshore digital EMIs without a full EU/EEA passport cannot lawfully service a French defense contractor. The ISIC 3040 armored vehicle scope requires underlying banking products that standard EMIs do not offer: documentary letters of credit, bank guarantees, escrow accounts, government reimbursements and structured trade finance. UK-FCA-only EMIs are non-viable after Brexit because their license no longer passes into France; even EEA-licensed EMIs should only process payroll, per-diem and small sub-supplier payments under EUR 10,000. They must never be used for DGA-licensed export proceeds, inbound capital injections, or diplomatic LC receipts.
