Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) held by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce du lieu du siège social; electronic filing processed through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

Code de commerce (France), Livre II — Book II on Commercial Companies: Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée — formation, capital, governance); Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (registration with the RCS, effects of the Kbis); Décret n° 2023-700 (Guichet unique — unified electronic formalities); Code de l'environnement Article R.122-2 for environmental screening thresholds.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration M0 — formulaire de création d'entreprise (electronic M0 data set) filed via the INPI Guichet Unique; the resulting proof of incorporation is the official 'Extrait Kbis' issued by the competent Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 9 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No de jure minimum share capital for an SARL (freedom of capital under Article L.223-2, Code de commerce); the capital must be fully subscribed and cash contributions fully paid up at formation, deposited in a blocked escrow account held by a French bank, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notaire (Article L.223-7); the bank's 'attestation de dépôt des fonds' must accompany the M0 filing, and funds are released upon issuance of the Kbis.

Funding & unlock path:

Paid-in capital rule: No de jure minimum share capital for an SARL (freedom of capital under Article L.223-2, Code de commerce); the capital must be fully subscribed and cash contributions fully paid up at formation, deposited in a blocked escrow account held by a French bank, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notaire (Article L.223-7); the bank's 'attestation de dépôt des fonds' must accompany the M0 filing, and funds are released upon issuance of the Kbis.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — Portal: impots.gouv.fr; online company formalities: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Incorporate the SARL via the French 'Guichet unique' formalities portal to obtain SIREN/SIRET; the DGFiP automatically opens the corporate tax file and allocates the TVA intracommunautaire number (FR + 2-digit key + SIREN). Foreign entities without a French establishment must apply for direct French VAT registration through the SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) and file under the non-resident taxpayer procedure.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is taxable at entity level under Impôt sur les Sociétés (25% standard, 15% reduced rate on profits up to EUR 42,500) on worldwide profits, with territoriality adjustments for foreign permanent establishments; no automatic pass-through status exists. — Incorporate the SARL via the French 'Guichet unique' formalities portal to obtain SIREN/SIRET; the DGFiP automatically opens the corporate tax file and allocates the TVA intracommunautaire number (FR + 2-digit key + SIREN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is taxable at entity level under Impôt sur les Sociétés (25% standard, 15% reduced rate on profits up to EUR 42,500) on worldwide profits, with territoriality adjustments for foreign permanent establishments; no automatic pass-through status exists.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime. A qualifying 'family SARL' may elect income tax (IR) treatment under CGI Art. 239 bis AA for a renewable 5-year term; hybrid mismatch anti-avoidance rules under CGI Art. 212 bis (ATAD) restrict deductions in EU/non-EU mismatched arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 91,900 annual turnover for goods sales (franchise en base, CGI Art. 293 B); EUR 36,800 for services; EU-wide distance-selling threshold of EUR 10,000 applies for OSS registration on cross-border B2C supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT exemption under CGI Art. 262-I-1 provided title and risk have passed to the non-EU buyer and physical export is evidenced within the prescribed documentation window; intra-EU B2B supplies are exempt under CGI Art. 262 ter I with a valid EU customer VAT number; export-related ancillary services may also be zero-rated under strict conditions.

  • Export customs declaration (DAU / DELTA electronic lodgement clearing the goods to exit the EU)
  • Commercial invoice showing HS 8306/7117 classification, EXW/FOB value, and INCOTERM
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from EU territory
  • Inward payment proof (SWIFT MT103 credit advice or Stripe/Etsy payout reports) establishing inward remittance
  • CARNET/transit documentation when goods transit through non-EU or bonded regimes

Phased e-invoicing and e-reporting under CGI Art. 289 bis: mandatory from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized companies and from 1 September 2027 for SMEs; B2B invoices must be exchanged through DGFiP-certified PDP platforms and transaction data transmitted to the Portail Public de Facturation. No national real-time pre-clearance stamping; periodic quasi-real-time reporting applies.

VAT authorities may claw back zero-rated benefits where export evidence is not retained for six years; CGI Art. 283-3 reversal applies with monthly late-payment interest of 0.40% (Art. 1727), a 40% bad-faith penalty, or 80% penalty for concealed activity (Arts. 1730/1731); customs fines under Code des douanes Art. 413 can reach the value of the goods; export receivables outstanding beyond 180 days trigger transfer-pricing and hidden-distribution recharacterization under CGI Art. 57.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France confirms full euro convertibility on both current and capital account; French exchange controls have been fully dismantled, and no convertibility restriction applies to legitimate commercial or treasury transactions. Capital movements with non-EU countries are free under TFEU Article 63; ISIC 3240 die-struck brass enamel-pin production is outside the foreign-investment screening sectors of décret n° 2018-1291 (defence, dual-use, critical infrastructure, cybersecurity, etc.), so no ex-ante FDI authorisation applies. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted and taxed only via withholding (default 30% under French law when paid to non-residents, reduced by applicable double-tax treaties). The SARL must comply with Banque de France statistical reporting: any single transfer of €12,500 or more must be routed through an authorised intermediary, and cumulative inbound FDI exposure above €1.5 million requires an annual BdF balance-of-payments return. No stabilisation levy, surrender quota or convertibility charge is imposed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory obligation to surrender, repatriate or convert export proceeds. Exporters may freely receive revenue in EUR, USD, GBP, JPY, CNY or any convertible currency, hold foreign-currency accounts and convert at authorised bank market rates. For compliance routing, all cross-border settlements of €12,500 or more must be transacted through a French or EU-passported authorised intermediary (l'intermédiaire agréé) and declared to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics. Marketplace proceeds aggregated by Etsy Direct Pay or Stripe are not exempt from these pass-through reporting duties; where the payment institution (e.g., Stripe Payments Europe Ltd) is itself the intermediary, its statutory reporting covers the underlying transactions. Finished goods exported directly from French customs to consumers require CN22/CN23 declarations on the postal/courier side and French export statistics (DEB/DAU) above the statistical threshold, but no foreign-exchange surrender ratio is applicable.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) and Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), acting within the Single Supervisory Mechanism of the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange policy competence is shared with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances). For payment statistics, foreign-exchange control reports and statutory intermediary oversight, Banque de France is the designated authority under the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

EU/EEA-authorised electronic money institutions (Qonto SA via ACPR licence; Treezor SA; Revolut Ltd via Lithuanian EMI passport; and similar DSP2-passported issuers) may lawfully provide payment accounts to the FR_SARL and are viable as primary or ancillary liquidity rails. Viability rating: High for EU-licensed EMIs. Non-EU or offshore EMIs (UK post-Brexit payment institutions without French/EU passporting, US payment facilitators, Caribbean or Asian EMIs lacking a French/EU authorisation) are legally acceptable only as technical settlement sub-layers: the actual outward cross-border payment must be cleared through an EU authorised intermediary, and an offshore EMI cannot replace or obfuscate that statutory routing. Sole reliance on a non-passported offshore EMI is rated Low; use as an embedded acceptance or expense-card layer above a mainstream French bank is rated Medium/Low due to enhanced ACPR scrutiny of chained disbursements from France.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections and shareholder-loan disbursements to the FR_SARL must be paid in EUR to the operational account through an authorised intermediary (the French bank or an EU/EEA bank passporting into France). Standard routing: SEPA Credit Transfer (IBAN FR76-...) for EUR, or SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR currencies; the transfer's purpose code must align with Banque de France statistical categories — '201'/inward direct investment (IDE) where share capital is increased, and 'credit financier' category for shareholder current-account advances. For all inward transfers of €12,500 or more, the deposit-holding intermediary reports under CMF Article R152-1 and must secure an FDI identification workflow when cumulative injected stock crosses the €1.5 million threshold (annual BdF IDE return). Since EUR is a reserve currency and France is an IMF Article VIII member, no IMF or dual-currency approval is required; verification comprises the shareholder resolution, deposit certificate, and a balance sheet updated to the value date of injection.Trade-payable settlements (brass sheet, copper-alloy blanks, enamel pigments, moulding tooling, subcontract polishing) are executed via SWIFT MT103 direct payments to the vendor's account; each message must quote the invoice number, HS codes for the underlying goods, and the final-beneficiary account details. For established relationships, commercial documentary credits under UCP600 remain permissible where foreign suppliers require bank confirmation; EU suppliers may alternatively be paid by SEPA Credit Transfer. The bank's international desk performs EUR-to-CNY and EUR-to-USD conversion at the same-day fixing using the MT103's settlement currency; no additional French Treasury declaration is required at transaction level because the intermediary files the Banque de France balance-of-payments return. Sole reliance on a non-passported offshore EMI is rated Low; use as an embedded acceptance or expense-card layer above a mainstream French bank is rated Medium/Low due to enhanced ACPR scrutiny of chained disbursements from France.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (IS); reduced 15% rate on the first €42,500 of annual taxable profit for qualifying SMEs (turnover ≤ €10 million and 100% paid-up capital); effective rate for a qualifying small SARL therefore 15% on the initial tranche and 25% on the excess.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% final withholding under Article 119 bis-2 CGI on dividends paid by a French SARL to non-resident corporate shareholders; 30% flat withholding (12.8% foreign levy + 17.2% social levies subject to treaty/EEA limitations) for non-resident individuals. The rate is reduced to 0% where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive applies (10% minimum shareholding held for 24 months) or under an applicable DTA (commonly 0%–15%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return is due within three months of the fiscal year-end. For a calendar-year SARL (typical for Etsy merchants), the FY-2024 return must be filed by 3 May 2025 (or the first working day immediately following 1 May); non-calendar year-ends follow the three-month closing rule.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Four instalments due 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December of the current tax year, computed on the previous year's taxable base; the balance (supplément/solde) under form 2572-SD is due on the annual return statutory deadline (3 May for calendar-year SARLs).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 (annual net turnover or gross assets of the French entity, or group consolidated turnover) triggers the mandatory Master File and French Local File under Articles L.13 AA/L.13 AB of the Tax Procedure Code; a group Consolidated Revenue threshold of EUR 750,000,000 requires Country-by-Country Reporting. A sub-threshold SARL buying brass blanks, enamel dyes, or design tooling from a related foreign supplier must nonetheless maintain contemporaneous arm's-length evidence, deliverable to the tax authority within 30 days on request.

Economic substance scope: France has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation filing; substance is enforced through the abuse-of-law doctrine (Article L.64 LPF), the 'acte anormal de gestion' test (any expense must serve the corporation's own commercial interest), the real-seat (siège réel) doctrine, thin-capitalization, and the Principal Purpose Test imported through BEPS MLI treaty clauses. A French SARL producing hard-enamel lapel pins must exhibit genuine operational substance in France: physical premises, employed personnel handling sales and production decisions, ownership of the enamel-pin design IP, and board functions in France. Absent such indicia, the tax administration may recharacterize the SARL as a fiscal conduit and re-attribute the Etsy/Stripe-derived margin to the controlling shareholder abroad.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI: fine up to €500,000 and five years' imprisonment; aggravated cases (use of offshore accounts, organized concealment) raise the exposure to €1,000,000 and seven years. Civil add-ons: 40% concealment/deceit penalty or 80% fraud-penalty on the CIT deficiency, 0.20% monthly interest, €150 per-document penalties, judicial publication, and confirmation of the assessment by the Comité de répression des abus de droit.

Director personal liability: The gérant, as legal representative, is personally indictable under Article 1741 and may be sentenced together with the entity: imprisonment, restitution of tax, loss of civic rights, interdiction de gérer, travel bans (interdiction de sortie du territoire), and seizure of personal assets. Veil-piercing tools: Article L.267 LPF allows direct tax recovery against the gérant who fraudulently obstructs collection; in insolvency, the action en comblement de passif (Article L.651-2 of the Commercial Code) holds the gérant civilly liable to the creditors for mismanagement.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Application of the abuse-of-law procedure (Article L.64 LPF): the arrangement is disregarded, full French CIT/VAT is re-established on the economic substance, and an 80% deliberate-abuse penalty plus 0.20% monthly interest is applied. Denied-deduction treatment under the acte anormal de gestion rule re-adds the inflated expenses for a 25% to 40% correction; the administration may also lift the filing burden of proof against the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: The gérant who engineered the shell structure risks criminal complicity in tax evasion, personal recharacterization of the tax debt under Article L.267 LPF, a ban on directing commercial companies for up to 15 years, travel restrictions, and civil restitution orders in liquidation proceedings.
Transfer Pricing and Thin-Capitalization Violations

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment re-adding the excess to French taxable profit, plus a 40% intentional misdeclaration penalty and 0.20% monthly interest; interest deduction disallowance under the 1.5:1 thin-cap ratio; a 25% withholding (75% to ETNC jurisdictions) is reinstated on the recharacterized interest and royalty flows; non-production of documentation reverses the burden of proof and can trigger the deliberative penalty without the administration needing to prove a wrong price.

Director personal liability: The gérant signing the returns and approving the intercompany pricing is personally exposed to criminal administrative concealment penalties, civil recovery of the unpaid tax via clawback actions, and professional disqualification where the pricing scheme was deliberately used to drain French profits.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Cash-Payment Infractions

Statutory sanction: Cash-limit breaches: contraventional fines up to €15,000 and disallowance of the unbanked expense for tax purposes; DBE omissions: fine up to €7,500 for natural persons and €37,500 for the SARL, plus possible imprisonment for deliberate concealment of the effective owner; undeclared foreign accounts: €1,500 fixed penalty per account (up to €10,000 per account in non-cooperative states) under Article 1729-0 A and the presumption that the hidden balance constitutes taxable income.

Director personal liability: The gérant who personally disposes of hidden accounts or signs the DBE containing false beneficial owners incurs criminal liability for money-laundering declarations (Article L.561-3 of the Monetary and Financial Code), a ban on management, confiscation of the sums, and personal enforcement for the corporate penalties under the fraudulent-obstruction rule.
Undisclosed Foreign Payment Rails and Digital-Sales Omission

Statutory sanction: Withholding the foreign-account declaration triggers the €1,500/account penalty and the statutory presumption that the funds are unreported taxable income; digital-sales concealment further attracts the 40%-80% penalties and late-payment interest on the omitted CIT base; the tax authority may also apply the 10% late-filing surcharge and seize settlement funds from Etsy/Stripe through third-party attachment.

Director personal liability: The gérant is criminally and civilly exposed when settlement accounts are under the gérant's private name while economically belonging to the SARL; courts may treat the structure as fraudulent concealment, triggering imprisonment, personal recoupment of tax, and a travel ban pending full restitution.
