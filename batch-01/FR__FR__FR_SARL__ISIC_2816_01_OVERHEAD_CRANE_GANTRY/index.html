Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Generale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - portal: www.impots.gouv.fr (professional portal 'Espace Professionnel', electronic filing via EFI). Central formalities portal for incorporation and registry: Guichet Unique des Formalites des Entreprises - formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Customs and export clearance authority: Direction Generale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) - www.douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated French SARL obtains its complete tax identity at registration. Step 1: file the statutory incorporation dossier through the Guichet Unique des Formalites des Entreprises, which transmits to the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Societes) and INSEE. Step 2: INSEE allocates the SIREN (9-digit national enterprise registration number) and the SIRET (establishment number); the RCS issues the Kbis extract evidencing legal existence. Step 3: the French intra-Community VAT number is automatically generated in the format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN. Step 4: the corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Societes) account is opened at the competent SIE (Service des Impots des Entreprises); annual tax returns (Form 2065-SD family) and monthly or quarterly VAT returns (Form 3310-CA3) are filed electronically via impots.gouv.fr. Alternative path for a foreign entity without a French fixed establishment: direct VAT identification application to the DGFiP non-resident VAT service, including the procedural forms (Form 13763 reference set) and, where required under Article 289 A CGI, the appointment of a French tax representative (representant fiscal), resulting in a French VAT number without local incorporation; the foreign entity must separately assess whether its commercial activity creates a permanent establishment or a taxable nexus for French corporate income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope; Pass_Through_Eligible (limited); Tax_Exempt_Zone (not applicable). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope; Pass_Through_Eligible (limited); Tax_Exempt_Zone (not applicable). A standard French SARL is subject to default taxation at entity level under the Impot sur les Societes (IS) and is governed by the territoriality principle of Article 209-I CGI: French IS is levied on profits derived from businesses operated in France, while profits attributable to foreign permanent establishments are excluded from the French tax base unless the taxpayer obtains the optional worldwide/consolidated taxation regime under Article 209 quinquies CGI.

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box system; the relevant statutory elections are: (i) option for pass-through income-tax (IR) treatment for a maximum of five fiscal years by newly created small SARLs meeting Article 239 bis C CGI conditions (individual associates and turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million); (ii) family SARL (SARL de famille) is taxable by default under the partnership/transparent regime of Article 239 bis AA CGI unless it irrevocably elects for IS; (iii) option for French fiscal integration (integration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for a parent holding at least 95% of the SARL; (iv) patent box regime under Article 238 CGI allowing a reduced 10% IS rate on qualifying net income from patented technology and licensed intangible assets; (v) optional worldwide taxation (benefice mondial/consolide) under Article 209 quinquies CGI; (vi) VAT simplification elections including the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) and Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) regimes for distance sales and non-established sellers operating in France.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established taxable persons: EUR 0 - no franchise en base de TVA is available; registration is mandatory from the first taxable euro. For domestic French micro-suppliers, the franchise en base de TVA exemption applies up to EUR 85,000 (supplies of goods) and EUR 37,500 (supplies of services), with tolerance thresholds at EUR 93,500 and EUR 40,500 respectively. The domestic franchise is not available to foreign sellers, and a French SARL engaged in manufacturing of crane and hoisting equipment typically operates on the standard regime with monthly VAT filing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of manufactured port gantry cranes, hoisting gear and spare parts are exempt with the right to deduct input VAT (effet zero-rating) under Article 262-1-I CGI where: (i) the transfer of ownership and the right to dispose of the goods as owner passes to the purchaser; (ii) the goods are physically transported outside the European Union (EU) by the seller, the purchaser, or a third party acting for their account; and (iii) the physical export occurs no later than the end of the third month following the month in which the transfer of ownership took place. Documentation must be in the exporter's possession at the date of the VAT return on which the exemption is claimed (BOI-TVA-CHAMP-30-10-10-20/30). Intra-Community supplies to EU-established taxable persons follow the separate zero-rating of Article 262-1-II CGI, conditional on the validity of the customer's EU VAT number (VIES validation) and equivalent evidentiary standards. Export-related services directly connected with the exported goods and technical engineering/design studies for exported machinery may qualify for zero-rating under Article 262-1-III CGI where the specific conditions of the BOI are met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE - Document Administratif Unique lodged via the DELTA-X customs system) bearing the MRN and evidencing physical exit from the EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR consignment note / air waybill) indicating the third-country port of discharge, final destination and consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation bancaire de rapatriement / certificat de change) evidencing collection and repatriation of export proceeds through the French banking channel
  • Commercial export invoice (facture d'exportation) itemising the goods, Incoterms and export destination together with the documentary letter of credit or project escrow payment schedule
  • Purchase order / project contract evidencing the transfer of ownership and the allocation of transport risk between the exporter and the non-EU buyer

France mandates structured electronic invoicing and e-reporting under the reform of facturation electronique, operating through the public platform Chorus Pro and certified private platforms (PDP - Plateformes de Dematerialisation Partenaires) connected to the National Invoice Directory (ANF). Mandatory structured formats: Factur-X (EN 16931), UBL 2.1 or cXML. Timetable: from 1 September 2026, all taxable persons must be able to receive e-invoices and large enterprises and mid-cap companies must issue e-invoices; from 1 September 2027, small and micro enterprises must issue e-invoices. E-invoice and e-reporting data must be transmitted to the ANF within ten (10) working days of issuance (or of payment acceptance for payment-related e-reporting data). France does not apply a transactional digital stamp or real-time fiscal clearance model; instead, DGFiP performs automated, systemic cross-checking of e-invoice, e-reporting and VAT return data, with a near-real-time compliance audit posture.

The DGFiP systematically reconciles customs export declarations (DAE flows from DGDDI), transport instruments and inward remittance (FX) certificates against the VAT returns on which the 0% rate was claimed. The clawback and reassessment window runs three (3) years under Article L.176 LPF for VAT and Article L.169 LPF for income/corporate taxes; where customs fraud is established, the customs reprise is extended to six (6) years under Article 354 of the Code des douanes. If export proof is missing, non-contemporaneous or inconsistent, the zero-rating is re-characterised as taxable French VAT at 20%, subject to interest de retard at 0.2% per month and graduated penalties under Articles 1728 and 1729 CGI: 10% (late payment after formal notice), 40% (failure to declare / bad faith) and 80% (active dissimulation or abuse of right). Falsified or fictitious export documents attract the 100% majoration under Article 1729-1 CGI and, where customs law applies, the sanctions of Articles 413 and 414 of the Code des douanes: confiscation of the goods, a fine of one (1) to five (5) times the value of the frauded goods and imprisonment of three (3) to ten (10) years in aggravated or organised fraud cases (including export VAT carousel schemes). FX compliance: although the dossier adopts a contractual outer cap of 180 days for repatriation of export proceeds, French customs foreign-exchange discipline requires collection within the banking and contractual deadlines (commonly thirty (30) days after the payment due date under Article 153 of the Code des douanes as referenced in DGFiP/DGDDI practice); failure to repatriate within the applicable window triggers retrospective denial of the zero-rating benefit and the clawback of VAT plus interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a member of the euro area and applies the EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital. The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Non-resident owners may freely inject capital, service debt, and remit dividends, subject to mandatory economic substance, anti-money laundering, and sanctions screening. Profit remittance is not subject to FX licensing or quantitative limits; however, balance-of-payments declarations and tax reporting obligations apply. The only residual restrictions arise from EU/UN sanctions, anti-money-laundering verification, and documentary evidence requirements for bank execution. For foreign currency exposures, conversion is freely permissible through the interbank market, but transactions to non-account holders or on behalf of OFAC-listed jurisdictions face stringent compliance blocking risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France currently imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescriptive deadline for repatriation of export receipts. French exporters are required to record all cross-border transactions in their annual balance-of-payments declaration to Banque de France when cumulative flows exceed EUR 15,000,000, and must supply evidence of the economic basis of any significant receivables or advance payments. Intra-EU exports are exempt from formal reporting. For extra-EU sales financed through documentary letters of credit, banks verify the underlying customs export declaration (Document Administratif Unique / DAU) and the financial settlement to ensure the transaction is genuine. The absence of a surrender rule means proceeds may be retained in hard currencies, but prudent treasury practice is to convert to EUR using covered forward contracts promptly to mitigate currency risk.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with foreign exchange and trade reporting administration jointly overseen by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) and the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. French-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions licensed by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) may legally open accounts for SARL clients and provide cross-border payment services under an EU passport. Non-EU offshore digital EMIs without an EU banking or e-money licence generally cannot clear EUR-denominated payments through the SEPA/TARGET2 rails, cannot offer French IBANs, and present acute anti-money-laundering and sanctions risk. A French or EU-passported EMI is viable only for low-risk operational cash flows, collection of customer disbursements, and inward remittances; it is not acceptable for capital escrow, documentary credit operations, or high-value project disbursements due to liquidity and regulatory limits on lending and trade finance.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Ultimate beneficial ownership opacity or undeclared non-resident investors

Root cause: Ultimate beneficial ownership opacity or undeclared non-resident investors

Defense evidence: Official KBIS excerpt listing statutory representatives; audited or notarised shareholders' register; completed UBO declaration form identifying each natural person with more than 25% direct or indirect capital; passport copies with apostille translation; source-of-funds evidence for capital injection (bank statements from the investor's originating bank); corporate constitution and shareholders' agreement signed before a French notaire.
Sanctions, PEP, or adverse media screening hits on the SARL manager or UBO

Root cause: Sanctions, PEP, or adverse media screening hits on the SARL manager or UBO

Defense evidence: Independent legal opinion or due diligence report from a licensed French avocat demonstrating the absence of sanctions designations; sworn justification of any PEP status; denies/political exposure documentation; negative media analysis; evidence of lawful prior business activity (supplier invoices, tax filings, trade registers); engagement letter with a French compliance advisor.
Unclear economic purpose or mismatch between declared object (overhead crane manufacturing) and fund flows

Root cause: Unclear economic purpose or mismatch between declared object (overhead crane manufacturing) and fund flows

Defense evidence: Detailed corporate business plan; executed port gantry crane sale agreements or preliminary contracts; invoices and purchase orders from counterparties; a certified flow chart linking each escrow receipt, letter of credit disbursement, and trade settlement to a specific cargo contract; declaration of the underlying commercial transaction to DGDDI; evidence of physical business premises and operational machinery.
Adverse credit history, litigation, or liquidation proceedings against the SARL

Root cause: Adverse credit history, litigation, or liquidation proceedings against the SARL

Defense evidence: Court extracts (extrait de casier judiciaire) for managers; a French registered auditor's report confirming solvency; declaration of no pending insolvency proceedings from the commercial court; payment default remediation evidence; an escrow arrangement assigning the incoming contract receivables as collateral to the bank.
Inadequate documentary licences or regulatory permits for manufacture of port cranes

Root cause: Inadequate documentary licences or regulatory permits for manufacture of port cranes

Defense evidence: Statement that the ISIC 2816 activity is not a licensed profession in France; CE conformity declarations for existing crane models; Type Examination certificates from notified bodies; ISO 9001; insurance certificates covering product liability and marine cargo risk; evidence of compliance with EU Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC and potential ATEX requirements for port zones.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound non-resident equity or shareholder loan capital is a SWIFT MT103 international wire to the SARL's €-IBAN, using the harmonised EU European Article Numbering Programme (EAN) / CF Code for balance-of-payments classification; the remitter must cite the cross-border capital transaction category under EU Regulation (EC) No 184/2005 and the Banque de France Standard clearing rails for over/under global trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct remittances covering invoice payments; (2) Documentary Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) issued or advised through the bank's trade finance platform with SWIFT MT700/MT707 messaging; (3) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EUR disbursements within the SEPA zone; (4) TARGET2-Ipane real-time gross settlement (RTGS) for high-value EUR wires and interbank obligations; (5) for cross-currency settlements, restricted-use correspondent accounts are used with simultaneous MT202 and MT103 pairs. For port gantry crane project contracts, banks commonly structure the outbound disbursements under a separate project escrow sub-account, with accounts payable released only upon presentation of a compliant certificate of shipment, customs export declaration, and the buyer's acceptance certificate. The SARL must ensure that all export payments are reported under the annual balance-of-payments declaration and that any pre-payment for imported components is justified by pro-forma invoices to the bank to avoid characterisation as capital flight.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. French-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions licensed by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) may legally open accounts for SARL clients and provide cross-border payment services under an EU passport. Non-EU offshore digital EMIs without an EU banking or e-money licence generally cannot clear EUR-denominated payments through the SEPA/TARGET2 rails, cannot offer French IBANs, and present acute anti-money-laundering and sanctions risk. A French or EU-passported EMI is viable only for low-risk operational cash flows, collection of customer disbursements, and inward remittances; it is not acceptable for capital escrow, documentary credit operations, or high-value project disbursements due to liquidity and regulatory limits on lending and trade finance.
