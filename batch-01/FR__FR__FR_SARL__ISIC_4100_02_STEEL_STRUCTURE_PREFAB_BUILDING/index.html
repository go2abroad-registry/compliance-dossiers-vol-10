Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) administered by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered office; all formalities are submitted electronically through the INPI Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II – Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (general formation rules), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 seq. (SARL formation, share capital, contributions, managers); Article 1832 of the French Civil Code (contract of company); applicable enforcement decrees (Décrets d'application) on RCS registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts constitutifs (Articles of Association) followed by the online immatriculation form (demande d'immatriculation principale au RCS); the definitive legal existence document is the Extrait Kbis (Kbis Registration Extract).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 11 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SARL has no statutory minimum share capital; the stated capital is freely fixed in the articles. At least 20% of cash contributions must be released at subscription, the balance within five years (Article L.223-7, Code de commerce); cash subscriptions must be deposited in a blocked bank account (escrow) until the company is registered and the Kbis delivered; in-kind contributions must be fully paid at once and, where any in-kind contribution exceeds €7,500, must be valued by a commissaire aux apports (contribution auditor).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique — operational portals: impots.gouv.fr (professional 'Espace Pro' for enterprises); business formalities and VAT number allocation through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr administered by the INPI; commercial registration with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce.

E-government filing portal

For a newly constituted SARL: (1) file the incorporation dossier (statuts, appointment of gérant, beneficial ownership declaration) with the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), which routes the file to the RCS; (2) upon registration the Greffe/INPI issues the SIREN (9-digit identification) and SIRET (establishment) numbers; (3) the DGFiP automatically allocates the intra-Community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 2-digit key + SIREN) and notifies the taxpayer via the SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises); (4) activate the professional online account (Espace Pro) on impots.gouv.fr; (5) for a non-established foreign entity carrying out taxable operations in France without a fixed establishment, direct VAT registration is filed with the territorial SIE (or via the OSS/non-EU OSS regimes), attaching the foreign certificate of incorporation, proof of economic activity, and statutory identification documents; a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) is mandatory for non-EU taxable persons in certain cases; processing time is approximately 2 to 4 weeks from complete filing.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a SARL is by default subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the 25% standard rate (reduced 15% rate for taxable profits up to EUR 42,500 per annum for qualifying small businesses with turnover below EUR 10 million); no pass-through classification is available as a default. Territorial scope (principe de territorialité): IS is levied only on profits attributable to operations carried out in France or to French assets/rights; foreign branch profits are generally outside the French tax net unless the option for worldwide taxation is exercised. Metropolitan France is not a tax-exempt zone, but targeted exemptions apply (e.g., jeune entreprise innovante, zones franches urbaines, stock-option/R&D credit regimes) and specific overseas-zone regimes exist. — For a newly constituted SARL: (1) file the incorporation dossier (statuts, appointment of gérant, beneficial ownership declaration) with the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), which routes the file to the RCS; (2) upon registration the Greffe/INPI issues the SIREN (9-digit identification) and SIRET (establishment) numbers; (3) the DGFiP automatically allocates the intra-Community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 2-digit key + SIREN) and notifies the taxpayer via the SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises); (4) activate the professional online account (Espace Pro) on impots.gouv.fr; (5) for a non-established foreign entity carrying out taxable operations in France without a fixed establishment, direct VAT registration is filed with the territorial SIE (or via the OSS/non-EU OSS regimes), attaching the foreign certificate of incorporation, proof of economic activity, and statutory identification documents; a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) is mandatory for non-EU taxable persons in certain cases; processing time is approximately 2 to 4 weeks from complete filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a SARL is by default subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the 25% standard rate (reduced 15% rate for taxable profits up to EUR 42,500 per annum for qualifying small businesses with turnover below EUR 10 million); no pass-through classification is available as a default. Territorial scope (principe de territorialité): IS is levied only on profits attributable to operations carried out in France or to French assets/rights; foreign branch profits are generally outside the French tax net unless the option for worldwide taxation is exercised. Metropolitan France is not a tax-exempt zone, but targeted exemptions apply (e.g., jeune entreprise innovante, zones franches urbaines, stock-option/R&D credit regimes) and specific overseas-zone regimes exist.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or entity classification election exists. Available statutory elections under French law: (1) option for income tax (IR, régime des sociétés de personnes) under CGI art. 239 bis AA for small SARLs whose members are individuals and which meet the revenue thresholds — option is irrevocable for five fiscal years; (2) VAT elections: option for the 'régime réel normal' from the simplified VAT regime, option for the franchise en base exemption within small-business thresholds, and option for VAT payment on debits; (3) fiscal integration election (intégration fiscale, CGI art. 223 A) permitting a parent holding at least 95% of an EU-established French subsidiary to consolidate the IS result of the group; (4) election for the parent-subsidiary regime (régime mère-fille) and for the participation-exemption on disposal gains; (5) hybrid mismatch rules (CGI art. 212 bis, implementing ATAD) apply automatically to deny deductions or include income for mismatched arrangements; no protective election is available to override them for cross-border hybrid structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For taxable persons established in France, VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction (no economic turnover threshold). However, the 'franchise en base de TVA' (small business exemption) applies to businesses whose preceding full-year turnover does not exceed EUR 85,000 (supply of goods, 'seuil normal') or EUR 37,500 (supply of services, 'seuil normal'), with 'seuils majorés' of EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250 respectively where the prior two-year turnover did not exceed the normal thresholds; once the majoré threshold is exceeded, registration becomes compulsory. Non-established EU suppliers exceed the EUR 10,000 cross-border distance-sales threshold into France to trigger registration (Union OSS scheme or standard French registration); non-EU suppliers of digital/electronic services must register or use the Non-Union OSS from the first sale with no threshold; B2B supplies of services by non-established vendors are subject to reverse charge with no French registration required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the EU are VAT-exempt with full right of deduction (effective 0% rate) under CGI art. 262-I-1° and EU Directive art. 146, provided the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU by the supplier or on its behalf within 90 days, evidenced by a validated export customs declaration (DELTA-X, EX/EXA) bearing the MRN. Intra-Community supplies of goods are exempt under CGI art. 262 ter only where the purchaser holds a valid EU VAT identification number and the goods are dispatched to another EU Member State, with the supplier required to obtain confirmation of the counterpart VAT number within one month. Services directly connected with the export operation (international transport, handling, loading, export-related engineering) may be zero-rated under CGI art. 259 and 262-II where the services are effectively and exclusively linked to the exported goods and rendered to a customer established outside France; for the steel structure prefab EPC model, zero-rating is claimable for the prefabricated building components only when title and physical risk transfer outside the EU and the goods do not enter French consumption; assembly services performed at the foreign site remain outside the scope of French VAT under B2B territoriality rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane EX/EXA lodged via the DELTA-X system, validated with the MRN/R recognises signature and the export summary declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or contract of carriage (B/L or sea waybill, CMR consignment note, CIM rail waybill, or ATA carnet, evidencing dispatch outside EU territory)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank statement or certificate of the authorized intermediary evidencing FX repatriation of export proceeds into France)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (including HS codes, gross/net weights, Incoterms, and value declarations)
  • Intrastat declaration of exchanges of goods (DEB) and the recapitulative statement of intra-Community supplies (with the customer's EU VAT number) for EU-destined deliveries
  • Incoterms-based proof of risk transfer, insurance certificate or surveyor's report, and port/terminal receipts supporting the physical exit

Mandatory e-invoicing reform (facturation électronique) for domestic B2B transactions is phased in from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and mid-size companies (ETI), and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises (PME/TPE). E-invoices must be issued in a structured electronic format (Factur-X, UBL, CII) and transmitted to the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) through an accredited Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP); there is no real-time fiscal clearance, stamping, or prior visa in the Italian SDI model — instead, near-real-time 'e-reporting' of B2C and cross-border transaction data flows to the PPF within one operating day (J+1) via the same pipeline; foreign taxable persons carrying out registrable French transactions must transmit e-reporting data through an appointed PDP or the simplified OSS procedure, subject to API interoperability standards set by the DGFiP.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to heightened audit scrutiny by the DGFiP's national and territorial VAT verification units and by the customs anti-fraud directorate (DNRED). Any substantive or documentary deficiency triggers immediate VAT reassessment (rappel de TVA) of the exempt amount plus late-payment interest of 0.20% per calendar month (CGI art. 1727) and penalties: 40% for deliberate negligence (mauvaise foi, CGI art. 1729 a) or 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres (manœuvres frauduleuses, CGI art. 1729 b); the penalty base may be increased in cases of occultation of activity. Criminal exposure under CGI art. 1741 for tax fraud reaches 5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine (aggravated to 7 years and EUR 3,000,000 for organised fraud), with an extended 6-year statute of limitation for hidden activities. Customs law (Code des douanes, arts. 414 and 423-2) additionally imposes fines of one to two times the value of the object of the fraud, confiscation of the transport means and goods, and up to 3 years' imprisonment; retrospective clawback may cover the full statutorily reopened prescription period (generally 3 years, extended to 6 years for undisclosed or fraudulent activity).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in France. Article 63 TFEU and the OECD Codes of Liberalisation guarantee unrestricted capital and current-account convertibility; no prior administrative licence is required for trade payments, service and royalty remittances, intra-group flows, dividend distributions, or capital injections. Capital-account liberalisation is tempered only by (i) the French FDI screening regime under Articles L.151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code and Decree 2019-1590 as amended, which is not triggered for non-sensitive steel structure prefab engineering and assembly (ISIC 4100 / NAICS 236220); (ii) EUR 10,000 cash cross-border declaration duties and Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical reporting applied automatically by resident banks; and (iii) profit remittance friction of a tax nature rather than an exchange-control nature. Dividends paid by the SARL to non-resident shareholders attract a 25% domestic withholding tax, reduced by EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive implementation (0% subject to conditions) and by applicable double tax treaties through refund procedures. The practical remittance pathway therefore requires corporate formality compliance (general meeting decision, certified financial statements where required, tax residency certificates and Form 2561/2777 filing where applicable), not administrative exchange authorisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirement exists for export receipts. A French exporter may retain receivables in euro or in any foreign currency indefinitely at any conversion ratio, and may use the proceeds directly for operational disbursements or within its FX risk-management policy. The applicable statutory obligations are: (i) export invoices and customs declarations to the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (Intrastat for intra-EU dispatches and extra-EU export declarations); (ii) the servicing bank's balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque de France, which statutorily codes all cross-border receipts and payments; and (iii) Tracfin reporting under Article L.561-15 of the French Monetary and Financial Code where trade-based money-laundering indicators are detected. Treasury records must therefore preserve the SWIFT MT103 advices, bills of lading, mill test certificates, packing lists, and VAT export-exemption evidence to substantiate each export receipt in a tax or AML review.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the European System of Central Banks led by the European Central Bank; the external relations and capital-movement supervision remit is co-administered by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty), with bank-level prudential enforcement by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High but structurally limited. Under PSD2 and the Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC), only an EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institution or Payment Institution duly passported into France with the ACPR may lawfully provide corporate payment accounts to the SARL. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK-only, Caribbean, Singapore-licensed, or UAE-based multi-currency wallets) cannot open a compliant French payment account; they may act solely as unregulated software access layers over an underlying authorised bank, which undermines their treasury usefulness. Furthermore, for the SARL's share capital, French company law requires an attestation de dépôt de fonds issued by a licensed credit institution (or a notaire acting as escrow agent); an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot lawfully certify capital deposits. EMI rails are therefore acceptable for small operational disbursements, petty expenses, and salary payments, but must never be positioned as the statutory escrow or as the primary trade-settlement lane for the EPC treasury.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wiring protocol: (1) open the SARL's blocked capital deposit account (compte bloqué de constitution) with a Tier-1 French bank or before a notaire; (2) the shareholder remits EUR through SWIFT MT103, or SEPA SCT when inside the Single Euro Payments Area, quoting purpose code 'FUND' or 'SHAR' where the bank supports it, with a clear remittance narrative 'Apport en numéraire – constitution de SARL'; (3) the receiving bank issues the attestation de dépôt de fonds required for the RCS filing; (4) after issuance of the Kbis extract, the account is upgraded to the operational current account. The bank will classify the transaction in its Banque de France balance-of-payments report as 'direct investment' (investissement direct), and the file must contain the SARL's SIREN registration, UBO declaration, share subscription agreement, and source-of-funds ledger. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High but structurally limited. Under PSD2 and the Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC), only an EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institution or Payment Institution duly passported into France with the ACPR may lawfully provide corporate payment accounts to the SARL. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK-only, Caribbean, Singapore-licensed, or UAE-based multi-currency wallets) cannot open a compliant French payment account; they may act solely as unregulated software access layers over an underlying authorised bank, which undermines their treasury usefulness. Furthermore, for the SARL's share capital, French company law requires an attestation de dépôt de fonds issued by a licensed credit institution (or a notaire acting as escrow agent); an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot lawfully certify capital deposits. EMI rails are therefore acceptable for small operational disbursements, petty expenses, and salary payments, but must never be positioned as the statutory escrow or as the primary trade-settlement lane for the EPC treasury.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax ('impôt sur les sociétés') under Article 219-I CGI, applicable to fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2022; reduced 15% rate applies to the first EUR 38,120 of taxable profit for qualifying small enterprises (turnover below EUR 10,000,000, fully paid-up share capital, and at least 75% held by individuals).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax on gross dividends under Article 119 bis 2 CGI applied to distributions made to non-resident shareholders; relief is available under double-taxation agreements (rates commonly 0%-15%); exemption applies for EU/EEA corporate shareholders satisfying the Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions under Article 119 ter CGI (direct holding of at least 10% of the French entity's capital for a continuous two-year period); the distributing French SARL also bears the 3% contribution on distributions under Article 235 ter ZCA CGI, which is neutralised or refundable for EU/EEA parent companies qualifying for the directive.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 annual turnover or gross assets for the mandatory French transfer pricing documentation ('documentation sur les prix de transfert') under Article L. 13 AA LPF, including the Local File and group Master File; EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group turnover triggers the French Country-by-Country Report obligations (Article 223 quinquies C CGI); Form 2257-SD must be filed annually by entities meeting the EUR 400,000,000 threshold.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations registry of the Gulf/UAE type, but substantive presence is rigorously enforced through the concepts of 'siège de direction effective' (place of effective management), 'acte anormal de gestion' (abnormal act of management), and the anti-abuse doctrine under Article L. 64 LPF; a French SARL executing steel-structure EPC contracts must maintain real French managerial capacity, payroll, local decisions, risks, and assets, failing which a foreign-controlled 'letterbox' is recharacterised, deductions are denied, and income is reallocated to the actual beneficiaries.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions under Article 1741 of the French General Tax Code (CGI): imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to EUR 500,000, increased to 7 years and EUR 3,000,000 where committed by an organised group or involving a non-cooperative State; civil tax law penalties of 40%-80% (Article 1729 CGI), late-payment interest at 0.20% per month, confiscation of the fraudulent proceeds, and publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors and the SARL gérant can be held jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt under Article L. 267 of the French Book of Tax Procedures for deliberate irregularities; a criminal travel ban ('interdiction de quitter le territoire'), restriction of management rights, personal bankruptcy measures, and, in liquidation, an 'action en comblement de passif' under Article L. 651-2 of the Commercial Code may follow.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation and upward adjustment of the French taxable base under Article 57 CGI; denial of the deduction for undisclosed miss-trust? administrative imposition of the majoration for failure to produce documentation equal to the greater of EUR 10,000 and 5% of the adjustment (Article 1740 A CGI); late-payment interest and penalty increases of 40%-80% for deliberate concealment; reciprocal adjustments may be denied in non-cooperative jurisdictions.

Director personal liability: The French gérant personally signs the tax representations; a director who knowingly signs manipulated intercompany steel-supply documentation is exposed to the Article 1741 criminal penalties (5 years imprisonment and EUR 500,000), civil restoration of the understated profit, and use of the corporate veil-piercing doctrine under Article L. 267 LPF; travel bans may be imposed during the penal inquiry.
