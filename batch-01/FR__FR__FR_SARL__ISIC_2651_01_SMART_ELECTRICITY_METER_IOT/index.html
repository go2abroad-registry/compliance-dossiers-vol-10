Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A purely virtual office is not acceptable for ISIC 2651 manufacture of smart electricity meters. French law (Law No. 84-148 and Articles L123-11-1 et seq. of the Commercial Code) permits administrative domiciliation with an approved domiciliation company, but industrial manufacturing requires a declared physical establishment, equipment, premises and local personnel. A domiciliation agreement can support the registered office for legal correspondence, but the operational production site must be identified and properly covered by permits, safety inspections and social declarations. A virtual office without actual industrial substance will expose the company to regulatory penalties, tax recharacterization, and inability to obtain the required manufacturing license under the Code de l'énergie and electrical equipment conformity regulations.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SARL. The capital is fixed freely in the articles and must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked French bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; funds are released only after registration and issuance of the K-bis. At least one fifth of each cash contribution must be paid up before filing unless the statutes require full payment; the remainder must be paid within five years. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up at formation and, if required, accompanied by a contribution auditor's report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety verification by the service départemental d'incendie et de secours for any open-to-public or large workplace area; structural load and electrical compliance attestation issued by an approved inspection body such as APAVE or Bureau Veritas; occupational risk assessment and DUERP (Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels) must be maintained; operator workplace clearance confirmed through URSSAF registration and payment of branch-specific social contributions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – registration through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); direct VAT/PE registration at the territorial SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises); digital portal impots.gouv.fr; customs EORI allocation by Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via DELTA-X

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated SARL is registered by operation of law through the Guichet Unique, which automatically delivers the SIREN/SIRET and the intra-Community VAT number (format FR + 11 alphanumeric digits, i.e., FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN) within approximately one week of incorporation; no separate VAT application is required for the domestic French establishment. For export operations, the SARL must additionally obtain an EORI number (FR + SIREN) from the DGDDI customs system DELTA-X. A non-established foreign entity engaging in French taxable transactions (e.g., a foreign parent selling IoT hardware in France) must apply directly to the SIE using Form 1442-SD, attach certified proof of incorporation, the list of directors, residency certificates of officers, and a signed power of attorney; if the entity is established outside the EU, appointment of a fiscally responsible representative (représentant fiscal) is compulsory under Article 289 A CGI. A foreign entity with a French permanent establishment must file Form 1477-SD with the SIE and, where it is the EU-registered operator, present its EORI and appoint a customs representative for export clearance. The SARL itself, being French, requires no parent-company tax approval; non-resident shareholders merely supply a tax residency certificate for treaty benefit claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

SARLs are default-taxed under French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés – IS) at 25% (reduced 15% rate for SMEs with turnover ≤ €10 million, share capital fully paid, and ≥75% individually-owned capital – Art. 219-I-b CGI). Available elective regimes: (1) Option for pass-through/partnership taxation (Impôt sur le Revenu – IR) under Article 239 bis AA CGI for family SARLs or SARLs less than five years old, meeting the statutory shareholder/activity conditions, irrevocable for five years; (2) Fiscal integration (intégration fiscale, Article 223 A CGI) for a French parent holding at least 95% of the SARL's shares, permitting full group consolidation of tax results; (3) Election to apply the participation exemption (régime mère-fille, Article 145 CGI) for qualifying French/EU/non-EU shareholdings of at least 5% held for two years, providing 95% exemption from corporate tax; (4) Anti-hybrid and notional-interest elections under Article 212 bis and BOI-INT-DIF-360-20 for cross-border financing and hybrid mismatch avoidance; (5) The statutory option for the franchise en base de TVA (VAT small-business exemption) for annual turnover under €85,000, which is a genuine election disapplying normal VAT rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic: no minimum threshold – any French business making taxable supplies of goods or services must be registered for TVA, and the SARL holds its VAT number automatically from incorporation. Franchise en base de TVA (optional small-business exemption): €85,000 annual turnover for all activities from 1 January 2025 (implementing the EU SME Directive 2020/285; replacing the former differentiated thresholds of €91,900 for goods and €36,800 for services); this exemption covers only pure domestic supplies and denies input VAT recovery. Intra-EU distance sales to French consumers: €10,000 EU-wide aggregation threshold, after which OSS registration or local French VAT registration is required. Non-established foreign suppliers that place IoT equipment or provide remote telemetry services in France have no exemption threshold and must register directly for TVA from the first taxable transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% TVA rate applies to: (a) definitive exports of smart electricity meters and related IoT hardware physically leaving the EU customs territory (Article 262-I-1° CGI), evidenced by a customs export declaration (H1/DAE accepted in DELTA-X with a Movement Reference Number), physical transport of goods, and exit confirmation recorded by the customs office of export within 90 days of acceptance, extendable to 150 days (Articles 329 and 333-0 CGI, Annex III); (b) intra-EU dispatches of manufactured meters to a VAT-registered customer in another Member State at 0% under Article 262-ter CGI, requiring the customer's valid EU VAT number, issuance of the invoice, and monthly filing of the ECSL/DEB declaration; and (c) technically connected offshore services – installation, calibration, firmware provisioning, and remote telemetry data services supplied to non-EU clients – which are outside French VAT territoriality under Articles 259, 259-A to 259-C CGI where the effective use and enjoyment occurs outside the EU. For all categories, the exporter must demonstrate the commercial reality of the flow (contract, Incoterms, transport instructions, payment trace); failure to substantiate the chain reclassifies the supply as taxable at 20% TVA.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE/H1 in DELTA-X) with MRN and customs exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic negotiable copy with discharge stamps) / CMR / IATA Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificat de change or bank Swift MT103) evidencing FX repatriation of the export proceeds

France operates a mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting regime for all VAT-liable persons established in France: large enterprises (grandes entreprises) and mid-size enterprises (ETI) must issue structured e-invoices from 1 September 2026; SMEs (including this SARL if it qualifies as an SME) must comply from 1 September 2027; all taxable persons must be able to receive e-invoices from 1 September 2026. The regime is a 3-corner Y-model without digital stamping: invoices are routed through the public platform PPF (Portail Public de Facturation) or a certified partner dematerialisation platform (PDS), exchanging the required data fields in Factur-X, UBL 2.1, or CII (D16B) formats via secure APIs (machine-to-machine). E-reporting (transmission to DGFiP of summary data for cross-border B2B supplies and B2C transactions) is mandatory from the same dates. Non-compliance with e-invoicing obligations triggers penalties under Article 1737 CGI (€15 per missing or non-compliant invoice, capped at €150,000 per calendar year, with reduced caps for SMEs) and obstruction of tax audit may lead to summary assessment under Articles 1730–1732 CGI.

DGFiP and French Customs (DGDDI) jointly audit export zero-rating claims; all export proof must be retained for 6 years (Article L102 B of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales) and produced within 30 days of an audit request. Where the 90/150-day evidence window is missed, the supply is reassessed at 20% TVA plus late interest (intérêts de retard at 0.20% per calendar month); in bad-faith cases a 40% penalty applies (Article 1729 CGI), escalating to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres, plus a €750 fine per documentary irregularity (Article 1788 A CGI). Although the general exchange-control repatriation obligation for goods export proceeds was liberalised, the 180-day benchmark remains the reference standard for export commercial terms and for documentary acceptance; payment terms exceeding 180 days require documented justification in the audit file. Criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI (false invoicing, round-tripping, fabricated customs certificates) is available, carrying up to €500,000 fine and 5 years' imprisonment for the responsible legal representatives. In a fraudulent chain, the State may declare the French exporting SARL jointly liable under Article 1691 bis CGI for the unpaid VAT of its non-established supplier or customer, and the exporter loses entitlement to the reduced corporate tax rate and innovation-related credits, with input VAT clawed back and secured by a customs guarantee (sûreté) for continuing zero-rated exports.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account operations; France applies the EU free-movement-of-capital principle (Art. 63 TFEU, Articles L151-1 and L151-2 CMF). No exchange-control authorization is required for profit remittance, dividends, loan repayments, royalties, or trade settlement. Residual barriers: (i) prior FDI screening authorization under Article L151-3 CMF for non-EU/EEA direct investments in sensitive sectors, extended by Decree 2019-1590 and the 31 December 2023 decree to activities affecting the integrity, security, or continuity of energy infrastructure — smart-grid metering equipment that interoperates with public distribution networks and supplies critical data to grid operators may be captured, requiring a legal perimeter memo; (ii) statutory balance-of-payments (BOP) reporting to the Banque de France for all flows, and specific reporting of direct investments above €1,000,000; (iii) dividend distributions to non-EU parents are unrestricted but may be subject to withholding tax (0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for eligible EU/EEA shareholders; 5–15% under DTTs such as FR–US, FR–CN). The capital account is open with only declarative and screening constraints, not quantitative exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds exists in France; the exchange-control regime was liberalized in 1990 (Law 89-1008). Exporters may retain receivables in any currency and are not required to convert into EUR within any statutory window. Obligations are limited to: (i) BOP reporting of every individual non-resident transaction equal to or exceeding €12,500 (aggregated weekly per counterparty where applicable) — typically discharged monthly by the intermediary bank in the 'filière déléguée' unless the company opts for direct reporting to the Banque de France; (ii) arm's-length pricing of export receivables under Article 57 CGI — receivables systematically outstanding beyond 12 months may be challenged as deemed distributions or transfer-pricing adjustments; (iii) statutory accounts must be kept in EUR, with unrealized FX differences booked per the French PCG or IFRS; (iv) export invoices for IoT hardware should specify a market-standard payment term (60–180 days) and be settled via SWIFT MT103 with a BOP category evidencing merchandise trade (goods sold), so the receivables remain traceable and hedgeable.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) — central bank vested with balance-of-payments statistics and cross-border transaction reporting under Article L141-6 of the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF); foreign-exchange and capital-movement policy administered jointly by the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; prudential supervision exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for credit institutions and payment/EMI institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High for operational treasury, Low for capital escrow. ACPR-licensed or EU-passported EMIs (Qonto, Shine, Wise Europe, Revolut Bank UAB under EU passporting into France) provide rapid remote onboarding, SEPA and SWIFT settlement, multi-currency IBANs (EUR, USD, GBP), and strong API integration for trade flows of a smart-meter manufacturer. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs cannot hold the SARL's initial share capital as a blocked escrow account because the certificat de dépôt des fonds required by Article L223-7 of the French Commercial Code for Greffe registration must be issued by a credit institution or a notary — a pure EMI certificate is not accepted by the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés; (ii) EMIs cannot provide overdrafts, documentary credits (LC), or trade-loan facilities; (iii) safeguarded client funds of pure EMIs are not covered by the Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution (FGDR) unless the entity holds a full banking licence (e.g., Revolut Bank UAB passported), although segregation requirements mitigate; (iv) some EMIs impose stricter sectoral filters on 'manufacturing' or dual-use-adjacent technology — a smart-meter IoT business must be prepared to supply EORI, HS-6 classification (9028.30 for electricity meters, 9030 for telemetry), and end-user attestations. Recommended architecture: EMI for day-to-day collections/disbursements and multi-currency balances, with a Tier-1 bank as the anchor for capital escrow, LC issuance, and FX/derivative lines. If the Tier-1 bank refuses an account on risk grounds, the company may exercise the right to a basic account under Article L312-1 CMF by requesting the Banque de France to designate an institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for share-capital injection from a non-resident parent: (1) FDI screening — if the parent is non-EU/EEA and the smart-meter activity touches the energy-infrastructure perimeter of Article L151-3 CMF (e.g., grid-network interoperability, cybersecurity functionality of the meter's telemetry module, or long-term supply to distribution system operators), file a prior authorization request with the DG Trésor before settlement; if outside the perimeter, retain a legal memo confirming exemption; (2) settlement via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's bank to the French receiving bank (BNP Paribas or Société Générale) with Field 70 citing 'APPORT_EN_CAPITAL_SOCIAL_2025_AUGMENTATION_DE_CAPITAL' and the BOP category for equity capital; include the parent's resolution and the subscription list for the bank's KYC file; (3) for an existing SARL, the funds must be deposited in a bank-blocked 'compte séquestre' until the gérant signs the declaration of subscription and payment and the Greffe updates the Kbis; alternatively a notary or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations may act as depositary; (4) report the direct investment to the Banque de France within 20 days following the month-end if the transaction value exceeds €1,000,000 (Form 'Investissements directs' via the bank's filière or direct filing); every flow above €12,500 is captured in the monthly BOP declaration; (5) verify origin of funds — the remitting bank must provide a correspondent reference and, where the amount is disproportionate to the parent's equity, the beneficiary bank will request audited accounts or a certified treasury statement; (6) FX conversion, if the parent remits USD or CNY, is a spot transaction against the ECB reference rate plus margin and is unrestricted; (7) after receipt, update the RCS and the share register through the Guichet Unique, and where capital introduces in-kind contributions (e.g., patents, prototypes), obtain a commissaire aux apports valuation before the notarial deed is completed.For operational procurement and project-milestone payments: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments in EUR/USD/CNY with Field 70 purpose-code referencing (merchandise purchases, components, or services) — 48–72-hour processing on the SWIFT network; (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and Instant SCT for EU-based suppliers and subcontractors settling in EUR (0–10 seconds for instant, ideal for urgent sub-assembly orders); (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 issued by the French anchor bank in favor of suppliers for raw-material or sub-assembly purchases, commonly confirmed by a bank in the supplier's jurisdiction — for milestone-based smart-meter projects, use a revolving LC in tranches triggered by warehouse inspection reports, packing lists, and EN 50470 / MID 2014/32/EU metrology certificates; (iv) Documentary collections under URC 522 for low-risk repeat shipments with bill of lading as the controlling document; (v) Escrowed milestone payments — use an escrow agent bank instruction letter for IoT deployment projects (e.g., 30% prepayment, 40% at DAP incoterms delivery, 20% at commissioning, 10% at the end of the 12-month performance guarantee); (vi) For export sales to French grid operators or international utilities, the SARL may act as beneficiary under an export LC, and the French bank's forfaiting desk can discount the LC proceeds to shorten receivables days; (vii) All outward wires require UN/EU/OFAC screening of the beneficiary and verification of the high-risk item list — the company should maintain a compliance pack per shipment (invoice, packing list, product datasheet, HS-6 classification, end-user certificate) to avoid payment holds; (viii) Statutory reporting of outbound payments is captured by the bank at source in the filière déléguée; single transactions above €1,000,000 may trigger a request for the underlying contract to close the BOP file.Plain-vanilla FX Forwards (change à terme ferme) on EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, EUR/GBP — with documented underlying commercial exposure evidenced by invoices, purchase orders, or LC values to satisfy the MiFID II 'actual or effective' hedging requirement for non-financial counterparties; tenors up to 18 months are standard for a hardware trade cycle.FX Swaps (swap de change) for short-date treasury liquidity management — simultaneous spot and forward legs to cover temporary currency gaps without creating an open speculative position.Cross-currency swaps (swaps de devises, XCCY) for long-term project receivables in USD or CNY matched against EUR funding — permissible for structured smart-meter export contracts with defined milestone cash flows and an interest profile.Vanilla FX Options (call/put options on EUR against USD and CNY) — purchased-premium strategies or zero-cost collars (achat de tunnel) to cap conversion downside in export tenders while retaining upside; covered option-writing by corporates is strictly limited and requires explicit bank approval.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on EUR/CNY (offshore CNH referencing the CFETS onshore fix) — permitted where the onshore CNY market is unavailable for hedging a documented cross-border receivable; settlement in EUR against the CFETS 9:15 am Beijing fix with full documentation of the physical trade flow.Trade-finance-package hedges — the French bank locks an FX forward together with the discounting rate of an export LC, providing a two-way price for dual-currency milestone LCs and eliminating basis risk between the LC proceeds and the EUR operating account.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium-High for operational treasury, Low for capital escrow. ACPR-licensed or EU-passported EMIs (Qonto, Shine, Wise Europe, Revolut Bank UAB under EU passporting into France) provide rapid remote onboarding, SEPA and SWIFT settlement, multi-currency IBANs (EUR, USD, GBP), and strong API integration for trade flows of a smart-meter manufacturer. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs cannot hold the SARL's initial share capital as a blocked escrow account because the certificat de dépôt des fonds required by Article L223-7 of the French Commercial Code for Greffe registration must be issued by a credit institution or a notary — a pure EMI certificate is not accepted by the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés; (ii) EMIs cannot provide overdrafts, documentary credits (LC), or trade-loan facilities; (iii) safeguarded client funds of pure EMIs are not covered by the Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution (FGDR) unless the entity holds a full banking licence (e.g., Revolut Bank UAB passported), although segregation requirements mitigate; (iv) some EMIs impose stricter sectoral filters on 'manufacturing' or dual-use-adjacent technology — a smart-meter IoT business must be prepared to supply EORI, HS-6 classification (9028.30 for electricity meters, 9030 for telemetry), and end-user attestations. Recommended architecture: EMI for day-to-day collections/disbursements and multi-currency balances, with a Tier-1 bank as the anchor for capital escrow, LC issuance, and FX/derivative lines. If the Tier-1 bank refuses an account on risk grounds, the company may exercise the right to a basic account under Article L312-1 CMF by requesting the Banque de France to designate an institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50 million annual turnover excluding tax for the French entity, or when the SARL directly or indirectly controls, or is controlled by, a foreign enterprise; EU master/local file also required where the group has consolidated turnover of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not administer a Gulf-style ESR code, but applies equivalent anti-abuse and substance doctrines through CGI Articles 209-B and 155-A and LPF Article L64. A French SARL must demonstrate genuine economic substance – premises, employees, management decisions, local books and records – in order to preserve treaty benefits and avoid recharacterisation of its operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / deliberate false return

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty under CGI Article 1741: up to EUR 500,000 fine (or 50% of the concealed tax amount, whichever is higher) and 5 years imprisonment. Civil/administrative re-assessment may apply an 80% penalty for abuse of law under CGI Article 1729(b), plus late-payment interest at 0.5% per month.

Director personal liability: The gérant or de facto director is jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, penalties and interest; criminal conviction triggers travel bans, disqualification, attachment of personal assets, and orders prohibiting exit from national territory.
Transfer pricing failure / TP documentation breach

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may reassess taxable income at arm's length; 40% penalty on unjustified benefit transfer plus 0.5% monthly interest; separate EUR 15,000 fine per French entity for non-communication of TP documentation under LPF Article L13-AA.

Director personal liability: A director who signs false invoices or deliberately approves fabricated intercompany agreements is exposed to criminal fraud liability and may be separately assessed for the tax due; management fault may lead to personal contribution toward the corporate deficit in insolvency proceedings (action en comblement de passif).
Shell company / artful abuse of law

Statutory sanction: Application of LPF Article L64: the transaction is set aside and an 80% penalty is imposed, plus interest; VAT registration may be terminated; sales may be re-qualified into the source state leading to reverse-charge penalties.

Director personal liability: French insolvency law permits piercing the veil where there is confusion of patrimonies or fictitious legal acts, resulting in personal liability and a travel ban; a nominee director faces up to EUR 5,000 fine and disqualification from acting as a manager.
AML / concealed beneficial ownership

Statutory sanction: Beneficial-owner declaration breach: EUR 5,000 fine for natural persons and EUR 25,000 for companies under Article L561-2-1 of the Monetary and Financial Code; money laundering under Article 324-1 of the Penal Code carries up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine, or 10 years and EUR 750,000 where aggravated.

Director personal liability: A director who intentionally conceals beneficial ownership, hides funds, or facilitates laundering of project payments faces criminal prosecution, asset seizure, international travel restrictions, and disqualification from managing any commercial company for up to 15 years.
