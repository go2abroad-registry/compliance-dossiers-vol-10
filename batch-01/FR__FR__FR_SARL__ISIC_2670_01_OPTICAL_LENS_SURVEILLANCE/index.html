Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum share capital applies to a SARL; the amount is freely fixed in the Articles of Association. However, cash contributions must be paid up to at least one-fifth (20%) upon subscription (Article L.223-7 C. com.), with the balance payable within five years. The paid-up portion must be deposited in a blocked account opened in the name of the company in formation with a bank lawfully established in France or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the funds are released only upon presentation of the K-bis extract proving RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety and structural clearance: technical control by an approved inspection body (e.g. APAVE, Bureau Veritas, SOCOTEC) delivering a fire-safety certificate and structural conformity attestation for the industrial building; compliance with the building and occupation permits (permis de construire / autorisation d'exploitation); fire-extinguishing equipment per the Code du travail; occupational clearance: DREETS inspection of working conditions, electrical installation conformity report, CE-marking of machinery with periodic mandatory inspections, safety training records, and the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques as the foundation of the workplace health and safety program.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum share capital applies to a SARL; the amount is freely fixed in the Articles of Association. However, cash contributions must be paid up to at least one-fifth (20%) upon subscription (Article L.223-7 C. com.), with the balance payable within five years. The paid-up portion must be deposited in a blocked account opened in the name of the company in formation with a bank lawfully established in France or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the funds are released only upon presentation of the K-bis extract proving RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and, for non-established businesses, the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). Official portal: impots.gouv.fr. Business incorporation and registration filings are centralized through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), with SIREN/SIRET information transmitted to the tax administration.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered through the Guichet unique before commencement of business. The process generally requires articles of association, registered-office evidence, beneficial-owner information, identity and authority documents for managers, capital-deposit evidence, and the activity declaration. The Guichet unique transmits the filing to the Registre national des entreprises and the relevant tax authorities. The entity receives a SIREN identifier and establishment-level SIRET identifier. The French VAT identification number is issued by the competent SIE and normally consists of the prefix FR, a two-digit control key, and the nine-digit SIREN number. A foreign entity carrying on taxable transactions in France must apply to the SIEE or otherwise register through the applicable tax channel. An EU-established business generally does not need a French fiscal representative; a business established outside the EU generally must appoint a French fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement removes that requirement. VAT registration is required where the business makes taxable supplies, imports goods, performs or receives reverse-charge transactions requiring reporting, or elects registration to recover input VAT. Registration does not replace customs registration: an EORI number is required for customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A standard SARL is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying SARL formed less than five years earlier may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code, subject to statutory conditions including size, ownership, activity, and revenue requirements; the election is time-limited and generally irrevocable once exercised. A qualifying family SARL may elect income-tax treatment under the family-company regime, subject to continuing eligibility. These are statutory French tax elections and not U.S.-style check-the-box elections. France has no general entity-classification check-the-box regime and no general election that converts a SARL into a fiscally transparent entity outside the statutory regimes. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive apply by operation of law; they are not elective classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the French domestic VAT franchise regime applicable principally to supplies of goods, the ordinary prior-year turnover threshold is EUR 85,000, with a current-year tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500, subject to the statutory rules and applicable year-specific legislation. A business operating under the franchise in principle does not charge VAT and cannot recover input VAT. The thresholds do not eliminate registration or reporting obligations for transactions that independently require a VAT number, including relevant imports, intra-EU acquisitions, reverse-charge transactions, and transactions for which the business elects ordinary VAT treatment. A French manufacturer exporting optical lenses or camera modules commonly registers for VAT to support input-tax recovery and transaction reporting, even where export supplies are exempt with credit.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

French exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the EU are exempt from French VAT with credit, functionally equivalent to zero-rating, where the statutory export conditions are met. The goods must leave the EU customs territory and the seller or an authorized intermediary must be able to substantiate the export. The file should include the commercial invoice, transport evidence, customs export declaration, export MRN, and electronic confirmation of exit such as the ECS exit result or equivalent customs evidence. The exemption is generally reported on the French VAT return and does not apply merely because the customer is established outside France. Intra-EU supplies are treated under the separate rules for exempt intra-Community supplies and require valid customer VAT identification and proof of dispatch to another Member State. B2B services are generally located where the business customer is established; services supplied to a customer outside the EU may therefore be outside the scope of French VAT rather than technically zero-rated. Specific use-and-enjoyment, immovable-property, transport, event, and consumer-service rules must be reviewed separately. Export VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices, proper accounting, timely VAT returns, and evidence that input VAT relates to taxable or exempt-with-credit activities.

  • Commercial export invoice identifying the French exporter, foreign customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and VAT exemption wording
  • Customs export declaration submitted through the applicable electronic customs system
  • Export Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Electronic customs exit confirmation, including ECS exit result or equivalent proof of exit
  • Packing list and transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or courier delivery record
  • Evidence linking the goods, invoice, customs declaration, and transport record, including serial numbers where commercially applicable
  • Proof of delivery or receipt outside the EU where available
  • Import customs or destination-country clearance evidence where available
  • Bank payment or other commercial settlement evidence where relevant; an inward-remittance certificate is not generally a French statutory condition for export VAT exemption
  • Foreign customer purchase order, sales contract, and correspondence supporting the commercial transaction where required for audit substantiation

France does not operate a universal real-time invoice-clearance system comparable to Italy. Domestic B2B e-invoicing will be transmitted through an approved private electronic-invoicing platform (Plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire, or PDP), with the public portal used for central data collection and reporting functions. The statutory timetable provides mandatory receipt of e-invoices for all businesses established in France from 1 September 2026; mandatory issuance applies from 1 September 2026 to large and medium-sized businesses and VAT groups, and from 1 September 2027 to small and micro-enterprises. The timetable is subject to applicable legislative amendments. E-reporting of transaction and payment data applies to in-scope transactions outside domestic B2B e-invoicing. Public-sector invoices continue to use Chorus Pro. Invoices must contain the statutory French VAT particulars, and electronic records must preserve authenticity, integrity, legibility, auditability, and required retention periods. There is no blanket requirement for a digital tax stamp on ordinary commercial invoices and no general requirement for real-time pre-clearance before issuance.

France has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. Export documentation must nevertheless be retained for the statutory accounting and tax-record retention periods and produced during a tax or customs audit. If the exporter cannot prove that goods left the EU, the tax administration may deny the VAT exemption and assess French VAT, normally at the applicable domestic rate, together with late-payment interest. A deliberate or abusive claim may trigger a 40% penalty for deliberate breach, an 80% penalty for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law, and in serious cases criminal tax-fraud proceedings. Ordinary late or inaccurate VAT filings may attract a 10% penalty, increased penalties for aggravated non-compliance, and late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month, subject to statutory adjustments. Customs violations may result in seizure, forfeiture, administrative fines, recovery of customs duties and import VAT, and criminal sanctions under the French Customs Code. Input VAT refunds may be denied or clawed back where invoices are invalid, transactions are fictitious, the goods did not qualify for export exemption, or the claimed input tax is not attributable to an eligible business activity.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Capital movements are fully liberalized within the EU/EEA under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier). Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject to standard anti-money laundering (AML) verification and tax compliance (e.g., withholding tax on dividends if applicable). No prior authorization is required for cross-border transfers, but statistical reporting to the Banque de France is mandatory for transactions exceeding €15,000 (or €50,000 for certain categories) under the balance-of-payments reporting regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists in France. Exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts (comptes en devises) or convert to EUR at their discretion. However, all export receipts must be reported to the Banque de France for statistical purposes within the prescribed deadlines (typically monthly or quarterly, depending on transaction volume). There is no statutory conversion ratio; the exporter is free to hold or convert. For transactions exceeding €50,000, a declaration via the Banque de France's 'Règlementation des changes' portal is required.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for foreign exchange policy and balance-of-payments reporting

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. France recognizes Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the PSD2 directive, and licensed EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Shine, or cross-border EMIs like Revolut Business) can provide multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment services. However, for a manufacturing entity with significant trade flows and LC requirements, traditional banks remain more robust. EMIs may lack full trade finance capabilities (e.g., issuance of commercial letters of credit) and may impose lower transaction limits. Legal constraints: EMIs must be authorized by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) and are subject to strict safeguarding requirements. They cannot provide credit or hold client funds for extended periods. For high-value optical lens exports, a hybrid approach (EMI for operational liquidity, traditional bank for LC) is viable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound capital injections (e.g., shareholder loans or equity contributions) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with appropriate purpose codes. For equity contributions, use purpose code 'P01' (Direct Investment) or 'P02' (Capital Contribution) as per the Banque de France's balance-of-payments classification. For shareholder loans, use 'P03' (Financial Loan). The receiving bank will require a copy of the board resolution and the share subscription agreement. Funds must be credited to the SARL's capital escrow account or operational account. Verification: The bank will perform a source-of-funds check and may request the underlying contract. For amounts exceeding €50,000, the SARL must file a declaration with the Banque de France via the 'Régime de déclaration des transferts de fonds' within 20 business days.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For export of precision optical lenses, the primary rails are SWIFT MT103 (for direct wire transfers) and Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) via SWIFT MT700/MT707. For LC transactions, the SARL's bank will act as the advising or confirming bank, and the importer's bank will issue the LC. The SARL must present compliant documents (commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin) to receive payment. For open account terms, MT103 is used with payment terms (e.g., 30/60 days). Additionally, the SARL may use the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA) for EUR-denominated transactions within the EU, but for non-EU counterparties, SWIFT is mandatory. The bank will require a trade finance facility for LC confirmations, which may involve additional due diligence.Forward contracts (plain vanilla FX forwards) for EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, or other major currencies, with tenors up to 12 months, available from Tier-1 banks.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for currencies with restricted convertibility (e.g., certain emerging market currencies) – permissible but subject to bank's internal policies and regulatory approval.Currency swaps (cross-currency interest rate swaps) for long-term hedging of both principal and interest, typically used for larger exposures.FX options (European style) for flexible hedging, though these are considered more complex and may require a higher credit rating.
