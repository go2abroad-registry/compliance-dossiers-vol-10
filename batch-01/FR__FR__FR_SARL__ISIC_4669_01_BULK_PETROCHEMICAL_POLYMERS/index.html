Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital (SARL may be formed with EUR 1). At least one-fifth (20%) of cash quotas must be paid up at subscription and the balance within five years. All cash contributions must be deposited to a blocked escrow account with a licensed French bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and remain frozen until the Kbis is issued. In-kind contributions exceeding EUR 30,000 in value or more than half of the share capital require a statutory contribution auditor (commissaire aux apports).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: maintenance of a registre de sécurité, portable extinguishers, emergency exits, and a fire safety visit by the municipal commission de sécurité if the premises qualify as accessibles au public; electrical installations verified per Code du travail Article R.4224-17 with a conformity certificate; structural verification of storage racking and floors for the mechanical load of bagged polymer granules; occupational health clearance through the company's service de prévention et de santé au travail, including an initial medical visit for personnel and the display of emergency instructions.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital (SARL may be formed with EUR 1). At least one-fifth (20%) of cash quotas must be paid up at subscription and the balance within five years. All cash contributions must be deposited to a blocked escrow account with a licensed French bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and remain frozen until the Kbis is issued. In-kind contributions exceeding EUR 30,000 in value or more than half of the share capital require a statutory contribution auditor (commissaire aux apports).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) — Portal: impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity without a French permanent establishment: appoint an accredited VAT tax representative (unless established in the EU), submit the VAT registration application (cerfa n° 13703) to the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) of the tax representative's local office, and obtain a SIRET/SIREN number and the French intra-Community VAT number. For a domestically established SARL: file incorporation with the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE), receive a Kbis extract and SIREN/SIRET, and obtain the VAT number automatically from the DGFIP. In cross-border cases, the VAT number is requested via form n° 1190 through the DGFIP's Service de la TVA; an online application and digital filing are mandatory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL may elect to be treated as fiscally transparent (option pour le régime des sociétés de personnes / impôt sur le revenu) for a five-year period if it meets the statutory conditions, including not exceeding 50 shareholders and not being listed. This election is available under Article 239 bis AA and 239 bis AB of the French General Tax Code. France does not operate a pure check-the-box election; separate anti-hybrid rules under ATAD require the neutralization of mismatches arising from hybrid entities or instruments. The default for a commercial SARL is entity-level corporate income tax, and no other broad entity classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-established B2B suppliers (first euro of taxable supply in France triggers registration); domestic small-business franchise threshold: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for goods; EU distance-selling threshold for B2C cross-border trade: EUR 10,000 (for remote sales from another EU state).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of goods physically exported outside the European Union are zero-rated for French VAT under Article 262-0 of the General Tax Code, provided the goods are ground out of France within 3 months of despatch and the exporter retains full documentary proof of exportation. The ex-warehouse and final export shipment are supported by the customs export declaration. Offshore B2B services supplied to a non-EU client are deemed to be supplied in the client's country under the place-of-supply rule (Article 259-1 CGI) and therefore fall outside the scope of French VAT; when required, the 0% rate or exemption is claimed on the VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / DAU)
  • Bill of Lading (Connaissement)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Avis de crédit / Certificat de rapatriement)

France mandates structured e-invoicing for B2B transactions under the 2024/2026 roadmap (Article 289 CGI and implementing decree 2022-1298). E-invoices must be transmitted via the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or an accredited partner platform (PDP) using structured data formats (UBL, Factur-X) through approved APIs. Cross-currency transactions and exports require e-reporting to the DGFIP. No traditional physical digital stamp is used; however, export goods must be electronically cleared via the DELTA-X customs declaration system operating within the Union Customs Code framework.

Unsubstantiated or fraudulent export zero-rating results in the immediate collection of French VAT at 20%, plus late-payment interest (0.20% per month), and penalties. Negligent error: 40% of the tax due; serious misconduct: 80%; intentional fraud: 100%. The tax authority may claw back VAT for up to six years (ten years for fraud). Criminal penalties under Article 1741 CGI may be imposed for deliberate falsification of export evidence, including imprisonment and monetary fines. Customs and exchange-control offices also review the inward remittance certificate; failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days triggers customs penalties and potential suspension of export zero-rating privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies the EU Treaty free movement of capital regime (Article 63 TFEU) and therefore maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for euro-denominated and major foreign-currency operations. There is no general exchange-control licensing requirement for remittances of profit, dividends, or trade-related payments. Profit remittance is subject to French corporate formalities rather than FX control: the SARL must document distributable reserves through approved annual accounts and a shareholder capital resolution; French withholding tax may apply to non-resident shareholders unless reduced by an applicable double-tax treaty, by the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive in an intra-EU context, or through other domestic exemptions. For non-EU investments, prior French foreign-investment authorisation is triggered only for listed sensitive or national-security sectors; wholesale petrochemical polymer distribution is not ordinarily listed, but the bank will still verify legal substance, source of funds, and the economic purpose of each inbound transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in France. As a Eurozone member, France permits exporters to receive and retain foreign-currency proceeds, to hold them in multicurrency accounts, or to convert them at market rates without central bank approval. Export-related cross-border financial flows must nevertheless be supported by underlying commercial documentation and may be subject to Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting requirements. All payments must include accurate originator and beneficiary data under Regulation (EU) 2015/847, and the sanctioned party screening obligations of the applicable French and EU regulations remain applicable.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank; foreign-financial-relations oversight and balance-of-payments statistical coordination are also exercised by the Direction Générale du Trésor and the Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for offshore digital electronic money institutions. Under the EU PSD2 and AML/CFT framework, a French ACPR-licensed electronic money institution may offer payment accounts and payment initiation services through the Single Euro Payments Area passporting regime, but such providers generally cannot offer documentary credits, supply-chain finance guarantees, or full SWIFT trade-clearing rails that are expected for bulk petrochemical polymer settlements. An offshore non-EU EMI would require separate registration or a local branch/model in France and is likely to be rejected as a primary treasury partner because of regulatory, sanctions, and correspondent-banking constraints. The recommended structure is a top-tier French or EU commercial bank as principal treasury counterparty, optionally complemented by a narrow-purpose ACPR-registered payment institution for low-value collection flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing must be executed through SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers or MT202 bank-to-bank transfers, depending on whether the beneficiary is the SARL directly or an intermediary nostro account. The payment originator must include under Regulation (EU) 2015/847 the full name, address, account number, and identifier of the originator; the SARL as beneficiary must provide its OECD Legal Entity Identifier or Kbis registration data and a clear purpose description. For a shareholder capital contribution, the corporate documentation should include a notarised or signed capital-increase deliberation, proof of deposit of funds with the bank where required, and registration updates at the RCS if the registered capital is amended. For a shareholder current-account advance, the governing resolution should authorise the advance and define its interest, maturity, and repayment terms. The bank will require a transfer-purpose code from its digital banking menu indicating 'capital contribution', 'shareholder advance/current account', or 'trade payment', and the SARL must be prepared to submit the underlying contract or board resolution for compliance review. French foreign direct investment authorisation is generally not required for petrochemical polymer wholesale trading, but a non-EU investor should nonetheless conduct a pre-filing legal check under Article R.151-2 of the Monetary and Financial Code to confirm the activity does not fall within a sensitive sector. Any statistical balance-of-payments declaration required by Banque de France must be made through the receiving intermediary bank.Outbound settlements should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 for direct customer trades, MT202 for bank-level nostro disbursements, and commercial documentary credits for structured supply-chain settlement. The standard rail for polymer imports is an irrevocable letter of credit issued via SWIFT MT700, amended through MT707 as necessary, and settled at sight or by negotiation/acceptance upon presentation of compliant documents. Required documentary packages generally include a signed commercial invoice, full-set clean-on-board bills of lading issued to the order of the bank or the SARL, packing list, certificate of origin, inspection certificate, and insurance certificate. For euro-denominated low-value payments, the SEPA Credit Transfer rail is available; for high-value euro urgency, TARGET2 is the definitive clearing system.
