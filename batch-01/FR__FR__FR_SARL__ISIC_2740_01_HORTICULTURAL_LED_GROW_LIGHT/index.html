Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (commercial court registry), accessed through Infogreffe or the relevant local commercial court registry; Kbis extract is the official corporate identity certificate.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée) and R.223-1 to R.223-31; Civil Code Article 1846-1; Law No. 2019-486 (PACTE) abolishing the minimum capital threshold.

Formation / constitutional filings

Dossier unique d'immatriculation containing the signed articles of association (statuts) and the official M0 form, filed with the Greffe via Infogreffe; registration is evidenced by the Kbis extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital. Cash contributions must be subscribed in full and paid up to at least 20% at incorporation or such higher fraction as required by the articles; funds may be deposited with a French bank or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and the depositary attestation must be filed. Contributions in kind must be fully paid up and assessed by a statutory auditor unless a derogation applies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification requires an inspection by the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) and compliance with ERP or ICPE fire codes depending on building occupancy; production premises must have electrical installation certificates, grounding checks, and structural load clearance for storage and assembly areas; the employer must prepare the document unique d'évaluation des risques (DUER) and arrange occupational medical surveillance for workers exposed to soldering fumes, noise, and electrical hazards.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital. Cash contributions must be subscribed in full and paid up to at least 20% at incorporation or such higher fraction as required by the articles; funds may be deposited with a French bank or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and the depositary attestation must be filed. Contributions in kind must be fully paid up and assessed by a statutory auditor unless a derogation applies.

Direction Régionale de l'Environnement, de l'Aménagement et du Logement (DREAL) and Direction Régionale de l'Économie, de l'Emploi, du Travail et des Solidarités (DREETS).

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the impots.gouv.fr portal; business formation and tax-ID issuance coordinated through the Guichet Unique des formalités d'entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), operated under the CCI/CMA network for CFE functions.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the French SARL online via the Guichet Unique portal, filing the M0 form and required statutes, to obtain SIREN, SIRET (with NIC), APE/NAF code 2740Z, and the statutory Kbis extract. Step 2: INSEE automatically transmits the registration to the DGFiP, which assigns the French EU VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire formatted FR + 11 digits). Step 3: For non-EU foreign entities that are not incorporated in France but perform taxable French supplies: file DGFiP Form No. 15537, appoint a DGFiP-approved fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) under Article 289 A of the CGI, and obtain a direct foreign-operator VAT number from the DGFiP; no SIRET is strictly required for such registration, but a French customs ID may be needed for imports. Step 4: Register for the corporate income tax regime (Impôt sur les Sociétés) via the tax office (SIE) and for the CET (CFE/CVAE transitional rules) where applicable.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; a commercial manufacturing SARL is subject to French corporate income tax (IS) at a 25% standard reduced to 15% on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs; no pass-through default. Territorial scope: French-resident entity taxed on French and foreign-source income with full liability; no tax-exempt zone status (no derogatory French free-zone applicable to a standard SARL). — Step 1: Incorporate the French SARL online via the Guichet Unique portal, filing the M0 form and required statutes, to obtain SIREN, SIRET (with NIC), APE/NAF code 2740Z, and the statutory Kbis extract. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a commercial manufacturing SARL is subject to French corporate income tax (IS) at a 25% standard reduced to 15% on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs; no pass-through default. Territorial scope: French-resident entity taxed on French and foreign-source income with full liability; no tax-exempt zone status (no derogatory French free-zone applicable to a standard SARL).

Available election forms

Option for the transparent/partnership regime (impôt sur le revenu) under Article 239 bis AA CGI for a maximum of 5 fiscal years, available only to SMEs with fewer than 50 employees and annual turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million; this is the only French check-the-box-like election for SARLs (no true US-style check-the-box). Hybrid mismatch adjustments under Articles 209-0 B and 212 bis C CGI (ATAD implementation) require French characterization to mirror EU classification rules and may override entity elections where mismatches cause double non-taxation; no other statutory entity-taxation elections apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A French-established SARL must register for VAT upon formation regardless of turnover volume. For non-established EU suppliers: mandatory local VAT registration once intra-EU distance sales exceed EUR 10,000/year (OMP regime) or for all B2B supplies at any value. For non-EU suppliers: no VAT exemption threshold applies; registration is required from the first taxable supply in France. The domestic 'franchise en base de TVA' threshold (EUR 85,000 for goods; EUR 37,500 for services, updated for 2023-2025) is not applicable to a commercial manufacturing SARL because an invoice-exempt franchise is incompatible with export and intra-community zero-rating and input VAT claims on raw materials; the SARL must be a fully registered VAT operator.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported to destinations outside the European Union are zero-rated (exonération à droit à déduction) under Article 262-I-1° CGI, provided the export is physically proven within three months of the date of the supply by a customs export declaration (DAU) or equivalent, and the goods leave the EU. Intra-EU supplies of the LED grow-light fixtures to a taxable person holding a valid French or other EU VAT number may be zero-rated under Article 262 ter CGI, subject to the customer's VAT ID, the intra-EU transport evidence, and the filing of the French Recapitulative Statement (DEB/DES). Eligibility is lost if the export proof is not obtained by the 3-month deadline, in which case French VAT at 20% becomes payable on the invoice and must be remitted to the tax authority.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU - Document Administratif Unique) lodged via DELTA G or ECS system, providing the MRN (Movement Reference Number) as the primary export proof
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill (AWB), CMR transport document, or multimodal transport contract evidencing dispatch outside the EU (or intra-EU transport for zero-rating under Art. 262 ter CGI)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Foreign Inward Bank Transfer Confirmation (Swift MT103 / Airwallex settlement receipt) evidencing FX proceeds, retained prudentially for the 180-day repatriation benchmark and for transfer pricing and customs audit defense
  • Commercial Invoice with the zero-rating reference (Article 262-I-1° or 262 ter CGI) and delivery terms (Incoterms), plus Export Packing List and signed Delivery Receipt / POD for cross-border traceability

France has adopted a mandatory national e-invoicing and e-reporting reform (Ordonnance 2021-1190 and Decree 2022-1299). Phased deadlines: large and mid-size enterprises are required to receive and issue structured electronic invoices (Factur-X, UBL, or CII) from 1 September 2026, with small enterprises including SARLs compliant from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be exchanged through the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation) or a DGFiP-registered partner dematerialization platform (PDP). E-reporting (données de transaction et de paiement) is mandatory for B2B, B2C, and cross-border supplies, transmitted to DGFiP via the PPF in near-real-time; full pre-clearance is not yet applied but the reform permits DGFiP digital control of invoice data. No physical digital stamp is required, but a validated transmission receipt (Ack) is the compliance record.

The DGFiP may reassess VAT within the standard three-year statutory limitation period from the end of the filing year (Article L.169 Livre des Procédures Fiscales); the period is extended to six years for omitted/undisclosed operations and to ten years where fraudulent intent or abusive behaviors (manœuvres frauduleuses) are proven. Where export zero-rating is claimed without valid proof within three months, the VAT is immediately clawed back at 20% plus monthly late interest of 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI); deliberate under-declaration or misstatement attracts a 40% penalty (manquement délibéré), rising to 80% for abusive fraud, plus possible criminal penalties. On intra-EU zero-rating, missing customer VAT ID, absent transport evidence, or late/incorrect DEB filings result in VAT reassessment and the same penalty scale. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days without documented commercial justification may also trigger transfer-pricing scrutiny under Article 57 CGI and French balance-of-payments reporting penalties for statistical breaches related to FX proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France participates in the euro area; the euro is fully and freely convertible under Article 63 TFEU. Capital account and current account transactions with non-EU counterparties are liberalized; no prior exchange-control authorization is required for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or capital repatriation, subject to EU financial sanctions screening and French AML notification obligations. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to corporate tax documentation, a 25% withholding tax on dividends paid to non-treaty jurisdictions (reduced to 10% under the France-China DTT), and standard bank documentary checks; all payments may be made without currency conversion restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no holding timeline for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts held with a Banque de France-approved credit institution, or freely converted into euro. However, for balance-of-payments statistics, exporters must submit a monthly declaration (bulletin de déclaration) to the Banque de France covering cross-border transactions exceeding the applicable regulatory threshold, with the statement deadline falling on the 15th working day of the following month; precise origin and destination data must be retained for 10 years.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for payment execution, multi-currency treasury, and FX conversion; Medium for the main operating bank relationship. Airwallex, operating under an EU-recognised EMI or passporting arrangement with ACPR notification, can provide a French IBAN, SWIFT/SEPA routing, and local collection accounts for cross-border trade settlements. Legal constraints: an EMI may not grant credit, must safeguard client funds in low-risk assets, cannot provide capital escrow attestation required for French SARL funding, and must be registered or notified with the ACPR before servicing French-resident clients; full licensed banking services remain conclusively provided by credit institutions.

Defense evidence: Executed commercial contracts, purchase orders, pro-forma invoices, bill of lading/CMR documentation, customs declaration (ENS/IM9), Incoterms confirmation, forecast cash-flow statement, and a detailed business plan demonstrating the horticultural LED grow-light export cycle.

Third-party or split payments from non-contractual accounts

Root cause: Third-party or split payments from non-contractual accounts

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires an SWIFT MT103 or SEPA Credit Transfer denominated in EUR to the French beneficiary account (FR76 IBAN) at a licensed French credit institution. The remittance must carry purpose code CAP or EQUI, and in field 70/72 a plain statement 'Equity capital contribution to FR_SARL'; the ultimate originator and beneficiary must be fully identified. For non-EU shareholder injection, the French entity must file an inward FDI statistical declaration with the Banque de France within 20 days of the transfer if the transaction is above the regulatory threshold; a bank capital-attestation certificate (attestation de dépôt de capital) is prepared for registration purposes only if formation capital is being deposited.Trade settlements flow primarily over SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA counterparties, and SEPA SCT (ISO 20022) instruments for intra-EU payments. Documentary commercial letters of credit under UCP 600 are the preferred secured rail for horticultural LED fixture exports, with receiving banks examining bills of lading, commercial invoices, packing lists, and certificates of origin. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard rate for FY2025 on taxable profits of French-resident sociétés; a reduced 15% rate applies to the first EUR 42,500 of profit for qualifying small and medium-sized enterprises).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under domestic law (Article 119 bis-2 CGI) on dividends distributed by a French SARL to non-resident corporate shareholders. The levy is applied at the moment the dividend is declared payable, unless the recipient simultaneously invokes the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or a double-tax treaty. Distributions to resident corporate shareholders are exempt from withholding under the parent-subsidiary participation exemption.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Master File and Local File documentation under Article L.13 AA of the French Tax Procedures Book (LPF) is mandatory where the French entity or the French group head achieves annual turnover or gross total assets of at least EUR 400,000,000. Simplified documentation obligations may apply between EUR 50,000,000 and EUR 400,000,000; enterprises with turnover and gross assets below EUR 50,000,000 are exempt from the formal transfer-pricing file but must still be able to demonstrate the arm's-length character of intra-group transactions on audit. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000, with the CbCR return filed via the DGFiP and exchanged automatically. The obligation extends to non-resident-owned French entities that control or are controlled by a group meeting the threshold.

Economic substance scope: France is not an offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction, but a robust economic substance doctrine applies through the general anti-abuse rule known as 'abus de droit' (Article L.64 LPF), the fiction of 'société écran' (letterbox company), and Article 57 CGI on profit shifting to related enterprises. A French SARL that lacks staff, premises, managerial decision-making, real commercial activity, and risk-taking for its LED grow-light manufacturing and distribution may be disregarded, with its profits re-attributed to the real foreign beneficiary. The EU ATAD rules (interest limitation, CFC regime, exit taxation, and GAAR) and the DAC6 mandatory disclosure regime further penalise artificial arrangements lacking genuine substance. The CFC regime under Article 209 B CGI can re-attract passive income of low-taxed controlled subsidiaries or foreign branches where the French company does not demonstrate real economic activity in the foreign jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI: criminal fine up to EUR 750,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; aggravated circumstances (committed in organised gang, involving accounts held outside France or shell companies) raise the penalty to EUR 2,000,000 and 7 years of imprisonment. Tax adjustments carry an 80% fraudulent-filing penalty plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers are prosecuted as principals or co-authors; penalties include imprisonment, fine, confiscation of assets, withdrawal of passports and judicial travel bans. The corporate veil can be pierced where the SARL is a sole conduit, entailing joint and several restitution of the evaded tax, social security contributions, and damages to the Treasury.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure (société écran and abuse de droit)

Statutory sanction: Under Article L.64 LPF the tax administration sets aside the entire arrangement, re-characterises the transactions at the economic owner's level, and denies all corresponding deductions; an 80% abuse-of-law penalty is imposed under Article 1729(b) CGI plus late-payment interest. The shell may be administratively struck off and its VAT registration cancelled retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors risk personal joint liability for the re-characterised tax debt, unlimited civil restitution in insolvency proceedings (action en comblement de passif), and criminal conviction for complicity in tax fraud; the court may issue travel bans and prohibit the directors from managing any commercial company.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment under Article 57 CGI with the re-allocation of profits to the French SARL; application of the normal arm's-length methods (CUP, resale price, TNMM or profit split); 40% penalty for deliberate failure to file or document related-party transactions, rising to 80% where abuse of law is proven. The related-party statement (Annex 2258-SD) is exclusionary: omission bars the deduction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or approve sham pricing documentation are personally liable for the restitution of adjusted tax and penalties; the criminal courts may impose imprisonment for complicity in tax evasion, freeze bank accounts, and order travel bans pending trial. Directors of foreign parent entities involved in the scheme may be prosecuted in absentia and subject to extradition requests.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / CbCR Violations

Statutory sanction: RBE omission is punishable by a fine up to EUR 7,500 and/or 6 months' imprisonment under the French Monetary and Financial Code; the registry may also refuse deposit of annual accounts, effectively freezing the company's legal existence. CbC non-filing exposes the group to an administrative penalty of up to EUR 100,000 and automatic exchange flagging. Laundering of tax-evasion proceeds carries 5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, increased to 10 years and EUR 750,000 for habitual or organised laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners and de facto managers are jointly criminally liable; sanctions include confiscation of corporate and personal assets, closure of bank accounts, travel bans, and prohibition orders. The corporate veil is pierced to reach directors who orchestrated layering, conversion, or transfer of dirty funds through the SARL's Swift/TT and Airwallex payment rails.
