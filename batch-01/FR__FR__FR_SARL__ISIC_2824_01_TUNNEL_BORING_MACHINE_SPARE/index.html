Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises (single business formalities portal) operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), with transmission to the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce for entry in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title III — 'Des sociétés commerciales' — Articles L. 223-1 through L. 223-43 governing Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL), together with the application of Decree No. 67-236 of 23 March 1967 (Articles 25-72) and the French Civil Code on general contract validity.

Formation / constitutional filings

Formulaire M0 (déclaration de création d'entreprise) filed electronically via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, accompanied by the executed Statuts (Articles of Association) and supporting annexes.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital is required for an SARL under French law; capital is freely set in the Statuts. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up upon subscription and the balance within five years (C. com. art. L. 223-7). Funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account or with a notary (dépôt des fonds) prior to registration, and released only upon issuance of the Kbis certificate. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up at subscription and valued by a commissaire aux apports.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) following an inspection of evacuation routes, fire zones, and extinguishing systems; structural load inspection by a qualified control body (bureau de contrôle) covering crane rails, mezzanines, and heavy machinery foundations; and occupational workplace clearance under the French Labour Code (Code du travail) covering ventilation, lighting, machinery guarding, lifting equipment periodic controls, and the registration of the plant to the CARSAT health-at-work service.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital is required for an SARL under French law; capital is freely set in the Statuts. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up upon subscription and the balance within five years (C. com. art. L. 223-7). Funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account or with a notary (dépôt des fonds) prior to registration, and released only upon issuance of the Kbis certificate. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up at subscription and valued by a commissaire aux apports.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique, Paris; online business tax portal: impots.gouv.fr; company registration front-end: Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (operated by INPI); non-resident VAT desk: DGFIP Direction des Non-Résidents, 10 rue du Centre, 93198 Noisy-le-Grand.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Submit the SARL incorporation file (statuts, identity and powers of the gérant, registered office proof) to the Guichet Unique (INPI) to obtain SIREN and SIRET numbers and the Kbis extract; issuance occurs within 4-7 business days. Step 2: For an entity established in France, the DGFIP automatically allocates the VAT identification number (FR + 11 digits) recorded in the SIRENE database; no separate application is required for domestic registration. Step 3: A foreign entity without a French establishment that makes taxable supplies in France must register directly with the DGFIP (Form Cerfa n° 13966*) and appoint a fiscal representative (Article 289 A CGI) when not resident in the EU; processing normally takes 3-8 weeks. Step 4: Where intra-EU B2B supplies exceed EUR 460,000 per year or intra-EU acquisitions exceed EUR 10,000, the entity must file the EC Sales List (recapitulative declaration) and monthly DEB/Intrastat returns. ISIC 2824 (TBM spare parts manufacturing) is not a licensed sector in France; no sector-specific tax permit is required beyond general registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL is by default a société de capitaux subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at the standard 25% rate (Article 219 CGI) on its annual profits; it is a separate legal person, a separate taxpayer and a separate VAT person (assujetti). Territorial scope per Article 209 CGI: IS is charged only on profits realized by establishments situated in France (strict territoriality); foreign permanent establishments are outside the French corporate tax net. Non-resident ownership is permitted and does not alter this classification; the SARL remains an entity-level taxpayer regardless of the residence of its shareholders. No tax-exempt zone status applies.

Available election forms

Election A - Fiscal transparency of the SARL (Article 239 bis AA CGI): a SARL exercising an industrial, commercial, artisanal or liberal activity, with fewer than 50 shareholders, shares not listed, and at least 50% of capital held by natural persons, may elect, irrevocably for a period of 5 fiscal years, to be taxed under the income-tax regime of sociétés de personnes, with profits taxed directly in the hands of the shareholders. Election B - Fiscal integration (Article 223 A CGI): a SARL holding at least 95% (directly or indirectly) of French subsidiaries may elect, with those subsidiaries, to form an integrated group neutralizing intra-group flows and offsetting group profits and losses in a single IS return. Election C - French VAT group (Article 257 C CGI): closely linked French entities may elect to form a single taxable person (assujetti unique) for VAT, eliminating VAT on internal transactions. Election D - No check-the-box regime exists in France; legal-form classification is mandatory and cannot be overridden by shareholder election. Hybrid-mismatch limitations (Articles 205 A and 212 bis A CGI, transposing ATAD) apply to interest deductibility and reverse-hybrid entities. No specific election is required for non-resident participation in a SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold for VAT registration applies to businesses established in France: any person carrying out an independent supply of goods or services (Article 256 A CGI) must register and file TVA from the first transaction; for this SARL (manufacturer of TBM spare parts) registration is unconditional. The franchise en base de TVA (Article 293 B CGI) is an exemption scheme, not a registration threshold: enterprises with prior-year turnover below EUR 91,900 (supplies of goods) or EUR 36,800 (services) may elect to issue invoices without TVA and to file on the simplified regime; eligibility is lost in the current year when turnover exceeds EUR 101,000 (goods) or EUR 39,100 (services). This SARL, being B2B and export-driven, falls under the normal (real) TVA regime with monthly or quarterly returns. Non-established foreign suppliers: B2B supplies of goods are reverse-charged to the French business customer without the supplier being required to register; distance sales of goods to French consumers benefit from the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold under the OSS special scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Non-EU exports: supplies of goods physically exported outside the European Union are exempt with the right to deduct input VAT (effective 0% rate) under Article 262-I-1 CGI (exonération à l'exportation), provided the seller holds documentary evidence that the goods left EU customs territory within 90 days from dispatch or shipment (BOI-TVA-CHAMP-30-10-30) and that the export declaration (MRN) has been lodged with DGDDI. Intra-EU dispatches: transfers of TBM spare parts to a customer in another EU member state are zero-rated under Article 262 ter CGI, subject to validity of the customer's European VAT number (VIES confirmation), physical cross-border transport of the goods, and the seller's filing of the EC Sales List and DEB/Intrastat declarations. Ancillary offshore services (warranty, installation, site commissioning outside France) are zero-rated under Articles 259, 259 A and 262-1-2 CGI where they are directly linked to exported goods or are supplied to non-resident business customers, subject to the general B2B territorial rule (services taxed at the customer's place of establishment). The goods (HSN 8430/8431, TBM spare parts) are ordinary industrial goods not subject to export controls; no sector-specific license conditions affect eligibility. Export refund of excess input VAT is repayable by the DGFIP on the normal filing schedule, and for non-established taxpayers under the 13th VAT Directive procedure. FX repatriation: French exchange control (Code monétaire et financier, Articles L151-1 et seq.) imposes no fixed statutory repatriation deadline; export proceeds must be collected within contractual payment terms and settled through authorized French banks, while the decisive French VAT deadline for export proof is the 90-day physical-export window reflected in the field below.

  • Transport document evidencing EU exit: Bill of Lading (connaissement maritime), Air Waybill (lettre de transport aérien) or CMR road consignment note

France has enacted a mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting framework (Article 32 of the Loi de Finances 2023, as postponed by the Loi de Finances 2024): from 1 September 2026 all large enterprises and enterprises of intermediate size (ETI) must issue e-invoices and all VAT-taxable persons with a French establishment must receive e-invoices; from 1 September 2027 all small and medium enterprises (PME/TPE), including this SARL if classified as PME, must issue e-invoices. Mandatory channels are the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified private Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP), with data exchanged via API using the EU EN 16931 semantic model and formats Factur-X, UBL 2.1 or CII (with the French CIUS extension). France applies a data-transmission model, not a real-time clearance or pre-clearance model: there is no digital stamping or pre-invoice validation by the tax authority; invoice data plus payment-status data must be transmitted to the PPF either daily or no later than the 10th day of the following month, including e-reporting for B2C transactions and for international/export flows. Non-compliance (Article 289 quater CGI) gives rise to an administrative fine of EUR 15 per non-compliant invoice and EUR 250 per unreported transaction, capped at EUR 15,000 per calendar year per company.

Assessment windows: DGFIP may reassess VAT within the standard 3-year period (Article L.169 LPF); the period is extended to 6 years where transactions relate to non-declared foreign establishments or undisclosed foreign accounts (Article L.169 B LPF), and to 10 years in cases of unreported foreign bank accounts or structures (Article L.169 C LPF). Clawback: if the export zero-rating is disallowed for failure to prove physical export within 90 days, for false or incomplete documentation, or for abusive use of the exemption, the full 20% TVA is assessed retroactively on the taxable amount, plus late-payment interest of 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI), together with penalties graded by the taxpayer's good faith: 10% for good-faith late payment or omission, 40% for deliberate misdeclaration (mauvaise foi, Article 1729-1 CGI) and 80% for dissimulation, false documents or fraudulent manoeuvres (Article 1729-2 CGI). Criminal exposure: tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI carries a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and 5 years' imprisonment, increased to EUR 3,000,000 and 7 years for aggravated fraud committed by an organized gang or when the fraud attempts to conceal part of the sums due; the DGDDI may separately prosecute export-customs fraud and seize assets. No de minimis waiver exists in the export context; administrative tolerance is limited to documented cases of force majeure.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility. As a euro-area member, France transposed Articles 63-66 TFEU into the CMF (Articles L152-1 to L152-4), eliminating all approval-based exchange controls for current-account and capital-account operations. The Euro is a reserve currency with no convertibility restrictions. Remaining obligations are administrative and non-discretionary: (i) statistical reporting to BdF for payment flows with non-residents aggregating above EUR 50,000 per counterparty per month, filed by the banks under Article R152-4 CMF; (ii) CRS/FATCA disclosure on accounts of non-French tax residents; (iii) cash-courier declaration above EUR 10,000 (Regulation (EU) 2018/1672); and (iv) compliance with EU consolidated sanctions lists. Profit remittance (dividends) is fully convertible, but the paying French entity must apply the applicable withholding tax, reduced to 0-15% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU or the relevant income tax treaty upon submission of tax-residence attestations. No licensing, waiting period or central bank report approval is attached to any capital movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. France abolished all statutory surrender and repatriation requirements for export proceeds. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no deadline for repatriation. The FR SARL may invoice in any freely agreed currency (EUR, USD, CNY, etc.), retain 100% of export revenues offshore, maintain multi-currency accounts with French licensed banks, and select conversion timing wholly at its commercial discretion. The only mandatory duty is the monthly FILEPA statistical declaration by the bank (not an exchange-control approval) and the archival of supporting trade documents for five years. Export receipts are fully convertible at the prevailing market rate; ratio: 0%; holding period: unrestricted; surrender timeline: N/A. Under UCP 600 documentary credits, the collecting bank may condition payment on presentation of conforming shipping documents, but this is a documentary-condition framework, not a public exchange rule.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) is the central bank and the authority administering the French balance-of-payments (Fichiers relatifs aux échanges avec l'étranger, FILEPA) statistical framework. Banking supervision and AML/KYC licensing of credit institutions and payment institutions is exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution), an independent authority legally attached to the BdF. The Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) at the Ministry of Economy and Finance retains residual foreign direct investment (FDI) screening powers for strategically listed sectors under Article L151-3 of the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF). The subject entity is an SARL engaged in ISIC 2824 (tunnel boring machine spare parts), a non-screened sector, so no ex-ante Treasury authorization is required for non-EU shareholding, capital injection or intra-group financing.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard CIT rate for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2022)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 (gross annual turnover or total balance sheet for entities whose ultimate group is also large): French entities subject to CIT must prepare and, on request, provide the full transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) where their annual turnover exceeds EUR 400 million and they hold directly or indirectly at least 25% of a foreign entity, or are themselves held 25% or more by a foreign group entity; Country-by-Country Reporting is required for ultimate French parents with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more

Economic substance scope: France does not operate the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime; substance is nonetheless enforced through the general anti-abuse rule (Article L.64 Livre des Procédures Fiscales), the concept of beneficial ownership, the abuse-of-law doctrine for letter-box entities, the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive, and the Pillar Two 15% global minimum tax; a French SARL that cannot demonstrate actual management, employees, premises and commercial functions cannot claim treaty or EU Directive benefits, and its existence may be recharacterised as a pure conduit

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI: five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, increased to seven years' imprisonment and EUR 1,000,000 fine for aggravated fraud carried out through an organised group or with cross-border concealment; civil reassessment carrying a 40% bad-faith penalty or 80% penalty for fraudulent manoeuvres (Article 1729 CGI) plus monthly late-payment interest

Director personal liability: The gérant may be criminally convicted, subjected to a travel ban (interdiction de quitter le territoire), disqualified from managing a company, and held jointly and severally liable for the SARL's tax debts under Article L.267 LPF where serious managerial fault caused the non-payment; civil restitution and seizure of personal assets may follow
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Article 57 CGI empowers DGFiP to recharacterise and reintegrate the shifted profit into the French tax base at arm's length, with a 40% penalty for unjustified documentation and, where the recipient is in a non-cooperative state, a penalty up to 150% of the recharacterised amount; interest accrues at 0.20% per month

Director personal liability: The managing director who knowingly signed false or incomplete declarations exposes himself to criminal liability as principal or accomplice to fraudulent declaration; courts may order disqualification from commercial management and personal contribution to the corporate tax debt under Article L.267 LPF
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Application of Article L.64 LPF permits the tax administration to disregard the sham entity and reattribute its income to the real beneficiaries; the abuse-of-right penalty amounts to 80% of the tax evaded; the entity may also be struck from the RCS and its tax and customs advantages revoked retroactively

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: the sole shareholder-manager faces personal liability for all French CIT, withholding tax and VAT under Article L.267 LPF; fictitious domiciliation is a criminal offence punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and EUR 75,000 fine; travel bans and asset freezes can be ordered during the investigation
AML / Sanctions / Fraudulent Use of Payment Channels

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Article 324-1 of the French Criminal Code carries five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine (aggravated form: ten years and EUR 750,000); ACPR and Tracfin may impose administrative sanctions including prohibition on operations, professional bans and pecuniary penalties of up to EUR 1 million for legal persons

Director personal liability: The gérant may be prosecuted as an accomplice to laundering, be personally fined, suffer confiscation of his own assets, be placed under judicial control with a ban on leaving French territory, and be subject to personal bankruptcy measures (faillite personnelle), including a permanent ban on managing any French company
