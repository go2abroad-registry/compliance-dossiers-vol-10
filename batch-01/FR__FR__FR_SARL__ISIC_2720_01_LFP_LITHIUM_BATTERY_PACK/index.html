Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum share capital is required for SARL; however, at least 10% of the subscribed capital must be paid up in cash on incorporation and placed in a bank deposit or notary custody account until registration. Contributions in kind must be fully paid up and appraised.

Funding & unlock path: Before commissioning, the site must obtain: (1) fire safety approval from the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) or the Joint Safety Commission for pickling/retail buildings, including compliance with fire-resistance ratings and lithium battery fire suppression; (2) structural load certification confirming floor capacity for loaded palletized cells and racking systems; (3) occupational risk assessment with PSE (Plan de Sécurité Établissement) or a non-classified installations occupational attestation, including charging area ventilation, emergency battery quenching, no-smoking rules, and personal protective equipment for lithium electrolyte exposure; (4) electrical installation conformity certification by an accredited bureau (e.g., Qualifelec via IRYS) and official commissioning notice to the Labour Inspectorate.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum share capital is required for SARL; however, at least 10% of the subscribed capital must be paid up in cash on incorporation and placed in a bank deposit or notary custody account until registration. Contributions in kind must be fully paid up and appraised.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL must complete company incorporation through the INPI one-stop-shop, obtaining the SIREN and SIRET numbers. DGFiP then attributes the French intra-Community VAT number in the standard national format: FR + two-digit automated key + nine-digit SIREN, i.e. a 13-character VAT identification number. A non-established foreign entity without a French permanent establishment must file a dedicated VAT registration application with DGFiP/SIEE, supply constitutional documents, proof of foreign tax registration, board authorization and a fiscal power of attorney, and, if established outside the EU, appoint an accredited French VAT tax representative before it can obtain an FR VAT number. Processing normally takes 15 to 30 working days from a complete dossier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box classification. An ordinary non-family SARL is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying 'SARL de famille' whose members are natural persons within the statutory family circle is treated by default as a partnership for income tax purposes unless it elects corporate income tax under Article 239 bis AA of the Code général des impôts. French groups may also elect tax consolidation under Article 223 A CGI. Hybrid mismatch outcomes stemming from incompatible foreign entity classifications are addressed through statutory anti-hybrid and controlled-company provisions, principally Articles 209 B and 212 bis CGI, rather than elective pass-through classification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France benefits from full capital and current account convertibility under Eurozone Treaty rules (TFEU Articles 63–65). There are no exchange controls on current-account trade settlements, intercompany financing, dividends, or liquidation proceeds, and no requirement for prior central-bank approval of EUR or foreign-currency transactions. The practical constraints are: (i) profit remittances cannot be made unless the French SARL has filed its tax returns and settled the corresponding corporate income tax and social liabilities, and only to the extent distributable reserves exist after the legal reserve (5% of share capital up to 10%) is constituted; (ii) dividend distributions to a non-resident parent are subject to a 30% domestic withholding tax that may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty (e.g., France–China DTA for a corporate parent holding at least 10% of the SARL's share capital, subject to treaty-look-through and substance conditions); (iii) Eurozone monetary integration implies that the euro is fully convertible, but foreign-currency flows remain subject to Banque de France statistical reporting obligations under the balance-of-payments (BOP) framework, including a monthly declaration where a client's cumulative foreign exchange transactions exceed the relevant notification thresholds. The SARL's functional currency is the euro; USD/CNY balances should be held only as strictly cash tiers within the liquidity waterfall and not as structural investments to avoid FX translation volatility under the ANSF (Règlement ANC n°2016-07) recognition rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion ratio applies in France or the Eurozone. The SARL may freely hold, use, or reinvest 100% of its export proceeds in EUR or any foreign currency. The only legally binding obligations are statistical and documentary: (a) any settlement with a non-resident counterparty reaching or exceeding EUR 15,000 per transaction must be reported to the Banque de France through the authorized intermediary bank; (b) when cumulative monthly settlements with the same non-resident counterparty exceed EUR 50,000, the bank must file a consolidated BOP declaration (capitulatif des opérations de change); (c) a resident entity crossing cumulative foreign-currency flows of EUR 15 million in a month must itself file the relevant BOP return through its bank; and (d) original trade documentation (commercial invoices, SWIFT MT103 records, transport documents, customs SAD/Intrastat evidence) must be retained for at least five years under Articles L.123-22 and L.123-23 of the French Commercial Code. No foreign-currency account authorization from ACPR is necessary beyond standard banking facility documents.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), acting with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) as the national foreign-exchange and investment authority, and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the licensing and supervisory banking regulator under Title I, Livre V of the Code monétaire et financier (CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for operational EUR disbursements under approximately €100,000 per month and for routine SEPA flows; LOW-MEDIUM for capital escrow, trade finance, FX hedging, and inbound FDI routing. French-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) under PSD2/EMD2 — including Qonto, Shine, N26 France, and Blank — offer accelerated remote onboarding, but they are legally incapable of: (i) granting credit, discounting commercial paper, or advancing trade funding (Article L.525-8 CMF); (ii) issuing an attestation de dépôt des fonds or capital-blocking certificate required for a SARL share-capital escrow (only a full-licensed credit institution can certify funds blocking under French corporate law); (iii) executing FX forwards, swaps, options, or NDFs, since these are MiFID II investment services requiring an investment-firm or credit-institution licence; (iv) submitting Banque de France BOP filings and DG Trésor FDI notifications directly. Post-Brexit, a UK-FCA-authorised EMI has no European passport into France and must obtain a French branch agreement from ACPR; consequently, the SARL should use EMIs solely as a disbursement automation layer beneath a primary tier-1 relationship, never as the sole banking rail for capital, FX, or letters of credit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol: (1) Equity capital injection — the SARL opens a dedicated capital escrow (compte bloqué) with a licensed credit institution; the non-resident parent remits via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT for EUR) with Field 70 declaring 'apport en capital social / augmentation de capital' and referencing the corporate resolution; the bank verifies the RNE UBO extract and issues the attestation de dépôt des fonds; funds are released to the operational account only after the deed is signed and registered with the Registre National des Entreprises. (2) Shareholder current account (compte courant d'associé) — funds may be credited directly to the operational account; the payment narrative must reference the signed intercompany loan agreement, and the Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose code '2300' (other investment loans/financing) must be transmitted to the bank; the interest rate and the 150% debt-to-equity thin-capitalisation ceiling must be reconciled before deposit to prevent partial disallowance under Article 212 CGI. (3) FDI screening — where the investing parent is established outside the EU and the French venture is poised to engage in energy storage or battery-related critical technologies, DG Trésor prior authorisation may be triggered under French FDI rules (Articles L.151-3 seq. CMF, evolutions by Decree 2023-1294); the SARL must ascertain the applicable perimeter and obtain either a prior clearance or a no-objection letter before the escrow is released; an ex-post ministerial declaration is insufficient for controlled entities. Verification on receipt: the bank matches the MT103 Field 70, the corporate resolution, the UBO register, and the bankinternal source-of-funds records; inbound transfers through third-country nominee or consolidation accounts are prohibited, as are crypto-bridge, cash-courier, and non-bank PSP credit structures.Primary clearing rails: (i) EUR supplier runs are settled via SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) on TARGET2, with SEPA Instant for sub-€100,000 urgent components; (ii) non-EUR disbursements (USD, CNY, HKD for LFP cathode powder, lithium carbonate, cells, busbars, and BMS boards) are executed by SWIFT MT103, with Field 70 containing the commercial invoice number, relevant HS codes (HS 8507.60 for LFP battery packs and cells; HS 8537.10 for battery management systems; HS 8507.60.00/HS 8506.80 for ancillary power equipment), and the ISIC-aligned payment narrative to satisfy Banque de France BOP matching and transaction monitoring; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600: import LCs issued via SWIFT MT700 and confirmed by CACIB, CIC, or Société Générale, available at sight for LFP cell purchases with a maximum tenor of 120 days from bill of lading, conditioned on full-set original bills of lading, signed commercial invoice, certificate of origin, and independent third-party inspection certificate (SGS/BV/Cotecna); export LCs may be transferred (MT720) upstream to the core cell supplier to compress the cash conversion cycle and to lock the financing margin; (iv) documentary collection (D/P, D/A) is permissible for mid-value spot purchases below LC thresholds; (v) every outbound transaction must be traceable from the ERP treasury ledger (X3/SAP or equivalent) to the SWIFT confirmation and customs SAD files; all settlement evidence is retained in a secured archive for five years under Article L.123-22 of the Commercial Code.FX Forward (change à terme) — plain-vanilla deliverable EUR/USD and EUR/CNY forwards matched to confirmed purchase orders or receivables, generally up to 12 months tenor without ACPR pre-clearance.FX Swap (swap de change) — short-term roll of maturity mismatches between intercompany shareholder advances and Chinese supplier payment dates, prudently limited to less than six months.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCIRS) — applicable where the SARL carries EUR-denominated capex debt against USD/CNY-denominated feedstock commitments and where the bank relationship supports ISDA 2002 CSA documentation.NDF (Non-Deliverable Forward) — available only where the underlying invoice currency is non-convertible or has a restricted deliverable market, e.g. offshore RMB NDF, and where the trade exposure is fully evidenced; the bank will require a hedge-adequacy opinion to satisfy the MIFID II suitability/appropriateness assessment.Plain-vanilla FX Options (EUR call / USD put, EUR put / CNY call) — legally permitted but recommended to be executed at the corporate-treasury level of the parent; the SARL must pass the bank's MIFID II appropriateness test and maintain a board-approved hedging policy (politique de couverture) describing permissible instruments, stop-loss limits, and tenor ceilings.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for operational EUR disbursements under approximately €100,000 per month and for routine SEPA flows; LOW-MEDIUM for capital escrow, trade finance, FX hedging, and inbound FDI routing. French-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) under PSD2/EMD2 — including Qonto, Shine, N26 France, and Blank — offer accelerated remote onboarding, but they are legally incapable of: (i) granting credit, discounting commercial paper, or advancing trade funding (Article L.525-8 CMF); (ii) issuing an attestation de dépôt des fonds or capital-blocking certificate required for a SARL share-capital escrow (only a full-licensed credit institution can certify funds blocking under French corporate law); (iii) executing FX forwards, swaps, options, or NDFs, since these are MiFID II investment services requiring an investment-firm or credit-institution licence; (iv) submitting Banque de France BOP filings and DG Trésor FDI notifications directly. Post-Brexit, a UK-FCA-authorised EMI has no European passport into France and must obtain a French branch agreement from ACPR; consequently, the SARL should use EMIs solely as a disbursement automation layer beneath a primary tier-1 relationship, never as the sole banking rail for capital, FX, or letters of credit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when related-party transactions exceed €1,000,000 annual aggregate; Master File mandatory when the group's consolidated revenue reaches €750,000,000; all taxpayers above €400,000 revenue or assets are subject to simplified transfer-pricing documentation if related-party cross-border flows exist.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate an ESR-style registry, but the general anti-abuse rule (Article L.64 LPF), substance-over-form doctrine, and BEPS Principal Purpose Test require the French SARL to demonstrate adequate premises, operational personnel, local board decision-making, and control over LFP battery-pack assembly risk to obtain treaty benefits and avoid sham-company recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent IS Filing

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty: 40% of understated tax for bad faith; 80% for organised fraud; monthly 0.20% late interest; criminal fine up to €1,500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (7 years when organised fraud is established).

Director personal liability: Civil joint liability for corporate tax under Article L.267 LPF; piercing of the corporate veil for abus de bien social; French travel ban, asset freeze, and up to 5 years' imprisonment for managers.
Shell Company / Artificial Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under Article 45 CGI; disallowance of deductible expenses; denial of treaty relief under the Principal Purpose Test; reassessment for six years; penalty up to 80% of additional tax.

Director personal liability: Joint liability for the entire reassessed tax and penalties; potential criminal tax-evasion convictions; personal travel restrictions and prohibition on managing any French company.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to €1,000,000; sanctions up to 10% of consolidated turnover for breaches of the French Monetary and Financial Code; suspension of corporate registration; penalties for failure to file RBE declarations up to €750,000 for legal entities.

Director personal liability: Personal fines, imprisonment up to 5 years for money-laundering facilitation, professional disqualification, international asset-freezing orders, and potential seizure of shares in the French SARL.
