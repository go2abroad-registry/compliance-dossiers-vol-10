Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual or domiciliation office is permissible for a French SARL, but only through a licensed 'société de domiciliation' registered with the local préfecture. The domiciliation agreement must be formalized in writing and the address must be geolocated in France and used exclusively for the company's registered office (siège social). For an electric passenger bus OEM, virtual office use is acceptable only for the early holding phase; actual manufacturing, customs clearance, and bid participation require a physical establishment with substantial local substance. If the company claims the title 'manufacturer' under ISIC 2910 and applies for municipal contracts, it must maintain a physical facility where buses are assembled and tested. Any virtual office that does not reflect genuine management and operational substance will expose the company to corporate tax re-domestication by the DGFiP under Article 211 of the Code général des impôts and to sanctions for artificial arrangement under France's domestic GAAR (L'Avis de l'Administration).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required by French law for a SARL. The Articles of Association fix the capital amount. Cash contributions must be fully paid at incorporation and held in a blocked account at a bank, Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or before a notary; the funds are released to the manager only after presentation of the RCS/Kbis registration certificate. Contributions in kind must be assessed in the Articles and transferred in full.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety engineering: the plant must obtain a fire safety certificate from the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) or a recognized inspection body, covering fire compartments, egress routes, extinguishing systems, and dangerous substance storage. Structural load and stability certification, based on a building inspection and the planning permission, is required for the assembly hall, roof, mezzanine, and bus lifts. Occupational clearance under the French Labour Code: installation must be designed following the occupational health and safety rules, CARSAT inspection, and DREETS validation of machine guarding, electrical installations, chemical risk assessment (document unique), ventilation, and emergency-response plans.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area jurisdiction with a freely convertible currency and no general exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions. The SARL may receive foreign capital, pay foreign suppliers, repatriate dividends, repay documented shareholder loans, and convert euros into other currencies, subject to corporate-law approvals, distributable-profit rules, withholding-tax analysis, transfer-pricing requirements, anti-money-laundering controls, sanctions screening, beneficial-ownership verification, and applicable balance-of-payments reporting. Dividend distributions require approved annual accounts and legally distributable reserves. Payments to related non-residents must be supported by arm's-length documentation. Foreign direct investment screening may apply to acquisitions or investments in protected strategic activities; ordinary electric-bus manufacturing is not automatically subject to screening, but activities involving critical technologies, defense, energy, transport infrastructure, data, or other protected interests require a case-specific assessment under French investment-control rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a French bank, convert export receipts into euros, or maintain a prescribed foreign-currency conversion ratio. Export proceeds may remain in foreign-currency accounts, subject to the bank's account terms, AML controls, accounting requirements, and tax rules. There is no general mandatory statutory deadline for conversion of export receipts. Banks may request commercial invoices, customs evidence, transport documents, contracts, and payment-purpose information. Banque de France or the relevant reporting institution may require balance-of-payments or statistical reporting for qualifying cross-border transactions. Cash and certain payment instruments transported across EU external borders are subject to applicable EU and French cash-control declarations.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank. Foreign-exchange, balance-of-payments, and international financial statistics are administered principally through Banque de France and the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment accounts and operational collections; low as a substitute for a full-service French relationship bank. An EU-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro payment accounts, SEPA transfers, foreign-exchange services, and certain card or collection products under its home-state authorisation and passporting framework. It may not be treated as a bank for all purposes, cannot provide bank deposits unless separately authorised, and customer funds are generally safeguarded rather than covered by the French deposit-guarantee regime. Offshore or non-EEA digital EMIs require appropriate local authorisation or passporting and may face correspondent-bank, safeguarding, sanctions, AML, tax-residency, and French IBAN acceptance constraints. A fintech account should not be the sole account for capital escrow, high-value industrial trade, payroll, public-sector collections, or complex LC activity without written confirmation of product capability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route paid-in capital and shareholder loans from an account held in the documented name of the investor or lender to the SARL's French bank account using SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments or SWIFT cross-border transfer for non-euro payments. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. Qualifying small and medium-sized companies may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, with the balance taxed at 25%, if their paid-up capital is fully paid and at least 75% owned by individuals or qualifying entities and their annual turnover, excluding tax, does not exceed EUR 10 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For dividends paid by a French SARL to a non-resident, the domestic withholding tax rate is generally 25% for a corporate recipient, subject to treaty relief and domestic exemptions. A qualifying EU parent company may obtain an exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive and French implementing rules, generally requiring a qualifying participation, holding period, beneficial ownership, and anti-abuse compliance. Rates and treatment for individual recipients differ.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. For a financial year ending on 31 December, it is due by the second working day following 1 May, subject to any officially announced electronic-filing extension. The corporate income tax balance is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a 31 December year-end, generally 15 May. Instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, subject to applicable rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million turnover or gross assets, generally, for a French entity or French tax group within the statutory scope, including specified ownership or control relationships; the threshold and scope must be tested under the rules applicable to the relevant fiscal year. French companies subject to the documentation requirement must prepare Master File and Local File documentation and provide it to the tax authorities upon request. A separate annual transfer-pricing summary declaration (form 2257-SD) may be required for entities meeting the applicable statutory criteria. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations as a separate general regime. French transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, permanent-establishment, anti-abuse, and general anti-avoidance rules require transactions and arrangements to reflect their actual functions, assets, risks, decision-making, and commercial purpose. A low-substance French entity may face challenges to treaty benefits, deductions, profit allocation, or the existence and characterization of its activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing and intercompany financing

Statutory sanction: Taxable-profit adjustments, late-payment interest, and applicable tax surcharges. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation may result in a penalty generally equal to the greater of EUR 10,000 or 5% of the transfer-pricing adjustments, subject to statutory rules and caps.

Director personal liability: A director is not automatically liable for company tax debts. Personal liability may arise under statutory conditions for fraudulent conduct, serious and repeated tax-law breaches, or insolvency-related misconduct; criminal tax fraud can result in individual fines and imprisonment.
Tax fraud and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, and surcharges that may reach 80% in specified cases. Tax fraud may be prosecuted criminally; individuals may face imprisonment and fines under the applicable provisions of the French Tax Code and Penal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally participate in, direct, or knowingly facilitate fraud may face criminal prosecution and personal penalties. Civil tax liability may be imposed on a director only where statutory conditions are met; imprisonment is not an automatic consequence of a company tax debt.
Undeclared permanent establishment or disguised French activity

Statutory sanction: Assessment of French corporate income tax, applicable withholding taxes, VAT and related liabilities, interest, and penalties. Treaty protection depends on the relevant treaty facts and cannot be assumed from the absence of a French subsidiary.

Director personal liability: Personal exposure depends on individual participation and statutory grounds, including tax fraud or insolvency misconduct; foreign residence does not itself eliminate potential French enforcement.
Improper dividend, royalty, or interest withholding

Statutory sanction: The French payer may be liable for unpaid withholding tax, interest, and applicable surcharges. Treaty relief may be denied where anti-abuse, beneficial-ownership, or principal-purpose requirements are not met.

Director personal liability: A director may face personal consequences where the statutory conditions for fraudulent conduct, personal participation, or tax-debt liability are satisfied; liability is not automatic.
Corporate insolvency and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: Civil and commercial consequences may include repayment, asset recovery, management bans, and liability for all or part of an insufficiency of assets where statutory conditions are met. Criminal sanctions may apply to specified bankruptcy offences.

Director personal liability: Managers may face personal civil liability, management prohibitions, or criminal prosecution for qualifying misconduct. Travel bans are not a standard automatic sanction for ordinary tax non-compliance.
Anti-money-laundering, sanctions, and payment controls

Statutory sanction: Consequences depend on the offence and may include asset freezing, transaction prohibition, regulatory or criminal fines, and imprisonment. Applicable sanctions screening and reporting duties must be assessed for the transaction and counterparties.

Director personal liability: Individuals who authorize, facilitate, or knowingly participate in unlawful conduct may face personal criminal or regulatory liability. Corporate status does not shield an individual from liability for personal involvement.
