Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), operating through the impots.gouv.fr portal; customs and export verification through Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI); corporate and VAT registration coordinated via the Réseau des Urssaf / Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a French SARL must file the statutory incorporation dossier with the CFE (now Guichet Unique) to obtain SIREN/SIRET numbers. VAT identification is issued automatically by DGFIP once the entity is registered; a non-established foreign entity may obtain a French intra-community VAT number by appointing a tax representative approved by DGFIP and filing the French VAT registration form (Form 3527-SD). For an existing SARL executing an ISIC 4220 EPC project, the company must ensure its SIRET and TVA intracommunautaire are active and, if intra-community acquisitions exceed €10,000 in a calendar year, file the EC Sales List and recapitulative statement.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is a separate French tax resident corporation subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on its worldwide profits; the territoriality principle applies, so certain foreign-source revenue may be excluded. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is a separate French tax resident corporation subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on its worldwide profits; the territoriality principle applies, so certain foreign-source revenue may be excluded. A SARL that opts for the micro-company regime is not eligible; although a small SARL may be taxed at the reduced 15% corporate rate under statutory limits, ordinary income remains entity-level taxable.

Available election forms

France does not operate a check-the-box classification system. Elections available include (i) the option for the SARL to be treated as a partnership (option pour l'impôt sur les sociétés individuals cannot reverse to transparent; conversely, a société de personnes may elect corporate taxation) but for a SARL the default is corporate, (ii) the option to join a fiscal integration group under Articles 223 A to 223 T of the CGI if the parent holds at least 95% of the SARL, (iii) the VAT option for a single tax group under Article 256 C bis of the CGI, and (iv) the option for the reduced 15% corporate rate on the first €42,000 of taxable profits subject to strict conditions. Hybrid mismatch elections are regulated under Article 212 bis CGI and do not confer elective US-style check-the-box relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For established French businesses there is no statutory VAT registration threshold: every person carrying out a taxable economic activity in France must register for VAT. Non-established foreign businesses must register from the first taxable transaction, unless they qualify for the distance-selling threshold. For intra-community acquisitions of goods by a non-taxable person the registration threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year; for taxable persons, no threshold applies. For a reverse-charge/cross-border service provider, the customer's VAT registration generally triggers the obligation through a reverse-charge mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under French VAT is available for (i) goods physically exported outside the European Union, provided the seller is a taxable dealer, the goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU by or on behalf of the seller, and the buyer is not liable for VAT in France; zero-rating also applies to the supply of goods installed offshore or to EU customers in certain contexts under Article 258 and Article 262 of the French General Tax Code. For services, zero-rating (exemption with credit) may apply to services directly related to the export of goods, to cross-border transport, and to certain services rendered on movable property temporarily exported; for municipal sewage treatment works, French VAT at 20% generally applies to construction and installation services performed on French territory, but the supply of equipment to be exported is zero-rated if proof of export is retained. The contractual foreign-aid/World Bank or escrow payment does not per se create export status; the actual removal of goods or delivery of offshore services evidenced by transport documents is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / ECS - Export Control System) lodged electronically via DELTA or NCTS
  • Commercial Invoice reflecting the terms, parties, and Harmonized System classifications
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from EU territory
  • Shipping/transport proof and certificate of origin (EUR.1 or A.TR where applicable)
  • Proof of receipt by the foreign customer, including delivery acceptance certificate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank documentation showing payment proceeds repatriated to the French entity

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is rolling out under the 2020 Finance Act: from 1 September 2026 all large and medium-sized entities must issue e-invoices and report transaction data through the Chorus Pro public portal or a certified Partner Dematerialisation Platform (PDP); all other taxable persons, including SMEs, must comply from 1 September 2027. E-reporting of payment data and cross-border supplies is mandatory. The system requires structured electronic invoices using EN16931-compliant formats (Factur-X, EDI or PEPPOL), with digital fiscal clearance through real-time validation by DGFIP. Tax clearance/fiscal stamping is not applied, but the transmission of each invoice to the Chorus Pro system or a certified PDP establishes the legal and fiscal timestamp. B2C and export invoices remain subject to standard archiving obligations and must be transmitted for e-reporting, not e-invoicing, under current rules.

French customs and tax authorities (DGFIP and DGDDI) audit export files retrospectively. If the exporter cannot produce the complete export proof dossier within a period of at least three years from the return filing date, the zero-rating is denied and VAT is retroactively assessed. The standard late-payment interest at 0.20% per month applies, and a 40% penalty (Article 1729 CGI) for intentional incorrect tax returns will attach; where documentary fraud is established, a 80% penalty for bad faith or fraud (Article 1729-a-1 CGI) and up to €45,000 fine plus imprisonment penalty can be imposed. Customs infractions may trigger discounting of the export registration and confiscation of the goods. In practice, the six-month foreign-exchange repatriation benchmark is contractual/ADB-clearing standard and not a French statutory deadline, but missing foreign receipts without valid commercial reason will trigger a transfer-pricing and tax-nexus audit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility for the euro across both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control permits, quotas, or ad hoc approval gates for ordinary inward/outward payments or profit repatriation. Capital movements between France and non-EU jurisdictions are liberalized under Articles 63-66 TFEU and the EU Capital Movements Directive, subject only to balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Banque de France, national security screening in exceptional circumstances, and tax treaty withholding mechanics. Profit remittances by a French SARL require corporate approval, audited accounts, and compliance with article L.227-? of the Code de commerce provisions on dividend distributions, but no FX authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios. Export receipts earned in foreign currency may be retained on account, converted freely, or used for foreign-currency disbursements. Banque de France requires periodic balance-of-payments reporting for aggregated cross-border flows and positions, but imposes no repatriation deadline, no conversion percent, and no obligation to deposit proceeds with any designated bank.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France in its capacity as French balance-of-payments, monetary and financial-stability authority, with prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment institutions by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); ECB retains external monetary competence for the euro; DG Trésor retains international financial policy functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a principal treasury rail; Medium as an ancillary payment channel. A non-EU or offshore digital EMI without an EU/EEA banking or e-money licence and without ACPR registration or passport notification is legally prohibited from operating payment or deposit-taking services in France. An EEA-licensed EMI may provide limited cross-border payment initiation and collection services after ACPR passport notification, but French banks and public-sector escrow counterparties will not accept such an EMI for high-value capital escrow, World Bank-backed receivables, or SWIFT-based settlement. Required legal constraint: the EMI must hold a valid e-money or payment institution licence within the EEA, complete the freedom-to-provide-services filing with ACPR, and maintain a physical place of business or bundled programme without accessing the SARL's segregated clients account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound capital injection must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or the ISO 20022 equivalent from the subscribing shareholder's account to the French SARL's designated operational account. The remittance instruction must include the mandatory ISO 20022 purpose code for equity capital contribution, commonly 'EQUI' or 'CAPT', and the beneficiary account must reference the SARL's SIREN identification and corporate name. For French balance-of-payments statistical purposes, the payment should be documented with the contributing shareholder, transaction nature, capital increase resolution, and confirmation of contribution without personal goodwill. Banque de France may require that the receiving bank file the flow under the French BOP declaration if the monthly aggregated threshold is crossed. The commercial bank will verify the debiting account ownership, corroborate the purpose with the underlying share subscription agreement and legalized board resolution, and may require a capital duty certificate or legal exemption letter before funds are credited without restriction.Outbound trade disbursements for equipment procurement, subcontractor payments, and EPC chain settlements should be made through SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for non-euro cross-border transactions or SEPA Credit Transfers for euro payments within the SEPA zone. For sovereign and multilateral project disbursements, the use of documentary letters of credit issued under UCP 600 is advised, with the French confirming bank receiving SWIFT MT700/MT710 messages. Large-value disbursements may additionally require structured codes covering the underlying invoice, contract milestone, commodity classification, and shipping documents. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Master File and Local File required if group turnover or assets exceed €400 million; simplified documentation (Form 2257) required for all other entities with intercompany transactions exceeding €50,000.

Economic substance scope: France applies the abuse of law doctrine (abus de droit) and substance-over-form principles. While no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist, tax residency requires effective management and control in France. For treaty benefits, the beneficial ownership test and principal purpose test (PPT) under MLI apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 80% of the evaded tax, plus interest at 0.2% per month. Criminal fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they have committed fraud or gross negligence. Civil restitution, travel bans, and up to 7 years imprisonment for aggravated tax fraud.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 40% penalty for lack of substance. The company may be dissolved by court order.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly liable for the company's tax debts if they are found to have used the company as a sham. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1 million for companies; criminal penalties for directors up to €375,000 and 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for AML breaches if they knowingly participated or failed to supervise. Travel bans and seizure of personal assets may occur.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty for lack of documentation, and interest. If deemed fraudulent, 80% penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation may face criminal charges for tax fraud, leading to imprisonment and personal fines.
