Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a fully convertible jurisdiction within the EU single market: current-account payments and capital-account operations benefit from free movement of capital under Article 63 TFEU. No exchange-control permit is required to inject equity, pay trade invoices, or repatriate profits. Practical hurdles are limited to documentary KYC/AML review and, for profits distributed from a French SARL, French withholding tax at the applicable rate or tax treaty rate. Neither current-account nor capital-account convertibility is restricted by foreign exchange laws; non-resident shareholders may freely receive distributable dividends after corporate formalities and tax declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory surrender ratio: 0% (none). France does not impose a statutory timeline for repatriation or conversion of export proceeds; exporters may keep foreign-currency receipts in French or EU bank accounts. The only compliance duties are (i) preserving underlying export invoices, transport documents and customs declarations for at least ten years for French corporate records, and (ii) supporting the Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration (REX) filed automatically by the bank for cross-border payment flows above the statutory threshold. No prior authorisation is needed for retention or conversion of export earnings.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French central bank), acting with the French Ministry of Economy and Finance; the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) supervises banking licensing, payment institutions and anti-money-laundering compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium (constrained). A regulated EEA/EMI passported to France may be used only as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail after a domestic French bank relationship is established. Such an EMI cannot validly hold the SARL statutory capital escrow, cannot issue the deposit certificate (certificat de dépôt des fonds) required for incorporation, and cannot replace the Banque de France reporting obligation. Offshore EMIs established outside the EEA are treated as non-resident financial institutions; French tier-1 banks generally refuse to treat them as forming a compliant KYC chain and will require source-of-funds documentation through a regulated correspondent bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: New SARL with no established French commercial footprint and no verifiable local buyers in the medical PPE sector.

Defense evidence: Provide the completed SIRENE/INSEE registration, VAT number, EORI number, signed purchase orders or distribution agreements with French hospitals or medical wholesalers, ISO 13485 certificate or CE technical documentation for sterile examination gloves, customs clearance evidence, registered office lease, and the SARL business plan demonstrating real economic activity.
Ultimate beneficial owners reside outside France and the corporate chain includes opaque or shell-like holding structures.

Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owners reside outside France and the corporate chain includes opaque or shell-like holding structures.

Defense evidence: Provide a certified group organisational chart, register of beneficial owners (RCS/UBO extract), apostilled constitutional documents of each intermediate holding company, audited financial statements, tax residence certificates, source-of-wealth reports, sanction/PEP screening documents for every shareholder above 25%, and a clear funding path from the ultimate shareholder to the French bank account.
Initial paid-up capital is to be injected by a non-resident shareholder via a third-country bank with no pre-existing banking relationship in France.

Root cause: Initial paid-up capital is to be injected by a non-resident shareholder via a third-country bank with no pre-existing banking relationship in France.

Defense evidence: Provide the bank reference and compliance score of the sending institution, six months of corroborating bank statements, the signed shareholder resolution approving the capital contribution, proof that the funds arise from lawful business profits, the underlying commercial contracts, and a sworn statement of source of funds acceptable to the French bank's AML officer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder must remit the SARL capital contribution by SWIFT MT103 in EUR or USD to a French bank or to the blocked capital account (compte bloqué) opened by the manager before incorporation. The bank issues a certificat de dépôt des fonds as required for the notary and the RCS registration. The payment narrative must state 'Apport en capital social – FR_SARL – [shareholder name]' and the French bank reports the flow to the Banque de France under REX where the statutory threshold is exceeded. After incorporation, the blocked account is released to the operational account upon presentation of the RCS registration certificate. No FX licence is required; the receiving bank will verify the amount against the SARL articles of association and the capital certificate.Use SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for routine commercial supplier payments, with full beneficiary IBAN/BIC, invoice number, Incoterms and customs export reference in the payment narrative. For high-value or strategic imports of raw materials and sterilisation equipment, use an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (Commercial L/C) issued by the French bank under UCP600 through a confirmed advising bank.
