Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital is required; EUR 1 is generally sufficient. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des depots before filing, and the deposit certificate must be submitted. The blocked funds are released after issuance of the registration extract (Kbis).

Funding & unlock path: Before occupation, the warehouse should satisfy applicable fire-safety and public-access requirements, including emergency exits, extinguishers, fire detection, compartmentation, access for emergency services, and storage-risk controls. Structural capacity and floor loading must be verified for cathode and pallet loads. The employer must complete occupational-risk assessment, prevention measures, worker training, personal protective equipment, handling procedures, and workplace inspections. A formal fire or occupational clearance is conditional on the premises classification and does not replace any required building, ICPE, or local-authority approval.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital is required; EUR 1 is generally sufficient. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des depots before filing, and the deposit certificate must be submitted. The blocked funds are released after issuance of the registration extract (Kbis).

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sector-specific industrial licensing board applies to ordinary wholesale trading of Grade A refined copper cathodes. For storage facilities or environmentally regulated installations, the competent authority is the relevant Regional Directorate for Environment, Planning and Housing (Direction regionale de l'environnement, de l'amenagement et du logement; DREAL), or DRIEAT in the Paris region.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) is the French tax administration responsible for corporate income tax, VAT, and withholding taxes. Its digital portal is impots.gouv.fr and the dedicated professional online portal is www.impots.gouv.fr. The Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) handles customs controls and export/import declarations, and collaborates with the DGFiP for export zero-rating evidence.

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL incorporated by foreign shareholders, the legal representative completes the single online registration at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique). The registration dossier triggers the issuance by INSEE of a SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit), and the Tax Office automatically allocates the VAT intra-community number (FR+11 digits). A foreign entity not yet registered in France but making taxable supplies must apply directly to the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) using Form Cerfa n°1547-M, accompanied by statutory documents (constitutive documents, identity of directors, evidence of economic activity). Non-EU entities are generally required to appoint a tax representative approved by the tax authority before registration is effective.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Under Article 239 bis AA of the French General Tax Code (CGI), a small SARL with fewer than 50 employees and annual turnover below certain thresholds may elect to be transparent for income tax purposes (i.e., taxed under impôt sur le revenu as an entity for five fiscal years). Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD are mandatory; Article 212 ter CGI disallows deduction of payments involving hybrid instruments or hybrid entities, with no elective 'choose-your-own' classification. A SARL can alternatively be treated as a partnership for income tax purposes only in limited family-run situations, but the default status is corporate tax (IS).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for a French-established taxable person; VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply. The small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base) applies to turnover below €85,000 per year for supplies of goods and €37,500 for services, but such businesses may opt to become VAT-registered. For sellers from another EU Member State to French private consumers, the €10,000 distance-sales threshold applies, after which French VAT must be charged and remitted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of Grade A copper cathodes to customers outside the European Union are zero-rated for VAT under Article 262-I-1° of the CGI, provided that the exporter proves that the goods have been dispatched or transported outside the EU, supports the movement with an export customs declaration (lodged via DELTA/EAS systems), and retains transport documents (bill of lading, CMR or air waybill). Intra-Community supplies to taxable purchasers in another EU Member State are also zero-rated under Article 256 bis CGI if the purchaser is VAT-registered and the goods are physically moved to that State; the supplier must hold a valid EU VAT ID and submit the recapitulative statement (DEB). Since copper cathodes are a commodity, the mere transaction on LME warrants without physical movement is not allowable for export zero-rating; physical export must be substantiated. Services directly related to the export or transport of these goods may also be zero-rated if the recipient is established outside the EU or is an EU trader in the case of intra-community services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (déclaration d'exportation) lodged electronically via DELTA or ECS system
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent transport document such as CMR or waybill) evidencing the movement leaving French and EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT confirmation proving receipt of the export proceeds in France within 180 days
  • Commercial invoice issued by the French supplier and accepted by the non-EU or EU customer
  • Packing list for the copper cathode consignments

DGFiP will phase in mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting in France. Obligated companies must use a certified Dematerialization Partner Platform (PDP) or the public Chorus Pro portal. The current timetable is September 1, 2026 for large and mid-sized companies and September 1, 2027 for small and micro-enterprises. Invoices must be submitted in structured formats (Factur-X, UBL or compatible formats) and transaction data (supplier/purchaser VAT IDs, invoice dates and amounts, payment terms) are reported to DGFiP in near real-time. France does not use pre-approval digital stamping; it operates a 'continuous transaction controls' model with mandatory e-reporting of all business-to-business and business-to-consumer transactions.

French customs and tax authorities apply strict cross-checking between the export declaration (DELTA), the transport documents and the export proceeds ledger. If the zero-rating is successfully challenged, the company is liable for the unpaid VAT plus late interest and penalties up to 80% for fraud (manoeuvres frauduleuses). Customs penalties for false export declarations can reach 1.5 to 3 times the evaded duties/taxes, and the Douanes may also impose customs detention and administrative fines. The controller may retrospectively disallow input VAT recovered in relation to the export if the documentation is incomplete or if the transaction is reclassified as a domestic or intra-union supply without reimbursement. Failure to provide evidence within the six-year records retention period or inability to demonstrate actual payment repatriation can lead to accounts reassessment, tax liens, and professional interdiction in serious fraud cases.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full legal and practical convertibility of the euro (EUR) and all major currencies, including the US Dollar (USD), for both current-account and capital-account transactions. No French exchange-control authorisation is required to open a USD account, convert export proceeds into EUR, remit dividends or interest to non-resident shareholders, repay intra-group loans, or repatriate liquidation proceeds. Eurozone membership and the EU capital-mobility guarantees under Article 63 TFEU effectively eliminate any material distinction between current and capital account convertibility for a French SARL. The residual regulatory burdens are procedural and statistical: (i) resident companies carrying out material cross-border operations must file balance-of-payments and external-position declarations with the Banque de France under the SAFE reporting system when annual external flows cross the applicable de minimis thresholds; (ii) loans from non-resident shareholders must be declared as external indebtedness to the Banque de France; and (iii) foreign investment in a French company operating in a sensitive sector may require prior approval under the IEF procedure, a regime that does not apply to wholesale copper-cathode trading. Profit remittance and dividend repatriation are freely transferable in the currency of distribution; taxation under the French General Tax Code and double-tax treaties is separate from, and does not condition, the foreign-exchange transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender ratio, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no requirement to convert export proceeds into EUR or deposit them with a French bank. A copper-cathode trading SARL may retain 100% of export proceeds in USD or any convertible currency indefinitely and may deploy those proceeds directly to fund upstream purchases, post hedge collateral, or service shareholder obligations. The only obligations are declarative and documentary: (i) monthly or quarterly balance-of-payments and external-financial-position declarations to the Banque de France when annual goods or financial flows cross the SAFE reporting thresholds, commonly observed at EUR 15 million of annual external goods flows for active trading entities; (ii) Intrastat declarations for intra-EU acquisitions and supplies, plus DGDDI customs export declarations and the EU Customs Security Filing for physical shipments; and (iii) the mandatory transmission of full originator and beneficiary information under the EU Funds Transfer Regulation in every SWIFT payment. No ministry approval, surrender certificate, or FX conversion receipt is required for the export of Grade A refined copper cathodes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting in coordination with the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) for banking and prudential supervision, the Direction Générale du Trésor within the French Ministry of Economy and Finance for balance-of-payments and foreign-investment screening, and the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) for customs and trade-statistics requirements. The legal bases are the Code monétaire et financier (CMF), Regulation (EU) No 648/2012 (EMIR), the EU anti-money-laundering framework implemented in the CMF, and the free-movement-of-capital provisions of Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union.
