Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry), acting through the national Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10, L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL provisions), and R.210-1 to R.223-1; Decree No. 84-406 of 30 April 1984 on the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts Constitutifs (Articles of Association) together with the Formulaire M0 – Déclaration de Constitution de Société; submitted electronically through the INPI Guichet Unique formalities portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under Article L.223-2 of the Code de commerce, the SARL has no statutory minimum share capital; the capital is freely fixed in the Articles of Association. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and, if the Articles so provide, at least one-fifth of each cash contribution must be paid up at formation (Article L.223-7), with the balance payable within five years. Contributions in kind must be fully transferred and released at incorporation. Before registration, cash capital is deposited in a blocked account with a bank or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the blocking certificate is issued for the registry and the funds are released only after the Kbis registration certificate is obtained.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) or an approved commercial safety body; structural load inspection by a certified engineer for the floor areas supporting rotocasting ovens and airbrush booths; workplace health and safety clearance under Articles R.4212-1 et seq. of the Code du travail, including adequate ventilation, chemical storage, personal protective equipment, first-aid provision, and emergency evacuation procedures.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), through the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE), with online services provided through impots.gouv.fr. Corporate formation and business identification are filed through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is incorporated through the INPI Guichet unique. The filing transmits the entity data to the Registre national des entreprises and the DGFiP. The entity receives a SIREN number for the legal entity, a SIRET number for each establishment, and a French VAT identification number in the format FR plus a two-digit key and the nine-digit SIREN when it is liable for VAT. The SARL must identify its competent SIE, provide constitutional documents, registered-office evidence, activity details, beneficial-owner information, bank details where requested, and expected turnover and VAT regime. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the DGFiP non-established business VAT service or the competent SIE, submits proof of foreign registration, tax residence, business activity, representatives and transactions, and obtains a French VAT number where French VAT registration is required. An EU-established business generally does not appoint a fiscal representative in France; a non-EU-established business generally must appoint an accredited French fiscal representative unless a statutory exception applies. EORI registration is separately required for customs activity through the French customs administration.

The filing transmits the entity data to the Registre national des entreprises and the DGFiP. EORI registration is separately required for customs activity through the French customs administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by default. A qualifying SARL may elect for income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu), including the family-company regime where all shareholders are qualifying family members and statutory conditions are satisfied. Certain newly formed qualifying companies may elect temporary income-tax treatment for up to five financial years. The election is subject to eligibility, unanimity and filing deadlines and is not an unrestricted United States-style check-the-box election. France applies mandatory anti-hybrid-mismatch rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework; no general elective hybrid-mismatch classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A French-established SARL carrying out VAT-taxable transactions must generally register and account for VAT; there is no general safe harbor that eliminates registration for a taxable French establishment. The domestic franchise en base de TVA may apply below the applicable turnover thresholds, generally EUR 25,000 annual turnover for the principal threshold and EUR 27,500 for the tolerance threshold under the 2025 framework, subject to activity-specific thresholds, legislative changes and the detailed date-of-cessation rules. Non-established businesses making taxable supplies in France generally have no general domestic turnover threshold and may require registration from the first taxable transaction, subject to reverse-charge, OSS and other statutory exceptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with a right to deduct input VAT, provided the supplier is the exporter or acts within an accepted export structure, the goods actually leave the EU customs territory, the transaction is correctly invoiced and recorded, and the supplier retains reliable proof of exit. The principal evidence is the electronic customs exit result or equivalent customs certification linked to the export declaration. Intra-EU supplies of goods are generally exempt with the right to deduct when the customer has a valid EU VAT number, the goods move to another Member State and EC Sales List and transport evidence requirements are met; they are not exports. Services supplied to a business established outside France may be outside the scope of French VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule rather than technically zero-rated. B2C services and services connected with goods, events, transport, immovable property or intellectual property require transaction-specific place-of-supply analysis. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, proper accounting and proof that the underlying transactions support a right of deduction.

  • Electronic customs export declaration filed through the applicable French customs system
  • Electronic proof of exit or customs exit certification, including the export movement reference where available
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, VAT treatment and delivery terms
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, airway bill, consignment note, carrier confirmation or delivery confirmation
  • Packing list and product description sufficient to reconcile the shipment with the customs declaration
  • EORI details and exporter's customs records
  • Proof of payment and customer documentation where relevant
  • For refund claims: VAT returns, purchase invoices, accounting ledger extracts and reconciliation of the VAT credit

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through accredited Partner Dematerialisation Platforms and the public directory. From 1 September 2026, all businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; large businesses and intermediate-sized enterprises must generally issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from that date. Small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises must generally issue them from 1 September 2027. Electronic reporting applies to specified B2C and cross-border transactions. France uses a platform-based exchange and reporting model rather than universal real-time invoice clearance. Invoices must preserve authenticity of origin, integrity of content and legibility. There is no general digital tax stamp requirement for ordinary French VAT invoices. Customs export declarations are filed electronically through the French customs systems, including DELTA IE where applicable.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline of 180 days for export proceeds. Export documentation must nevertheless support the reality, value, destination and payment terms of the transaction, and customs, banking and anti-money-laundering records must be retained. If export VAT exemption is unsupported or falsely claimed, the DGFiP may reassess output VAT, deny related input-VAT deductions or refunds, charge late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month, and impose penalties generally ranging from 10% for specified filing or payment failures to 40% for deliberate non-compliance and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abusive concealment, subject to statutory conditions and proportionality. Incorrect refund claims may be clawed back with interest and penalties. Customs breaches may generate separate customs duties, VAT, administrative penalties and seizure consequences. Deliberate tax fraud may also result in criminal prosecution, with statutory imprisonment and fines that can be increased for aggravated or organized fraud. The applicable assessment period depends on the breach and fraud classification; ordinary VAT reassessment periods and extended periods for serious or concealed fraud must be analyzed for the specific case.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Euro (EUR) is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions. France is an EU Member State applying Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) Article 63 and Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF) Articles L151-1 to L151-4, guaranteeing unrestricted capital movements. No exchange-control license, no prior central-bank approval and no repatriation quota apply to trade payments, service fees, equity contributions, loans or dividend distributions. Profit remittance (SARL dividend distribution) is legally unrestricted but requires: (i) approval by an ordinary general meeting; (ii) statutory audited accounts where size thresholds are met; (iii) withholding tax at the default rate of 25% (reduced to 0% for EU/EEA corporate parents benefiting from the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, or further reduced under an applicable double tax treaty); and (iv) certification that the SARL has positive distributable reserves. The only material operational constraint is the mandatory monthly statistical declaration to Banque de France of any cross-border transaction exceeding EUR 12,500 (or FX equivalent). The manufacturing scope (ISIC 3240 / NAF APE code 3240Z, designer blind-box vinyl toys) is not a sensitive sector under the French foreign-investment control (Décret n°2019-1590), therefore no IEF (Investissements Étrangers en France) prior-authorization filing is triggered for foreign direct investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes NO statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, NO mandatory conversion ratio and NO forced repatriation timeline for export receipts. The SARL may lawfully retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign-currency denominated accounts (comptes en devises) with an ACPR-licensed bank, or convert freely into EUR at market rates. Residual compliance obligations are limited to: (1) monthly reporting in the Banque de France SIT system of all cross-border receipts and payments above the EUR 12,500 de minimis threshold (reports due by the 15th of the following month); (2) full adherence to EU/UN/OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control programs implemented via EU Regulations) sanctions screening; (3) transfer-pricing documentation where export sales are made to related parties; and (4) documentary archiving of customs declarations (DAU/DOC-DEC) evidencing the export underlying each foreign-currency receipt.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (operational central bank arm of the Eurosystem headed by the European Central Bank); foreign-exchange (FX) policy and capital-flow law administered by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy and Finance); prudential supervision and AML/CFT enforcement delegated to ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution); national transaction-reporting system for balance-of-payments operated by Banque de France via the SIT portal (Système d'Information sur les Transactions).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for day-to-day EUR operational banking, e-commerce merchant settlement (Shopify Payments, Stripe, Local POS), expense cards and cross-border payment execution; LOW and NOT RECOMMENDED for capital escrow, documentary credit (LC) issuance, material-term deposit holding or complex trade-finance structures. Legal constraints: under PSD2 and CMF Article L521-1, only credit institutions licensed by ACPR or payment/e-money institutions passported from an EU/EEA home state may lawfully provide payment accounts to the French SARL. Non-EU digital EMIs (United Kingdom, Switzerland, Hong Kong, Singapore) have lost EU passporting rights and are de facto BLOCKED from onboarding a French corporate client unless they establish an EU/EEA licensed subsidiary. EMIs are not deposit-taking institutions: client balances do not benefit from the FGDR (Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution) deposit guarantee and overdraft facilities are prohibited. Recommended viable EU EMIs for auxiliary treasury: Qonto (cross-border EUR/GBP/USD accounts), Manager.one, Shine, Lydia Solutions, and iBanFirst (specialist in FX-forward, multi-currency treasury flows for French SMEs).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: (1) execute inbound capital contributions exclusively by SWIFT MT103 from non-EU shareholders or SEPA Credit Transfer from EU/EEA shareholders, directed to the SARL's ACPR-licensed bank account using the company's BIC+IBAN; (2) include in Field 70 the precise purpose: 'Capital Increase / Apport en capital — SARL [name] — SIREN [number] — Resolution dated dd/mm/yyyy'; (3) for USD or CNY(CNH) denominated wires, instruct the receiving bank to convert via its Paris FX desk during the 09:00-17:00 CET window, requiring a confirmed spot rate and SWIFT MT300 confirmation; (4) apply IEF pre-authorization check: none required because ISIC 3240 (designer vinyl toy manufacturing) is outside the List of sensitive activities of Décret n°2019-1590; (5) after credit, file the Banque de France SIT declaration within the statutory calendar month if the single transaction or aggregated monthly cross-border flow exceeds EUR 12,500; (6) retain in the corporate file the banking order, statement, equity subscription agreement, resolution of partners, and updated registre des décisions for the statutory auditor; (7) do NOT route funds through private digital wallets, crypto exchanges, or third-party intermediaries, as such indirect transfers trigger automatic enhanced due diligence and possible account freezing.Clearing rails for the manufacturing workflow: (1) EU/EEA suppliers (packaging, paints, machinery parts): SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst) using IBAN routing; Legal constraints: under PSD2 and CMF Article L521-1, only credit institutions licensed by ACPR or payment/e-money institutions passported from an EU/EEA home state may lawfully provide payment accounts to the French SARL. Non-EU digital EMIs (United Kingdom, Switzerland, Hong Kong, Singapore) have lost EU passporting rights and are de facto BLOCKED from onboarding a French corporate client unless they establish an EU/EEA licensed subsidiary. EMIs are not deposit-taking institutions: client balances do not benefit from the FGDR (Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution) deposit guarantee and overdraft facilities are prohibited.
