Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), France's national tax authority, operating through the professional portal impots.gouv.fr (Espace Abonné / Espace Professionnel); customs export clearance is separately handled by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via the DELTA-TI e-clearance platform and douane.gouv.fr; company formalities for the SARL are centralized on the INPI-operated Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

For the French SARL: (i) file statutory incorporation through the Guichet Unique / Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce; INSEE then issues SIREN (9 digits) and SIRET (14 digits); (ii) the fiscal registration annex is transferred automatically to the territorial Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE), which opens the corporate income tax (IS) account and issues the intra-Community VAT identification number (format FR + 11 characters); (iii) foreign entities establishing a French permanent establishment (PE) or branch file a parallel RCS/branch registration and obtain their own SIREN/SIRET and French VAT number; (iv) non-resident entities with no French PE but direct French VAT liabilities use the dedicated Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) registration procedure and, if established outside the EU, must appoint an accredited tax representative (représentant fiscal). Registration is free of charge and generally completed within 1–3 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope — a SARL is by default subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) under Article 206-I CGI at the standard rate of 25% (reduced IS rate of 15% on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs fulfilling the ≤€10 million turnover condition); France does not accord pass-through status to SARLs by default, and the territoriality principle of Article 209-I CGI limits IS to profits derived from activities carried on in France, including French PE profits, rather than taxing the SARL's unremitted foreign-branch income. — For the French SARL: (i) file statutory incorporation through the Guichet Unique / Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce; INSEE then issues SIREN (9 digits) and SIRET (14 digits); (ii) the fiscal registration annex is transferred automatically to the territorial Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE), which opens the corporate income tax (IS) account and issues the intra-Community VAT identification number (format FR + 11 characters); (iii) foreign entities establishing a French permanent establishment (PE) or branch file a parallel RCS/branch registration and obtain their own SIREN/SIRET and French VAT number; (iv) non-resident entities with no French PE but direct French VAT liabilities use the dedicated Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) registration procedure and, if established outside the EU, must appoint an accredited tax representative (représentant fiscal). Registration is free of charge and generally completed within 1–3 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope — a SARL is by default subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) under Article 206-I CGI at the standard rate of 25% (reduced IS rate of 15% on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs fulfilling the ≤€10 million turnover condition); France does not accord pass-through status to SARLs by default, and the territoriality principle of Article 209-I CGI limits IS to profits derived from activities carried on in France, including French PE profits, rather than taxing the SARL's unremitted foreign-branch income.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in France. Available elections: (1) SARL transparency election under Article 239 bis AA CGI — a qualifying SARL (capital not listed, at least 50% held directly or indirectly by individuals or by similarly qualifying SARLs, no institutional corporate majority) may opt, for a non-renewable or renewable 5-fiscal-year period, to be taxed as a société de personnes (impôt sur le revenu) instead of IS; (2) VAT option under Article 293 B CGI to waive the small-business franchise en base de TVA so the SARL can invoice French VAT and recover input VAT despite being below the mandatory registration threshold; (3) French fiscal integration election under Article 223 A CGI where a French-resident parent holding ≥95% consolidates the SARL into a single IS group; (4) hybrid mismatch rules under Article 212 bis CGI (ATAD 2 transposition) are mandatory and residual rather than elective, disallowing deductions for hybrid-instrument and hybrid-entity arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA: compulsory registration is triggered by annual turnover above €37,500 for service providers (SCADA/PLC integration is a services activity) and above €91,900 for sellers of goods, with reduced/tolerance thresholds of €34,400 for services and €84,900 for goods; where the applicable threshold is exceeded in a calendar year, French VAT registration becomes mandatory with effect from the day of excess; a taxable person below threshold may nonetheless elect to register; non-resident suppliers of pure B2C remote/internet services have no French registration threshold and fall within the OSS/guichet unique reporting mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: 0% TVA applies under Article 262-I CGI where goods physically leave EU customs territory and the SARL (or a commissioned agent acting in its name) holds a validated customs export declaration and transport documents proving exit from the European Union. Services exports: B2B services supplied to a non-EU taxable customer — including remote SCADA programming, licences, design and off-site configuration of PLCs for foreign grids — have their place of supply outside France under Article 259-2 CGI / Article 44 of Directive 2006/112/EC and are accordingly invoiced without French VAT; services physically connected to immovable property (for example on-site commissioning of a substation located in France) are taxable at 20% under Article 259-1 CGI regardless of the foreign customer's residence, while services connected to a non-EU substation follow the situs rule and escape French VAT; intra-EU B2B services are outside French scope and are submitted to the customer's reverse charge upon VIES validation.

  • Customs Export Declaration — the SAD/DAE (Déclaration d'Échange de Biens) formally lodged on the DGDDI's DELTA-TI electronic clearance system and evidenced by the accepted Movement Reference Number (MRN) confirming exit from EU customs territory
  • Commercial transport documentation — bill of lading, air waybill, CMR or rail consignment note showing physical exportation to the non-EU destination, together with the commercial invoice, packing list and SCADA delivery/installation acceptance certificate
  • Financial remittance evidence — SWIFT MT103 or bank-account credit statement tracing receipt of the export consideration from the non-EU buyer; French law does not issue an Inward Remittance Certificate, but DGFiP requires banking corroboration on audit to confirm the commercial substance of the export

France does not operate a pre-issuance real-time fiscal clearance or digital-stamping regime. The mandatory e-invoicing/e-reporting reform applies from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small enterprises: structured B2B e-invoices (Factur-X/UBL or PPF-native formats) must be transmitted to the buyer and to the DGFiP's Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the public API; e-reporting of transaction and payment data likewise feeds the PPF database; although invoices are not statutorily pre-authorized, the DGFiP performs near-real-time automated matching and post-issuance audit reconciliation, and export invoices must be supported by the export documentary chain where a French VAT liability is displaced by the 0%/outside-scope rule.

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline; the export declaration must, however, be followed by physical exit of the goods within the 90-day window of Article 279 UCC / Article 197 of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/2446, failing which the DAE is invalidated and the 0% treatment collapses. On a DGFiP contrôle fiscal, the full export chain — customs release, transport movement, commercial contract and payment trace — is scrutinized; insufficient or contradictory evidence causes the supply to be reclassified as domestic at 20% TVA, with retroactive clawback of input VAT recovered on goods, software or subcontracting costs consumed in the false export. Penalties range from 5%/10% late-filing increases, 40% for bad-faith understatement (Article 1729 CGI) and 80% for fraud or abuse of right, plus 0.2% monthly late-payment interest under Article 1731-A CGI; deliberate falsification of export justifications additionally risks criminal tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI (fine up to €500,000 and up to 5 years' imprisonment) and customs penalties under the Code des Douanes, with recovery window extended up to 10 years in non-disclosure scenarios.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and operates within the euro area single-currency and single-payment framework. The euro is freely convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, prudential, and foreign-investment-control rules. A French SARL may generally receive foreign equity, shareholder loans, trade receipts, dividends, interest, royalties, and other legitimate cross-border payments without a general exchange-control approval. Profit remittances require distributable profits, properly approved financial statements, compliance with statutory reserve and corporate-law requirements, withholding-tax analysis, beneficial-owner and sanctions screening, and appropriate transfer-pricing support. Payments to related parties may require arm's-length pricing, documentation, and treaty analysis. French banks may request underlying contracts, invoices, board or shareholder resolutions, tax evidence, source-of-funds evidence, and economic-purpose explanations before execution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement for exporters to surrender foreign-currency export proceeds to the Banque de France, no general mandatory conversion ratio, and no universal statutory deadline requiring conversion into euros. Export receipts may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted commercially at the company's discretion. The company must maintain accurate accounting and tax records, satisfy bank reporting and payment-screening requirements, comply with applicable customs and VAT rules, and respond to statistical or balance-of-payments data requests. Restricted-country, sanctioned-party, cash, and unusual-payment rules may impose separate controls. Export proceeds should be reconciled to contracts, customs documentation, invoices, shipping evidence, and customer receipts.

Central bank supervision: European Central Bank, with Banque de France as the French national central bank and competent authority for relevant payment, balance-of-payments, statistical, and banking-system functions. Foreign-exchange and financial-crime oversight also involves the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty, the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution, the Autorité des Marchés Financiers, and TRACFIN.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational payments and collections, but not a complete substitute for a French commercial-bank relationship. An EU-licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, SEPA transfers, cards, foreign-exchange services, and limited collection functionality under its passporting, safeguarding, and product permissions. It generally cannot provide ordinary deposit-taking, a statutory capital-deposit certificate unless expressly accepted for that purpose, or the full range of French bank credit, overdraft, documentary-credit, guarantee, and project-finance services. Offshore digital EMIs outside the EU require an appropriate French or EU authorization or local regulated partner and may face enhanced correspondent-bank, sanctions, source-of-funds, data, and tax-residency scrutiny. Use of an EMI should be limited to documented operating flows after confirming safeguarding, settlement, insolvency, access to SEPA, permitted currencies, account-closure procedures, and contractual governing law.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route euro capital contributions through SEPA Credit Transfer where the originating and receiving institutions participate; use SWIFT cross-border transfer where required for non-SEPA currencies or banks. Fund the SARL's capital-deposit account only from the subscribing shareholder or clearly documented contributor, using the exact legal name of the company or subscriber and a reference such as 'share capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan' consistent with the underlying agreement. For a new SARL, obtain the bank or notary capital-deposit certificate, complete incorporation, and transfer funds to the operational account after registration in accordance with the bank's release procedure. For later injections, use a documented equity increase, additional paid-in contribution, current-account advance, or loan, supported by corporate approvals and accounting entries. The bank may request subscription agreements, resolutions, source-of-funds evidence, UBO information, remitter identification, and economic-purpose documentation. There is no general French FX purpose-code regime equivalent to a mandatory Chinese-style foreign-exchange code; banks instead use payment messages, internal categories, invoice references, and regulatory reporting data. Do not split transfers to avoid controls, route funds through unrelated third parties, or describe a loan as equity or vice versa. Reconcile bank statements, general-ledger entries, shareholder accounts, and statutory filings.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the scheme limits and bank availability. Use SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly represented by an MT103 legacy message or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for non-SEPA payments, foreign currencies, and correspondent-bank routes. Use documentary letters of credit subject to UCP 600, standby letters of credit or demand guarantees under applicable ICC rules, documentary collections under URC 522, and bank guarantees for equipment, construction, performance, advance-payment, or warranty obligations. Match each payment to the purchase order, contract, invoice, customs or shipping record where relevant, beneficiary UBO, sanctions screening, tax treatment, and Incoterms allocation. For imports of industrial automation equipment, retain technical descriptions, origin documents, export-control classification, end-use information, transport evidence, and VAT/customs records. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for operational payments and collections, but not a complete substitute for a French commercial-bank relationship. An EU-licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, SEPA transfers, cards, foreign-exchange services, and limited collection functionality under its passporting, safeguarding, and product permissions. It generally cannot provide ordinary deposit-taking, a statutory capital-deposit certificate unless expressly accepted for that purpose, or the full range of French bank credit, overdraft, documentary-credit, guarantee, and project-finance services. Offshore digital EMIs outside the EU require an appropriate French or EU authorization or local regulated partner and may face enhanced correspondent-bank, sanctions, source-of-funds, data, and tax-residency scrutiny. Use of an EMI should be limited to documented operating flows after confirming safeguarding, settlement, insolvency, access to SEPA, permitted currencies, account-closure procedures, and contractual governing law.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Qualifying small and medium-sized companies may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, with the excess taxed at 25%, if annual turnover excluding tax does not exceed EUR 10 million, capital is fully paid up, and at least 75% of the capital is held by individuals or qualifying companies meeting the ownership condition.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic withholding rate on dividends paid to non-residents is generally 25%, subject to exemptions, applicable tax treaties, and the recipient’s status. The domestic rate may differ for specified recipients or circumstances. A qualifying EU parent may obtain exemption under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to ownership, holding-period, beneficial-entitlement, anti-abuse, and other conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the filing deadline is the second business day following 1 May, subject to the applicable DGFiP electronic-filing extension. The IS balance is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month after year-end; for a 31 December year-end, ordinarily 15 May. Instalments are generally due on 15 February, 15 May, 15 August, and 15 November.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The principal full-documentation threshold is generally EUR 400 million: it applies to qualifying French entities or French permanent establishments meeting the statutory turnover or gross-asset threshold, and to certain entities belonging to groups meeting that threshold. French transfer-pricing documentation requirements may also apply to entities with annual turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 150 million, or to entities meeting specified control or group-relationship conditions. Form 2257-SD summary reporting applies to qualifying entities under its separate statutory conditions. Thresholds and filing scope must be tested against current French law and group facts.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime for ordinary SARLs. Nevertheless, French tax law applies substance-over-form, abuse-of-law, anti-hybrid, beneficial-ownership, permanent-establishment, and anti-abuse principles. The SARL should maintain genuine governance, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or operational resources appropriate to its functions, decision-making evidence, and contemporaneous records supporting its contractual allocation of functions, assets, and risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax fraud and false corporate tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, late-payment interest, and surcharges that may reach 80% in specified cases; criminal tax fraud may be punished by up to five years’ imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, with higher penalties possible in aggravated cases under the French Tax Code and Penal Code.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly participates may face personal criminal penalties, including imprisonment and fines. A court may order confiscation and other ancillary sanctions. Personal civil liability or recovery from a director is not automatic and requires a statutory basis or proof of the relevant misconduct; veil piercing is exceptional.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Taxable-profit adjustments, interest, and applicable tax penalties; failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation may trigger a penalty generally equal to the greater of EUR 10,000 or 0.5% of the transactions covered by the missing or incomplete documentation, subject to statutory limits and applicable assessment rules.

Director personal liability: The SARL is ordinarily liable for corporate tax and penalties. Directors may incur personal criminal or civil exposure if they knowingly organize fraud, conceal records, or satisfy statutory liability conditions; ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments do not by themselves pierce the corporate veil.
Failure to remit withholding tax

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unpaid withholding, interest, and applicable penalties. Treaty relief applied without sufficient eligibility evidence may be denied, with the unpaid balance recoverable from the payer under the applicable rules.

Director personal liability: The company is generally the withholding agent. Personal liability is not automatic but may arise under applicable tax or criminal provisions where a director knowingly directs or participates in non-remittance or fraudulent conduct.
Undeclared foreign accounts, assets, or arrangements

Statutory sanction: Penalties depend on the specific disclosure duty and facts and may include fixed fines, tax reassessment, interest, and enhanced fraud surcharges. Deliberate concealment may lead to criminal tax-fraud proceedings.

Director personal liability: A director or representative who knowingly authorizes concealment may face personal penalties under the relevant tax or criminal provisions. Liability depends on proof and the particular statutory disclosure obligation.
Money laundering and proceeds of crime

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is punishable under French criminal law by up to five years’ imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with higher penalties for aggravated forms; confiscation and other ancillary sanctions may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and other individuals who knowingly participate may be prosecuted personally and may face imprisonment, fines, and confiscation. Corporate criminal liability may also apply where the offense is committed on the company’s behalf by an organ or representative.
Corporate asset misuse and insolvency-related misconduct

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include repayment, damages, tax reassessments, insolvency proceedings, management bans, and criminal penalties where the conduct constitutes an offense such as misuse of corporate assets or bankruptcy-related misconduct.

Director personal liability: A director may face civil liability, an insolvency-related contribution to the shortfall, a management ban, or criminal liability if statutory elements are established. Personal liability is fact-specific; ordinary limited liability is not automatically displaced.
