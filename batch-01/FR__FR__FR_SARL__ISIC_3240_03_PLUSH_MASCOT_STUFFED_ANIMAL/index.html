Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SARL must have a registered office in France. A commercial domiciliation company may be used if it is properly authorized and the company has a written domiciliation agreement satisfying the French Commercial Code. A director may, subject to statutory conditions and duration limits, use a personal residence as the registered office. A bare postal box or address lacking legally valid premises access is insufficient. For computerized embroidery, ultrasonic seaming and plush-toy OEM activities, a virtual registered office may be acceptable for incorporation and administrative purposes, but it does not replace premises, equipment, staff, inventory controls, product-safety records, customs records or operational substance where those functions are actually performed in France. The company must comply with applicable EU and French toy-safety, product-safety, labeling, traceability, consumer-protection, VAT, customs and environmental obligations. No general sector incorporation license is assumed, but product compliance and importer or manufacturer obligations must be separately verified.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum SARL capital is imposed; the share capital is fixed freely in the articles. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and at least one-fifth (20%) of each cash contribution must be paid up at formation, with the resulting funds deposited into a blocked bank account or held by a notary until the RCS registration is obtained. In-kind contributions must be fully paid and transferred on incorporation; unpaid cash balances may be called by the gérant within five years.

Funding & unlock path: For premises open to the public, fire safety approval by the commission de sécurité is required. For the production workshop, compliance with Code du travail requirements on fire exits, electrical installations, ventilation, first aid and building structural load shall be confirmed by the building owner or a licensed structural engineer.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) acting through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for income tax and VAT; company registration with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the commercial court clerk (Greffe), now centralised through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL: (1) file articles of association and legal representative details with the RCS via the Guichet Unique; (2) obtain the Kbis extract, which issues the SIREN/SIRET identifiers; (3) the DGFiP automatically allocates the intra-EU VAT number (FR + two-digit key + SIREN) and registers the company for corporate income tax and VAT; (4) separate registration for French VAT is required if the entity is non-established and has no French permanent establishment, submitted to the SIE (or via the European Commission's one-stop-shop portal) using the French VAT registration form; (5) if importing/exporting physically, obtain an EORI number from the French Customs (Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SARL: (1) file articles of association and legal representative details with the RCS via the Guichet Unique; (2) obtain the Kbis extract, which issues the SIREN/SIRET identifiers; (3) the DGFiP automatically allocates the intra-EU VAT number (FR + two-digit key + SIREN) and registers the company for corporate income tax and VAT; (4) separate registration for French VAT is required if the entity is non-established and has no French permanent establishment, submitted to the SIE (or via the European Commission's one-stop-shop portal) using the French VAT registration form; (5) if importing/exporting physically, obtain an EORI number from the French Customs (Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at entity level. However, certain family SARLs or eligible small-scale SARLs (under CGI article 239 bis AA) may elect to be treated as pass-through partnerships (régime des sociétés de personnes), retaining shareholder-level income taxation. Additionally, a qualifying French group may elect for fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under CGI articles 223 A et seq. Anti-hybrid mismatches are addressed through EU ATAD and French hybrid mismatch rules rather than elective mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No universal turnover-eligibility threshold for mandatory VAT registration: any taxable person carrying out taxable supplies in France must be registered for VAT. For small traders, the 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption applies below €85,000 annual turnover for sale of goods (2024), but that is an exemption from charge rather than a registration threshold. Once turnover exceeds the threshold, registration and VAT collection are compulsory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies under CGI article 262-I-1 where tangible goods (including plush mascots and stuffed animals) are physically transported outside the EU by the supplier, by a customer not established in France, or by a third party acting on their behalf; the goods must be delivered outside the EU and the export must be documented in accordance with the proof-of-export rules (CGI annex I articles 74-0 F to 74-0 J). Services directly tied to exported goods (such as custom embroidery or OEM manufacture for non-EU clients) are generally outside the scope of French VAT where the recipient is a foreign taxable person under B2B place-of-supply rules, often subject to reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration Administrative Unique / DAU with EXIT or ECS confirmation from French Customs)
  • Proof of physical exit from the EU (DELTA exit record / États de suivi de sortie)
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, CMR, AWB or multimodal document showing exporter and EU exit point)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (with clear reference to the export declaration)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank statement demonstrating export proceeds received within the mandatory FX repatriation deadline

France is implementing mandatory structured electronic invoicing in a phased timetable: from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for SMEs and SARLs. All domestic B2B invoices must be transmitted through the national Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified private platform (PDP/OD) using standard formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII). The same phase requires real-time e-reporting of transaction data to the DGFiP, including cross-border supplies covered by the reverse charge. For typical OEM plush-mask/garment manufacturing, no digital stamping or continuous transaction metering is required; however, VAT returns must be filed electronically via the professional tax portal (e.g., Form CA3/CA12 using EDI mode).

If the export zero-rating is challenged or the required documentation is missing, the DGFiP may recharacterise the sale as taxable and assess French VAT at 20%, deny input VAT deduction, and apply late interest (0.20% monthly) plus negligence penalties of 40% or 80% for deliberate intent; fraudulent fictitious export transactions can incur criminal penalties up to €1.5 million fine and imprisonment. Any previously granted VAT export refund or credit may be clawed back as an undue repayment, and non-repatriation of export proceeds within the 180-day legal deadline triggers additional customs and exchange-control penalties under the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is an IMF Article VIII member and the euro is fully convertible for current-account transactions and capital-account transactions within the EU. No prior exchange-control authorisation is required for equity injections, trade credits, profit distributions, or repatriation of branch proceeds, except for transactions with persons listed under EU financial sanctions or with entities domiciled in the non-cooperative jurisdictions named by the EU. Profit remittance from an SARL is subject only to corporate-law conditions, such as approval by the partners, absence of net-asset erosion, and demonstrated liquidity. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive eliminates French withholding tax when an EU parent holds at least 5% of the SARL's capital; for a non-EU treaty parent, treaty withholding tax rates generally range from 0% to 15%. Hurdles are therefore tax compliance and documentary formalities, not FX conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France maintains no mandatory export-proceeds surrender rule and no statutory time-window for converting export receipts. Export proceeds may be freely retained in a foreign-currency account for the commercial credit period, and no conversion ratio or central-bank allocation is imposed. Reporting obligations remain: every flow between a resident and a non-resident must be declared to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics through the reporting bank or directly; physical cash movements of €10,000 or more are separately declarable to customs.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting as the national central bank, with foreign exchange regulatory and balance-of-payments functions exercised jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) at the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary collection accounts, Low for primary treasury rails. Lawful French digital EMIs, such as Qonto and Shine, operate under ACPR licences and provide efficient remote onboarding, SEPA and SWIFT access in EUR, but as monoline payment institutions they lack credit lines, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, do not possess a universal correspondent banking passport for non-EU clearing, and are prohibited from paying interest or executing proprietary foreign-exchange forwards. Offshore EMIs not authorised in the EU are neither viable nor acceptable because they would trigger a negative AML determination from the appointed French correspondent bank.
