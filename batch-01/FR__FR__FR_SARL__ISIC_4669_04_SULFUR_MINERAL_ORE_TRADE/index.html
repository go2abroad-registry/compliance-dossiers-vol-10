Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) within the Ministry of Economy and Finance; principal portal: impots.gouv.fr; statutory business formalities and single declaration portal (Guichet Unique): formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SARL or French branch must first complete legal registration via the Guichet Unique (operated with the INPI) to obtain the INSEE-issued SIREN/SIRET identifiers. VAT registration follows automatically for established entities through the same filing, with the DGFiP local SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) issuing the intra-Community VAT number under format FR + two-digit computer key + SIREN. A non-established foreign entity that imports goods, holds stock in France, or carries out taxable supplies in French territory must file form 10813-SD (Cerfa 15429) with the DGFiP Non-Resident Tax Service, and may be required to appoint a fiscal representative (jointly and severally liable for French VAT) where the DGFiP deems such security necessary; supporting documents include powers of attorney, proof of foreign VAT registration, and commercial contracting evidence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Statutory elections include: (1) the option under Article 206-3 CGI for an SARL with no corporate shareholder to be taxed as a partnership (transparent régime), filed jointly by all associated shareholders; (2) election of the fiscal integration regime (régime d'intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for French groups holding at least 95% of the company capital, allowing the parent to be the sole taxpayer for the group; (3) election between the simplified real VAT regime and the normal real regime (CA3 monthly / CA12 annual); (4) the small-business franchise (franchise en base) election for start-ups with negligible turnover. France has no check-the-box election per se; classification of hybrid entities is determined under the anti-hybrid rules (ATAD transpositions – Articles 209-0 A et seq. CGI) with mandatory filing of the standard corporate return (2015/2065 series).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold relieves a business from VAT registration in France: any taxable person making a taxable supply of goods or services in French territory must register with the SIE from the first transaction. The small-business exemption (franchise en base) under Article 293 B CGI applies only where annual turnover stays below the statutory ceilings (as indexed, approximately €85,800 for goods and €34,400 for services for recent years), but the targeted sulfur mineral ore trading company, being engaged in export zero-rating and domestic logistics purchases, must register immediately and file monthly CA3 VAT returns to claim credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% export rate applies under Article 262-I-1° CGI to sales of movable goods that are shipped or transported out of the EU (including shipments from France by sea to third countries), if the exporter holds conclusive evidence of physical exit within the statutory period: 90 days from the supply for direct exports by sea or air, or up to one year for goods delivered through successive international transports with customs supervision. For dry bulk granular elemental sulfur traded by marine carriage, evidence of loading aboard an ocean vessel and customs export validation (DAU from DELTA-D) will satisfy the zero-rating test. Auxiliary services performed abroad and certain services directly linked to exported goods are zero-rated under Articles 259 A and 262-II CGI. Excess input VAT on purchases (freight, handling, banking) is claimed on the monthly CA3 VAT return as crédit de TVA and refunded by the SIE.

  • Single Administrative Document (DAU) — Customs Export Declaration validated by French Customs (DELTA-D / NCTS) evidencing the EU exit freight bill
  • Bill of Lading (Connaissement or Sea Waybill) naming the French port of loading, the export vessel, and the third-country discharge port
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list reflecting contract terms, INCOTERMS, quantities, and the zero-rated status of the supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank SWIFT MT103 or Letter of Credit negotiation record evidencing payment receipt for the exported cargo
  • Shipper's export manifest or vessel departure certificate (proof of sailing and cargo quantity)
  • Customs valuation and certificate of origin documentation for the sulfur cargo where exit controls or random documentary checks are performed

France has enacted a phased mandatory electronic invoicing and e-reporting regime under Article 289-0 CGI and Articles 109 to 193 of Annex II to the CGI. From 1 September 2026, all taxable persons must be able to receive e-invoices, and large/mid-sized enterprises must issue e-invoices through an accredited dematerialisation platform (PDP) connected to the Public Invoicing Portal (PPF); from 1 September 2027, all SMEs (including SARLs) must issue structured e-invoices (FacturX, UBL, or CII) with real-time e-reporting of transaction data to the DGFiP. Each invoice requires the SIREN number, an electronic signature/unique invoice identifier, and transmission through the PPF; fiscal clearance is effectively achieved by automated DGFiP matching of the e-reporting data with CA3/CA12 declarations and Intrastat filings.

The DGFiP audits export substantiation strictly: insufficiency of documentary proof or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day foreign exchange deadline triggers reassessment at the 20% standard rate on the full sale invoice, denial of input VAT credits, late-payment interest at 0.20% per month, plus the Article 1729 CGI penalties — 40% for negligent under-declaration, 80% for bad faith (mauvaise foi), and up to 100% for abuse of law. Fraudulent zero-rating with fabricated bills of lading or false export declarations carries criminal exposure under Article 1741 CGI (€500,000 fine and five years' imprisonment) and under the Customs Code (fine of one to two times the customs value); the SARL manager (gérant) may be personally liable for the unpaid VAT. Records must be retained for at least six accounting years, and the clawback capacity extends to the full statutory audit limitation period plus the ten-year criminal prescription in cases of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account; TFEU Article 63 guarantees free movement of capital between France, EEA member states and third countries. Profit remittance by the French SARL to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject only to dividend withholding documentation, bank AML/KYC clearance and EU/CFSP sanctions screening. Residual hurdles are limited to: (i) mandatory balance-of-payments reporting by French banks for every resident–non-resident flow of EUR 15,000 or more; (ii) standard know-your-client checks applied to a commodity trader; and (iii) enhanced scrutiny where sulfur cargoes touch high-risk origins such as Russian-origin granulated sulfur or Gulf trans-shipment hubs. No foreign-exchange licence, allocation rationing or repatriation approval applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists; France imposes zero mandatory conversion of export receipts. The SARL may retain 100% of USD or other foreign-currency proceeds in a designated foreign-currency account, whether in France or abroad, and may net those receipts against import payables, freight or other trade liabilities. The only binding duties are statistical reporting (the intermediary French bank files each cross-border transaction of EUR 15,000 or more with Banque de France for balance-of-payments purposes) and AML/CFT documentation of the origin of the proceeds. No conversion ratio is imposed and the exporter retains unrestricted economic use of foreign exchange earnings.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (the French central bank within the Eurosystem); ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) supervises licensed banks, EMIs and payment institutions; Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) implements exchange-control, sanctions and international financial policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: HIGH for treasury execution, LOW as a trade-finance substitute. ACPR-licensed French EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Penta, Wise Business, Revolut Business) may open payment accounts, hold safeguarded client funds, perform FX conversion at reference rates and initiate cross-border payments under PSD2 passporting. However, an EMI cannot by law grant credit, issue or confirm letters of credit, discount trade bills or provide supplier-advance financing; therefore a documentary LC facility and significant clearing balances must remain with a systemic credit institution. Offshore non-EEA digital EMIs, and UK-authorised EMIs post-Brexit, cannot serve a French resident SARL on a cross-border basis without ACPR branch licensing or an EEA passport; French correspondent banks will reject transfers routed through such unregulated intermediaries.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an initial formation deposit, funds are paid to a temporary blocked account (compte bloqué) opened in the SARL's name and are released upon presentation of the Kbis and signed statuts. For a subsequent capital increase, non-EEA shareholders transmit contributions via SWIFT MT103, while EEA shareholders may use SEPA Credit Transfer; the beneficiary line must show the SARL's French IBAN and tax identifiers (SIREN/VAT). The remittance must carry a Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose code designating direct-investment equity participation; the receiving bank files the statistical declaration automatically, but treasury must supply the extraordinary general meeting resolution, the subscription certificate (attestation de dépôt des fonds), the UBO declaration and source-of-funds evidence. Funds are provisionally credited and may be placed on a compliance hold for up to five banking days; if the transferring shareholder is resident in a high-risk state, provide the transferor's bank statement and a written sanctions-compliance opinion from the SARL's counsel.Primary rails: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for EUR payments to EEA counterparties, settled through T2/CORE same-day with a cut-off of 16:00 CET; (2) SWIFT MT103 for USD and other foreign-currency trade disbursements via the French bank's correspondent network, with USD cleared over CHIPS/Fedwire through a New York correspondent; (3) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 and amended via MT707 under UCP600, advised or confirmed by the beneficiary's bank and settled by MT202 or MT103; (4) documentary collections and standby LCs for freight-stage or demurrage payments. Compliance discipline: each payment must map one-to-one to an underlying commercial contract, freight statement or marine insurance invoice; LC presentations must contain a signed commercial invoice, full set of clean on-board bills of lading, certificate of origin, quantity and quality survey certificate and insurance certificate as required, to avoid discrepancy-related demurrage and bank charges.FX Forwards (achat et vente à terme, commonly referenced under the AFB FX master agreement) with tenors up to 24 months, executed through a licensed French credit institutionFX Swaps for short-term rollover and funding liquidity between EUR, USD and freight-payment currenciesCross-Currency Swaps matching committed supply contracts or long-term offtake agreementsVanilla FX Options, collars and participating forwards for seasonal or charter-party freight exposuresNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for origin-country currencies where physical deliverability is restricted but the underlying trade is genuineRegular EUR/USD spot operations executed on the bank's treasury desk and settled through T2

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000 in controlled transactions with related parties (consolidated turnover threshold) OR EUR 400,000,000 in sales/turnover for the multinational group; the French TP documentation obligation (Master File and Local File) is mandatory when the French entity's controlled transactions exceed EUR 50 million or the group surpasses EUR 400 million. Below these thresholds, a simplified TP declaration (Form 2257-SD) is still required if the group meets the thresholds.

Economic substance scope: French jurisprudence (e.g., abusive tax position under Articles L.64 and L.64A of the LPF) requires economic substance, real business purpose, and actual decision-making in France. The SARL must demonstrate active commercial functions, staff, and logistics to avoid being characterised as an artificial arrangement. As France is not an offshore ESR jurisdiction, the OECD BEPS substance standards and the EU anti-tax avoidance directive apply, including the requirement of sufficient substance to access tax treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Undeclared Profit

Statutory sanction: Fiscal penalty of 40% (non-fraud) or 80% (fraud) of additional tax, plus interest at 0.20% per month. Criminal liability: imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to EUR 75,000 for fraudulent tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Court can apply the 'abus de bien social' doctrine to hold directors individually liable for hidden profits. Directors may face personal tax enforcement for penalties, asset seizure, travel bans, and sentence of up to 5 years. A formal declaration of forbidden foreign bank accounts can trigger a EUR 1,500 monthly penalty per non-declared account.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of transactions under Art. 57 of the French Tax Code; the synthetic arrangement is treated as evidence of profit transfer, and any profit foreign-shifted is re-included in the French tax base. Additional 40% penalty (80% if the principal motive is tax avoidance).

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to breach-of-director duties; a court may pierce the veil under Luxembourg/European case law. Directors could be subject to personal liability for the re-integrated taxes, civil restitution of profits, travel limitations, and criminal conviction for tax evasion if intent is proven.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: French Monetary and Financial Code imposes administrative fines up to EUR 5 million (or double the amount laundered) under the French FIU (Tracfin) referral. Criminal penalty: up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are liable under Art. 324-1 of the French Penal Code. Travel bans may be issued in criminal proceedings; a director found to have laundered funds may be prohibited from managing a company for up to 10 years. Personal assets may be seized to recover criminal profits.
Fraudulent Booker / False Trade Documentation

Statutory sanction: Customs and tax fraud penalties: additional tax equal to 100% of the assessed tax, plus fine up to EUR 750,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years in aggravated cases. Forfeiture of shipped goods and vehicles may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for customs and tax penalties; courts may order disqualification from corporate management, seizure of personal bank accounts, travel ban, and imprisonment if the fraud was orchestrated by the director.
Failure to File / Late Payment

Statutory sanction: Procedure for late filing: 10% increase (after automatic filing) or 40% after formal notice; monthly late payment interest 0.20%; mandatory penalty of 0.2% per day on late payments after 5 days. Failure to provide the transfer pricing annex (Form 2257-SD) incurs a penalty of 5% of missing documentation value or a minimum EUR 100,000 for large groups.

Director personal liability: Directors can be jointly liable for tax arrears if they previously profited from the evasion; a travel ban can be ordered in debt collection proceedings. Administrative ban of 3 to 5 years may be imposed in addition to criminal fines.
