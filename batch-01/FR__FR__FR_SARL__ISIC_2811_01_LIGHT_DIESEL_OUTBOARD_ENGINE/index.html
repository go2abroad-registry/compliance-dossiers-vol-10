Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — impots.gouv.fr professional portal 'Espace Professionnel' for corporate income tax and TVA/VAT filings; business incorporation and tax registration formalities via the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI); customs and export controls via the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI / douane.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the SARL through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr): issuance of SIREN/SIRET (INSEE) and automatic allocation of the intra-Community French VAT number (format FR + 14 characters) upon statutory registration. Step 2 — Activate the corporate tax account with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and enrol in the professional 'Espace Pro' for filing corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés) and VAT (TVA). Step 3 — Foreign entities without a French establishment must either (i) register directly with the dedicated SIE des non-résidents, appoint a fiscal representative if established outside the EU, and obtain a direct French VAT number where a French taxable nexus or permanent establishment arises, or (ii) use the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS/IOSS) for distance sales and qualifying B2B services, which avoids full local entity registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a standard commercial SARL is by default subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) under Articles 206(1) and 209 CGI regardless of profit distribution; the entity is a separate taxable person for corporate income tax and VAT, and shareholders are taxed only upon distributions. Flow-through/transparent treatment is reserved to specific opt-in cases (e.g., family SARL under Article 239 bis AA CGI).

Available election forms

French law has no US-style 'check-the-box'; entity classification derives from legal form. Available elections: (1) SARL de famille — option for partnership-style transparency (Article 239 bis AA CGI) where all members are spouses or same-family relatives; (2) option for corporation tax by otherwise transparent partnerships (Article 206(3) CGI) — not relevant for a standard SARL; (3) fiscal integration ('intégration fiscale') election under Article 223 A CGI for groups holding ≥95% subsidiaries; (4) exit-tax deferral election (Article 167 bis CGI). Hybrid mismatch outcomes are non-elective: French ATAD implementation (Article 212 bis CGI) automatically denies deductions for hybrid mismatch transactions; no elective hybrid regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None (EUR 0) — established French suppliers and manufacturers must register for VAT from the first taxable transaction; the only de minimis rule is the EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual distance-sales ceiling (Directive 2006/112/EC) for cross-border EU B2C supplies, above which French VAT must be charged via OSS or local registration; non-established foreign sellers with French B2C nexus must register via the SIE non-résidents or use OSS/IOSS and enjoy no domestic exemption threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Article 262-1 CGI for tangible goods sold by the taxpayer and physically transported or dispatched outside the EU, provided transport is evidenced and goods leave EU territory within 90 days of delivery (administrative tolerance). Article 262 ter CGI zero-rates intra-EU B2B supplies when the purchaser communicates a valid EU VAT number and the EC Sales List obligation is fulfilled. Offshore services are zero-rated only where (i) supplied to a customer established outside the EU, (ii) directly related to exported goods (e.g., transport, handling, processing, repair) under Article 261 C-1-2 CGI, or (iii) place-of-supply rules (Articles 259–259 D CGI) locate the transaction outside French territorial VAT. Domestic sales of light diesel outboard engines remain taxable at 20%.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged via DELTA (Déclaration d'exportation with MRN — Movement Reference Number) evidencing exit from the EU
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air) or CMR (road) proving physical export
  • Commercial invoice (facture commerciale) and packing list matching the customs declaration in value, quantity and tariff classification
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice proving receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency and reconciling to invoice value
  • Export contract, purchase order and Incoterms/insurance evidence (including letter of credit where used) supporting commercial substance

France applies a semi-deferred clearance model (no real-time stamping): under Loi de finances 2023, ordonnance n° 2024-316 and décret n° 2024-708, all domestic B2B invoices must be issued and received in structured electronic form through a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); B2C and international transaction data must be reported electronically through the same channel. Mandatory implementation dates: 1 September 2026 for large companies (grandes entreprises) and mid-caps (ETI), 1 September 2027 for small and medium enterprises (PME/TPE). API integration between accounting/e-invoicing systems and the chosen PDP is required; accepted syntaxes include Factur-X, UBL and CII; no per-invoice fiscal authorisation or digital stamping is applied.

The DGFiP and DGDDI jointly audit export zero-rating claims; where export evidence is missing, false or irreconcilable, the 0% rate is retroactively clawed back and 20% VAT becomes immediately payable, plus: (i) late-payment interest (intérêts de retard) of 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI); (ii) 40% penalty for deliberate understatement (manquement délibéré) or 80% for fraud/abuse of law (Article 1729 CGI); (iii) criminal tax-fraud exposure under Article 1741 CGI — up to 3 years' imprisonment and a EUR 75,000 fine (aggravated to 7 years/EUR 250,000 for organised fraud or laundering); (iv) customs penalties for false export declarations under the Code des Douanes — fines up to half to twice the value of the goods plus confiscation; and (v) extension of the statute-of-limitations to a 10-year re-opening window for undisclosed fraud (Article L.186 Livre des Procédures Fiscales). Supporting records must be retained for at least six years and presented on official demand.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is part of the Economic and Monetary Union; the euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. No exchange-control authorizations are required for ordinary trade, capital injections, or dividend remittances. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are subject solely to applicable corporate withholding tax (EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive reduces to 0% where qualifying; otherwise domestic law withholding up to 30%, often reduced by treaty), and such payments can be executed freely through a bank wire upon approval of the corporate accounts and declaration of the dividend. The current account is fully liberalized under EU Treaty obligations, and capital movements are unrestricted except for a narrow, prior-authorization screening of foreign direct investments in defence/security sectors under Article L151-3 of the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio in France. Exporters may retain and freely manage euro or foreign-currency proceeds, hold foreign-currency clearing accounts, and use them for payments or hedging. The sole obligation is statistical: cross-border transactions must be recorded for Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting, ordinarily through the credit intermediary or via a direct corporate statement for large or significant flows (e.g., direct investment aggregating above the applicable Banque de France reporting threshold).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as the designated central bank and foreign-exchange reporting authority, acting jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for exchange-market policy and with the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial Sovereignty for investment controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for transactional euro clearing and FX payments; Low for core treasury and trade-finance instruments. A PSD2-licensed Electronic Money Institution in the EEA may open a business payment account with an IBAN, execute SEPA/wire-style transfers, and offer limited FX contracts, but it cannot independently issue commercial letters of credit, provide documentary collections, structure cross-currency swaps, or extend liquidity facilities. These products require a fully-regulated credit institution. Legal constraints include requirement under PSD2 to segregate client funds, unavailability of Banque de France refinancing access, and lack of supervision as a credit institution; therefore the SARL's primary treasury architecture should rest on a tier-1 French bank, with an EMI used only for alternative collection/operational flow in low-risk markets.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injection for the French SARL is fully liberalized and does not require prior Banque de France authorization unless the activity falls under Article R151-3 foreign-investment screening (defence/security or sensitive technology). The compliant wire routing is a SWIFT MT103 from the parent corporate account to the SARL's French IBAN, with the payment defined as 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital Contribution' and the transfer instruction including the corporate purpose code equal to 'Foreign Direct Investment' / category 'Equity Injection'. The receiving French bank, as an authorized financial intermediary, records the statistical balance-of-payments (Ifib/FCIR) reporting automatically. Where the aggregate annual direct-investment flow exceeds the Bank's applicable direct reporting threshold (currently EUR 15 million per the French rule), the SARL or its intermediary must file the prescribed Banque de France declaration using form 'Établissement direct' within 20 days of the operation. Verification is made by ensuring the remitter is the real beneficial parent, the amount matches the capital subscription resolution, and the SARL files the capital increase with the RCS and official publication.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements follow: (1) for EUR payments to EEA counterparties, SEPA Credit Transfer; (2) for non-EEA or USD/CNY/other currencies, SWIFT MT103 wire transfers routed through the French bank's correspondent network; (3) for documentary marine-engine deliveries, a commercial Letter of Credit issued by the buyer's bank may be settled under SWIFT MT700/MT710 with presentation of a full documents set including bill of lading, commercial invoice, Fumigation Certificate, Marine Insurance Certificate, engine inspection certificate, and certificate of origin. For significant euro liquidity, TARGET2 is the RTGS clearing rail; SWIFT FIN remains the standard messaging protocol. The SARL should establish a dedicated clearing account at a tier-1 bank, use a single gateway for all outward payments to ensure efficient know-your-counterparty and sanctions screening, and record all cross-border FX flows under the relevant Banque de France statement or via the bank's transaction-reporting facility.Plain vanilla foreign-exchange forwards on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CNH with an authorized French credit institution, limited to documentary underlying trade exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on currencies with convertibility restrictions, permitted where the SARL has a genuine trade or balance-sheet exposure in that currency and where the NDF is entered with an authorised counterparty under EMIR.Cross-currency swaps addressing long-dated EUR/foreign-currency cash-flow mismatches from international contracts, subject to EMIR collateralisation and trade-reporting duties.FX options not used for speculative purposes, but accepted as hedges in a treasury policy memorandum supporting underlying trade commitments.Interest rate derivatives (e.g., EURIBOR/EURDR swap) when paired with cross-currency financing or capital-funding needs of the SARL.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for transactional euro clearing and FX payments; Low for core treasury and trade-finance instruments. A PSD2-licensed Electronic Money Institution in the EEA may open a business payment account with an IBAN, execute SEPA/wire-style transfers, and offer limited FX contracts, but it cannot independently issue commercial letters of credit, provide documentary collections, structure cross-currency swaps, or extend liquidity facilities. These products require a fully-regulated credit institution. Legal constraints include requirement under PSD2 to segregate client funds, unavailability of Banque de France refinancing access, and lack of supervision as a credit institution; therefore the SARL's primary treasury architecture should rest on a tier-1 French bank, with an EMI used only for alternative collection/operational flow in low-risk markets.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Qualifying small and medium-sized enterprises may apply 15% to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to the statutory conditions, including turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up share capital, and at least 75% individual ownership or qualifying ownership.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate shareholder, the domestic withholding rate is generally 25%, subject to treaty reduction or exemption. A qualifying EU parent may obtain exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive and French implementing rules, generally requiring a qualifying minimum holding, holding period, eligible legal form, and subject-to-tax conditions. Non-resident individual shareholders are generally subject to a different domestic rate, commonly 12.8%, subject to applicable rules and treaty relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The IS return is generally due within three months after the fiscal year-end. For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return is ordinarily due on the second business day following 1 May, with the DGFiP electronic-filing extension where applicable. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Generally EUR 400 million in annual turnover or gross assets for the French entity, or specified group-relationship conditions involving an entity meeting that threshold, triggers full transfer-pricing documentation under Article L.13 AA of the French Tax Procedures Code. A separate annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration (Form 2257-SD) generally applies to companies meeting the relevant EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold or belonging to a qualifying group. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. French tax law nevertheless requires transactions and arrangements to reflect economic reality and may deny benefits or recharacterize arrangements under domestic anti-abuse, abuse-of-law, transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, and EU anti-abuse rules. A French SARL should maintain commercially credible French functions, personnel, decision-making, assets, and records consistent with the risks and profits it reports.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion or fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest, and penalties that may reach 80% in aggravated cases; tax fraud is punishable under the French Tax Code and Penal Code, potentially by imprisonment and substantial criminal fines. Enhanced penalties may apply to organized or aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: A director who personally participates in fraud may face criminal prosecution, imprisonment, fines, and professional consequences. Civil recovery against a director is not automatic; statutory conditions or a court finding of fraudulent or serious and repeated tax failures may permit personal recovery. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax sanction.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and inadequate documentation

Statutory sanction: Taxable-profit adjustments, interest, and applicable penalties; failure to provide documentation when legally required may attract the specific penalty generally measured as the greater of EUR 10,000 or 5% of reassessed profits, subject to statutory provisions and procedural requirements.

Director personal liability: The company is ordinarily liable for tax and penalties. Directors may incur personal criminal or civil liability if they knowingly participate in fraud or satisfy statutory conditions for personal liability; ordinary documentation failures do not automatically pierce limited liability.
Improper dividend or payment withholding

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, interest, and applicable penalties. False certificates or sham arrangements may trigger anti-abuse reassessment and, in deliberate cases, criminal proceedings.

Director personal liability: The SARL is generally the withholding agent and primary debtor. A director's personal liability requires an applicable statutory basis or personal participation in wrongdoing; it is not automatic solely because withholding was omitted.
Shell, sham, or conduit arrangement

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities may deny treaty or statutory benefits, recharacterize transactions, reassess tax, and impose interest and penalties. Deliberate fraudulent conduct may also result in criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability if they knowingly implement or facilitate fraud. Limited liability is not displaced merely because the company has limited operations; personal exposure depends on the relevant statutory test and proven conduct.
Anti-money-laundering and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Potential criminal fines, imprisonment, asset confiscation, transaction restrictions, and administrative measures under the applicable French and EU regimes. The specific sanction depends on the offence, intent, amount, and applicable sanctions instrument.

Director personal liability: Directors and other responsible individuals may be prosecuted where they commit, order, or knowingly facilitate an offence. Corporate liability may coexist with individual liability; travel bans are not an automatic consequence.
Failure to maintain accounting records or make required filings

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, loss of unsupported deductions or credits, late-filing or late-payment penalties, interest, and, where facts warrant, accounting or criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal consequences for intentional misconduct or specified serious breaches. Routine company tax debts and filing penalties do not automatically create personal director liability.
