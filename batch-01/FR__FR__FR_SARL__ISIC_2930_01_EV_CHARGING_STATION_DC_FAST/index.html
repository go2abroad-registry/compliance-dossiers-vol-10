Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Clerk of the Commercial Court) of the department of the registered office; receives and rules on the registration (immatriculation) of the company in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the INPI Guichet Unique.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce): Title III of Book II, Articles L. 223-1 to L. 223-43 (SARL), General company provisions at Articles L. 210-1 to L. 210-10; Decrees Nos. 2007-431 (23 March 2007) and 67-236 (23 March 1967); Order of 2 Feb 1998 (ICPE) not applicable to corporate formation.

Formation / constitutional filings

Dossier d'immatriculation au Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), comprising the executed Articles of Association (statuts), the funds-deposit certificate, the registered-office justification, and the mandatory electronic declaration transmitted through the INPI Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (Formalité M0).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for SARL (Article L. 223-1, Code de commerce); the capital is fixed freely in the Articles of Association (at least EUR 1 and in divisible units). For cash contributions (apports en numéraire), at least 20% of the nominal amount must be paid up at subscription (Article L. 223-7, Code de commerce), and the remaining 80% must be paid within 5 years. Funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow bank account (compte bloqué) in the name of the company being formed, or deposited with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the depositary issues a certificat du dépositaire which must be produced for registration, and the funds cannot be released until the company is registered (Article L. 223-10).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) of the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique; taxpayer portal impots.gouv.fr with the professional space 'Espace Professionnel'; business creation and VAT registration filed through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); export and customs clearance administered separately by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via the DELTA-X system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent forms the SARL through the Guichet Unique; DGFiP delivers SIREN, SIRET, and the intra-Community VAT number (format FR followed by the 11-digit SIREN and a two-digit control key) at incorporation. A non-established EU/EEA company supplying goods or services in France must complete the non-resident VAT registration procedure using Form Cerfa n° 14373 (Demande d'immatriculation TVA) and obtains a French FR-VAT number before making any taxable supply. A third-country (non-EU) entity without an EU establishment is subject to the 'service des impôts des non-résidents' procedure and must appoint a DGFiP-approved fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) for VAT unless a mutual administrative assistance treaty grants a dispensation. The export entity must separately obtain an EORI number (format FR + SIREN) through the DGDDI electronic registration portal before lodging export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in France; classification follows the legal form in the Code Général des Impôts. The SARL may, however, elect: (i) income-tax passthrough treatment as a 'SARL de famille' under CGI Art. 239bis AA where all partners are natural persons within the same family, binding for five years; (ii) inclusion in a VAT group ('assujetti unique') under CGI Art. 256C where close financial, economic, and organisational links exist with the group head; (iii) inclusion in a fiscal-integration group under CGI Art. 223A if a French parent holds at least 95% of the SARL's capital and voting rights, with a five-year commitment; (iv) the statutory reduced corporate income tax rate of 15% on the first EUR 42,500 of profit under CGI Art. 219 for SMEs meeting the EUR 10 million turnover and ownership conditions, which is a rate abatement rather than a free election. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; French ATAD transposition (CGI Arts. 212bis and 209B) applies mandatorily.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 - French-established taxable persons must register from the first taxable transaction, with no domestic de minimis threshold. Non-established EU suppliers benefit from the harmonised intra-EU distance-sales threshold of EUR 10,000 annual EU-wide turnover (OSS special scheme), above which French VAT registration and French VAT apply; low-value import consignments not exceeding EUR 150 per shipment may be cleared under the Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) without full French VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of manufactured DC fast-charger hardware outside the European Union qualify for the zero-rated export exemption under CGI Art. 262-I-1° where: (i) the transfer of the power of disposal in the goods is made to a purchaser not established in France or the EU; (ii) the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of EU territory by the seller, the purchaser, or a third party acting for either; (iii) the export declaration is lodged with DGDDI through DELTA-X and accepted before the end of the third calendar month following the transfer of ownership (BOFiP-TVA-EXP); and (iv) the French supplier retains full documentary proof (export declaration, transport evidence, commercial invoice, and payment evidence) for the statutory retention period. Intra-EU dispatches of chargers to a VAT-registered customer in another Member State also qualify for 0% under CGI Art. 262ter where the customer's intra-Community VAT number is valid and the transaction is reported in the DES/Intrastat framework.

  • Export customs declaration (Déclaration d'Accompagnement Unique - DAU) lodged and validated through DELTA-X, evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, multimodal CMR, or Air Waybill) proving removal of the goods from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms showing delivery to the third-country purchaser and transfer of power of disposal
  • Proof of payment / inward remittance certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice) confirming repatriation of export proceeds to the French SARL
  • Consignee delivery receipt or customs-stamped landing certificate in cases where DGFiP requests supplementary evidence for high-value units
  • EORI registration confirmation and, where preferential tariff treatment is claimed, certificate of origin or EUR.1 movement certificate

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions is phased in under Ordonnance n° 2024-609: all VAT-registered businesses must accept e-invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance becomes mandatory for large and mid-size enterprises from 1 September 2026 and for small/micro enterprises from 1 September 2027. Invoices must comply with the European standard EN 16931 (Factur-X hybrid, UBL, or CII formats) and must be transmitted to the recipient through a certified Dematerialisation Partner Platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire - PDP) or the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation - PPF), using the PDP's REST API; transaction e-reporting of B2B and B2C data to DGFiP is simultaneous. France does not operate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for manufactured goods; separate customs e-clearance applies via DELTA-X for export declarations and via DES declarations for intra-EU dispatches.

Export zero-rating claims are a priority audit target of DGFiP's cross-border control division. Missing, contradictory, or forged export evidence triggers retrospective reassessment of VAT at the 20% standard rate on the export proceeds, plus statutory late-payment interest (intérêt de retard) and graduated civil penalties: 40% for deliberate inaccuracy or bad faith (mauvaise foi), 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law (manœuvres frauduleuses / abus de droit), and up to 100% for concealed business activity. The clawback/limitation period generally runs to the end of the third calendar year following the year the VAT became exigible, extended to six years for undisclosed activities, and further extended where foreign-based evasion or asset concealment is established; export evidence must be preserved for six years. Criminal exposure includes tax fraud under CGI Art. 1741 (up to EUR 500,000 fine and five years' imprisonment) and customs evasion under Code des Douanes Art. 414 (up to EUR 750,000 and seven years' imprisonment), with DGFiP able to publish the sanctions and to set off any refund against the reassessed liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EUR is fully convertible under the eurozone framework, OECD codes, and EU Treaty free-movement-of-capital rules. France maintains full current-account and capital-account liberalisation for EU and third-country counterparties, with no prior authorisation for capital injection or profit remittance. Dividends and other distributions may be freely remitted to non-resident shareholders subject to standard KYC/AML documentation, a compliance check under the Monetary and Financial Code, and statistical balance-of-payments reporting by the bank. Practical hurdles are limited to correspondent-bank sanctions screening and enhanced due diligence on non-EU payment chains; no legal convertibility restriction applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory export-proceeds surrender ratio and no statutory repatriation deadline for sales receipts. Exporters of DC fast-charging equipment may retain 100% of foreign-currency revenue and are not obligated to convert into EUR. However, all cross-border transactions must be declared to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes via the resident bank; transactions may be subject to monthly or quarterly reporting thresholds under the 'EEC – États récapitulatifs des échanges' regime, and non-compliance carries administrative penalty risk.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, with foreign-exchange policy oversight delegated from the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium under strict conditions. An offshore digital EMI domiciled outside the EEA has Low viability as a primary treasury rail because the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) requires banking and e-money services to be provided by licensed credit institutions or ACPR-registered electronic-money institutions; a non-EEA EMI cannot obtain a French license and cannot legally open a settlement or liquidity account in the name of the SARL. An EU/EEA-licensed EMI, however, can passport into France under PSD2 and may be used for disbursements, but it cannot act as a substitute for a French bank account for capital escrow, documentary credits, or balance-of-payments reporting obligations.
