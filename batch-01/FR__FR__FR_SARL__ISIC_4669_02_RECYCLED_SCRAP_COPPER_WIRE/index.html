E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SARL default: corporate income tax, impôt sur les sociétés, at the French headline rate of 25%; reduced rate of 15% applies to the first EUR 42,500 of annual profit for small enterprises with turnover below EUR 10 million); Pass_Through_Eligible (small SARLs may elect income-tax treatment for 5 years under CGI art. 239 bis AA); Territorial_Scope (France applies the territoriality principle under CGI art. 209-I, taxing French-source profits of the French operation while foreign branch profits are generally excluded from the French corporate base, subject to documentation); Tax_Exempt_Zone (not applicable to the French mainland SARL; no free-zone status for this scrap-trading activity).

Available election forms

France has no US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. Available elections and statutory mechanisms: (1) Option for pass-through taxation under article 239 bis AA CGI for newly formed small commercial SARLs, irrevocable and limited to five fiscal years; (2) Option for corporate tax (IS) by otherwise tax-transparent entities under articles 206 and 239 CGI; (3) Group fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under articles 223 A et seq. CGI by election of a 95%-or-more French parent; (4) The Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) election for intra-EU distance sales of goods to consumers and the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) election for imported consignments below EUR 150; (5) ATAD-based anti-hybrid measures under articles 209, 212 bis CGI and BOI-IS-BASE-35 neutralize hybrid mismatches without an election; foreign entity status is classified by the DGFiP using the 'comparison method' (BOI-IS-DEP-20-30).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis registration threshold for established French domestic businesses; registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply. For small businesses, the franchise en base de TVA exempts sales of goods up to EUR 85,000 annual turnover (EUR 37,500 for services) with mandatory VAT registration above these limits; for intra-EU distance sales of goods, the harmonized EUR 10,000 threshold applies, after which the seller must charge the VAT of the destination Member State or elect OSS. Non-established foreign suppliers selling B2B goods to French VAT-registered customers are not required to register in France, as the customer applies the reverse charge; registration is triggered only for B2C distance sales, imports enabled by the IOSS option, or local warehousing operations.

French mandatory e-invoicing reform (OGD/En 16931 standard, format Factur-X/UBL/CII) is phased in from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises and 1 September 2027 for small enterprises and SARLs. B2B invoices must be issued as structured e-invoices transmitted through a certified Partner Dematerialisation Platform (PDP) or the State Public Portal; B2C transactions in scope are covered by mandatory e-reporting of transaction data to DGFiP. Real-time fiscal clearance is effectuated through the platform network with structured e-invoice transmission, although France does not apply a pre-clearance digital stamping system to invoices; export transactions rely on the DGDDI DELTA electronic customs platform (DELTA-x/DELTA-G) for the Export Declaration DAE, with shipment data transmitted automatically for customs clearance and statistical risk management.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions under EU Capital Movement Directive 88/361/EEC and the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (art. 63). There are no exchange control restrictions on profit or dividend remittance by a French SARL to non-resident shareholders, provided the company has duly filed its corporate income tax return, balanced the statutory annual accounts, and complied with anti-money laundering / beneficial ownership reporting obligations. Any capital inflow above EUR 15,000 must be reported to the French customs and tax administrations for statistical and anti-fraud surveillance under the French Monetary and Financial Code, but no prior administrative authorisation is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose any statutory export proceeds surrender requirement. A French SARL may freely hold export receipts in euro or foreign currency, whether on domestic or foreign accounts without conversion obligations. However, all export invoices and collection proceeds must be recorded in the statutory accounting books; any invoice exceeding EUR 100,000 must be itemised for customs/intra-EU statistical filing, and funds received on a French bank account should carry a compliant payment description referencing the underlying commercial invoice to facilitate cross-border verification.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, with ongoing coordination for exchange control oversight and capital reporting by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the core cross-border scrap settlement function. French-regulated electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Treezor) can provide local EUR IBAN operating accounts for payroll and inventory expenses, but they generally cannot open documentary letters of credit, handle trade title documents, or support large USD/CNH wires for underlying physical consignments. Offshore EMIs that are not passported under PSD2 cannot legally solicit French clients or hold settlement funds for a French SARL; using an unauthorised offshore EMI to settle commercial trade debts may constitute illegal payment activity and expose the company to fines, seizure of funds, and refusal of VAT/tax deductions. Use a fully licensed French or EU-licensed bank for all trade payments, reserving fintech EMIs for ancillary domestic expenses.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial share capital for the French SARL must be deposited into a blocked capital escrow account opened at a French bank, per Articles L.223-7 and R.223-4 of the French Commercial Code, before incorporation. The bank issues a certificate of deposit to the business registrar. For subsequent shareholder loans or equity top-ups, use a SWIFT MT103 wire from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the SARL's euro IBAN. The payment must clearly quote: (i) purpose code 'Capital Contribution or Direct Investment', (ii) the underlying board resolution authorising the injection, (iii) the shareholder's exact legal name matching KYC records, and (iv) a reference to the French capital transaction code 'FR-OPP' where required by Banque de France statistical declarations. For injections above EUR 15,000, the SARL must file the associated balance-of-payment statistical declaration electronically with Banque de France within 20 business days; all wires must pass through a French-authorised payment or credit institution and never through an unlicensed third-country EMI.Trade settlements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for immediate TT settlement against scanned bills of lading and commercial invoice, or through an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit under UCP600 (SWIFT MT700) for larger volumes requiring title document verification. Each outbound payment to the scrap supplier must include the SARL's corporate name, VAT/EORI registration, invoice number, material classification code, and country-of-origin reference in field 70. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for the core cross-border scrap settlement function. French-regulated electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Treezor) can provide local EUR IBAN operating accounts for payroll and inventory expenses, but they generally cannot open documentary letters of credit, handle trade title documents, or support large USD/CNH wires for underlying physical consignments. Offshore EMIs that are not passported under PSD2 cannot legally solicit French clients or hold settlement funds for a French SARL; using an unauthorised offshore EMI to settle commercial trade debts may constitute illegal payment activity and expose the company to fines, seizure of funds, and refusal of VAT/tax deductions. Use a fully licensed French or EU-licensed bank for all trade payments, reserving fintech EMIs for ancillary domestic expenses.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporation tax (IS); 15% reduced rate applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit per fiscal year for qualifying SMEs (turnover below EUR 10 million and at least 75% of the share capital held by individuals or an SME).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% outbound withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders (Art. 119 bis 2 CGI); non-resident individuals are subject to a 12.8% withholding tax within the 30% prélèvement forfaitaire unique (PFU); the rate rises to 75% for dividends paid to persons established in non-cooperative States (ETNC).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Returns must be filed within 3 months of the close of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year SARL, the deadline is generally 3 May of the following year; where that date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or public holiday, the deadline shifts to the first following working day.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File are mandatory where the French entity or its ≥50% parent exceeds EUR 400,000,000 in either annual turnover or gross balance sheet assets; smaller SARLs are not exempt from the obligation to hold contemporaneous evidentiary documentation (invoices, contracts, margin analyses) and are subject to simplified documentation upon audit; all cross-border transactions with entities in non-cooperative States (ETNC) trigger the full documentation requirement regardless of turnover.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code comparable to offshore regimes, but economic substance is tested through the general anti-abuse rule (abus de droit, Art. L. 64 LPF), the definition of permanent establishment, and the OECD BEPS Action 6 principal purpose test (PPT) incorporated into French DTAs. The SARL must demonstrably maintain local management, commercial premises, logistics oversight, headcount, and an auditable contract chain for the recycled copper wire trade; a letterbox or conduit structure interposed solely to divert French-source trading profits will be re-characterised and treaty benefits will be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 of the French Tax Code: up to 5 years' imprisonment (7 years where committed by an organised gang) and a fine of up to EUR 500,000; the tax administration issues a re-assessment plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month and penalty surcharges of 40% for bad faith or 80% for fraudulent manipulation; criminal confiscation of assets is available.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted personally; joint and several civil liability for the company's tax debts is imposed under Art. L. 267 LPF for manœuvres frauduleuses or grave and repeated tax-law violations; applicable consequences include asset freezes, travel bans, passport confiscation, and imprisonment for resident and non-resident directors alike.
Artificial Shell / Abuse of Law (Abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of the arrangement under Art. L. 64 LPF with an 80% penalty on the recomputed tax base plus monthly late interest; the abusive scheme is voided for tax purposes, and dividends, loans, or fees are reclassified as taxable distributions or deemed income; the authority may also re-file a criminal complaint for fraudulent declaration.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly design or validate the artificial arrangement face personal joint liability for the entire reassessed tax and penalties; travel bans and director disqualification may be imposed; criminal prosecution under Art. 1741 for tax evasion is possible, with up to 5 years' imprisonment.
AML / Money Laundering (Blanchiment)

Statutory sanction: Articles 324-1 to 324-5 of the French Penal Code: up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 375,000 for legal entities; administrative sanctions by the ACPR/AFA may include fines up to EUR 100,000 or 10% of turnover; the prefectural commercial licence for regulated scrap dealing may be suspended or withdrawn; confiscation of the SARL's assets is mandatory for proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors can be indicted for money laundering and conspiracy; personal assets may be seized; professional bans and travel bans are commonly imposed; non-resident directors may face extradition requests under bilateral treaties; the failure to implement an adequate AML compliance programme constitutes an aggravating factor.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Transactions with Non-Cooperative States (ETNC)

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of the French taxable base; penalty of 40% for bad faith or 80% for abusive scheme; specific sanction of 5% under Art. 1732 CGI for failure to produce TP documentation; payments to ETNC recipients lose deductibility under Art. 238 A CGI, and a 75% withholding tax is applied to dividends, interest, and royalties paid to such recipients (Art. 119 bis 2 bis, Art. 125 A, Art. 182 B CGI); cumulative interest at 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax whenever they directed the transfer pricing policy or neglected the obligation to maintain documentation; criminal exposure under Art. 1743 for obstruction of fiscal audit adds further risk; administrative sanctions may include a bar from management duties and a travel ban for non-resident directors.
