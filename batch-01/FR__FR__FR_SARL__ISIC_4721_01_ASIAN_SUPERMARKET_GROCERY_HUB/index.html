Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SARL must have a declared registered office in France. Domiciliation at a commercial domiciliation provider may be permissible if the provider is authorized and the statutory conditions are satisfied. A manager may, subject to statutory limits and contractual or local restrictions, use a qualifying personal residence for the registered office. A virtual office is not an adequate substitute for operational premises for a physical grocery retailer. The business should maintain an actual retail location, storage and food-safety arrangements, inventory records, supplier documentation, consumer-facing disclosures, and evidence of effective management. A retail grocery activity may require food-business registration or notification, hygiene compliance, import and customs compliance, and an alcohol licence or declaration if alcoholic products are sold.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), with registration data maintained in the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE): https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr/

Name reservation / lock period

France does not generally provide a standalone statutory company-name reservation period equivalent to a 30-day reservation system. Conduct clearance through INPI, the RNE, the Registre du commerce et des sociétés, the INPI trademark database, EUIPO, domain registries, and relevant trade-name databases before filing. If the name is rejected or the filing is suspended, obtain the electronic rejection or deficiency notice, identify the legal ground, and submit corrected documents or a reasoned response through the Guichet unique within the stated correction period. If the decision is attributable to the competent registry or administrative authority and remains disputed, request a reasoned review and pursue the available administrative or judicial remedy, including referral to the competent commercial court for registry-related disputes where applicable. A modified name should be filed promptly; no assumption of name protection arises merely from a draft or rejected filing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French Public Finance Directorate, accessible via the official portal impots.gouv.fr; corporate income tax, VAT, and withholding tax filings are managed through the DGFiP online professional tax portal (Espace Professionnel).

        
E-government filing portal
The SARL must be incorporated via the formalities portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) or before the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE). The registration generates the SIREN and SIRET numbers and the French tax identification number (Numéro de TVA Intracommunautaire). The VAT number is applied for through Form MO-1 addressed to the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) or through the DGFiP non-resident service; the EU VAT ID is generally formatted FR followed by a two-digit key and the nine-digit SIREN. Foreign companies without a French establishment may apply directly by filing Form 3551-SD with the non-resident tax office.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — The SARL must be incorporated via the formalities portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) or before the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE). The registration generates the SIREN and SIRET numbers and the French tax identification number (Numéro de TVA Intracommunautaire). The VAT number is applied for through Form MO-1 addressed to the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) or through the DGFiP non-resident service; the EU VAT ID is generally formatted FR followed by a two-digit key and the nine-digit SIREN. Foreign companies without a French establishment may apply directly by filing Form 3551-SD with the non-resident tax office.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box regime exists in France; the SARL is statutorily subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Under Article 239 bis A of the CGI, a qualifying SARL may, with unanimous shareholder consent, irrevocably elect to be treated as fiscally transparent, subject to strict size and composition conditions. Furthermore, the reduced 15% corporate tax rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profits is available for eligible SMEs. Hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD) are implemented under Articles 212 bis and 212 quater CGI.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
20%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Businesses established in France must be VAT-registered from the first taxable transaction; there is no general minimum turnover registration threshold for established entities. The franchise en base exemption is available only to established businesses with annual turnover not exceeding €85,800 for retail sales, but such businesses are exempt from VAT and cannot recover input VAT. For non-established foreign suppliers making distance sales of goods into France, registration is required when the annual cross-border turnover exceeds €10,000 (Union-wide distance selling threshold).

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
0% VAT applies to supplies of goods physically exported outside the European Union under Article 262-I-1 CGI if the goods leave EU territory within 90 days of the invoice date and the exporter keeps sufficient documentary evidence. Services directly connected to the export of goods may also be zero-rated when supplied to a non-EU recipient. Supplies delivered in France, including grocery retail sales to consumers, are subject to the standard 20% rate or the reduced 5.5% / 2.1% rates for foodstuffs; they cannot be zero-rated.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The euro is fully convertible. France maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-65 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Capital Liberalisation Codes. There is no licensing or prior-authorisation obstacle to dividend distributions, interest payments, or repatriation of contributed capital by non-resident shareholders of a French SARL. Practical friction arises only in the documentary chain: financial flows between a French resident company and non-resident counterparties in excess of EUR 15,000 must be captured in the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting system, and profit remittances require an ordinary shareholders' resolution, confirmation of legally distributable reserves from the audited financial statements, and standard banking source-of-funds verification at the paying institution. For the Asian supermarket grocery retail business, the relevant bottleneck is not convertibility but rather bank de-risking behaviour and merchant-acquiring compliance for cash-intensive grocery operations.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Non-euro export receipts may be held in permitted foreign-currency accounts or converted freely. The only obligation is statistical: banks must report cross-border transactions and balance-of-payments flows to the Banque de France, with transactions above EUR 50,000 being reportable within 20 business days of value date. For this retail grocery scope, EUR-denominated proceeds require no FX conversion and no special declaration beyond standard VAT and accounting records. All underlying import, export, customs, and supplier documentation must be retained for at least 10 years to evidence tax, customs, and anti-money-laundering compliance.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) and Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury), operating under the Ministry of Economy and Finance

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Medium-High, but strictly limited to EEA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions passporting into France under PSD2 and the ACPR registration regime. Reputable EU-regulated digital banks (e.g., Qonto, Shine, N26) may open an account for a French SARL remotely and provide SEPA instantaneous payment rails, card acquiring, and expense management. Offshore EMIs domiciled outside the EEA have Low viability: they may not passport into France and may not legally provide payment services to a French merchant without a French branch licence or agent registration. Use of an unregulated offshore EMI would expose the SARL to ACPR enforcement risk, rejection of merchant-acquiring settlements, and frozen funds. Mandatory legal constraints include: the EMI must hold a valid ACPR registration or EEA passport notice, client funds must be safeguarded in segregated accounts, and the EMI must support purpose-code messaging and documentary verification for capital-injection and trade-related transfers.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Non-resident UBO structure or offshore parent company in a low-tax or high-secrecy jurisdiction
        
Root cause: Non-resident UBO structure or offshore parent company in a low-tax or high-secrecy jurisdiction

        
Defense evidence: Legalised corporate registry extract, current shareholder ledger, apostilled passport and proof of identity, European tax-residence certificate, comprehensive source-of-wealth statement, written legal opinion explaining the purpose of each intermediate holding vehicle, and audited financial statements of the immediate parent.

      

      
      

        High-volume cash retail trajectory for grocery sales with a material cash-to-card mix
        
Root cause: High-volume cash retail trajectory for grocery sales with a material cash-to-card mix

        
Defense evidence: Monthly POS terminal reconciliation reports, cash register audit trails, TVA (VAT) returns corroborating declared cash sales, payroll records, supplier invoices, insurance certificates, and an internal AML control manual documenting cash thresholds, segregation of duties, and suspicious-transaction escalation procedures.

      

      
      

        Recurring import payments to Asian supplier banks in jurisdictions with elevated counterparty risk
        
Root cause: Recurring import payments to Asian supplier banks in jurisdictions with elevated counterparty risk

        
Defense evidence: Signed supplier purchase agreements, commercial invoices, customs import declarations and clearance records, bills of lading or airway bills, certificates of origin, prior stable transaction history with the identical beneficiary, and a written bank reference from the beneficiary's remitting institution confirming the underlying trade purpose.

      

      
      

        Large one-off inbound equity injection from a foreign shareholder exceeding the bank's monitoring threshold
        
Root cause: Large one-off inbound equity injection from a foreign shareholder exceeding the bank's monitoring threshold

        
Defense evidence: Shareholders' resolution approving the equity increase, French share-capital subscription certificate, identity of the remitting bank and purpose code (investment capital), source-of-funds letter from the remitting bank, audited source-of-wealth documentation for the contributing shareholder, and the SARL's business plan demonstrating intended use of the injected funds.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Approved routing: the non-resident shareholder issues a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer, or MT202 COV where cover is separately transmitted, to the SARL's Tier-1 French bank, quoting purpose code 'C19 – Investment in a French resident company / equity contribution' together with the share-capital increase contract reference. The receiving bank must match the beneficiary name to the kbis registration, validate the SARL's bank account reference against the KYC file, and accept the funds only as a book-entry credit to the capital contribution account prior to transfer to the operational account. All capital injections above EUR 15,000 must be included in the monthly or quarterly Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration within 20 business days. Euro-area currency contributions require no FX conversion; non-euro contributions must be converted at an arm's length market rate with documentation retained.
