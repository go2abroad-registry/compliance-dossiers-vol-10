Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current account and capital account; France applies no exchange-control authorization for ordinary trade payments, profit remittances, dividends, royalties, intra-group loans, or portfolio capital movements. Free movement of capital is guaranteed by Article 63 TFEU, with restrictions permitted only under EU sanctions regimes, asset-freezing measures (Article L561-2 Code monétaire et financier), or the strategic-sector foreign-investment review process. A peptide-synthesis CMO operating in pharmaceutical contract manufacturing should treat non-EU direct investment as potentially within the sensitive health/security perimeter; prior authorization from the French Treasury may be required before a non-EU shareholder acquires control or a material interest in activities touching infectious-disease R&D or essential medicines. Profit and capital repatriation to foreign shareholders is otherwise unrestricted, subject only to ordinary audit, reserve requirements and AML-source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. France has abolished all foreign-exchange surrender and forced-conversion rules. Export proceeds may be collected in euros or any freely convertible currency, retained indefinitely in foreign-currency bank accounts, and converted at the CMO's commercial discretion. There is no statutory repatriation deadline and no mandatory percentage of export receipts that must be exchanged into EUR. Practical constraints: every cross-border financial flow must be reported by the bank to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics, and physical cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more require a customs monetary declaration.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) is the central bank and the national competent authority for balance-of-payments statistics, monetary stability and payment system oversight. France being a euro-area member, monetary policy and euro foreign-exchange strategy are shared with the European Central Bank (ECB). The Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy and Finance) administers foreign-investment screening (Articles L151-3 et seq., Code monétaire et financier) and external financial relations; the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) supervises bank licensing, conduct and AML/CFT enforcement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (secondary overlay only). Regulated EEA fintech/EMI providers such as Airwallex, Revolut Business or Qonto can be opened remotely and provide useful multi-currency collection sub-accounts for sponsor invoicing and low-value supplier expenses. Legal constraints are material: EMIs do not hold a full credit-institution licence, cannot issue documentary credits or provide overdrafts, are not covered by the French deposit-guarantee scheme, and must safeguard client funds separately rather than treat them as deposits. For a Medium-Risk-Enhanced pharmaceutical CMO, fintech onboarding committees will frequently decline or impose highly restrictive velocity limits; non-EEA EMIs (notably UK-licensed entities) lost EU passporting rights post-Brexit and may not lawfully provide payment services to French clients without a French/EEA establishment. The blueprint therefore uses the EMI layer purely for collection/expense routing, never as the primary treasury or settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the SARL should be routed exclusively over institutional clearing rails: EUR transfers via TARGET2 or SEPA to the French bank's IBAN; USD transfers via SWIFT MT103 through the bank's New York correspondent, referencing the share-subscription certificate. Each incoming wire must be tagged with a clear purpose reference, e.g. 'AUGMENTATION DE CAPITAL / EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION – FR_SARL – [date]' and identify the subscriber and LEI. The bank will require the supporting shareholders' resolution, proof of deposit attestation, and source-of-funds documentation prior to releasing funds to the operating account. Funds originating from non-EEA shareholders are subject to standard balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque de France; if the non-EEA investment would transfer control or a significant interest in a pharmaceutical/health-security sensitive activity, the French Treasury prior-authorisation process must be cleared before the injection is completed. The capital injection must never be split into structured smaller wires to avoid transaction thresholds; transparency and traceability are mandatory.Outbound treasury settlements for the CMO laboratory should use: (i) SWIFT MT103 wire for routine vendor invoices (amino-acid derivatives, resins, reagents, consumables) in EUR, USD or CHF; (ii) SEPA Credit Transfers for EU suppliers; (iii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit under UCP600 issued by the French house bank via MT700 for high-value strategic raw materials or capital equipment (with confirmation from the beneficiary's Chinese/Indian bank where appropriate); and (iv) documentary collections under URC522 for smaller bulk shipments. Every MT103 must carry the underlying purchase-order and commercial-invoice reference; the French bank will match the payment to the import customs declaration (SINDE/DAU) and will reject disbursements to shell or non-vetted counterparties. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. A qualifying EU parent may obtain exemption under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring a qualifying legal form, subject-to-tax condition, minimum holding and holding-period conditions, and anti-abuse compliance. A lower rate or exemption may apply under the relevant bilateral tax treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150,000,000 of French turnover or gross assets, or membership in a group meeting the statutory threshold, generally triggers full Local File and Master File documentation under French law. A separate annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration generally applies at EUR 50,000,000 of turnover or gross assets, or where another statutory qualifying condition is met. The documentation requirement may also be imposed following a tax audit or under other statutory conditions.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone regime identical to the United Arab Emirates or other formal Economic Substance Regulations. French anti-abuse rules, the general anti-abuse principle, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled foreign company rules, interest-limitation rules, anti-hybrid rules, principal-purpose analysis, EU anti-abuse directives and permanent-establishment doctrine apply. A French SARL performing peptide synthesis should maintain genuine personnel, laboratory capability, equipment, decision-making, risk control, premises, books and records in France. Conduit financing, artificial royalty flows, circular transactions, unsupported management fees and profit allocations inconsistent with functions and risks may be challenged.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late interest, 40% or 80% tax surcharges, and criminal tax-fraud penalties. Natural persons may face up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, with aggravated cases reaching seven years' imprisonment and EUR 3,000,000; fines may be increased based on proceeds or statutory aggravating circumstances.

Director personal liability: Managers and directors may be criminally prosecuted and may be held personally liable for tax debts under Article L.267 of the French Tax Procedures Book where their fraudulent or repeated non-compliance makes recovery impossible. Civil restitution, asset seizure, professional disqualification and court-imposed travel restrictions may apply. Travel bans are not automatic for every tax violation.
Shell Company, Artificial Substance and Beneficial-Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, treaty benefits or exemptions; reassessment under abuse-of-law principles; penalties generally up to 80% for the principal participant or beneficiary and 40% in other serious cases, plus interest and possible criminal fraud charges.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal civil liability, criminal liability where fraud is established, asset confiscation and management bans. Imprisonment may arise from associated tax fraud, money laundering or false-document offenses.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Profit reassessment, interest, tax surcharges, documentation penalties generally based on EUR 10,000, 0.5% of covered transactions, or 5% of reassessed profits or transactions, depending on the statutory conditions, and possible treaty-corresponding-adjustment disputes.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for intentional or grossly negligent tax misconduct and may face criminal exposure if the manipulation involves false records or fraudulent concealment.
VAT, Customs and Excise Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Recovery of VAT, customs duties and interest; VAT penalties commonly range from 5% for late payment to 40% for deliberate failure and 80% for fraudulent conduct, with customs penalties, seizure and criminal prosecution available for serious or prohibited-goods violations.

Director personal liability: Managers may face customs or tax prosecution, personal civil liability in cases of deliberate misconduct, confiscation, professional bans and imprisonment for organized or fraudulent conduct.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist-Financing Controls

Statutory sanction: Competent supervisory authorities may impose warnings, temporary or permanent bans, license restrictions and monetary sanctions. For regulated entities, sanctions may reach EUR 100,000,000 or 10% of annual worldwide turnover, subject to the applicable regime; criminal money laundering may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with aggravated cases reaching ten years and EUR 750,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally prosecuted for participation, complicity or deliberate failure. Asset freezing, confiscation, professional bans and imprisonment may apply; travel restrictions may be ordered by a court.
Sanctions, Export-Control and Controlled-Chemical Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, transaction blocking, license withdrawal, asset freezing, confiscation and criminal penalties under French, European Union and applicable international sanctions regimes. Penalties depend on the specific regulation and conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face criminal prosecution, substantial fines, imprisonment, professional bans and asset confiscation. A travel ban may be imposed by judicial order but is not automatic.
Unlicensed Pharmaceutical, Laboratory and Chemical Operations

Statutory sanction: Administrative closure, suspension or withdrawal of authorization, remediation orders, environmental fines, product seizure, civil damages and criminal penalties under pharmaceutical, environmental, occupational-safety and chemical-control laws.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally liable for environmental remediation and workplace or public-health offenses. Serious breaches may result in imprisonment, fines, professional disqualification and court-ordered travel restrictions.
Withholding-Tax and Profit-Distribution Failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, late interest, tax surcharges, denial of treaty relief and potential penalties for concealed distributions. Unlawful distributions may also create civil repayment claims.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for wrongful distributions, tax-payment failures and breach of management duties. Personal liability, asset seizure and criminal exposure arise where the conduct is deliberate, fraudulent or prejudices creditors.
Insolvency, Asset-Diversion and Creditor Fraud

Statutory sanction: Clawback of transactions, insolvency proceedings, director liability for all or part of the shortfall, management bans and criminal bankruptcy offenses. Bankruptcy fraud may carry imprisonment and substantial fines.

Director personal liability: Managers may face personal contribution claims, civil restitution, disqualification from management, asset confiscation and imprisonment. Judicial travel restrictions may be ordered in criminal or insolvency proceedings.
Scientific-Integrity, Product-Safety and Data Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Regulatory suspension, product recall, contract damages, civil liability, administrative data-protection fines and criminal penalties for fraud, forgery, breach of professional secrecy or unlawful data processing.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal and civil liability where they ordered, authorized or knowingly tolerated falsification or unlawful processing. Imprisonment, professional bans and confiscation may apply to serious offenses.
