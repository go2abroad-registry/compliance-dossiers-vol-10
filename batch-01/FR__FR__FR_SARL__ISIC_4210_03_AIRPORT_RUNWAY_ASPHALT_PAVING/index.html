Competent corporate registry

Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) / Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce, filed electronically through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

Code de commerce (French Commercial Code), articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (Société à Responsabilité Limitée) and articles L.210-1 to L.210-10; French Commercial Code implementing Decree n° 2023-292 of 17 April 2023.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'une société (Cerfa n° 16309*02) accompanied by the signed Statuts (Articles of Association), filed via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 19 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Minimum share capital is EUR 1 (Art. L.223-2, Code de commerce). Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked French bank account or with a notaire/Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the company is registered. If share capital exceeds EUR 5,000, at least 20% of each cash contribution must be paid up at incorporation and the balance within 5 years; if capital is EUR 5,000 or less, all cash contributions must be fully paid upon subscription.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) for fuel storage, hot works, and temporary site installations; structural and stability clearance of site cabins, scaffolding, and heavy machinery lifting systems (VGP inspection) by an accredited body; occupational health clearance under the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques (DUERP) and Code du travail, including a medical fitness statement for East airside personnel; security screening for crews working in airport security restricted areas as required by the airport operator.

Paid-in capital rule: Minimum share capital is EUR 1 (Art. L.223-2, Code de commerce). Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked French bank account or with a notaire/Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the company is registered. If share capital exceeds EUR 5,000, at least 20% of each cash contribution must be paid up at incorporation and the balance within 5 years; if capital is EUR 5,000 or less, all cash contributions must be fully paid upon subscription.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP); online tax procedures are available through impots.gouv.fr and the professional tax account (espace professionnel). Business formation is filed through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

Register the SARL through the INPI Guichet unique to obtain its SIREN and establishment-specific SIRET numbers. Apply to the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) for the French VAT number, generally formed as FR plus a two-character key and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign business with no French establishment that makes taxable supplies in France must register with the competent non-resident business tax service before or when taxable activity begins; EU-established businesses generally do not need a fiscal representative, while businesses established outside the EU generally must appoint an accredited French fiscal representative unless an applicable exception or mutual-assistance arrangement applies. French VAT registration does not itself establish a permanent establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Register the SARL through the INPI Guichet unique to obtain its SIREN and establishment-specific SIRET numbers. Apply to the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) for the French VAT number, generally formed as FR plus a two-character key and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign business with no French establishment that makes taxable supplies in France must register with the competent non-resident business tax service before or when taxable activity begins; EU-established businesses generally do not need a fiscal representative, while businesses established outside the EU generally must appoint an accredited French fiscal representative unless an applicable exception or mutual-assistance arrangement applies. French VAT registration does not itself establish a permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Certain qualifying family-owned SARLs may elect partnership treatment without the ordinary time limit while eligibility continues. Other qualifying newly formed SARLs may elect partnership treatment for up to five financial years, subject to statutory ownership, activity, size, and timing conditions. France has no general US-style check-the-box election. French and EU anti-hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or require inclusion where classification mismatches produce deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the domestic small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base), the general services threshold is EUR 37,500 of prior-year turnover, with a EUR 41,250 current-year tolerance; the corresponding general goods threshold is EUR 85,000, with a EUR 93,500 tolerance. These thresholds are not a general exemption for foreign suppliers and do not displace registration obligations arising from particular transactions. A French-established contractor exceeding the applicable threshold, or electing to charge VAT, must account for VAT. Construction work on French immovable property is generally located in France; for qualifying B2B supplies by a non-established supplier, the customer may account for VAT under the domestic reverse-charge rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the EU may be zero-rated when statutory conditions are met and the supplier retains reliable evidence that the goods physically left the EU. Intra-EU supplies are generally exempt with credit (zero-rated) only where the customer is VAT-identified in another Member State, the goods are dispatched there, and the supplier satisfies reporting and evidentiary requirements. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer or payment is offshore: place-of-supply rules determine whether French VAT applies. Services directly connected with French immovable property, including runway paving, are generally located where the property is situated; services supplied on a French airport runway are therefore generally subject to French VAT unless a specific exemption or reverse-charge rule applies.

  • For goods exported outside the EU: export customs declaration and MRN with electronic exit confirmation or other accepted evidence of exit from the EU customs territory.
  • Commercial invoice, contract or purchase order, packing list where applicable, and transport evidence such as bill of lading, CMR, air waybill, or carrier confirmation.
  • For intra-EU supplies: customer VAT identification evidence, transport evidence, and required recapitulative statement and VAT return reporting.
  • For services: contract, customer and project-location evidence, VAT-status checks, and records substantiating the applicable place-of-supply or exemption rule.
  • Bank remittance evidence may support the commercial audit trail but is not a general statutory prerequisite for French export zero-rating; France does not generally require an inward remittance certificate.

France is introducing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction/payment e-reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all in-scope businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices, and large and intermediate-sized businesses must issue them; SMEs and microbusinesses are scheduled to issue from 1 September 2027. In-scope invoices must be transmitted through an approved private platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire) or the public invoicing portal, subject to applicable rules. France does not generally operate a real-time pre-clearance or digital-stamping system for ordinary VAT invoices. Cross-border transactions and transactions outside the domestic B2B e-invoicing scope may be subject to e-reporting rather than e-invoicing.

France imposes no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Unsupported or ineligible zero-rating may result in assessment of output VAT, statutory late-payment interest, and tax surcharges, generally 10% for specified filing or payment failures, 40% for deliberate failure, and up to 80% for fraud or specified concealment, subject to the facts and statutory rules. Fraudulent conduct may also trigger criminal sanctions. The administration may deny input VAT deductions or refund claims and recover amounts previously refunded, with interest and applicable penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully convertible euro currency regime aligned with EU and OECD standards. Both capital and current account transactions face no prior approval or quantitative restrictions. Profit remittances by SARL entities are permitted without limitation when supported by verifiable trade documentation, director declarations, and standard KYC/AML verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export of construction services including runway paving contracts does not trigger mandatory surrender or forced conversion requirements. Repatriation of service proceeds must occur within 12 months for corporate tax and VAT declaration purposes. Voluntary conversion to euro is standard; all transfers require matching invoices, delivery proofs, and purpose codes to satisfy French tax authorities.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Cross-border EMIs such as Wise Business or Revolut Business are viable for inbound remittances but constrained by French law requiring local banking partner integration for EUR clearing and full compliance with AMF and ACPR regulations. Offshore digital EMIs must route final settlements through regulated French correspondent banks.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax generally applies to dividends and other distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient. The rate may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, or a domestic exemption. The EU parent exemption generally requires qualifying ownership, holding-period, legal-form, subject-to-tax, and anti-abuse conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the filing deadline is generally the second business day following 1 May of the following year. A newly incorporated company may have a specific filing deadline based on its first accounting period. Corporate income tax instalments are generally due by 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, subject to statutory exceptions. The final balance is generally due by the fifteenth day of the fourth month following the financial year-end, or by 15 May for a 31 December year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally due by 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December. The final instalment may be subject to a special percentage based on the preceding year's tax liability and the company's size or turnover.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer-pricing documentation is generally mandatory where the French entity or French tax group has annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, or belongs to a group meeting the statutory control or threshold conditions. A simplified documentation regime generally applies to other taxpayers engaged in controlled transactions, and materiality, audit, and disclosure requirements may still apply. A Country-by-Country Report is generally required for a qualifying multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. The annual transfer-pricing summary declaration, Form 2257-SD, may be required where statutory transaction and group thresholds are met.

Economic substance scope: France has no single stand-alone economic substance regulation equivalent to certain offshore ESR regimes. Substance is enforced through general anti-abuse rules, the principal-purpose test in treaties, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled foreign company rules, permanent-establishment principles, anti-hybrid rules, the abuse-of-law doctrine, and transfer-pricing rules. A French SARL performing runway paving should maintain genuine French personnel, management, equipment access, contracting authority, operational premises, banking, accounting, insurance, project records, and commercial risk commensurate with its reported profit. Artificial conduit arrangements, unsupported management fees, back-to-back financing, and profit allocation inconsistent with functions and risks are high-risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties generally ranging from 10% to 80% depending on conduct, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that may include imprisonment of up to five years and fines of up to EUR 500,000, with higher penalties in aggravated or organized cases.

Director personal liability: The managing director may incur criminal liability, personal tax liability in cases of fraudulent manoeuvres or serious and repeated non-compliance, civil restitution obligations, management bans, and asset seizure. A travel restriction is not automatic but may be ordered or imposed in criminal or enforcement proceedings; imprisonment is possible for proven tax fraud.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation and Hidden Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment under the arm's-length principle, denial of deductions, recharacterization as a hidden distribution, withholding tax exposure, late-payment interest, documentation penalties, and potential 40% or 80% penalties where deliberate or fraudulent conduct is established.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for intentional participation, fraudulent management, or serious repeated tax non-compliance. The corporate veil is not automatically pierced, but personal liability can arise under statutory tax-liability procedures, civil fault, criminal law, or insolvency proceedings.
VAT Fraud and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, denial of input tax, late-payment interest, penalties generally up to 40% for deliberate conduct and up to 80% for fraudulent or abusive conduct, and criminal prosecution for organized or aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may be prosecuted personally where they authorized or knowingly facilitated false invoicing or VAT fraud. Civil tax-liability orders, asset seizure, management bans, and imprisonment may apply depending on the conduct.
Shell Company, Artificial Residency, or Permanent-Establishment Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of the place of effective management or permanent establishment, French tax reassessment, withholding tax, penalties, interest, and possible abuse-of-law sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil and criminal exposure if they knowingly created or operated an artificial arrangement. Travel bans or imprisonment are not automatic and require a competent authority's order or criminal conviction.
Beneficial Ownership and Anti-Money-Laundering Failure

Statutory sanction: Regulatory enforcement, fines, account restrictions, transaction blocking, confiscation, professional sanctions, and criminal money-laundering penalties that may include imprisonment and substantial fines. Beneficial-owner filing failures may attract separate statutory fines and director liability.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally prosecuted for knowing participation, concealment, or negligent failure to implement required controls. Confiscation, civil restitution, management bans, travel restrictions, and imprisonment may apply according to the offense and judicial order.
Bribery, Public Procurement, and Aviation-Infrastructure Corruption

Statutory sanction: Criminal bribery penalties, confiscation, exclusion from public procurement, debarment, contract invalidation, corporate fines, and possible compliance-monitoring measures. Accounting entries used to disguise bribes may create separate tax and false-accounting exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors, employees, intermediaries, and beneficial owners may face individual criminal prosecution, imprisonment, fines, confiscation, management bans, and civil damages. Corporate limited liability does not protect a person who personally committed or authorized bribery.
Illegal Labor, Payroll Tax, and Social Security Evasion

Statutory sanction: Backdated payroll taxes and social contributions, surcharges, labor-law fines, contract and procurement exclusion, site sanctions, and criminal penalties for concealed employment or illegal labor supply.

Director personal liability: The managing director may be personally liable for concealed employment, unlawful subcontracting, worker safety failures, or contribution evasion. Civil compensation, management bans, asset seizure, and imprisonment may apply in serious cases.
Construction Safety and Environmental Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative orders, suspension or withdrawal of authorization, remediation costs, environmental fines, contract termination, exclusion from public tenders, civil damages, and criminal penalties for serious safety or environmental offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors and site managers may incur personal criminal or civil liability for knowing or negligent breaches, including bodily injury, death, pollution, or obstruction of regulators. Imprisonment and management bans may apply where statutory elements are proven.
Accounting Manipulation and Insolvency Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Rejection of accounts, tax reassessment, civil damages, clawback of improper transactions, bankruptcy-related sanctions, personal liability for all or part of insufficient assets, and criminal penalties for fraudulent bankruptcy or false accounting.

Director personal liability: Managers may be ordered to contribute to the shortfall in company assets, prohibited from managing companies, required to restore diverted assets, and prosecuted criminally. Travel restrictions and imprisonment may occur only under applicable judicial or criminal procedures.
