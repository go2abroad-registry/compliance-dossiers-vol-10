Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies to a SARL (Code de commerce, L.223-2); the capital is freely fixed in the Articles of Association. At incorporation, cash contributions must be fully subscribed, and at least one-fifth of the cash capital must be released and deposited on a blocked account with a bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations. Contributions in kind are fully paid in at incorporation and require approval by the partners or a contribution auditor if applicable.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the SDIS 75 or a certified building control agency for the workshop and cleanroom systems; structural load inspection and certification for partition wall systems, suspended ceilings and HVAC equipment support; occupational health clearance by the Service de Prévention et de Santé au Travail confirming compliance with the Labour Code on ventilation, lighting, noise, electrical safety, machine guarding, first aid and emergency response.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – principal tax authority for corporate taxation and VAT; online portal www.impots.gouv.fr. Foreign direct indirect tax registration is processed by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) in Paris.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident SARLs must obtain a French tax identification number (TIN) and a VAT number by filing Form Cerfa n° 16431 with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) before carrying out taxable operations in France. The application must be accompanied by proof of legal existence (certificate of incorporation from the foreign registry, articles of association), a copy of the foreign VAT registration certificate, and power of attorney for the appointed tax representative. The SIEE normally issues the French SIRET/SIREN number within 2–4 weeks, after which a French intra-Community VAT number is assigned and registered on the EU VAT Information Exchange System (VIES).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Non-resident SARLs must obtain a French tax identification number (TIN) and a VAT number by filing Form Cerfa n° 16431 with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) before carrying out taxable operations in France. The application must be accompanied by proof of legal existence (certificate of incorporation from the foreign registry, articles of association), a copy of the foreign VAT registration certificate, and power of attorney for the appointed tax representative. The SIEE normally issues the French SIRET/SIREN number within 2–4 weeks, after which a French intra-Community VAT number is assigned and registered on the EU VAT Information Exchange System (VIES).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not apply the US check-the-box system. SARLs are generally subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Under Article 239 bis A of the French General Tax Code, a small SARL may elect to be taxed as a pass-through partnership (régime des sociétés de personnes) for up to five years if the legal form qualifies and the election is filed with the DGFiP. Additionally, French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) is available to companies controlling 95%-owned EU subsidiaries. With respect to indirect taxes, no hybrid mismatch election exists for VAT, but a taxpayer may request a specific ruling (rescrit fiscal) to confirm the VAT treatment of cross-border transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For non-resident suppliers making taxable supplies in France, there is no de minimis VAT registration threshold: any B2B service or good supplied to a French customer requires registration. For existing French-established small businesses, the annual turnover thresholds for the VAT exemption (franchise en base de TVA) are €85,000 for sales of goods and €34,400 for provision of services; exceeding these thresholds triggers mandatory registration. The 2025 thresholds may be adjusted for inflation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods shipped outside the European Union are zero-rated (0% VAT) under Article 262 CGI provided the supplier meets the proof-of-export requirements. For intra-EU supplies to a VAT-registered customer, the supplier may apply the exemption (0% rating) for intra-Community supply under Article 262-2 CGI. For services (including cleanroom fit-out services supplied to a non-EU client), the place of supply is considered outside France under Article 259A CGI (B2B rule), making the service either out of scope or zero-rated if the recipient is a taxable person established outside the EU, subject to supporting documentation. Eligibility is denied if the service is physically rendered in France for a French recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE – Déclaration d'échange de biens / export summary declaration via DELTA)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR consignment note, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit note) proving receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice with incoterms and reference to the export exemption
  • Proof of exportation within the time limit (normally 12 months from supply)

France is implementing compulsory electronic invoicing and e-reporting for all established taxpayers. From 1 September 2026, all taxable persons must be able to receive electronic invoices, and large enterprises must issue them; from 1 September 2027, the requirement extends to all enterprises. Invoices must comply with a structured format (Factur-X, UBL 2.1, or CII 16.1) and be transmitted through the mandatory public access portal (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF) or a certified partner platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire, PDP) connected via API. There is no digital stamping duty for standard intra-EU/export invoicing, but customs import/export declarations are made electronically via the DELTA/CDU system.

The French tax administration scrutinises export zero-rating claims by cross-checking customs export data (DX7/DXG). Failure to produce the required export documentation within the statutory period results in reversal of the zero-rate, recovery of VAT at 20%, plus late interest (intérêt de retard) at approximately 0.20% per month and manipulation penalties of 80% of the tax due in cases of deliberate non-compliance. Criminal fines up to €75,000 and imprisonment are possible for tax fraud (fraude fiscale). A retrospective clawback can be assessed up to the normal tax limitation period (usually three years).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under the principle of fully liberalized and convertible capital and current accounts. Article L.151-1 of the Code monétaire et financier declares that financial relations with foreign countries are free, without prejudice to international sanctions, prudential regulations, and anti-money-laundering statutes. The euro is fully convertible for current-account flows (trade, services, dividends, interest, royalties, salaries) and capital-account flows (equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, repatriation of liquidation proceeds, portfolio investment) in accordance with Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the Eurosystem's foreign-reserve policy. No exchange-control authorization, license, or convertibility certificate is required for profit remittances by an SARL; the practical hurdles are domestic corporate and tax gateways rather than FX controls: the SARL must have legally distributable profits approved by the shareholders within the statutory period following the financial year-end, the statutory balance sheet must not trigger the prohibition on distribution where net assets would fall below capital, and non-resident shareholders must apply applicable tax treaty relief or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive in order to reduce the default domestic dividend withholding tax. All non-resident settlements are subject to threshold-based statistical reporting discharged by the French order bank on behalf of the Banque de France.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no compulsory conversion ratio. The pre-1990 obligation to repatriate and surrender export revenues to the national foreign-exchange system was abolished as part of the full liberalization of French exchange controls and is incompatible with the euro-area legal framework. A French SARL may lawfully retain export receivables in any convertible currency in either domestic or foreign bank accounts, may disburse them directly in the same currency for imported inputs, subcontracting, or hedging settlements, and is merely obliged to translate and revalue foreign-currency positions at each statutory reporting date under French accounting standards. Practical constraints are limited to (i) the threshold-based reporting of cross-border payment transactions with non-residents, discharged by the order bank for the Banque de France; (ii) the obligation to maintain normal commercial documentation (invoices, delivery proofs, customs declarations) evidencing the underlying export transaction; and (iii) the anti-money-laundering requirement that any large or unusual receipt be traceable to a genuine trade transaction.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), the national central bank of the French Republic within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank (ECB), is the principal monetary, payment-systems, and balance-of-payments surveillance authority. Exchange control competence is exercised jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the Code monétaire et financier (in particular Articles L.151-1 et seq. on financial relations with foreign countries), while the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) licenses and supervises credit institutions, payment institutions, and electronic-money institutions for banking, FX, and AML/CFT compliance. The euro is the legal tender and the sole domestic currency; no separate national exchange control agency issues foreign-exchange licenses in France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for European Economic Area electronic-money institutions (EMIs) that hold an ACPR e-money license in France or benefit from an EU/EEA payment-institution or EMI passport into France under the Revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2), provided they offer euro and multi-currency accounts, SWIFT and SEPA outbound rails, and expense cards. Representative French or passporting players can serve the SARL for day-to-day working-capital management and cross-border treasury reconciliation. Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore digital EMIs lacking a French or EEA license: such entities cannot lawfully provide payment services in France, cannot open a French IBAN-based collection account, have no standing under the French deposit-guarantee framework, and their safeguarding arrangements would not be supervised by the ACPR. Legal constraints: an EMI may not grant credit, issue documentary letters of credit, or issue bank guarantees, so the cleanroom-fit-out contracting SARL must retain a licensed credit institution as its primary banking and trade-finance provider; an EMI's client funds must be held in segregated safeguarding accounts, and the EMI must apply the same KYC/AML standards required by the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax, generally applicable to taxable profits of a French SARL.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic French withholding tax rate on dividends and similar distributions paid to a non-resident is generally 25%, subject to treaty relief, statutory exemptions, and anti-abuse rules. A qualifying EU or EEA parent may obtain an exemption under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring qualifying ownership, minimum holding conditions, and anti-abuse compliance. Dividends paid by a French SARL to a French-resident corporate shareholder are generally not subject to dividend withholding tax, although the participation exemption and 5% share of costs and expenses must be analyzed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full French Local File and Master File documentation is generally mandatory where the French entity or French tax group has annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, or belongs to a group meeting the EUR 400 million threshold. Documentation may also be required in a tax audit for entities below the threshold, and material cross-border related-party transactions must be supportable regardless of threshold. The French simplified transfer-pricing declaration, generally Form 2257-SD, may apply to entities meeting the statutory conditions, including relevant turnover or gross-asset thresholds and controlled transactions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to qualifying multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone regime titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore ESR regimes. Nevertheless, substance is material under French transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, anti-abuse provisions, permanent-establishment principles, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, treaty principal-purpose tests, and the general abuse-of-law doctrine. A French SARL should maintain genuine French management, decision-making, personnel or appropriately controlled operational capacity, premises where commercially appropriate, accounting records, bank activity, project execution evidence, and functions consistent with its remuneration. A shell company, conduit, circular payment structure, or entity lacking decision-making capacity may lose treaty benefits and deductions and may face reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and deliberate misstatement

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late interest, 40% penalty for deliberate conduct, 80% penalty for fraudulent maneuvers or abusive concealment, and potential criminal tax fraud penalties of up to five years' imprisonment and EUR 500,000 fine; aggravated cases can reach seven years and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Managers may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, management bans, civil liability for intentional misconduct, and exceptional tax-liability proceedings where the director's fraudulent or seriously negligent conduct made recovery impossible. Corporate limited liability is not a defense to personal criminal conduct.
VAT carousel, false subcontracting, or fraudulent reverse charge

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, recovery of improperly credited VAT, 40% or 80% penalties, late interest, possible joint-and-several recovery, and criminal sanctions for aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize or facilitate fraudulent VAT arrangements may incur criminal liability, personal fines, professional disqualification, and civil recovery claims.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit diversion

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to arm's-length profit, withholding tax, denial of deductions, interest limitation consequences, penalties for inadequate documentation, and possible 40% or 80% penalties where deliberate evasion or abuse is established.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for fraudulent arrangements, inaccurate declarations, or deliberate destruction or concealment of supporting records; ordinary transfer-pricing reassessment does not automatically pierce limited liability.
Treaty abuse and beneficial-ownership failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, withholding tax at the domestic rate, interest, penalties, recovery from the payer in certain circumstances, and application of principal-purpose or abuse-of-law rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face civil and criminal exposure if they knowingly certify false treaty documentation or organize fraudulent distributions; a travel ban is not an automatic tax sanction.
Shell company, fictitious management, or permanent-establishment concealment

Statutory sanction: French corporate tax and VAT assessment, permanent-establishment attribution, withholding tax, penalties, interest, and possible abuse-of-law treatment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for deliberate concealment, fraudulent insolvency, or false accounting. French law does not automatically pierce the corporate veil solely because a company has limited substance, but courts may impose personal liability for separable personal misconduct.
Accounting fraud and document falsification

Statutory sanction: Tax penalties, rejection of accounts, statutory accounting sanctions, criminal charges for forgery, use of forged documents, fraud, or fraudulent bankruptcy, and possible prohibition from managing a business.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability may include imprisonment and fines, together with management bans and civil restitution. Travel restrictions may be imposed only by a competent judicial authority and are not automatic.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing controls

Statutory sanction: Reporting and control failures may lead to administrative, professional, and criminal consequences; laundering offenses can carry imprisonment and substantial fines, with enhanced sanctions for aggravated conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and employees may face individual criminal liability where they knowingly participate in laundering or deliberately obstruct required controls. Internal AML failures alone do not automatically create veil-piercing liability.
Undeclared employment and social-security evasion

Statutory sanction: Backdated social contributions, surcharges, labor-law penalties, loss of public contracts or subsidies, tax reassessment, and criminal sanctions for concealed work.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability, management bans, and civil claims for intentional concealed work or serious breaches of payroll and worker-protection obligations.
Construction, safety, and statutory insurance non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative orders, fines, work stoppage, civil damages, contract termination, exclusion from public procurement, and criminal liability for serious safety breaches.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal criminal exposure for deliberate safety violations or negligent homicide or injury, while civil liability may extend to uninsured or improperly supervised works.
Customs, sanctions, and export-control violations

Statutory sanction: Customs duties, import VAT, seizure, administrative fines, loss of customs authorizations, sanctions enforcement, and criminal penalties depending on the breach.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face personal criminal sanctions for knowing customs fraud or sanctions evasion; asset freezes and travel restrictions may apply under sanctions or judicial measures but are not automatic consequences of ordinary customs errors.
Fraudulent insolvency and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: Avoidance of transactions, repayment orders, liability for all or part of insolvency shortfall, bankruptcy-related penalties, management bans, and criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Courts may order contribution to insufficient assets and impose personal civil or criminal sanctions. This is a principal route through which limited liability can be displaced by director misconduct.
Failure to preserve tax and commercial records

Statutory sanction: Rejection of accounting records, estimated assessment, reversal of deductions or VAT credits, documentation penalties, and increased penalties where concealment is inferred.

Director personal liability: Directors may be sanctioned for intentional destruction, obstruction, or false responses. Ordinary record deficiencies generally create corporate tax liability rather than automatic personal liability.
