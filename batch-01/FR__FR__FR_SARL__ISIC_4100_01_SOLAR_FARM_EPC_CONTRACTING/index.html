Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), Ministry of Economy and Finance; official tax portal: impots.gouv.fr; company formation and registration portal (Guichet Unique / INPI): formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

For a newly established French SARL: (1) file incorporation documents through the Guichet Unique operated by INPI, triggering automatic registration with the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés) and INSEE, which issues the 9-digit SIREN and SIRET establishment numbers; (2) the DGFIP Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) of the registered office allocates the intra-Community VAT number under Article 286 CGI, formatted as FR-AB-123456789, where AB is the two-digit control key and 123456789 is the SIREN; (3) file the taxpayer existence return (Imprimé M0), choose the VAT reporting regime (normal régime réel normal or simplified régime réel simplifié under Article 302 septies A CGI) and file the initial IS return (form 2065-SD); (4) for a non-resident company with French VAT nexus but no fixed establishment, direct registration with the DGFIP non-resident tax department (Direction des Non-Résidents / DRFIP) is required, and appointment of an accredited tax representative is mandated under Article 289 A CGI where taxable supplies in France exceed EUR 10,000 annually; (5) alternatively, non-established EU suppliers of distance sales or certain B2B services may report French VAT through the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS/IOSS) schemes to obtain a French VAT reference without local registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on worldwide profits under Article 206-1 CGI. The standard headline IS rate is 25% (from 2022), with a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit for eligible SMEs satisfying the Article 219-I-b CGI criteria (turnover below EUR 7.6 million and paid-in capital fully released, at least 75% held by individuals). No territorial scope privilege or tax-exempt zone status applies to a standard commercial SARL. Profits are reported on forms 2065-SD / 2058-SD with annual filing by the second business day following 1 May of each fiscal year; instalments are due 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December. — For a newly established French SARL: (1) file incorporation documents through the Guichet Unique operated by INPI, triggering automatic registration with the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés) and INSEE, which issues the 9-digit SIREN and SIRET establishment numbers; (2) the DGFIP Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) of the registered office allocates the intra-Community VAT number under Article 286 CGI, formatted as FR-AB-123456789, where AB is the two-digit control key and 123456789 is the SIREN; (3) file the taxpayer existence return (Imprimé M0), choose the VAT reporting regime (normal régime réel normal or simplified régime réel simplifié under Article 302 septies A CGI) and file the initial IS return (form 2065-SD); (4) for a non-resident company with French VAT nexus but no fixed establishment, direct registration with the DGFIP non-resident tax department (Direction des Non-Résidents / DRFIP) is required, and appointment of an accredited tax representative is mandated under Article 289 A CGI where taxable supplies in France exceed EUR 10,000 annually; (5) alternatively, non-established EU suppliers of distance sales or certain B2B services may report French VAT through the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS/IOSS) schemes to obtain a French VAT reference without local registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on worldwide profits under Article 206-1 CGI. The standard headline IS rate is 25% (from 2022), with a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit for eligible SMEs satisfying the Article 219-I-b CGI criteria (turnover below EUR 7.6 million and paid-in capital fully released, at least 75% held by individuals). No territorial scope privilege or tax-exempt zone status applies to a standard commercial SARL. Profits are reported on forms 2065-SD / 2058-SD with annual filing by the second business day following 1 May of each fiscal year; instalments are due 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December.

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box or equivalent entity-classification election. Available statutory elections: (a) eligible family SARLs ('SARL de famille') may irrevocably elect income-tax pass-through treatment (régime des sociétés de personnes, Article 239 bis AA CGI) provided all associates are natural persons; (b) fiscal integration (régime de l'intégration fiscale, Article 223 A CGI) allows a 95%-or-more French parent to consolidate IS results of its French subsidiaries; (c) VAT filing-mode election between the normal and simplified regimes under Article 302 septies A CGI; (d) participation exemption on qualifying dividends under Articles 145 and 216 CGI with a 5% recapture; (e) hybrid mismatch alignment is statutory under Articles 209 and 212-1 bis CGI, with no elective hybrid-entity treatment; (f) arm's-length documentation and transfer-pricing election (Article L13 AA/LP) for controlled transactions. Renewable-energy EPC contractors should also consider the specific regime for long-term construction contracts covering more than 12 months under the IS realisation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

French-established taxable persons must register for VAT as soon as taxable supplies commence; no economic turnover threshold applies for registration once operations are carried out. The small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA, Article 293 B CGI) removes the obligation to charge VAT and to register until turnover exceeds EUR 91,900 for sales of goods and EUR 38,700 for services (2025 thresholds; intermediate thresholds of EUR 101,000 and EUR 47,700 trigger loss of exemption). Non-established foreign suppliers making taxable supplies in France have no exemption threshold and must register from the first euro of French taxable turnover; for EU sellers, distance sales of goods are reportable via the Union OSS scheme once cross-border turnover exceeds the EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual threshold. For an EPC contractor invoicing French VAT-liable clients, issuance of invoice with French VAT number and monthly or quarterly TVA returns (form 3310-CA3 or 3517-SD) is required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: export supplies of goods physically dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union are legally exempt from French VAT at the 0% rate under Article 262-I-1° CGI, provided the goods leave EU customs territory within 90 days of dispatch (extendable up to 14 months for specific construction or assembly sites outside the EU, which covers solar-farm equipment shipped to non-EU projects). The exporter must hold a valid customs export declaration (DAU / ENS) with a movement reference number (MRN). Services: contract work, processing, repair, maintenance and ancillary services (including engineering and installation work) directly relating to exported goods qualify for zero-rating under Article 262-II-3° CGI if the services are physically performed on goods destined for a third country; in addition, pure B2B engineering and consultancy services supplied to an overseas customer are outside French VAT where the place of supply is the customer's country of establishment under Article 259-1 CGI, and are therefore not chargeable with French VAT. For an EPC solar contractor, materials and components exported as part of a delivery order should be covered by one single export declaration; services performed at the overseas site are offshore supplies falling outside French VAT jurisdiction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / ENS) with BAE/Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR transport document evidencing physical cross-border movement
  • Commercial invoice issued to the non-EU buyer with the legal zero-rating reference (Article 262-I-1° CGI)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/MT103 credit advice demonstrating settlement of the export proceeds
  • Delivery note and site acceptance certificate (procès-verbal de réception) for EPC milestone or completed export
  • Supplementary transport documents (packing list, insurance certificate, EUR.1/CoO where preferential tariff claims are made)
  • Proof of on-site installation / commissioning report for solar EPC components exceeding the mere supply of goods where zero-rating of services is claimed

Mandatory national e-invoicing and digital clearance (Article 289 bis CGI, Post-2026 French e-invoicing reform): all French-established businesses must receive structured electronic invoices, and phased issuance mandates apply — large and mid-size enterprises from 1 September 2026 and small/micro enterprises from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be issued in one of the authorised formats (Factur-X hybrid PDF/XML containing a 2D-Doc QR code and digital signature, UBL, CII or CEN-EN 16931 EDI) and routed through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) operated by DGFIP or through an accredited partner platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire, PDP). The PPF and PDPs perform real-time transmission and validation of invoice metadata and payment data, constituting an automatic fiscal-clearance mechanism that enables DGFIP to cross-check VAT returns, intra-community recapitulative statements and export flows. B2B invoices issued for solar EPC milestone payments must therefore bear the French VAT number of the purchaser and, where applicable, the reverse-charge or zero-rating mention; export invoices benefitting from Article 262-I CGI exemption must include a customs disposition in the invoice container and be transmitted with the underlying MRN via interconnectivity between the PPF and the national customs single window.

DGFIP and customs authorities apply strict audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. Where documentation is incomplete, the tax authorities may requalify the supply as domestic and assess French VAT at 20% at the same time as debiting the exporter for input VAT that was deducted on the purchase of exported goods, producing a full reversal clawback plus interest. Penalties under Articles 1728 to 1730 CGI apply: late-filing assessment, 10% for negligence, 40% for deliberate inaccuracies, 80% for bad-faith (mauvaise foi) concealment, and 100% for fraudulent manipulation (manœuvres frauduleuses) of export evidence. Late-payment interest is charged at 0.2% per month (Article 1727 CGI), and an additional 5% audit surcharge applies for failure to respond to requests for information. In cases of customs export fraud (Code des douanes, Article 343-1 et seq.), seizure of the goods or their monetary equivalent and fines of up to twice the value of the undeclared goods may be imposed, together with prohibition from managing a business and criminal sanctions under Article 1741 CGI for tax fraud, including imprisonment up to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. No exchange controls exist under EU law and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier). Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate tax withholding (if applicable) and anti-money laundering (AML) verification. Dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are generally exempt from withholding tax under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, provided the parent holds at least 10% for 24 months. No prior authorization is required for capital movements, but statistical reporting to Banque de France is mandatory for cross-border transactions exceeding €12,500 per month.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds exists in France. Exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts or convert freely. However, all cross-border transactions must be reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics, typically via the bank intermediary. The reporting threshold is €12,500 per transaction or cumulative monthly flows. No conversion ratio is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) and the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR) under the aegis of the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and foreign exchange operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. France recognizes Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the EU PSD2 directive, regulated by ACPR. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., from UK or non-EU) may operate in France only under a passport or a French license. For a solar EPC contractor requiring project milestone wires and LC, a full-service commercial bank is preferable. EMIs often lack LC issuance capabilities and may have lower transaction limits. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot provide credit or hold client funds for extended periods; they are not suitable for escrow accounts. Viability is conditional on the EMI having a French or EU passport and robust correspondent banking network.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Use SWIFT MT103 for direct transfers. The purpose code must be 'G100' (Investment in equity capital) or 'G200' (Loans) under the French balance-of-payments classification. For capital injection, the bank will require a copy of the shareholders' resolution, the share subscription agreement, and proof of the source of funds. The transfer must be routed through a correspondent bank with a clear reference to the beneficiary's IBAN (FR76...). Verification includes anti-money laundering checks and confirmation that the sender is the actual investor. For loans, a loan agreement and interest rate documentation are required.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For project milestone payments to suppliers or subcontractors, use SWIFT MT103 for direct payments. For larger, structured transactions, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) is recommended. The LC can be issued by the French bank and advised through a confirming bank in the beneficiary's country. For EPC contracts, a standby LC (SBLC) may be used as a performance guarantee. All outbound payments must be reported to the Banque de France if they exceed €12,500. The bank will require supporting documents (invoices, contracts, customs declarations) to ensure compliance with trade sanctions and export controls.Outright forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, etc.) with a maturity up to 12 months, available from French banks without underlying transaction requirement (subject to credit line).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible for exotic currencies (e.g., certain African or Asian currencies) where deliverable forwards are not available, but the EUR is fully convertible so NDFs are rarely used.Cross-currency swaps (EUR vs. USD or other major currencies) for long-term hedging of project cash flows, subject to ISDA documentation and credit support annex.Currency options (European style) for hedging uncertain future cash flows, available from major banks.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard rate; 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs (turnover < €10 million, share capital fully paid, 75% owned by individuals or SMEs) plus 3% social contribution surcharge on taxable income exceeding €2.5 million

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory withholding tax under Article 187-1 CGI on dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders, reduced to 0% for qualifying EU corporate shareholders meeting the Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions (minimum 5% shareholding held for 2 years) and further reduced under applicable double tax treaties for non-EU residents.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return must be filed by the second business day after 1 May (typically 3 May in 2024). For off-calendar fiscal year ends, the return is due within 30 days of the statutory deadline for shareholder approval of the annual accounts, which in all cases falls no later than 6 months from the fiscal year close.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Formal Local File and Master File are mandatory where the French entity (or the French permanent establishment) reports annual turnover or balance-sheet assets of at least €400 million. A Country-by-Country Report is additionally required where the group consolidates revenue above €750 million and the French entity is an ultimate parent, surrogate parent, or designated constituent entity. Below the €400 million threshold, documentation must still be prepared and produced in response to a DGFiP request.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a separate ESR code, but applies the abuse-of-law doctrine (Article L64 LPF), EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules, limitation-of-benefits provisions in DTAs, and the DTA permanent-establishment requirement. A French SARL carrying on solar farm EPC contracting must demonstrate actual substance (real premises in France, employees with technical authority, operational management meetings, and commercial decision-making in France); an artificial shell or treaty-shopped structure will be recharacterized, denied treaty relief, and subjected to 80% penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false corporate declarations

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment plus late-payment interest and a penalty of 80% of the unpaid tax for bad faith or fraudulent behavior; criminal conviction under Article 1741 CGI carries up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of €500,000 (or double the value of amounts evaded).

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly participates in the false declaration is personally liable for tax fraud; the court may order civil restitution of the evaded tax, a travel ban, a management prohibition up to 10 years, and disqualification from public procurement contracts.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment using the best available comparables; penalty of 5% on the reassessed amount of cross-border transfers when documentation is absent, plus a €15,000 fine per missing file; if abuse of law is established, an 80% penalty applies.

Director personal liability: If the artificial structure is found to be an abuse of DGFiP, directors may be jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; repeat or fraudulent schemes trigger the criminal penalties of Article 1741 CGI, including imprisonment, management bans, and in serious cases a travel ban.
Abuse of rights / shell company (abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: Application of Article L64 LPF results in recharacterization of the arrangement, an 80% penalty plus interest, and denial of treaty relief; where the violation triggers the concealment doctrine, a formal notice may lead to an additional 10% criminal surcharge.

Director personal liability: The courts can pierce the corporate veil: directors who are the effective authors of the abusive scheme may be personally assessed for the unpaid corporate taxes, subject to civil liability under company law, and may receive a French territorial travel ban and prohibition from managing a commercial company for up to 15 years.
Money laundering / aggravated tax fraud (AML-CTF)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 324-1 of the French Criminal Code, money laundering is punishable by a term of 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of €375,000; aggravated offences involving an organized gang or abuse of professional functions carry 10 years' imprisonment and a fine of €750,000, with asset forfeiture orders.

Director personal liability: Directors can be charged individually as principals or accomplices; the corporate veil will be ignored where the company is a mere instrument. Penalties include criminal fines, confiscation of assets, a travel ban, and an absolute prohibition on exercising any business activity in France.
