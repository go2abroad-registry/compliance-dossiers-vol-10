A purely virtual mailbox is impermissible for this high-risk industry. Under Article L.123-11-2 of the French Commercial Code, a SARL may initially domicile its registered office with a licensed domiciliation provider (domiciliataire agréé) for a maximum of two years, provided that the domiciliary provider maintains a physical business address and the company performs administrative/accounting substance there. Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry) acting as the registra for the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); filings are transmitted exclusively through the Guichet Unique operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI); INSEE allocates SIREN/SIRET and the French Tax Directorate issues the intra-community VAT number.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 to R.223-29 (SARL), Articles L.210-2 to L.210-10 (general incorporation); Code de l'Énergie, Articles L.642-4 et seq. (petroleum wholesale strategic-stock obligations); Directive (EU) 2016/802 and MARPOL Annex VI (marine fuel quality/sulphur); beneficial-owner registration under Code monétaire et financier, Article L.561-46.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'une société (Cerfa-style electronic equivalent of Form M0) together with the signed Statuts Constitutifs (Articles of Association); registration is evidenced by the Extrait Kbis.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SARL (Article L.223-2 permits capital as low as €1); cash contributions must in practice be deposited in a blocked bank account until registration; for petroleum wholesale, market practice requires €10,000–€50,000 paid-up capital and adequate financial guarantees to satisfy the CPSSP strategic-stock contribution and bank bonding obligations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification (commission de sécurité) for the head office premises and for any depot: fire-resistance compartmentation, explosion-zone structural-load inspection, and emission-control validation; workplace health-and-safety clearance through CARSAT registration, occupational health checks, fire-drill and personnel-training records, and compliance with Code du travail screening for the identified Seveso/ICPE risk classes.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SARL (Article L.223-2 permits capital as low as €1); cash contributions must in practice be deposited in a blocked bank account until registration; for petroleum wholesale, market practice requires €10,000–€50,000 paid-up capital and adequate financial guarantees to satisfy the CPSSP strategic-stock contribution and bank bonding obligations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); official portals: impots.gouv.fr for corporate tax and VAT filings, formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (INPI single business formalities portal) for entity and SIREN/SIRET registration; non-resident VAT matters are handled by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), Nantes; excise and customs controls for energy products are administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via douane.gouv.fr and the GUN/Profil Fiscal platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the French SARL through the INPI single formalities portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), obtaining SIREN, SIRET, and APE/NAF code 4661Z (Wholesale of solid, liquid and gaseous fuels and related products). Step 2: Upon SIRENE registration, the DGFiP automatically assigns the French intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN; the VAT number is delivered electronically to the entity's impots.gouv.fr professional space. Step 3: For a foreign entity not yet established in France but performing taxable supplies (e.g., commission brokerage, B2B energy trading), the taxpayer must file a direct VAT identification application with the SIEE in Nantes using Cerfa form n° 15192 (demande d'immatriculation à la TVA hors Union européenne / assujetti non établi); the SIEE issues the national VAT number and appoints the competent SIE. Step 5: Open a French-domiciled Euro account with an authorized credit institution and, if the principal is non-EU, appoint a fiscal and customs representative having authority in France.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (with Territorial_Scope): A SARL is by default a taxable corporation subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) on all profits derived from enterprises operated in France; under the French territoriality principle (Article 209-I CGI), profits attributable to genuinely distinct foreign permanent establishments are not re-included, while French-source trading, brokerage and re-invoicing profits are fully taxable. Pass_Through_Eligibility exists only by election for qualifying small SARLs; Tax_Exempt_Zone status is not applicable. The IS headline rate is 25% (standard), with the reduced 15% rate for the first €42,500 of profit in bands for SARLs with turnover below €10 million; an additional 3% contribution on distributions (Article 235 ter ZCA CGI) and the corporate social solidarity contribution (Cotisation sur la Valeur Ajoutée des Entreprises) may apply.

Available election forms

Option for income tax (pass-through) under Article 3-I CGI and Article 239 bis AA CGI: a SARL satisfying the statutory small-entity tests (fewer than 50 employees and turnover below €10 million) may elect to be taxed as a partnership (régime des sociétés de personnes) for 5 consecutive fiscal years; the election is revocable under the conditions of BOI-IS-CHAMP-40-10. Group consolidation election under Article 223 A CGI: a 95%-controlled French parent may elect 'intégration fiscale', pooling results of French subsidiaries into a single IS result. VAT single-taxable-person election under Article 256 C III CGI: qualifying related entities established in France may form one VAT 'assujetti unique'. France does not apply a US-style 'check-the-box' mechanism; instead, hybrid mismatch recharacterization rules (Articles 212 bis and 212 bis A CGI, implementing ATAD II) apply mandatorily, and the taxpayer may make an annual coherence election to align French treatment with another EU Member State's characterization of the same hybrid instrument or entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible on both current-account and capital-account transactions without exchange-control licensing. Capital movements, including direct investment, shareholder loans, profit repatriation, and trade payments, are free under TFEU Articles 63-65 and CMF Article L151-1, provided the counterparty is not listed on EU/OFAC/UN sanctions. No prior authorization is required for ordinary oil-and-refined-products brokerage settlements, but financial relations with non-EU countries must be channelled through ACPR-authorized intermediaries for statistical reporting to Banque de France. Profit remittance hurdles are limited: dividends paid by a French SARL to an EU parent may benefit from the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% withholding); dividends to non-EU recipients incur 12.8% dividend withholding tax unless reduced under an applicable double-tax treaty (e.g., 0-5% typical). The principal practical barrier to repatriation is not FX control but bank AML/KYC verification of the recipient's ultimate beneficial owners and sanctions status.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for foreign-currency receipts. The SARL may retain USD, GBP, or other foreign currency indefinitely in comptes en devises at ACPR-licensed institutions. However, CMF Articles R221-1 to R221-8 require that payments from France to foreign countries be executed through a recognized credit institution or authorized payment intermediary so that Banque de France can compile balance-of-payments statistics; this is a reporting obligation on the intermediary, not a surrender or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) is the central bank and target balance-of-payments statistical authority; prudential licensing and AML supervision for credit institutions and payment/EMI service providers is exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) within the Banque de France umbrella. Foreign-exchange and capital-movement rules derive from the Code monétaire et financier (CMF), particularly Articles L151-1 through L165-1, implementing EU Treaty freedoms.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary settlement rail; MEDIUM-LOW for auxiliary treasury services. A French SARL may open accounts with ACPR-licensed EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Treezor, Lydia) or passported EU EMIs, but ACPR-regulated EMIs cannot perform the following critical functions for a marine-fuel brokerage: issue standby or documentary letters of credit, grant trade credit, or cover US-dollar SWIFT high-value commodities flows at competitive rates. Offshore digital EMIs located outside the EEA (e.g., UK or Singapore) have no EU pass-porting post-Brexit and would require a French branch or agent; most French banks reject payments routed via non-EEA EMIs for high-risk commodities due to AML opacity. Legal constraints: EMI accounts are segregated e-money accounts, not deposits, and confer no payment guarantee under the French deposit-guarantee scheme (Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution); onboarding AML requirements are typically lighter but high-volume energy trading will trigger daily velocity limits and enhanced due-diligence freezes. Recommendation: use Tier-1 banks for settlement and an ACPR-licensed EMI only for petty operational foreign-exchange or payroll top-ups.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contribution or shareholder loan from a foreign parent: wire via SWIFT MT103 with field 50K (remitter foreign entity), field 57A/56A (beneficiary French bank and correspondent), field 59 (FR SARL account), and field 70 clearly labelled with purpose code 'CAPITAL INCREASE' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN' and reference to the French direct-investment notification regime. Under CMF and Banque de France BOP rules, the receiving bank must report the inflow to Banque de France within 5 business days as a direct-investment flow; no pre-clearance is required unless the investor is from a non-EU country and the investee operates in a sensitive sector (energy is not among the four pre-screened categories for indiscriminate control of hydrocarbons; but the 'energy supply' sector may trigger FDI prior-authorization if the investor acquires control of an entity controlling key energy infrastructure - this SARL is a pure broker, so no prior filing is expected). Verification: French bank will hold funds in a Capital Escrow Account until share-capital increase is perfected through publication in a legal journal, statutory amendment filing, and KBIS update; for shareholder loans, the bank will require a written loan agreement, arm's-length interest rate (within the allowed safe-harbour range per bond/draft), and subordination clause where applicable; both routes require proof of origin of funds and passport copies of the lender.Trade settlements to foreign suppliers and freight counterparties should be executed directly on SWIFT MT103 with full field 70 remittance information (invoice number, contract reference, port of loading, vessel name if applicable). For high-value million-dollar cargoes of marine fuel oil, purchase counterparties generally request a documentary credit; the SARL should open a commercial import letter of credit governed by UCP 600 and possibly eUCP, payable at sight, with documents including commercial invoice, full set of bills of lading/warehouse warrants, certificate of quality and quantity, notice of readiness, and insurance certificate where CFR/CIF; for export sales, the SARL may demand a standby letter of credit (SBLC) under ISP98 from the buyer's issuing bank, especially for Turkish, pan-African, or Middle-Eastern buyers. All USD payables clear via the US Federal Reserve network through the French bank's correspondent account (typically BNP Paribas New York/JPMorgan Chase), attracting correspondent-bank fees of $30-$80 per wire; EUR payables inside SEPA are free and instant (Target2). To avoid duplicate clearing, the SARL should maintain a dedicated USD concentration account (vostro) with one correspondent bank and a EUR operational account in France, with daily sweeping between them.Outright EUR/USD and EUR/GBP forward contracts with ACPR-licensed credit institutions, maturing 1-12 months, covering forecast receivable/payable flows; required to be documented under ISDA or a bank's French-law master agreement (Convention-cadre FBF) with daily collateral margining.FX swaps (swap de change) for near-dated domestic versus foreign-currency funding bridges, typically 1-7 days, permissible as balance-management tools for matched oil-cargo payments.Cross-currency swaps (basis swaps) for hedging longer-term foreign-currency inventory or ship-financing liabilities, permitted for EUR/USD and EUR/GBP under French financial-market rules (AMF regulation).Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted non-convertible currencies (e.g., certain LATAM, CIS, MEA emerging currencies) where the EUR/USD leg is deliverable and the non-deliverable leg is cash-settled in EUR; NDFs on EUR/USD itself are not permissible as that market is fully deliverable and any NDF would be considered unregulated speculative over-the-counter activity without proper ISDA/CSA documentation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for primary settlement rail; MEDIUM-LOW for auxiliary treasury services. A French SARL may open accounts with ACPR-licensed EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Treezor, Lydia) or passported EU EMIs, but ACPR-regulated EMIs cannot perform the following critical functions for a marine-fuel brokerage: issue standby or documentary letters of credit, grant trade credit, or cover US-dollar SWIFT high-value commodities flows at competitive rates. Offshore digital EMIs located outside the EEA (e.g., UK or Singapore) have no EU pass-porting post-Brexit and would require a French branch or agent; most French banks reject payments routed via non-EEA EMIs for high-risk commodities due to AML opacity. Legal constraints: EMI accounts are segregated e-money accounts, not deposits, and confer no payment guarantee under the French deposit-guarantee scheme (Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution); onboarding AML requirements are typically lighter but high-volume energy trading will trigger daily velocity limits and enhanced due-diligence freezes. Recommendation: use Tier-1 banks for settlement and an ACPR-licensed EMI only for petty operational foreign-exchange or payroll top-ups.
