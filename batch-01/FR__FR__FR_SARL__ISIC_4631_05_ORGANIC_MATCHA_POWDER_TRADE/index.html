Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); official taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel); business registration via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

The French SARL is incorporated through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises; INSEE issues the statutory SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) identifiers, after which DGFiP activates the tax file at the competent SIE and allocates the intra-Community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 11 characters). Non-established foreign entities carrying out taxable French transactions must apply directly for French VAT registration at the SIE (CGI Art. 286) and, when established outside the EU, generally must appoint a DGFiP-accredited fiscal representative (CGI Art. 289 A) for VAT filings and refunds.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the SARL is subject by default to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on its worldwide taxable profits; the standard IS rate in mainland France is 25%, and an additional 3.3% social solidarity contribution applies to IS exceeding EUR 763,000 for high-turnover companies. The SARL is not tax-transparent by default and is scoped according to permanent-establishment and treaty territorial rules. — The French SARL is incorporated through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises; INSEE issues the statutory SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) identifiers, after which DGFiP activates the tax file at the competent SIE and allocates the intra-Community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 11 characters). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the SARL is subject by default to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on its worldwide taxable profits; the standard IS rate in mainland France is 25%, and an additional 3.3% social solidarity contribution applies to IS exceeding EUR 763,000 for high-turnover companies. The SARL is not tax-transparent by default and is scoped according to permanent-establishment and treaty territorial rules.

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box regime. Under CGI Art. 239 bis AA, a qualifying closely held SARL owned by natural persons may elect the pass-through/partnership tax status (régime fiscal des sociétés de personnes, taxed at impôt sur le revenu) for a renewable five-fiscal-year period; the election is filed with the corporate income tax return and binds all members. Elective group relief is available through the parent-subsidiary regime (régime mère-fille) and tax consolidation (intégration fiscale), while ATAD hybrid mismatch rules apply mandatorily without elective alignment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for established taxable persons; a French SARL effecting taxable matcha-trading supplies must be VAT-registered from the first euro of taxable turnover (CGI Arts. 256 and 286). For non-established sellers, the annual EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-sales threshold applies to intra-Community B2C distance sales; the existence of a French warehouse, fulfilment centre, or permanent establishment removes the threshold and triggers compulsory French VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Matcha powder physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU is exempt from French VAT (zero-rated) when the seller establishes that the goods left EU customs territory within 90 days of the supply by documentary evidence (CGI Art. 262-I; BOI-TVA-CHAMP-30-20). Intra-Community supplies to an EU VAT-identified customer are exempt where the customer's valid intra-Community VAT number is quoted on the invoice and the recapitulative statement/EC Sales List is filed. International B2B auxiliary services (freight, insurance, inspection, store-keeping) supplied to a customer established outside the EU are outside French VAT by the place-of-supply rule (CGI Art. 259), and input VAT attributable to zero-rated export sales remains deductible.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export declaration lodged through the French Single Window/DELTA customs system)
  • Bill of Lading (connaissement) or equivalent transport document (sea waybill, air waybill, CMR consignment note) proving physical dispatch outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: SWIFT MT103 credit advice or bank statement evidencing receipt of the foreign sales proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial export invoice, order documentation, and certificate of origin or health/certification papers for organic ceremonial-grade matcha
  • Intra-Community recapitulative declaration (Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens and EC Sales List) for goods delivered to VAT-registered customers in other EU member states

Phased mandatory structured e-invoicing for all domestic B2B transactions through an accredited Dématérialisation Partenaire platform (PDP) or the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF), using standard formats Factur-X, UBL, or CII, with electronic signature/sealing and archiving obligations: from 1 September 2026 all French-established taxable persons must be able to receive e-invoices and large/mid-size taxpayers must issue them; from 1 September 2027 small and micro taxpayers must also issue them. Cross-border B2B and B2C transaction data are submitted by e-reporting to DGFiP through the same transmission channels; no SDI-style real-time pre-approval is currently required, but platform custody and clean-data invoice numbering apply.

DGFiP reconciles export zero-ratings against customs exit data and banking flows; missing, inconsistent, or late export documentation triggers reinstatement of French output VAT, disallowance of related input VAT, 0.20%-per-month statutory late interest (CGI Art. 1727), and penalties of 40% or 80% for bad-faith misdeclaration, fraud, or obstruction (CGI Arts. 1729 and 1732). Circular or staged export arrangements, false certificates of shipment, and systematic non-repatriation of proceeds may additionally trigger criminal tax-fraud proceedings (CGI Art. 1741), customs seizure, joint personal liability of the SARL gérant, and exclusion from simplified legalization and export-refund procedures.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no general French foreign-exchange approval, capital-control registration, mandatory repatriation requirement, or profit-conversion restriction for a French SARL. Dividends, interest, royalties, shareholder-loan repayments, and trade payments may generally be remitted abroad through regulated payment institutions, subject to corporate-law solvency and distributable-profit rules, withholding-tax requirements, transfer-pricing documentation, sanctions screening, beneficial-owner verification, and bank evidence of the underlying transaction. EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules, tax reporting, customs controls, and possible foreign-investment or national-security screening may still restrict particular counterparties or transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or universal deadline for converting or repatriating export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in euros or foreign currency and credited to accounts in France or elsewhere, subject to bank due diligence, accounting records, customs evidence, sanctions controls, and applicable tax reporting. The company should retain commercial invoices, transport and customs records, contracts, payment confirmations, and reconciliation records sufficient to establish the economic purpose and source of funds.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank; prudential and anti-money-laundering supervision is also performed by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution and relevant European Union authorities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for operational payments, foreign-currency collections, expense management, and SEPA transfers where the provider is licensed as an EU electronic-money institution or payment institution and passported or otherwise authorised to serve France. An offshore digital EMI without EU authorisation, passporting, a regulated correspondent, or satisfactory French customer due diligence is not a reliable substitute for a French bank account and may be unable to hold client funds, provide a French IBAN, process regulated payment services, or support capital escrow. Fintech accounts should not be used for statutory capital deposits unless the provider and the receiving notary or bank expressly confirm legal acceptance. The SARL remains responsible for sanctions screening, AML controls, accounting evidence, VAT and customs reconciliation, and payment-service-provider limits.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a new SARL, route subscribed capital to a French bank's blocked capital escrow account or another institution legally accepted by the receiving notary or registration process. Use a transfer from the shareholder's own verified account, with the beneficiary name matching the SARL or the designated capital-deposit arrangement. State the transfer purpose as paid-in share capital or capital increase, identify the shareholder and subscription documents, and avoid unexplained third-party remitters. Provide the bank with the signed articles, subscription or capital-increase resolution, shareholder register, ownership chart, Kbis or formation file, beneficial-owner information, identification documents, and source-of-funds evidence. After incorporation or registration, obtain the deposit certificate and request release to the operating account. For subsequent shareholder loans or equity injections, use a separate documented purpose, executed loan or subscription agreement, board or shareholder approval where required, and accounting classification. Incoming EUR payments should preferably use SEPA Credit Transfer; non-EUR or non-SEPA payments should use SWIFT with complete originator, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, purpose, and fee instructions. Reconcile each receipt to the general ledger and retain the wire confirmation.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments to eligible European suppliers and customers, with verified IBAN and beneficiary-name controls. Use SWIFT MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent for non-SEPA or foreign-currency payments, with complete ordering-customer, beneficiary, intermediary-bank, invoice, and purpose information. Use documentary commercial letters of credit, preferably confirmed where counterparty or country risk justifies confirmation, for new or higher-risk suppliers; define documentary conditions precisely, including commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, certificate of origin, organic certificate where contractually required, insurance document, inspection evidence, and customs-related records. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to High for operational payments, foreign-currency collections, expense management, and SEPA transfers where the provider is licensed as an EU electronic-money institution or payment institution and passported or otherwise authorised to serve France. An offshore digital EMI without EU authorisation, passporting, a regulated correspondent, or satisfactory French customer due diligence is not a reliable substitute for a French bank account and may be unable to hold client funds, provide a French IBAN, process regulated payment services, or support capital escrow. Fintech accounts should not be used for statutory capital deposits unless the provider and the receiving notary or bank expressly confirm legal acceptance. The SARL remains responsible for sanctions screening, AML controls, accounting evidence, VAT and customs reconciliation, and payment-service-provider limits.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard French corporate income tax rate applicable for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2022; transitional rates of 26.5% for 2021 and 28% for 2020 do not apply to a newly established SARL in the current filing cycle unless grandfathering arises).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed by a French SARL to a non-resident corporate shareholder (Article 119 bis and 243 bis CGI); where the shareholder is an individual, the flat levy of 12.8% plus 17.2% social levies applies (total 30%) unless treaty relief is established.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December: the return must be filed by 3 May of the following year; an extended deadline of 15 May applies when the return is filed electronically via the authorised EDI/EFI channel. For any fiscal year ending on another date: the deadline is the end of the third month following the close of the fiscal year. A supplementary declaration of items and tax credits (for example, Form 2069-A for the R&D credit) is due within the same statutory period; the balance of corporate income tax is payable electronically on the 15th day of the month following the filing date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File under Article L. 13 AA of the French Book of Tax Procedures apply when the French SARL (together with associated French enterprises) has annual gross turnover or total balance sheet assets of at least €400,000,000. Separately, when the ultimate parent group's consolidated turnover reaches €750,000,000, the French entity is obliged to file a Country-by-Country report (Form 2258-SD) and retain the Master File and Local File. Below these thresholds, there is no automatic annual filing obligation, but the French tax administration may serve a formal request (jeu de documents) during an audit and the taxpayer has 30 days to produce all transactional benchmark documentation. In practice, an SARL engaged in cross-border matcha procurement and distribution should maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation wherever controlled transactions with the Japanese or other non-resident parent/affiliates exceed €1,000,000 in aggregate, and must be able to demonstrate an arm's-length margin for its distribution function.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) analogous to the UAE or other offshore jurisdictions; however, the concept of real economic substance is embedded in French anti-abuse jurisprudence, the principal purpose test of Article 29 of the OECD Model, and the abuse-of-law doctrine (Article L. 64 of the French Book of Tax Procedures). A matcha powder distribution SARL must demonstrably maintain a physical French office or warehouse, employ personnel with decision-making authority in France, keep complete local accounting records, and execute procurement, logistics, inventory and sales functions locally. If the French SARL operates as a letterbox company without substance, the French tax authorities may re-characterise the interposed arrangements, deny treaty benefits, and impose the 80% penalty for abusive tax arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Tax penalties: 40% surcharge for serious misstatement (Article 1729 CGI) or 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres, plus 0.20% monthly late interest. Criminal penalties under Article 1741 CGI: imprisonment of the managing director for up to five years, fine of up to €500,000 for the legal entity, and publication of the judgment at the convicted party's expense.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is available under Article L. 223-22 of the French Commercial Code and the 'action en comblement du passif' doctrine: directors who committed faults causing the tax debt may be judicially ordered to satisfy the SARL's tax liabilities personally; travel bans (interdiction de sortie du territoire), prohibition of commercial management for fifteen years, seizure of personal assets, and imprisonment are all judicially available in aggravated tax evasion cases.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law (Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may invoke Article L. 64 of the Book of Tax Procedures to nullify the artificial arrangement and re-tax the transaction as if it had not been interposed; the 80% abusive-arrangement penalty applies in addition to the corrected tax and interest; treaty relief is automatically withdrawn if the principal purpose is tax avoidance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly executed the sham are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the evaded tax, civil damages to minority shareholders, and may be barred from exercising managerial functions; criminal exposure arises where the scheme is aggravated by cross-border money laundering, leading to confiscation of assets and imprisonment.
Transfer Price Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Correction under Article 57 CGI with re-computation of the French taxable basis; penalties of 40% (or 80% in case of deliberate fraudulent transfer pricing) plus late interest at 0.20% monthly; a formal documentation summons under Article L. 13 AA carries a €10,000 fine for non-compliance and per-request €15,000 for repeat failure.

Director personal liability: The managing director may be civilly liable to compensate the SARL for the additional taxes, penalties and interest if the transfer-pricing policy was an abnormal management act (acte anormal de gestion); French courts have ordered personal restitution where the director knowingly signed artificial invoice flows; aggravated cases involving export to sanctions-affected jurisdictions additionally trigger travel restrictions and criminal charges.
Dividend-Stripping and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of the reduced withholding tax rate, reassessment at 25% (legal entities), 30% (individuals) plus anti-abuse penalty of 80% under the purpose test; late-payment interest is charged on the restored amounts; specific French anti-avoidance provisions (Article 119 ter CGI for intra-EU loopholes) allow retroactive reclassification of the distribution as taxable dividends.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the distribution knowing it was designed to exploit treaty provisions are jointly liable for the shortfall in withholding taxes; where the SARL is insolvent, the director's personal assets can be reached; criminal sanctions are possible where manifestly fraudulent declarations were made to the tax authorities in support of the reduced rate.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines under the French Monetary and Financial Code of up to €7,500 for the legal entity and up to €30,000 for directors for each omitted declaration; freezing of bank accounts upon suspicion of money-laundering; a 75% withholding tax on income remitted to a non-cooperative jurisdiction combined with denial of deductibility; criminal prosecution for laundering proceeds of tax fraud carries a five-year prison sentence and €375,000 fine for the entity.

Director personal liability: The director acts as the SARL's legal representative and must exercise gatekeeper duties; French courts can order the director to personally return illicitly transferred funds; combined AML-tax sanctions allow a travel ban, confiscation of corporate and personal assets, and a commercial ban of up to fifteen years for being the effective conduit of the unlawful arrangement.
