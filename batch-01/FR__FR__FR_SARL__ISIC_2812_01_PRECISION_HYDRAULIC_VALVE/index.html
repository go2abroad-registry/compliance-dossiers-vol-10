Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – portal impots.gouv.fr for corporate income tax and VAT administration; Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) – portal douane.gouv.fr for customs export clearance and EORI registration.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident entity establishing a French SARL: (1) file incorporation documents through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, operated by INPI); (2) the SARL obtains SIREN and RCS registration, and DGFiP automatically issues an intra-community VAT identification number; (3) if the foreign entity is not established in France but performs direct taxable supplies, it must appoint a French fiscal representative (intermédiaire fiscal) and apply for a French VAT ID under Article 277 CGI (or register directly if established in the EU); (4) register for an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number with DGDDI if goods are exported outside the EU Customs territory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Option for French income tax (impôt sur le revenu) for small SARLs under Article 239 bis AA CGI when comprising at least two natural persons and meeting size tests; election for tax consolidation under the régime d'intégration fiscale (Article 223 A CGI) for groups with at least 95% common control; branch election allowing a foreign corporation to be taxed only on profits attributable to a French permanent establishment; hybrid mismatch notification and reporting elections required under EU ATAD into French law (Article 212 bis CGI).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic threshold; full VAT liability from first taxable transaction in France. Small-enterprise exemption (franchise en base de TVA) applies below EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services, but is an optional relief, not a registration threshold. Intra-community distance sales threshold for EU sellers: EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supply of goods physically transported outside the European Union qualifies for 0% VAT (Article 262 I CGI) provided the vendor proves export dispatch within three months of supply. Services directly linked to exported goods (e.g., engineering, calibration, installation of precision hydraulic valve systems) may be zero-rated under Article 259-A-1° CGI if the services relate to property outside France. Export of goods to another EU Member State is exempt under intra-community dispatch rules (Article 262 ter CGI), subject to a valid EU VAT number and recapitulative statement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU with valid Movement Reference Number and export summary declaration through DGDDI's DELTA system)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document showing consignee outside EU
  • Commercial invoice stating French VAT at 0% and citing 'exportation' or 'exonération TVA'
  • Inward remittance certificate (SWIFT MT103 / bank statement evidencing payment from the foreign buyer)
  • Supporting export file (email correspondence, packing list, cargo insurance certificates) retained for six years

Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing phased in from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises, transmitted to the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF) or through certified Partner Dematerialisation Platforms (PDPs) using the Y-clearing (non-intermediated) model. E-reporting of transaction data is mandatory for all non-exempt supplies. Invoices must comply with EN 16931 (Factur-X or UBL) and no real-time fiscal stamp is applied; the PPF receives structured data for tax control.

DGFiP and DGDDI cross-check export declarations against payment flows and transport records. Improper or fraudulent zero-rating triggers immediate VAT recovery plus late-interest (interest at 0.2% per month) and penalties under Article 1788 A CGI: 40% of the tax amount for negligence, 80% for deliberate intent, 100% for organised fraud or abusive manoeuvres. Customs authorities may also impose separate fines under Article 414 of the French Customs Code for irregular export declarations. For a deliberate fraudulent claim, the responsible managers may face personal liability and criminal sanctions (up to EUR 1.5 million fine and imprisonment for aggravated tax fraud).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible and France maintains a completely liberalised capital and current account regime under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No exchange control approvals, quotas, prioritisation, or licensing requirements apply to trade transactions, profit remittances, dividend distributions, or capital injections by a French SARL. Profit distribution to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted from an FX perspective and requires only corporate formalities: approval of the annual accounts by the associates, satisfaction of the balance-sheet impairment test (net assets must exceed share capital plus non-distributable reserves), filing of the distribution minutes with the tax administration, and the applicable dividend withholding tax of 30 percent under French domestic law unless reduced by an income tax treaty. Payments are screened solely for EU financial sanctions, anti-money-laundering obligations, and FATCA/CRS reporting requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no time limit on retention of export receipts. A French SARL may hold export proceeds indefinitely in EUR or in foreign currency accounts either in France or with authorised banks abroad. The only non-prudential requirements are (i) customs export declarations submitted through the DELTA/DEB electronic system, (ii) statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque de France for transactions exceeding the reporting threshold (ordinarily EUR 750,000 per transaction or aggregated flows, with reporting performed by the resident bank or the company), and (iii) compliance with the EU Consolidated Financial Sanctions List when the originating buyer is located in a sanctioned jurisdiction.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (the French central bank), acting together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) within the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; prudential banking supervision and AML/KYC enforcement over credit institutions are exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), an autonomous authority attached to the Banque de France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a complementary disbursement wallet, LOW as the sole treasury bank for an industrial exporter. French e-money institutions and payment institutions under Directive 2009/110/EC (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Lydia Pay) and EU passporting EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Bunq) can open operational accounts remotely for a French SARL using the Kbis extract, beneficial-owner declaration, and video KYC. However, an EMI is not a credit institution and therefore cannot lawfully grant credit, open or confirm commercial letters of credit, provide overdraft facilities, or act as an issuing agent for trade guarantees. EMI client funds are safeguarded rather than held as bank deposits, and account statements do not carry the full statutory audit weight of a licensed banque-assureur confirmation. Consequently, the primary clearing account and the capital-escrow account must sit with a fully licensed French credit institution, while EMIs may serve ancillary collection and cross-border sub-account purposes.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident shareholder chain with an intermediate holding entity in a non-EU or low-transparency jurisdiction.

Root cause: Non-resident shareholder chain with an intermediate holding entity in a non-EU or low-transparency jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Provide the current Kbis extract; the Declaration of Beneficial Owner (Formulaire de bénéficiaire effectif) filed with the RCS; a complete organizational chart from each natural person to the ultimate shareholder; notarised and apostilled passport copies of each director and shareholder; share register (registre des mouvements de titres); 12-month source-of-funds bank statements; tax residence certificates of all physical persons; legal opinion from the entity's French counsel on the FATF Recommendation 24 beneficial-ownership standard; and, where a tax-haven-flagged jurisdiction is present, a full economic-substance file comprising local premises, payroll, and real business purpose evidence.
Potential dual-use or export-control exposure arising from precision hydraulic and fluid-power component manufacturing.

Root cause: Potential dual-use or export-control exposure arising from precision hydraulic and fluid-power component manufacturing.

Defense evidence: Although ISIC 2812 high-pressure fluid-power valves are not listed in Annex I of EU Dual-Use Regulation 2021/821, banks will screen the product diligently: assemble technical data sheets showing non-military specifications (pressure ratings, industrial tolerances, compliance with Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC and Pressure Equipment Directive 2014/68/EU); furnish the CE certificate, customs export declarations in DELTA/DEB, INCOTERMS 2020 contract, signed end-user undertakings and non-diversion clauses, and an export-compliance code signed by the gérant; maintain segregated procedures excluding military-grade or weapon-system configurations and reject any transaction involving the EU consolidated sanctions list.

Root cause: High-velocity inbound or outbound wire transfers concentrated in the first six months with unrelated third-country counterparties, creating a straw-man or round-tripping red flag.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital subscriptions and paid-in contributions from a non-resident parent must be executed by SWIFT MT103 or, for EU-based shareholders, by SEPA Credit Transfer referencing the capital increase resolution. The remittance instruction must state the purpose as apport en numéraire (cash contribution) or augmentation de capital (capital increase), the corporate LEI, the beneficiary IBAN held with the French tier-1 bank, and the resolution date. A bank attestation of funds deposit (attestation de dépôt des fonds) must be obtained from the receiving bank, followed by filing of the modified articles and the M2 form with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce and publication in a legal gazette. France does not maintain a government-issued purpose-code registry such as the Indian FIRMS regime; instead the French reservation focuses on sanctions screening and balance-of-payments statistics. For non-EU foreign investment in a non-regulated industrial activity, no prior authorisation is required under Decree 2019-1590; however, a statistical declaration may be collected by DG Trésor when the investment reaches the reporting threshold. The capital account should never be commingled with operational trade receipts; a dedicated Capital Escrow Account (compte séquestre) preserves the clean audit trail required by the French statutory auditor (commissaire aux comptes).Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for all EU and SEPA-area supplier invoices, referencing the supplier IBAN and the French or EU VAT number; use SWIFT MT103 with full covering transfer details for non-SEPA third-country suppliers, placing the commercial invoice number and vessel/flight bill of lading number in Field 70-72. For large original-equipment contracts above EUR 500,000, issue or advise an irrevocable documentary letter of credit under the ICC Uniform Customs and Practice UCP600, with settlement through TARGET2 for EUR leg and through correspondent banking (CHIPS, CHATS, or CMU) for USD, GBP, and JPY legs. Each outbound payment must be underpinned by a valid customs export declaration in DELTA/DEB, the zero-rated export invoice, the intra-EU EC Sales List filing where applicable under Article 262 ter of the French General Tax Code, and the delivery evidence (CMR, bill of lading, or waybill). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a complementary disbursement wallet, LOW as the sole treasury bank for an industrial exporter. French e-money institutions and payment institutions under Directive 2009/110/EC (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Lydia Pay) and EU passporting EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Bunq) can open operational accounts remotely for a French SARL using the Kbis extract, beneficial-owner declaration, and video KYC. However, an EMI is not a credit institution and therefore cannot lawfully grant credit, open or confirm commercial letters of credit, provide overdraft facilities, or act as an issuing agent for trade guarantees. EMI client funds are safeguarded rather than held as bank deposits, and account statements do not carry the full statutory audit weight of a licensed banque-assureur confirmation. Consequently, the primary clearing account and the capital-escrow account must sit with a fully licensed French credit institution, while EMIs may serve ancillary collection and cross-border sub-account purposes.
