Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum legal share capital is imposed for a French SARL; the statutes freely fix the capital that must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be paid up to at least one-fifth (20%) of the subscribed amount upon subscription, the balance payable within five years. The paid-up funds must be deposited with a lawyer, notary, or a prohibited-payment regulated bank account pending registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the Commission Consultative Départementale de la Sécurité et de l’Accessibilité for visited premises; structural load inspection and approved compliance certificate for the building; occupational workplace clearance through the local health and safety inspection unit ensuring conformity with the Labour Code (Articles R.4212-1 et seq.) for ventilation, lighting, personal protective equipment, and machine safety guards.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), within the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; centralized business registration via the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent establishing a French SARL must file the incorporation dossier through the Guichet Unique (single window operated by INPI) with the statutory declarations (statuts, registered-office lease, proof of manager identity and foreign criminal-record extract, compliance with non-resident ownership rules). Upon registration, the company is issued SIREN/SIRET numbers and the DGFiP automatically allocates the French intra-Community VAT identification number (format: FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN) via the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises. Issuance typically occurs within 1–2 weeks after the company becomes effective (extrait Kbis). The VAT number must be activated in the VIES database; the company must then file the standard VAT status declaration (déclaration de TVA et/ou option fiscale) on impots.gouv.fr and may need to appoint a fiscal representative if it has no French tax domicile for VAT filings.

Upon registration, the company is issued SIREN/SIRET numbers and the DGFiP automatically allocates the French intra-Community VAT identification number (format: FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN) via the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box election. A SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level. Statutory elections available: (i) fiscal transparency election for small family SARLs under Article 239 bis AA CGI (all shareholders natural persons, turnover and workforce limits, option valid for five fiscal years under conditions, making profits/losses taxable in shareholders' incomes); (ii) VAT group election (assujetti unique) under Articles 256 C III and 286 quater CGI for closely related French entities, with joint and several liability for a renewable five-year period; (iii) fiscal integration (régime de groupe, Art. 223 A CGI) for a French parent holding at least 95% of the SARL for 12 months; and (iv) optional approach to euro-denominated accounting and immediate deduction of certain provisions. Hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD/Art. 212 bis CGI) are mandatory and cannot be elected out of; taxpayers may, however, file binding rulings (rescrit) with DGFiP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold for registration: any taxable person carrying out an independent economic activity in France must register for VAT from the first taxable transaction (Articles 256 et seq. and 286 CGI). The small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA, Art. 293 B CGI) of €91,900 of annual turnover (2024–2025) for the supply of goods, including manufactured wooden toys, relieves the SARL from charging VAT but does not excuse registration; taxable persons making intra-Community acquisitions or exercising the option to tax cannot use the franchise. If turnover exceeds €91,900 (with transitional tolerance up to €113,500), mandatory VAT charging begins from the day of overshoot.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations are zero-rated (véritable exonération with right to deduct input VAT) under Article 262-I-1° CGI, provided the goods physically leave EU customs territory, title and risk pass to the foreign buyer, and the SARL retains the complete proof bundle. Delivery to non-EU Amazon fulfillment centers (US/UK) qualifies as an export only if formally customs-cleared for export from the EU. Intra-Community supplies to a buyer holding a valid EU VAT registration are zero-rated under Article 262 ter CGI subject to issuance of a complete invoice, verification of the buyer's VAT number in VIES, and submission of EC Sales Lists (DEB) within the statutory deadlines; proof of transport must be gathered before the end of the month following the supply (Art. 289 A CGI). Delivery of goods to Amazon French or EU fulfillment centers does not qualify as an export and is domestic/ICV-eligible only with the buyer's VAT ID.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / DAU) lodged via DELTA or GUN-X, identifying the SARL as exporter and the exit customs office from EU territory
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical dispatch and proof of exit from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of the foreign exchange / payment of the invoice by the non-EU customer
  • Commercial invoice on company letterhead matching Incoterms, payment terms, and the DAU
  • Proof of the foreign buyer's non-EU status and delivery acknowledgement/customs seal of the destination territory
  • For intra-Community zero-rating: buyer's valid EU VAT identification number, EC Sales List (DEB), and transport evidence gathered within the statutory deadline of Article 289 A CGI

France is implementing a phased national e-invoicing mandate (réforme de la facturation électronique) for B2B transactions. As of 1 September 2026, all French VAT debtors must receive structured e-invoices through the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation — PPF) or a DGFiP-certified partner platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire — PDP); issuance of e-invoices becomes mandatory for large and mid-size enterprises from that date and for all taxable persons (including the SARL) from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be in Factur-X, UBL, or CII formats pursuant to European standard EN 16931; the PDP/PPF performs format and semantic controls (receipt validation) but there is no pre-clearance 'digital stamp' by DGFiP. B2C sales are outside the e-invoicing obligation but require transactional and payment-data e-reporting to the PPF within ten working days after the calendar month end. For intra-Community transactions, e-reporting complements the EC Sales Lists (DEB).

DGFiP applies strict documentary enforcement: the statute of limitations for reassessment is three years (Art. L. 176 LPF for VAT), extended up to ten years in cases of fraud or abuse of law (Art. L. 169, L. 64 LPF). Unsubstantiated zero-rating is reclassified as a taxable domestic supply, and the SARL is assessed for outstanding output VAT, late-payment interest (0.20% monthly, Art. 1727 CGI), and penalties equal to 40% (bad faith) or 80% (fraudulent maneuvers) of the rectified sums under Article 1729 CGI. Criminal sanctions for tax fraud apply under Article 1741 CGI: up to 5 years' imprisonment and €500,000 fine, increased to 7 years and €3,000,000 when committed by an organized gang. The gérant (manager) may be held jointly and severally liable for the company's VAT debt (Art. L. 266 LPF); failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days under French foreign exchange rules triggers additional administrative and penal consequences.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full euro convertibility on both current and capital account transactions under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the Code Monétaire et Financier. No exchange-control authorisation is required for dividend distributions, royalty payments, loan repayments, or repatriation of capital to non-resident shareholders. Practical obstacles are limited to anti-money-laundering source-of-funds documentation, balance-of-payments statistical reporting to the Banque de France for transfers exceeding €10,000 (financial relations with foreign countries), and French dividend withholding tax (standard 30% domestic rate, reducible under an applicable double tax treaty where the shareholder is treaty-entitled).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation, or conversion of export proceeds. Exporters are free to hold foreign currency balances in territorialised foreign-currency accounts (comptes en devises) or convert at market rates through the interbank foreign-exchange market. The only obligation is statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque de France for individual cross-border transfers or aggregate foreign-exchange relations exceeding the regulatory thresholds (per-transaction/aggregate annual threshold framework under the French foreign-exchange regulations).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem, in coordination with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking supervision and the Direction Générale du Trésor (Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Relance) for foreign-exchange and capital-movement policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for ancillary payment flows (Amazon Pay, Stripe payouts, marketplace collections) via an ACPR-licensed French e-money institution or an EEA-passported EMI (e.g., Qonto, Mangopay, Lydia Solutions). Not viable as the authorised repository for statutory capital: EMIs cannot hold a blocked capital account (compte bloqué), cannot provide credit, current-account overdrafts, or documentary credit services, and are not full CRR credit institutions capable of issuing the attestation de blocage required for SARL capital formation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For Eurozone shareholders, capital injections route via SEPA Instant or SEPA Credit Transfer in ISO 20022 XML into the SARL's blocked capital account (compte bloqué) at the French credit institution. For non-EU shareholders, use SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary IBAN and BIC of the blocked account. The payment narrative must state 'Apport en numéraire / Augmentation de capital'; use EU purpose category F05 (direct investment) or standard code DEIN for inward direct investment where an explicit purpose code is mandatory. The bank verifies the transfer against the attestation de blocage and issues the deposit certificate required for the KBis post-filing; no administrative pre-approval is needed, and Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting is handled by the receiving bank for transactions exceeding €10,000.Intra-EU supplier settlements (beechwood lumber, packaging, pigments) are executed via SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 XML) and SEPA Instant for urgent disbursements; extra-EU payments are cleared via SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary bank (either a commercial letter of credit under UCP 600 or documentary collection for large consignments), accompanied by commercial invoices, bill of lading/CMR, and Customs export declarations (ECS) to satisfy balance-of-payments statistics. Export proceeds remain freely held in euro or foreign currency; there is no mandatory conversion or surrender. Amazon Pay and Stripe marketplace settlements are onboarded as separate payout rails into the operational euro account, with applicable FX conversion executed at market rates.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for ancillary payment flows (Amazon Pay, Stripe payouts, marketplace collections) via an ACPR-licensed French e-money institution or an EEA-passported EMI (e.g., Qonto, Mangopay, Lydia Solutions). Not viable as the authorised repository for statutory capital: EMIs cannot hold a blocked capital account (compte bloqué), cannot provide credit, current-account overdrafts, or documentary credit services, and are not full CRR credit institutions capable of issuing the attestation de blocage required for SARL capital formation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million consolidated group turnover threshold mandates Master File; Local File documentation and transfer pricing declaration (Formulaire 2257-SD) are mandatory for any French entity with related-party transactions exceeding EUR 50,000 in aggregate per fiscal year, regardless of group size. For the subject SARL, documentation is required if intercompany purchases of timber, sales of finished toys, or IP charges exceed EUR 50,000 annually, or if the entity operates in a jurisdiction listed as non-cooperative.

Economic substance scope: France applies the economic substance doctrine through the anti-abuse rules of Article 155 A CGI (services rendered by non-residents), Article 119 bis 2, and the abuse of law procedure (abus de droit) under Article L64 of the Livre des procédures fiscales. For EU-based intermediary or shell entities, the ATAD 1 anti-hybrid rules and the EU substance requirements (DAC 6 hallmark C.1) are incorporated in CGI Articles 209 B and 205 A; a French-relevant shell entity with no local employees, premises, or commercial activity in an NFT context will be deemed to lack substance, and the tax administration may recharacterise all profit repatriation as French-source taxable income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Fine of EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of five years (Article 1741 CGI); tax reassessment with 80% penalty (Article 1729 CGI) plus 0.20% monthly interest and publication of the penalty decision. Sanctions may be doubled where proceeds laundered or organised group involvement is established.

Director personal liability: The gérant may be jointly and severally liable for company tax debt upon a finding of serious or repeated misconduct (Article L267 LPF); criminal conviction triggers individual asset seizure, travel bans, and a five-year imprisonment term; professional ban under Article 1742 CGI and European arrest warrant risk for non-cooperation with judicial proceedings.
Fraudulent Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the transfer price under Article 57 CGI; 40% bad faith penalty or 80% for abusive manipulation; EUR 5,000 penalty per missing TP document; potential permanent establishment recharacterisation of the non-resident parent.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally participate in profit shifting face personal liability under Article L266 LPF for unpaid corporate tax when they have caused the company's deficiency; criminal referral may impose personal fines not covered by the company and extradition risk under mutual legal assistance agreements.
Abusive Shell Entity / Absence of Substance

Statutory sanction: Rebutting of tax residence or denial of treaty benefits through the principal purpose test; re-attribution to the parent's French PE; 80% penalty on the undisclosed tax base under Article 1729 CGI; DAC 6 disclosure penalties of EUR 10,000 per scheme, and EUR 50,000 for intentional failure to disclose cross-border arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell identified under ATAD 1 are jointly liable for the re-attributed taxes under Article L267 LPF; civil liability on grounds of artificial legal arrangement, travel restrictions imposed by the judicial authorities, and up to three years for false declarations under Article 441-1 French Criminal Code.
Money Laundering / AML Infraction

Statutory sanction: Under the French Monetary and Financial Code, Article 465-1 provides a five-year prison term and EUR 375,000 fine for money laundering; aggravated money laundering raises penalties to ten years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine; TRACFIN investigation and asset confiscation orders apply.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners face personal criminal exposure independent from the SARL shield; confiscation of personal assets, bank account freezes, travel bans and prohibition to manage a business; non-compliant compliance officers face a EUR 10,000 fine and two years' imprisonment for failure to report suspicious transactions to TRACFIN.
Violation of Profit Repatriation Rules / Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Requalification of fees as dividends with assessment of 30% withholding, 40% or 80% penalty, plus legal interest; the beneficiary is not entitled to a foreign tax credit in France.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to pay the withheld tax personally if they knowingly structured the distribution without withholding payment (Article L266 LPF) and face criminal fines for misrepresentation under Article 433-4 Criminal Code (of forged administration documents) up to three years' imprisonment.
