Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital; the partners are free to set the capital. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and paid up to at least 20% (one-fifth) at formation, with the balance payable within 5 years. Cash funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (séquestre) with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the banker's deposit certificate is a mandatory filing document and funds are released only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the SDIS and the departmental safety commission (CCDSA) covering evacuation and extinguishing provisions; structural load inspection and certification by a technical controller (contrôleur technique) for the production floor; electrical installation conformity per NF C 15-100 and Labour Code requirements; labour inspectorate clearance of workstations, personal protective equipment, chemical exposure limits, and emergency procedures prior to industrial operation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) for French-established entities and the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) for non-established businesses. Main portal: impots.gouv.fr. Customs and EORI matters are administered separately by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered through the unified business formalities portal operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). The registration data are transmitted to the Registre national des entreprises, the commercial register and the DGFiP. The entity receives a SIREN number for the legal entity and a SIRET number for each establishment. The DGFiP allocates the French VAT identification number, generally formatted as FR followed by a two-character check key and the nine-digit SIREN. The SARL must activate its professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr and submit the applicable VAT registration and tax-option forms to its competent SIE. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the SIEE for French VAT registration, generally using Form 3550-SD or the applicable electronic process and providing constitutional documents, foreign tax registration evidence, business activity details, representatives and proof of taxable transactions. EU-established businesses may use the applicable EU VAT registration or One Stop Shop procedures where eligible. A non-EU business may require a French tax representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement removes that requirement. An EORI number must be obtained from French customs when the business imports into or exports from the customs territory of the European Union; an EORI is not a VAT or corporate income tax identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS) by default. An eligible SARL may elect temporary income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, or IR) for up to five fiscal years if statutory conditions are satisfied, including qualifying ownership, size, age and activity conditions. A qualifying family SARL may elect IR treatment without the ordinary five-year limitation, subject to the family-company requirements. The election must be filed with the competent tax authority within the statutory period and is generally irrevocable for the relevant period. France has no US-style general check-the-box election for SARLs. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the EU anti-tax-avoidance framework and may deny deductions or require income inclusion for hybrid arrangements; they do not constitute a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For businesses eligible for the French franchise en base de TVA, the ordinary annual turnover threshold is generally EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods, restaurant sales and accommodation, and EUR 37,500 for services, with higher tolerance thresholds generally of EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250 respectively, subject to the applicable year and statutory conditions. A business exceeding the applicable threshold becomes liable under the relevant rules. These thresholds do not provide a general exemption for non-established businesses making French-taxable transactions: a foreign business may require French VAT registration from its first taxable transaction unless the transaction is subject to a reverse charge or another special regime. For intra-EU distance sales and certain electronically supplied services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold and One Stop Shop rules may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption at export where the goods leave the EU customs territory and the supplier can substantiate the export and the link between the supply and the exported goods. Core evidence is the electronic export declaration and customs confirmation of exit, normally identified through the Movement Reference Number and exit results. The supplier must retain the commercial invoice, purchase order or contract, transport documentation, packing records and proof of delivery or receipt where relevant. Supplies to customers in another EU Member State are generally treated as intra-Community supplies, not exports, and require valid customer VAT identification, dispatch evidence and the applicable recapitulative reporting. Offshore or cross-border services are not automatically zero-rated. Business-to-business services generally follow the customer-location rule and may be outside French VAT with reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction. Services physically connected with French immovable property, admission to events, passenger transport, short-term vehicle hire and other special categories follow specific place-of-supply rules. VAT recovery requires valid invoices, a direct business purpose and retention of supporting records; export exemption does not remove documentation requirements.

  • Electronic customs export declaration and Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs confirmation that the goods exited the European Union customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms and destination
  • Sales contract, purchase order or equivalent transaction evidence
  • Packing list and transport documentation, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note or courier manifest
  • Evidence linking the exported goods to the French VAT-exempt supply
  • Proof of delivery or customer receipt where available
  • Import or destination-country customs evidence where reasonably available
  • Accounting records, VAT return support and correspondence sufficient to reconcile the export with the zero-rated turnover
  • Inward remittance evidence is not generally a mandatory French VAT export document, but payment records should be retained as supplementary commercial evidence and may be requested in an audit

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and transaction-data reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all French-established businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices, while large and intermediate enterprises must generally issue compliant electronic invoices and transmit required transaction data from that date. Small and medium-sized businesses are generally scheduled to issue compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2027. Invoices will be exchanged through an approved private platform or the public invoicing platform designated under the reform, with transaction-data reporting to the tax administration. France does not operate a universal real-time pre-clearance or digital-stamping model equivalent to Italy's SDI for all domestic invoices. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. Foreign businesses should monitor whether they have a French establishment, a French VAT liability or French customer-reporting obligations before determining whether the reform applies.

France has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. A French VAT exemption may be reassessed where customs exit cannot be proven, the goods were not exported, the transaction documentation is inconsistent, or the exemption was applied to an ineligible service. The reassessed VAT is generally recoverable from the supplier, together with late-payment interest and applicable surcharges. Intentional or serious non-compliance may attract a 40% surcharge for deliberate failure, an 80% surcharge for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, or other statutory penalties. False invoices, concealed turnover or organized VAT fraud may also trigger criminal prosecution, tax-fraud penalties and customs sanctions. Refund claims may be denied, reduced or clawed back, and the administration may audit prior periods within the applicable limitation period. Export records, customs messages and accounting evidence should generally be retained for the French statutory record-retention period, commonly six years for tax documentation, subject to longer periods where litigation, customs or corporate-law obligations apply.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility is mandatory in France under TFEU Articles 63-66 (free movement of capital); no exchange-control licence, quantitative ceiling, or prior authorisation applies to trade payments, dividends, or inbound equity contributed by non-resident shareholders. The only binding constraints are (i) EU/UN financial sanctions screening, (ii) EU Regulation 2019/452 inbound FDI screening, transposed by Décret 2019-1590 for listed sensitive sectors (smart-watch manufacture is not listed), and (iii) Banque de France statistical reporting of cross-border direct-investment flows. Profit remittance is legally unrestricted but triggers French withholding tax at 30% under CMF Article 119-bis, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a qualifying EU/EEA corporate parent holding at least 10%, or to 10-15% under an applicable double tax agreement (e.g., France-China DTA 2013); SARL dividendability must be verified under Code de commerce distributable-reserve rules (Articles L232-11 et seq.).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France, as a Eurozone member, imposes no statutory surrender, no repatriation deadline, and no mandatory conversion to EUR of export proceeds. The SARL may freely retain USD, CNY/CNH, HKD, or other currency receipts in foreign-currency accounts held with ACPR-licensed credit institutions. The only obligations are (i) bank-filed Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical declarations for transaction-level flows, (ii) direct reporting by the resident company for direct-investment flows equal to or above EUR 1 million per operation under A152-2 CMF, and (iii) transactional AML and sanctions screening by the banking counterparty. For statutory accounts, foreign-currency balances must be translated to EUR at the year-end closing rate.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Paris), exercising delegated foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments statistical authority under Articles L151-1 et seq., R152-1 et seq. and A152-2 of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, 'CMF'), in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry for the Economy and Finance); monetary and exchange-rate policy is ceded to the European Central Bank (ECB) under TFEU Article 127, the Eurozone being the SARL's single monetary area.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The subscription funds must arrive by straight SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the foreign shareholder's bank to the SARL's blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) or dedicated euro account, using ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' or 'SUBS' and the French reference 'Apport en numéraire - acte statutaire no.___' in field 70, with full beneficiary BIC/IBAN. Verification protocol: (1) receiving bank performs sanctions and source-of-funds screening; (2) the resident company files the Banque de France balance-of-payments direct-investment declaration where the operation equals or exceeds EUR 1 million under A152-2 CMF; (3) any physical cash movement above EUR 10,000 requires an EU cash-control declaration under Regulation 2018/1672; (4) the SARL books the capital and files the modification with the greffe within eight days of deposit; (5) if routed through an intermediate non-EU holding company in a low-tax jurisdiction, additional transfer-pricing and economic-substance documentation is required to prevent recharacterisation of equity as debt; alternatively, a shareholder loan may be used as intercompany financing provided the loan agreement, arm's-length interest rate and withholding-tax position are documented. Crypto or cash-equivalent routing is unacceptable to any French Tier-1 bank.Three qualified clearing rails are available. (a) Direct supplier payments: SWIFT MT103 with GPI and UETR tracking via TARGET2 for EUR or CHIPS for USD, purpose code 'TRAD' or ISO 20022 merchandise code, invoice and Incoterm references in field 72, and sanctions screening of the beneficiary bank. (b) Commercial letters of credit: MT700/MT707 irrevocable confirmed at-sight LC issued by the Tier-1 bank's trade desk, followed by presentation through the supplier's bank of commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, and certificate of origin; LC discounting without recourse may accelerate supplier cash. (c) Documentary collections (MT400/MT421) for established counterparties, or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR disbursements up to EUR 100,000 within the EEA. For USD or CNH settlement with Asian component suppliers, correspondents in London, New York or Hong Kong are used as applicable; France applies no FX surrender or conversion restriction, and any derivative embedded in the disbursement facility must be EMIR-reported by the SARL as a non-financial counterparty below the clearing threshold.Outright FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/CNH, EUR/GBP) with tenors up to 24 months executed OTC with the Tier-1 bank, limited to committed operational, capex and trade exposures, with EMIR trade reporting for the SARL as a non-financial counterparty.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or onshore currencies (e.g., USD/CNY) where the offshore CNH line is insufficient; permitted only through the French bank's offshore desk under ISDA/CSA collateral terms.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD, EUR/CNH) for term-matched hedging of capital expenditure or equity-like funding positions, subject to ISDA/CSA margining.Vanilla European FX options, including zero-cost collars and participating forwards, used to hedge budgeted rates on sales/distribution contracts, with premium payable upfront in EUR.FX swaps and tom-next swaps for working-capital settlement within daily liquidity windows, executed with the same Tier-1 credit institution to ensure hedge effectiveness and netting eligibility.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Operational feasibility rating: HIGH for treasury operations (SEPA/SWIFT payments, spot FX conversion, multi-currency e-wallets); LOW for trade finance and capital-escrow mandates. French ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (Qonto, Kauli, Mangopay) and EU EMIs passported into France under PSD2 (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex, Ebury) may hold e-money float, provide EUR/GBP/USD/CNH accounts, execute MT103 outflows and integrate accounting APIs. Legal constraints: an EMI cannot take repayable deposits or grant credit/overdrafts, cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, and cannot act as a regulated escrow for capital-call funds; therefore the SARL must place its share-capital block and LC activity with a Tier-1 credit institution and may use the EMI only as a disbursement and collection overlay.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File obligations arise when annual after-tax turnover or gross balance-sheet assets of the French entity (or consolidated group including it) exceed EUR 400 million in at least one of the two preceding fiscal years. A simplified 'light' TP documentation must be maintained and produced upon request for companies or groups with turnover between EUR 50 million and EUR 400 million. The French SARL operating an OEM smartwatch manufacturing model in ISIC 2652 will generally fall below the mandatory full threshold but must nonetheless keep contemporaneous arm's-length data for related-party procurement contracts, licence fees and intercompany management charges.

Economic substance scope: France does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, it applies a deep economic-substance and anti-abuse doctrine under Article L. 64 of the French Tax Procedures Book (abus de droit). A SARL manufacturing smartwatches must have actual operational substance in France—its own workforce, manufacturing facilities, inventory risk, management structure, and commercial decision-making. The place of effective management determines tax residency; a company with nominal French registration but without real activity may be re-qualified as a hybrid entity or treated as acting through a French permanent establishment of its foreign parent, leading to full French taxation and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 1729 of the French Tax Code applies an 80% penalty for manoeuvres abusives or bad faith concealment, plus 0.20% monthly interest. Article 1743 CGI provides criminal liability of up to EUR 500,000 fine and five years' imprisonment for fraudulent filing or forcible collection of taxes. Cumulative penalties may reach over 100% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The gérant (managing director) of the SARL may be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts under Article L. 267 of the Tax Procedures Book when he/she has committed abnormal, grave and repeated fraud. Criminal conviction additionally triggers a travel ban, director disqualification, and confiscation of personal assets where the evasion is linked to laundering the proceeds.
Shell Company / Abuse of the Corporate Structure

Statutory sanction: Application of abuse-of-law doctrine under Article L. 64 of the Tax Procedures Book, voiding the artificial legal arrangement and reassessing 25% corporate tax plus a mandatory 80% penalty. The denied treaty/directive relief results in a 25% dividend WHT, and invisible profit transfers are taxed as deemed distributions to the foreign shareholder.

Director personal liability: Lifting of the corporate veil under Article L. 651-2 of the Commercial Code for fictitious activity; directors become personally liable for the corporate tax debts if the shell was used to frustrate tax claims. Criminal prosecution for 'abus de biens sociaux' may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine for the gérant.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the French taxable profit under Article 57 CGI, with the disallowed payments recharacterised as constructive dividends subject to 25% withholding tax. Absence of valid transfer-pricing documentation leads to a summary penalty under Article 1735 CGI of 5% (up to EUR 100,000) and a monthly interest surge; if the abuse is deliberate, the 80% fraud penalty applies.

Director personal liability: The gérant who knowingly signed off the artificial invoices is liable for the supplementary tax as a joint and several debtor; personal burden of proof is reversed under anti-abuse rules. Directors may also face civil liability if the tax reassessment leads to insolvency and creditor loss.
AML / Undeclared Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Article 324-1 of the French Criminal Code is punishable by five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine (aggravated to ten years and EUR 750,000 when involving a habitual professional use). Non-compliance with AML register duties carries a corporate fine of up to EUR 5 million for the entity and administrative penalties from ACPR, including a prohibition for the SARL to exercise the commercial activity or use payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately authorise or facilitate launder flows are criminally liable as principals. French courts frequently order travel bans, extradition letters and personal seizure of the director's real estate if evidence links the SARL's account to tax-evasion proceeds.
