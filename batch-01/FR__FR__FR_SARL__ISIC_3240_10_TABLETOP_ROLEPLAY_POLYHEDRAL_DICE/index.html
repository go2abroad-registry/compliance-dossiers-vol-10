Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official impots.gouv.fr portal. Company incorporation and statutory business identifiers are processed through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), which transmits registration information to the tax authorities.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL obtains its SIREN and SIRET identifiers through the INPI Guichet unique incorporation filing. The DGFiP then assigns the French VAT identification number, generally consisting of the country prefix FR, a two-digit key, and the nine-digit SIREN. The company must provide its incorporation documents, registered-office details, business activity, directors or managers, beneficial-owner information, bank details where requested, and expected taxable turnover. A foreign entity without a French establishment that carries out transactions requiring French VAT registration applies to the designated non-established-business tax service, normally the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères, using the applicable VAT-registration process and supporting evidence of business activity. VAT registration is generally required from the first taxable transaction where no domestic registration threshold applies to the foreign taxable person.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). An eligible SARL may elect for income-tax treatment under the regime applicable to qualifying closely held companies, including the temporary five-year election available to certain newly incorporated companies meeting statutory ownership, size, age, and activity conditions. A SARL constituted solely among qualifying family members may elect for the family-company income-tax regime, subject to statutory conditions. France has no general United States-style check-the-box election and no general election that permits contractual hybrid classification for French tax purposes. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions, require inclusion, or restrict exemption where arrangements produce deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business eligible for the domestic VAT franchise, the ordinary franchise threshold is generally EUR 25,000 of prior-year turnover, with a current-year tolerance threshold generally of EUR 27,500 under the applicable rules. A taxable person exceeding the applicable threshold becomes liable under the statutory transition rules. The franchise does not generally protect a non-established foreign taxable person from French VAT registration where French VAT is due. Special lower thresholds apply to certain activities, and the applicable thresholds must be verified for the relevant tax year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT if the exporter is able to prove export and the goods leave the EU customs territory under the applicable customs procedure. Evidence normally includes an accepted export declaration, a customs exit result or equivalent electronic exit evidence, transport documentation, commercial invoices, and consistent accounting records. Intra-EU supplies to a VAT-identified customer may qualify for exemption subject to customer VAT-number validation, transport evidence, recapitulative reporting, and other statutory conditions. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: B2B services are generally taxed at the customer’s location under the place-of-supply rules, while B2C services and exceptions require transaction-specific analysis. VAT refunds require valid invoices, proof of taxable business use, and compliance with the relevant French or EU refund procedure.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, customer, goods, value, origin where relevant, and delivery terms
  • Electronic customs export declaration and Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs exit certification or equivalent electronic evidence that the goods exited the European Union customs territory
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, air waybill, courier record, or carrier delivery confirmation
  • Packing list and product description sufficient to reconcile the shipment with the customs declaration
  • Accounting records linking the export invoice, customs declaration, transport evidence, and payment
  • Customer and destination evidence supporting the non-EU export or applicable intra-EU exemption
  • Inward remittance evidence where relevant to commercial substantiation; France does not impose a universal requirement for an Inward Remittance Certificate as a condition of export VAT exemption

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting. From 1 September 2026, all businesses must be capable of receiving compliant electronic invoices; issuance obligations apply from that date to large and medium-sized businesses and tax groups, and from 1 September 2027 to small and micro-enterprises, subject to legislative or administrative changes. Invoices must be transmitted through an approved private platform (plateforme agréée), with data exchanged through the French public directory. Business-to-consumer and certain cross-border transaction data must be reported electronically through the required e-reporting channel. France does not operate a universal real-time VAT clearance model for all invoices; however, structured invoice data, platform interoperability, authenticity, integrity, archiving, and required transaction reporting will be mandatory. Standard paper or PDF invoices alone will not satisfy the applicable mandatory e-invoicing requirements once the relevant phase applies.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export VAT zero-rating. The exporter must retain reliable evidence that the goods left the EU or that the relevant place-of-supply and exemption conditions were satisfied. If the exemption or zero-rating is unsupported, the DGFiP may reassess French VAT, deny or claw back VAT refunds, assess late-payment interest generally at 0.20% per month, and impose penalties including 10% for certain late or incorrect filings, 40% for deliberate breach, and up to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, subject to statutory conditions. False customs or tax documentation may also trigger customs penalties and criminal tax-fraud exposure, including imprisonment and fines under the French Tax Code. Repeated or intentional non-compliance may result in enhanced audit activity, recovery proceedings, and restrictions on VAT-refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully and freely convertible. France imposes no exchange-control authorization for current-account payments (goods, services, royalties) and maintains unrestricted capital-account convertibility consistent with Article 63 TFEU and the OECD Liberalisation Codes. The only formalities are (i) fiscal withholding at source on dividends paid to non-residents (statutory 12.8% flat-tax alignment for EU/EEA individual shareholders; 30% maximum for third-country individuals; treaty and Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief available to corporate shareholders), (ii) the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical declaration for cross-border transfers equal to or exceeding EUR 15,000 (generally filed by the instructing bank), and (iii) mandatory EU/US sanctions screening. For an online tabletop-dice SARL with receivables collected via Stripe and PayPal, profit remittance and capital repatriation are low-friction operations subject to ordinary bank KYC and documentary profit evidence rather than to any prior administrative license.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. French law imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or statutory deposit timeline for export proceeds. The SARL may retain foreign currency in an ACPR-licensed 'compte de devises', convert at market rates at any moment, or receive euro payout streams from Stripe/PayPal without repatriation windows. No product-specific export license is required for ISIC 3240 polyhedral dice; the sole obligations are standard customs declarations (import/export declarations, intrastat thresholds) and the EUR 15,000 Banque de France cross-border flow declaration where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) under the European System of Central Banks and the Single Supervisory Mechanism; foreign-exchange regulatory authority is the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the Code Monétaire et Financier; banking and payment conduct supervision is delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary settlement rail, but legally unsuitable as the sole treasury account for a French SARL. Constraint analysis: a French corporate registration, the bank attestation for the share-capital deposit, and the durable 'référence bancaire' expected by the commercial registry require an account with a French-based credit institution or an ACPR-registered payment/e-money institution. Offshore non-EU EMIs (e.g., Post-Brexit UK-licensed entities) cannot provide payment services in France without a separate French licence or an EU-registered affiliate; consequently, Wise (EU entity with Belgian licence), Payoneer (EU/Malta branch), Qonto (French licensed EMI), Revolut Business (Lithuanian bank), and Shine (Arkéa) are all viable for collecting Stripe/PayPal payouts, provided they hold an e-money or banking licence passported into France via the ACPR registers, are SEPA-compliant with valid IBANs, and impose the same AML/CTF controls under Articles L. 561 et seq. of the Code Monétaire et Financier. The principal legal pitfalls are the fragmentation of the reconciliation trail, the need to re-route balances to the principal French bank account, and the creation of an audit gap if the EMI does not provide line-item settlement reference data.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) open a temporary 'compte de dépôt de fonds' at the chosen bank (or at Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations if the bank refuses the blocking role); (2) each shareholder remits via SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022) for inflows below EUR 100,000, or via SWIFT MT103 for larger or non-EEA source currencies, with remittance information strictly formatted: 'APPORT DE CAPITAL [Company] SARL — [Shareholder name] — quote [number] — statuts dated [DD/MM/YYYY]'; (3) tag the flow with the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical category 'Direct Investment — Equity Capital' (BPM6/DI-1), and ensure the instructing bank files the declaration if the amount equals or exceeds EUR 15,000; (4) deliver the KYC/AML source-of-funds bundle (personal bank statements, identity documents, employment or tax records) already described in the defence items; (5) upon immatriculation at the RCS, convert the blocked account into the operating account and release the attestation. No prior foreign-exchange authorization from the French Treasury is required for euro-zone SEPA transfers; the bank is prohibited from imposing exchange-control formalities on intra-euro payments (Regulation (EU) 260/2012).Clearing rails: (i) intra-EU raw-material suppliers: SEPA Credit Transfer using ISO 20022 structured remittance with purpose code 'TRAD' or 'PURC' and the invoice reference; execution is T+1 with EBA Clearing, at negligible fees; (ii) non-EU suppliers (China/US): SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer for trade payables, with documentary bundle comprising supplier pro-forma invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, and the customs import declaration; standard terms '30% advance / 70% against copy of B/L' suit the modest transaction sizes of a dice-casting workshop; (iii) for individual consignments above approximately EUR 50,000, Documentary Collection (D/P at sight) or an irrevocable, confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit is legally permissible and recommended to secure supplier performance, although not expected as a recurring rail; (iv) merchant settlements: Stripe and PayPal execute payouts as SEPA credit transfers with merchant-identification codes and settlement references, which automatically reconcile against the operative IBAN; maintaining a single designated payout IBAN prevents fragmented banking references and simplifies the annual statutory auditor's cash-flow review.EUR/USD OTC outright FX forwards (plain vanilla, physically settled or cash-settled under ISDA 2002/CSA or the bank's French-law master agreement) — permitted without prior regulatory authorization for hedging of USD-denominated Stripe/PayPal payout exposures; subject to EMIR reporting and to the non-financial counterparty documentation evidencing the underlying commercial exposure.EUR/CNH and EUR/CNY Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) — permitted for hedging Chinese-sourced payables where onshore CNY convertibility is restricted; the French bank requires documentary evidence of the import trade (purchase orders, invoices, customs declarations).Cross-currency swaps (XCCY) on EUR/USD and EUR/CNH — permitted for aligning USD/CNY liabilities with EUR assets, executed under the same ISDA/EMIR framework; exotic or leveraged barrier structures are generally unavailable to small non-financial counterparties under internal bank policy.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary settlement rail, but legally unsuitable as the sole treasury account for a French SARL. Constraint analysis: a French corporate registration, the bank attestation for the share-capital deposit, and the durable 'référence bancaire' expected by the commercial registry require an account with a French-based credit institution or an ACPR-registered payment/e-money institution. Offshore non-EU EMIs (e.g., Post-Brexit UK-licensed entities) cannot provide payment services in France without a separate French licence or an EU-registered affiliate; consequently, Wise (EU entity with Belgian licence), Payoneer (EU/Malta branch), Qonto (French licensed EMI), Revolut Business (Lithuanian bank), and Shine (Arkéa) are all viable for collecting Stripe/PayPal payouts, provided they hold an e-money or banking licence passported into France via the ACPR registers, are SEPA-compliant with valid IBANs, and impose the same AML/CTF controls under Articles L. 561 et seq. of the Code Monétaire et Financier. The principal legal pitfalls are the fragmentation of the reconciliation trail, the need to re-route balances to the principal French bank account, and the creation of an audit gap if the EMI does not provide line-item settlement reference data.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) is mandatory for companies with annual turnover or gross assets exceeding €400 million, or with related-party transactions exceeding €1 million. For smaller entities, a simplified documentation requirement applies if the total related-party transactions exceed €100,000.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) like the UAE, but the general anti-abuse rule (Article 64 of the French Tax Procedure Code) and the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) require that entities have real economic substance (e.g., premises, employees, decision-making) to benefit from treaty or EU directives. A shell company without substance may be denied treaty benefits and subject to penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (Article 1741 of the French General Tax Code); additional civil penalties of 40% to 80% of the evaded tax, plus interest at 0.20% per month

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they have committed fraud or used the company as a shield (piercing the corporate veil). They may face travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment. In cases of serious mismanagement, courts can impose a ban on managing any company for up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 40% penalty for abuse of law (Article L64 of the Tax Procedure Code); potential criminal charges for tax fraud if intent is proven

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for the company's tax debts if they have acted in bad faith or committed gross negligence. They may also face a ban on holding directorial positions and criminal penalties.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €500,000 for individuals and €1 million for companies (Monetary and Financial Code); criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent money laundering. They may face imprisonment, fines, and a ban on managing commercial enterprises.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty for lack of documentation (or 80% if fraudulent), and interest; potential criminal charges for tax evasion if deliberate

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the company's tax adjustments if they have signed the tax return or approved the transfer pricing policy. They may also face criminal prosecution for tax evasion.
