Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

French SARL has no statutory minimum share capital. Cash contributions must be subscribed in full and paid up to at least one-fifth on incorporation, with the balance paid within five years; contributions in kind must be fully paid up on incorporation. Share capital must be deposited into a blocked bank account (séquestre) and released only after RCS registration and issuance of the Kbis extract. An independent contribution auditor is required if a contribution in kind exceeds EUR 30,000 or represents more than half of the share capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); central portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate registration and TIN application via the Guichet Unique Électronique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; French intra-community VAT identification allocated by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) of the registered office; customs clearance is supervised separately by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) through the DELTA/TRACE platform.

E-government filing portal

The foreign parent/shareholder must file the French SARL incorporation dossier through the electronic Guichet Unique; INSEE then issues the SIREN/SIRET identification; DGFiP subsequently attributes the French tax reference number (n° d'identification fiscale) and the intra-community VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire). A non-resident entity making taxable supplies in France without a French fixed establishment must appoint a fiscal representative satisfying article 289 A of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI), file the specific non-resident registration form (CERFA n°10479*13 / CA-16) with the non-resident SIE, and submit a power of attorney, proof of actual business activity in France, evidence of economic substance, and standard AML/KYC documentation; alternatively, registration of a French branch or permanent establishment will generate full SIREN/SIRET numbering.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: the SARL is treated as a corporate resident entity generally subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at the standard 25% rate, with profits from businesses operated in France taxed (article 209-I CGI), while profits of an autonomous foreign permanent establishment benefitting from a tax treaty are exempt under the territorial credit system; no Tax_Exempt_Zone status is available for this wholesale import activity; Pass_Through_Eligible: limited eligibility exists for a family SARL to opt for income tax treatment (Impôt sur le Revenu) under article 239 bis AA CGI.

Available election forms

Family SARL election under article 239 bis AA CGI: the shareholders may deliberate, with unanimous consent, to be taxed as a transparent partnership at the personal income tax level; small and medium SARLs may also elect IR treatment over IS for a maximum of five fiscal years under article 239 bis AB CGI, subject to turnover thresholds. France does not adopt the U.S. check-the-box system, but fiscal classification of hybrid entities and hybrid branches is controlled by the ATAD 2 anti-hybrid rules implemented under articles 212 bis and 209 B CGI, requiring mismatches to be neutralised; optional VAT grouping (assujetti unique) is available under article 256 C-III CGI since 1 January 2023 for eligible French/EEE entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis VAT registration threshold applies to the non-resident/importing entity: French VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply or intra-community acquisition in France. For a French-established trader, the small business exemption (franchise en base) is set at EUR 91,900 annual turnover for goods and EUR 36,800 for services (2025 limits), but this relief is inapplicable to the present wholesale importer because import VAT is legally due at customs irrespective of turnover; for EU-established distance sellers, the EUR 10,000 cross-border annual threshold may apply, whereas non-EU sellers have zero threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment (0% TVA) is available for direct exports outside the EU under article 262-I-1° CGI where the seller proves that the goods have actually and definitively left the EU within 90 days of dispatch; related ancillary services (cold storage, customs handling, freight in connection with exported goods) qualify under article 262-II CGI; intra-community supplies to a VAT-registered EU customer qualify for zero-rating under article 262-II-1° CGI subject to a valid French VAT number of the buyer and filing of the recapitulative statement (DEB); the 0% rate is claimed on the sales invoice only if the exporter holds the full evidential bundle, and refund of input VAT on export-related purchases follows the ordinary DGDDI/DGFiP refund procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/Déclaration en Douane) electronically validated in the DELTA/Export system with the Movement Reference Number (MRN) showing clearance from the office of export
  • Bill of Lading (Connaissement maritime) or Air Waybill (Lettre de transport aérien) proving physical departure of the frozen beef/seafood from a French or EU port of exit
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Certificat de Change / attestation de domiciliation bancaire) issued by the French domestic bank evidencing collection of the export proceeds in EUR or foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms, weight, volume, and cold-chain specifications, matching the customs declaration values
  • Packing list and, where applicable, validated import document from the destination country and insurance certificate

Mandatory French electronic invoicing applies from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises; the SARL will therefore be required to issue structured e-invoices (EN 16931 / PEPPOL BIS 3.0) and to transmit in near real-time the transaction and payment data to DGFiP through an accredited PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) or the free Chorus Pro public portal, operating under the centralised clearance model with API-based integration; digital stamping is effected by the PDP certification and the DGFiP transaction reference; paper invoices for domestic B2B flows are prohibited, and non-compliance triggers the penalties of article 289 B CGI, including invoice suppression fines and inadmissibility of the VAT deduction.

DGFiP may retroactively claw back the 0% rate and reassess TVA at 20% on the entire export consideration when the evidential burden is not met; penalties include the deliberate misconduct penalty of 40% (article 1729 a CGI) or 80% for abusive tax fraud/dissimulation (article 1729 b CGI), late-payment interest of 0.20% per month, and an additional 10% increase if correction is not filed within 30 days; joint and several liability may be asserted against the French commissioning customer under article 283-4 CGI; the statutory audit look-back period is six years (extended to ten years in fraud cases) under article L. 176 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales, and customs false declaration or export cover-up triggers the criminal sanctions of article 414 of the Code des Douanes, with fines up to EUR 1,500,000 for organised-group customs fraud.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Articles L.151-1 et seq. of the French Code monétaire et financier. No prior administrative authorisation, exchange-control approval or declaration is required for a non-resident shareholder to subscribe equity in a French SARL engaged in wholesale importation of deep-frozen meat carcasses and pelagic fish. The business does not fall within the scope of the French foreign-investment screening mechanism (Articles L.151-3 and R.151-3 CMF), which is limited to defence, security, critical-infrastructure and public-order sectors. Profit remittance is legally unrestricted, but dividend pay-outs abroad may be subject to French withholding tax under Article 119 bis of the Code général des impôts (domestic rate 25%, reducible under double-taxation treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive regime), and the paying bank will require the standard tax-residence formality.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory holding or repatriation timeline. Export receipts and foreign-currency trade earnings may be retained freely in foreign-currency accounts ('comptes en devises') opened with French-resident banks or held abroad. The only regulatory obligations are statistical: resident enterprises must report cross-border transactions to the Banque de France through their banking intermediary under the monthly balance-of-payments reporting framework, using the direct-investment category ('Investissement Direct') for inbound equity subscriptions and the applicable trade/current-account codes for goods and services payments. Banking practice requires the customer to supply the underlying invoice and contract reference for each cross-border transfer so that the bank can satisfy its reporting duty.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Central Bank) and Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique (French Treasury / Foreign Exchange Administration)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility for ACPR-licensed French electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine) and EEA-passported EMIs that have completed the ACPR notification procedure. Such institutions are legally sound for EUR-denominated domestic and SEPA payments and can support basic SWIFT TT activity, but most have limited USD correspondent coverage, no true trade-finance product suite and no ability to issue or advise commercial letters of credit. Consequently, they are viable for ancillary treasury operations but not for the primary revolving-LC/MT103 rails required by a deep-frozen meat and pelagic fish importer. Non-EEA offshore EMIs (UK-only, Caribbean, Middle East or Asian digital banks without a French or EEA licence) are not viable from a legal standpoint: Articles L.521-1 and L.572-1 et seq. CMF criminalise the unauthorised provision of payment services to French residents, and such institutions cannot service the SARL unless they obtain an ACPR licence, open a French branch or operate through a compliant registered agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder remits the registered-capital subscription in EUR or USD via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the SARL's blocked-capital account (compte séquestre / compte de dépôt des fonds) established with the French bank. The payment reference must read 'Capital subscription – [SARL legal name] – SIREN [registration number] – Incorporation deed dated [date]'. The incoming transfer is classified under the Banque de France balance-of-payments framework as direct investment – business creation ('Investissement Direct – Création d'Entreprise' or ID/CE), and the receiving bank or notary issues the statutory depositary certificate (certificat de dépôt des fonds) required by Article L.223-7 of the Code de commerce for RCS registration. EUR remittances should use SEPA Credit Transfer over TARGET2 for fastest settlement; EUR/USD and USD remittances use SWIFT CBPR+ MT103 over the correspondent banking network. For an ordinary SARL, the minimum paid-in capital is €1 and may be paid in cash or in-kind; the bank's onboarding officer must verify availability of funds and origin of wealth before unblocking the capital account for operational use.Trade settlements to third-country frozen beef and seafood suppliers are executed through (i) SWIFT MT103/MT202 tracked telegraphic transfers for cash-against-documents and advance-payment arrangements; (ii) commercial letters of credit issued under ICC UCP 600 (MT700 issuance with MT707 amendments and MT752/MT754 drawing/negotiation), with revolving LC structures recommended for repeating monthly seafood and meat programmes; (iii) standby letters of credit under ISP98 (MT760) as bid/performance guarantees; (iv) documentary collections under URC 522 for lower-risk, established supplier relationships; and (v) SEPA Credit Transfer for EU/EEA suppliers. All outbound payments require the underlying purchase contract, invoice and, where applicable, veterinary/customs documentation to be lodged with the bank; French banks will code each operation in accordance with the Banque de France external-transactions reporting rules.
