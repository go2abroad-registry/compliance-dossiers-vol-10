Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a French SARL; the share capital is freely fixed in the articles of association. Cash contributions are fully paid into a blocked bank account established for the formation and released only upon registration; contributions in kind require an independent capital appraisal (commissaire aux apports) when the value exceeds EUR 30,000, unless statutory exemptions apply.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and structural load inspection are required for any office/warehouse premise occupied by staff. The employer must produce a workplace risk assessment (DUERP) under Article R4121-1 et seq. of the French Labour Code, install fire extinguishers and emergency exits, and comply with occupational safety inspection requirements applicable to the storage of used construction equipment.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a French SARL; the share capital is freely fixed in the articles of association. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — portals: impots.gouv.fr and pro.impots.gouv.fr; corporate registration at guichet-entreprises.gouv.fr; customs and EORI administration: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) — douane.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

For a French-incorporated SARL: (1) file incorporation via guichet-entreprises.gouv.fr with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce, obtaining the 9-digit SIREN and SIRET establishment number; (2) DGFiP allocates the VAT intra-Community number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN) on request, typically within 4 weeks; (3) apply to DGDDI for an EORI-FR customs identifier (FR + SIREN) before first export; (4) if the SARL is controlled by a non-resident parent supplying into France without a domestic establishment, DGFiP requires direct VAT registration (no threshold) and, for non-EU owners, appointment of a VAT fiscal representative under Article 289-A CGI; non-established EU companies may register directly; VAT refunds follow the EU refund portal or the 13th Directive procedure for non-EU parties.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) at the 25% headline rate, with a 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of profit for companies under €10 million revenue; profits and capital gains from used excavator remarketing are ordinary trading income taxed on French territoriality (Article 209 CGI); distributions to non-resident owners carry a 25% withholding tax unless reduced by a tax treaty.

Available election forms

Small SARLs may elect fiscal transparency under Article 239 bis AA CGI (five-year option for Impôt sur le Revenu, subject to <50% corporate shareholding, <€10m turnover and no listed securities) or Article 239 bis AB CGI for new SMEs (first three years); a 95%+ corporate parent may elect tax consolidation (intégration fiscale, Article 223 A CGI); France has no US-style check-the-box, but anti-hybrid, CFC and interest-limitation rules apply under Articles 209 0 B, 212 bis, 212-0 bis and 238 A CGI consistent with ATAD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established foreign sellers: mandatory VAT registration from the first taxable transaction (no threshold); domestic micro-business franchise en base de TVA: €91,900 annual goods turnover (2025), unavailable to foreign-domiciled sellers; intra-EU distance sales to consumers triggered at €10,000 EU-wide threshold; optional Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) for consignments up to €150; used-machinery resale may fall under the margin scheme (TVA sur la marge, Article 297 A CGI) when acquired from non-VAT-recoverable suppliers, but the margin scheme is incompatible with export zero-rating and should be renounced for export deals.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically leaving the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Article 262-I-1° CGI, including secondhand hydraulic earthmovers sold for overseas remarketing; intra-Community supplies can qualify at 0% under Article 262 ter 2° CGI only where the purchaser holds a valid EU VAT number and the seller files the recapitulative statement (DEB); supporting export proof must be collected within 90 days of the supply (délai de trois mois, BOI-TVA-CHAMP-40-10-20); late or missing proof converts the transaction into a taxable domestic supply at 20% with the seller as debtor of output VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU) filed on DELTA with confirmed EXIT status
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice (and pro forma / Swift LC documents) cross-referenced to the export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Swift MT103 evidencing collection of the foreign-currency proceeds
  • Provenance and title file for the used excavator (purchase invoice, previous registration papers, emission/technical certificate, valuation report)
  • EORI numbers of both the French exporter and the overseas importer
  • Signed delivery acceptance from the overseas consignee and customs broker attestation

Mandatory electronic invoicing for all French B2B supplies under Article 30 Loi de Finances pour 2024 (as amended) — large and mid-size enterprises obliged from 1 September 2026 and SMEs/micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027; structured e-invoices must be cleared through the free Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Dematerialization Partner Platform (PDP) using CEN formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII) with API transmission; e-reporting of full transaction data to DGFiP is near-real-time, but France does not apply digital stamping or pre-clearance validation of individual invoices; export customs declarations are filed electronically on the DELTA system with automated risk assessment, pre-lodgement and electronic EXIT confirmation.

DGFiP has a three-year general assessment window (Article L169 LPF), extended to six years where an undisclosed foreign bank account or asset is involved; zero-rating proof must be retained six years (Article L102 B LPF); unjustified or fraudulent export zero-rating triggers reassessment of 20% output VAT on the gross sale price, late-payment interest at 0.20% per month, and penalties of 40% for deliberate non-disclosure (manquement délibéré, Article 1729 CGI) or 80% for artificial transactions (abus de droit, Article 1729-0 bis CGI); criminal prosecution for VAT fraud under Article 1741 CGI exposes the entity and managers to a fine up to €500,000 and five years' imprisonment; customs export fraud under Article 414-1 Code des douanes carries up to three years' imprisonment, confiscation and a fine up to the value of the machinery; export proceeds not repatriated within 180 days without documented credit terms may be challenged as disguised capital export under the Code monétaire et financier, and participation in intra-EU fraud carousels triggers joint liability of the entity and its managers (Article L267 LPF).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full member of the euro area and operates no exchange-control licensing regime. The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions pursuant to EU Treaty freedoms, the EU Capital Movement Directive, and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. Profit remittance by a French SARL is unrestricted: dividend distributions require a valid shareholder approval (Assemblée Générale Ordinaire), audited financial statements where statutory thresholds are met, and standard withholding tax treatment under French tax law, with no residual convertibility hurdle. Cross-border financial flows retain only statistical reporting obligations: any individual non-resident-to-resident transaction or offsetting item exceeding €750,000 must be reported by the intermediary bank or, where applicable, the SARL, to the Banque de France within the prescribed balance-of-payments survey deadlines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender rules, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no minimum conversion ratio for export receipts. A French SARL may freely hold, convert, or offset foreign-currency receivables from used heavy-machinery exports, whether invoiced in EUR, USD, or third-country currencies. The only compliance duty is statistical: export flows exceeding €750,000 per transaction or aggregated annual thresholds must be captured in the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting framework, in practice submitted by the SARL banking counterparty through the OF (Opérations Financières) reporting channel; exporters opening dedicated foreign-currency accounts (comptes en devises) must disclose the account relationship to the bank, which handles ongoing reporting declarations.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier), with prudential supervision delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) and balance-of-payments statistical governance coordinated with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the French Ministry of Economy and Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a sole banking underpinning; Medium viability only as a non-critical ancillary account. ACPR-regulated French electronic-money institutions and payment institutions (Qonto, Shine, Mangopay) can provide euro-denominated operational accounts for day-to-day expense flows under PSD2, and EEA-licensed EMIs may passport into France under the single-licence regime. However, non-EEA offshore digital EMIs (UK post-Brexit, US, BVI, or Asian virtual-banking platforms) cannot legally offer payment services to a French SARL without a full French/EEA licence, and none can issue documentary commercial letters of credit, standby guarantees, or structured FX hedging lines. Given the medium-risk enhanced profile of used-construction-excavator remarketing and the mandatory SWIFT/LC clearing rails in the target payment architecture, a tier-1 commercial bank account is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital inflows are liberalized: an EU or third-country shareholder may inject funds into the SARL as either equity (augmentation de capital) or shareholder loan (apport en compte courant d'associé) without Banque de France authorization. The compliant routing protocol is a SWIFT MT103 wire to the SARL's euro-denominated IBAN at its tier-1 French bank, referencing the purpose code covering 'equity injection for working capital' (EC01/EC02 category mapping) or, for loans, the transaction-type code for 'loans to affiliated enterprises.' For clarity in bank compliance review, the originator's payment message must identify the ultimate originator and the underlying capital-deed date. For share capital increases exceeding a minimal threshold, the SARL files the M-form (Acte de constitution de garantie/modification) with the Guichet Unique and the Greffe; cash injections above €10,000 sourced from cash require a fund-origin declaration. Cross-border capital contributions above €750,000 are captured automatically in the bank's balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque de France; the bank issues a traçabilité record for audit. If funds arrive in a convertible currency (USD, JPY), the receiving bank executes the conversion on a spot deal and books the euro proceeds; no FX license is required.Outbound settlement relies on three rails: (1) SEPA Instant/SCT for euro payments to EEA suppliers and intra-EU trade, cleared via TARGET2; (2) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for extra-EEA payments (Japan, Korea, Gulf states, Africa) with all commercial terms governed by the underlying sales contract; and (3) documentary commercial letters of credit (sight or usance) issued under ICC UCP 600 by the French bank in favor of the machinery supplier, secured by the import contract, proforma invoice, and shipping documents (bill of lading/CMR, inspection certificate, insurance policy). Where the SARL sells to auction houses or sovereign buyers, standby letters of credit under ISP98 or upstream documentary collections under URC 522 may be used. For export-risk mitigation, Bpifrance Assurance Export guarantees and post-shipment financing can be mobilized on the dossier of the export receivable. Contractual payment splits (e.g., 30% advance via MT103, 70% against documents under LC) are standard for used excavator transactions; every MT103 must carry the SARL's bank (Originator) or beneficiary (for supplier-side) reference and the exact invoice number to avoid FATF-related wire-transmission opacity flags.Outright EUR/USD and EUR/JPY forward contracts (marché à terme) documented under a French bank master agreement (often the FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) master agreement or ISDA 2002 with French-law governing provisions), requiring board authorization and a signed stabilization/treasury line.FX swaps (rollover/simplified swaps) to manage short-term mismatches between USD/JPY receipt streams and EUR disbursement obligations.Vanilla European-style currency options, including zero-premium collars and participating forwards, for asymmetric protection on large-ticket excavator trades.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (XCCY swaps) permissible solely for long-term shareholder capital or intercompany financing structures where a matched-maturity base risk exists.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for the EUR/USD, EUR/JPY, or EUR/GBP pairs because those currencies are fully convertible; NDF availability is restricted to transactions against non-convertible currencies, which do not arise in this SARL's ordinary used-construction-machinery trade.

ACPR-regulated French electronic-money institutions and payment institutions (Qonto, Shine, Mangopay) can provide euro-denominated operational accounts for day-to-day expense flows under PSD2, and EEA-licensed EMIs may passport into France under the single-licence regime. However, non-EEA offshore digital EMIs (UK post-Brexit, US, BVI, or Asian virtual-banking platforms) cannot legally offer payment services to a French SARL without a full French/EEA licence, and none can issue documentary commercial letters of credit, standby guarantees, or structured FX hedging lines. Given the medium-risk enhanced profile of used-construction-excavator remarketing and the mandatory SWIFT/LC clearing rails in the target payment architecture, a tier-1 commercial bank account is mandatory.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400 million turnover or assets (or group threshold) for full Local File and Master File; below this, simplified documentation (Form 2257) is required for companies with turnover > €50 million or with related-party transactions exceeding €1 million.

Economic substance scope: France applies substance-over-form and anti-abuse rules (e.g., Article 155-A, abuse of law doctrine). Companies must demonstrate real economic activity (employees, premises, decision-making) to benefit from tax treaties and EU directives. The French tax administration may challenge shell companies lacking substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (Article 1741 CGI); civil penalty of 80% of evaded tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they participated in fraudulent acts; travel bans and asset freezes possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, 40% penalty for lack of substance, and potential criminal charges for abuse of law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for additional taxes and penalties; possible ban from managing companies in France.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to €5 million or 5% of turnover (Monetary and Financial Code); criminal penalties for money laundering up to 10 years imprisonment and €750,000 fine.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable for AML breaches; personal fines and imprisonment; travel bans may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment with 40% penalty (or 80% if deemed abusive); interest at 0.20% per month; potential criminal charges for tax evasion if intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-arm's length transactions; possible criminal prosecution.
