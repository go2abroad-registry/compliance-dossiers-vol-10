Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); taxpayer portal impots.gouv.fr; incorporation and registration portal formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique); non-resident corporate and VAT file: Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), Paris.

E-government filing portal

Form the SARL via the Guichet Unique at the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); INSEE automatically allocates SIREN (9 digits) and SIRET (14 digits); local SIE opens the corporate tax file under the SIREN; intra-Community VAT number (FR + 2-digit control key + SIREN, 13 characters) is issued by the DGFiP after filing form P0 (n° 3310) at incorporation or on demand. Non-resident companies file an identification application with the SIEE, attaching certificate of existence, constitutional documents, power of attorney, proof of French permanent establishment, and VAT representative certificate where applicable; EU-resident applicants receive the VAT ID within days and third-country applicants within 2-3 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a commercial SARL is default taxable to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at the standard 25% rate (2025), reduced to 15% on the first EUR 42,000 of profit for SME SARLs with turnover under EUR 10 million; profits are taxed at entity level and no shareholder pass-through applies unless a valid election is made; head-office and export trading profits fall within French territorial scope under Article 209 I CGI.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box; statutory elections: (1) Article 239 bis AA CGI — a small SARL (turnover below EUR 10 million, qualifying shareholding and management condition) or family SARL may elect pass-through taxation at personal income tax (IR); (2) Article 223 A et seq. CGI — a 95% parent SARL may elect French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale); (3) anti-hybrid rules enacted by the 2019 Finance Act — no elective neutrality; deduction/inclusion mismatches with a US or Chinese shareholder are automatically disallowed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise base thresholds for VAT exemption: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for sales of goods (tolerance ceiling EUR 93,500) and EUR 37,500 for services (tolerance EUR 41,400); the baijiu goods-export activity exceeding the ceiling is mandatorily VAT registered; non-resident traders have no exemption threshold and must register for any taxable French supply unless the customer is reverse-charge liable; exporters claiming Article 262-I zero-rating must hold a valid French VAT number and file periodic TVA returns to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 262-I 1° CGI zero-rates exports of goods physically dispatched to a destination outside the European Union, with full input VAT deduction and refundable credit; eligibility requires the exporting SARL to invoice at 0%, hold the DAU customs export declaration in its own name, prove physical departure from the EU within 90 days from the date of supply, and document buyer identity, quantity and transport; services and commission directly connected to goods exported to non-EU destinations may also be zero-rated under Article 259-1° CGI; baijiu exports are simultaneously released from French alcohol excise duty (droits de consommation) under the duty-suspension regime of Article 302 D CGI, while domestic French sales remain taxable at 20%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration Unique d'Exportation, DAU) mit EX1 status via the DELTA customs clearance system
  • Bill of Lading or International Air Waybill evidencing physical EU exit and transfer of risk
  • Commercial invoice issued by the exporting French SARL at 0% TVA
  • Packing list and certificate of origin for the Chinese baijiu
  • Inward remittance certificate — SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing foreign currency receipt
  • Export sales contract or proforma invoice with Incoterms and pricing

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing reform: B2B invoices must comply with the EN 16931 semantic model (Factur-X, UBL 2.1 or UN/CEFACT CII) and be transmitted to the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF / Chorus Pro) through an accredited Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP); phased obligations run from 1 September 2026 (all VAT payers must receive; large enterprises must issue and transmit e-invoices and B2C e-reporting) to 1 September 2027 (all enterprises must issue); France applies data-driven clearance via certified PDP-PPF API interfaces without per-invoice digital stamping; penalties: EUR 15 per missing e-invoice (cap EUR 15,000 per year) and EUR 250 per missing e-reporting transmission (cap EUR 2,500 per year).

Incomplete export evidence triggers retrospective recharacterization as a domestic taxable delivery with 20% VAT clawback on the full invoice value plus deduction reversal; penalties: 10% on wrongly zero-rated invoices (Article 1788 A CGI, minimum EUR 480 per infraction), 50% for fictitious invoicing (Article 1737 ter CGI), 80% for concealed activity and 100% for organized VAT fraud, plus 0.2% monthly late-payment interest; customs prosecution under the Code des douanes can impose fines of one to three times the value of undeclared goods and imprisonment up to 6 months (up to 2 years for alcohol export fraud schemes); DGFiP datamining cross-checks DAU records, transport documents and SWIFT receipts, and any FX repatriation delay beyond 180 days creates a presumption of non-genuine export.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro. The euro is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, customs, prudential, and financial-market controls. There is generally no French exchange-control approval for a SARL receiving foreign equity, shareholder loans, trade receipts, dividends, interest, royalties, or sale proceeds. Profit remittances are not subject to a statutory foreign-exchange quota, but require corporate authorization, legally distributable profits where applicable, withholding-tax analysis, transfer-pricing support for related-party payments, beneficial-owner verification, and evidence of the underlying transaction. Non-EU payments may be subject to bank transaction screening, sanctions screening, correspondent-bank requirements, and regulatory or statistical reporting. Payments involving sanctioned jurisdictions, sanctioned persons, dual-use goods, or unusual trade routes may be blocked or require enhanced due diligence. Dividend distributions require approved financial statements, satisfaction of French legal-reserve requirements, solvency and corporate-law conditions, and withholding-tax and treaty analysis. Interest on shareholder loans requires arm's-length pricing, thin-capitalization and interest-limitation analysis, beneficial-ownership review, and applicable withholding-tax documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory obligation requiring exporters to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts into euros. The SARL may retain eligible foreign-currency balances with a regulated bank, subject to the bank's account terms and applicable payment, sanctions, AML, accounting, tax, and reporting rules. Export transactions must still be supported by commercial invoices, transport and customs records, proof of delivery where relevant, and accurate accounting. For exports outside the European Union, customs declarations and applicable French and European Union trade-statistics obligations must be satisfied. Alcohol exports may additionally require excise, customs, and movement-control documentation, including applicable EMCS procedures. Any bank-specific conversion, balance, or repatriation requirement is contractual rather than a general French exchange-control rule.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, as the French national central bank within the Eurosystem and under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank. Foreign-exchange activity is principally governed by European Union law, French financial legislation, the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution for supervised financial institutions, the Autorité des marchés financiers for investment-services and market-conduct matters, and customs and statistical authorities for trade reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ordinary EUR collections and payments, but not a complete substitute for a French relationship bank. An EU-authorized electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, SEPA transfers, cards, and selected foreign-currency services, subject to its authorization perimeter, passporting status, safeguarding model, limits, and risk policy. An EMI account is not automatically a bank deposit account and normally does not provide statutory SARL capital-deposit certification unless the provider is legally authorized and willing to perform that service. Access to cash services, French bank drafts, sophisticated commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, borrowing, overdrafts, guarantees, and certain trade-finance products may be unavailable. Offshore digital EMIs without appropriate European Union authorization, passporting, or a regulated European payment partner should not be used for core operating flows. The SARL must maintain documentary evidence of source of funds, beneficial ownership, business purpose, counterparties, expected volumes, jurisdictions, and alcohol-trade compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity capital through the designated French capital-deposit account before SARL registration, using a traceable transfer from the subscriber's account and identifying the payer as the shareholder or subscribing entity. Provide the bank with the draft articles, subscription schedule, shareholder resolution or subscription instrument, source-of-funds evidence, beneficial-owner information, and incorporation documents. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for ordinary EUR collections and payments, but not a complete substitute for a French relationship bank. An EU-authorized electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, SEPA transfers, cards, and selected foreign-currency services, subject to its authorization perimeter, passporting status, safeguarding model, limits, and risk policy. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000: a French group or standalone SARL with annual consolidated turnover or total assets exceeding EUR 400m is required to prepare and file the Local File and Master File under French TP documentation obligations; all taxpayers are nonetheless expected to retain basic TP adjustment documentation adequate for audit.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime like offshore standard ESR, but economic substance remains decisive in determining corporate tax residency under Article 209, in applying the French GAAR (abus de droit and Loi de régulation sociale), and in obtaining treaty benefits under BEPS Action 6; a Chattel and network substance review is required for all SARL shareholders and beneficial owners to avoid sham declarations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud (fraude fiscale) is punishable by up to 5 years’ imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine; aggravated cases carry 10 years and EUR 4.5m; administrative reassessment applies an 80% penalty for intentional concealment plus 0.40% monthly interest and forfeiture of deductions.

Director personal liability: The gérant may be personally ordered to pay the totality of tax debts, interest and penalties; courts can issue travel bans, prohibit management activity, order restitution of company assets, and impose civil restitution when the company is insolvent or the veil-piercing conditions are met.
Abusive Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Application of the French anti-abuse provision (80% penalty under Article 1729 b), denial of treaty benefits and extraterritorial reattribution of profits; the tax administration can recharacterise the SARL as a sham and tax its entire profit at 25% in the hands of the foreign parent.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers are jointly and severally liable for tax and penalties; a ‘gestion de fait’ finding can make them personally responsible for 100% of the tax liability; significant fraud charges may result in arrest guarantees and prohibition from running French entities.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment with reimbursed tax, 40% penalty for severe lack of documentation or false information under Article 1735-B, 80% penalty for sham price manipulation, and monthly late-payment interest of 0.40%; total sanctions may exceed 100% of the corrected tax.

Director personal liability: Officers who signed false transfer pricing certificates can be held personally liable for penalty amounts; criminal tax fraud proceedings can lead to imprisonment and confiscation of personal assets when the pricing is knowingly fraudulent.
Money Laundering and AML Deficiency in Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: French Monetary and Financial Code sanctions include administrative fines up to EUR 1m or 1% of cleared transactions for failing CDD/AML controls, criminal prosecution for laundering (up to 10 years’ imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine) and confiscation of funds/inventory related to the laundering scheme.

Director personal liability: Banking and AML supervisors may prohibit the manager from exercising managerial functions for up to 10 years; criminal courts may impose personal fines, disgorge profits, and seize personal bank accounts where the SARL director knowingly implements sham payment structures and receives commission on concealed exports.
Breach of Excise and Alcohol Licensing Regulations

Statutory sanction: Fines of EUR 750 per missing declaration; excise duty at the rate applicable per hectolitre of pure alcohol (approximately EUR 1,740 per hlP); confiscation of seized inventory; surcharge and interest for late reporting, and prohibition of the activity in the case of repeat violations.

Director personal liability: The gérant is personally liable for penalties when he/she personally authorised the undeclared importation; under customs legislation, the registered customs representative and the managing director may be jointly liable and subject to seizure of personal property if the company has no substantial assets to satisfy the excise penalty.
