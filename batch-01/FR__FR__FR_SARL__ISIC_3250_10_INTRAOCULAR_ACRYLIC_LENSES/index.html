Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), administered by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce having territorial jurisdiction over the registered office (e.g., Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce de Paris); filings are transmitted electronically through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) Guichet Unique in accordance with the French business formalities portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (general commercial company provisions) and Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL formation, management, and obligations); implementing provisions of Decree No. 2023-218 via the Guichet Unique; for market access, Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (MDR) and French Public Health Code (Code de la santé publique), Articles L.5211-1 seq. and R.5211-1 seq.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) executed either sous seing privé (private deed) or by notary, together with the digital 'Création d'entreprise' dossier (M0-equivalent declaration) lodged through the INPI Guichet Unique, the attestation de blocage des fonds (certificate of capital deposit) and, upon completion, issuance of the Extrait K-bis (final RCS registration certificate).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Minimum share capital for a SARL is EUR 1 with no statutory maximum; however, any cash component must be fully paid up and subscribed at incorporation and the funds must be deposited and locked in escrow in a regulated French bank account, with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations (CDC), or with a notary pursuant to Article L.223-7 of the French Commercial Code; released funds are transferred only upon production of the RCS registration (K-bis). For the intraocular acrylic lens manufacturing activity, a commercially prudent paid-in capital of EUR 10,000 to EUR 50,000 is recommended to satisfy bank KYC, equipment leases, quality-management (ISO 13485) and environmental commitments.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety compliance certificate based on the SDIS / Fire and Rescue Service prescriptions for industrial workshops (number of extinguishers, evacuation plan, flammable-solvent storage cabinets, electrical conformity); structural certification built on the construction-code 'controle technique' covering roof load, floor loading for CNC lathes, and hostile air extraction; occupational clearance by CARSAT / labour inspection including the mandatory Document Unique d'Evaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUER), electrical inspection of machines, EN/ISO machine-guard conformity, IEC wiring checks of clean-room and polishing equipment, and occupational-health surveillance arrangements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France applies no US-style check-the-box regime. Default classification: SARL is subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) at entity level at 25% standard rate (15% reduced rate on the first €42,000 of taxable profit for small enterprises meeting Article 219 CGI conditions, i.e., turnover ≤ €10M and 75% capital held by individuals). Pass-through election available solely under Article 239 bis AA CGI: for the first 5 fiscal years following creation, the SARL may elect to be taxed as a partnership (Impôt sur le Revenu, IR) if fewer than 50 employees and turnover below €10M (industrial/commercial activity) or €5M (services), by filing a written opt-in with the SIE within the statutory deadline; exit from election triggers deemed disposal rules. ATAD hybrid-mismatch reporting under Article 205 CGI applies to cross-border structures; controlled-foreign-company rules and interest limitation under Articles 209 B and 212 bis CGI; group fiscal integration available under Article 223 A CGI for ≥ 95% commonly held French subsidiaries; CVAE (contribution sur la valeur ajoutée des entreprises) applies at 0.75% maximum on value added, and CET (contribution économique territoriale) on rental/asset values for manufacturing sites.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions; France imposes no exchange control licence, conversion ratio, or approval requirement on trade settlements, dividend remittances, intercompany loans, or share capital inflows. Free movement of capital is guaranteed by TFEU Article 63 and Directive 88/361/EEC. Residual restrictions apply only under EU/UN sanctions regimes (with asset-freeze screening), financial embargoes on listed counterparties, and the French FDI screening decree (Decree 2019-1590, with EU Regulation 2019/452) for non-EEA investors acquiring control over sensitive sectors with state-security or health-vulnerability exposure. Greenfield manufacturing of intraocular acrylic lenses by an existing SARL does not, in itself, trigger prior FDI authorisation unless the technology is classified as dual-use or is deemed critical health infrastructure. Dividend and profit repatriation are unrestricted once annual accounts are approved, corporate tax filings are current (IS, CET, TVA), and transfer-pricing documentation substantiates arm's-length pricing. Payments to blacklisted non-cooperative jurisdictions (juridictions non coopératives) may be blocked for tax deduction but not exchange-rationed. Practical residual friction: banks apply internal country-risk overlays, prolonged Beneficial-Owner (UBO) due diligence, and SOF (source-of-funds) documentary requirements on inbound capital injections from high-risk jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion into EUR, no repatriation deadline, and no export proceeds retention quota. Export invoices may be collected in EUR, USD, or any freely convertible currency, and the resulting balances may be maintained indefinitely in a foreign-currency current account with an ACPR-licensed credit institution. However, the SARL must comply with (i) Automatic cross-border transaction reporting executed by the paying/receiving bank into the Banque de France balance-of-payments dataset — the SARL must respond to the bank's periodic statistical requests; (ii) The annual foreign account declaration (next to the income tax return) under Article 1649 A of the Code général des impôts — form n° 3916-bis-SD — covering every bank, securities, or crypto account held outside France, with fines for omission; (iii) EU customs Extrastat declarations for extra-EEA shipments, and Intrastat if intra-EEA dispatches exceed threshold; (iv) Transfer-pricing documentation under Article 57 CGI demonstrating that export proceeds are economically aligned with invoiced values and payment terms; a persistent build-up of unrepatriated receivables at a related offshore distributor can trigger reallocation of profits by the French tax administration.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France — Direction Générale de la Stabilité Financière et des Opérations (DGSO) and the balance-of-payments statistical directorate; prudential supervision and licensing of credit institutions is exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); AML/CFT financial intelligence supervision by TRACFIN. Legal basis: Code monétaire et financier (CMF), Articles L.141-6, L.151-3, L.511-1 et seq., and R.561-1 et seq.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard rate); 15% applies to the first €42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying small and medium enterprises (turnover ≤ €10m, paid-up capital ≤ €750k)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% dividend withholding tax under Article 119 bis CGI, reduced to 0% for qualifying EU parent companies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (at least 10% holding for two years) and further reduced under applicable double-taxation agreements (typically 0-15%)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Transfer Pricing Master File and Local File mandatory for taxpayers exceeding EU SME thresholds: turnover (excl. VAT) > €50 million, balance sheet total > €43 million, or 250+ employees; Country-by-Country Report required when consolidated group revenue exceeds €750 million

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a separate Crown-dependency-style ESR, but requires genuine economic substance through the abuse-of-law doctrine (Article L64 LPF), beneficial-ownership concepts and CFC rules (Article 209 B CGI). An intraocular lens manufacturer must maintain real industrial presence, including physical facilities, qualified technical staff, strategic decision-making and risk-taking functions in France; arrangements with foreign affiliates must be justified by actual operational activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Concealed Profit

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment with 40% penalty for bad faith or 80% penalty for fraudulent manoeuvre; criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI carries five-year imprisonment and a €500,000 fine; mandatory publication of the judgment; denial of tax deduction rights for clandestine transactions

Director personal liability: Directors, de facto managers and majority shareholders can be held criminally liable; piercing the corporate veil is permitted where the SARL is a mere alter ego; civil restitution of surreptitious profits; travel bans and exit controls during fraud investigations; disqualification from managing French companies
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Article L64 LPF abuse-of-law procedure recharacterises the claimed tax base; penalty increased to 80% where the arrangement has no commercial rationale; denial of treaty benefits; administrative fine for reporting abusive schemes; disallowance of the French corporate personality for tax purposes

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for recaptured corporate tax and penalties; courts may pierce the corporate veil to enforce personal liability; criminal exposure for organised tax fraud; asset freezes and travel restrictions during judicial review
AML / Money Laundering and Export-Control Violations

Statutory sanction: Money-laundering offence carries up to ten years' imprisonment and €750,000 fine; non-compliance with AML registration and reporting duties triggers administrative fines by the ACPR; asset forfeiture for instruments and proceeds; revocation of CE-mark or import/export licences required for intraocular lens distribution

Director personal liability: AML obligations bind the SARL's directors and effective beneficial owners; corporate veil is pierced to reach personal assets; travel bans, passport withdrawal and freezing orders are routinely imposed; conviction leads to prison, disqualification from company management and publication of the sanction
