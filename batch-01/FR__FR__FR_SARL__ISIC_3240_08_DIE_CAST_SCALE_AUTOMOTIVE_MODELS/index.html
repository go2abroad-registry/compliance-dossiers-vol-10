Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Authorized subject to strict conditions. A French SARL must maintain a registered office (siège social) in France. A virtual office is permitted only through (i) a licensed domiciliation company (société de domiciliation holding a prefectoral agrément under Articles L123-10-1 to L123-11-2 of the French Commercial Code) or (ii) the manager's residential address, which is limited to a maximum of five years unless renewed by relocation. The registered office must be open for service of process and inspection during normal business hours.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital: the stated capital is freely fixed in the Articles (Art. L.223-2). At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation; the balance must be released within five years (Art. L.223-7). The paid-up cash portion is blocked in a dedicated deposit account with a French bank or the Caisse des Depots et Consignations until the registration certificate (Extrait Kbis) is obtained. In-kind contributions are fully vested in the company at formation and must be appraised by a commissaire aux apports unless the value does not exceed EUR 30,000 and all members unanimously waive the appraisal (Arts. L.223-9 and L.223-13).

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), France — main taxation portal: impots.gouv.fr; professional filings via pro.impots.gouv.fr; company incorporations and formalities via Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

Non-resident entities establishing a French SARL must file formation documents through the Guichet Unique / Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE). Upon incorporation, the company receives its SIREN, SIRET, and Kbis extract from the Register of Commerce and Companies. The DGFiP automatically assigns a fiscal identification number and, once the entity confirms taxable activity, the company applies for an intra-community VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire) via the DGFiP online portal. If no French permanent establishment is created, the foreign entity may be required to appoint a VAT tax representative (représentant fiscal) and apply for direct VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Non-resident entities establishing a French SARL must file formation documents through the Guichet Unique / Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is by default subject to French corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level. Under Article 239 bis AA of the French Tax Code (CGI), an eligible small/mid-size SARL may elect to be taxed as a fiscally transparent partnership subject to income tax (IR). Additionally, an option for group tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) exists under Article 223 A CGI. France has no generic 'check-the-box' election; hybrid mismatch arrangements are constrained by EU ATAD directives.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual turnover threshold triggering mandatory VAT registration: for sales of goods, €91,000 (with an €85,000 lower threshold for certain cases); for services, €36,800 (2025 thresholds per Article 293 B CGI).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of goods physically dispatched from French territory to a destination outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate ('exportation') under Articles 258 and 262(I) CGI. To apply, the VAT-exempt supplier must hold sufficient documentary evidence of export (customs declaration and transport documentation) and maintain the records for the statutory retention period. Certain services rendered to non-EU B2B customers fall outside the scope of French VAT under place-of-supply rules and may similarly qualify for zero-rating or non-taxation.

  • Export customs declaration (DAU / EXA) indicating the exporter of record
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note
  • Commercial invoice with express export reference
  • Proof of goods exit from the EU (e.g., electronic customs movement record or port/airport exit confirmation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation de rapatriement des recettes) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Packing list and transport insurance or freight documents

Mandatory electronic invoicing (facturation électronique) is being phased in through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF). Large enterprises must comply from 1 September 2026, SMEs/micro from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be sent via API or certified dematerialization platforms; e-reporting of cross-border data to DGFiP is required. Additionally, cash registers used by retailers must be certified against the French anti-fraud law.

Claims for export zero-rating are scrutinized during VAT audits. If export evidence is missing or false, the VAT exempted is clawed back by reassessment, plus monthly late interest of 0.40%. Penalties range from 40% of the reassessed duty for wilful failure (manquement délibéré) to 80% for fraudulent manipulation or abuse of right. Criminal tax fraud sanctions under Article 1741 of the French Tax Code may include up to five years of imprisonment and a fine of €500,000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the EU Treaty and OECD liberalization codes. No material profit repatriation hurdles for French resident SARLs other than standard corporate tax withholding if applicable and BNCT customs/statistical reporting. Dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are generally subject to a 30% withholding tax, reduced under tax treaties; capital repatriation via share capital reduction or liquidation is free of FX restriction but requires notarial and legal formalities. All transactions exceeding €50,000 require declaration to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export proceeds surrender or conversion ratio exists in France. Export receipts may be held in foreign currency or converted freely; however, the exporter must repatriate proceeds to the EU within the invoicing terms and report export flows through the Banque de France's cross-border statistics (Bank of France 'Reporting of cross-border payments'). Late or missing declarations may expose the entity to administrative penalties, not FX conversion requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, with foreign exchange policy coordination by the Direction Générale du Trésor (Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EU-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the PSD2 passport. Legally permissible only if the EMI is registered with the ACPR (Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority) or holds a valid EU passport into France. Offshore non-EU EMIs cannot provide euro-denominated French IBANs or access SEPA/instant clearing without a French branch or agent arrangement; direct onboarding as a cash-management rail is generally unviable for trade settlement above de minimis thresholds.
