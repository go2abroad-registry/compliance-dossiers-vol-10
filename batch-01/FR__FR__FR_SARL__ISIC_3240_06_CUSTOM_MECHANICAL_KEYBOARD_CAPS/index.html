Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — Service des Impôts des Entreprises; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; customs and import VAT/cross-border clearance: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), portal: douane.gouv.fr; centralized company registration via the Guichet Unique administered by INPI and the RCS (Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce).

E-government filing portal

French-established SARL: (1) incorporate through the Guichet Unique at INPI; INSEE issues the 9-digit SIREN company number and the SIRET establishment identifier, with registration at the RCS; (2) DGFiP automatically assigns the French VAT identification number using the format FR + 2-digit control key + SIREN; (3) the same VAT number covers all establishment-level VAT filing obligations and must be quoted on invoices, EC Sales Lists and customs declarations; (4) for a non-EU parent/seller without a French permanent establishment, VAT registration is effected with the competent non-resident taxpayer desk (Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères) under Article 283 CGI, normally through an accredited fiscal representative for the standard scheme; alternatively the Non-Union OSS or IOSS schemes may be used for cross-border B2C supplies and low-value consignments; (5) EU-established sellers may elect Union-OSS for distance sales without French domestic registration until the €10,000 pan-EU threshold is exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SARL is by default subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (corporate income tax) at the entity level at the standard rate of 25%; French-established profits are taxable in France on a territorial basis, with foreign permanent-establishment profits tax-exempt in France where an applicable tax treaty grants exclusive taxing rights to the foreign State.

Available election forms

Available elections: (i) pass-through/transparency election under CGI Article 239 bis AA for small 'family-type' SARLs owned by natural persons and meeting the turnover ceiling (€10,000,000) and shareholding conditions, causing the company to be taxed in the hands of the partners; (ii) tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for French groups holding at least 95% of subsidiaries; (iii) VAT fiscal-unity election under Article 256 C III CGI for closely-held French entities; (iv) France applies comprehensive ATAD 2 anti-hybrid rules (CGI Articles 208 B, 209-X, 212-1 bis) and offers no domestic 'check-the-box' regime, so entity classification for French tax purposes follows French commercial/Tax Code status and the applicable treaty classification rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR — No general registration threshold for established taxable businesses: any habitual economic activity by a French SARL requires VAT registration from the first taxable transaction (CGI Articles 259 and 283). Small-business relief (franchise en base, CGI Article 293 B and Annex IV) applies to taxable turnover not exceeding €91,900 for supplies of goods and €36,800 for services, but this relief is optional and does not constitute a registration threshold; an exempt business may voluntarily register. For EU-established distance sellers, registration in France becomes mandatory only after the pan-EU intra-Community distance-sales threshold of €10,000 is exceeded (Union-OSS); for non-EU non-established suppliers, mandatory VAT registration from the first supply unless Non-Union OSS is used for B2C e-services or IOSS for imported consignments not exceeding €150.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate (exonération) applies to supplies of tangible goods physically exported or dispatched outside the EU customs territory (CGI Articles 262-I and 262-ter), provided ownership has transferred to a non-EU buyer and the goods are dispatched/transported out of the EU, with proof of departure generally required within 90 days of the supply; intra-EU supplies to VAT-registered customers qualify for exemption under Article 262-ter only when the customer's EU VAT number is valid and an EC Sales List (état récapitulatif / DEB) is filed. Services directly connected to exported goods (Article 262-II) are exempt, and B2B services supplied to a customer established in a third country fall outside the French VAT scope under the general place-of-supply rules (Articles 259 and 259-B). Supporting evidence must be retained for six years (LPF Article L102-B). Exporters may claim the resulting input VAT credit through the periodic CA3 VAT return or a refund application (remboursement de crédit de TVA) filed with the competent SIE.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation, ECS) lodged electronically via DELTA and bearing the customs office of exit discharge or digital acceptance reference
  • Bill of Lading, CMR waybill or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch of the goods outside the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Justificatif de Rapatriement des Recettes d'Exportation) issued by the credit institution evidencing collection and repatriation of the export proceeds

Mandatory structured e-invoicing for domestic B2B supplies will phase in from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises under the amended reform (Article 85, Loi de Finances 2025); B2C e-reporting obligations follow the same timetable. Acceptable formats: Factur-X (hybrid), UBL and CII; invoices must be transmitted via an accredited PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) API or through the public state portal PPF (Portail Public de Facturation, successor of Chorus Pro). E-invoices must be sent to the PDP/PPF within 2 business days of issuance and B2C transaction data within 10 days of the transaction; no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control is imposed for the keyboard-keycap manufacturing and e-commerce retail sector, although export/import clearances are processed electronically by DGDDI through the DELTA customs system.

DGFiP may retrospectively claw back export VAT exemptions where the documentary evidence is missing, incomplete or inconsistent, generally covering four fiscal years, extended to ten years for concealed or fraudulent activity (LPF Article L169). Penalties under CGI Article 1788-A: 50% of the tax understated for deliberate misconcealment or gross negligence (manquement délibéré) and 80% for fraud (abus de droit or manœuvres frauduleuses), in each case plus late interest of 0.2% per month (intérêt de retard). Independent customs penalties under the French Customs Code apply for false export declarations (fines from €150 to €7,500 per irregularity plus confiscation of goods), and criminal exposure exists for document falsification. Export zero-rating claims are subject to heightened audit scrutiny through cross-referencing of ECS/DELTA customs records, bank remittance data and commercial shipping documents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. There is no exchange-control licensing, no central-bank permission, no mandatory conversion, and no waiting period for lawful import, export, e-commerce settlement, profit distribution, dividend wire, equity injection, or intercompany loan flows. Administrative requirements are limited to: (i) routing all cross-border payments through an ACPR-licensed bank or payment institution; (ii) providing transaction-level documentation and purpose codes for anti-money-laundering and external-statistics compliance; (iii) reporting cross-border monetary movements above EUR 12,500 to Banque de France where applicable; and (iv) the exceptional foreign-investment notification to the French Treasury under Articles L151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code for non-EU investors acquiring control of a French entity or operating a business branch in sectors of strategic sensitivity. Profit remittance from the French SARL is unrestricted once legally distributable profits exist, the legal reserve obligation of 5% of profits until the reserve equals 10% of share capital is respected, and a shareholder decision is adopted. EU/EEA shareholders receive dividends with no French withholding tax; non-EU shareholders are subject to the domestic 25% withholding rate unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty, and the bank compliance file must evidence the underlying distribution decision and certified accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory-conversion ratio applies to export receipts in France: the mandatory surrender ratio is 0%, and there is no central-bank deadline requiring conversion of USD, GBP, CNY, or other export currencies into EUR. French exporters are free to hold export proceeds in foreign-currency accounts with an ACPR-authorized bank as part of their cash management policy. However, the exporter must ensure that export proceeds are received on or before the contractual due date set out in the commercial invoice, which by French late-payment rules is ordinarily a maximum of 30 days for professional invoices unless the parties agree otherwise; receipts must be traceable to the SARL's actual export transactions and supported by commercial invoices, export customs declarations, bills of lading/airway bills, and bank wire references. Cross-border receipts above the statistical threshold may be included in the obligatory Banque de France external-transaction declaration filed via the credit institution.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF, the French central bank), acting with the Direction générale du Trésor of the Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty, and the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) as the banking and payment-institution licensing and AML/CFT supervisory authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary settlement rail, Low as a primary treasury architecture. An offshore digital EMI outside the EU/EEA has no standalone passport in France and may not lawfully provide payment services to a French SARL as primary account holder unless the service is provided through an EU-subsidiary or an authorized payment institution operating under the PSD2 passporting framework. French banking monopoly rules under Article L311-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code require all accounts against which cheques or payment orders may be drawn to be held with an authorized credit institution or appropriately licensed payment/electronic-money institution. The SARL should therefore maintain its Tier-1 French bank account as the final settlement point; a non-EU EMI may be used only as a flow aggregator for customer collections, with daily sweeps to the French account and full transaction-level reporting. Any use of an opaque offshore EMI, crypto-based treasury, or shell payment account will create a significant ACPR AML/CFT blocker and may jeopardize the bank relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for the French SARL must be made by SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the bank account opened in the SARL's name with an ACPR-licensed French credit institution. The remittance instruction must clearly identify the SARL's full corporate name and legal entity identifier references, and include an ISO 20022 purpose category such as 'EQUI' for equity contribution; if the bank requires a French 'motif de paiement', the code for 'Apport en capital / augmentation de capital' must be used. For capital subscribed before incorporation, the French bank will usually require an 'attestation de blocage' confirming that funds are blocked for the benefit of the company in formation; after registration, the bank will require the manager's resolution approving the capital increase, updated KYC/UBO extracts, and a copy of the statutory deposit certificate. The payment must match the exact amount resolved by the shareholders, name only the actual shareholder as remitter, and carry full source-of-funds evidence. No third-party payment, family-member substitute, or commingled personal-funded transfer should be used. For intercompany loans instead of equity, the payment should be routed with a signed shareholder loan agreement and an intercompany purpose code, with the loan terms clearly documented to satisfy France's thin-capitalization and transfer-pricing rules.For EUR clearing within the Single Euro Payments Area, the SARL should use SEPA Credit Transfers initiated through EBICS or the bank's corporate e-banking channel, with supporting invoice references and the ISO 20022 purpose category 'TRAD' for trade transactions. For non-EUR disbursements to suppliers outside the EEA, such as USD, GBP, or offshore CNY payments, the standard rail is SWIFT MT103 through a Tier-1 correspondent bank, with full beneficiary bank details (SWIFT BIC), invoice number, and commercial contract references. Where documentary credit is required, the French bank may issue an irrevocable commercial letter of credit under UCP 600 via SWIFT MT700/MT707, with the SARL as applicant and the foreign supplier as beneficiary; the LC must be supported by the export/import contract, pro-forma invoice, transport documents, and certificate of origin. For fast discretionary prepayments to Chinese suppliers, the SARL may use an auditable telegraphic transfer against a supplier invoice and proof of shipment commitment, but should avoid circular or back-to-back payments without underlying goods movement. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary settlement rail, Low as a primary treasury architecture. An offshore digital EMI outside the EU/EEA has no standalone passport in France and may not lawfully provide payment services to a French SARL as primary account holder unless the service is provided through an EU-subsidiary or an authorized payment institution operating under the PSD2 passporting framework. French banking monopoly rules under Article L311-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code require all accounts against which cheques or payment orders may be drawn to be held with an authorized credit institution or appropriately licensed payment/electronic-money institution. The SARL should therefore maintain its Tier-1 French bank account as the final settlement point; a non-EU EMI may be used only as a flow aggregator for customer collections, with daily sweeps to the French account and full transaction-level reporting. Any use of an opaque offshore EMI, crypto-based treasury, or shell payment account will create a significant ACPR AML/CFT blocker and may jeopardize the bank relationship.
