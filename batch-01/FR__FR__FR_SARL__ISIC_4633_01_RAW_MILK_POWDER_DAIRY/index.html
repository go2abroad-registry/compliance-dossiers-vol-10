Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registry of the Commercial Court) of the territorial district of the registered office, operating exclusively through the national business formalities portal Guichet Unique des formalités d'entreprises, administered by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 et seq. governing the formation and governance of the Société à Responsabilité Limitée (SARL); complemented by the general provisions of the Civil Code (Code civil), Articles 1832 to 1844-17.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création de société (standard creation/filing form) lodged electronically through the Guichet Unique portal; official incorporation is evidenced by the Kbis extract issued upon entry into the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), together with the certified statuts.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital: founders may fix capital at or above EUR 1.00. Subscribed cash contributions must be fully paid at incorporation and deposited within eight days of receipt into a blocked escrow account held by an approved credit institution, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary; the block certificate is filed with the registration dossier and funds are released only after the RCS/Kbis registration. In-kind contributions exceeding EUR 30,000 or representing more than half of the share capital require valuation by an appointed contributions auditor (commissaire aux apports) unless the shareholders unanimously waive this appointment.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the Commission de sécurité for warehouses and workspaces under the Labor Code R.4227 series, including extinguishers, detection, egress, and annual drill records; structural load inspection evidenced by a certified engineer's attestation de résistance des planchers validating racking and palletized powder load thresholds; workplace health and safety clearance through CARSAT registration, establishment of the DUERP risk assessment document, worker medical surveillance, and CSE documentation where staff thresholds are met.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital: founders may fix capital at or above EUR 1.00. Subscribed cash contributions must be fully paid at incorporation and deposited within eight days of receipt into a blocked escrow account held by an approved credit institution, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary; the block certificate is filed with the registration dossier and funds are released only after the RCS/Kbis registration. In-kind contributions exceeding EUR 30,000 or representing more than half of the share capital require valuation by an appointed contributions auditor (commissaire aux apports) unless the shareholders unanimously waive this appointment.

Each subscriber's allocation is definitively accepted as set out in the subscription schedule; the manager is authorized to collect any final cash instalments and to secure escrow release upon registration. 2. RESOLVED, approved and passed.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

Direction Départementale de la Protection des Populations (DDPP) under the functional supervision of the Direction Générale de la Concurrence, de la Consommation et de la Répression des Fraudes (DGCCRF) for food business operator registration and dairy import/wholesale controls; Direction Régionale de l'Environnement, de l'Aménagement et du Logement (DREAL) for facilities-based environmental admissions.

Category: Registre sanitaire / déclaration d'entreprise alimentaire (sanitary registration as a food business operator) for the wholesale distribution of whole and skimmed milk powder, together with the SIVEP/SIGAL import notification registration where third-country-origin goods are brought into the EU; any physically operated cold-storage warehouse requires the ICPE environmental operating authorization as applicable.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; taxpayer portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; business registration portal (Guichet Unique): formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; customs and indirect duties: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), www.douane.gouv.fr; EU-wide VAT number validation: European Commission VIES portal.

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL (including 100% non-resident ownership): (1) file the formation dossier through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, a single INPI-operated electronic counter; certified corporate documents of the foreign parent and the appointed gérant are attached; (2) INSEE allocates the 9-digit SIREN and the 14-digit SIRET upon registration in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); (3) DGFiP automatically allocates the intra-Community VAT number (format FR + 2 control digits + 9 digits, VIES-validated) after validation of the début d'activité; the VAT number appears in the Sirene directory; (4) obtain a French EORI number (FR + SIREN) from DGDDI for customs operations; (5) a non-established foreign trader without a French fixed establishment may obtain a direct French VAT number from the DGFiP (Service des Impôts des Entreprises or the Direction des impôts des non-résidents) under the standalone non-resident registration procedure. The SARL must file its first periodic CA3 VAT return from the month of its first taxable transaction and appoint a fiscal representative only when required for non-established operators.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — default regime is Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at the standard 25% rate, with a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of annual taxable profit if the SARL qualifies (turnover below EUR 10,000,000, capital fully paid up, at least 75% held by individuals or qualifying entities, Article 219-I-b CGI). Under the territoriality principle (Article 209-I CGI and BOFIP-IS-GPE-20-20-20), profits realized through a French establishment are taxable in France while profits of a distinct foreign permanent establishment are exempt, absent a rare worldwide (consolidated) election under Article 209 E CGI. The SARL is a French tax resident under Article 209-A CGI; distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to withholding tax under Article 119 bis 2 CGI at treaty rates. The SARL is not located in a tax-exempt zone as standard French territory applies. — For a French SARL (including 100% non-resident ownership): (1) file the formation dossier through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, a single INPI-operated electronic counter; certified corporate documents of the foreign parent and the appointed gérant are attached; (2) INSEE allocates the 9-digit SIREN and the 14-digit SIRET upon registration in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); (3) DGFiP automatically allocates the intra-Community VAT number (format FR + 2 control digits + 9 digits, VIES-validated) after validation of the début d'activité; the VAT number appears in the Sirene directory; (4) obtain a French EORI number (FR + SIREN) from DGDDI for customs operations; (5) a non-established foreign trader without a French fixed establishment may obtain a direct French VAT number from the DGFiP (Service des Impôts des Entreprises or the Direction des impôts des non-résidents) under the standalone non-resident registration procedure. The SARL must file its first periodic CA3 VAT return from the month of its first taxable transaction and appoint a fiscal representative only when required for non-established operators.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope — default regime is Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at the standard 25% rate, with a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of annual taxable profit if the SARL qualifies (turnover below EUR 10,000,000, capital fully paid up, at least 75% held by individuals or qualifying entities, Article 219-I-b CGI). Under the territoriality principle (Article 209-I CGI and BOFIP-IS-GPE-20-20-20), profits realized through a French establishment are taxable in France while profits of a distinct foreign permanent establishment are exempt, absent a rare worldwide (consolidated) election under Article 209 E CGI. The SARL is a French tax resident under Article 209-A CGI; distributions to non-resident shareholders are subject to withholding tax under Article 119 bis 2 CGI at treaty rates. The SARL is not located in a tax-exempt zone as standard French territory applies.

Available election forms

France has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms: (1) pass-through election under Article 239 bis AA CGI for qualifying SARLs (capital continuously held at least 50% by individuals or by an IR-subject company, 50 employees or fewer, and annual turnover not exceeding EUR 10,000,000 for goods traders) for a limited irrevocable period of 5 fiscal years, renewable; (2) VAT group (assujetti unique) election under Article 267 C CGI for closely linked entities with financial, economic and organizational interdependence; (3) option under Article 260 C CGI to waive the franchise en base de TVA and apply normal VAT rules where turnover remains below the statutory thresholds; (4) options under Articles 260 A and 260 B CGI for VAT treatment of specific real estate and financial services, not relevant to dairy commodity trading; (5) no elective mechanism for hybrid mismatches: the ATAD 2 anti-hybrid rules (Articles 212 bis, 209-0 B, 239 octies A CGI) apply automatically and mandatorily.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA (Articles 293 B and 293 B quinquies CGI; 2025 revalued thresholds): for sales of goods (ventes de marchandises) the normal threshold is EUR 91,900 and the upper tolerance limit is EUR 105,000; for services EUR 39,100 / EUR 43,400. A wholesale dairy milk powder trader exceeds the goods threshold and must register for VAT from the transaction exceeding the upper tolerance, or immediately upon exceeding the normal threshold where prior-year turnover already exceeded it; in practice the SARL registers with the SIE before its first taxable supply or import. Non-established foreign suppliers are required to register regardless of threshold when making taxable supplies in France, and import VAT is due at clearance unless the company holds a DGFiP authorization to self-assess import VAT on its CA3 return.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération) applies to definitive exports of goods outside the EU customs territory under Article 262-I-1° CGI: sales of whole and skimmed milk powder to non-EU buyers qualify when the seller proves physical removal of the goods from the EU within the accounting period and holds transport documentation in its name or that of its customer. The French SARL should be named as exporter of record (exportateur) on the DAU/ECS customs declaration. Intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered customers are exempt, not zero-rated, under Article 262 ter I CGI, subject to valid EU VAT number, EC Sales List (DEB/DEPS) filing and customer VIES validation. Under Article 262-I-2° CGI, directly related offshore services (handling, transport, logistics) supporting the exported goods may also be zero-rated. Domestic sales of dairy products for human consumption attract the reduced 5.5% rate (Article 278 bis CGI) but exports always benefit from 0% or exemption, with full input VAT recovery via the periodic CA3 return.

  • Customs Export Declaration: French DAU/ECS declaration (or the export declaration where the seller is exporter of record) validated by DGDDI, showing the SARL's French EORI and VAT number, HS codes 0402.10 (whole milk powder) or 0402.10/0402.91 (skimmed milk powder), the exit customs office and the date of exit from EU customs territory.
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR, air waybill or multimodal transport document): evidencing physical exit from the EU, with shipment route, port/airport of exit, consignee and cargo description matching the commercial invoice and customs declaration.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank payment confirmation (SWIFT MT103 cash transfer or MT700 documentary credit), or the French bank credit advice / attestation de domiciliation bancaire showing receipt of export proceeds in the SARL's French bank account within the commercial payment cycle.
  • Supporting commercial evidence: signed export sales contract or purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin (EUR.1 where required), and, for intra-Community exempt supplies, VIES validation evidence of the customer's EU VAT number and the recapitulative DEB/DEPS statement.

France operates a 'data-hub' e-invoicing model with no Italian-style real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping. Under the 2024 Finance Act (postponed calendar): from 1 September 2026 all taxable persons must be able to receive e-invoices and large/mid-size companies must issue e-invoices; from 1 September 2027 small/micro companies must issue e-invoices. E-invoices are issued via a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the state-run Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); formats must comply with EN 16931 (Factur-X, UBL, CII); transmission and e-reporting of transaction and payment data to the PPF occurs normally within one business day of issuance. Cross-border B2B supplies (intra-EU and extra-EU) are subject to e-reporting; B2G invoicing remains on Chorus Pro; API specifications for PPF/PDP interconnection are published by DGFiP. Sanctions under Article 1737 CGI apply per non-compliant invoice (up to EUR 15 per invoice, with annual caps from EUR 15,000 to EUR 60,000 by company size).

The DGFiP applies an extended 10-year reassessment right for VAT where export substantiation is concerned (Article L176 LPF), against the ordinary 3-year window (Article L169 LPF). If the export documentation bundle is missing or inconsistent, the zero-rating is clawed back: VAT is reassessed at 20% on the sales price, plus monthly late interest (intérêt de retard at 0.2% per month), plus penalties: 40% for bad-faith understatement (Article 1729-a CGI) and 80% for abusive or fraudulent maneuvers (Article 1729-b CGI), including fictitious exports and carousel-type arrangements. The abuse-of-law procedure (Article L64 LPF, 80% penalty) and joint liability under Article 283-4 CGI may apply where the supplier knew or should have known of customer-side fraud. Criminal exposure under Article 1741 CGI reaches EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment (aggravated fraud: EUR 3,000,000 and 7 years); customs fraud under Articles 414-415 of the Code des douanes attracts penalties of one to three times the evaded duties, confiscation of goods and vehicles, and imprisonment of 3 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. France is an IMF Article VIII member; capital movements are liberalised under Articles 63-65 TFEU; the euro (EUR) is a fully convertible EU currency. No prior exchange-control approval, license, conversion quota, or repatriation restriction applies to trade payments, shareholder capital injections, loan disbursements, or profit remittances by a French SARL engaged in ISIC 4633 dairy wholesale. The compliance architecture is limited to: (i) statistical DTF reporting of every cross-border transfer to/from France of EUR 10,000 or above per transaction (Code monétaire et financier, Articles L.152-1 and R.152-1 to R.152-2-2), filed electronically on the BdF ONE portal – normally discharged automatically by the French instructing bank, or within 20 working days by the French-resident account holder where the transfer bypasses a French intermediary; (ii) EU and national financial sanctions screening of counterparties; (iii) foreign-direct-investment prior-authorisation only for sensitive sectors under CMF Article L.151-3 – not applicable to raw milk powder wholesale (ISIC 4633/NAICS 424430); and (iv) corporate-law and tax formalities on profit distribution: a valid shareholders' decision complying with Code de commerce Article L.232-11 (positive distributable profit, net-asset test), followed by dividend withholding at 30% PFU for non-resident individuals (treaty-reduced as applicable) or 0% for qualifying EU corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Article 119 ter CGI, subject to the 5% holding and 2-year minimum thresholds). No BdF or DG Trésor clearance is required for any such remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France imposes no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio into EUR, no repatriation deadline, and no holding-period requirement on export proceeds. A French SARL may retain foreign currency indefinitely in an authorised foreign-currency account (compte en devises), use such balances directly for supplier settlements, or convert them via spot/forward contracts. The sole obligation is the DTF statistical declaration for any cross-border fund movement of EUR 10,000 or more; when the export proceeds are received into a French bank account, the receiving bank files the declaration as statutory intermediary. If proceeds are collected in a non-French account and thereafter remitted to France, the SARL itself must ensure the DTF is filed with the BdF within 20 working days.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) – national central bank, authority for balance-of-payments statistics and the Déclaration de Transfert de Fonds (DTF) reporting system; exchange-control policy coordinated with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique. The European Central Bank (ECB) exercises monetary sovereignty over the euro.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium – Conditionally Viable for operational disbursements only; non-viable for escrow or trade-finance functions. ACPR-registered French/EEA electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Manager.one, Treezor-based platforms) are acceptable for SEPA CT/SCT, supplier payouts, and multi-currency FX conversion, but none provide commercial LC issuance, standby LC, bank guarantees, or commodity pre-finance. Offshore non-EEA EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business with non-French IBANs) are treated as heightened-risk by French tier-1 banks and ACPR inspectors; they may be used only as supplementary collection channels, never as the primary treasury anchor or as holder of equity escrow funds. Legal constraint: EMIs are not credit institutions (no deposit-taking licence, no Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution cover), so escrowed equity capital and client trade deposits must remain with an ACPR-licensed credit institution subject to the French deposit-blocking regime for SARL incorporation capital.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Wholly non-resident ownership of the SARL with limited apparent French economic substance (shell-company red flag)

Root cause: Wholly non-resident ownership of the SARL with limited apparent French economic substance (shell-company red flag)

Defense evidence: (i) Certified KBIS extract and notarised articles of association; (ii) full UBO chain to natural persons filed at the INPI Registre National des Bénéficiaires Effectifs; (iii) apostilled passports and utility-bill proof of address for each UBO; (iv) French registered-office lease; (v) substance evidence – French payroll, professional indemnity insurance, signed supply and distribution contracts with French/EU dairy counterparties; (vi) reference letter and 12 months of statements from the shareholder's home-country primary bank; (vii) audited or professionally prepared opening accounts and a 3-year business plan with transaction-throughput projections.
Routed third-party payments through brokers, tolling processors, or non-invoiced intermediaries in dairy commodity flows

Root cause: Routed third-party payments through brokers, tolling processors, or non-invoiced intermediaries in dairy commodity flows

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity or shareholder-loan injections must arrive via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT where the shareholder is EEA-resident) with an accurate purpose code and narration. For statutory capital: the funds must first be deposited at a licensed bank or with a notary before RCS registration (Code de commerce Article L.223-7 – at least 20% of each in-cash share paid at subscription; balance within 5 years); upon immatriculation, the depository releases the funds to the SARL's operational account. The MT103 must reference the BdF DTF purpose category 'Investissements directs – prises de participation' (shareholder loans: category 'Crédits financiers'), with narration citing the articles of association or the EGM capital-increase resolution. Verification protocol: the receiving French bank requires the attestation de dépôt de fonds or notarial confirmation of deposit; the bank files the DTF automatically where the inbound amount equals or exceeds EUR 10,000; for transfers from a third-country bank, the complete originator chain and ultimate-beneficiary references must be provided. If the SARL is the indirect recipient via a foreign treasury account, a full chain-of-title schedule must accompany the onward remittance.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 open-account settlement to EU and third-country dairy suppliers (e.g., New Zealand, Ireland, Germany, Netherlands) with the purpose-code category 'Paiements de marchandises' and invoice/contract references in fields 70-71A; executed in EUR via SEPA/TARGET2 or in USD/NZD via BNP Paribas/Société Générale correspondent banks in New York/Wellington, with FX trades cut before 10:00 CET for same-day value; (ii) Documentary Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP 600 issued by the French opening bank (BNP Paribas, Société Générale, Crédit Agricole CIB) in favour of the offshore supplier, available by sight payment or deferred acceptance, covering whole/skimmed milk powder shipments; LC issuance requires margin collateralisation typically 10-30% for a newly onboarded SARL; (iii) Standby LC / demand guarantee under ISP98 to collateralise prepayments to suppliers; (iv) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer (SCT Inst, EUR 100,000 per-transaction ceiling) for European suppliers requiring real-time settlement; (v) consolidation of all outbound flows through a single French treasury account to preserve an automatic, complete DTF audit trail. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium – Conditionally Viable for operational disbursements only; non-viable for escrow or trade-finance functions. ACPR-registered French/EEA electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Manager.one, Treezor-based platforms) are acceptable for SEPA CT/SCT, supplier payouts, and multi-currency FX conversion, but none provide commercial LC issuance, standby LC, bank guarantees, or commodity pre-finance. Offshore non-EEA EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business with non-French IBANs) are treated as heightened-risk by French tier-1 banks and ACPR inspectors; they may be used only as supplementary collection channels, never as the primary treasury anchor or as holder of equity escrow funds. Legal constraint: EMIs are not credit institutions (no deposit-taking licence, no Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts et de Résolution cover), so escrowed equity capital and client trade deposits must remain with an ACPR-licensed credit institution subject to the French deposit-blocking regime for SARL incorporation capital.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 (gross turnover or gross balance-sheet total of the French entity or the worldwide group); below this threshold, basic contemporaneous documentation must nevertheless be prepared and produced at the tax auditor's request.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulation comparable to common-law offshore centres. However, under Article L. 64 of the French Tax Procedures Book the abuse-of-law doctrine permits disregard of artificial arrangements lacking genuine commercial substance; the OECD principal purpose test under treaties further denies tax benefits if obtaining treaty relief was a principal purpose. A French SARL engaged in raw milk powder trade must demonstrate actual management decisions, physical office or storage facilities, staff, commercial correspondence, logistics control, and real intra-group substance, particularly when claiming DTA benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Administrative: 80% fraud penalty under CGI Art. 1729-0 and late-payment interest at 0.20% per month. Criminal: under CGI Art. 1741, a fine of up to EUR 3,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years for the natural persons organising the evasion; the court may also order closure of the business, publication of the judgement, and forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: In cases of fraudulent misstatement or concealment of assets, managers may be declared personally liable for the SARL's tax debt and penalties under Article L. 267 LPF, thereby piercing the corporate veil. Travel bans, asset freezes and judicial supervision are common during criminal investigation; conviction may result in imprisonment and a professional ban on acting as a company director.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Where the French tax authority exercises the abuse-of-law doctrine under LPF Art. L. 64, the artificial arrangement is set aside; the taxpayer must bear a 80% penalty on the resulting under-assessed tax, interest is computed at 0.20% per month, and all DTA benefits may be denied. Additional criminal prosecution for fraud is possible where intent is proven.

Director personal liability: A director of a sham company is exposed to joint and several liability for the enterprise's tax and penalties under Article L. 267 LPF if he or she used the structure to avoid or defeat tax collection. Courts may impose a ban on managing commercial enterprises and a prohibition from leaving French territory for the duration of the criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may adjust the transfer price to the arm's length level and reallocate profit to France; the reassessment is increased by a 40% penalty under CGI Art. 1729 in cases of deliberate or negligent omission, and to 80% if payments are made to a judicial non-cooperative state or if abuse of law is found. Late-payment interest and the 3.3% social contribution on reassessed profit also apply.

Director personal liability: When a manager signs the corporate income tax return containing manipulated transfer-pricing figures and knowingly participates in the scheme, personal liability may arise under criminal tax law and under Article L. 267 LPF for debts owed by the company; a sentence may include imprisonment up to 5 years for customs or false-document offences in cross-border transactions.
AML / Unidentified Payment Counterparties

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is punishable under Articles 324-1 and 324-2 of the French Criminal Code by 5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine; aggravated laundering (participation in a criminal organisation, habitual laundering, use of the financial system) raises the penalty to 10 years and EUR 750,000. Tracfin notification failures may trigger separate administrative fines and confiscation of goods and bank accounts.

Director personal liability: The SARL's legal fiction does not protect directors when they order or tolerate the use of false invoices or foreigners' shell accounts; the directors are prosecutable as principals or accomplices. During the investigation, the court may order travel bans, seizure of passports, freezing of bank assets, and, upon conviction, imprisonment and prohibition from professional activity.
Non-disclosure of DTA Beneficiary Identity

Statutory sanction: The application of the reduced withholding rate is revoked; the tax administration collects the difference between the treaty rate and the statutory 30% withholding, together with interest for late payment; a fine of 80% is added for intentional misrepresentation or abuse of law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the Form 2562-TB knowing the identity of the ultimate recipient to be false can be held jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax and may be subject to criminal penalties for tax fraud; travel restrictions may be ordered to guarantee the investigation.
