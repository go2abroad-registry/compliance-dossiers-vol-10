Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital threshold; the shareholders freely fix the capital. Cash contributions must be released in at least one-fifth immediately and deposited into a blocked account or a French bank; the remaining balance must be paid within five years. In-kind contributions are fully subscribed and released as assessed by the commissaire aux apports.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certificate delivered by the local commission de sécurité (or SDIS) for storage premises exceeding 500 m², including an approved emergency plan, fire extinguishers, and smoke-detection signage; structural soundness certificate prepared by an approved building engineer; and an occupational workplace clearance issued by the DDETS/DRIEETS confirming compliance with health and labor inspection standards.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital threshold; the shareholders freely fix the capital. Cash contributions must be released in at least one-fifth immediately and deposited into a blocked account or a French bank; the remaining balance must be paid within five years. In-kind contributions are fully subscribed and released as assessed by the commissaire aux apports.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — taxpayer portal https://www.impots.gouv.fr; business formalities portal https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique operated by INPI); local competent office: Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) of the registered office.

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, the legal representative files the incorporation and registration dossier with the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). DGFiP assigns the SIREN number, SIRET establishments, and the French VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire: FR + 2-digit key + SIREN). Foreign non-established entities making taxable supplies in France must file a direct VAT registration (simplified form CERFA, Service des Impôts des Entreprises) and obtain a French VAT number from the first euro of French taxable activity; exporters use this number to zero-rate exports and recover input VAT.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (opaque SARL subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at 25% standard rate; reduced 15% rate on taxable profits up to EUR 42,500 per fiscal year under art. 219 CGI); territorial scope: world-wide profits for a French-resident SARL subject to the foreign PE exception (art. 209 I CGI); no tax-exempt zone status. — For a French SARL, the legal representative files the incorporation and registration dossier with the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (opaque SARL subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at 25% standard rate; reduced 15% rate on taxable profits up to EUR 42,500 per fiscal year under art. 219 CGI); territorial scope: world-wide profits for a French-resident SARL subject to the foreign PE exception (art. 209 I CGI); no tax-exempt zone status.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in France. Options available: (i) family SARL election under art. 239 bis AA CGI to be taxed transparently at the partner level (impôt sur le revenu), available only for SARLs whose capital is held by related individuals; (ii) tax consolidation (intégration fiscale, art. 223 A CGI) for a 95%-controlled French group; (iii) optional 5-year alignment of accounting year-end (art. 208 CGI). Hybrid mismatch rules (art. 212 bis, 209 B CGI, ATAD 2016/1164) restrict deduction/non-inclusion outcomes and reverse mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 annual turnover for supplies of goods under the franchise-en-base exemption (art. 293 B CGI applies to established businesses); foreign non-established entities and exporters must register for French VAT from the first euro of taxable French supplies — no threshold applies. Cross-border wholesale exports of goods are outside the French turnover threshold and are zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods dispatched outside the EU qualify for VAT exemption with credit (0%) under art. 262-I-1° CGI, provided the goods leave the French or EU customs territory within 90 days of dispatch and the supplier holds proof of shipment in its commercial file; ancillary services (freight, customs handling) linked to exported goods and certain services to non-EU business customers are also zero-rated under art. 259 A/B CGI. Dried goji berries and processed herbal botanicals (ISIC 4631) exported in bulk qualify as physical goods; the exporter retains the right to deduct input VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / e-Export via DELTA-X)
  • Bill of Lading (or CMR / Air Waybill) evidencing physical exit
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • SWIFT MT103 / Inward Remittance Certificate as payment proof
  • EUR.1 movement certificate or REX statement for preferential origin claims

Mandatory nationwide B2B e-invoicing and cross-border e-reporting under the 2024-2025 budget law, phased in from 1 September 2026 (large/medium enterprises) and 1 September 2027 (SMEs including SARLs). Invoices must be exchanged via the Public Invoicing Portal (Portail Public de Facturation - PPF) or a certified PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) using structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII); export transactions are transmitted as e-reporting data to DGFiP through the PPF/PDP APIs. France uses continuous transaction controls for reporting, not per-invoice fiscal stamping/real-time authorisation.

DGFiP audits the complete export file, the 90-day exit deadline and the consistency of customs status with transport and payment flows. If export evidence is missing or fraudulent, the VAT is retroactively claimed (clawback with issuance of VAT assessment), input VAT credits are denied, and penalties apply: 80% of the tax for bad faith or manoeuvres fraudulentes (art. 1729 CGI), 40% late-payment interest, and up to 100% additional penalty for fraud (art. 1729-0 CGI). Criminal fines apply under the customs code for false declarations; failure to repatriate FX proceeds within 150 days exposes the exporter to administrative penalties and foreign-exchange irregularities under art. R561-5 CMF.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible. France and the EU generally permit current-account and capital-account payments and transfers, subject to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing controls, tax rules, banking due diligence, and any applicable reporting requirements. A French SARL may receive foreign investment, pay foreign suppliers, and remit lawfully distributable profits abroad without a general FX approval. Dividend distributions remain subject to French corporate-law procedures, distributable-profit and reserve tests, withholding-tax rules, treaty or EU relief conditions, and required tax documentation. Banks may request contracts, invoices, ownership information, source-of-funds evidence, and payment-purpose details.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio or general deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, subject to bank terms, accounting and tax requirements, sanctions screening, and any transaction-specific reporting duties. EU and French cash-control declarations apply to qualifying physical movements of cash or bearer instruments across relevant borders; they do not constitute an export-receipt conversion rule.

Central bank supervision: The European Central Bank (ECB) conducts euro-area monetary policy. Banque de France is France’s national central bank and participates in the Eurosystem. France has no general foreign-exchange administration imposing routine exchange controls; the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI) administers customs and certain reporting obligations, not general FX permissions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for supplementary payment services, subject to the provider’s geographic eligibility, French or EEA authorization/passporting, permitted account features, safeguarding model, and risk appetite. An electronic-money institution account is not necessarily a bank deposit, may not provide a French IBAN or full trade-finance services, and may restrict high-value commodity, third-country, or cash-intensive flows. Use a regulated bank as the principal operating and trade-finance relationship; verify the provider’s status in the relevant regulator register before onboarding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit
