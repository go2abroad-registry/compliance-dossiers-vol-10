Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), with registration data recorded in the Registre national des entreprises; the official portal is formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation period of 30 days for a SARL. A proposed name may be checked through INPI, Infogreffe, trademark databases and domain registries, but legal protection generally arises from valid use, registration and applicable intellectual-property rights rather than from a universal reservation certificate. If the INPI single window or the competent registry rejects the proposed name, obtain the electronic rejection notice and stated legal basis, correct the name or supporting documents, and resubmit within the prescribed correction period. For an administrative decision that remains disputed, file a reasoned administrative challenge with the competent authority and, where appropriate, an administrative appeal before the competent administrative court within the applicable time limit stated in the decision. If the issue concerns a trademark, company name or unfair competition claim, use the appropriate civil or commercial court and, for trademark matters, the relevant INPI opposition or cancellation procedure. A new non-conflicting name is usually faster and less costly than litigating a preventable similarity objection.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital is imposed for a SARL (may be set at EUR 1). Cash contributions must be paid up to at least one-fifth (20%) at subscription; the remaining balance must be released within five years. Capital deposits must be held on a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts until incorporation is completed, and a certificate of deposit is required for the RCS filing.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: attestation of compliance with ERP (Établissement Recevant du Public) rules if a public reception area is open; otherwise, employee-only office buildings must comply with labor fire prevention rules and obtain a landlord or DREETS inspection certificate. Structural load inspections are required for high-bay racking in any warehouse. Occupational clearance: preparation of the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques (DUER), registration of workstations with the occupational health service, medical surveillance for staff, and a DREETS labor inspection clearance where applicable.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital is imposed for a SARL (may be set at EUR 1). Cash contributions must be paid up to at least one-fifth (20%) at subscription; the remaining balance must be released within five years. Capital deposits must be held on a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts until incorporation is completed, and a certificate of deposit is required for the RCS filing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); official portal: impots.gouv.fr. Non-resident taxpayers are handled by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) and the Centre des Impôts des Non-Résidents (CINR), 10 Rue du Centre, 93160 Noisy-le-Grand. Corporate formation is effected via the mandatory single electronic window (Guichet unique) operated by INPI at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Customs clearance and export control are administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), portal: douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SARL through the Guichet unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); the resulting registration in the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) automatically generates the 9-digit SIREN and the SIRET establishment code. Step 2: DGFiP automatically allocates the French intra-Community VAT identification number (FR + 2-digit cryptographic key + 9-digit SIREN) to the registered head office pursuant to Articles 286-I and 286 quater CGI. Step 3: A non-established foreign wholesaler with taxable French supplies (e.g., goods stored in French third-party logistics) must file a 'Demande d'immatriculation d'un assujetti non établi' with the SIEE, attaching constitutional documents, a copy of the trade/business register of the foreign entity, the underlying supply contract, the French EORI number, directors' identification, and a power of attorney. Step 4: A foreign entity without any EU fixed establishment must appoint an accredited tax representative ('représentant fiscal agréé') who is jointly and severally liable for French VAT (Article 289 A CGI) and must post a bank guarantee. Step 5: After VAT registration, the taxpayer must file the DeB/DEB (Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens) for intra-EU goods movements and maintain an EORI number issued by DGDDI for customs declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – A French SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax ('impôt sur les sociétés', IS) under Article 206-1 CGI, taxable on profits derived from businesses operated in France in accordance with the territoriality principle of Article 209-I CGI. The SARL is not fiscally transparent; distributions to shareholders are separately taxed (in France, generally a 30% flat tax for individuals or a withholding tax regime for non-residents). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – A French SARL is by default subject to French corporate income tax ('impôt sur les sociétés', IS) under Article 206-1 CGI, taxable on profits derived from businesses operated in France in accordance with the territoriality principle of Article 209-I CGI. The SARL is not fiscally transparent; distributions to shareholders are separately taxed (in France, generally a 30% flat tax for individuals or a withholding tax regime for non-residents). The company may, under strict statutory conditions, elect transient income-tax transparency, but the default classification is entity-level corporate taxation.

Available election forms

Available elections: (a) Income-tax transparency option under Article 239 bis AA CGI, allowing a small SARL to be taxed at the level of its individual partners ('régime fiscal des sociétés de personnes') for five financial years, renewable, subject to cumulative conditions: at least 50% of the capital held by natural persons, fewer than 50 employees, annual turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, and exercise of a commercial, industrial, artisanal or liberal activity; (b) French fiscal consolidation ('intégration fiscale') under Article 223 A CGI, available to a French parent holding at least 95% of the shares of each French subsidiary, elected for five years and renewable; (c) VAT grouping election ('assujetti unique') under Article 256 C CGI for closely linked French companies; (d) hybrid mismatch recharacterization under Article 38 bis CGI is automatic under ATAD and not elective. France does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established SARL carrying on wholesale of goods, there is no monetary threshold excluding registration: the company is a taxable person and must obtain a French VAT number from its first transaction, notwithstanding the theoretical micro-business VAT exemption ('franchise en base de TVA', Article 293 B CGI) available up to EUR 101,000 annual turnover for sale of goods and EUR 39,100 for services (2025 thresholds, with upper ceiling of EUR 111,000 / EUR 44,700 after which exemption lapses entirely). For non-established foreign enterprises supplying goods or B2B services into France, registration is compulsory from the first euro of taxable French turnover; the EU-wide distance-sales threshold of EUR 10,000 for cross-border B2C distance sales does not apply to wholesale B2B supplies. Intra-Community acquisitions by a French VAT-registered SARL require a valid French VAT ID, and no local IT procurement threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération à 0% with full right to input VAT recovery) applies under two parallel regimes: (1) Non-EU exports under Article 262-I-1° CGI, for tangible goods dispatched or transported by or on behalf of the seller to a destination outside the European Union, provided the seller is the exporter on the French customs export declaration and the goods physically leave EU customs territory; (2) Intra-Community supplies under Article 262-I-2° CGI, where goods are shipped from France to a taxable customer registered for VAT in another Member State, with the customer's valid intra-Community VAT number quoted and the supplier filing the EC Sales List (Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens) and the recapitulative statement under Article 289 B CGI. Offshore services directly linked to exported goods may also be zero-rated in limited cases under Article 259 A CGI and Article 262-I-1°, but pure digital or remote services are taxable at 20% at the customer's place of establishment under the B2B reverse charge (Article 283-2 CGI). For the wholesale of SSDs and server processors, physical export or intra-EU dispatch must be fully evidenced before the zero rate is claimed on the periodic VAT return; in the absence of compliant evidence, the supply is reclassified as taxable at 20%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation – DE) lodged electronically in the DELTA customs system, with the assigned MRN (Movement Reference Number) and the electronic proof of exit transmitted by DGDDI through the European Export Control System (ECS/ARC).
  • Commercial invoice and packing list reflecting precise quantities, serial numbers, unit values, Incoterms and the buyer's identity; the invoice must state the applicable zero-rating provision (Article 262-I-1° or 262-I-2° CGI).
  • Transport documentation evidencing exit from EU territory: Bill of Lading (B/L) for sea freight, CMR/consignment note for road freight, or Air Waybill (AWB) for air freight, issued to the foreign buyer or to an authorized customs broker/forwarder.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice proving settlement of the export sale in convertible currency and repatriation of proceeds to the French exporting entity.
  • For intra-Community supplies: EC Sales List (DEB) and the recapitulative statement of Article 289 B CGI filed with the French statistical/customs authorities, plus documentary proof of transport into the destination Member State and the customer's valid intra-Community VAT number.
  • EORI number issued by DGDDI and retained in the exporter's register; where applicable, the 'preuve de l'exportation' file must be completed with the customs exit certification before the VAT return deadline.

Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing enters into force under Article 289 bis CGI in two phases: from 1 September 2026 all taxable persons must be able to receive structured electronic invoices, and large and medium-sized enterprises must issue them; from 1 September 2027 small and micro-enterprises must issue them. Invoices must use the French normative structured format (Factur-X / EN 16931) and be exchanged through an accredited 'Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire' (PDP) or the national public portal 'FNFE-M' via secure APIs. E-reporting obligations require transmission of transaction data for cross-border supplies of goods and services and for B2C supplies to DGFiP through the same channel. France follows a post-audit continuous-transaction-control model rather than real-time governmental clearance; no digital stamp or real-time fiscal approval is required. Sanctions for non-compliance include graduated fines of EUR 15 per missing or non-compliant invoice and EUR 20 per omitted e-reporting record, subject to annual caps of EUR 15,000 and EUR 20,000 respectively, increased in cases of bad faith.

French customs and tax authorities conduct systematic post-clearance audits of export evidence. There is no statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline in French VAT law; the 180-day figure is a conservative internal compliance benchmark reflecting the payment horizon accepted by fiscal auditors for documentary substantiation; a payment outstanding beyond 180 days triggers heightened scrutiny and burden of proof. If the export proof is missing, invalid or forged, the zero-rated supply is recharacterized as taxable at 20% (Article 272 CGI), with immediate VAT recovery. Standard penalties: late-payment interest of 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI), a 40% penalty for bad faith (mauvaise foi) and an 80% penalty for deliberate fraud or 'manœuvres frauduleuses' (Article 1729 CGI). Criminal exposure under Article 1741 CGI reaches EUR 500,000 fine and five years' imprisonment, increased to EUR 3,000,000 and seven years for organized VAT fraud. In MTIC (Missing Trader Intra-Community) cases, the exporter may lose the exemption even where goods physically left France if it knew or should have known of the fraud, with joint and several liability under Article 283-4 CGI. The reassessment window is three years under Article L.168 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales (LPF), extended to six years where a return was omitted or fraudulent (Article L.176 LPF). Recovery is enforced through administrative attachment ('saisie administrative à tiers détenteur') and customs seizure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member state with full currency convertibility for both capital and current account transactions. There are no exchange control restrictions on the conversion of euros to foreign currency or vice versa. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is freely permitted, subject to standard corporate tax and withholding tax obligations, and no prior administrative authorization is required. Capital repatriation and dividend distributions are equally unrestricted, cross-border payments are processed via the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA) and SWIFT rails. The only obligations are reporting and declaration to the Directorate General of Public Finance (DGFiP) and the customs authority for statistical and tax purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or any government-controlled entity, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export receipts may be held in foreign currency accounts with any licensed credit institution in France or abroad, subject to mandatory declaration of cross-border physical cash movements exceeding EUR 10,000 (under EU Regulation (EC) No 1889/2005) and electronic payment reporting to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics. All invoicing and settlement documentation must be retained for at least six years for audit and tax inspection purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting under the regulatory oversight of the European Central Bank (ECB) and the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal constraints. French-registered Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto and Shine, or ACPR-licensed fintechs, can provide fully digital onboarding and core settlement accounts in euros. However, EMIs are generally not licensed to provide foreign exchange hedging derivatives or complex trade finance instruments such as letters of credit, and may not hold funds in segregated escrow with the same creditor protection as full banks. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK-licensed or Asia-Pacific) face substantial legal limitations; they cannot hold French corporate accounts without a branch or passported licence, and French tax authorities will not recognize them for escrow or trade settlement purposes. T1 strategy therefore should pair an EMI for operational agility with a T1 bank for escrow, hedging, and LC issuance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from non-resident shareholders must be executed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer with purpose code 'G302' (EU/EEA statistical purpose code for direct investment in the reporting country) or 'CG01' for capital transactions, depending on the sending bank's mapping to the IMF Balance of Payments Manual (BPM6). The corporate treasury must provide supporting documentation to the receiving French bank, including the shareholders' resolution, share subscription agreement, and a notarized copy of the French entity's Kbis. Wire transfers over EUR 10,000 must be reported to the Banque de France within 20 business days after the transaction (monthly reporting for recurring flows). All funds must be credited to the SARL's capital escrow account before any operational disbursement to comply with French money-laundering rules and to secure the bank's 'customer acceptance' pattern. For immediate liquidity, funds may be transferred via SWIFT as an 'equity contribution in kind' or 'shareholder current account advance', but such advances must be documented with an advance agreement (convention de trésorerie) to avoid deemed dividend treatment.Trade settlements to foreign suppliers and service providers must be executed primarily through SWIFT (MT103/202) for T/T operations and commercial letters of credit (documentary credits, UCP600-compliant) for larger or riskier procurement volumes. The standard clearing rail is SEPA in euros for EU counterparties (hard-coded creditor account with IBAN), and SWIFT for non-EU suppliers in USD, CNY, or HKD. For each outbound disbursement, the French bank requires a supporting commercial invoice, a shipping document (bill of lading or air waybill), and a customs declaration (if physical export from France). Purpose codes for outbound payment should align with BPM6: 'G401' (imports of goods) for inventory restocking, 'G502' (trade credit) for pre-import financing, and 'G306' (direct investment abroad) for funding overseas branches. There is no statutory settlement timeline restriction, but the bank's AML call-back procedures may require a 'purpose of payment' statement for each transfer. Escrowed payment linked to an inspection certificate (SOC or SGS) is the recommended clearing rail for transactions with untested suppliers; the bank may issue a 'sight payment under instruction' confirming the exporter will be paid only after presentation of the pre-issued inspection certificate/quality compliance rating.Outright FX Forward Contracts: OTC forward purchase or sale of EUR against USD, CNY, HKD, or other non-EUR trade currencies for periods of 1 week to 12 months, executable with a licensed French bank subject to ISDA or FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) master agreement, no margin requirement for SME clients on covered trades.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs): Suitable for currencies with limited onshore liquidity (e.g., low-liquidity Asian or CIS currencies), though for the primary trade currencies (USD, CNY, HKD) deliverable forwards are preferable and cheap; NDFs require separate ISDA-style documentation and a European Market Infrastructure Regulation (EMIR) reporting obligation.Cross-Currency Swaps: EUR-USD or EUR-CNY cross-currency interest rate swaps, used to hedge both FX and interest-rate exposure on intra-group loans or supplier financing facilities; must be entered into under an approved ISDA 2002 Master Agreement; EMIR centralized clearing exemption available for the SARL as a non-financial counterparty below the clearing threshold.FX Options (Plain Vanilla): OTC call or put options providing conditional hedging flexibility against contingent tender or procurement outcomes; allowed with bank approval under the same ISDA/FBF documentation architecture.FX Swaps (Long-term): Net-settlement swap agreements hedging principal conversion for loan servicing or cross-border share subscription efforts.
