Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique — the French national tax authority responsible for corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) and value added tax (Taxe sur la Valeur Ajoutée, TVA). Business-facing portals: impots.gouv.fr (professional online tax space, TVA and IS declarations, VAT refund claims); formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet unique électronique for incorporation, statutory registration, and automatic tax-folder creation); Chorus Pro (national electronic invoicing hub). Customs export clearance and export zero-rating substantiation are jointly handled by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via the DELTA-X declaration system (douane.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporation through the single electronic filing portal (Guichet unique, formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr): file the SARL statutes (statuts), registered-office proof, identity and domicile of the gérant(s), shareholding registry, UBO declaration (Articles L561-2 et seq., Code monétaire et financier), and a commencement-of-activity statement. Step 2 — INSEE assigns the statutory identifiers: SIREN (9-digit legal-unit number) and SIRET (14-digit establishment number). Step 3 — DGFiP automatically opens the IS and TVA tax folders and issues the French intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 11 characters (SIREN plus 2-digit control key); activation of the professional online tax account is confirmed on impots.gouv.fr. Step 4 — A foreign non-EU parent with VAT obligations in France, or a non-resident treasury/principal acting without a French fixed establishment, must appoint a DGFiP-approved fiscal representative (Article 289 A CGI); the SARL itself, being a French-established entity, files its own taxes. Step 5 — The liposomal vaccine-adjuvant/LNP manufacturing scope requires an ANSM (Agence nationale de sécurité du médicament) pharmaceutical establishment licence and, for certain controlled activities, an export authorisation; the tax registration dossier must be cross-referenced with the ANSM operating authorisation. Non-resident corporate shareholders without a French permanent establishment register with the non-resident taxpayer desk (Service des impôts des particuliers non-résidents) for withholding-tax and procedural purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — The French SARL is an opaque corporate legal entity resident in France by place of effective management and registered office (Article 2 CGI). It is by default subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) at entity level on worldwide profits: 25% standard headline rate for fiscal years from 2024, with a 15% reduced rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying companies with turnover below EUR 10,000,000. French worldwide taxation applies; a territorial exemption for foreign permanent-establishment profits is not automatic, and treaty relief operates through tax credits. Pass-through treatment is not the default; it is available only upon a special election for family-owned SARLs (Article 239 bis AA CGI). No domestic check-the-box regime exists under French law.

Available election forms

1) SARL family pass-through election (Article 239 bis AA CGI): an SARL whose members are natural persons from the same family group may make an irrevocable 5-year option to be taxed under the personal income-tax (IR) transparent regime, with profits taxed at member level. 2) Groupe d'intégration fiscale (tax consolidation, Article 223 A CGI): a French parent holding at least 95% of the SARL's shares may elect to consolidate the SARL's results into a single group IS base filed by the parent. 3) Option for IS by transparent or civil-law entities (Article 206(4) CGI) is available to partnerships but is not needed for the SARL, which is already opaque. 4) Hybrid-mismatch neutralisation (ATAD 1 and 2, implemented by Articles 209 0 B and 212 ter CGI) disallows double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes for hybrid transfers, hybrid entities, and hybrid financial instruments; a high-value MNE SARL potentially subject to the Pillar Two GloBE rules (Law no. 2024-364) must evaluate registration of a supplementary tax declaration where group turnover reaches EUR 750,000,000. No US-style check-the-box mechanism exists; the regimes above are the only statutory routes to modify the default entity-level IS treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Zero de minimis threshold for French-established taxpayers: the SARL must obtain and use a French TVA number from its first taxable transaction in France (Article 286(a) CGI). Simplified VAT filing (régime réel simplifié) applies while annual turnover stays within approximately EUR 840,000 for sales of goods and approximately EUR 254,000 for services; above these ceilings the normal real regime with monthly or quarterly declarations applies. For non-established foreign suppliers selling goods by distance selling into France, registration under French VAT becomes compulsory once their annual distance sales into the EU exceed the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold (Article 258 D CGI); for suppliers storing goods in France under consignment/fulfillment-stock models, immediate TVA registration is required from the first transaction, and non-EU suppliers must appoint a DGFiP-approved fiscal representative as required by Article 289 A CGI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is governed by Article 262-I CGI: supplies of goods dispatched or transported by the seller or for its account to a destination outside the European Union are exempt with credit for input VAT (zero-rated) provided (i) a French/EU export customs declaration (Déclaration Administrative Unique, DAU) is lodged through DELTA-X in the name and tax account of the exporting SARL, (ii) the goods physically leave the EU customs territory within the statutory deadline, and (iii) the exporter holds the Export Accompanying Document (EAD) and third-party proof of exit (bill of lading or air waybill) within three months. Directly related services — quality-control testing, microfluidic encapsulation support, cold-chain logistics, freight forwarding, and international transport services connected to the exported liposomal vaccine-adjuvant/LNP materials — qualify under Article 262-I-2° read with Article 259 CGI. Deliveries to EU VAT-registered customers are not zero-rated but are intra-Community supplies exempt with right to deduction under Article 262 ter CGI, conditional on the customer's TVA number, EC Sales List reporting, and adequate delivery proof. Input VAT on phospholipids, mRNA payloads, buffers, cleanroom capital equipment, and subcontract processing is creditable via the ordinary monthly TVA returns or the annual credit-refund procedure (remboursement de crédit de TVA) before the DGFiP.

  • Export customs declaration archived in DELTA-X (Déclaration Administrative Unique — DAU) together with the Export Accompanying Document (EAD) and the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the exporting SARL, matching the customs statistical value and stated Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing physical exit of the goods from the EU customs territory
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank credit-confirmation package (SWIFT MT103 pairing with the invoice, or equivalent banque de France payment-reporting evidence) proving receipt of export proceeds in the invoiced currency
  • Certificate of origin (EUR.1, ATR, or Registered-Exporter REX statement) where a preferential tariff origin claim is made at the EU border
  • ANSM export authorisation (autorisation d'exportation) under the Code de la santé publique for vaccine-adjuvant and lipid-nanoparticle pharmaceutical materials, where applicable to the specific shipment

France's mandatory structured e-invoicing reform (Ordonnance no. 2022-1372 and Decree no. 2022-1299): from 1 September 2026, large and mid-size enterprises must issue and receive electronic invoices; from 1 September 2027, small and micro enterprises (including the SARL class) become fully obliged. Invoices must be transmitted through the DGFiP public portal (Chorus Pro) or through an accredited Partner Dematerialisation Platform (PDP) in a format validating EN 16931 (e.g. Factur-X); transaction-level data must be reported to DGFiP in near real time under the mandatory e-invoicing/e-reporting data flows, including e-reporting for cross-border transactions. France operates a structured digital-invoice model without pre-clearance digital stamping: DGFiP conducts risk-based automated analysis of the transmitted data, requiring maintenance of the annual FEC audit file (Fichier des Écritures Comptables, NF v2 format) and, for intra-Community supplies, the periodic EC Sales List (état récapitulatif de TVA, Article 289 B CGI).

Export zero-rating is a priority audit axis for both DGFiP and DGDDI. The SARL must preserve the full export file — EAD/MRN, physical exit proofs, commercial contracts, and payment evidence — for at least six years (Article L102 B, Livre des procédures fiscales), with the audit right extended to ten years where activity was concealed or fraud is established (Article L169 LPF). If the administration recharacterises an alleged export as a domestic taxable supply because proof is insufficient or the export documents are fabricated, it immediately assesses TVA at 20% together with late-payment interest of 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI); deliberate inaccuracies attract the 40% penalty (Article 1729-1 CGI), and fraudulent maneuvers or abuse of law attract the 80% penalty (Article 1729-2 CGI). Failure-to-file and obstruction penalties under Article 1728 CGI range from 10% to 40%. Refunds of input VAT credit obtained through fabricated export files are clawed back with interest and penalties, and the case may be referred to the criminal court for tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI (fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years). On the customs side, false declarations and irregular export documentation engage Articles 414, 426, and 435-436 of the Code des douanes: fines of one to five times the sums evaded, seizure of the goods or their value, suspension or withdrawal of exporter authorisations, and imprisonment up to five years in aggravated cases. A failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day statutory window constitutes a monetary customs infraction under Articles L151-1 et seq. of the Code monétaire et financier and Article 459 du Code des douanes, exposing the SARL to a customs fine equal to 100% of the non-repatriated amount, in addition to any tax reassessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is part of the eurozone and maintains full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under EU treaty law on free movement of capital. Profit remittance is unrestricted; practical hurdles are limited to standard tax withholding procedures, financial sanctions screening, and anti-money-laundering declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in France. French exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in authorised accounts, but must declare cross-border flows exceeding notification thresholds to Banque de France for statistical and monetary monitoring purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) as the foreign exchange administration and balance-of-payments reporting authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility for ancillary payment services only when the fintech holds an EU/EEA electronic money institution licence passported into France or a French ACPR licence. Offshore digital EMIs without a European passport are legally unable to maintain French domestic accounts or provide treasury services to a high-risk pharmaceutical SARL. They are not viable as a primary clearing rail for capital escrow or commercial letters of credit.
