Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office cannot be used as the sole registered office for a manufacturing company such as a SARL engaged in ISIC 3240 (tailoring of licensed theatrical costume robes and synthetic wigs). French law requires an identified physical address within the national territory at which official notices may be served and statutory books kept. The manager's home may serve as registered office for a maximum of five years under Article L.123-14 of the Code de commerce, and a commercial domiciliation company is permissible provided it is registered as an approved domiciliataire. For production activities, the company must maintain a separate physical facility where actual manufacturing, stock, and personnel are established.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the jurisdiction of the registered office — Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); filings are transmitted electronically through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) Guichet Unique portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce): Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL formation and operation), L.210-1 to L.210-10 (general company provisions), and L.227-1 et seq. as applicable; Code de l'artisanat Articles L.112-1 and R.112-1 (professional qualification for craft enterprises); Décret n° 2023-312 (single-window electronic formalities); and applicable INPI/RCS NAF codification rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Single electronic registration file ('dossier unique de création': CERFA/Formulaire M0 — Déclaration de constitution de société) accompanied by the signed Articles of Association (statuts) and supporting annexes; registration results in the official 'extrait Kbis' with SIREN/SIRET.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 4 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for a SARL (freely fixed in the statuts; practical floor €1). Cash contributions must be deposited in a French bank, with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or with a notaire; at least 20% of the cash capital must be released and blocked at signature of the constitutive deed, with the balance payable within 5 years after RCS registration. Contributions in kind must be fully released before signing and remain blocked until issuance of the Kbis.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – impots.gouv.fr; VAT registration and filing via Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); corporate registry via Infogreffe / Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register for a French stand-alone tax identity before commencing taxable activity. Procedure: (1) if non-EU resident, appoint an established fiscal representative acceptable to DGFiP; (2) file a business creation declaration with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (form M0/PR Complet) to obtain SIREN and SIRET numbers; (3) for VAT purposes, submit the relevant VAT registration form (form Montant sur demande, including MOUSA / SF3010 or ca13 depending on activity) to the competent SIE; (4) receive intra-community VAT number (FR followed by 9-digit SIREN) for EU cross-border transactions; (5) where required, register on Chorus Pro for e-invoicing. Steps must be completed prior to any taxable establishment or first B2B invoice.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must register for a French stand-alone tax identity before commencing taxable activity. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a check-the-box regime. A SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS). Statutory elections include: (a) option for income tax (IR) under CGI art. 206-3 and 239bis AB for small enterprises (up to 5 fiscal years, subject to turnover and ownership conditions); (b) option for family-owned SARL under CGI art. 239bis A; (c) election for tax consolidation (régime de groupe) under art. 223A at 95% ownership; (d) hybrid mismatch impact per ATAD principles as implemented for cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-resident taxable persons: each taxable supply of goods in France triggers mandatory VAT registration from the first euro. For resident SMEs, the small business exemption (franchise en base de TVA) applies for taxable turnover below 91,900 EUR for goods sales (2025 limits) or 36,800 EUR for services, but such exemption is not available to foreign entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to physical export of tangible goods outside the European Union provided the seller proves dispatch or transport out of the EU within 3 months (generally 90 days, extendable to one year with authorized proof) and maintains records identifying the goods, commercial invoice, destination, and recipient status. Services physically performed outside the EU or services related to directly exported goods (e.g., export logistics and invoicing services) may also be zero-rated under CGI articles 262-I and 262-II. The zero-rate is denied where transportation is not evidenced, the goods are diverted, or the exporter acts in bad faith. For intra-EU supplies, VAT is exempt under art. 262-I but requires a valid intra-community customer VAT number and summary declaration (recapitulative statement) entry.

  • Single Administrative Document (Déclaration en Douane, DAU/EXS) with customs proof of exit
  • Bill of Lading (if sea freight) or Air Waybill (if air freight)
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the French seller
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / proof of foreign currency payment received
  • Proof of physical transport to non-EU destination (e.g., CMR or delivery confirmation)
  • Export summary declaration and UK/EU Intrastat/Extrastat records

France mandates full B2B electronic invoicing and B2C e-reporting under the 2026-2027 timeline. Large taxpayers must issue and transmit e-invoices from 1 September 2026; all other taxpayers (including SMEs) from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be lodged on the Chorus Pro public portal or through an immatriculated/registered Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP). The system is clearance-based: invoice facts (issuer, transaction, VAT breakdown) must be transmitted to real-time tax reporting infrastructure for validation and audit; electronic stamping is not used but a digital audit trail with unique invoice ID and certified transmission is mandatory.

The French tax authorities audit export zero-rating claims with a high degree of scrutiny, particularly for invoices issued to related/related-party foreign entities, split shipments, or quick turnovers. Where fraud or abusive transaction is found: (a) VAT is reassessed at the 20% standard rate alongside a 40%-80% intentional error penalty (CGI art. 1729-2); (b) an additional 100% rate is applied for bad faith or false declarations; (c) interest for late payment is charged from the due date; (d) the taxpayer's VAT number can be suspended and directors can be held personally liable; (e) criminal sanctions for tax fraud under art. 1741 include up to 5 years imprisonment and fines up to 500,000 EUR; (f) export credit refunds are retroactively clawed back and cannot be offset against subsequent tax periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a eurozone member, maintains full convertibility of the euro for both current and capital account transactions under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). Cross-border profit remittances by a French SARL are permitted without exchange control approval, subject to mandatory tax reporting and, where applicable, dividend withholding tax at the statutory rate of 25% (reduced to 0% or a treaty rate for qualifying EU majority shareholders under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive). No foreign exchange licensing regime applies to ordinary trade settlements; however, financial transfers exceeding €10,000 (or carrying cash) require declaration to the French Customs and the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in France to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency into euros within any specified timeline. Exporters may retain balances in foreign currency indefinitely and use them for any legitimate purpose. For statistical balance-of-payments reporting, French resident companies must report cross-border payments and transfers through the bank intermediary, and periodic declarations to the Banque de France may be required if direct reporting thresholds are triggered. No mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMIs (Electronic Money Institutions) such as Qonto, Shine, or Lilo can be viable as an alternative banking channel for a small SARL, subject to ACPR authorisation and passporting rights. A foreign-owned (non-French) EMI passporting into France under PSD2 may operate legally, but the SARL must ensure the EMI holds a valid ACPR or European Economic Area licence. For cross-border payments to China and other Asian suppliers, the EMI may provide efficient multi-currency accounts, but the receipt of online marketplace proceeds (Stripe/Shopify) is facilitated. Viability rating: High for operational processing, Medium for full treasury services (lending, trade finance, FX lines), and the legal constraint remains the absence of a physical branch and the possible refusal by certain French counterparties to accept EMI credentials.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial capital injection and subsequent equity or loan financing from foreign shareholders must be routed through SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank) using an appropriate purpose code. For a French SARL, the relevant balance-of-payments (personne morale) code for direct investment is '3501' (direct investment in a French company) or '3502' for loans. The bank will require a copy of the shareholder resolution, the shareholder loan agreement, and confirmation of the ultimate source of funds. Funds must be credited to a French bank account or a EU-EMI account with an IBAN; no exchange control authorisation is required. The funds should be declared in the annual customs statistical survey if the amount exceeds thresholds.Settlements for imported raw materials (fabric, synthetic hair, etc.) and outbound repayments of shareholder loans can be executed via SWIFT MT103. For enhanced transaction security and when the supplier requests documentary assurance, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (L/C) issued by a French bank may be used. Typical clearing rails for a French SARL include SEPA CT (Credit Transfer) for EU-denominated euro payments, SWIFT MT103 for cross-border currency payments, and interbank payment systems (T2/TARGET2, EURO1/STEP1 for paneuropean).
