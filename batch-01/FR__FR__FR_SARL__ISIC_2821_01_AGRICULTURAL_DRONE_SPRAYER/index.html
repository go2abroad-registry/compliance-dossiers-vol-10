Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Articles L. 223-1 to L. 223-43 (SARL) and implementing Decree Articles R. 223-1 et seq.; EU Regulation 2019/947 (unmanned aircraft operation) applicable after registration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'une société commerciale (electronic M0 form) together with the signed Articles of Association (statuts) and supporting acts, submitted online via the Guichet unique portal.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital (Article L. 223-2 C. com.); capital is fixed freely in the articles. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation and the balance within 5 years from RCS registration. Cash funds are deposited in a blocked escrow account at an authorized French bank, with a notary, or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and released only upon issuance of the K-bis extract or production of the registrar's refusal.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate: compliance with the Code du travail fire-resistance, alarm and evacuation provisions (Articles R. 4227-1 et seq.) and inspection by the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS); structural load control: technical building inspection verifying roof, floor and mezzanine loads for assembly jigs, drones and lithium-battery stores; workplace clearance: declaration of opening of the establishment to the DREETS, validation of the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP), and periodical third-party verification of electrical installations, lifting equipment and pressure vessels.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital (Article L. 223-2 C. com.); capital is fixed freely in the articles. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) exercises corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés) and VAT (TVA) administration; Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) operates export customs control and clearance. Digital portals: impots.gouv.fr (tax accounts and declarations), formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique for corporate formation and statutory filings), and Pro.douane.gouv.fr (DELTA-X for customs export declarations).

E-government filing portal

A French SARL receives its national identifiers — SIREN (9-digit corporate number) and SIRET (establishment number) — automatically from INSEE upon registration through the Guichet Unique; no separate domestic tax registration filing is required. The DGFiP then issues the intra-Community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN) and the IS corporate tax account code (code TVA/IS), notified electronically without a separate application. Non-resident foreign entities lacking a French establishment must obtain a French TIN by filing a specific non-resident identification request (formulaire n° 3571 or the simplified SIE non-résidents procedure), submitting certified proof of legal existence in the home state, a notarial power of attorney appointing a French fiscal representative where required (Article 164 quinquies CGI), and documentary evidence of the intended taxable French activity. Registration is effective for VAT from the first taxable transaction where no established business exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — An SARL is by default a corporation fully subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) on its taxable profits (Article 206-1 CGI). France applies a modified territorial scope: profits realized through a foreign permanent establishment are exempt from French IS (Article 209-I CGI), while French-source commercial, industrial, and passive income remains taxable at the standard rate of 25% (15% reduced rate on first EUR 42,500 of annual profit for SMEs meeting independence and turnover conditions). — A French SARL receives its national identifiers — SIREN (9-digit corporate number) and SIRET (establishment number) — automatically from INSEE upon registration through the Guichet Unique; no separate domestic tax registration filing is required. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — An SARL is by default a corporation fully subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS) on its taxable profits (Article 206-1 CGI). France applies a modified territorial scope: profits realized through a foreign permanent establishment are exempt from French IS (Article 209-I CGI), while French-source commercial, industrial, and passive income remains taxable at the standard rate of 25% (15% reduced rate on first EUR 42,500 of annual profit for SMEs meeting independence and turnover conditions).

Available election forms

(i) Transparent IR election: A 'family SARL' — whose shares are owned exclusively by natural persons connected by direct line, between siblings, spouses, or PACS partners — may elect within the first three months of the financial year to be taxed on the members' personal income (impôt sur le revenu) for a non-renewable five-year period (Article 239 bis AA CGI). (ii) IS election: Otherwise transparent entities may opt for corporate income tax under Article 206-3 CGI, which is then irrevocable except in defined circumstances. (iii) Fiscal integration: A parent holding at least 95% of an SARL may elect the régime de l'intégration fiscale (Article 223 A CGI) to consolidate group results. (iv) Foreign PE loss election: A parent company may elect, under Article 209-I al. 3 CGI, to deduct foreign permanent establishment losses in exchange for taxing that PE's future profits; recapture applies on a five-year basis. (v) Hybrid mismatch rules: France has implemented ATAD 1/2 anti-hybrid provisions (Articles 212 bis and 209 B CGI) with mandatory, not optional, disallowance; there is no US-style 'check-the-box' election under French domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA (Article 293 B CGI), effective 1 January 2025: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for the supply of goods (agricultural drone manufacturing being goods), with a tolerance ceiling of EUR 93,500 for the immediately preceding year; entrepreneurs falling below both thresholds may elect to opt for normal VAT to preserve input deduction rights. Foreign suppliers not established in France benefit from no threshold and must register from the first euro of taxable turnover in France. Upon exceeding the threshold, registration is mandatory within 15 days, with VAT chargeable from the date of the transaction crossing the limit.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of agricultural drone hardware to destinations outside the EU are VAT-exempt at 0% with full recovery of input VAT under Article 262-I-1° CGI, conditional upon physical movement of the goods out of French and EU territory within 90 days of dispatch and retention of direct documentary proof of departure. Intra-Community dispatches to EU business customers qualify for exemption under Article 262 ter-I CGI where the recipient's valid EU VAT number is stated on the invoice and the EC Sales List and DEB declaration are filed. For offshore services related to the drones (e.g., software licensing, training, telematics services supplied to non-EU agronomists), the B2B place-of-supply rule at Article 259-1° CGI (implementing Art. 44 VAT Directive) places the supply at the customer's establishment, taking the supply outside French VAT where the customer holds a non-EU VAT or equivalent identifier; services directly connected to exported goods may also qualify for the Article 262-I-2° CGI export exemption. Prohibitive conditions apply where the customer is a private individual resident in France, or where services relate to immovable property in France.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation — DAE) lodged electronically in the DELTA-X system and bearing the approved Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading (connaissement maritime) or Air Waybill / CMR road consignment note evidencing physical departure of the goods from the EU
  • Commercial invoice (facture commerciale) with Incoterms and matching commodity tariff codes (HS Harmonized System code for agricultural UAV sprayers)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation de rapatriement des devises) issued by the receiving bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds in euros or convertible currency
  • Proof of delivery and receipt by the non-EU customer (delivery order or signed Proof of Delivery), supplemented where applicable by EUR.1 movement certificate or EUR.2 declaration for preferential origin claims

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting reform (2024 Finance Act, Law No. 2023-1322, amending Article 289 bis CGI) is phased as follows: from 1 September 2026 all taxable persons must accept structured electronic invoices (Factur-X, UBL, CII formats) and large enterprises and mid-caps (ETI) must issue them; from 1 September 2027 small and medium-sized enterprises (PME and micro-enterprises) must issue them. Invoices must be transmitted through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation — PPF). Transaction-level e-reporting data (B2B and B2C sales, and purchase data) is transmitted to DGFiP via API or file transmission within one day of dispatch. France does not operate real-time continuous fiscal clearance or digital stamping as in Italy's SDI; the mandate is pre-audit data reporting with transactional granularity, enabling automated VAT matching and margin analysis.

The DGFiP audits export exemption strictly, requiring the complete documentation bundle to be retained and produced on demand; under cross-border exchange mechanisms (FECC/DAC2 and administrative assistance treaties), the DGFiP verifies customs MRN data against VAT returns and bank remittance records. Where zero-rating is claimed without valid or recoverable proof, the administration retrospectively re-applies 20% VAT on each export invoice and clamps back any input VAT credited against those sales, adding late-payment interest (intérêts de retard at 0.20% per month, Article 1727 CGI). Inaccurate or incomplete documentation attracts a 10% assessment penalty under Article 1728 CGI; bad-faith omissions (mauvaise foi) attract 40%, and fraud or concealment (manœuvres frauduleuses) 80%. Criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI exposes the management to imprisonment of up to five years and fines up to EUR 500,000, doubled to EUR 1,000,000 where organized fraud is established. The clawback statute of limitation is the general six-year French tax period (Article L.169 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales), extended to ten years for deliberate concealment or where the activity was undisclosed, and the first-time offender leniency procedures are unavailable where export proceeds were not repatriated within the 180-day statutory term.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. France applies the EU Treaty foundation of free movement of capital; current and capital account transactions are unrestricted except for anti-money laundering, sanctions, or public-policy requirements. Profit remittances by non-resident shareholders are unconditional after corporate tax filing and audit obligations are satisfied; banks may require supporting Board resolutions and proof of beneficial ownership under Article L.561-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Article R.152-3 of the French Monetary and Financial Code, export receivables must be collected and repatriated to an authorized French bank within 30 days of the contractual due date (or the actual receipt date for goods). No mandatory conversion ratio into euros applies; foreign-currency balances may be held for commercial hedge needs, but retention must be documented for DGFiP audit purposes for 5 years. For intra-EU sales, the DEB (Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens) triggers reporting thresholds, while extra-EU receipts are reported via customs declarations and the DGFiP cash register.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, with the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR) for banking supervision and the Directorate General of Public Finances (DGFiP) for FX reporting and controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for French-registered EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Shine) as operational accounts for low-value transactions, but not for the statutory capital escrow account which requires a full-scope credit institution. Offshore EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business) lack French banking licence and cannot issue RIB/IBAN for regulatory capital deposits, nor confirm export LCs; they are therefore non-viable for the primary treasury account structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity injection from the non-resident parent or a shareholder loan, route via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code '3000' (Investment without real estate) as defined by ACPR Order 2016-06. The beneficiary field must include the SARL name and its RCS number, and the reference note must cite the APE code 2821Z. To comply with French anti-money laundering rules, the bank requires a signed copy of the contribution resolution, a notary attestation for the creation of the SARL, and the electronic transfer must be executed before EUR 10,000 to avoid additional threshold verification. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000 in annual related-party purchases and sales triggers the simplified French transfer pricing documentation; full Master File and Local File (Art. L.13 AA LPF) are mandatory where consolidated group turnover or gross assets exceed EUR 400 million; Country-by-Country filing applies at EUR 750 million consolidated group turnover; failure to respond to a documentation request triggers a flat EUR 15,000 penalty per document plus a 5% surcharge on adjusted amounts.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a formal ESR registry but applies a substantive abusive-arrangement doctrine (Art. L.64 LPF, GAAR) and the OECD beneficial-ownership and shell-company tests. A French SARL manufacturing and exporting agricultural drones must demonstrate real management, R&D, logistics and strategic substance in France; treaty and Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits are denied to letterbox structures. EU ATAD anti-hybrid and substance rules permit recharacterisation, 80% penalties and denial of double-tax-relief claims.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at the 80% fraud penalty plus 0.20% monthly late interest; forfeiture of tax credits; criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years (CGI Art. 1741); confiscation of drone production assets and export proceeds.

Director personal liability: The SARL gérant is personally and solidarily liable for the tax debt in fraud cases; the criminal court may impose a management ban, a travel ban and up to five years' imprisonment.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Flat EUR 15,000 penalty per missing document; 5% surcharge on reassessed cross-border profits; automatic 40% deliberate-omission penalty where concealment is established; disallowance of deductions for undocumented intra-group charges.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved inflated intra-group purchase prices or undocumented fees are civilly liable for the resulting corporate loss and may be personally fined for deficient documentation governance.
Shell company / economic substance (GAAR)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under the abuse-of-law procedure (Art. L.64 LPF) with the 80% penalty on the entire tax adjustment; denial of the 0% dividend WHT rate; retroactive taxation of disguised profits and penalties.

Director personal liability: The gérant faces solidary liability for the 80% penalty, a management ban (interdiction de gérer), disqualification and travel restrictions during criminal fiscal investigation.
AML / dual-use export control violation

Statutory sanction: Customs confiscation of equipment, fine up to twice the transaction value and imprisonment up to five years; money-laundering sanctions under Art. 324-1 Code pénal carry up to ten years and EUR 750,000 when committed habitually or by a professional.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability attaches directly to the gérant who signs export declarations; sanctions include imprisonment, travel bans, personal confiscation of assets and veil-piercing through misuse of corporate assets.
Failure to withhold source taxes on repatriation

Statutory sanction: The SARL is held as debtor of the uncollected withholding tax plus 0.20% monthly interest and 40% late-payment penalty; treaty relief is lost and the full statutory withholding becomes final.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised cash transfers despite the withholding gap are jointly liable for the unremitted tax and face criminal exposure for distraction of corporate assets during recovery proceedings.
