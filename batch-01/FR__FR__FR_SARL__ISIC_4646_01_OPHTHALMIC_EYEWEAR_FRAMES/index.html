Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies to a SARL. The capital must be divided into quota shares. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions are deposited into a blocked account with a bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations and released after registration. Contributions in kind must be described in the Articles; an auditor of contributions is generally required unless statutory waiver conditions are satisfied.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain premises fire-risk assessment, compliant emergency exits, extinguishers, alarms, electrical inspection, evacuation signage, and employee safety documentation. An establishment open to the public may require approval under the Établissement recevant du public (ERP) rules from the competent municipality or prefecture. Warehouse structural load and racking safety must be verified by competent professionals where relevant. Occupational-risk assessment, workplace accident prevention, training, and the mandatory document unique d'évaluation des risques professionnels are required. No general pre-opening industrial clearance applies unless the premises or activity falls within ERP, ICPE, building, or workplace-specific regimes.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies to a SARL. The capital must be divided into quota shares. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions are deposited into a blocked account with a bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations and released after registration. Contributions in kind must be described in the Articles; an auditor of contributions is generally required unless statutory waiver conditions are satisfied.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), operating through impots.gouv.fr and formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique/INPI).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SARL obtains its French tax identity as follows: (1) file constitution through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; (2) receive SIREN/SIRET numbers from INSEE; (3) register as an employer and tax payer with DGFIP; (4) apply for a French intra-Community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) via DGFIP; non-European non-resident operators generally must appoint an accredited tax representative in France for VAT purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned SARL obtains its French tax identity as follows: (1) file constitution through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; (2) receive SIREN/SIRET numbers from INSEE; (3) register as an employer and tax payer with DGFIP; (4) apply for a French intra-Community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) via DGFIP; non-European non-resident operators generally must appoint an accredited tax representative in France for VAT purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Limited statutory elections exist for small/family SARLs under CGI Article 239 bis AA to be taxed in the name of partners; groups may elect tax consolidation under CGI Article 223 A. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD apply, but no broad pass-through election is available for a regular SARL.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 (domestic 'franchise en base' threshold for sales of goods). Non-established foreign suppliers generally have no turnover-based exemption and must register from the first taxable transaction; EU distance-selling thresholds of EUR 10,000 apply under the VAT OSS scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies on proof that goods have physically left the customs territory of the EU under an electronic customs export declaration and that title/risk passed to a non-EU buyer. Offshore services may qualify when supplied to a non-EU taxable person or when directly and effectively connected with exported goods. The exporter must hold transport documents in its own name and provide evidence within the statutory retention period.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Mandatory e-invoicing for domestic B2B supplies is phased from 1 September 2026 for large/mid-sized enterprises and 1 September 2027 for smaller businesses, using certified Partner Dematerialisation Platforms (PDPs) transmitting structured invoices (Factur-X/UBL/CII) by API to DGFIP; B2C and cross-border flows require e-reporting to DGFIP. There is no physical stamping or continuous transaction clearance for VAT; Chorus Pro remains mandatory for public-sector invoices.

Export VAT zero-rating is rigorously audited by DGFIP and French customs. If conditions are not met, VAT becomes due together with late interest (0.20% per month) and penalties of up to 40% for bad faith and 80% for fraud; fraudulent export schemes may also incur criminal penalties, loss of tax-representative relief, and joint liability for collection.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area Member State; the euro is fully convertible and France operates no exchange-control rationing on current-account or capital-account transactions. Current-account payments, trade settlements, service payments, and profit remittances are unrestricted under EU capital and payment movement rules. A French SARL may remit profits to non-resident shareholders after ordinary corporate formalities; such distributions may be subject to French dividend withholding tax at 30% by default or a lower rate under an applicable tax treaty, but no exchange-control authorization is required. Capital-account convertibility is likewise free: non-resident ownership of a French SARL, inbound capital contributions, shareholder loans, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds are permitted. France only imposes statistical balance-of-payments reporting to Banque de France for cross-border direct investment and, for narrowly defined sensitive sectors, prior review by the French Treasury; wholesale distribution of ophthalmic eyewear frames is not a sensitive sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion requirements exist. France imposes no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts received in foreign currency. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or convertible foreign currency, reflected in the SARL's statutory accounts, and used for operational treasury purposes. Exporters only need to satisfy customs declaration rules, tax reporting, and, where a bank requests it, the underlying commercial documentation supporting the SWIFT credit.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France); the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) within the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique is the competent foreign-exchange and foreign-investment administration authority; ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) supervises credit institutions and electronic money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate, institution-specific, and subject to legal constraints. An EU/EEA licensed electronic money institution can provide a payment or e-money account usable for platform-pay receipts and low-value disbursements, relying on the PSD2 passport into France. A UK or non-EEA offshore EMI does not benefit from EU passporting after Brexit and cannot provide the SARL with a French-classified account or a transparent domestic payment environment without a licensed EU partner bank. Such offshore EMIs should not be used as the primary treasury, capital escrow, commercial-LC, or FX-hedging platform. A French ACPR-regulated credit institution remains mandatory for the blocked capital deposit needed at company formation and for documentary trade finance and lawful access to bank-provided FX hedging.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a French SARL, cash capital must be deposited before company registration into a blocked account opened in the company name with a bank, a notary, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or Banque de France. The compliant wire protocol requires the non-resident shareholder to send a SWIFT MT103 to the blocked account with the beneficiary stated as the SARL in formation, the account reference of the blocked account, and an unambiguous remittance narrative such as 'Souscription et libération du capital social' and the future RCS/SIREN reference. The bank will verify the sending account, the identity of the shareholder, the source of funds, and the legal basis of the contribution. The banking purpose should be presented as an inbound direct-investment capital contribution, not as a trade payment or ambiguous personal transfer. Upon receipt, the bank issues a deposit certificate confirming the blocked funds; following publication/registration of the SARL, the funds are released to the operational account.For European Economic Area suppliers, the normal clearing rail is the SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR, which requires no SWIFT messaging. For suppliers in China, the United States, or other third countries, the recommended rail is a SWIFT MT103 over SWIFT gpi with full beneficiary bank identifiers, commercial invoice references, and the goods description 'ophthalmic eyewear frames'. For large-value or new-supplier transactions, a confirmed irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit issued subject to UCP600 through the SARL's French bank is the appropriate security-clearing rail; documentary collection with documents against payment may also be used. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax rate. A reduced 15% rate may apply to qualifying small companies on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to ownership, turnover and paid-in-capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to domestic exemptions, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, and applicable tax treaties. A qualifying EU or EEA parent may obtain an exemption or refund subject to minimum ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, anti-abuse and beneficial-ownership conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the corporate income tax return is generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the following year. For other year-ends, filing is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. The tax balance is generally payable by the applicable statutory payment deadline, commonly the 15th day of the fourth month after year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The electronic VAT return and payment are generally due on the date assigned to the taxpayer's professional tax account, commonly during the month following the reporting period. Intra-Community acquisition, dispatch and recapitulative filings have separate statutory deadlines.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 group or company turnover or gross assets generally triggers full French transfer-pricing documentation obligations, including a Master File and Local File, subject to statutory scope rules. Entities below the threshold may still be required to substantiate arm's-length pricing during an audit and may be subject to simplified documentation or information requests.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation regime. Substance is nevertheless required under French transfer-pricing rules, the abuse-of-law doctrine, beneficial-ownership principles, controlled-foreign-company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules and general anti-abuse provisions. The SARL should maintain real French commercial activity, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, inventory and logistics controls, decision-making evidence, premises where commercially appropriate, bank accounts, customer contracts and contemporaneous board and management records. A company with nominal activity, pass-through invoicing, circular cash flows or no decision-making capacity may face denial of deductions, withholding-tax relief, treaty benefits or tax residence claims.
