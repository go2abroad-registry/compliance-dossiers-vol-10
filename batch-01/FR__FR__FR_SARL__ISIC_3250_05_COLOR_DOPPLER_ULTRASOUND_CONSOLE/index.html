Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A licensed professional domiciliation service may provide the legal registered office (siège social), but for an ISIC 3250 medical-imaging manufacturer this must be complemented by a physical operational establishment in France where manufacturing, storage, technical documentation, quality management and vigilance files are kept. Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Name reservation / lock period

French law does not provide a formal RCS corporate-name reservation right. Name availability is assessed at filing and no extension is available. In the event of refusal by the greffe, the applicant may (1) request a formal re-examination by the same greffe within 15 days of notification; (2) appear before the judge charged with registry supervision (juge commis à la surveillance du registre du commerce) or apply in référé proceedings before the President of the Tribunal de commerce to compel registration; (3) resolve any trademark conflict by filing opposition or cancellation proceedings before INPI or the competent civil court; and (4) appeal the judicial decision to the Paris Court of Appeal or the relevant appellate court. As a practical matter, a fresh availability report should be obtained immediately before final submission because the clearance result has no legal holding period.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), with registration in the Registre national des entreprises and, where applicable, the Registre du commerce et des sociétés maintained by the competent commercial court registry.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 and R.223-1 to R.223-36 governing sociétés à responsabilité limitée; Articles L.123-1 et seq. governing the Registre national des entreprises and commercial registration; French Civil Code Articles 1832 et seq. governing companies generally.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of creation of a legal entity submitted through the INPI Guichet unique, accompanied by the executed SARL articles of association, beneficial-owner declaration, and supporting corporate documents.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 110 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital is required; EUR 1 is legally sufficient. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation and the balance within five years of registration. Capital must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts depositary, which issues the certificate of deposit required for registration. Contributions in kind are subject to valuation rules and, unless legally waived, an apport auditor.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain building and occupancy approvals where required, including fire-safety review for premises open to the public and any applicable ERP requirements through the mairie and fire-safety authorities. Maintain fire detection, extinguishing, emergency-access, evacuation, electrical, and structural-load controls appropriate to the premises. Complete occupational-risk assessment, workplace safety documentation, employer declarations, medical surveillance, and training under the French Labour Code. There is no single general occupational operating clearance for this manufacturing activity; site-specific fire, building, environmental, and labor inspections remain applicable.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital is required; EUR 1 is legally sufficient. At least 20% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation and the balance within five years of registration. Capital must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts depositary, which issues the certificate of deposit required for registration. Contributions in kind are subject to valuation rules and, unless legally waived, an apport auditor.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for direct/VAT collection; Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) for customs and export control. Official portals: impots.gouv.fr and formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SARL obtains its statutory TIN and VAT ID through a single online registration at the Guichet Unique operated by INPI (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). Step 1: File the statuts and incorporate the SARL with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); the system automatically issues the SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit INSEE identifiers). Step 2: The SIE attached to the registered office automatically allocates the 13-character intra-community VAT number (FR[two-digit key][SIREN]) and notifies the taxpayer via the impots.gouv.fr professional account. Step 3: No French permanent establishment is required for registration; non-resident officers may register directly, but a fiscal representative is advisable for VAT refund claims. Step 4: Obtain a customs EORI number (FR + SIREN) from DGDDI through the online SIE registration process. Step 5: File the definitive business status (form 11450) and VAT declaration (form 11585); the VAT return period is monthly or quarterly.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – SARL defaults to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS). Standard IS rate 25%; reduced 15% applies to first €42,500 annual profit for SMEs satisfying Art. 219 CGI conditions. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – SARL defaults to Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS). Standard IS rate 25%; reduced 15% applies to first €42,500 annual profit for SMEs satisfying Art. 219 CGI conditions. Territoriality: profits of activities carried out through a French establishment are taxable in France; foreign branch profits are generally outside the French IS base under the French territoriality rule.

Available election forms

(i) SARL transparency election under Art. 239 bis AA CGI for small family SARLs meeting ≤5 natural-person shareholders and kinship tests; (ii) French fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Art. 223 A CGI where a French 95%-or-more parent heads a group and elects consolidated IS treatment; (iii) EU ATAD anti-hybrid measures under Arts. 212 bis and 209 B CGI disallow mismatched payments; no US-style check-the-box exists; (iv) M&A reorganization regimes available on merger/transfer; (v) no elective territorial option for foreign permanent establishments is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€85,000 annual turnover for supplies of goods (Art. 293 B CGI franchise en base de TVA; tolerance band up to €93,500). A medical device manufacturer/exporter registers voluntarily to recover input VAT; foreign non-established sellers conducting distance sales to French B2B customers must identify at the SIE and appoint a fiscal representative where required under Art. 286 CGI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to supplies of goods exported outside the EU (Art. 262-I-1° CGI) and to services directly linked to those exports (Art. 262-I-2° CGI). Conditions: (i) goods leave EU customs territory within 90 days of the time of supply; (ii) the export declaration is cleared by DGDDI with exit validation; (iii) transport documents evidence delivery to the non-EU destination; (iv) risk and title have passed to the foreign buyer; (v) settlement is received through regulated banking channels. Intra-EU dispatches to VAT-registered customers are exempt under Art. 262 ter I CGI subject to valid EU VAT ID and inclusion in the recapitulative statement (DEB).

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/SAD) lodged via DELTA-D, cleared by DGDDI, with MRN and actual exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or CMR/AWB evidencing removal from EU territory and delivery at the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 confirmation / Letter of Credit settlement evidence
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list used in customs clearance
  • Proof of physical exit within 90 days (cargo manifest, customs exit message, inspection report)
  • For intra-EU transactions: buyer's VAT number verified via VIES and copy of the DEB recapitulative statement

Mandatory e-invoicing under 2023 Finance Act (Art. 23): from 1 Sept 2026 large and mid-size companies must issue structured e-invoices (Factur-X, UBL, CII/D16B formats) through a certified PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) interconnected with the PPF (Portail Public de Facturation); SMEs/VSEs follow from 1 Sept 2027. E-reporting of B2C and cross-border data to the PPF is concurrently mandatory. DGFiP does not perform real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping in the Latin-American model; validation occurs at the PDP layer, PPF aggregates data, and a 10-year electronic archive is mandatory.

DGFiP and DGDDI audits reconcile export declarations, e-invoices, customs exit records, and bank settlement evidence; the standard statute of limitations is three years, extended to ten years for undisclosed offshore arrangements. Unsupported 0% claims are reassessed at the 20% standard VAT rate plus intérêts de retard under Art. 1727 CGI. Penalties under Art. 1728 et seq. CGI escalate: 40% for simple inaccuracy, 80% for bad faith (mauvaise foi), up to 100% for fraudulent maneuvers; criminal exposure under Art. 1741 CGI reaches €45,000 fine and three years imprisonment. Clawback is retrospective to the first unreported period and covers principal, interest, penalties, and may suspend VAT refund credits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, prudential requirements, tax rules, and transaction-reporting obligations. A French SARL may generally receive foreign investment, make cross-border shareholder loans, pay suppliers, remit dividends, repay qualifying debt, and transfer sale proceeds without a general foreign-exchange approval or mandatory central-bank authorization. Dividend and interest payments remain subject to corporate-law requirements, distributable-profit limitations, withholding-tax analysis, beneficial-owner verification, transfer-pricing rules, and bank documentation. Distributions require properly approved financial statements and must not breach legal-reserve, solvency, creditor-protection, or applicable financing-covenant restrictions. Cross-border payments involving sanctioned persons, embargoed jurisdictions, restricted dual-use goods, or unusual payment chains may be blocked or require regulatory authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement for exporters to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, convert a prescribed percentage into euros, or repatriate receipts within a fixed foreign-exchange deadline. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's discretion, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, AML, customs, and payment-service controls. Export transactions must be supported by commercial and customs records, and statistical or regulatory reporting may apply through customs, Banque de France, the bank, or intra-EU reporting systems depending on transaction type and reporting thresholds. Cash or equivalent instruments physically entering or leaving the European Union at EUR 10,000 or more must be declared to customs. Bank-specific evidence of the underlying export, invoice, customs declaration, transport documents, and beneficial recipient may be required before crediting or releasing funds.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange policy is principally governed by the European Union and applicable French rules administered by the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty, including the Directorate-General of the Treasury. Prudential and payment-services supervision is conducted by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, with market-conduct supervision by the Autorité des marchés financiers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment operations and low-to-medium as a substitute for a primary French banking relationship. A licensed European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro accounts, payment accounts, SEPA transfers, foreign-exchange services, and, where authorized, safeguarding arrangements. It does not necessarily provide a deposit account, statutory capital-deposit service, full trade-finance capability, commercial letters of credit, cash-management credit, or direct access to every clearing system. An offshore digital EMI outside the European Economic Area cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to the French SARL on a passporting basis without an applicable French or EEA authorization, branch, agent, or other lawful market-access structure. The SARL must verify the provider in the relevant national register, confirm safeguarding and insolvency treatment, assess IBAN and payer-name compatibility, and maintain a regulated French or EEA bank as the principal account for capital formation, tax, payroll, and critical suppliers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity through the French bank's capital-deposit account during incorporation or through the designated operational account after registration. Use a SEPA Credit Transfer for euro funding where the sending and receiving institutions participate in SEPA; use SWIFT cross-border payment messaging for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions. The payment reference should identify the French SARL, the subscriber, the instrument, and the purpose, such as share capital contribution, share premium, or shareholder loan. Provide the bank with the executed subscription documentation, shareholder or board resolution, source-of-funds evidence, sender-account ownership evidence, loan agreement where applicable, and expected settlement date. The bank should verify the sender against the beneficial owner or documented funder, screen sanctions and AML risks, validate the legal purpose, and reconcile the amount with the accounting entry. Capital deposited for formation must be evidenced by the bank's certificate of deposit and released according to French incorporation procedures. For non-euro funding, document the exchange rate, conversion date, fees, and amount credited in euros. Do not route capital through personal accounts, unrelated third parties, anonymous payment services, or unexplained offshore intermediaries.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro suppliers within the SEPA area, subject to bank limits and sanctions screening. Use SWIFT cross-border payment instructions for non-SEPA payments; MT103 remains a legacy message description, while banks are migrating to ISO 20022 payment messages such as pacs.008. For documentary trade, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued or advised through a regulated bank under UCP 600, with documentary examination under applicable ICC rules and, where appropriate, an eUCP framework. Match the credit or wire to the purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, customs data, insurance, Incoterms, beneficiary details, HS code, and end-use information. For imports into France, retain customs declarations, import VAT records, proof of delivery, and supplier-screening evidence. For exports, retain invoices, transport documents, customs export declarations, proof of shipment, and customer or end-user screening records. Confirm fees, correspondent-bank routes, value dates, payment purpose, and intermediary-bank data before release. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment operations and low-to-medium as a substitute for a primary French banking relationship. A licensed European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro accounts, payment accounts, SEPA transfers, foreign-exchange services, and, where authorized, safeguarding arrangements. It does not necessarily provide a deposit account, statutory capital-deposit service, full trade-finance capability, commercial letters of credit, cash-management credit, or direct access to every clearing system. An offshore digital EMI outside the European Economic Area cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to the French SARL on a passporting basis without an applicable French or EEA authorization, branch, agent, or other lawful market-access structure. The SARL must verify the provider in the relevant national register, confirm safeguarding and insolvency treatment, assess IBAN and payer-name compatibility, and maintain a regulated French or EEA bank as the principal account for capital formation, tax, payroll, and critical suppliers.
