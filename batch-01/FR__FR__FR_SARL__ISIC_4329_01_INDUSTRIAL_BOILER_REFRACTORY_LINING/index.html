Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du tribunal de commerce for the registered-office district, operating through the Guichet unique managed by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; post-registration records are maintained in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

Code de commerce (France), Livre II, Titre II, Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL), together with R.123-1 et seq.; Law n°2008-776 (LME) abolishing minimum capital; Law n°2019-486 (PACTE) and Decree n°2021-300 providing for the single electronic filing portal (Guichet unique).

Formation / constitutional filings

Constitutive statuts executed by all shareholders, together with the single legal-form registration dossier (télédossier de création d'entreprise, successor to the former M0/S0 forms), submitted via the Guichet unique.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum share capital for a French SARL; the capital is fixed freely in the statuts and must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be paid at least to one-fifth (20%) at constitution, unless the statuts require full release; any balance must be paid within five years. The cash is deposited in a blocked escrow account opened for the company in formation, and the bank's attestation de blocage is released only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Company offices, workshop and storage areas must satisfy standard fire-safety, structural load and workplace health rules and are monitored by the labor inspectorate. On client industrial boiler sites, the company must obtain and follow the site fire permit (permis feu), apply ATEX-zone restrictions, use a method statement for hot refractory work, implement working-at-height protection, provide PPE, and complete mandatory occupational health checks and asbestos-awareness training before handling old insulation.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum share capital for a French SARL; the capital is fixed freely in the statuts and must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be paid at least to one-fifth (20%) at constitution, unless the statuts require full release; any balance must be paid within five years. The cash is deposited in a blocked escrow account opened for the company in formation, and the bank's attestation de blocage is released only upon RCS registration.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable at company level; no separate industrial operating licence is required for a mobile refractory-masonry and thermal-insulation contracting SARL in France. Worksite and environmental compliance is supervised by the DREETS/DREAL and by the relevant local unit when the company intervenes on an ICPE-classified client facility.

Category: Not applicable; the RCS registration is the sole commercial authorization. The company may voluntarily obtain Qualibat professional certification for refractory and thermal-insulation works, and must carry mandatory professional civil liability plus décennale construction insurance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; primary portals: impots.gouv.fr (fiscal identity, VAT returns, company file) and formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique operated with INPI for SIREN/SIRET allocation and automatic VAT registration). Customs export competences are exercised by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) through the DELTA/LEX electronic clearance platform.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned French SARL: (1) File all constitutive formalities through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) attaching the articles of association, the gérant's identity document, proof of registered office and the anti-money-laundering declarations; INPI/INSEE delivers the SIREN (9 digits), SIRET (14 digits), the NAF/APE activity code and the Kbis extract. (2) The Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) automatically allocates the intra-Community VAT identification number (format FR + 2 check digits + SIREN) after registration is completed; this is evidenced by the Kbis extract and the avis de situation SIRENE. (3) A foreign entity with no French permanent establishment must instead file Form Cerfa No. 13826*04 (demande d'attribution d'un numéro de TVA intracommunautaire pour un assujetti non établi) with the DGFiP non-resident tax unit, and, if not established in the EU, must appoint a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) jointly and severally liable for VAT under Article 289 A CGI. (4) The SARL must additionally obtain an EORI number (FR + SIREN) from the DGDDI through the electronic EORI/DELTA service before lodging any export declaration. All post-registration changes (manager, registered office, activity, cessation) must be declared through the Guichet Unique within 30 days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Available mechanisms: (1) Article 239 bis AA CGI: a SARL whose shareholders are exclusively physical persons may opt irrevocably within five years of creation for corporate-tax transparency (pass-through taxation of partners at impôt sur le revenu), with the option being filed via the annual income tax return; otherwise the SARL is definitively opaque and subject to corporation tax (IS). (2) Article 206-3 CGI: a default pass-through partnership (SNC, SCI) may elect compulsory entity-level IS. (3) Article 256 C CGI and article 22 decies of the annex: election to form a TVA group (Groupe TVA) among closely connected established French entities, with the représentant membre filing a single VAT return. (4) Hybrid mismatch treatment: ATAD 2 is implemented by Articles 212 bis and 212 sexies CGI; there is no domestic unilateral election, so the foreign classification of a hybrid entity determines the application of the neutralisation rules. (5) Customs elections relevant to the refractory activity include outward processing (perfectionnement passif) and drawback regimes enabling duty/VAT suspension or remission for exported processed goods.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for non-established foreign suppliers: any taxable supply deemed to take place in France triggers registration (or OSS declaration) from the first euro of turnover, and the franchise en base de TVA of Article 293 B CGI is unavailable to non-established entities. Intra-EU distance sales of goods to private consumers and B2C electronically supplied services may be remitted through the OSS system without separate French VAT registration up to a pan-European EUR 10,000 annual turnover ceiling (Article 296-0 ter CGI). French-established small operators enjoy the franchise en base de TVA unified at EUR 85,000 annual turnover (goods and services) effective 1 January 2025; exceeding this threshold makes VAT registration compulsory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports: tangible movable goods (refractory bricks, ceramic modules, castable/pre-cast refractory shapes) sold and definitively dispatched or carried outside EU customs territory by or on behalf of the supplier are exempt at 0% under Article 262-I-1° CGI, provided physical exit is proven. Intra-EU supplies to a VAT-identified customer are exempt under Article 262 ter CGI with a valid VIES number, transport evidence and submission of the EC sales list (DES). Services nuance for refractory lining: (a) on-site masonry and relining performed on kilns and boilers physically attached to land or buildings in France are supplies of services connected with immovable property taxable in France at 20% under Article 259 A CGI, even for a foreign principal; no 0% zero-rating is available, but the foreign customer may recover French input VAT through the non-established taxable person refund procedure (Article 271 CGI / 8th Directive portal). (b) Services performed on movable refractory equipment (removable boiler sections, ladles, torpedo cars, detached modules) for a foreign customer whose goods are exported may qualify for the exemption under Article 262-II-1° and -2° CGI if the customs declaration, transport documents and service contract evidence the direct link with the export. (c) Outward processing (perfectionnement passif) permits duty/TVA-suspended treatment for foreign-owned refractory goods processed in France and re-exported to the principal; the service invoice can then benefit from the 0% export exemption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration Unique d'Exportation / DAU-EAD) with the MRN (Movement Reference Number) issued via DELTA/LEX and the customs office of exit certificate proving departure from EU territory.
  • Bill of Lading (maritime), CMR road consignment note (Genève Convention), Air Waybill, multimodal bill, or GPS/terminal exit records as transport evidence evidencing the goods left France and the EU.
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List with precise HS classification (refractory articles of headings 6902/6903), Incoterms, EUR currency, and reference to the underlying order and the SARL export contract.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT or attestation de change proving export proceeds credited in EUR to the French bank account of the SARL.
  • For intra-EU supplies: buyer's VIES VAT validation output, EC Sales List (état récapitulatif clients / DES) filed on impots.gouv.fr, transport ticket showing delivery in another Member State, and the customer's purchase order.

Mandatory B2B domestic e-invoicing (facturation électronique) applies in waves: all French-established businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices from 1 September 2026; large enterprises and ETIs must issue via a certified PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) or the public PPF (Portail Public de Facturation) of DGFiP from 1 September 2026; SMEs and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027. Permitted formats are FACTUR-X (hybrid), UBL 2.1 and CII, and invoices must contain the mandatory mentions of Article 242 nonies A CGI. Cross-border supplies do not require e-invoicing but must be reported through the e-reporting channel (transaction and payment data) to DGFiP on the same timeline. The architecture uses PDP-to-PDP APIs and the DGFiP central directory, creating near-real-time transmission (within one working day of issuance), but France does not operate per-transaction fiscal stamping by the tax authority; retail cash registers remain subject to the NF 525 anti-fraud software certification (Law No. 2018-898).

DGFiP export audits (vérification de comptabilité) cross-match MRN/DAU data from the customs DELTA system with bank inflows, transport tickets, downstream customer reconstruction and the accounting trail. Standard VAT reassessment is 3 years (Livre des procédures fiscales Article L. 169), extended to 10 years in case of concealed activity or fraud, and to 6 years for cross-border operations. A false or fictitious export zero-rating triggers: (1) retroactive VAT clawback reinvoiced at 20% on the full taxable amount; (2) the 80% penalty of Article 1729 CGI for manœuvres frauduleuses or opposition à contrôle; (3) late-payment interest at 0.20% per month under Article 1727 CGI; (4) criminal VAT-fraud prosecution under Article 1741 CGI (up to 5 years imprisonment, EUR 500,000 fine, confiscation, publication and exclusion from public procurement); (5) customs penalties under Code des douanes Articles 413-1 to 414 (fines of one to two times the embezzled sums plus confiscation of goods and vehicle where applicable); and (6) joint and several liability of the fiscal representative (Article 289 A CGI), personal liability of the gérant for administrative penalties, and disqualification risks where organised fraud (fraude carrousel/fly-by-night) is established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully liberalised exchange control regime as a Eurozone member. Current account convertibility is unrestricted under EU Treaty articles on free movement of capital (Article 64 TFEU) and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier). Capital account transactions are also free, subject only to statistical reporting thresholds, EU financial sanctions, and anti-money laundering obligations. Profit remittances by a SARL to non-resident shareholders are permitted without prior ministerial authorisation; a withholding tax may apply unless an EU tax treaty or the Parent-Subsidiary Directive reduces or eliminates it. No lingering exchange control approval is required for EUR-denominated transfers, and foreign currency accounts may be held domestically with authorised banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export proceeds surrender or forced conversion into EUR exists in France or the Eurozone. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency revenues in authorised foreign currency accounts. However, for balance-of-payments statistics purposes, French resident companies must report each cross-border transaction with a non-resident counterparty when the individual transaction amount exceeds €50,000, and a monthly aggregated declaration is required when the cumulative amount exceeds €750,000, filed with the Banque de France through calibrated reporting channels (reporting code 'SURRI' or the Banque de France 'Balance des Paiements' portal). Invoicing in EUR is standard but not compulsory.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (in coordination with the European Central Bank for Eurozone monetary policy and with the French Treasury for cross-border capital reporting under the French Monetary and Financial Code)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for full operational primary banking. A French-regulated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) authorised by the ACPR (e.g., a licensed fintech with an établissement de monnaie électronique status) can legally hold client funds and execute SEPA and SWIFT transfers, but it generally cannot provide credit, trade finance, or foreign exchange advances without a separate credit institution licence. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK or Lithuania-authorised EMIs) may operate temporarily in France under the EU passport if properly registered and supervised, but they are often rejected by procurement clients and tax authorities for large refundable deposits or escrow arrangements. For an industrial boiler refractory contracting SARL, an EMI is viable only for low-value progress collections and FX card payments; core clearing and escrow must sit with a tier-1 credit institution.

Root cause: Frequent large cash advances or abortive intragroup transfers during the first 12 months of the account

Defense evidence: Executed commercial contracts with milestone descriptions; purchase orders and boiler/refractory materials invoices; proof of customs clearance and VAT treatment; monthly bank narrative and treasury forecast; notice of future transactions exceeding €10,000; sworn statement that no cash payments above €1,000 are made (strict French legal cap).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident shareholders or intra-group treasury must be routed via a bank-compliant SWIFT wire with a precise purpose code. For France, the wire must designate either 'Business or commercial purpose (capital contribution)' or a specific BOP code such as 'FXS' or 'Capital Share of Investment' depending on the subscribing bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for full operational primary banking. A French-regulated Electronic Money Institution (EMI) authorised by the ACPR (e.g., a licensed fintech with an établissement de monnaie électronique status) can legally hold client funds and execute SEPA and SWIFT transfers, but it generally cannot provide credit, trade finance, or foreign exchange advances without a separate credit institution licence. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK or Lithuania-authorised EMIs) may operate temporarily in France under the EU passport if properly registered and supervised, but they are often rejected by procurement clients and tax authorities for large refundable deposits or escrow arrangements.
