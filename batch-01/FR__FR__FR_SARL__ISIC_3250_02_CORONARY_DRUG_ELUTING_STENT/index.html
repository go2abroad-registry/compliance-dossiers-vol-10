Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for a SARL; the articles may fix capital at €1. If cash capital is established above €1, at least 20% of cash contributions (apports en numéraire) must be paid up before registration, and the balance must be paid within five years. Cash contributions must be placed in escrow with a French bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released only after the company obtains its Kbis extract. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up at formation and, if material, verified by a statutory contributions auditor (commissaire aux apports).

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification from the competent local commission for non-residential premises, structural load verification by an agreed technical controller (e.g. APAVE, SOCOTEC, DEKRA) for installed lasers and heavy cleanroom equipment, and workplace health clearance from the occupational health service to cover lasers, solvent hazards, ergonomic risks and cleanroom-class ISO 8 operation; evacuation plans, gas suppression systems and inspection reports must be recorded before industrial production begins.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – impots.gouv.fr (corporate income tax, VAT and beneficial-ownership reporting); online company registration via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique).

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, formation through the Guichet Unique automatically delivers the SIREN/SIRET identifiers; the VAT number (n° de TVA intracommunautaire) is subsequently assigned by the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). A foreign investor establishing a SARL uses the same portal and may obtain the TIN via DGFiP's non-resident taxpayer unit; the standard tax status document is the 'avis de situation' or 'extrait DGFiP'.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – the SARL is automatically taxed at corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) regardless of shareholder tax residence; pass-through eligibility is not inherent to SARLs, and the territorial doctrine limits the French tax base to profits derived from French business operations or attributable to a French permanent establishment. — For a French SARL, formation through the Guichet Unique automatically delivers the SIREN/SIRET identifiers; the VAT number (n° de TVA intracommunautaire) is subsequently assigned by the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the SARL is automatically taxed at corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) regardless of shareholder tax residence; pass-through eligibility is not inherent to SARLs, and the territorial doctrine limits the French tax base to profits derived from French business operations or attributable to a French permanent establishment.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. SARL may elect for group taxation under 'intégration fiscale' (CGI art. 223A) when it holds at least 95% of the voting rights in one or more French subsidiaries; partnerships may elect for IS; taxpayers must file ATAD hybrid-mismatch documentation with DGFiP; a VAT franchise can be waived for small-turnover businesses to validate input-VAT recovery.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€85,000 annual net turnover for the supply of goods and €37,500 for services below which the 'franchise en base' exemption may apply; however a commercial manufacturer engaged in ongoing taxable activity in France must register for TVA regardless of turnover. Non-established electronic sellers trigger distance-selling registration thresholds at €35,000 per calendar year (subject to temporary derogations).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exportation of goods to a destination outside the EU is taxable at 0% provided the seller proves physical exit of the goods from the EU within 90 days of supply and retains transport agreements, customs declarations, and bills of lading. Services connected with exported goods, or rendered to non-resident business customers outside the EU, may also be zero-rated under CGI arts. 262-1-I, 259, 259-1 and 259B.

  • Customs export declaration (Document Administratif Unique / DAU) cleared at the EU exit point
  • Bill of Lading or CMR waybill or Air Waybill evidencing delivery outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and purchase order
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement proving receipt of export proceeds from the foreign customer

Mandatory structured e-invoicing (Factur-X or XML) for all VAT-registered businesses and e-reporting to DGFiP will become obligatory from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-cap entities and from 1 September 2027 for SMEs. Invoices must be transmitted through the public invoice portal (PPF) or an accredited private platform via secure API; no direct digital stamping is currently required, but anti-fraud real-time clearance architecture is under phased implementation.

DGFiP may require export substantiation within a 30-day reply period; absence or falsity of export proof triggers the 80% bad-faith penalty (CGI art. 1729), monetary interest (art. 1727), and a retrospective assessment window extending to three years, or six years in cases of fraud. Criminal enforcement can be pursued against the managing directors personally, with confiscation of goods and court-ordered closures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member with full capital and current account convertibility under EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63-66 TFEU). No exchange controls exist for trade, investment, or profit remittance. However, all cross-border transactions exceeding €15,000 must be reported to the BDF for statistical and balance-of-payments purposes. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate documentation (e.g., audited financials, tax clearance) and anti-money laundering (AML) checks by the remitting bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists in France. Exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts. However, all export receipts must be declared to the BDF via the 'Réglementation des changes' reporting system within 30 days of receipt if the transaction exceeds €15,000. There is no statutory conversion ratio; companies may hold EUR or foreign currency freely.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BDF) as the national central bank and the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy; the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) oversees exchange control policy and balance-of-payments reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. France authorizes Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) regime. Cross-border EMIs (e.g., from other EU states) can operate via passporting, but for a high-risk medical device manufacturer, banks may impose additional due diligence. EMIs are viable for operational accounts but not for capital escrow or complex trade finance (LCs). Legal constraints include mandatory client asset safeguarding and transaction limits.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard corporate income tax rate under Article 219 CGI; reduced 15% rate applies to eligible SMEs on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% (Article 119 bis CGI), applied to gross dividends paid to legal persons resident outside France; social levies may also apply depending on the recipient's residence and treaty coverage.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Late filing attracts a 10% surcharge on the assessed corporate tax, increased to 40% if filed after a formal notice and to 80% in abusive or fraudulent conduct, plus monthly late-payment interest of 0.20%. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million in annual consolidated turnover or total balance-sheet assets; companies meeting this threshold must prepare a French transfer-pricing file equivalent to Master File and Local File and file Form 2257-SD related-party declaration with the corporate tax return.

Economic substance scope: France applies the anti-abuse and economic-substance doctrine through Article 57 CGI and Article L.64 of the Book of Tax Procedures. A medtech SARL must have real managerial premises, qualified personnel, clinical and regulatory activity, and business assets in France; otherwise, intra-group contracts to a low-tax jurisdiction can be disregarded, income reallocated, and abusive arrangements sanctioned at 80%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI: imprisonment up to five years and fine up to EUR 500,000; administrative re-assessment with a 40% penalty for gross negligence or 80% for abuse of law; confiscation of sums involved; public naming in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for tax debts, subject to travel bans during investigation, commercial court prohibition from management up to 15 years, and criminal conviction with confiscation. In insolvency, the court may impose 'comblement de passif' against de facto directors.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Article 57 CGI permits reallocation of turnover to France; penalties of 40% for gross negligence and 80% for sham or abusive arrangements; hidden distributions may be re-characterized and subjected to dividend withholding and social levies.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly arrange the transfer are personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes plus penalties; criminal liability may arise for fraud or money laundering; the court can issue a prohibition to manage and a departure or administrative travel ban.
Shell Company / Abusive Entity Structure

Statutory sanction: Disregard under Article L.64 LPF; annulment of tax effects and an 80% abusive penalty; denial of CIR and patent box relief; re-attribution of income to the true economic owner. If the company appears fictitious, criminal 'gérant de fait' sanctions apply.

Director personal liability: Administrators, nominal directors, and de facto managers face personal tax assessment, joint liability with the company, asset seizure, and criminal imprisonment up to seven years in cases involving organized shell-company money laundering.
Money Laundering / Corruption in Medical Device Commerce

Statutory sanction: Money laundering offences under Articles 324-1 to 324-6 of the French Penal Code: up to 10 years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine, confiscation of the SARL's assets, and monetary penalty up to twice the laundered value; customs and health authority licence revocation for Class III medical devices.

Director personal liability: Directors bear criminal liability for laundering offences, receive individual fines, travel bans, restrictive judicial controls, and, if the SARL is used as an instrument, may be stripped of management rights and required to compensate the State and injured parties from personal assets.
