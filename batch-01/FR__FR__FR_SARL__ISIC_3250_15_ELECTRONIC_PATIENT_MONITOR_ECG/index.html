Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – French public finance authority; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; business entity registration through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, the statutory representative files the incorporation dossier with the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). The CFE/Guichet Unique transmits the data to INSEE and DGFiP; the entity receives a SIREN (9-digit) and, after starting taxable activity, a French intra-Community VAT number (FR + 11 characters). Non-French entities with no establishment in France that owe French VAT must apply directly to the competent SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) using form 1547-SD, and non-EU applicants are generally required to appoint a tax representative in France. EU-established suppliers may instead use OSS/IOSS where their distance sales stay below prescribed thresholds. A foreign parent owning shares in the SARL does not trigger a separate French tax ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election in France. A small/medium 'family' SARL meeting the criteria of CGI Art. 239 bis AA may elect to be taxed as a partnership under the personal income tax regime (IR) instead of the default corporation tax (IS). A French-headquartered SARL group may elect 100% tax consolidation ('intégration fiscale') under CGI Art. 223 A et seq. Other options include the 'régime mère-fille' participation exemption and ATAD hybrid-mismatch neutralizing adjustments (CGI Art. 212 bis).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for foreign or French taxable persons making taxable supplies in France. A French-established SME may use the franchise en base exemption only if annual turnover does not exceed EUR 85,000 for sales of goods (EUR 34,400 for services; 2024/2025 limits), but a SARL engaged in manufacturing and selling patient monitors normally must obtain a VAT number from inception. Non-established non-EU sellers must register from the first French sale unless using the IOSS/OSS regimes for distance sales; the EU OSS non-EU scheme threshold applies where relevant.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods to destinations outside the EU are exempt with credit for input VAT under CGI Art. 262 I where legal title passes to the non-EU customer and the goods are physically shipped to a non-EU country. Intra-EU supplies are also eligible for zero-rating if the customer supplies a valid VAT ID and the goods are transported to another Member State. Services directly relating to exported goods (e.g., assembly, calibration, logistics) may be zero-rated under CGI Art. 262 II provided the exporter possesses the required evidential documents. The zero-rating must be justified by documentary proof and reflected on the French EC Sales List.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / DAU)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or IRC evidencing receipt of export proceeds)
  • Commercial invoice with explicit 'TVA non applicable – exportation' or 'exonération de TVA' mention
  • Packing list and proof of shipment to non-EU destination

France is progressively mandating structured electronic invoicing ('facturation électronique') for B2B and B2C transaction reporting: the obligation starts on 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for SMEs/VSEs. Invoices must be transmitted via a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public 'Portail Public de Facturation' in EN 16931 syntax, with e-invoice data sent to DGFiP and Customs for e-reporting. There is currently no real-time physical-fiscal stamping regime (unlike the Italian 'stampa fiscale'); clearance is achieved through structured transmission and post-transaction audit.

If a French VAT inspector finds that export proof is missing, false, or the goods are returned to France or another EU Member State, the zero-rating is retroactively reversed: the supplier must pay the 20% VAT and is assessed late-payment interest (0.2% per month). Penalties under CGI Art. 1728 and 1729 range from 40% (deliberate failure to collect/pay VAT) to 80% (fraudulent or abusive 'abus de droit'); customs fraud under the Code des douanes can expose the exporter to a fine of up to three times the value of the goods and, in serious cases, criminal prosecution.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible, freely floating, and classified as a reserve currency. France operates no exchange-control authorisation system, no administrative convertibility gate, and no legal separation between current-account and capital-account approvals; capital movements are liberalised under Article 63 TFEU and codified in the Code monétaire et financier (Article L.151-1: 'les relations financières avec l'étranger sont libres'). Profit remittance is burdened only by ordinary corporate-law preconditions and tax, not by FX licensing: a French SARL may distribute dividends only out of legally distributable profits verified against the statutory accounts, provided the legal reserve (10% of share capital until it reaches 10% of capital) is funded and net assets remain above share capital. Tax-wise, dividends to an EU/EEA corporate shareholder holding at least 5% for two years attract 0% French withholding under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Articles 119 ter and 145 CGI); treaty-reduced rates (typically 5-15%) apply to third-country corporate shareholders, or local 30% flat withholding if no treaty benefit is claimed. The practical constraints are: (i) all cross-border wire transfers must be routed through an ACPR-licensed credit institution under the French intermediation rule; and (ii) the SARL's bank must transmit each cross-border payment to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics; no treasury licence or prior central-bank approval is required for the medical-electronics sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender regime exists in France. There is no mandatory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to credit export proceeds to a designated national account. The SARL may invoice in EUR, USD, or any other currency, hold foreign currency indefinitely in a foreign-currency account with a French authorised intermediary or a foreign bank, and determine freely the commercial moment of conversion. The only legal duties are statistical: (1) credit institutions report cross-border payments with non-residents to the Banque de France as part of balance-of-payments statistics whenever transactions meet the applicable per-transfer itemisation thresholds; (2) where the SARL maintains foreign bank accounts or executes flows outside an authorised intermediary, it must file NORIF (notice of financial relations with the outside world) returns on a monthly/quarterly basis for aggregate annual cross-border flows exceeding the regulatory de minimis, commonly EUR 750,000 as amended, itemising flows above EUR 12,500; and (3) separate customs goods declarations (Intrastat/EX or the common export declaration) continue in parallel. Non-compliance exposes the SARL to administrative fines under Title II of Book III of the Code monétaire et financier, but never blocks convertibility or compels the sale of export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), operating within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and Governing Council, with monetary sovereignty over the euro delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB). The Board's foreign-account and balance-of-payments statistical regime (NORIF) is administered by the Banque de France as agent of the Direction Générale du Trésor at the Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances; supervision of credit institutions is exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), a twin-peak authority attached to the Banque de France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Low for core treasury and Medium for ancillary euro collections. An EU-passported fintech electronic-money institution (under French law, 'établissement de monnaie électronique', or an EU/EEA EMI passporting into France) may lawfully hold the SARL's e-money, execute SEPA credit transfers, process card receipts, and offer retail-grade FX conversion. It is, however, not a credit institution: it cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, cannot discount or forfait LC documents, cannot grant overdrafts, banker's guarantees, or corporate loans, and generally lacks SWIFT MT103 correspondent relationships and NORIF balance-of-payments reporting infrastructure. For an ISIC 3250 exporter settling through Swift_LC and TT_Wire rails, the EMI can serve only as an auxiliary collection and disbursement channel. A non-EU offshore EMI (e.g., UK, Singapore, or UAE-licensed) has no passport into France and may not provide payment services to French residents, hold the SARL's client funds, or open quasi-IBANs representing French relationships without ACPR registration; using such providers for main trade settlement creates an AML/transborder-banking breach and exposes the gérant to sanctions under Article L.561-4 Code monétaire et financier.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Formative capital: non-resident shareholders subscribe the SARL's share capital by wire-transferring the agreed amount to a French blocked capital-deposit account (compte bloqué) opened in the SARL's name before registration; the bank issues an attestation of the capital deposit, the gérant signs the declaration of no cash in-kind fraud, and the funds are released only after immatriculation at the Greffe. Wire protocol: SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's own account at an authorised bank, quoting the beneficiary IBAN (FR76-BIC format), the SARL's SIREN/EORI, and the ISO 20022 purpose code 'FIPE' (foreign direct investment in equity) or the bank's internal Banque de France balance-of-payments code for 'apports en capital / souscription d'actions dans une société française'; the remitter name must match the subscriber name exactly — third-party wires are rejected. Treasury injections after formation (shareholder loans or current-account advances): SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'FIDL' or 'LOAN' backed by a written convention de compte courant d'associé, restated in the annual accounts; inflows exceeding the balance-of-payments de minimis thresholds are reported to the Banque de France either by the receiving credit institution or directly by the SARL under NORIF. A pre-close perimeter check against the French foreign-investment screening decree (Décret n°2023-1293 and Article R.151-3 Code monétaire et financier) must confirm that electronic patient-monitor production is not on the sensitive-activity list; a negative legal memorandum should be retained evidencing that no prior ministerial authorisation is required.Primary clear rails: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) for all euro-denominated payments to EU/EEA suppliers — mandatory for intra-EEA euro flows, settled within one TARGET2 business day with no correspondent charges; (2) SWIFT MT103 via the correspondent network (CHIPS/Fedwire for USD, CHAPS for GBP) for non-euro invoices; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700, subject to UCP 600, confirmed where appropriate by a prime bank in the beneficiary country, with settlement via payment against MT754/MT756 and document examination; this is the recommended rail for cross-border procurement of calibrated sensors, PCBs, and display sub-assemblies for the ECG monitor line; (4) Documentary collections (D/P, D/A) and standby LCs (URDG 758 for guarantees) as intermediate instruments for repeat counterparties. All outgoing payments must be matched to underlying contracts, invoices, and customs declarations to withstand bank AML transaction-monitoring; the SARL should maintain an annual rolling trade-finance umbrella limit with its Tier-1 bank, with per-beneficiary KYC updates on any change in the foreign supplier register.Outright foreign-exchange forward contracts (achat/vente à terme de devises) on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CNH with a licensed French credit institution, covered by a treasury policy and board delegation of authorityFX swaps (swaps de change) for short-term rolling of USD/EUR payable and receivable positions, documented under the bank's standard treasury master agreementCross-currency interest-rate swaps for long-term financing of the French production facility or equipment imports, permitted subject to EMIR and cleared or margined as applicableNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) against non-convertible or restricted currencies (e.g., CNH or emerging-market currencies as required by distributor invoicing), documented under a 1992/2002 ISDA Master Agreement and subject to EMIR reporting, variation margining, and initial-margin rules if the counterparty group exceeds the applicable average-aggregate-notional thresholdVanilla FX options (options de change) for sourced-basis hedging of large tender or distribution contracts, subject to MiFID II professional-client suitability assessment and, where applicable, uncleared-margin regulation (UMR) initial-margin obligations
