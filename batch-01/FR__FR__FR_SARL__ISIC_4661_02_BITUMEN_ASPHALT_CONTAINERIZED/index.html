Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry) within the judicial jurisdiction of the registered office, filing channeled through the INPI Registre National des Formalities (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce) — Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (common company provisions), L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL governance), and R.123-1 to R.123-40 (Register of Commerce and Companies / RCS registration procedure).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) submitted through the national French corporate formalities online platform, culminating in RCS registration and issuance of the Extrait Kbis.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital requirement (freely fixed, minimum EUR 1 under Article L.223-2 C.com.). Cash contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation where capital is below EUR 10,000, and at least 50% paid up where capital equals or exceeds EUR 10,000, with the balance payable within five years. All cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) held with a commercial bank or with a notary/Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and released only upon RCS registration. A subscribed capital of EUR 5,000 to EUR 10,000 is strongly recommended for credible bankability in the bitumen wholesale and containerized logistics trade.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate and technical inspection delivered by the Commission Consultative Départementale de Sécurité et d'Accessibilité (CCDSA) and the SDIS (Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours) covering evacuation plans, fire detection/extinction systems, and accessibility; structural load inspection certificate issued by an approved control body (Bureau de contrôle, e.g., SOCOTEC) attesting the floor/racking capacity for container stacking; occupational clearance under the French Labor Code including the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUER), conformity of electrical installations, first-aid organization, and registration of the workplace with the Inspection du Travail.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital requirement (freely fixed, minimum EUR 1 under Article L.223-2 C.com.). Cash contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation where capital is below EUR 10,000, and at least 50% paid up where capital equals or exceeds EUR 10,000, with the balance payable within five years. All cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) held with a commercial bank or with a notary/Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and released only upon RCS registration. A subscribed capital of EUR 5,000 to EUR 10,000 is strongly recommended for credible bankability in the bitumen wholesale and containerized logistics trade.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministry of Economy and Finance; principal taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, incorporation is registered with the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) which transmits to the CFE (Centre de Formalités des Entreprises) and INSEE. INSEE then issues a SIREN (9-digit statutory TIN) and SIRET (14-digit establishment number), while DGFiP issues the intra-community VAT number (FR + 11 digits) upon submission of form Cerfa 07142-2 or 07024-4. A foreign entity not established in France and making taxable French supplies must apply to the SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) using form Cerfa 15579*01, and, if established outside the EU, must appoint a deemed VAT representative who is jointly and severally liable for French VAT.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SARL, incorporation is registered with the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) which transmits to the CFE (Centre de Formalités des Entreprises) and INSEE. INSEE then issues a SIREN (9-digit statutory TIN) and SIRET (14-digit establishment number), while DGFiP issues the intra-community VAT number (FR + 11 digits) upon submission of form Cerfa 07142-2 or 07024-4. A foreign entity not established in France and making taxable French supplies must apply to the SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) using form Cerfa 15579*01, and, if established outside the EU, must appoint a deemed VAT representative who is jointly and severally liable for French VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. A SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) but may elect, under article 239bis CGI, to be taxed in the hands of shareholders at the income tax level if it qualifies as a small family SARL and the election is for five years. Other statutory elections include the option for the 'régime fiscal des sociétés de personnes' in limited cases and the EU anti-hybrid declaration under Article 212bis CGI for hybrid mismatch rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold applies; every taxable person making taxable supplies in France must register from the first euro. Micro-businesses may rely on the 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption thresholds of 85,000 EUR for goods sales and 37,500 EUR for services, above which registration becomes compulsory. Non-established foreign taxable persons enjoy no turnover threshold and are registrable from the first euro.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of goods physically transported from France to a destination outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under article 262(I) CGI if the exporter retains conclusive evidence of export, including a valid customs export declaration (ELD/EXA), transport contracts, and proof of removal from the EU within the statutory period. Services can be zero-rated where they are directly linked to exported goods or rendered for a customer established outside the EU under articles 259 and 262(II) CGI, provided the foreign status of the recipient and the extraterritorial performance are substantiated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en douane EXA/ELD with MRN) recorded in the exporter's DEB/DPD registers
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing removal of the goods from the EU territory
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Purchase order / sales contract indicating Incoterms and transport destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT proof of payment / repatriation to France

France is implementing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and e-reporting under the 2023 Finance Act. E-invoicing is phased in from 1 September 2026 for large entities and 1 September 2027 for SMEs/VSEs through accredited dematerialisation platforms (PDPs) or the public portal Portail Public de Facturation. Taxpayers must transmit structured invoices and B2B transaction data to DGFiP via the PDP/PPF pipelines; no pre-clearance digital stamping is imposed, but VAT returns and recapitulative statements must be filed electronically on impots.gouv.fr.

DGFiP and the customs administration (DGA) may verify export evidence and reassess the VAT exemption if documentation is missing or false. Reclaims are accompanied by interest under article 1727 CGI (0.2% per month) and penalties under article 1729 CGI: 40% for deliberate misconduct and 80% for abuse of rights or fraud-related behaviour. In addition, customs fraud rules may apply with fines under the Code des douanes, and excessive refunds obtained through false export declarations are clawed back within the three-year standard assessment period (longer in fraud cases), with possible criminal exposure for the corporate officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full EU capital and current-account liberalization jurisdiction under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The euro and all Eurozone-linked currencies are freely convertible and remittable in both current and capital account. Profit distribution from a French SARL is lawful provided French Commercial Code solvency tests are met, and no exchange-control license is required. Dividend remittance abroad may be subject to French withholding tax at 12.8% or 25% as applicable, unless reduced by an applicable double-tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; this tax treatment is prudential and does not constitute exchange control. Cross-border payments to non-resident counterparties are monitored for balance-of-payments statistics and AML/CTF but are not subject to prior administrative authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no fixed repatriation timeline, and no statutory conversion ratio in France. French resident exporters may retain foreign-currency earnings in foreign or domestic accounts. For statistical and monitoring purposes, transactions with non-resident counterparties equal to or exceeding the Banque de France reporting threshold, generally EUR 12,500 or the equivalent, must be declared by the intermediary French banking institution in the balance-of-payments system. The SARL must ensure the invoice, SWIFT reference, and goods-customs declarations are kept to evidence the free disposal of export receipts and to permit smooth bank compliance clearance.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Direction des Statistiques Monétaires et Financières) together with the Direction Générale du Trésor at the Ministry of Economy and Finance for foreign exchange control, balance-of-payments reporting, and foreign direct investment statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low_Medium. An EU/EEA-licensed EMI or payment institution can offer operational multi-currency IBANs and SEPA/SWIFT payment execution for collections and operational outflows, but it cannot legally originate commercial letter-of-credit instruments, purchase trade receivables, lend, or provide classic bank guarantees. Because the French SARL is a wholesale physical-commodity trader with need for documentary trade rails, a licensed payment institution is viable only as a secondary collection channel for small counterparty settlements and must have safeguarding accounts at a French credit institution. It should not be used for capital escrow or large bitumen trade disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contribution from a non-French shareholder should be executed by SWIFT MT103 in EUR to the SARL's dedicated EUR account at the French relationship bank, using the receiving French bank's BIC and the SARL's FR76 IBAN. The remittance should carry the explicit purpose reference 'Capital increase SARL' together with the SARL's SIREN number and the corporate decision date. The French bank will capture the transaction for Banque de France balance-of-payments statistics under the foreign direct investment category. The SARL must retain the notarised or in writing shareholders' resolution, bank certificate of receipt, and the Trade and Companies Registry capital-contribution attestation. For a shareholder loan instead of equity, use SWIFT MT103 with reference 'Shareholder current account/subordinated loan', supported by an executed loan agreement, and report accrued interest separately under the France's external-payments statistical filing. Funds received must not be used before completion of the legal verification that a capital increase has been duly decided and, if applicable, filed by the SARL manager.Settlement for bitumen ex-works and free-on-board purchases should be routed through the French bank's trade-liquidity desk using commercial documentary credits under SWIFT MT700 and cash disbursements under SWIFT MT103. In Eurozone clearing, use SEPA Credit Transfer for low-value Euro payments; for high-value or USD-denominated disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 with explicit payment-stipulated costs and purpose text referencing the invoice number, ISIC 4661 scope, and Harmonisised System tariff heading 2713 or 2715 as appropriate. The bank may require an underlying commercial contract, cargo evidence, and insurance certificate before release of disbursements under a Letter of Credit.
