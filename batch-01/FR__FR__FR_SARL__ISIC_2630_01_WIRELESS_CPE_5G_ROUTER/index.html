Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SARL must have a registered office in France under Articles L.123-11 and following of the French Commercial Code. Domiciliation at a commercial domiciliation provider is permitted if the provider is authorized and the arrangement complies with Articles L.123-11-2 and following and the related regulatory provisions. A manager may, subject to statutory conditions and time limits, use a personal residence for domiciliation. A purely virtual address is insufficient if it prevents receipt of official correspondence, maintenance of mandatory records, effective management, or access by authorities. For a telecom-equipment OEM, a virtual office may be accepted for initial incorporation and administrative headquarters, but it is weak evidence of commercial substance where the company imports, stores, tests, certifies, sells, or provides technical support for routers and optical terminals. A real operating location, documented staff or outsourced functions, inventory controls, technical documentation, product-compliance records, customer contracts, and customs capability are recommended.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), primarily through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official impots.gouv.fr portal. Customs and import VAT matters are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI) through the douane.gouv.fr portal.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is registered through the electronic business formalities one-stop shop operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). The filing generates or links the entity's SIREN and SIRET identifiers. The competent SIE assigns or confirms the French tax account and corporate income tax registration. A VAT identification number in the format FR plus a two-character check key and the nine-digit SIREN is obtained from the SIE. A non-resident foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the relevant non-resident tax service for a French tax number and, where required by taxable French transactions, a French VAT number. Importers must obtain an EORI number from French customs before customs activity. VAT registration may be required immediately for imports, intra-Community acquisitions, taxable French supplies, or an election out of the VAT franchise; the domestic turnover franchise does not prevent earlier registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) by default. An SARL may elect for income-tax transparency (impôt sur le revenu, IR) if statutory conditions are satisfied, including the special regime for qualifying newly formed companies and the family SARL regime. The election is subject to eligibility, shareholder, activity, and time-limit requirements and is not a general U.S.-style check-the-box election. No general hybrid-mismatch entity election is available. Qualifying small companies may access the reduced 15% IS rate on a specified portion of taxable profit, while the standard IS rate is 25%, subject to statutory conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the domestic VAT franchise applicable principally to supplies of goods, the ordinary turnover threshold is EUR 85,000, with a higher tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500 under applicable rules. These thresholds are not a universal VAT registration safe harbor: imports, intra-Community acquisitions, certain cross-border supplies, and voluntary VAT registration can create registration obligations below the threshold. France also applies separate thresholds to services, generally EUR 37,500 and EUR 41,250 tolerance, subject to statutory sector and period rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced at 0% French VAT if the exporter can prove both the export transaction and the physical exit of the goods from the EU, normally through the electronic customs export declaration, Movement Reference Number (MRN), and exit confirmation or equivalent customs evidence. The exporter must retain commercial invoices, transport records, packing lists, order documents, and customs records. Services are generally governed by place-of-supply rules rather than a technical export zero-rating rule. B2B services supplied to a customer established outside France may fall outside French VAT where the customer-location rule applies; evidence of customer status and establishment is required. Input VAT refunds remain subject to normal deduction, documentation, and limitation rules. There is no French statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days as a condition for applying the export VAT exemption.

  • Electronic export customs declaration containing the correct commodity classification, customs value, origin, exporter, consignee, and destination data
  • EORI number and customs authorization or representation documentation
  • MRN and customs exit confirmation, or equivalent evidence that the goods exited the European Union
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, sales contract or purchase order, and transport document such as bill of lading, airway bill, or CMR
  • Proof of delivery or transport evidence where required to substantiate the export chain
  • Import-country customs or delivery evidence where available
  • Bank payment evidence or accounting records where relevant to substantiate the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a generally mandatory French export-VAT document
  • For telecom equipment, records supporting applicable Union product-compliance, classification, licensing, sanctions-screening, and dual-use assessments where relevant

France does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice clearance system. Electronic invoicing between French-established businesses subject to VAT will be mandatory for receipt from 1 September 2026, and for issuance by large enterprises and certain designated entities from 1 September 2026. Small and medium-sized enterprises, including most SARLs, will generally be required to issue compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be transmitted through an approved private platform or the public invoicing portal under the final implementation framework. E-reporting of designated B2C and cross-border transaction data will be phased in on the same timetable. Existing invoice integrity, authenticity, data retention, and anti-fraud requirements remain applicable. Customs import and export declarations are filed electronically through customs systems and require appropriate EORI and declarant authorization.

If export conditions or documentary proof are not established, the DGFiP may reassess French VAT at the applicable rate, deny or claw back related input VAT deductions or refunds, and charge late-payment interest. The standard late-payment interest is generally 0.20% per month, subject to statutory rules. Procedural penalties may include a 10% surcharge for late or incorrect payment, with higher surcharges commonly applicable for deliberate failure, abuse of law, or fraudulent conduct, potentially reaching 40% or 80%; a 100% surcharge may apply in specified obstruction or concealed-activity cases. False customs declarations may trigger customs duties, import VAT, customs penalties, seizure, and criminal liability. Intentional VAT fraud can result in criminal prosecution, fines, and imprisonment under applicable French law. There is no automatic 180-day foreign-exchange clawback rule for French export VAT; reassessment is based on failure to satisfy VAT exemption, customs, accounting, or anti-fraud requirements.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU) Articles 63–66, subject to EU-wide derogations and national security/statistical reporting measures. France imposes no blanket profit remittance restrictions; however, dividend distributions must satisfy French corporate law solvency tests and are subject to anti-abuse rules under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) if applicable. Capital inflows for equity subscription can be transferred freely into France, but must be reported to the Banque de France for external balance-of-payments statistics via the resident receiving bank. No prior administrative authorization is required for ordinary foreign investment, but investments in sectors listed under the French foreign investment control (IEF France) regime, including dual-use telecommunications infrastructure equipment, may trigger a prior approval requirement. For a 5G wireless CPE manufacturer under ISIC 2630, a pre-filing notification to the Direction générale du Trésor is required if the non-EU investor acquires control or a threshold of 25% of voting rights, assuming the SARL’s activities touch on France's critical technology perimeter.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not mandate any export proceeds surrender requirement or a compulsory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency or convert to Euro freely. All export transactions exceeding EUR 12,500 must be declared, through the intermediary bank (établissement de crédit), to the Banque de France for the balance-of-payments statistical collection under Regulation (EU) 2016/867 and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier) Articles R. 152-1 to R. 152-6. The declaration must be made within 12 months of the transaction, and the documentary evidence (invoice, customs declaration, bank statement) must be retained for 5 years for audit.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in conjunction with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the licensed supervisory body for credit institutions and investment firms.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium for treasury operational purposes (e.g., low-value supplier payments) under the ACPR e-money institution (EMI) regime or an EU passport from another member state. Offshore EMIs domiciled outside the EEA require full ACPR licensing and are not viable for capital escrow accounts. For statutory capital injection into a French SARL, a licensed credit institution (bank) is required to hold client funds and provide the capital certificate; an EMI cannot legally provide this capacity in France.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The legal capital injection for the French SARL must be executed via SWIFT wire (MT103) from the shareholder’s corporate account to the dedicated escrow account at a French licensed bank. The wire must reference the following purpose: 'Souscription au capital – SARL [entity name] – [notarial/court registration reference]' and must include precise bank identifiers (IBAN, BIC). The French receiving bank will require a business purpose code (Code type d'opération) — commonly 'G0' for capital operations under the Banque de France nomenclature — and will submit the external balance-of-payments declaration to the central bank automatically. The sending bank must provide a copy of the payment order and the affidavit of the capital deposit from the French bank (attestation de blocage de fonds) before the notary or court can register the SARL. Inbound verification requires reconciliation of the SWIFT sender bank name (SWIFT BIC) against the UBO documentation, and the bank may request the underlying capital subscription agreement and shareholder resolution to satisfy Article L. 225-13 (SARL equivalent) and AML rules.Outbound trade settlements for the CPE 5G router business will be conducted through two principal rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for supplier payments, customs duties, logistics fees, and general operational disbursements, using purpose codes 'R' (imports of goods) or 'S' (services) as per Banque de France reporting; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) for high-value raw-material or electronic-component imports from Asia (e.g., Chinese Wi-Fi chipsets and memory), with the French bank issuing at sight or at maturity upon presentation of compliant shipping documents. For inbound sales from EU distributors, SEPA CT inst or TARGET2 is used, but for non-EU customers, SWIFT MT103 remains the standard. The FR SARL should maintain a payroll and French tax payment rail via the commercial bank as well (DAS, DSN weekly).Outright FX forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY) with an ACPR-licensed credit institution or EU investment firm under EMIR rules, provided the hedge is linked to a firm commercial invoice or supply contract.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD) for matched principal and interest flows under the SARL’s working capital facility.FX options (plain-vanilla European) for purchase of USD trade payables, subject to forward validation of trade receipts.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for EUR-denominated use against freely convertible currencies (e.g., USD, GBP, CNY) because the Euro has full onshore convertibility; NDFs are reserved for restricted currencies (e.g., INR, KRW) and are not recommended. Only OTC products with ACPR-licensed counterparties are permitted; exchange-traded FX futures are permissible but must be cleared via an EU CCP.Foreign currency savings accounts (e.g., USD savings) are permissible but are considered uncovered exposure and should be pre-approved by the bank’s treasury risk committee.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is Medium for treasury operational purposes (e.g., low-value supplier payments) under the ACPR e-money institution (EMI) regime or an EU passport from another member state. Offshore EMIs domiciled outside the EEA require full ACPR licensing and are not viable for capital escrow accounts. For statutory capital injection into a French SARL, a licensed credit institution (bank) is required to hold client funds and provide the capital certificate; an EMI cannot legally provide this capacity in France.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The standard French withholding tax on dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate shareholder is generally 25%, subject to applicable treaty relief. A 0% exemption may apply under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France where the qualifying parent, holding period, subject-to-tax, beneficial ownership, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. A 75% rate may apply to payments to non-cooperative states or territories or in specified anti-abuse situations.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the annual return is generally due by the second working day after 1 May of the following year; an additional 15-calendar-day electronic filing tolerance is commonly available. For a non-calendar year, filing is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. Corporate income tax balance payment is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a 31 December year-end, the balance is generally due by 15 May.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million. Full transfer-pricing documentation requirements generally apply where the French entity or French tax-consolidated group has annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, or belongs to a group meeting relevant statutory conditions. The documentation package generally comprises a Master File and Local File. A Country-by-Country Report generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Even below the EUR 400 million threshold, the French tax administration may request evidence that related-party transactions are arm's length, and general transfer-pricing documentation and reporting duties can still apply.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a single standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to the UAE ESR framework. Substance is nevertheless enforced through the French general anti-abuse rule, abuse-of-law doctrine, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, principal-purpose and treaty anti-abuse standards, and business-purpose requirements. A French SARL should maintain genuine local management, premises or operational capacity appropriate to its functions, employees or outsourced resources under effective control, French accounting and banking records, commercial risk assumption, inventory and logistics evidence, and decision-making records. Conduit financing, royalty routing, artificial management, circular funds, and arrangements lacking economic purpose are high-risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and concealed turnover

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, late-payment interest, penalties generally up to 80% of the evaded tax, and criminal tax-fraud sanctions that may include imprisonment and substantial fines. Serious organized or aggravated fraud can attract enhanced criminal penalties and asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Managers may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, imprisonment, professional disqualification, tax-publication consequences, and civil or tax liability where their personal conduct or grossly negligent management caused the loss. Corporate limited liability does not protect intentional fraud.
Shell company, artificial arrangement, or treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization, reassessment of corporate income tax and withholding tax, 40% or 80% abuse-related penalties, late-payment interest, and possible application of the general anti-abuse rule.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for mismanagement, tax liability in cases of fraudulent or deliberate conduct, and criminal exposure where false records, money laundering, or fraudulent insolvency is involved. Travel restrictions and imprisonment are possible only through applicable judicial proceedings, not automatically.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, denial of deductions, withholding-tax adjustments, interest, transfer-pricing documentation penalties, and penalties potentially based on 0.5% of undocumented transaction amounts or 5% of reassessed profits, subject to statutory rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for deliberate misstatements, obstruction, fraudulent accounting, or serious management fault. Routine assessment generally remains a corporate liability unless statutory personal-liability conditions are met.
VAT carousel and fraudulent invoicing

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, denial of input credits or refunds, penalties up to 80% in aggravated cases, payment of interest, seizure, and criminal VAT-fraud sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment, confiscation, and tax-liability proceedings where they directed, knowingly facilitated, or failed to prevent fraudulent conduct.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-ownership failure

Statutory sanction: Suspicious-transaction reporting and supervisory sanctions, freezing or confiscation of assets, administrative fines, criminal money-laundering penalties, and possible prohibition from conducting business.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face criminal fines, imprisonment, confiscation, management bans, and civil liability. The SARL must maintain accurate beneficial-owner filings and appropriate risk-based controls.
Sanctions, export controls, and dual-use telecom equipment

Statutory sanction: Administrative or criminal sanctions, transaction nullification, asset freezing, confiscation, substantial fines, export-license revocation, and imprisonment depending on the breached EU, French, or international restriction.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for intentional or negligent violations, including fines, imprisonment, confiscation, and management disqualification. Screening, end-use controls, classification, licensing, and record retention are required.
Customs undervaluation and origin misstatement

Statutory sanction: Additional customs duties, import VAT, customs penalties, seizure or forfeiture of goods, delayed clearance, loss of simplified procedures, and potential criminal customs liability.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for intentional customs fraud, false declarations, or serious failures of customs governance, including fines, imprisonment, confiscation, and civil recovery.
Illegal dividend or capital distribution

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, shareholder and director liability, corporate-law penalties, tax adjustments, and potential insolvency or criminal sanctions for misuse of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may owe civil restitution, face management bans, contribute to insolvency shortfalls in qualifying cases, and incur criminal liability for misuse of company assets or presentation of false accounts.
Accounting, corporate-register, and beneficial-owner non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Court orders, civil fines, tax penalties, presumptive assessments, refusal of compliance certificates, and enhanced scrutiny of all tax filings.

Director personal liability: Managers may face civil liability for mismanagement, fines, management prohibitions, liability for insolvency shortfalls, and criminal consequences where records were falsified or deliberately withheld.
Permanent-establishment and management-location mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Retroactive French corporate income tax, VAT, payroll and social-security assessments, withholding tax, interest, penalties, and possible permanent-establishment profit attribution adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal exposure for intentional concealment, fraudulent accounting, or deliberate failure to register and report French activities.
