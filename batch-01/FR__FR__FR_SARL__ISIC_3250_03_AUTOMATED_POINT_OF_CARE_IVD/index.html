Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Directorate-General of Public Finance (DGFiP), Paris; online portal www.impots.gouv.fr. Customs and indirect tax matters are administrated by the Directorate-General of Customs and Indirect Rights (DGDDI) via www.douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned SARL: (1) incorporate and file the M0 form at the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE) to obtain SIREN and SIRET numbers; (2) the corporate tax office (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) issues the French intra-community VAT number (numéro intracommunautaire) after VAT registration; (3) a non-resident taxable person without a French establishment must register using the simplified Form 1487-SD and produce a foreign VAT ID; (4) an EORI number is obtained separately from DGDDI for customs formalities; (5) if a non-EU or non-EEA parent has no permanent establishment, a fiscal representative must be appointed for VAT purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned SARL: (1) incorporate and file the M0 form at the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE) to obtain SIREN and SIRET numbers; (2) the corporate tax office (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) issues the French intra-community VAT number (numéro intracommunautaire) after VAT registration; (3) a non-resident taxable person without a French establishment must register using the simplified Form 1487-SD and produce a foreign VAT ID; (4) an EORI number is obtained separately from DGDDI for customs formalities; (5) if a non-EU or non-EEA parent has no permanent establishment, a fiscal representative must be appointed for VAT purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

SARL is default-assessed under corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). It may elect, within the first five fiscal years and subject to conditions, to be taxed like a partnership under personal income tax (option IR). A French corporate group may elect fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) for loss offsetting, and VAT grouping (assujetti unique) may be elected. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are imposed under ATAD implementation (Art. 205 quinquies CGI); there is no US-style check-the-box election in France.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 annual taxable turnover for supplies of goods / EUR 37,500 for supplies of services (CGI Art. 293 B); an upper abatement band applies (EUR 93,500 / EUR 41,250). Mandatory VAT registration is required once the base threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under CGI Art. 262-I to goods physically exported to destinations outside the EU, including IVD analyzers and microfluidic cartridges, provided the supplier holds documented proof of transport out of the EU and a valid customs export declaration has been accepted. Certain related services (freight, loading, international transport) are zero-rated as export-linked services when supplied directly to the exporter. Offshore digital services supplied to non-EU businesses are outside the scope of French TVA when the customer is in a third country.

  • Customs export declaration (DAU/ENS) accepted by French Customs (DGDDI) with validated Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Through bill of lading / air waybill (or equivalent transport document) proving physical removal of goods from EU customs territory
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT payment confirmation showing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs value and declared HS classification
  • Transport evidence (CMR, transit accompanying document, cargo manifest) where applicable
  • CE certificate of conformity and IVDR technical documentation for automated point-of-care IVD analyzers

France mandates structured electronic invoicing for B2B transactions under the reform of TVA e-invoicing. Domestic invoices must be exchanged via Chorus Pro or an accredited dematerialization platform (PDP) in the EN 16931 format, with mandatory e-reporting for cross-border supplies. The phased deadlines apply from 2026/2027. There is no physical revenue stamp or general real-time pre-clearance for IVD goods; compliance is enforced through digital transmission, API validation, and online tax e-filing (GIVRE).

Incomplete documentation or failed proof of physical export causes immediate reversal of the zero rate, recovery of French VAT plus late-interest, and application of negligence or bad-faith penalties (40% / 80% of the VAT due). Fraudulent structuration may lead to criminal liability under CGI Art. 1741 (up to EUR 45,000 fine and 3 years imprisonment) and additional customs penalties under the Code des Douanes. DGFiP audit windows extend up to six years for undisclosed or fraudulent positions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full member of the eurozone; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance is unrestricted under EU capital movement rules (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Articles 63-66), subject to standard anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-terrorist financing (CTF) verification. No exchange control approval is required for dividend distributions, loan repayments, or repatriation of invested capital, provided the underlying transactions are properly documented and reported to the Banque de France for statistical purposes (balance of payments reporting).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds is imposed in France. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency earnings in domestic or foreign bank accounts. However, all cross-border transactions exceeding €12,500 must be reported to the Banque de France via the 'Déclaration de Transfert de Fonds' (DTF) for statistical and balance-of-payments purposes. The reporting deadline is within 20 business days following the month of the transaction. There is no conversion ratio requirement; companies may hold foreign currency balances or convert them at their discretion.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) under the authority of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Recovery; EU-level coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and the European Systemic Risk Board (ESRB) for macroprudential oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. French-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine, and Lydia Solutions offer multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment services. However, for a manufacturing entity with significant trade flows and LC requirements, traditional banks remain more robust. EMIs may lack full trade finance capabilities (e.g., issuance of commercial letters of credit) and may have lower transaction limits. Legal constraints: EMIs must be licensed by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) and are subject to the same AML/CTF obligations as banks. They cannot provide credit or hold client funds as deposits; they are limited to payment services. For capital escrow or large-scale trade settlements, a full-service bank is recommended.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound capital injections (shareholder loans or equity contributions) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202. The payment must include a clear purpose code (e.g., 'Equity injection' or 'Shareholder loan') and reference the SARL's SIREN number. For amounts exceeding €12,500, the bank will require a DTF declaration. The funds must be received in the SARL's designated capital escrow account or operational account. Verification: The bank will request a copy of the shareholder resolution, the bank statement of the sending entity, and a certificate of the source of funds. For non-EU transfers, additional sanctions screening and enhanced due diligence may apply.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for direct payments to suppliers; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) via SWIFT MT700 series for documentary credit transactions; (3) Documentary collections (D/P, D/A) via SWIFT MT400 series. For high-value or recurring payments, the SARL may use the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA) for euro-denominated transactions within the EU, but for non-EU suppliers, SWIFT is mandatory. The bank will require underlying trade documents (invoice, bill of lading, packing list) for LC issuance. For FX conversion, the bank will execute at the prevailing interbank rate plus a margin; forward contracts can be arranged.Outright Forward Contracts (FX Forwards) with a maturity up to 12 months, available from major French banks for EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, and other major pairs.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for currencies with restricted convertibility (e.g., certain emerging market currencies) – permitted under French regulations, subject to underlying exposure verification.Currency Swaps (FX Swaps) for simultaneous spot and forward transactions, used for liquidity management and rollover of positions.Options on FX (European-style) for hedging against adverse movements while retaining upside potential – available from tier-1 banks.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) for long-term hedging of both principal and interest flows, particularly for shareholder loans or project financing.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax rate (CGI Art. 219); reduced rate of 15% applies to qualifying SMEs on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit per fiscal year.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory dividend WHT for non-resident corporate recipients (CGI Art. 119 bis 2); 12.8% for non-resident individuals, subject to treaty reduction and possible 2% solidarity levy for non-EU/EEA residents.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full OECD-style Master File/Local File mandatory when group-wide gross turnover or gross assets reach EUR 400,000,000 (CGI Art. L.13 AA/L.13 AB; BOFIP conditions); annual controlled-transaction declaration on Form 2257-SD is triggered when foreign related-party transactions exceed EUR 100,000.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone ESR regime, but applies a substantive fiscal-residence test (CGI Art. 209: place of effective management in France), the judicial abuse-of-law doctrine (LPF Art. L.64), the OECD Principal Purpose Test, and anti-artificial-scheme rules. A French SARL must maintain genuine local premises, staff, board minutes, bank accounts, operational decision-making and physical substance; a shell with sham management in an offshore jurisdiction risks treaty denial, 80% penalties and criminal tax fraud referral.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude Fiscale

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to EUR 500,000 (CGI Art. 1741); aggravated fraud using fictitious entities or offshore accounts: 7 years imprisonment and EUR 3,000,000 fine; tax reassessment with 40% intentional-misstatement penalty or 80% fraudulent-abuse penalty plus 0.20% monthly late interest.

Director personal liability: The SARL gérant may be criminally convicted, personally ordered to pay all evaded taxes and penalties, banned from managing, subjected to travel restrictions, and in insolvency held liable for corporate debts under Art. L.223-22 Code de commerce where managerial fault caused the shortfall.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Hidden Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Tax redetermination of profits to arm's length; denial of deductibility for tax-haven payments under Art. 238 A CGI; 40% penalty for deliberate transfer-pricing misstatement; 80% penalty for abusive transfer to a related entity in a preferential tax regime plus late payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false documentation or orchestrate sham intercompany contracts may be joint debtors for corporate tax and penalties, face criminal tax evasion charges, and be disqualified from management.
Shell Company / Fictitious Substance

Statutory sanction: Abuse-of-law procedure under LPF Art. L.64; the SARL is disregarded, taxable income is attributed to the true beneficial owner, 80% penalty is imposed on redirected profits, and mandatory referral to the criminal prosecutor occurs for abuse of law.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil is available in cases of fictitious management or grave misconduct; the manager and real beneficial owners may be jointly and severally liable for corporate debts, subject to civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for tax fraud.
Abusive Treaty/Directive Relief and Beneficial-Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or EU directive relief; retroactive reinstatement of 25% statutory WHT; 80% abusive-arrangement penalty; potential withholding-tax evasion prosecution under Art. 1741 CGI and monetary penalties under the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Director personal liability: Signing officers who issue or present false TRC/beneficial-ownership certificates are personally liable for resulting tax deficiencies and penalties, may face criminal prosecution for forgery and tax fraud, and may be subject to imprisonment and prohibition from corporate management.
