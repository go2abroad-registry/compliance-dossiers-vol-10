Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French Public Finance Directorate, acting through the Divisional Tax Offices for Enterprises (SIE) and the non-resident tax services; online portal: www.impots.gouv.fr. Customs export and import formalities are separately administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned French SARL is first registered with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the Guichet Unique formalities portal operated by INPI, obtaining a SIREN number and SIRET establishment number. The DGFiP then allocates the French corporate income tax (IS) account and the French VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) to the registered entity. A foreign entity with a permanent establishment or direct taxable activity in France must file a tax registration application with the competent SIE or the DGFiP non-resident service, provide corporate authorisation, proof of foreign registration, director identification and a fiscal representative appointment where the entity is not established in the EU. The issued VAT number is also used as the French tax identification number for dealings with customs (EORI), suppliers and customers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SARL is by default subject to entity-level corporation tax (IS). Eligible SARLs may elect, under Article 239 bis AA of the French General Tax Code, to be taxed as a partnership (régime des sociétés de personnes) for a limited period, causing profits to be taxed at the shareholder level as passthrough income; a similar family-company option exists under Article 239 bis AB. No check-the-box election is available in France; the IS/IR election is the functional equivalent for entity classification. For VAT, affiliated persons may elect to form a single taxable person (assujetti unique / VAT group) under Article 256 C III of the CGI. France does not allow election around hybrid mismatch rules; EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) provisions are mandatorily applied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

An entity established in France must register for VAT when it commences regular economic activity, regardless of turnover, unless it qualifies for the small-business exemption known as 'franchise en base de TVA' (annual turnover not exceeding EUR 85,000 for sales of goods and EUR 34,400 for services; higher adjusted thresholds apply in certain cases). A manufacturing SARL making surgical sutures will generally be required to be VAT-registered because it engages in continuous supplies and must recover input VAT; the franchise exemption is not available where the business applies export zero-rating and needs input VAT recovery. For non-resident suppliers of intra-EU distance sales to France, the annual distance-selling threshold is EUR 10,000 for all cross-border distance sales within the EU; exceeding this threshold triggers French VAT registration unless the OSS/IOSS regime is used.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union qualify for the French 0% VAT rate (exonération de TVA avec droit à déduction) under Article 262-I of the French General Tax Code (CGI) when the seller proves that the goods were physically transported outside the EU in accordance with customs formalities, a valid export declaration exists, and transport and commercial documentation demonstrate delivery to the foreign destination. Directly connected logistics services and certain offshore services supplied to a non-EU recipient may also be zero-rated under Articles 262-II, 263 and 259 CGI where the place of supply is outside France and the benefit is genuinely located abroad. The exporter must retain contemporaneous, consistent and credible evidence; without this proof, the transaction is reclassified as a taxable domestic supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration Unique d'Exportation / DAU) accepted by the DGDDI
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, CMR or equivalent transport document evidencing movement from France to a non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice / export sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 evidence of receipt of export proceeds
  • Packing list, certificate of origin and, where relevant, exit summary declaration validation

France is progressively mandating electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting. From 1 September 2026, all VAT-taxable businesses must be able to receive e-invoices, and large businesses must issue e-invoices through either the public Chorus Pro portal or a certified Partner Dematerialisation Platform (PDP). From 1 September 2027, e-invoicing becomes mandatory for smaller entities, including micro-enterprises, and e-reporting of cross-border transaction data must be delivered electronically to the DGFiP. The regime operates through structured PDF and API/AS4 exchanges to the PDP; there is currently no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping/crypto-stamping system at DGFiP level, although the PDP must transmit invoice data in a standardised format. Invoicing to public-sector bodies already requires structured e-invoices through Chorus Pro.

DGFiP conducts strict export-audit scrutiny of zero-rate claims, especially for goods with high unit value such as surgical consumables. If the export evidence is incomplete, contradictory or fabricated, VAT is reassessed at 20% on the invoice amount, with monthly late-payment interest (intérêt de retard), and penalties of 40% are applied for intentional fault (mauvaise foi) or up to 80% for fraudulent behaviour; restitution and clawback of previously refunded input VAT and export VAT refunds are ordered. Criminal prosecution for tax fraud may result in fines of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years. Customs authorities may also impose penalties for false customs declarations or unlawful use of the export regime. The ordinary audit limitation period is six years, extended to ten years (or longer) where fraud, fictitious invoices or concealment are established.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax, generally applicable to French companies and French permanent establishments. A reduced 15% rate may apply to qualifying small and medium-sized companies on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to applicable turnover, capital ownership and paid-up-capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate shareholder are generally subject to a 25% withholding tax under French domestic law, subject to exemptions and treaty relief. The French parent-subsidiary regime may reduce withholding to 0% for a qualifying EU or European Economic Area corporate parent meeting the minimum ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax and anti-abuse conditions. Domestic withholding may also be reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the return is generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the following year. For a financial year ending on another date, the return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The final corporate income tax balance is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, subject to the applicable electronic payment calendar. Four instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, unless an exception applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full French transfer-pricing documentation is generally mandatory where the French enterprise or relevant French tax group meets the statutory EUR 150 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, or where it is owned by, owns, or belongs to a group containing an enterprise meeting the threshold, subject to the applicable statutory rules. A country-by-country report generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million. A simplified annual transfer-pricing declaration may apply from lower thresholds, including where annual turnover or gross assets reach EUR 50 million or the relevant controlled-transaction criteria are met. Materiality and transaction-category thresholds must be checked for the filing year.

Economic substance scope: France has no single standalone economic-substance regulation equivalent to certain offshore economic-substance regimes. Substance is enforced through transfer-pricing arm's-length rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, the abuse-of-law doctrine, the principal-purpose test, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation, anti-hybrid rules, withholding-tax anti-abuse provisions and general anti-avoidance principles. A French SARL manufacturing surgical consumables should maintain genuine French premises, equipment, personnel, production records, quality-control capacity, inventory and decision-making appropriate to its functions and risks. Intercompany royalties, management charges, financing, guarantees and procurement arrangements must correspond to actual services or rights and be priced at arm's length.
