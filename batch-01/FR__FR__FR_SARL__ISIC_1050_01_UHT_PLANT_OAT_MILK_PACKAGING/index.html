Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is a fully convertible currency. France maintains full liberalization of capital movements and payments under Articles 63 to 66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), subject only to public-policy and security exceptions under the Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier). Profit remittances by a French SARL in the form of dividends or shareholder advances of retained earnings are unrestricted provided that corporate law formalities are observed (ordinary shareholders' meeting, certification of balance sheet, and the prohibition on distribution where net assets are reduced below the share capital plus non-distributable reserves). No exchange-control permission or administrative license is required for conversion of euros into foreign currency, and no form A/Form B exchange-approval regimes apply. Conversions must pass through an established credit institution or licensed payment/currency-exchange provider and are subject only to anti-money-laundering customer due diligence, tax transparency declarations, and statistical reporting to Banque de France where the transaction constitutes a cross-border direct investment exceeding the thresholds established by regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in France or in the wider euro area requiring export proceeds to be surrendered, repatriated, or converted into euros. Exporters may hold foreign-currency receivables in accounts with French or EU-authorized credit institutions or, subject to French banking authorization requirements, in qualifying foreign bank accounts. The only obligations are record-keeping, the submission of the S13/Balance-of-Payments declaration by enterprises to Banque de France for operations exceeding EUR 750,000 per transaction or linked series (déclaration d'échange de biens et de services), and the filing of the

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the supervisory authority for banking and insurance institutions; the euro foreign exchange operations are conducted within the Eurosystem framework, and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) retains limited authority over external financial relations and balance-of-payments statistics.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Squarely compliant inbound routing consists of: (1) SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder or lender to the French SARL account at a Tier-1 French bank, with the field 50 instruction designating the sender actual client, and field 72 'BENEFICIARY SARL' followed by the IBAN; (2) a 'capital account' purpose code referencing the Statutes and a specific legal basis (share capital contribution to be recognized under sector law: SAS/SARL rules) for equity-like injections, or purpose code 'Financial transaction' for shareholder loans; (3) for amounts above EUR 750,000, the initiating bank or beneficiary bank forwards the information to the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical collection system via S13 declarations; (4) underlying legal documentation is preserved, i.e., duly signed deed of contributions or loan agreement, registry filing of annual accounts, and where a capital increase is executed, the certificate of incorporation after modification of statutory capital; (5) the transaction must not be split, fragmented, or restructured in order to evade the reporting threshold, and the beneficial owner must be disclosed under the Fourth and Fifth Anti-Money-Laundering Directives, as implemented in the French Monetary and Financial Code.For trade settlements of the product imports or raw material purchases, the permitted clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 direct campus transfer where counterparties maintain IBAN in the EEA, and SWIFT EUR payments passing through the TARGET2 system for non-SEPA creditors; (b) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT, XML ISO 20022) for all euro-zone counterparties; (c) documentary commercial letters of credit (ICC UCP 600 Rules) opened at a Tier-1 bank on the French buyer's behalf, with documents presented to the beneficiary bank for negotiated settlement; (d) standby letters of credit (ISP98) or bank guarantees attached to settlement; (e) where a physical shipment of aseptic packaging material is effected, clean-on-board bills of lading, phytosanitary certificates or certificates of conformity, commercial invoices, and the required EU Single Administrative Document customs entry clearance must accompany the disbursement instruction. Purpose codes on outbound wires are to be set as 'Trade payments – goods' or, alternatively, 'Financial service fees' only if the remittance is genuinely for freight, insurance, or technical services. No French exchange-control approval is required for outbound trade-debt payments in euro or foreign currency, but the transaction must satisfy the French foreign-exchange record-keeping regime and be evidenced by the underlying contract and invoice.Outright FX Forwards (EUR against USD, GBP, or destination-market currencies) executed with a French credit institution under an ISDA or French Master Agreement, documented under EMIR trade reporting requirements.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) with respect to currency pairs having no usable onshore deliverable due to unofficial exchange rates or restricted convertibility; such NDFs must be economically necessary for the settlement of real business trade exposure, not as speculative contracts.Currency swaps (cross-currency interest rate and principal-exchange swaps) covering the term of the foreign shareholder loan or capital-injection debt to convert variable foreign-currency cash flows into EUR fixed-rate exposures.FX options (call and put) purchased by the French SARL to hedge projected procurement and remittance flows, subject to the bank's capacity and MiFID-appropriateness test, and prohibited from layering or inducing speculative uncovered positions.Rolling FX forwards or window forwards ensuring the treasury matches monthly oat-milk packaging plant disbursement cycles against monthly invoiced receivables.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate (impôt sur les sociétés); reduced 15% rate applies to small businesses on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit per fiscal year.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding for non-resident corporate shareholders; 30% final withholding for non-resident individuals (12.8% income tax plus 17.2% social levies); 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds at least 5% for two years or more.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File are mandatory where the worldwide group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 50 million; a French entity engaged in related-party cross-border transactions below that threshold must still prepare, on request, transactional documentation for individual controlled transactions above EUR 400,000 or intercompany services above EUR 100,000.

Economic substance scope: No separate Economic Substance Regulation exists in France, but France applies a robust abuse-of-law doctrine (Article L.64 LPF) and OECD Action Plan ground-rules requiring real management presence, local staff, risk absorption, and commercial purpose for Permanent Establishment status and DTA relief; a French SARL that holds IP or groups activities without genuine substance will be challenged by DGFIP, with the related dividend, royalty, or capital gains benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / accountancy fraud

Statutory sanction: 40% bad faith penalty and 80% concealment penalty plus late-payment interest; criminal prosecution under Article 1741 French Tax Code with fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be declared jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debts; personal travel bans, passport seizure, suspension of commercial license, and disqualification from company management for up to ten years.
Shell company or artificial arrangement

Statutory sanction: DGFIP may set aside the arrangement under Article L.64 or L.64A of the French Tax Procedures Book; treaty benefits lost; penalty of up to 80% of the amount of improperly avoided tax; recharacterization of the SARL as a transparent entity.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for the additional tax, penalties and interest; potential criminal proceedings for fraudulent reorganization; judicial ban on leaving France and seizure of personal assets.
Anti-money laundering violations via payments

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine and financial sanctions under the French Monetary and Financial Code; imprisonment up to five years and fines up to EUR 375,000 for responsible managers; seizure of shipping containers, plant equipment, bank accounts, and payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors treated as 'person's liable' under AML law; personal indictment, travel bans, professional disqualification, asset freezing, and confiscation in connection with laundered proceeds from tax fraud.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative income tax reassessment with 40% penalty for deliberate lack of arm's length pricing, plus late-payment interest; separate autonomous fine for failure to deliver the transfer pricing documentation upon request; possible exclusion from foreign tax credit for the French branch.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax deficiencies and surcharges; in severe cases, criminal fraud penalties apply where the TP documentation is knowingly falsified; directors face banishment from future commercial management.
