Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office is accepted for the purpose of establishing a registered administrative seat, provided it consists of a physical address situated in France and corresponds to a genuine functional presence such as a serviced business centre or rented office that receives statutory communications. However, for a manufacturing business under ISIC 3091 (electric cargo bicycles and step-through scooters), tax authorities, the trade registry, and customs authorities expect a real operational establishment — production space, import-export warehouse, or at minimum a dedicated logistics location — within the jurisdiction. A pure virtual office without any physical manufacturing or warehousing capacity exposes the entity to challenges regarding commercial substance, VAT registration domiciling, and abuse-of-law recharacterisation under French General Tax Code Article L64.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum share capital for SARL; shareholders freely determine capital. Cash contributions must be deposited on a dedicated bank account opened in the company's name or held by the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations / notaire before registration; blocked until RCS issuance; in-kind contributions valued and described in Articles of Association with potential commissaire aux apports.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique — official portal: impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel for e-filing); business registration gateway: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique, operated by INPI); EU VAT number validation: VIES (ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies); customs export clearance authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

The non-resident-owned SARL completes online incorporation through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) with bylaws, registered-office proof, gérant identification and capital-deposit certificate. INSEE automatically issues the SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) identifiers and the company is entered in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS). DGFiP then allocates the intra-Community VAT number (format FR + 11-digit INSEE key) and opens the corporate income tax (IS) file at the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); the SARL sets up an e-filing account (Espace Professionnel) on impots.gouv.fr. Export operations require prior allocation of an EORI number by DGDDI and filing of export declarations via the DELTA-X system. Non-EU shareholders require no French fiscal representative for a French-resident SARL; only a non-EU person carrying out French taxable supplies without a French establishment must appoint an approved fiscal representative (Article 289 A CGI).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (default SARL subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés per Article 206 CGI; Territorial_Scope: only profits attributable to French operations are taxable under Article 209 A CGI; no pass-through status; no tax-exempt zone default; a family SARL may elect income tax only if all members are natural persons of the same family lineage per Article 239 bis AA CGI). — The non-resident-owned SARL completes online incorporation through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) with bylaws, registered-office proof, gérant identification and capital-deposit certificate. INSEE automatically issues the SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) identifiers and the company is entered in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS). Non-EU shareholders require no French fiscal representative for a French-resident SARL; only a non-EU person carrying out French taxable supplies without a French establishment must appoint an approved fiscal representative (Article 289 A CGI).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (default SARL subject to Impôt sur les Sociétés per Article 206 CGI; Territorial_Scope: only profits attributable to French operations are taxable under Article 209 A CGI; no pass-through status; no tax-exempt zone default; a family SARL may elect income tax only if all members are natural persons of the same family lineage per Article 239 bis AA CGI).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in France. Available elections: (i) SARL de famille income tax election (Article 239 bis AA CGI); (ii) reduced 15% IS rate on taxable profit up to €42,500 per year if the SARL meets SME conditions (Article 219 I-b CGI: fully paid-up capital; annual revenue ≤ €10 million; direct/continuous 75%+ ownership by individuals); (iii) French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI if the SARL is directly or indirectly held 95%+ by a French parent; (iv) VAT cash-flow election (option pour le paiement de la TVA d'après les encaissements) and election of the 'régime réel normal'; (v) anti-hybrid mismatch compliance: the annual IS return requires the annex hybrid statement (Form 2258-SD) per ATAD 2 rules where cross-border instruments or entities create deduction/non-inclusion mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€85,000 annual turnover for supplies of goods and €37,500 for supplies of services under the 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption (Article 293 B CGI, thresholds restated to these levels from 1 January 2025). The threshold is an exemption from VAT collection, not a registration moratorium: VAT registration is mandatory from the first euro for importers, non-established businesses and intra-Community distance sellers; an electric cargo bike manufacturer exceeding the €85,000 goods threshold must file monthly (CA3) VAT returns and may not apply the franchise. Exporting manufacturers are structurally required to register regardless of turnover in order to deduct input VAT on export production costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of electric cargo bikes and spare parts to non-EU destinations qualify for exemption with right to deduction (effective 0%) under Article 262 I-1° CGI. Conditions: (i) the goods must physically leave the EU and dispatch must commence no later than 3 months after transfer of ownership; (ii) the exporter files a customs export declaration (Déclaration d'exportation via DELTA-X) from France; (iii) the purchaser must be established outside the EU and the exporter retains title/control evidence; (iv) transport documents (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR) must evidence actual border departure; (v) proceeds must be repatriated in convertible currency through banking channels (TT_Wire / Swift LC). Intra-Community dispatches to EU customers are zero-rated under Article 262 ter I-1° CGI, subject to a valid EU customer VAT number, DEB/Intrastat filing and dispatch within 3 months. Ancillary export services (international transport, docking, loading and supervision directly connected with the exported goods) are exempt under Article 262 I-2° CGI; logistics services inseparably connected with the exported goods may also qualify under the same provision.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation filed via DELTA-X as EXA/EX B) proving customs release from France
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure of the goods from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Swift MT103 (or TT_Wire advice) evidencing conversion and repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice marked 'Exportation - TVA non applicable - Article 262 I-1° CGI' with Incoterms 2020 (FOB/EXW/DAP rules)
  • EORI certificate and, when the destination market requires preferential origin treatment, EUR.1 certificate, Form A or REX statement

France will require mandatory structured B2B e-invoicing under the Facturation Électronique reform: from 1 September 2026 all large enterprises (≥5,000 employees) and mid-size enterprises (250-5,000 employees or revenue > €50M) must receive and issue e-invoices; from 1 September 2027 all SMEs and micro-businesses must receive and issue e-invoices. The issuer transmits structured invoices (Factur-X, UBL 2.1 or CII/UN-CEFACT formats) through a DGFiP-certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the Public Portal (PPF). Cross-border transactions (exports and intra-Community supplies) require transaction e-reporting to the administration. No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or Italian/Spain-style invoice-controller applies; compliance is enforced ex post via electronic VAT returns (CA3/CA12), EC Sales Lists (DEB) and Intrastat declarations; late or non-compliant e-invoice transmission triggers fines (€15 per invoice, capped at €15,000 per year) under Article 1737 CGI.

DGFiP applies risk-based export-audit density to a high-value electric-bike producer. Failure to produce the documentation bundle within the audit cycle reinstates the VAT on the exported invoices at 20% (Articles 262/272 CGI), plus monthly late interest at 0.2% (Article 1681 I CGI). Penalties are graduated: 40% for insufficiency of declaration/bad faith (Article 1729 CGI), escalating to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres (Article 1729-1 CGI). Fabricated export documents, backdated consignments or a non-existent foreign buyer trigger parallel DGDDI customs enforcement with fines up to 100% of the transaction value in aggravated cases; criminal tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI may reach a €3 million fine and up to 10 years' imprisonment in aggravated cases, with a further clawback of input VAT deducted on the exported goods when zero-rating is overturned. Repatriation delays beyond the 180-day deadline increase the recharacterization risk of the sale as a domestic taxable resale and expose the remaining proceeds to interest and recovery.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full current and capital account convertibility as an EU and eurozone member. No general exchange control approval is required for profit remittances, dividends, loan repayments, or capital contributions. Remittance hurdles are limited to corporate law formalities, tax withholding on outbound dividends (subject to tax treaty relief), EU/UN/OECD sanctions screening, and mandatory customs cash reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies in France. Exporters may freely hold foreign currency balances in resident accounts, provided all wire transactions are reported for balance of payments statistical purposes and screened for sanctions, AML, and tax transparency obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with prudential supervision executed by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); the French Treasury also exercises limited licensing and reporting oversight under the EU capital movement regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms require either a full French (or EEA) electronic money institution licence, passporting into France under the EU PSD2/EMD2 regime, or a French branch/agent arrangement. Unlicensed offshore EMIs generally cannot provide client euro IBAN accounts, process French payroll or supplier payments, or execute French B2B trade settlements without breaching reserve, UBO, and ACPR rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection is executed via SWIFT MT103/MT202 or TARGET2 settlement from the non-resident investor to the SARL's euro-denominated French bank account. The bank's balance of payments reporting to Banque de France uses EU/IMF standard classification 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital'. Required documentation includes a certified share subscription resolution, French shareholder register update, KYC/AML pack, foreign direct investment notification as applicable, and the purpose code reflecting cross-border direct investment. Funds must originate from the investor's own bank account and be retained in escrow until the legal share issuance is completed.Outbound trade settlement uses SWIFT MT103 (wire transfer) for prepayments and open account invoices, and SWIFT MT700 (commercial letter of credit) for documentary collections, down payments, and performance guarantees. For eurozone suppliers, SEPA Credit Transfer provides lower-cost rails. Each SWIFT message requires accurate purpose codes, e.g., 'Goods: electric cargo bicycles and components', and customs invoice references. All disbursements are screened automatically for sanctions, ECB restrictive measures, and anti-money laundering triggers.
