Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce de Lyon (Commercial Court Registry maintaining the Register of Commerce and Companies, RCS), which receives the dossier electronically through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce): Articles L.210-1 et seq. (general company law), L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL); Articles R.223-1 et seq. and R.123-101 et seq. (registration with the Register of Commerce and Companies); Ordinance No. 2023-77 of 8 February 2023 establishing the single digital filing portal (Guichet Unique).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) together with the single Cerfa declarative form of incorporation, the depositary certificate, the legal announcement receipt, and the statement of beneficial owners.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 11 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

France imposes no statutory minimum share capital for a SARL; the Statuts set the capital freely. At incorporation at least one-fifth (20%) of cash-subscribed capital must be paid up (Article L.223-7 of the Code de commerce). Funds must be deposited in a blocked account with a bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, evidenced by an attestation de dépôt des fonds, and are released to the manager only upon RCS registration. In-kind contributions must be fully transferred upon signature and require a statutory appraiser (commissaire aux apports) where the individual value exceeds EUR 30,000 or the aggregate value exceeds half of the share capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety and civil-defense compliance with the Labor Code (Code du travail) and the departmental fire authority (SDIS), including building evacuation certification, fire detection, and the intervention plan; structural load certification by an approved technical control office (Bureau de Contrôle) confirming floor capacity of at least 5 kN/m² for the SMT and wave-soldering lines; occupational health and workplace clearance delivered by the Inspection du Travail with the intervention of CARSAT and the occupational-medicine doctor, covering passive compliance with ESD protection areas, fume extraction, and medical-fitness checks for operators.

Paid-in capital rule: France imposes no statutory minimum share capital for a SARL; the Statuts set the capital freely. At incorporation at least one-fifth (20%) of cash-subscribed capital must be paid up (Article L.223-7 of the Code de commerce). Funds must be deposited in a blocked account with a bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, evidenced by an attestation de dépôt des fonds, and are released to the manager only upon RCS registration. In-kind contributions must be fully transferred upon signature and require a statutory appraiser (commissaire aux apports) where the individual value exceeds EUR 30,000 or the aggregate value exceeds half of the share capital.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; online portal impots.gouv.fr; e-invoicing portal Chorus Pro / Portail Public de Facturation (PPF)

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is incorporated through the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE) or the single electronic portal formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; the CFE files the company formation dossier with the RCS and DGFiP, which then allocates SIREN/SIRET and, where applicable, a French TVA intracommunautaire number (FR + OCR). For a non-resident foreign entity without a French establishment, VAT identification is filed directly with the DGFiP non-resident taxpayer unit using the dedicated form (cerfa 1598-SD), accompanied by constitutive documents, proof of foreign tax residence and, when required by Article 302 D CGI, a French fiscal representative. The representative must be established in France and approved by DGFiP. Processing generally takes two to four weeks from submission of a complete file.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A French SARL is incorporated through the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE) or the single electronic portal formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; the CFE files the company formation dossier with the RCS and DGFiP, which then allocates SIREN/SIRET and, where applicable, a French TVA intracommunautaire number (FR + OCR). For a non-resident foreign entity without a French establishment, VAT identification is filed directly with the DGFiP non-resident taxpayer unit using the dedicated form (cerfa 1598-SD), accompanied by constitutive documents, proof of foreign tax residence and, when required by Article 302 D CGI, a French fiscal representative. The representative must be established in France and approved by DGFiP. Processing generally takes two to four weeks from submission of a complete file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not apply a US-style check-the-box election. A SARL is by default opaque and subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) under CGI Article 206/219. A qualifying small or family SARL may elect for pass-through income tax treatment under CGI Article 239 bis AA (SARL de famille) for a five-year irrevocable period. No hybrid mismatch election is available; anti-hybrid rules apply under CGI Article 205 quinquies B (ATAD II).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

French-established taxpayers benefit from the small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base de TVA) up to EUR 85,000 of annual turnover for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for supplies of services. For a non-established foreign taxable person, no de minimis registration threshold applies: registration is required before the first taxable supply in France. Intra-EU distance sales thresholds are EUR 10,000 per calendar year unless the Union OSS scheme is elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies of goods physically exported from the French customs territory to a destination outside the EU, provided title passes to the non-EU customer and the exporter retains documentary proof of removal from the Union. Under CGI Article 262 and BOI-TVA-CHAMP-40-10, the export declaration must be lodged with the French customs authorities and transport documents must demonstrate departure. B2B services supplied to third-country customers are outside the scope of French VAT where the place-of-supply rule points to the customer's establishment (CGI Article 259-1 B), unless the service is connected with French real property; contract assembly services performed on tangible goods may be zero-rated as part of an export supply when the goods are destined for the non-EU buyer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/SAD) with movement reference number (MRN), EORI number and AES/Export Control System release record
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, CMR road consignment note, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, export packing list, sales contract and purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank confirmation evidencing receipt and FX repatriation of export proceeds
  • Freight engagement and carrier instruction records proving the goods were exported by or on behalf of the French supplier
  • VAT zero-rating substantiation file containing export attestations and the taxpayer's internal checklist under BOI-TVA-CHAMP-40-10

France has implemented a sequenced e-invoicing mandate. All established taxable persons must issue B2B e-invoices using a certified dematerialisation platform (PDP) or the public portal PPF/Chorus Pro: from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises, and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises. Structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII) and API transmission to the PPF are required. E-reporting of transaction and payment data is mandatory in parallel. There is no physical digital stamping or real-time pre-clearance approval; data is transmitted in near-real time for tax authority audit clearance.

The French tax administration applies strict documentary scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. Failure to produce the mandatory customs and transport documentation within thirty days of an information request triggers reversal of the 0% rate and reassessment at 20% VAT, plus late-payment interest and a 5% penalty for late filing under CGI Article 1788 A. Deliberate or fraudulent claims attract an additional 40% penalty (mauvaise foi) or 80% penalty (manœuvres frauduleuses), and criminal tax fraud under CGI Article 1741 is punishable by a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and five years’ imprisonment. The DGFiP may also apply the abuse-of-law procedure under Book of Tax Procedure Article L.64 to disregard artificial arrangements created to obtain export VAT benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member with fully liberalized capital and current accounts under Article 63 TFEU and Articles L.151-1 et seq. of the Code monetaire et financier; there are no convertibility quotas, bilateral payment agreements, or exchange-control approvals blocking euro conversion for a French-resident SARL. The practical remittance constraint is fiscal, not regulatory: dividends paid by a French SARL to a non-resident corporate shareholder are subject to French withholding tax under CGI Article 119 bis 2 at 25%, reducible to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU) where the parent EU company holds at least 5% of the SARL for at least 2 years, or to a lower treaty rate for non-EU shareholders (e.g., 5% or 10% under the applicable China-France income tax treaty). Foreign investors may freely inject equity and debt into the SARL, subject only to statistical reporting to the BdF and, for non-EU investors, to the FDI screening regime of Article L.151-2 et seq. CMF, which does not normally capture commercial PCB assembly unless the buyer or end-use is in a strategic defense or critical-infrastructure sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export proceeds; a French exporter may hold foreign-currency receivables in authorized foreign-currency accounts at ACPR-licensed banks. However, export collections must be channeled through an authorized intermediary credit institution under Article L.511-1 CMF, and the receiving bank automatically reports the transaction to the BdF balance-of-payments system for statistical and compliance purposes. There is no statutory conversion ratio, but transactions exceeding EUR 750,000 in a calendar month trigger the BdF's direct-investment and financial-flow declaration framework, and the SARL must be able to evidence the underlying export sale (invoice, transport document, customs declaration, and proof of settlement) within the ordinary commercial payment terms stated in the contract.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), acting as the French central bank and statistical agent for the European Central Bank, in conjunction with the Direction Generale du Tresor (French Treasury) of the Ministere de l'Economie, des Finances et de la Souverainete industrielle et numerique, which holds the regulatory authority for foreign-exchange and foreign-investment control, and the Autorite de Controle Prudentiel et de Resolution (ACPR), which licenses and supervises credit institutions and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

France permits ACPR-licensed electronic-money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Lydia, Treezor) to open operational IBAN-based accounts and execute SEPA and SWIFT transfers; feasibility for use as a secondary operating account is Medium-High, but as a primary cross-border treasury rail for a PCBA contract manufacturer the feasibility is Low. Legal constraints: a French EMI cannot issue documentary letters of credit or guarantees, cannot provide trade finance or receivables discounting, cannot hold secured capital-escrow balances for share-capital constitution, and is not an 'authorized intermediary' for the full export-import collection and balance-of-payments reporting chain. Client funds must be safeguarded with a French credit institution, and correspondent clearing is routed through partner banks, adding latency and potential intermediary fees for USD, CNY, and exotic-currency flows.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident parent funding the SARL's entire share capital without local banking history and without a French credit footprint.

Root cause: Non-resident parent funding the SARL's entire share capital without local banking history and without a French credit footprint.

Defense evidence: Current RNE/Kbis extract, certified articles of association, parent's audited financial statements for the last 2 years, 12 months of parent bank statements, board resolution approving the capital increase, signed source-of-funds declaration, ownership chart to the ultimate beneficial owner(s), and a projected 12-month cash-flow statement from the SARL.
Gerant or majority shareholder being a non-French resident or a person connected to a jurisdiction with elevated AML/CFT sensitivity.

Root cause: Gerant or majority shareholder being a non-French resident or a person connected to a jurisdiction with elevated AML/CFT sensitivity.

Defense evidence: Original passport, French residence permit or equivalent visa, official criminal-record certificate from the country of residence, proof of professional activity (pay slips, tax return, professional references), and a signed source-of-wealth statement supported by bank or investment statements covering at least 12 months.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital must be routed through an ACPR-authorized French bank. Recommended protocol: the parent's bank sends an SWIFT MT103 with beneficiary details as the SARL's French IBAN and BIC, clear reference in field 70 'CAPITAL INCREASE - DIRECT INVESTMENT', and a purpose code corresponding to the BdF balance-of-payments category for direct investment (equity capital); the French beneficiary bank must receive a copy of the shareholders' resolution, the capital attestation, and the RNE/Kbis extract before crediting the blocked capital account. If cumulative flows exceed EUR 750,000 in a calendar month, the bank files the direct-investment declaration with the BdF under Article L.151-2 CMF. For shareholder loans, execute a formal loan agreement with arm's-length interest, file the loan in the SARL's annual tax return (form 2777), and note that French withholding tax of 25% may apply to interest paid to a non-resident lender unless a treaty reduces or eliminates it. If the SARL itself maintains any foreign bank account abroad, it must be declared annually to the DGFiP via form 3916-bis.For the PCBA contract-manufacturing model, two primary clearing rails apply: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for pre-shipment deposits, milestone payments, and settlement of open-account balances in EUR, USD, or CNY; and (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued under UCP600 via SWIFT MT700 for large component consignments or where the Asian supplier requires bank confirmation. The French bank will apply EU sanctions screening, dual-use goods controls under EU Regulation 2021/821, and standard anti-money-laundering checks; the SARL must present the underlying purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, transport documents, Incoterms, and customs declarations for each transaction. For collection-based cross-border settlements, use documentary collection under URC 522 rather than open-account TT where payment risk is elevated.
