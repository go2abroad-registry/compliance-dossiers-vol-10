Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for a SARL under Article L.223-2; capital is freely fixed in the Statuts (recommended EUR 1,000). Under Article L.223-7, at least 20% of cash contributions must be paid up before filing, in-kind contributions must be fully paid up, and the balance must be paid within five years. The subscribed funds must first be deposited into a blocked attestation account held by a bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and are released only upon presentation of the Kbis extract after RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance required under the Labor Code (arrêté of 25 June 1980) and the local safety commission: installation of fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and signage; electrical inspection certificate issued before first commissioning of the injection molding presses; structural load inspection of the floor for machinery exceeding 2 t/m²; preparation of the DUERP and workplace health plan under Article L.4121-1 of the Labor Code; CE marking conformity declaration for all machinery; consultation of the CSE once the workforce reaches 11 employees.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is a fully convertible currency for both current and capital account transactions in France; free movement of capital applies under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Articles L.221-1 and R.221-1 of the Code Monétaire et Financier. Profit remittances by a SARL to non-resident shareholders are not subject to FX control, but dividend distributions trigger France withholding tax, generally 25% on gross dividends absent a tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, and cannot be made if accumulated losses would erode share capital; the legal reserve of at least 5% of annual net income must be funded until the reserve equals 10% of share capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation or mandatory conversion requirement for export proceeds; foreign currency can be retained in a foreign-currency account or converted to EUR at the entity's discretion. However, all transfers of funds between France and third countries aggregating more than EUR 10,000 per calendar month per country must be declared through the Banque de France déclaration de transfert de fonds (DTF) process, and the Banque de France also collects direct investment balance-of-payments surveys when cross-border equity and loan flows exceed statutory reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting as the central bank and balance-of-payments authority, together with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking supervision, the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for investment screening and the Commission Bancaire under the Code Monétaire et Financier.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million in group consolidated annual turnover or total assets triggers mandatory Local File and Master File under Article L.13 AA CGI; EUR 750 million in group revenue triggers Country-by-Country Reporting under Article 223 quinquies C; French entities with EUR 50 million annual turnover (and intragroup operations) must provide a simplified file.

Economic substance scope: France applies no Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as found in offshore centres; however, Article 57 CGI and the Article L.64 LPF abuse of right doctrine require genuine French economic substance. A French SARL must maintain real presence, staff, and operational control. Lack of substance may lead to re-attribution of profits to France, denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test, and 40% penalty for abusive tax mitigation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine under Article 1741 CGI of EUR 250,000 for corporate offenders (fivefold: EUR 1,875,000) and up to five years' imprisonment; additionally administrative 100% penalty on evaded tax plus 0.20% monthly interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrating the fraud may be personally convicted, face travel bans, have personal assets frozen or seized, and may be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt when the SARL is a sham or used as a conduit.
Willful Non-Filing and Tax Audit Obstruction

Statutory sanction: Ex officio assessment (taxation d'office) plus 10%/20% filing penalty and 0.20% monthly interest; EUR 150 fine per omitted form or false schedule; serious obstruction may lead to an order to close the company for up to five years.

Director personal liability: The manager may incur a personal fine of EUR 750 under Article 1732 CGI; if gross negligence is established, imprisonment of up to six months and a EUR 7,500 fine, a ban on management, and a travel ban can be imposed.
