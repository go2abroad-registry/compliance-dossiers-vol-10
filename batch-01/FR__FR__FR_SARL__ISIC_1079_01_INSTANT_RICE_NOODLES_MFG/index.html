Governing statute

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for the SARL (Article L.223-2). A minimum of 20% of each cash subscription must be paid up before registration and deposited in a blocked escrowed account with an authorised bank, notary or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the balance is payable within five years. The funds are released only upon issuance of the K-bis; in-kind contributions must be fully transferred at incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued by the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) for the production hall, valid emergency exits and extinguishing systems; structural load clearance for retort and boiler foundations; inspection of pressure equipment in compliance with Directive 2014/68/EU (DESP) and the Order of 30 December 2015; occupational clearance evidenced by the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP) with heat, steam, noise and electrical risk control measures.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for the SARL (Article L.223-2). A minimum of 20% of each cash subscription must be paid up before registration and deposited in a blocked escrowed account with an authorised bank, notary or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the balance is payable within five years. The funds are released only upon issuance of the K-bis; in-kind contributions must be fully transferred at incorporation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – impots.gouv.fr; SIRENE/INPI for entity registration; Centre de Liaison des Impôts (CLI Montreuil) for VAT registration of non-residents.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity procedure: (1) If a permanent establishment is created in France, register via the INPI/Guichet Unique to obtain SIREN/SIRET numbers. (2) If not established in France, file VAT registration application directly with DGFiP (SIE non-résident) using Form 15335 and appoint a fiscal representative if the entity is established outside the EU (jointly liable for VAT). (3) Obtain the French intra-EU VAT number (FR + 11 digits) and request an EORI number for customs exports. (4) For corporate income tax, file an initial return with the competent SIE; non-resident entities are subject to limited territorial taxation on French-source income unless a tax election applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

SARL is subject to corporate income tax (IS) by default. Election for pass-through income tax (IR) may be available for small SARLs (e.g., SARL de famille, SARL operating for less than five years, or under Chapter 239 CGI). French group tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI is available for groups holding at least 95% of subsidiaries. Hybrid mismatch rules under Article 212bis CGI (ATAD implementation) must be evaluated for all cross-border payments, but no US-style check-the-box election exists in French law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No threshold for non-established foreign entities (registration required before first taxable transaction); domestic small business exemption: EUR 85,000 for sales of goods / EUR 37,500 for services (2023/2024 thresholds).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods physically exported outside the EU if proof of export is supplied within 90 days (Article 262-I CGI). Services directly related to exported goods (e.g., freight, handling) qualify under Article 262-II CGI. Intra-EU dispatches to a taxable recipient holding a valid EU VAT number are zero-rated under Article 256bis CGI. The exporter must prove the goods left France/EU and identify the recipient, nature, and value of the goods.

  • Customs export declaration (DAU/ENS)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or CMR/Airway Bill
  • Proof of physical export (PBE) or third-party transport evidence
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank settlement proof for export proceeds

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is phased from 1 September 2026 for established taxpayers. Invoices must be transmitted through a certified PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) or the public portal Chorus Pro in structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII). E-reporting data (settlement and transaction data) must be delivered to DGFiP within one business day. No real-time clearance or digital stamping regime is imposed; compliance is ensured through periodic VAT returns and audit trails.

If zero-rating is applied without substantive export or intra-EU supply evidence, DGFiP may retrospectively assess VAT at 20% on the transaction, add late-payment interest (0.20% per month per Article 1727 CGI), and apply penalties under Article 1729 CGI: 40% for gross negligence or deliberate misstatement, 80% for abusive fraud. Substantial penalties may also be imposed for failure to maintain contemporaneous records. Criminal tax fraud (Article 1741 CGI) can trigger fines up to EUR 750,000 and imprisonment for senior officers. In an audit, the burden is on the exporter to demonstrate physical departure within the 90-day legal period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is freely convertible in France for both current and capital account transactions, reflecting the full liberalisation of capital movements under TFEU Articles 63 to 66 and the complete absence of currency conversion quotas or prior FX licensing for a resident SARL. A French resident company may freely settle commercial invoices, remit profits, pay royalties, reimburse shareholder loans and repatriate liquidation proceeds; the only formal hurdle is the Banque de France statistical declaration of every cross-border flow equal to or greater than EUR 50,000 under Code monétaire et financier Articles R. 152-1 et seq. Profit remittance hurdles are purely tax-driven: dividends to an EU parent holding at least 10% for two years are withholding-tax free under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; dividends to a non-EU shareholder attract domestic withholding subject to reduction under an applicable double-tax treaty upon proof of beneficial ownership. Inward foreign investment in this food-manufacturing sector is outside the strategic sectors list and is processed under the simple declaratory investment-control regime, not an authorisation regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion timeline and no conversion ratio; export receipts in USD, CNY or any freely convertible currency may be retained in the SARL's foreign-currency accounts indefinitely. The only binding procedure is statistical and declarative: export receipts equal to or greater than EUR 50,000 per calendar month must be captured in the Banque de France balance-of-payments return, which is normally auto-generated from the collecting bank's files, and the SARL must retain the SWIFT MT103/MT202 references, customs SAD exit declarations and commercial invoices for at least six years. Export proceeds held offshore remain fully legal provided they are properly booked in the SARL's French accounts and reconciled with the trade-receivable register.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Central Bank of France) — monetary authority and balance-of-payments administrator; Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) — licensing and prudential supervisor of credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Medium as an auxiliary treasury rail, Low as the primary operating bank. A PSD2 electronic money institution holding an EU licence can passport into France via the ACPR and lawfully maintain euro e-money accounts for the SARL, but cannot take deposits granting interest, cannot grant overdraft or credit, and cannot open or service commercial letters of credit; it is therefore unsuitable as the sole bank and unsuitable for the blocked capital escrow. An offshore non-EU EMI without an ACPR-recognised passport is a legal compliance blocker: using such an instrument would violate the French Code monétaire et financier and expose the SARL to TRACFIN intelligence scrutiny. Acceptable fintech substitutes are French-licensed neobanks or European-headquartered EMIs such as Qonto, Shine, Wise Europe or similar, limited to B2B collections and low-value operational disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire protocol for incorporated share capital: funds are wired via SWIFT MT103 to a blocked or escrow account opened in the SARL's name with the notary or a tier-1 bank, using ISO 20022 purpose code 'INTC' for intra-company capital support or the bank's defined equity-injection code, and the depositary immediately issues the attestation de dépôt de fonds; funds are released only after RCS/Kbis registration. For shareholder advances (compte courant d'associé), the same MT103 protocol applies with purpose code 'TRAD' or 'INTC', supported by a shareholder resolution and documented interest terms. Verification requires exact match of the beneficiary corporate name with the Kbis extract and the FR76 IBAN in the French FICOBA account-matching register; every inbound flow equal to or above EUR 50,000 is auto-declared to the Banque de France, and the SARL retains the UETR, SWIFT gpi tracker and settlement confirmation as the six-year audit trail.The primary clearing rail for trade settlements is the SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer 'TT Wire' in EUR, USD or CNY, with EUR intra-EU payments routed over SEPA Credit Transfer and larger cross-border transactions cleared through TARGET2 and SWIFT gpi, always carrying a UETR for interbank tracking. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €50 million consolidated group turnover for Master File; €100,000 intra-group transactions for Local File

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities with relevant activities; manufacturing operations generally require only operational presence in France; failure to demonstrate adequate substance may result in recharacterization and denial of tax benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalty: fines up to 80% of evaded tax plus imprisonment for up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, restitution orders, and potential imprisonment in aggravated cases
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to 500% of tax adjustment; criminal sanctions if fraudulent intent proven

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for fines and restitution; possible disqualification from management
Failure to Comply with Economic Substance Requirements

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax deductions or benefits; potential recharacterization of transactions and additional tax

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for resulting tax losses or penalties
Non-Filing or Late Filing of Corporate Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of 0.5% per month of the tax due (minimum €50), up to 25% of tax due; possible criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for penalties and must ensure compliance
