Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registry of the Commercial Court) of the judicial district of the registered office; filings are transmitted electronically via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (INPI portal, formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce): Articles L. 210-1 to L. 210-10 (general provisions on commercial companies), L. 223-1 to L. 223-43 (SARL), R. 123-1 et seq. (RCS registration procedure); Regulation (EU) 852/2004 Art. 6 and Code rural et de la pêche maritime L. 233-1/2 for food-business declaration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création de société au Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), accompanied by the statuts constitutifs, certificat de dépôt des fonds, attestation de parution au Journal d'Annonces Légales, déclaration du bénéficiaire effectif, and the organs' acceptance documents.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital (Art. L. 223-2); capital is freely fixed by the statuts and can be as low as EUR 1. All shares must be fully subscribed. If capital is below EUR 10,000, the entire cash capital must be paid up at formation; if EUR 10,000 or more, at least 20% of each cash share must be released at subscription and the balance within 5 years. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked funds account (compte bloqué) with a French credit institution, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary within 8 days; the certificate of deposit of funds (attestation de dépôt des fonds) is required for registration, and funds are released only upon issuance of the K-bis.

Funding & unlock path: Maintain a Registre de sécurité (fire-safety register) at the premises; annual verification of extinguishers, emergency exits, and evacuation lighting per Code du travail Arts. R. 4227-1 et seq.; complete a Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP) covering handling of jute/grain bags, forklift traffic, and dust; obtain Consuel certificates for electrical/gas installations and the DAA/DDT declaration of completion of works for any fit-out; if the premises are open to the public, ERP classification and fire-commission inspection apply; workplace medical clearance and safety training for all employees before the start of activity.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital (Art. L. 223-2); capital is freely fixed by the statuts and can be as low as EUR 1. All shares must be fully subscribed. If capital is below EUR 10,000, the entire cash capital must be paid up at formation; if EUR 10,000 or more, at least 20% of each cash share must be released at subscription and the balance within 5 years. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked funds account (compte bloqué) with a French credit institution, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary within 8 days; the certificate of deposit of funds (attestation de dépôt des fonds) is required for registration, and funds are released only upon issuance of the K-bis.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under EU Treaty provisions (Article 63 TFEU) and the French Monetary and Financial Code. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayments, and investment returns are freely transferable to non-residents without exchange-control approval. Real-world frictions: strict AML/KYC documentation, €12,500 individual cross-border transaction reporting threshold for the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistics, and EU/OFAC/UN sanctions screening. For a specialty green coffee importer, no special convertibility licence is required; the euro is the domestic currency and all commercial payments to non-EU coffee suppliers are treated as ordinary trade payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements in France. French exporters are not obliged to convert foreign currency receipts into euros, nor to repatriate them within a specific statutory timeline. However, all cross-border monetary transactions exceeding €12,500 (or equivalent) must be reported to the Banque de France as part of the balance-of-payments statistics via the EMTA (Entreprises de Montant Trimestriel) or single-transaction declaration system. The SARL may retain USD proceeds from third-country re-exports, but treasury efficiency is maximised by converting INR?/EUR/USD through approved banks with documented trade cover.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French Banking and Monetary Control) in cooperation with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) under the Ministère de l'Économie, and the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) for banking/banking conduct supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for routine payment initiation and FX conversion, Low for documentary trade finance. Regulated French EMIs (e.g. Qonto, Blackbird, Treezor) operate under an ACPR/DPG permit and can hold client funds in safeguarded accounts, but they are not credit institutions and cannot issue or confirm letters of credit or take term deposits. Therefore, Qonto/EMIs are suitable for operational EUR treasury and low-risk supplier payments, while the core of import settlement for specialty green coffee containers (especially origin-country confirmed LCs) must be executed through a tier-1 bank with an international correspondent network.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route capital contributions via SWIFT MT103 in EUR or USD through a comptoir in the shareholder's jurisdiction to the SARL's French euro account. Use BIC/IBAN with TARGET2 Eurosystem clearing. For statutory incorporation: (a) open a temporary blocked account with the chosen bank; (b) file the capital deposit undertaking (attestation de blocage de fonds) in the Kbis/registration; (c) after Kbis issuance, the bank releases funds into the SARL operational account. Purpose code under French balance-of-payments: use ‘S001 – Equity capital on incorporation’ or equivalent nomenclature for non-resident direct investment share capital. If the capital contribution exceeds €12,500, submit the Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration (EMTA or single transaction form) within the statutory period. The shareholder must document source of funds to avoid AML inquiries: notarised transfer deed, approved minutes of Shareholders’ Meeting, tax integrity certificate from the contributing entity, and bank reference letter.For specialty green coffee bean container imports: (1) direct payment via SWIFT MT103 from the French bank to the origin-country exporter after presenting invoice, bill of lading, packing list, phytosanitary certificate, and 20-digit tariff code 0901.11; (2) stronger protection: irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit (LC) issued by the French bank (SWIFT MT700) in favour of the exporter, with the exporter presenting documents through a confirming bank at origin; (3) trade loans or trust receipt financing at the custom’s arrival, using the SARL warehouse receipts as security. All outbound disbursements require a stated payment narrative matching customs invoices, and the bank must receive the BOP declaration for payments to non-residents > €12,500. In EUR, settlement is handled via TARGET2; in USD via the French bank’s correspondent USD account (e.g. through a New York clearer). LC confirmation costs and interest are borne by the import entity. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax is generally imposed on dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. The rate may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. The French parent-subsidiary exemption generally requires at least a 5% qualifying holding, a minimum holding period or commitment, and satisfaction of anti-abuse and beneficial-ownership conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and tax package are generally due within three months after the fiscal year-end. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the filing deadline is generally the second working day after 1 May of the following year. Electronic tax payment and the balance are generally due by the same statutory deadline, subject to the applicable French tax calendar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory full transfer-pricing documentation generally applies where the French entity or group meets the EUR 150 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, or where the French entity is owned or controlled by an entity meeting that threshold, subject to statutory conditions. A French entity may also be required to maintain documentation for controlled transactions below that threshold, and simplified documentation or annual reporting can apply, including where annual turnover or gross assets reach EUR 50 million or where statutory related-party reporting thresholds are met. There is no general safe harbor based solely on the EUR 50,000 value of an individual transaction.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a single standalone regime titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless relevant under French transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-abuse rule, beneficial-ownership analysis, EU anti-avoidance rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, treaty principal-purpose testing, and recharacterization doctrine. The SARL should maintain real French decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises appropriate to its activity, inventory and logistics evidence, commercial risk control, banking activity, accounting records, and contemporaneous evidence supporting the importation and distribution functions. A French coffee distributor should align profits with functions, assets, risks, inventory ownership, credit risk, foreign-exchange risk, and market-development activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, 40% penalty for deliberate breach, 80% penalty for fraudulent maneuvers or abusive fraud, and criminal prosecution. Tax fraud can carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated cases reaching seven years and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Managers may incur personal criminal liability where they personally participated in the offense. Courts may impose confiscation, professional prohibitions, director-management restrictions, and judicial travel restrictions. The tax administration may also pursue a director whose serious and repeated misconduct made recovery from the company impossible.
Shell company, fictitious residence, or permanent-establishment concealment

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax, VAT, payroll-tax, customs, and withholding-tax reassessments; anti-abuse recharacterization; penalties of up to 80% in fraudulent cases; and potential criminal tax-fraud proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for intentional participation, fraudulent management, or failure to maintain required records. Criminal measures can include imprisonment, fines, confiscation, management bans, and judicial travel restrictions. Civil liability may include restitution of tax losses and damages.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and undocumented related-party transactions

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment, withholding-tax exposure, interest, penalties, and a documentation penalty generally calculated by reference to undocumented transaction amounts or reassessed profits, subject to statutory minimums and maximums. Customs authorities may separately adjust import values.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal exposure for deliberate concealment, false accounting, or fraudulent documentation. Ordinary assessment generally remains a company liability, but serious managerial misconduct can support personal recovery actions.
VAT carousel, false invoicing, or customs fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT and customs reassessment, seizure, interest, penalties that can reach 80% for fraudulent conduct, customs fines and confiscation, and criminal prosecution. False invoices and organized VAT fraud can produce substantially higher criminal penalties under applicable circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners involved in the scheme may be prosecuted personally, face confiscation and imprisonment, and be subject to management prohibitions and judicial travel restrictions. Customs and tax authorities may seek civil recovery from responsible persons where legally available.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing breach

Statutory sanction: Regulatory reporting, account restrictions, asset freezing, confiscation, administrative sanctions, and criminal money-laundering liability. Money laundering may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine; aggravated money laundering may carry up to ten years' imprisonment and a EUR 750,000 fine, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, and beneficial owners may be personally prosecuted for knowing participation or facilitation. Conviction can lead to imprisonment, fines, confiscation, management bans, judicial supervision, and travel restrictions.
Sanctions, export-control, and prohibited-counterparty breach

Statutory sanction: Payment blocking, asset freezing, administrative and criminal penalties, confiscation, and possible imprisonment under applicable French and EU sanctions legislation.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors may be personally prosecuted where they knowingly authorized or facilitated the breach. Personal fines, imprisonment, confiscation, management bans, and judicial travel restrictions may apply.
Accounting, corporate-record, and beneficial-owner filing violation

Statutory sanction: Civil and criminal penalties, refusal or loss of tax deductions, tax reassessment, accounting sanctions, and potential insolvency-related liability. Fraudulent accounting and misuse of company assets may trigger imprisonment and fines under French commercial and criminal law.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally liable for misuse of corporate assets, fraudulent accounting, serious management faults, and contribution to an insufficient insolvency estate. Courts may impose personal bankruptcy or management prohibitions, civil restitution, confiscation, and travel restrictions.
Improper profit distribution and shareholder extraction

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, corporate-tax and withholding-tax reassessment, penalties, interest, and potential misuse-of-company-assets or fraudulent-management prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors approving unlawful distributions may face personal civil restitution, shareholder and creditor claims, criminal liability for misuse of company assets, management bans, confiscation, and in serious cases imprisonment.
Insolvency delay and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: Court-supervised insolvency proceedings, clawback of transactions, director contribution to liabilities, bankruptcy restrictions, and criminal or commercial sanctions for fraudulent insolvency.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to contribute to all or part of the insufficient assets and may receive personal bankruptcy or management prohibitions. Fraudulent conduct can result in imprisonment, fines, confiscation, civil restitution, and judicial travel restrictions.
