Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum; capital may be €1 or higher. Capital must be fully subscribed and paid up in cash or in kind; for cash contributions, funds must be deposited in a blocked bank account (compte bloqué) until registration, then released to the company.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) for any onshore facilities. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) via the official portal impots.gouv.fr; VAT registration and corporate formalities coordinated through the Guichet Unique Électronique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; SIREN/SIRET identifiers issued by INSEE through the SIRENE national registry.

E-government filing portal

For a French SARL, incorporation documents are filed with the Guichet Unique (Business Formalities Centre), which transmits registration data to INSEE for allocation of a SIREN company number and SIRET establishment number. The DGFIP then issues an intra-Community VAT number (Numéro de TVA Intracommunautaire) prefixed FR, ordinarily notified by post and via the online impots.gouv.fr account within approximately two to four weeks. A non-EU parent without a French establishment that performs taxable supplies in France must register directly with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for VAT and may be required to appoint an accredited tax representative under Article 289A of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI), unless the representative obligation is waived. The applicant must submit extract of registration from home jurisdiction, evidence of economic activity, domestic VAT certificate or equivalent, and identity documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SARL is taxable at entity level on corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) by default. Small and medium-sized SARLs satisfying the size and ownership conditions of Article 239 bis AA CGI may elect for a limited five-year period to be taxed on shareholders under the income tax regime (Impôt sur le Revenu). A SARL may also join a French fiscal group (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI as a fully owned subsidiary of a French-resident parent, allowing tax consolidation of group profits and losses. Domestic law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; hybrid mismatch consequences are addressed under the ATAD 1 rules codified in Articles 212 bis and 209 B CGI, including anti-arbitrage adjustments for inverted hybrids and double deduction arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For an established French SARL, the VAT exemption threshold is €85,000 for the principal supply of goods and €34,400 for services (2024 thresholds under Article 293 B CGI), but once the supply involves installation, configuration or works connected with real property, the service-related services threshold of €34,400 generally applies. A legal person carrying on a taxable economic activity in France must register if taxable turnover exceeds these thresholds. Non-established foreign suppliers have no registration threshold: any taxable transaction in France, unless subject to reverse charge in B2B context for certain services or simplified IOSS for distance sales, requires a French VAT number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under CGI Article 262-I to physical exports of goods transported directly outside the European Union by the supplier or the purchaser, provided the goods leave France accompanied by a customs export declaration in DELTA. French-established suppliers may also zero-rate intra-Community supplies under Article 262-II when the purchaser provides a valid EU VAT number and a transport document proves movement to another Member State; such supplies must be reported on the EC Sales List. For offshore wind turbine pilings, if the monopiles are incorporated into an immovable foundation affixed to the seabed in France, the supply and installation is generally treated as a supply of goods with installation with the place of taxation in France, subject to 20% VAT, unless the contract demonstrably transfers risks and title to the goods before installation and the goods are exported as movables. Services physically rendered in France and directly linked to goods that will be exported may qualify for zero-rating where expressly provided by French administrative doctrine, provided the services are performed for a customer established outside the EU and the goods are subsequently transported outside France or the EU. Exporters must retain proof of export and evidence of direct shipment; VAT credit/refunds are claimed via the foreign VAT refund procedure (EU Directive 2008/9/CE for EU-established claimants or the EU 13th Directive refund for non-EU claimants) or through the periodic VAT return (CA3) in connection with the French SARL's export activity.

France has introduced a mandatory e-invoicing reform for domestic B2B transactions: from 1 July 2026 all VAT-registered taxpayers must be able to receive electronic invoices through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or an accredited private dematerialisation platform, and e-emitting obligations apply from the same date for medium and large enterprises; small and medium enterprises are phased in by 1 July 2027. Required standards are Factur-X, UBL, or UBL 2.1/UNTD. E-reporting transmits transaction data to DGFIP for VAT reconciliation in near real-time via API. Tax clearance is not a wholesale pre-authorisation system; however export declarations are processed through the French Customs DELTA system using real-time interoperability, and public-sector invoicing to French government entities already mandates Factur-X via Chorus Pro. Invoices for intra-Community supplies must appear in the recapitulative EC Sales List; foreign persons with French VAT numbers must observe the same e-reporting procedures.

French Customs and DGFIP jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Fraudulent claims or artificial export structures trigger immediate reassessment of VAT, with a 40% penalty for substantial inaccurate declarations under Article 1728 CGI and an 80% bad-faith penalty plus potential 40% additional penalty for abusive manipulation under Article 1729 CGI. Interest at 0.20% per month accrues from the original due date. Customs proceedings under the French Customs Code Articles 414 and 425 may impose fines of up to two times the evaded VAT or value of the goods, confiscation of the goods and transport, and suspension of the economic operator's customs authorisations. In criminal cases, false export declarations carry penalties of up to five years imprisonment and €450,000 fines. Retroactive clawbacks may be raised for up to six years from the tax year (extended to ten years for hidden operations), and administrative tolerance is limited where the exporter fails to produce the mandatory customs documentation bundle or cannot demonstrate repatriation of foreign exchange within the statutory deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full convertibility for both current and capital accounts with no exchange controls. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted following corporate tax compliance and beneficial ownership verification. Hurdles are minimal for EU-domiciled entities and standard cross-border project finance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds under French law. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in bank accounts without conversion to euros. Statutory requirements focus solely on accurate customs declarations and invoicing via the Single Administrative Document (SAD).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France and Direction Générale du Trésor

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility via authorized EMI providers integrated with French banking rails, subject to strict compliance with the French Monetary and Financial Code and Payment Services Directive (PSD2). Offshore digital EMIs require local partner validation and ongoing regulatory reporting.
