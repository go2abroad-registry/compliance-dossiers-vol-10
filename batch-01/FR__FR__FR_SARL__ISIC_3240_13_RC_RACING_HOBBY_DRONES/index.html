Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office acceptance is not absolute and is strictly constrained under Article L123-10 & R123-30 of the French Commercial Code. A SARL must maintain a physical seat and a corresponding legal address within France; domiciliation by a commercial third-party provider is permitted only for hold and service companies provided that the activity is not a regulated sector requiring immediate physical concentration, and the domiciliary provider must hold a valid INSEE-approved agreement and be registered as a legal agent of the company. For FPV racing drone manufacturing and retail, a virtual office is permissible for central management mail reception and regulatory communication, but if substantial physical fabrication occurs, a real operational site must be separately registered with the Social Security and local tax center.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS) held by the Greffe du Tribunal de commerce of the registered office; the incorporation file is submitted exclusively through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) Guichet unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Code de commerce: Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (general company provisions), L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL formation and governance), L.227-1 et seq. (rue de la dépénalisation), R.223-1 to R.223-22; Articles L.123-1 to L.123-18 and R.123-1 to R.123-16 (RCS registration); Décret n°2020-106 (Guichet unique / INPI digital filing).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) executed by all associates, together with the unified declaration form (Déclaration de création d'entreprise, formerly formulaire M0), the attestation de parution in an authorized Journal d'annonces légales (JAL), the attestation de dépôt des fonds (capital block certificate), and the declaration of beneficial owners.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for a SARL; the share capital is set freely in the articles (Code de commerce Art. L.223-2). All cash contributions must be fully subscribed and paid at incorporation (Art. L.223-7); funds must be deposited within 8 days of receipt with a notary, a credit institution, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and are released only upon presentation of the RCS registration certificate. In-kind contributions must be fully transferred and require a statutory appraiser (commissaire aux apports) where the value exceeds EUR 30,000 unless all associates waive.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy and Finance; operational portals: impots.gouv.fr, Espace Professionnel (professionnels.impots.gouv.fr), and the single company-formation portal formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique under the INPI). Customs and excise competence lies with the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) at douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SARL is incorporated via the Guichet Unique, triggering automatic INSEE assignment of the SIREN (national corporate TIN), its SIRET establishment code and the company tax file at the territorial SIE; the DGFiP subsequently issues the intra-Community VAT identification number for EU cross-border transactions. For a non-established foreign vendor, VAT registration is made by direct application to the competent SIE using Cerfa form 3310-CA, appointment of a French fiscal representative where statute so requires (CGI art. 289 A) for non-EU applicants, and evidence of the foreign seat and effective management; the SIE then enters the entity and allocates the FR-prefixed VAT number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A French SARL is incorporated via the Guichet Unique, triggering automatic INSEE assignment of the SIREN (national corporate TIN), its SIRET establishment code and the company tax file at the territorial SIE; the DGFiP subsequently issues the intra-Community VAT identification number for EU cross-border transactions. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box system; classification follows the legal form, so a standard SARL takes company personality and is by default taxed at entity level to corporate income tax (IS, CGI art. 206-1). Express statutory elections do exist: (i) a qualifying family SARL may irrevocably opt into the transparent societe de personnes income-tax regime (CGI art. 239 bis AA); (ii) a 95-per-cent parent may opt into French tax consolidation under CGI art. 223 A; (iii) a VAT-group election is available for the status of a single taxable person (CGI art. 256 C); (iv) hybrid-mismatch alignment under ATAD implementation requires symmetric income classification with counterpart jurisdictions. Elections are formally filed with the SIE and are generally irrevocable or subject to statutory lock-in periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in France. There are no French exchange controls on current-account or capital-account operations (TFEU Articles 63-66; CMF Articles L.151-1 to L.165-2). The SARL may freely denominate contracts in EUR, USD, RMB or any other currency, convert funds without central-bank authorization, and remit dividends, interest, service fees or loan repayments to non-resident shareholders without FX licensing. Profit remittance hurdles are purely fiscal and documentary: distributions are subject to a 25% French withholding tax on dividends paid to non-French tax residents (Article 119 bis 2 of the French General Tax Code), which is reduced to 10% where the Chinese parent company holds directly at least 25% of the SARL's capital (and to 15% in other cases) under the France-China Double Tax Treaty of 26 November 2013, provided the beneficial-owner form RF1 procedure and a tax-residence certificate are submitted. Physical cash transport within the EU is unrestricted but a customs declaration is mandatory for cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more under Regulation (EC) No 1889/2005. No French treasury or central-bank permit is required for any bank transfer in connection with capital injections, trade settlements or profit repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The French regulatory framework imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no repatriation timeline and no conversion ratio for foreign-currency export receipts. The SARL may retain export revenue indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts and convert it at market rates at its commercial discretion. The only compliance obligations are statistical: French banks report the SARL's cross-border financial transactions to the Banque de France balance-of-payments (BOP) office, and the SARL must cooperate in direct-investment BOP declarations whenever annual direct-investment flows between residents and non-residents exceed EUR 15 million or a direct-investment relationship is established or terminated. Dual-use export screening under EU Regulation 2021/821 applies only to drone hardware meeting specific control parameters; standard consumer FPV racing drones (ISIC 3240, EU class C0-C2 under Regulation (EU) 2019/947) do not trigger control-list classification.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acts as the central bank, the balance-of-payments statistical authority and the operator of the French TARGET2/T2 euro RTGS settlement infrastructure. The Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) is the prudential supervisor for French credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic-money institutions. The Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) administers the foreign-investment screening regime (IEF - investissements étrangers en France) under Articles L.151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, CMF), while EU capital-movement, sanctions and anti-money-laundering rules are applied at national level by French banks under CMF Articles L.561-1 et seq. and the AMLD5 framework.
