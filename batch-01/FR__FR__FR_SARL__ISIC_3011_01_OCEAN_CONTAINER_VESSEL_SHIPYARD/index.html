Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Capital and current account convertibility are unrestricted under Article 63 TFEU and the eurozone framework. France imposes no exchange-control authorization on ordinary trade transactions, profit repatriation, or non-sensitive equity/loan injections. The practical hurdles are documentary and administrative: balance-of-payments reporting by resident banks, EU sanctions/AML screening, foreign investment screening for sensitive sectors, and tax compliance for dividend distributions. A SARL may declare and pay dividends to non-resident shareholders after approval of audited annual accounts; withholding tax at domestic or treaty rate applies unless the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or an applicable double tax treaty reduces it. No convertibility restriction blocks remittance of profits in EUR or foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion obligation exists. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or foreign exchange indefinitely without a mandatory conversion ratio or time limit. Resident exporters may hold foreign-currency bank accounts in France; banks report transactions for balance-of-payments statistics. Physical cash carried over EUR 10,000 must be declared to customs under EU cash control rules, and underlying export documentation must be retained for at least six years.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the monetary and financial code; no separate foreign exchange administration exists in France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for secondary disbursements and low for core escrow/LC functions. Fintech EMIs are not credit institutions; they cannot offer true capital escrow accounts, maritime installment letters of credit, or advance payment bank guarantees. An EEA fintech EMI holding an ACPR passport may open a payment account and process SWIFT/SEPA transfers, but the French shipyard's treasury backbone must remain at a licensed credit institution. Legal constraints include the French Monetary and Financial Code, EMI-specific activity limitations, inability to extend credit or provide bank guarantees, and enhanced reputational risk in the medium-risk shipbuilding sector.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be made by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer to the SARL's operational or escrow account at the selected French bank. The remittance must clearly state the corporate beneficiary, the purpose code in the payment message, and an explicit description in field 70 such as 'INVESTISSEMENT DIRECT – APPORTS EN CAPITAL' for equity or 'NANTISSEMENT DE COMPTE – PRÊT ACTIONNAIRE' for shareholder loans. The French bank will verify the UBO structure, screen sanctions lists, request the share subscription agreement or promissory note, and report the transaction to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics. No prior exchange-control authorization is required for non-sensitive sectors; however, French foreign investment screening may apply if the shipyard qualifies as a protected sector, in which case prior notification or authorization to the Direction Générale du Trésor must be completed. Escrow accounts must be opened with a domestic credit institution to be recognized for capital release and shareholder protection.Outbound trade settlements for shipbuilding materials, equipment, and subcontractors should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA suppliers, SEPA credit transfers for EUR payments within the EEA, and commercial documentary letters of credit for maritime installment disbursements to overseas buyers or importers. Each disbursement requires matching the underlying contract schedule, Incoterms obligations, and transferable advance payment guarantee arrangements. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (IS) for fiscal years closed since 1 January 2022; reduced 15% rate applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs with turnover below EUR 10 million, subject to conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% as the domestic withholding tax on dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders. The 25% rate is reduced or eliminated under applicable double tax treaties and, within the EU, under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds a qualifying participation of at least 5% for at least two years.

Additional fines apply for missing schedules and electronic filing failures.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File apply where the French entity or group exceeds the EU SME exemption thresholds: more than 250 employees and either annual turnover above EUR 50 million or balance-sheet total above EUR 43 million. Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated turnover of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. Ordinary French corporate residence relies on effective management and core commercial activities in France. Substance is nevertheless indispensable for treaty benefits under the beneficial-owner test, the OECD Principal Purpose Test, and the French general anti-abuse rule. A shipyard SARL without genuine staff, physical yard, risk management, and commercial decision-making in France will be challenged as a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 of the French Tax Code, tax fraud is punishable by imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of EUR 250,000, with increases to seven years and EUR 500,000 in aggravating circumstances. Administrative penalties include a 40% bad-faith majoration or 80% penalty for concealment, plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally participate in or authorise fraudulent filings face criminal conviction, disqualification from management up to five years, publication of the judgment, and civil liability for the State's lost tax revenue. Corporate veil protection does not shield deliberate fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment with interest, 40% bad-faith majoration generally, 80% for abuse of law, and refusal to deduct expenses paid to non-cooperative jurisdictions. Incomplete transfer pricing documentation attracts autonomous penalties and a 5% penalty on any reassessment attributable to missing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally for penalties where they signed false returns or knowingly organised artificial transfer pricing structures. Criminal tax fraud charges can attach to cross-border profit stripping schemes and trigger travel bans and asset confiscation.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The French administrative anti-abuse rule (Art. L64 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales) allows recharacterisation of the entire arrangement; abusive schemes are subject to penalty of up to 80% plus interest. Treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary relief is denied for lack of beneficial ownership and substance.

Director personal liability: Directors of a sham company may be personally liable for the corporate tax obligations under faute de gestion. In insolvency, the court may impose comblement de passif, forcing directors to restore the company's liabilities. Criminal money laundering charges may follow where the conduit is used for fraudulent transfers.
Anti-Money Laundering / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the French Monetary and Financial Code, aggravated money laundering is punishable by ten years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 750,000. Violations of financial and trade embargoes may trigger seven years' imprisonment and EUR 350,000 fines. The vessel, corporate assets, and accounts are subject to confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to criminal prosecution for inadequately monitoring beneficial ownership or knowingly processing prohibited funds. Personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and disqualification from corporate office can be imposed. D&O insurance typically excludes intentional criminal conduct.
Late and Non-Filing / Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: Late filing majoration of 10%, increased to 40% after formal notice and 80% for hidden activity; late-payment interest of 0.20% per month; enforced recovery by avis de mise en recouvrement, bank seizure, and sale of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives who fail to pay corporate tax while orchestrating insolvency can be made personally liable by the commercial court. The manager may also face a travel ban, prohibition on managing a business, and civil liability for the resulting fiscal debt.
