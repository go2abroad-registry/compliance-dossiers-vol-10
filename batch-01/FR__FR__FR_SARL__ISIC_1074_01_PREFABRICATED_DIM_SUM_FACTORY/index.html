Virtual offices are not per se unlawful for a French SARL, but their use is strictly limited. The registered office may be domiciled with a licensed commercial domiciliation company or for certain cases at the manager's residence, but for an ISIC 1074 frozen dim sum and dumpling production factory the company must also maintain and declare a physical industrial plant address. Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) — https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; the underlying company register is the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) maintained with the competent commercial court registry.

Name reservation / lock period

France does not operate a separate statutory pre-incorporation name reservation register. Name availability is checked during the RCS/Guichet unique filing process. If the INPI/RCS registry rejects the proposed name, the applicant may submit a written recours gracieux to the relevant INPI/RCS service within 30 days of the notification, attaching the corrected name and supporting legal arguments; if the administrative refusal is maintained, a formal appeal (recours pour excès de pouvoir) may be filed before the competent tribunal administratif within two months of the refusal notification under Articles R421-1 and R421-2 of the French Code de justice administrative. Since no reservation or extension window exists, the applicant should immediately re-file with an alternative compliant name without abandoning the incorporation. A private trademark opposition, by contrast, must be resolved through the tribunal judiciaire in a separate civil action.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

There is no statutory minimum capital for a French SARL; the capital is freely fixed in the articles. Cash contributions must be deposited on a blocked account with a licensed bank or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and at least 20% of the subscribed cash value must be paid up at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. The bank blocking certificate is submitted with the incorporation file and released upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the local fire and rescue service (SDIS) covering emergency exits, fire compartments, extinguishing systems, and evacuation routes; structural load inspection for the building and freezer storage racks; electrical and gas installation conformity certificates; and occupational health clearance under the French Labour Code covering noise, cold stress, food allergens, and worker exposure prevention.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy and Finance; official portal: impots.gouv.fr; business creation and formalities portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SARL is formed electronically through the Guichet Unique (single business formalities portal) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. The registration generates the SIREN/SIRET identifier, and the DGFiP assigns the French intra-Community VAT number (FR plus 11-digit SIREN) after validation of the VAT-status declaration through the same portal; the number is then activated in VIES for intra-EU transactions. For export operations, the SARL must additionally obtain an EORI number via the customs administration. Non-resident shareholders require no prior authorization and there is no minimum paid-in capital for a SARL.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned SARL is formed electronically through the Guichet Unique (single business formalities portal) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. The registration generates the SIREN/SIRET identifier, and the DGFiP assigns the French intra-Community VAT number (FR plus 11-digit SIREN) after validation of the VAT-status declaration through the same portal; the number is then activated in VIES for intra-EU transactions. For export operations, the SARL must additionally obtain an EORI number via the customs administration. Non-resident shareholders require no prior authorization and there is no minimum paid-in capital for a SARL.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Under CGI Article 239A, a family SARL whose shares are held exclusively by natural persons connected by marriage, direct lineage or sibling relationship may elect to be fiscally transparent and taxed in the partners' hands by filing the relevant declaration with the service des impôts des entreprises. All other SARLs are mandatorily taxed at entity level to French corporate income tax. A French parent holding at least 95% of the SARL may elect group consolidation (intégration fiscale) under CGI Article 223A. France does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; hybrid-mismatch outcomes are neutralized mandatorily under the ATAD rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Under CGI Article 293B, the annual VAT small-business exemption threshold is €85,000 of turnover for supplies of goods (including industrialized frozen-food production) and €37,500 for services; once the applicable threshold is exceeded, the SARL must file a VAT registration application. An exporting manufacturer is nevertheless normally registered from inception in order to recover input VAT on plant, machinery and operating purchases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods sold by the SARL and transported outside the European Union by or on behalf of the seller are within the VAT export exemption (exonération à l'exportation) and are effectively zero-rated with full deduction of input VAT. To qualify, the SARL must prove that the goods physically left EU territory, that the export customs declaration has been accepted, and that the outward transportation and commercial documentation are consistent with the declared non-EU destination. Services directly linked to exported goods and services supplied to a non-EU recipient outside France are also outside French VAT under place-of-supply rules. The supporting evidence must be retained for audit review, ordinarily for six years.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/DAU lodged electronically in the DELTA export control system)
  • Export Movement Certificate or customs exit confirmation evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing transportation to a non-EU destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export customs declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 or other bank statement) showing export proceeds receipt and repatriation
  • Delivery terms and proof of transfer of ownership or risk to the foreign buyer

France is implementing a phased mandatory structured e-invoicing regime for all established VAT-payable persons. For domestic B2B transactions, e-invoices in Factur-X or UBL format must be issued through certified dematerialization platforms and/or the Public Invoicing Portal (Portail Public de Facturation), with full invoice-data transmission to DGFiP; the obligation is phased from 1 September 2026 for large businesses and 1 September 2027 for SMEs. Cross-border transactions must be reported through e-reporting (transaction-level data sent to DGFiP) using the same public/API interface. The system is not an Italian-style continuous fiscal-clearance stamping model, but it gives the administration near-real-time access to invoice flows and automated VAT-control scoring.

If DGFiP establishes during audit that the export evidence is false, incomplete or internally inconsistent, the export exemption is nullified and the SARL is reassessed at 20% VAT on the sale price, with late-payment interest under CGI Article 1727. The administration may also claw back input VAT deducted on the underlying export purchases. Where the failure is characterized as bad faith (manquement délibéré) or fraudulent concealment, an additional penalty of 40% to 80% under CGI Article 1729 applies; criminal exposure for intentional tax evasion may reach a fine of up to €75,000 and imprisonment of up to five years under Article 1741, with further publication measures in cases of aggravated or organized fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. The EUR is the currency; France has abolished all exchange-control approval requirements since 1 January 1990. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible pursuant to EU free-movement-of-capital rules (TFEU Articles 63-66), with no French statutory prior-approval requirement for inbound share-capital subscription, trade settlements, or profit remittance. Remaining controls: (i) balance-of-payments statistical declarations to Banque de France for any cross-border settlements aggregating above EUR 12,500 per calendar month (CMF Arts. R.152-1 to R.152-4), intermediated automatically by the collecting/remitting bank; (ii) cash-control declaration for physical movement of EUR 10,000 or more (EU Reg. 1889/2005); and (iii) DG Trésor prior-authorization screening for foreign direct investments in statutorily designated strategic sectors (Art. R.151-3 CMF). Industrial quick-freezing of dim sum and dumplings (ISIC 1074, NAICS 311412) is not a listed strategic sector, hence no IEF approval is required. Profit remittance: dividends may be freely paid to non-resident shareholders after the shareholders general meeting approves distribution, subject to the 30% French flat-tax dividend withholding (prélèvement forfaitaire unique) unless reduced by a tax treaty (e.g., France-China DTA: 10% on dividends for a qualifying corporate shareholder holding at least 25% of the capital).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or in freely held foreign-currency accounts opened with an ACPR-licensed French bank; there is no statutory timeline for repatriation or conversion. Operational compliance steps: (i) each export customs declaration (SAD/DAU) remains the operative document; (ii) the exporting company must provide the payment purpose or nature code to its bank on each incoming SWIFT transfer; (iii) the bank aggregates all receipts and automatically files the monthly Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration when the EUR 12,500 threshold is met; (iv) foreign-currency account balances are subject to no amount restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF, the French central bank; member of the Eurosystem/ESCB). Banking prudential supervision is delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution), while capital-account approval and foreign-investment screening (IEF) are administered by the French Treasury, Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor), under Articles L.151-1 et seq. and R.151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier, CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Rating: MEDIUM (complementary side rail only). ACPR-licensed FinTech/EMI players - including Qonto (licensed as an établissement de crédit), Shine (a Société Générale subsidiary), N26 and Revolut (EU passporting under EEA freedom-of-services) - accept SARL accounts with fast remote onboarding and SEPA/SWIFT disbursement. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs may not issue UCP600 documentary credits or act as confirming banks; (ii) most do not intermediate the Banque de France balance-of-payments declarations automatically in the same manner as tier-1 banks; (iii) the French beneficial-ownership transparency requirement (register of beneficial owners, CMF Art. L561-2-2) applies with equal force; and (iv) standing credit and FX derivative lines are generally unavailable. They are viable for payroll, sub-account waterfall disbursements, and intercompany sweeps, but not for the principal import-LC or capital escrow structure.

Legal constraints: (i) EMIs may not issue UCP600 documentary credits or act as confirming banks; (ii) most do not intermediate the Banque de France balance-of-payments declarations automatically in the same manner as tier-1 banks; (iii) the French beneficial-ownership transparency requirement (register of beneficial owners, CMF Art. L561-2-2) applies with equal force; and (iv) standing credit and FX derivative lines are generally unavailable. They are viable for payroll, sub-account waterfall disbursements, and intercompany sweeps, but not for the principal import-LC or capital escrow structure.
