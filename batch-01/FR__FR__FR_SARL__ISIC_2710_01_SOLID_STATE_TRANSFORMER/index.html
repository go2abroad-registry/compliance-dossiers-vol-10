Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — operational portal www.impots.gouv.fr; company creation via Guichet Unique administered by INPI; VAT registration and administration through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); non-established foreign taxpayers file through Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), Noisy-le-Grand.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SARL via the Guichet Unique (INPI) electronic portal, obtaining the SIREN and SIRET registration numbers and automatic notification to the DGFiP tax registry. Step 2: Apply for a French VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) by filing Form 4873-SD (Cerfa 15908) with the competent SIE; activation is normally issued with the statutory Kbis attestation. Step 3: For non-established foreign entities, file Form 5078-F (registration of a non-French taxable person) or Form 8474-SD for VAT representative appointment if no permanent establishment and no EU establishment; the SIEE allocates a SIRET with a temporary institutional segment and a French intra-EU VAT number. Step 4: Electronically transmit the VAT registration confirmation to the customs office of economic registration (via POL / DAE) for export privileges. Expected turnaround: 5–10 working days post-incorporation, longer for SIEE file review with apostilled foreign constitutional documents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in French law. A SARL is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the standard rates, except for a 'SARL de famille' exercising an ‘option for the personal income tax regime’ under Article 239 bis AA CGI, which is available only for eligible close-held family SARLs and is largely incompatible with institutional third-party investors. Options relevant to hybrid instruments: designation of equity versus debt status under Article 239 octies and formal documentation of hybrid branch mismatch arrangements under Article 209 III CGI transposing ATAD; no branch territorial election into a foreign transparent regime unless treated as a French permanent establishment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established SARL in the goods manufacturing sector, VAT registration is obligatory from the first taxable transaction with no de minimis turnover allowance. Deductibility franchise thresholds: €91,000 net annual turnover for goods activities to claim the base exemption (régime de franchise en base) — ineligible for an industrial manufacturer that invoices input VAT. Intra-EU distance sales (B2B/B2C) trigger French registration under the €10,000 European distance-sales threshold under Article 256-0 CGI. Non-established foreign suppliers generating French supply chains: €10,000 annual distance-sales threshold (global EU turnover) for OSS enrollment, and principal place of supply rules for B2B sales to French taxable persons; immediate registration required when the SARL is the importer of record on customs declarations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods outside the EU are zero-rated under Article 262-I-1° CGI provided that: (i) the goods are physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the supplier, on behalf of the supplier, or by a third-party carrier acting on the supplier’s account within the statutory export period; (ii) the SARL is the exporter of record and proof of departure from the EU customs territory is evidenced by the validated electronic Export Declaration (DAE) with the customs IT system (DELTA / POL); (iii) the simultaneous condition of direct transfer of property and actual effect of the sale to the non-EU customer is documented; (iv) payment is received by an authorized FX channel and booked to a registered business bank account in the SARL’s name; (v) the exporter holds the commercial records proving the goods have left the union within 90 days of the release for export (extendable to a reasonable period in justified cases with freight delay customs annex). Export services and related supply of transformer installation services onto third-country sites are zero-rated under Article 262-III-3°C (supplies directly linked to export and associated services closely linked to export, subject to the freight export evidence).

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE) — EX-A or EX-A-CO type accepted with the Movement Reference Number (MRN) transmitted by DELTA/POL; customs invoice classified under tariff heading 8504 (and 8537 for switchgear) with correct Schedule B / TARIC nomenclature
  • Commercial export invoice and packing list (in English) showing Incoterms, unit prices, total invoice value, and ISIC 2710 product description as solid-state power transformer
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing loading and arrival at non-EU port of discharge for maritime shipments; CMR consignment note for exemption from VAT on subsequent transport and insurance services (Article 262-III-3°C)
  • Evidence of payment and FX repatriation: SWIFT MT103 / documentary letter of credit negotiation advice demonstrating receipt of proceeds in the SARL’s French bank account
  • Customs broker declaration and proof of exporter status (registered EORI number with the French customs authority)
  • Certificate of origin (optional but recommended to substantiate tariff preference claims under FTAs and bank-requested documents pursuant to LC terms)

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing in France phases in under law 2023-1322 (anti-fraud provisions): by 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises, and by 1 September 2027 for all taxable persons (including SMEs). Invoices must be transmitted using a Dematerialization Partner (PDP) connected to the Public Invoicing Portal (PPF) via the Chorus Pro system. The PPF performs structured data collection, cross-checking, and VAT clearance. API requirements: mandatory use of the Factur-X (hybrid PDF), UBL, or CII syntaxes; structured invoice fields must include VAT identification, B2B customer SIREN/SIRET validation, legal characteristic of the operation, and invoice-level class codes. No physical fiscal stamping applies; instead, the impots.gouv.fr portal and FNFE (Fichier National des Factures Électroniques) repository provide the digital clearance mechanism with real-time transmission obligations and a 72-hour transmission window from invoicing event.

Export zero-rating claims are audited against the DEM-application within the EU VAT refund window and the 3-year customs time-bar (Articles 73–84 Union Customs Code). False export declarations or failure to prove effective exit trigger: (i) retrospective VAT repayment at the headline rate as at the date of the unduly zero-rated transaction; (ii) impossibility claim of VAT input deduction on the underlying export contracts; (iii) applying the 80% ‘de mauvaise foi’ increase on amounts evaded under Article 1729-b CGI and 10% late-payment interest under Article 1727 CGI (0.20% per month cap 10%); (iv) penal surcharge of 40% if lack of declaration; (v) criminal prosecution for fiscal fraud under Articles 1741–1743 CGI — up to €500,000 fine and 5 years’ imprisonment for sophisticated export dummy destination schemes; additionally, revocation of the exporter’s EORI license and 100% denial of export privilege on subsequent transactions, plus joint financial liability of the customs representative (déclavant en compte d’autrui) if complicit. Mandatory annual EURESIA Tax Audit Certificate is required where exported turnover exceeds EUR 33 million under the corporate income tax audit framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full Eurozone convertibility jurisdiction. Legal tender is the euro and the capital account is fully liberalised under EU TFEU Articles 63–66, with no French exchange-control permit for lawful cross-border investment by or into a French SARL. Current-account items such as dividends, royalties, commissions, interest and technical service fees are remittable without prior administrative approval, provided corporate tax residence documentation, transfer pricing documentation, and anti-money-laundering source-of-wealth checks are completed. The only operational obstacle is banking KYC, the beneficial-ownership register, and the balance-of-payments reporting requirement: every French resident cross-border flow at or above EUR 50,000 must be identified by the appointed bank under an agreed Banque de France BOP classification code. Profit remittances require distributable reserves verified by the statutory auditor or certified accounts; withholding tax on French dividends (25% standard rate) is reduced by applicable double-taxation treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds or to convert foreign currency into euro, because France has no exchange surrender or compulsory conversion regime. Export receipts may be retained in USD, CNY or other freely convertible currencies in an account held in France or in another EU/EEA jurisdiction, subject to French balance-of-payments reporting. If the cross-border trade-in-goods payment is below EUR 50,000, no special declaration is required. At or above EUR 50,000, the settlement bank assigns the BOP economic code for merchandise exports, and the remitter must provide a commercial invoice, transport documentation, and the customs export declaration (SAD/ENS). Where the payment resides in a non-euro currency, the SARL may request conversion to EUR at the bank's intraday rate; no surrender law applies. The SARL should include in each SWIFT instruction a clear reference such as 'export of solid-state transformer equipment under ISIC 2710' to expedite BOP classification.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) on exchange control, balance-of-payments statistics, and prudential compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for operational treasury, LOW for documentary trade finance. A fintech e-money institution licensed by the ACPR under the French Monetary and Financial Code or passported from another EU authority can provide multi-currency IBANs, payment accounts, SEPA and SWIFT wire disbursement for routine supplier payments. However, an EMI cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, accept term deposits, extend real credit lines, or execute derivative FX hedging, because it is not a credit institution. For high-value solid-state transformer export/import settlements, LC issuance and complex liquidity management, a fully licensed French commercial bank remains mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan capital must be wired to the French SARL's dedicated capital escrow or operational account using a French IBAN (FR76 + BIC) and SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA transfers. The payment instruction must state the Banque de France BOP direct-investment category and the explicit purpose phrase: 'APPORT EN COMPTE COURANT D'ASSOCIE' for shareholder loans or 'APPORT DE CAPITAL SOCIAL' for equity. The bank will require the updated KBis, dated contribution deed, shareholder resolution, UBO declaration, proof of source of funds, and confirmation of the bank's conversion rate if the remittance is in a currency other than euro. Funds may only be deployed after the capital increase has been registered and a creditor notice period under French law, if applicable, is fulfilled.Outbound supplier payments in the EU are cleared through SEPA Credit Transfer with the beneficiary's IBAN. Non-EU supplier payments, local freight, spare parts and warranty disbursements use the SWIFT MT103 with the correct BOP trade code and the counterparty bank BIC. For large-scale transformer component purchases, the primary clearing rail is an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit issued by the French bank via SWIFT MT700 and amended through MT707. The UCP600 and ISBP rules govern document presentation; settlement occurs by the issuing bank paying the nominated bank through SWIFT MT103 on presentation of compliant documents. Standby LC and bank guarantees are issued via SWIFT MT760. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for operational treasury, LOW for documentary trade finance. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard rate from fiscal year 2022 onward; reduced 15% rate applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for a qualifying small-and-medium enterprise with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 10,000,000 (CGI art. 219 I).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% on gross dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders in the absence of treaty or EU protection (CGI arts. 119 bis and 187); 0% for EU/EEA corporate shareholders qualifying under the Parent–Subsidiary Directive (arts. 145 and 216 CGI, direct 10% or 25% holding maintained for two years, 95% exemption with 5% reattribution); typical treaty rates: 15% general United States rate, 5% where the US parent owns at least 10% of voting power, 15% general United Kingdom rate, 0% for UK companies controlling at least 25% of the paying company under substance conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full mandatory documentation (local file, master file and country-by-country report) applies where (i) the French entity's annual turnover or gross balance-sheet assets equal or exceed EUR 400,000,000, or (ii) the French entity is owned, directly or indirectly, more than 50% by a group entity meeting that threshold (art. L.13 AA LPF; BOI-BIC-BASE-40-10-40). Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory where the consolidated group revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000, with local submission of form 2258-SD. For a manufacturing SARL below these thresholds, contemporaneous light documentation (intercompany agreements, benchmarks for the sale of transformers to related distributors, margin analysis at 8-12% cost-plus or TNMM) must nevertheless be maintained for audit defense.

Economic substance scope: France is not an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction in the offshore sense, but applies equivalent substance doctrines: siège de direction effective (art. 8 CGI) for tax residence; the abuse-of-law procedure (abus de droit, art. L.64 LPF); the general anti-abuse rule for treaty benefits (art. 205A CGI); the beneficial-ownership requirement of the OECD Model and EU Directives; and the anti-artificial-arrangement rule of the Parent–Subsidiary Directive (art. 119 ter CGI). A French SARL manufacturing high-voltage transformers and switchgear from a real French plant with employed industrial staff, inventory and Capex maintains valid economic substance; a letterbox SARL without personnel or premises will be treated as an artificial entity, denied treaty benefits, and reassessed at the 30% domestic withholding on all distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Civil: reassessment d'office of the concealed profit, penalty of 40% for bad faith or 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres (arts. 1729 and 1732 CGI), and 0.20% per-month late-payment interest; the 3-year reopening period is extended to 6 years in cases of abuse. Criminal: up to 5 years' imprisonment and EUR 75,000 fine under art. 1741 CGI; aggravated fraud (organized gang) carries 7 years and EUR 100,000; the court may order confiscation, publication of judgment, and deprivation of civic, civil and family rights.

Director personal liability: The gérant may be declared personally liable for the company's tax debt and penalties when the administrative court finds manœuvres frauduleuses or inobservation grave et répétée des obligations fiscales (art. L.267 LPF); a criminal conviction may impose travel bans (interdiction de quitter le territoire), prohibition on managing a business (faillite personnelle), freezing of personal assets, and joint civil restitution of the amounts evaded.
