Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), French Public Finances Directorate; official portal: impots.gouv.fr. VAT and corporate tax registration for businesses is administered through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); customs export and import controls are administered by Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a standard SARL, statutory registration is completed at incorporation through formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, automatically generating SIREN/SIRET legal identifiers and an active corporate tax account with DGFiP. The DGFiP allocates the intra-Community VAT number when the entity begins or plans taxable French supplies. A non-established foreign entity must file a French VAT registration dossier with the competent SIE containing certified incorporation documents, proof of foreign tax residence, bank details, description of intended French operations, and a fiscal representative’s power of attorney if required. Upon approval, the entity receives a numéro de TVA intracommunautaire and must separately obtain an EORI number from DGDDI for customs export declaration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL is a capital company subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on its worldwide profits from actual French operations; profits of foreign permanent establishments may be excluded under France’s territoriality rule. For VAT, the SARL is a separate taxable person in its own right. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SARL is a capital company subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on its worldwide profits from actual French operations; profits of foreign permanent establishments may be excluded under France’s territoriality rule. For VAT, the SARL is a separate taxable person in its own right. A non-resident owner does not alter the entity’s default legal-form-based classification.

Available election forms

France does not apply US-style check-the-box elections; legal form determines tax treatment. Statutory elections available include: (i) a qualifying small SARL may opt for pass-through taxation under personal income tax for a limited period under Article 239 bis AA of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI); (ii) a 95% parent company may elect group tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI; (iii) financially integrated entities may elect VAT group treatment as a single taxable person under Article 1693 ter CGI; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under the ATAD implementation prevent mismatched deduction outcomes but do not provide elective entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 annual taxable turnover for French-established suppliers of goods and EUR 37,500 for service suppliers under the franchise en base de TVA (small-business VAT exemption); non-established foreign suppliers have no de minimis threshold and must register for French VAT before making taxable supplies in France unless the transaction is reverse-charged to a French VAT-registered customer or falls under the OSS/IOSS special schemes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: 0% French VAT applies to supplies physically exported outside France and the EU, provided the seller has the power to dispose of the goods, transport is arranged by the seller or on behalf of the foreign customer, and the goods leave EU customs territory under a completed DGDDI export declaration (EX). Services sufficiently linked to exported goods (e.g., loading, transport, ancillary handling) or performed on foreign immovable property may also be zero-rated; B2B cross-border services to a non-EU recipient generally have place of supply in the customer’s country and are invoiced outside French VAT. Intra-EU B2B dispatches are VAT-exempt in France subject to customer VAT-ID validation and EC Sales List reporting. Refund of residual input VAT or recovery through French VAT returns requires matching export invoices to customs evidence.

  • Customs export declaration (EX / déclaration en douane) accepted by DGDDI
  • Transport evidence: bill of lading, CMR, airway bill or equivalent international consignment note
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank SWIFT confirmation of payment received from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract with clearly identified foreign customer
  • Packing list and, where applicable, insurance certificate evidencing physical movement outside the EU

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions is phased in by law: large taxpayers from 1 September 2026 and small/medium taxpayers from 1 September 2027 via accredited Tax Dematerialisation Providers (PDPs) connected to the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF). Invoices must be transmitted in CII, UBL or FACTUR-X formats. There is no real-time pre-clearance stamp; e-reporting is transmitted periodically to DGFiP, while exchange data and transaction-status metadata must be preserved for audit.

French tax administration may re-invoice VAT on export if documentation does not substantiate physical exit within the required period or if payments are not repatriated by the 180-day deadline. On audit, DGFiP verifies EX data against manifests and bank statements; denial of zero-rating generates a VAT assessment plus late-payment interest and administrative penalties (40% for insufficient declarations; 80% for deliberate dissimulation or bad faith under Articles 1728–1729 CGI). Fraudulent export-credit claims or sham export schemes also carry criminal exposure under Article 1741 CGI and customs-fraud provisions, including heavy fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax-reporting, and prudential controls. A French SARL may receive foreign investment, hold foreign-currency accounts, pay non-resident suppliers, receive export proceeds, distribute dividends, repay shareholder loans, and remit profits without prior foreign-exchange approval. Dividend and interest remittances remain subject to corporate-law requirements, distributable-profit and reserve rules, withholding-tax analysis, beneficial-owner verification, treaty eligibility, bank compliance review, and documentation evidencing the underlying transaction. Banks may delay or reject transactions involving sanctioned parties, high-risk jurisdictions, opaque ownership, inconsistent commercial rationale, or inadequate source-of-funds evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory mandatory surrender requirement, no general requirement to convert export proceeds into euros, and no prescribed conversion ratio or universal statutory deadline for repatriating export receipts. Export proceeds may remain in euros or foreign currency and may be credited to accounts in France or abroad, subject to accounting, tax, customs, sanctions, AML, and bank-reporting obligations. The company should retain contracts, invoices, customs declarations, transport evidence, payment confirmations, and reconciliation records. Large or unusual cross-border payments may be subject to bank information requests and applicable statistical, tax, or regulatory reporting.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank. France has no separate foreign-exchange administration imposing general exchange controls on ordinary corporate transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection, card acquiring, SEPA transfers, and limited multi-currency balances; Low to Medium as a substitute for a principal French relationship bank. An EU-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution may passport services into France, but it is not a bank and generally cannot accept deposits or provide unrestricted credit. Account safeguarding, payment-services terms, transaction limits, settlement timing, chargeback exposure, sanctions screening, and fund-freezing rights must be reviewed. Offshore digital EMIs outside the EEA may require an EU-authorised intermediary, local licensing, or a regulated correspondent arrangement and should not be used to evade French or EU AML, sanctions, tax, or payment-reporting requirements. Stripe and similar providers should be treated as payment-service and acquiring channels, not as the company's sole treasury bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder equity through a regulated bank to the SARL's designated capital-deposit account before incorporation or capital increase, using the exact legal entity name and a clear payment reference such as 'capital contribution' or 'capital increase'. For a post-incorporation contribution, transfer funds to the operational account only after the required corporate resolutions and filings. Route shareholder or intercompany debt under a signed loan agreement with principal, maturity, interest, repayment, transfer-pricing, and withholding-tax terms. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments and SWIFT cross-border transfers for non-SEPA currencies. Provide the bank with the subscription or loan agreement, corporate resolutions, source-of-funds evidence, ownership chart, and expected-use schedule. There is no general French statutory capital purpose-code regime; banks may require internal purpose codes or ISO 20022 purpose information. Reconcile the bank receipt to the accounting ledger and retain payment proof, especially where funds originate outside the EEA.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro supplier payments and SWIFT customer-credit transfers, commonly represented operationally by an MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for non-SEPA payments. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000 (no statutory transfer pricing documentation obligation for the automatic Local File/Master File where annual related-party transactions remain below EUR 400,000, but the French tax administration can request documentation on taxpayer initiative and penalties apply for incomplete documentation)

Economic substance scope: France applies substantial economic substance and anti-abuse doctrines, including the abuse of law procedure (procédure de l'abus de droit), the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR), the principal purpose test under DAC6, and the French subsidiary economic substance requirement for treaty benefits; for the AI robotic pet sector, a French SARL must demonstrate operational substance through local R&D, employment of qualified engineers, actual product design or assembly oversight, physical premises, and active management decisions; failure to demonstrate substance for tax resident status or treaty relief triggers re-characterisation of arrangements and denial of tax treaty benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Fine of EUR 500,000 (or up to double the amount evaded) and imprisonment of up to 7 years; confiscation of assets; administrative penalties of up to 80% of the understated tax plus daily surcharges and publication of the sanction

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable to pay the corporate tax arrears through the abuse of law doctrine and directors' tort liability (faute de gestion); criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and travel bans; French courts can disallow the separate personality of the SARL (actio pauliana) where assets were fraudulently diverted
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Mandatory documentation penalties of up to 5% of the gross amount of the undocumented transfers or EUR 10,000, plus adjustive tax reassessments, late interest and 40% penalties for lack of good faith or 80% for serious misrepresentation

Director personal liability: Directors who approved distorted transfer prices may face personal liability for restitution of tax damage caused to the SARL, disqualification from management functions, civil damages under corporate law, and criminal sanctions in cases of deliberate misstatement
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangements and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterisation of the entity under DAC6 and ATAD rules, reclassification of profits as constructive dividends subject to withholding tax, penalty of up to 100% of the re-characterised tax advantage, and nullification of legal acts under abuse of law procedure with interest

Director personal liability: Personal liability for abuse of law where directors knowingly executed sham arrangements; foreign directors face immobility measures, seizure of assets located in France, and potential extradition for criminal tax fraud if accomplice liability is established
Money Laundering (AML-Bloomberg FATF Compliance)

Statutory sanction: Under the French Monetary and Financial Code, fines up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for laundering; Tracfin referrals and asset forfeiture; suspension of banking licences or prohibition from accepting payments via Stripe/credit cards

Director personal liability: Corporate directors and compliance officers are personally subject to criminal liability for failure to establish AML compliance, mandatory reporting of suspicious transactions, and beneficial ownership transparency (RBE); non-resident directors may be prohibited from opening a bank account in France and removed from corporate registry
Dividend or Interest Withholding Tax Evasion and Profit Repatriation without DTA Protocol

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax assessments, 40% to 80% penalties for bad faith or intentional concealment, late payment interest of 0.20% per month, plus joint and several liability of the paying company for the unreported withholding tax

Director personal liability: Managers who knowingly fail to withhold and remit dividends tax are personally liable for the tax under article 1754 of the French General Tax Code; criminal conviction can extend to directors' personal bank accounts and real estate through asset seizure and restrictive travel bans
