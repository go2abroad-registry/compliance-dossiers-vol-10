Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SARL share capital is freely fixed in the Articles — no statutory minimum (may be as low as EUR 1). Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and at least one-fifth paid up at incorporation, with the balance due within five years. The paid-up funds must be blocked in escrow (attestation de blocage des fonds) at a French bank or notary until issuance of the Kbis. In-kind contributions must be paid up in full at subscription and assessed by a contributions auditor (commissaire aux apports) unless unanimously dispensed by the shareholders.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the commission de sécurité (SDIS prefecture inspection); structural load inspection by an approved technical control bureau; occupational workplace clearance by DREETS/Inspection du travail, including the mandatory DUERP, CARSAT registration, medical fitness surveillance via the service de prévention et de santé au travail, and electrical installation verification per NF C 15-100.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – impots.gouv.fr; professional portal: pro.impots.gouv.fr; corporate tax filings via EDI/EFI and online services.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity forms a French SARL by registering with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). INSEE then issues the SIREN/SIRET identifiers. The company is automatically allocated a French intra-community VAT number for EU transactions; the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) assigns the definitive domestic VAT identification number. Non-EU companies without an EU establishment must appoint a fiscal representative and may use the simplified VAT registration procedure.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity forms a French SARL by registering with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). INSEE then issues the SIREN/SIRET identifiers. The company is automatically allocated a French intra-community VAT number for EU transactions; the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) assigns the definitive domestic VAT identification number. Non-EU companies without an EU establishment must appoint a fiscal representative and may use the simplified VAT registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box. An SARL may elect to be taxed at the shareholder level as a pass-through under Article 239 bis AA of the French Tax Code (FTC), subject to eligibility (turnover ≤ €875,000 for trading activities, management held by individuals, less than 50 registered partners). Otherwise it is definitively subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) is available for groups holding at least 95% of subsidiaries. Hybrid mismatch structures are constrained by ATAD transposition, DAC6 reporting, and French anti-hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold for French-established taxable persons; mandatory VAT registration applies from the first euro of taxable sales. The small-business exemption ('franchise en base de TVA') applies to French-established businesses with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services, and may be waived at the taxpayer's option.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported outside the European Union are VAT zero-rated under Articles 262-I and 269 of the French Tax Code, provided title has passed to an overseas customer and the goods are physically evidenced as leaving the EU within 90 days. Certain ancillary services to exports, and services deemed supplied outside France to non-EU business customers under the B2B place-of-supply rules, may also qualify for exemption with credit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane – DAU via DELTA G/ELISE)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR or equivalent transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice and export contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation where payment evidence is requested

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing will be phased in from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for SMEs; e-invoices must be emitted and transmitted via a certified dematerialization platform (PDP) or through the public fiscal portal, with e-reporting for B2C and cross-border transactions. B2G invoices are issued via the Chorus Pro platform. No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is currently in force.

If the exporter cannot present valid export proof within the statutory retention period, the DGFiP reassesses 20% VAT on the export proceeds, plus late interest at 0.2% per month, and applies penalties: 10% for non-deliberate inaccuracies, 40% for deliberate concealment or bad faith, and 80% for fraudulent schemes or abuse of law. Over-claimed input VAT credits are clawed back with interest, and the statute of limitations is extended to 10 years in cases of zero-rating fraud. Customs offenses may also carry criminal fines up to EUR 1,500,000 and confiscation of goods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member, an IMF Article VIII jurisdiction and a party to the EU capital-movements liberalisation set out in Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The euro is fully convertible in both current-account and capital-account transactions. Dividend declarations and profit remittances to shareholders, including non-residents, require no prior exchange-control approval; the conditions are the existence of positive distributable reserves shown in the approved annual accounts, a negative balance-sheet solvency test, payment of any applicable dividend withholding tax (French domestic source rate of 25% unless reduced by a double-tax treaty), and provision of standard KYC/AML documentation. Share capital contributions, shareholder loans and trade payments may be freely converted into EUR at commercial rates. The principal practical obstacles are procedural: enhanced due diligence on beneficial owners, proof of source of funds, EU sanctions compliance, the EU travel-rule information requirements for wire transfers under Regulation (EU) 2015/847 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2023/1113, and foreign-investment screening in the narrow set of strategic sectors under Decree No. 2014-479 and Articles L.151-3 et seq. of the Code monétaire et financier. Surgical laparoscopy tower manufacturing does not ordinarily trigger FDI screening unless combined with critical health-technology or dual-use activities, but the onboarding bank must nevertheless verify that no proposed investment falls within a regulated sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory-conversion regime for export proceeds. Since the liberalisation of French exchange-control regulations, a resident exporting SARL is free to invoice in EUR or a foreign currency, to retain foreign-currency export receipts in a foreign-currency account (compte en devises) with a French authorised bank, and to decide whether and when to convert into EUR. There is no statutory conversion timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. The applicable duties are documentary and statistical rather than monetary: every inward payment must be evidenced by the underlying commercial invoice, transport document and customs declaration; the receiving bank must report the payment to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics; and physical cash crossing the EU border of EUR 10,000 or more must be declared to the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672. In practice, a French bank may require immediate conversion of foreign-currency proceeds only where the company has separately agreed with the bank to manage currency risk, and never under a statutory mandate.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) is the central bank and the authority that, together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy and Finance), administers the liberalised foreign-exchange regime and balance-of-payments statistics. The Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) licenses and supervises French credit institutions, payment institutions and electronic money institutions for banking, AML/CTF and conduct-of-business compliance, while TRACFIN receives suspicious-transaction reports. For foreign-exchange policy purposes, the Banque de France is the institution to which banks file statistical declarations on direct investment and cross-border payment flows.
