Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) held by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered office jurisdiction; electronic filings processed via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.223-1 to L.223-43 (SARL), Article L.123-35 et seq. (Guichet Unique formalities), Articles R.123-1 et seq. (RCS registration); Ordonnance n°2021-1389 and Décret n°2022-946 implementing the electronic single window.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'une société (standardized digital incorporation declaration replacing the former M0 dossier) filed through the Guichet Unique, accompanied by the statuts, share capital deposit certificate, legal announcement attestation, and the beneficial owner declaration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 24 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for SARL (Code de commerce, art. L.223-2; a de facto minimum of one euro applies). The capital must be fully subscribed at formation; cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% upon subscription and deposited in a dedicated blocked account (compte bloqué) opened with a French bank or consigned with a notary through the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the deposit certificate accompanies the RCS filing and the remaining 80% falls due within five years of registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: maintain the registre de sécurité and obtain the applicable ERP or workplace occupancy clearance from the fire prevention commission where the premises receive employees or the public; electrical installation conformity (CONSUEL attestation) for new fit-outs; annual verification of storage racks under Code du travail art. R.4323-72; structural load validation by a certified engineer for mezzanines or racking systems; workplace compliance inspection under Code du travail art. L.4211-1 et seq., with CARSAT notification where applicable.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for SARL (Code de commerce, art. L.223-2; a de facto minimum of one euro applies). The capital must be fully subscribed at formation; cash contributions must be paid up to at least 20% upon subscription and deposited in a dedicated blocked account (compte bloqué) opened with a French bank or consigned with a notary through the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the deposit certificate accompanies the RCS filing and the remaining 80% falls due within five years of registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) of the French Ministry of Economy and Finance; principal taxpayer portal impots.gouv.fr; business incorporation and SIREN/SIRET administrative formalities administered via the INPI Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; VAT registration, CA3 filing, and VAT identification handled through the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE).

E-government filing portal

For a resident SARL: (1) file incorporation through the INPI Guichet Unique; (2) INSEE assigns the 9-digit SIREN and the SIRET establishment identifier and automatically communicates these to DGFiP; (3) DGFiP attributes the French intra-community VAT number (format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN) upon completion of registration and validation of the file; the entity must be registered without delay from the effective date of taxable activity. For a non-established foreign entity: (1) an EU-established operator may register directly, while a non-EU operator must appoint an ad hoc joint-and-severally liable VAT fiscal representative under CGI Art. 289A; (2) submit the non-resident VAT identification application (cerfa n° 1590-DUE or the harmonized foreign-branch registration form via the Guichet Unique) together with the SIE file evidencing the French taxable transactions; (3) upon approval, obtain the 13-character French VAT ID and thereafter file monthly or quarterly CA3 returns electronically, comply with e-invoicing/e-reporting obligations via the Portail Public de Facturation or an accredited PDP, and maintain a transfer-pricing and documentary file in French.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS): reduced flat rate of 15% up to €42,500 of annual taxable profit for Small and Medium Enterprises meeting the statutory size and shareholding conditions, and the standard rate of 25% on the excess; the entity is also a VAT taxable person and remains liable to the territorial economic contribution (CET: CFE plus CVAE, the latter being progressively phased out, and effectively eliminated from 2024) and to the annual 3% levy on distributions where the CGI Art. 235 ter ZC size, turnover, and paid-up-capital conditions are met. A French permanent establishment of a non-resident corporation is taxed on a net-basis at the standard IS rate and, additionally, bears the new equalization minimum tax of 25% on profits deemed distributed unless retained and reinvested for at least two years (CGI Art. 209 al. 4 as amended by the Loi de finances 2023). — For a resident SARL: (1) file incorporation through the INPI Guichet Unique; (2) INSEE assigns the 9-digit SIREN and the SIRET establishment identifier and automatically communicates these to DGFiP; (3) DGFiP attributes the French intra-community VAT number (format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN) upon completion of registration and validation of the file; the entity must be registered without delay from the effective date of taxable activity. For a non-established foreign entity: (1) an EU-established operator may register directly, while a non-EU operator must appoint an ad hoc joint-and-severally liable VAT fiscal representative under CGI Art. 289A; (2) submit the non-resident VAT identification application (cerfa n° 1590-DUE or the harmonized foreign-branch registration form via the Guichet Unique) together with the SIE file evidencing the French taxable transactions; (3) upon approval, obtain the 13-character French VAT ID and thereafter file monthly or quarterly CA3 returns electronically, comply with e-invoicing/e-reporting obligations via the Portail Public de Facturation or an accredited PDP, and maintain a transfer-pricing and documentary file in French.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SARL is by default subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS): reduced flat rate of 15% up to €42,500 of annual taxable profit for Small and Medium Enterprises meeting the statutory size and shareholding conditions, and the standard rate of 25% on the excess; the entity is also a VAT taxable person and remains liable to the territorial economic contribution (CET: CFE plus CVAE, the latter being progressively phased out, and effectively eliminated from 2024) and to the annual 3% levy on distributions where the CGI Art. 235 ter ZC size, turnover, and paid-up-capital conditions are met. A French permanent establishment of a non-resident corporation is taxed on a net-basis at the standard IS rate and, additionally, bears the new equalization minimum tax of 25% on profits deemed distributed unless retained and reinvested for at least two years (CGI Art. 209 al. 4 as amended by the Loi de finances 2023).

Available election forms

Available elections and mandatory markers under French law: (1) CGI Art. 239 bis AA option allowing a qualifying small family SARL - fully paid-up capital and a restricted natural-person family shareholder base - to be taxed as a pass-through partnership-style entity (régime des sociétés de personnes) for a fixed period of five consecutive fiscal years, after which IS taxation resumes; (2) CGI Art. 223 A election enabling a 95%-owned French tax group to consolidate IS liabilities through a société mère intégrée; (3) ATAD anti-hybrid notification and neutralization regime implemented under CGI Arts. 205, 209, and 1649 AD serving as mandatory filing and electivity markers for hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatch arrangements, including DAC6 cross-border arrangement disclosures; (4) no check-the-box election exists in French domestic law — classification as transparent or entity-level is statutory and derives from the CGI and Book IX accounting rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For deliveries of goods by a French-established distributor the annual franchise en base de TVA exemption ceiling is €85,000 of turnover (CGI Art. 293 B), with a majoration tolerance of €93,500 for two consecutive years; below this ceiling the SARL may refrain from registering and from charging VAT, but it also forfeits input VAT recovery. Above the franchise ceiling VAT registration is compulsory under the simplified or normal real-regime returns. Non-established foreign suppliers have no equivalent turnover franchise: registration is required by reference to the first taxable supply in France, subject to the €10,000 EU-wide annual distance-sales threshold (Union OSS regime) for EU-established sellers of goods to French final consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported to a destination outside the European Union qualify for zero rating (exonération, equivalent to 0% VAT) under CGI Art. 262-I-1° provided (i) the sale transfers the power of disposal over the goods to the non-EU customer, (ii) the goods are dispatched out of the EU within 12 months of the sale per BOI-TVA-CHAMP-40-20-10, and (iii) the supplier retains full documentary proof of export and physical exit. Intra-EU dispatches of goods to a customer holding a valid EU VAT ID qualify for zero rating as intra-community supplies under CGI Art. 262 ter-1, subject to validation of the customer's VAT number through VIES and filing of the recapitulative statement (Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens, DEB). Offshore services supplied to non-EU customers are outside the scope of French VAT where B2B place-of-supply rules located the service at the customer's non-French establishment (CGI Arts. 259 A and 259 D); however, services relating to tangible goods located in France or services actually performed in France remain subject to French VAT regardless of the customer's residence.

  • French customs electronic export declaration (Déclaration en douane DELTA/Z or DPE) evidencing the EX1 status and the EU exit date
  • Transport document proving physical export dispatch: Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR consignment note (road), Air Waybill (air), or rail waybill
  • Commercial invoice and packing list evidencing the underlying sale, quantity, unit values, and consignment particulars
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT statement proving the receipt and crediting of export proceeds to the French exporting SARL, supporting the FX repatriation benchmark and transfer-pricing documentation
  • For intra-EU dispatches: validated EU customer VAT ID (VIES certificate) and the recapitulative DEB statement; also the transaction-level e-reporting records transmitted via PPF/PDP for customs statistics

National structured e-invoicing mandate phased under the Loi de finances 2023 reform: from 1 September 2026 all French-established taxable persons must be able to receive structured electronic invoices (Factur-X, EN 16931-conformant UBL, or CII format), and large enterprises and ETIs must issue them; from 1 September 2027 SMEs and micro-entities must also issue structured e-invoices. Issuance and transmission must run through an accredited Partenaire de Dématérialisation (PDP) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF), which replaces Chorus Pro. The e-invoice data-set must be delivered to the PPF/PDP within 15 days of invoice issue, and parallel e-reporting of cross-border transaction data and payment data must be transmitted to DGFiP. France does not operate a pre-issue electronic authorization or digital stamping model; the mechanism is interoperable transmission and centralized tax-data warehousing followed by audit inspection rather than real-time fiscal clearance.

The taxpayer is exposed to the ordinary 3-year statutory reassessment window (LPF Art. L169), extended to 10 years for international transactions and operations involving entities established abroad under LPF Art. L169 B. If zero rating is refused because export documentation is missing, inconsistent, or goods are shown never to have left the French/EU customs territory, VAT is recomputed at 20% with retroactive effect, previously recovered input VAT is clawed back, and late interest accrues at 0.20% per month. Applicable penalties escalate from 40% for material under-declaration (CGI Art. 1731), to 80% for deliberate concealment (CGI Art. 1729), and to 100% for fraudulent maneuvers (CGI Art. 1732). Criminal tax-fraud prosecution under CGI Art. 1741 may result in up to five years imprisonment and a fine of up to €500,000 for fictitious or sham export schemes, coupled with customs smuggling penalties up to three years imprisonment under Code des douanes Art. 414 and joint-and-several civil liability of the management and the appointed French VAT fiscal representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible and the capital account is completely liberalised under Articles 63-66 TFEU. There is no national exchange-control authorisation for trade payments, profit repatriation, equity injections, intercompany loans or divestment proceeds. Current-account transactions (goods, services, royalties) are unrestricted; capital-account transactions (equity, loans, guarantees) are equally unrestricted but carry statistical declaration duties to Banque de France. Profit remittances by a French SARL to non-resident shareholders are free from exchange-control approval but remain subject to French withholding tax at the statutory 25% on dividends (Article 119 bis CGI), with full release at 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate shareholders holding at least 10% (2-year holding condition), and 0-15% treaty relief for other treaty jurisdictions; the underlying SARL profit is taxed at the 25% standard French corporate income tax rate (15% reduced rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs). Practical hurdles are documentary only: balance-of-payments reporting, UBO filing in the RCS, source-of-funds evidence and EU/UN sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio exist in France. Export proceeds from the distribution of MLCCs and resistors may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency bank accounts (comptes de devises) held with French authorised credit institutions, or converted freely into EUR or other currencies. No statutory timeline requires repatriation or conversion. The only binding obligations are statistical and customs-related: cross-border payments or collections of EUR 15,000 or more must be captured in the bank balance-of-payments reporting to Banque de France; commercial goods exports must be declared to customs under SAD/ECS and, when volume thresholds in the calendar year are exceeded, to Intrastat; direct investment and intercompany financing transactions of EUR 15,000 or more must be declared separately by the enterprise to Banque de France within 20 calendar days. Penalties for non-declaration follow the French Customs Code and Article L132-2 of the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) as foreign-exchange policy co-authority, and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the licensing and AML supervisory authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a secondary operational account for SEPA, virtual IBANs and low-value outbound wires, but Low as the primary vehicle for SWIFT MT103 trade settlements, documentary letters of credit, or FX hedging. An ACPR-licensed fintech EMI (Qonto, Shine, Treezor) can lawfully hold safeguarding accounts for the SARL under the PSD2 and EMD2 frameworks, yet such an account is not a bank deposit and is not covered by the Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts; EMI licences also do not authorise lending, credit intermediation, LC confirmation or the issuance of guarantees. Foreign non-EU digital banks without an ACPR branch, an EU passport, or a French credit-institution partner cannot lawfully open a resident corporate account and would constitute unauthorised payment-service activity under Articles L511-1 et seq. and L521-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are executed by SWIFT MT103 for the euro leg (or MT202 for nostro covers) from the foreign parent to the SARL account held at a French Tier-1 bank. The remittance instructions must carry the ISO 20022 purpose code 'CABP' (capital payments) for shareholder loans or 'EQUI' (equity) for capital contributions, together with a French balance-of-payments economic nature (direct investment equity or intercompany financing). The SARL must file the Banque de France direct-investment declaration for any transaction of EUR 15,000 or more within 20 calendar days; a copy of the filed declaration, notarised corporate minutes approving the funding, the legalised shareholders' agreement, signatures on the account mandate verifying the gérant, RCS UBO update, and evidence of registration of any capital increase (fixed registration duty of EUR 500) complete the verification pack. No prior exchange-control authorisation is necessary.Outbound settlements use: (i) SWIFT MT103 open-account wires to suppliers in EUR, USD, CNH, JPY, THB and KRW with invoice-referencing remittance fields; (ii) documentary commercial letters of credit issued by the French bank under UCP600 for large MLCC/resistor procurement, normally confirmed by a bank in the supplier country; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EU suppliers; (iv) TARGET2 for high-value euro payments within Europe, with USD cleared via CHIPS/FedWire and CNY via CIPS as required. The French bank performs automatic Banque de France balance-of-payments capture for all cross-border payments of EUR 15,000 or greater, and the SARL must keep invoice, transport and customs declarations for each disbursement to satisfy evidentiary audit.Deliverable spot and forward foreign-exchange contracts (change à terme) up to 24 months on EUR/USD, EUR/CNH, EUR/JPY and EUR/KRW, supported by documented underlying trade exposure and reported under EMIR.FX swaps and swap-rollover treasury operations in G10 currencies executed with the relationship bank's capital-markets desk.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CIRS) converting foreign-currency intercompany funding into EUR for the SARL's funding model.Vanilla purchased FX options (EUR/USD and EUR/CNH) hedging import costs and preserving upside on AP spreads.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on non-fully-convertible Asian and African currencies only where the trade receivable or payable is non-deliverable; EMIR reporting and uncleared margin rules apply.Commodity swaps and commodity-indexed derivatives are not permissible for this passive distribution scope because no physical commodity inventory is held for hedging against input-price movements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a secondary operational account for SEPA, virtual IBANs and low-value outbound wires, but Low as the primary vehicle for SWIFT MT103 trade settlements, documentary letters of credit, or FX hedging. An ACPR-licensed fintech EMI (Qonto, Shine, Treezor) can lawfully hold safeguarding accounts for the SARL under the PSD2 and EMD2 frameworks, yet such an account is not a bank deposit and is not covered by the Fonds de Garantie des Dépôts; EMI licences also do not authorise lending, credit intermediation, LC confirmation or the issuance of guarantees. Foreign non-EU digital banks without an ACPR branch, an EU passport, or a French credit-institution partner cannot lawfully open a resident corporate account and would constitute unauthorised payment-service activity under Articles L511-1 et seq. and L521-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code.
