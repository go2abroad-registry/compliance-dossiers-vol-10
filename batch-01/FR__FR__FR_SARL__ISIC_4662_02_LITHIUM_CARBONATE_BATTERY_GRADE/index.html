Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital; SARL may be formed with a nominal capital of €1. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and paid in at signature and blocked in a French credit institution or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until registration; funds are released to the gérant only upon presentation of the Kbis extract. In-kind contributions must be fully transferred and, if their value exceeds legal thresholds, valued by a commissaire aux apports.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and structural load inspections are required for any occupied commercial or warehouse premises, including an inspection by the SDIS for fire resistance, exits, extinguishers and emergency signage. New electrical installations require an attestation de conformité by CONSUEL. Racking or mechanical handling areas require a structural load certificate. The company must prepare a Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP) and provide occupational medical follow-up before employees are exposed to chemical or powder hazards.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Public Accounts and Budget; online portals: impots.gouv.fr and formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; customs oversight for imports/exports is exercised by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned SARL registers through the national single electronic portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) by filing the unified registration/incorporation form (M0) with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce. The Registrar assigns the SIREN/SIRET; the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) enters the company into the VAT taxpayer register and allocates a French intra-Community VAT number (FR + 11 digits / IFU). Non-resident shareholders and managers must provide certified identity documents, proof of a registered office in France, bank references, and AML/KYC documentation; there is no minimum capital requirement. VAT registration is effective from commencement of taxable activities, and notification is delivered via the taxpayer's online professional account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Under Article 239 bis AA of the French General Tax Code (CGI), an SARL carrying on an industrial or commercial activity may elect, for a non-renewable maximum period of five fiscal years, to be taxed under the transparent 'sociétés de personnes' regime, unless the election would result in tax avoidance by a member holding at least 50% of the capital. No US-style check-the-box election exists under French law; the SARL's default remains entity-level corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés under Article 206-1 CGI), at the standard 25% rate with a reduced 15% rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit, subject to statutory conditions. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU Directive (EU) 2017/952 (ATAD 2) and OECD BEPS Action 2 apply to cross-border structures where a foreign check-the-box classification creates double non-taxation or double deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for registration: any independent taxable economic activity carried on in France by the SARL requires immediate VAT registration. For intra-EU distance sales of goods into France, the €10,000 annual pan-European threshold (aggregated cross-border B2C supplies) triggers French VAT; imports into French customs territory attract VAT at clearance under Article 291 CGI irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Article 262-I-1° CGI zero-rates supplies of goods exported by the SARL to destinations outside the EU, provided the exporter holds conclusive documentary proof that the goods physically left EU customs territory within the prescribed dispatch period. For the battery-grade lithium carbonate arbitrage model, each export consignment requires a complete export-proof file (dossier de preuves) covering sale, transport, and customs exit. Services directly connected to exports and B2B services rendered to third-country recipients fall outside French VAT or qualify for zero-rating under Articles 259 A and 262-I-2° CGI.

  • Customs export declaration (Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens / EXA authentication in the DGDDI SDE/ICS system)
  • Bill of Lading (Connaissement) or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice and Incoterm (typically FOB/CIF for battery-grade lithium carbonate) evidencing transfer of title outside France
  • Bank payment evidence (SWIFT MT103) or Inward Remittance Certificate (certificat domiciliaire)
  • Warehouse departure certificates or third-party logistics records supporting physical exit in the export-proof file

Mandatory structured e-invoicing and e-reporting regime under Article 289 CGI as amended by the 2022 Finance Act and Decree No. 2022-1298. Large enterprises and mid-caps (ETI) must issue and receive structured e-invoices from 1 September 2026; all SMEs/VSEs from 1 September 2027. Transmission occurs via a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the free Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) using Factur-X/FNFE, UBL, or CII formats, with transaction datasets forwarded to DGFiP in near real time for fiscal reconciliation. France does not impose pre-transaction fiscal stamping or central clearance authorization; instead, the PPF/PDP architecture enables continuous e-reporting, and platform-to-platform APIs are governed by the Dynamic Sharing Protocol (DSP). Every invoice must identify both parties by SIREN and FR VAT number.

The DGFiP conducts targeted flow-matching audits of export zero-rating files; unsupported or false zero-rating triggers reassessment of undeclared VAT, late-payment interest of 0.20% per month under Article 1727 CGI, bad-faith penalty of 40% and fraudulent-maneuvers penalty of 80% under Articles 1728 and 1729 CGI, denial of input VAT deduction, and criminal liability for tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI (up to five years' imprisonment and a fine up to €500,000 or twice the sums evaded). The retroactive clawback period is three years from the end of the reporting year under Article L.169 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales, extending to six years for unreported transactions and up to ten years for deliberately concealed activity; customs penalties under Articles 410 et seq. of the Code des Douanes additionally apply to false export declarations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full capital account convertibility and current account convertibility under EU regulations and national law. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are unrestricted and may be effected without prior approval, subject only to standard tax filing and beneficial ownership disclosure requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export or import proceeds is required under French or EU law. All transactions are subject to free convertibility at market rates. Import and export receipts must be reported to the Banque de France for statistical purposes within ten working days, but funds may remain in foreign currency accounts without forced conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not viable for cross-border corporate treasury operations in France due to strict AML and payment services regulations. All liquidity clearing must be conducted through licensed traditional correspondent banking channels.
