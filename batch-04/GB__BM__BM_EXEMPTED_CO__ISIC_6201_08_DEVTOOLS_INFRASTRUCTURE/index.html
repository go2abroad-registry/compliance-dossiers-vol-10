Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at a 1:1 parity to the United States Dollar (USD); USD is legal tender and circulates interchangeably in the domestic payment system. Bermuda maintains no material exchange controls for exempted companies. The Exchange Control Act 1972 grants the BMA transaction-level oversight, but liberalized general permissions permit unrestricted current-account conversion, dividend distribution, capital repatriation, and liquidation proceeds remittance to non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal: the exempted company must file annual economic substance declarations, maintain or file financial statements with the Registrar of Companies as applicable, and satisfy the Companies Act 1981 solvency test before paying dividends or reducing capital. All routine remittances are executed in USD without formal BMA pre-approval, although designated banks will require board resolutions and source-of-funds evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts in USD, GBP, EUR, or any convertible currency may be retained in full, freely held in external or domestic accounts, and redeployed for trade disbursements without BMA conversion obligations. Non-resident-owned exempted companies are classified as holding external accounts, enabling full retention of foreign currency without reporting thresholds beyond standard AML transaction monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, the Exchange Control Act 1972, and the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for foreign EMIs as payment-gateway collection rails, Low as a primary treasury custodian. Legal constraints: foreign electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not licensed by the BMA under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 or the Digital Assets Business Act 2018; they may settle subscription collections from merchant gateways (Stripe Billing, Mercury ACH) as unregulated cross-border payment facilitators, but they cannot lawfully hold Bermudian deposit liabilities or offer local BMD-denominated accounts without a BMA licence. The Bermuda exempted company may open a non-Bermuda EMI account (e.g., Mercury US) as a permitted external account, subject to contemporaneous books and records in Bermuda for substance reporting. The prudent structure is a two-bank model: a domestic BMA-regulated settlement account plus a fintech collection account, with nightly or weekly sweeps to the Bermuda treasury account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (no corporate income tax for a standard Bermuda Exempted Company outside the OECD Pillar Two scope; a 15% rate applies only to multinational enterprise groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million under the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends and no capital gains tax or remittance tax; an Exempted Company may pay dividends freely out of profits without approval, and non-resident shareholders receive distributions gross with no Bermuda source taxation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Standard Bermuda Exempted Company outside an in-scope MNE group: no mandatory Local File or Master File at domestic law. In-scope MNE groups under the Bermuda CIT (Transfer Pricing) Rules: mandatory Local File where related-party cross-border controlled transactions (goods, services, IP, financing) exceed BMD 1,000,000 in a fiscal year; mandatory Master File where total related-party transactions exceed BMD 1,000,000 on an aggregated group basis or where controlled transactions involve high-risk jurisdictions; documentation must be contemporaneous and in English.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Economic Substance (Companies) Act 2018 and the Economic Substance Regulations apply to all exempted companies carrying on 'relevant activities', which expressly include 'Intellectual Property Business' and 'Holding Business'. A DevOps/API monitoring cloud company earning subscription and usage revenue must demonstrate full economic substance by proving (i) management and direction in Bermuda through physical board meetings with genuine strategic oversight; (ii) adequate qualified employees in Bermuda, including a majority of senior management; (iii) adequate physical premises in Bermuda; (iv) adequate annual operating expenditure in Bermuda; and (v) performance of core income-generating activities (R&D, contract negotiation, service delivery, and risk management) in Bermuda. A pure equity holding entity benefits from reduced substance requirements, but a company holding IP licences with no active Bermuda business is classified as an 'Intellectual Property Business' and is subject to the most stringent regime. Failure to satisfy the substance test exposes an IP business to a BMD 250,000 financial penalty (BMD 50,000 for other relevant activities), compulsory strike-off, and exchange of information with the UK tax authorities under the Bermuda-UK TIEA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations (Economic Substance and CIT)

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance (Companies) Act 2018: financial penalties up to BMD 250,000 for IP business (BMD 50,000 for other relevant activities) plus daily penalties for continuing breach; under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023: penalties up to 100% of the under-assessed tax plus statutory interest; fraudulent evasion on indictment may attract a fine of up to BMD 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly sign false declarations are personally liable for penalties, subject to court-ordered restitution to the Crown, travel bans imposed during enforcement proceedings, and imprisonment up to 10 years for fraudulent evasion; the corporate veil is pierced where the company operates as a mere façade and directors exercise de facto control.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Failure to satisfy the economic substance test attracts a financial penalty of BMD 250,000 for an IP business (BMD 50,000 for other relevant activities), compulsory strike-off from the Bermuda Register, and reporting of the entity to UK HMRC and the OECD as a non-compliant offshore structure; directors involved in the breach may be disqualified from acting as directors of any Bermuda company.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause or permit the failure may be ordered by the Bermuda Court to pay the penalty personally, barred from future directorships, and, where fraud is proven, subjected to travel bans and criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Companies Act 1981.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Bermuda Proceeds of Crime Amendment Regulations and the Beneficial Ownership (Amendment) Act 2020: fines up to BMD 50,000 for each undisclosed beneficial owner, imprisonment up to 2 years for individuals, revocation of the corporate service provider licence, and compulsory dissolution of the offending company.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines, statutory disqualification, travel bans where concealment impedes an ongoing money-laundering investigation, and imprisonment up to 2 years on indictment; courts in Bermuda and the UK may concurrently freeze and confiscate the personal and corporate assets of directors under the applicable confiscation regimes.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation (Intercompany IP, API Licensing, and Financing Mis-Pricing)

Statutory sanction: Under the Bermuda CIT (Transfer Pricing) Rules: adjustment restoring the arm's-length result, top-up tax up to 15% on the adjusted GloBE profits plus interest at 8% per annum, and penalties up to 100% of the additional tax for negligent or deliberate understatement; HMRC may cross-refer the case for a UK Diverted Profits Tax assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally set intercompany prices to frustrate taxation may be joined as parties to the penalty proceedings, held personally liable for the corporate penalty and costs, disqualified from managing companies, and prosecuted for cheating the public revenue with imprisonment up to 7 years.
Registration Default, Non-Filing, and Compulsory Strike-Off

Statutory sanction: Default penalties of BMD 100 per month for the Annual Return; ESD penalties of BMD 3,000 (first offence) and BMD 10,000 (subsequent offences); the Registrar may strike off the company, after which all company assets vest automatically in the Crown and directors lose the power to trade or dispose of company property.

Director personal liability: After strike-off, former directors remain personally liable for debts incurred following the strike-off, may be sued by creditors for wrongful trading, and cannot incorporate a new Bermuda company without leave of the Court; the Court may issue a restitution order requiring directors to restore company assets or pay their value, with a travel ban imposed where the director fails to comply.
