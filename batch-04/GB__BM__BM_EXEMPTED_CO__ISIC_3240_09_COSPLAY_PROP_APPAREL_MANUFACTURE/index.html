Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Registrar of Companies, Government of Bermuda (corporate incorporation and Certificate of Registration under the Companies Act 1981); Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda (Payroll Tax and employer registration); Bermuda Customs Department (import and export duty administration). Primary portals: https://www.registry.gov.bm and https://www.gov.bm. Her Majesty's Revenue & Customs (HMRC) exercises no fiscal administration over Bermuda, which is a self-governing British Overseas Territory with fiscal autonomy; however, HMRC CFC and anti-avoidance rules may apply to UK-connected shareholders.

E-government filing portal

Bermuda levies no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or withholding tax, and therefore issues no income-tax TIN or VAT ID. A foreign-owned Bermuda Exempted Company obtains its statutory legal identity and unique company registration number exclusively through the Certificate of Incorporation issued by the Registrar of Companies, filed by a licensed Bermuda corporate service provider under the Companies Act 1981; same-day incorporation is available and no minimum capital is required. If the entity employs personnel present in Bermuda, the employer must register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner and obtain a Payroll Tax Account Number. For physical imports into Bermuda, the entity must appoint a licensed customs broker and file electronic customs entries; no VAT/GST registration is possible or required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda does not operate a domestic check-the-box, partnership, or hybrid-mismatch election. The principal statutory election is the application under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, by which the Minister of Finance issues a written undertaking exempting the company from any future Bermuda tax on income, profits, capital gains, or appreciation until 31 March 2035. Foreign tax classification is determined by the shareholder's jurisdiction: e.g., a US owner may file Form 8832 to classify the Bermuda entity as a disregarded entity or corporation, and UK owners are assessed under the UK CFC and transfer-pricing rules. In addition, the Economic Substance (Bermuda) Regulations 2018 require an annual Economic Substance Declaration for entities carrying on relevant activities, including an election to evidence tax residence elsewhere where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax. The applicable indirect tax system is Bermuda Customs Duty (import duty), levied on the CIF (cost, insurance, and freight) value of imported goods under the Bermuda Customs Tariff; the general ad valorem standard rate is 22.25%, with rates ranging from 0% on essential goods to higher rates on luxury items and motor vehicles. A hotel guest occupancy tax applies to accommodation services. Payroll tax is a direct employment tax, not an indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: Bermuda has no VAT/GST and thus no turnover-based VAT registration threshold. For customs purposes there is no monetary registration threshold; every import into Bermuda must be declared on a Customs Entry (Single Administrative Document) filed electronically by the importer or a licensed customs broker, and duty must be secured before release. For payroll tax, any employer engaging Bermuda-resident employees must register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner regardless of turnover. Local currency is the Bermudian Dollar (BMD), pegged at 1:1 to the United States Dollar. A pure holding or foreign-income Exempted Company with no Bermuda employees and no physical imports has zero indirect-tax registration exposure.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda has no VAT, and therefore no statutory 0% export zero-rating regime and no input-VAT refund mechanism exists. As a fiscal matter, goods manufactured in Bermuda and exported are generally not subject to export duty: the Bermuda Customs Tariff imposes export duty only on specifically listed natural-resource items (e.g., certain excavated products), and manufactured cosplay costumes, theatrical robes, and synthetic wigs fall outside the dutiable export class. Where raw materials are imported into Bermuda for manufacture and the finished goods are subsequently exported, the importer may claim duty relief, drawback, or temporary-importation treatment under the Customs Act on production of satisfactory proof of export within the prescribed period; otherwise, import duty becomes a sunk cost. Therefore, eligibility for tax neutrality on the export supply chain depends on documenting that goods never entered Bermudian home consumption.

  • Bermuda Customs Export Declaration (e-SAD/ASYCUDA World entry) evidencing the physical export from Bermuda
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing outbound shipment, consignee, quantity, and date of departure
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the company's bank or EMI account (Bermuda has no exchange controls, so this certificate is not a Bermuda statutory requirement, but it is the pipeline's mandatory audit-trail artefact for export-proceeds substantiation)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the customs export declaration
  • Certificate of Origin (required only where a preferential tariff claim or other regulatory certification is made)

No real-time electronic-invoicing, continuous-transaction-control (CTC), fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists for ordinary commercial B2B/B2C invoices in Bermuda. Customs clearance, however, is fully electronic: Bermuda Customs operates the Single Administrative Document (SAD) platform built on ASYCUDA World, requiring advance electronic lodgement, tariff classification, valuation, and broker certification before physical release of goods. Supporting documentation — commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill — together with any e-invoices must be retained for at least six years to substantiate customs entries, economic substance filings, and shareholder-level tax audits.

Although no VAT zero-rating clawback can arise in Bermuda, the following scrutiny and penalty layers apply: (1) Bermuda Customs Act 1965 and the Customs Tariff — false declarations, undervaluation, or fraudulent export/import entries attract forfeiture of goods, fines up to three times the value of the goods or the duty evaded, and imprisonment; (2) the Minister of Finance may revoke the Exempted Undertakings tax-exemption certificate for misrepresentation, retroactively exposing the entity to the Tax_Exempt_Zone advantage being clawed back; (3) the Economic Substance (Bermuda) Regulations 2018 impose civil penalties, enforcement notices, and possible strike-off for failure to file the annual economic substance declaration or to satisfy the substance test for relevant activities; (4) UK-connected shareholders face UK CFC and DPT challenges with interest and penalties under TMA 1970 and FA 2009 where export profits are artificially diverted through the tax-neutral Bermudian entity; and (5) for US shareholders, the pipeline's 180-day export-proceeds repatriation benchmark (which is a covenant standard, not a Bermuda statutory rule) is audited in the context of Subpart F/GILTI classification, with adverse recharacterisation if remittance evidence is missing. The exporter must therefore maintain a clean customs trail, bank/EMI remittance records, and economic substance documentation to defend the zero-tax export position.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates a fully convertible currency regime pegged to the US dollar at a fixed rate of BMD 1: USD 1. There are no material exchange control restrictions on current account transactions, including payments for imported goods and services. Capital account transactions are generally free, but the BMA maintains supervisory oversight via the Exchange Control Act 1972 and associated regulations, which require certain transactions involving residents and non-residents to be reported or licensed where they touch prescribed assets or where the counterparty is a resident of a sanctions-targeted jurisdiction. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, and capital repatriation by a Bermuda Exempted Company are not subject to statutory surrender or conversion hurdles; however, the company must maintain a valid permit under the Exchange Control Act and comply with the BMA's notification requirements for issuances and transfers of shares to non-residents. The practical constraints on profit remittance are minimal and arise primarily from corporate law solvency tests and anti-money laundering (AML) verification obligations rather than from currency convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda does not impose any statutory timeline for the surrender of export proceeds. A Bermuda Exempted Company engaged in the manufacture and online retail of cosplay prop apparel is free to retain export receipts in foreign currency, including USD, EUR, or GBP, in any BMA-licensed bank or a permitted foreign account. Mandatory conversion ratios do not apply because the Bermuda dollar is fully convertible and the Exchange Control Act permits the retention of foreign currency for business purposes. Nevertheless, for trade settlement efficiency, the company may elect to denominate accounts in USD given the fixed peg, and all inward remittances should be tagged with the appropriate purpose code (e.g., trade in goods) to facilitate seamless clearing and auditability.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High with legal constraints. Bermuda-licensed banks do not issue accounts to pure online sellers as readily as modern EMI providers do, and the domestic banking market is small. A Bermuda Exempted Company may lawfully open accounts with authorised UK Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex, provided the EMI is authorised by the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) and the entity passes the EMI's own KYC/AML review. The legal constraint is that these UK EMIs are not 'banks' for Bermuda Exchange Control purposes; they operate as e-money issuers holding funds in segregated accounts at UK clearing banks. For a company selling cosplay props online via Stripe and Shopify Payments, UK EMI virtual IBANs are highly viable for inbound card settlement collections and outbound supplier payments. However, the company must ensure that the EMI's permitted business list includes 'online retail of costumes and apparel' and that the end-beneficiary supply chain (e.g., fabric suppliers in China) can receive payments via SWIFT or local rails. Bermuda's Exchange Control Act does not prohibit overseas accounts, but a Bermuda Exempted Company should disclose foreign-currency accounts in its annual economic substance declaration and maintain appropriate accounting records. The company should also retain the option of opening a full bank account in the UK via a UK branch presence or through an intermediary corporate service provider where physical presence is difficult.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Bermuda Exempted Company, initial shareholder capital and subsequent capital injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the shareholder's home jurisdiction to the company's operating account. The company must obtain a specific 'Permission under the Exchange Control Act' if shares are issued to a non-resident; the BMA grants a general permission to licensed CSPs for standard exempted company capitalisation, so most injections do not require individual approval. The wire should clearly state the purpose code as 'Capital contribution' or 'Equity investment' and include the company's BMA permit number and registration number. Funds must be remitted from an account in the shareholder's name to avoid third-party donation flags; a copy of the bank credit advice and an audited statement of capital should be retained. If injecting more than USD 100,000 in a single transaction, be prepared to show source of funds and board approval. For inbound trade receipts from online sales, the routing is via card network settlement into Stripe or Shopify Payments, which then batch-settles to the company's base currency account; the payment gateway must be configured with the company's bank details and a settlement currency (USD recommended). The legal purpose code for these receipts is 'Sale of goods via e-commerce' or equivalent; no BMA approval is needed for trade receipts.Outbound disbursements for material suppliers, wig manufacturers, packaging suppliers, and logistics providers should be executed via SWIFT MT103 for single payments, via batch CHAPS/Faster Payments for UK supplier payments where applicable, and via commercial letter of credit (LC) for large recurring purchase orders exceeding USD 250,000. For most cosplay apparel manufacturing supply chains, the preferred rail is SWIFT MT103 from the company's Bermuda or UK EMI account to supplier banks in China, Vietnam, or India. The MT103 should include the correct purpose code (e.g., 'Trade payable - goods import'), the supplier's full beneficiary name and account number, and the invoice reference. The company must maintain a trade file for each outbound payment: supplier proforma invoice, purchase order, packing list, and expected shipping terms. Commercial LCs are permissible but are not common for SMEs in this sector due to banker's acceptance charges; instead, usance documentary collections or open account with trade credit insurance may be used. All outbound payments must be screened against UK and US sanctions lists; UK EMIs apply automated screening that may reject payments to certain high-risk corridors, requiring pre-approval by the EMI's compliance team.Bermuda has no domestic restrictions on FX derivatives; a tax-exempt company can trade deliverable forwards (outright FX forwards) with Bermuda-licensed banks or UK/FCA-regulated brokers for maturities from 1 week to 12 months, provided the transactions are conducted under an ISDA master agreement or a simple FX forward confirmation. These are permissible for hedging USD/CNY supplier exposures and GBP/USD settlement exposures.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing currencies with restricted deliverability (e.g., CNY, INR, VND) are permissible when the counterparty is an authorised bank in London or Hong Kong, because Bermuda is not a primary NDF trading center; the company may use NDFs to hedge emerging-market supplier costs without needing an onshore local account.Cross-currency swaps are permissible for managing longer-term mismatches between USD capital, GBP payables, and EUR customer receipts, especially if the company opens a multi-currency account with Butterfield or a UK EMI; a standard ISDA CSA may be required if the swap is collateralised, but the company should first obtain board approval and confirm that the transaction does not trigger any restriction under the Bermuda Companies Act on financial assistance or speculative trading.Plain vanilla FX spot trades are always permissible and can be executed at the bank's spot rate without documentation beyond the FX deal ticket.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High with legal constraints. Bermuda-licensed banks do not issue accounts to pure online sellers as readily as modern EMI providers do, and the domestic banking market is small. A Bermuda Exempted Company may lawfully open accounts with authorised UK Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex, provided the EMI is authorised by the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) and the entity passes the EMI's own KYC/AML review. The legal constraint is that these UK EMIs are not 'banks' for Bermuda Exchange Control purposes; they operate as e-money issuers holding funds in segregated accounts at UK clearing banks. For a company selling cosplay props online via Stripe and Shopify Payments, UK EMI virtual IBANs are highly viable for inbound card settlement collections and outbound supplier payments. However, the company must ensure that the EMI's permitted business list includes 'online retail of costumes and apparel' and that the end-beneficiary supply chain (e.g., fabric suppliers in China) can receive payments via SWIFT or local rails. Bermuda's Exchange Control Act does not prohibit overseas accounts, but a Bermuda Exempted Company should disclose foreign-currency accounts in its annual economic substance declaration and maintain appropriate accounting records. The company should also retain the option of opening a full bank account in the UK via a UK branch presence or through an intermediary corporate service provider where physical presence is difficult.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for standard Bermuda Exempted Companies: Bermuda imposes no general corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or distribution tax. Separate regime: the Corporations (Income Tax) Act 2023 (as amended in 2025) levies a 15% Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax, with transitional rate modifications for tax year 2025, but applies only to constituent entities of multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%: Bermuda imposes no statutory withholding tax on dividends, no remittance tax, and no capital gains tax on distributions paid by an Exempted Company to a non-resident shareholder.
