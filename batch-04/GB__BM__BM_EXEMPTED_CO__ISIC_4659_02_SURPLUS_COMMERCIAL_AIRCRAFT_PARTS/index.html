E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company is incorporated under the Companies Act 1981 without automatic tax registration. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar (USD), and USD is legal tender and freely circulating in Bermuda. Current account transactions, including trade in services and the remittance of profits, dividends and intercompany payments, are generally freely permitted pursuant to the Exchange Control Act, subject to an annual reporting framework and, for resident entities, standard authorised dealer approvals. For non-resident controlled Exempted Companies (such as the Bermuda Exempted Company in this blueprint), the BMA typically grants 'Non-Resident Status' permission which eliminates capital account controls on inward and outward capital movements, permitting unlimited conversion between BMD and USD for operating and treasury purposes. Capital account operations, including direct equity injections, shareholder loans and liquidation proceeds repatriation, are largely unrestricted under such permission, provided that the company is not deemed resident in Bermuda for exchange control purposes. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal in practice; however, banks may seek evidence of the non-resident status permission letter from the BMA before executing large outbound dividends. There is no meaningful distinction between capital and current account convertibility for a properly licensed Exempted Company with non-resident status. The practical constraint is not BMA convertibility but the hedging market depth for BMD, which is effectively redundant given the USD peg.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the BMA or to any domestic bank. An Exempted Company with exchange control non-resident status is not mandated to convert hard currency export receipts into BMD. The company may hold export proceeds in USD-denominated current accounts or multi-currency accounts without a mandatory conversion ratio. The only statutory reporting obligation is the annual Exchange Control return filed through the appointed authorised dealer/custodian bank, requiring a schedule of non-resident capital movements and external liabilities. Operationally, for commercial aircraft parts trading settled in USD, no conversion or surrender applies; USD cash balances may remain in escrow or operational accounts indefinitely. If any Bermudian-resident counterparty is involved, the local commercial bank may act as an authorised dealer and require a standard declaration under the Exchange Control Act, but no express percentage surrender timeline exists.

Central bank supervision: The Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, acting as the central bank, monetary authority and exchange control administrator for the Bermuda jurisdiction, which is a British Overseas Territory with the UK as the sovereign state.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility for fully offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs). The BMA requires any entity carrying on banking or money service business in or from Bermuda to obtain a licence under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 or the Digital Assets Business Act 2018, as applicable. Pure offshore EMIs domiciled in the UK or Europe (e.g., FCA-authorised EMI or EU EMI) can provide ancillary payment services to a Bermuda Exempted Company, but they are not permitted to hold the company's primary capital escrow balance or to represent the company as a Bermuda-licensed bank. The BMA will view material treasury and trade settlement routed through unlicensed third-country EMIs as an AML/CFT concern unless a clear contractual and beneficial-ownership nexus to Bermuda is documented. For an aviation parts distributor with counterparties across the US, Europe and Asia, UK or European EMIs may be acceptable as secondary rails for small operational disbursements, but the core cross-border liquidity platform must be anchored by a Bermuda-licensed or high-street UK bank (e.g., an FCA-regulated UK branch) to sustain commercial credibility and BMA reporting obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Bermuda Exempted Company, the most compliant inbound route is a SWIFT wire from the shareholder's bank in the UK, US or Hong Kong to the company's USD demand deposit account maintained at a Bermuda-licensed tier-1 bank, using the OBU/standard USD correspondent routing via the New York Fed (CHIPS/Fedwire) through the bank's US correspondent. The SWIFT MT103 field 70 (remittance information) must state the underlying transaction purpose, preferably 'CAPITAL INJECTION – SHARE PURCHASE PAYMENT FOR BM EXEMPTED CO' or the standard bank-specific purpose code for non-resident entity funding. The sending bank, receiving bank and correspondent bank should align the purpose code with the beneficiary's BMA non-resident status permission. The company's registered agent must file the required exchange-control notification with the BMA within the timeline specified by the authorised dealer, and the debit/credit must be documented in the company's capital register. For private placement structures, a capital escrow account letter of instruction and a share subscription agreement must accompany the wire. The BMA does not restrict the amount, but any odd-dollar amount or rapid subsequent onward transfer may trigger additional source-of-funds scrutiny.The primary outbound clearing rails are (i) standard SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for operational payments, supplier invoices and intercompany treasury sweeps when the counterparty maintain a conventional bank account; (ii) commercial letters of credit (LCs) issued by the Bermuda bank or its UK/US correspondent through SWIFT MT700 series for large aircraft part procurement from US/EU suppliers, particularly when open-account terms are not accepted; (iii) escrow-release instructions on the aviation escrow platform (if the surplus parts sale is structured as an escrow against delivery and certification), using a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 ultimately; and (iv) for USD-denominated supplier payments within the United States, a local USD clearing via ACH or Fedwire through the bank's New York correspondent, though this is operationally a USD-rail rather than a separate Bermuda clearing system. The Bermuda clearing house operates in BMD for domestic transactions, but for cross-border aviation trade USD is the dominant currency, and the company should maintain a US-dollar account at a New York correspondent to achieve real-time settlement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold in Bermuda (national currency: BMD linked to USD). In absence of corporate income tax, formal Local File and Master File thresholds are not prescribed; nevertheless, entities engaged in traded goods (e.g., aviation parts) that are part of a multinational group must support intercompany pricing with contemporaneous documentation when the group's revenue exceeds USD 100 million equivalent, in line with OECD DDIP and ESR monitoring

Economic substance scope: Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2018, as amended, require entities undertaking 'relevant activities' (including distribution and service centres) to demonstrate: (i) management and control in Bermuda; (ii) adequate physical presence, employees, and expenditures proportionate to income; (iii) core income-generating activities performed in Bermuda. For distribution of surplus commercial aircraft parts, the activity is explicitly covered under 'distribution and service centres'; full compliance is mandatory for exempted companies regardless of tax exemption status. Failure results in significant penalties and potential strike-off

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Anti-Avoidance)

Statutory sanction: Under Bermuda’s Economic Substance Amendment Act 2019, penalties up to BMD 250,000 plus daily fines of BMD 10,000 for persistent breach; disclosure orders to foreign tax authorities via TIEA; loss of tax-exempt status

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be criminally liable if knowingly participating in sham arrangements; civil restitution orders for taxes evaded in foreign jurisdictions; personal travel bans and extradition requests under mutual legal assistance treaties
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR assessment deems entity as 'non-compliant'; penalty up to BMD 500,000; automatic information exchange (first automatic exchange from 2024) to OECD/U.K./U.S. authorities; possible strike-off and dissolution under the Companies Act 1981

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for penalties up to BMD 50,000 each; prohibition from acting as a director for 5 years; restitution of public funds; travel bans for non-compliance with regulatory summonses
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2018, fines of up to BMD 250,000 per breach; revocation of the company’s exempted status; freezing of assets; criminal confiscation

Director personal liability: Individual criminal imprisonment up to 14 years for knowingly laundering aviation commodity proceeds; civil asset seizure; personal travel bans; director disqualification for 10 years
False Reporting / Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent return penalties: up to BMD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years under Bermuda’s Criminal Code; revocation of tax-exemption certificate; publication of non-compliance in Bermuda Gazette

Director personal liability: Directors and officers face criminal fines equal to four times the financial benefit obtained; mandatory extradition to foreign authorities under TIEA; lifetime prohibition from holding corporate offices in Bermuda
Cross-Border Tax Evasion (U.S. / U.K. Nexus)

Statutory sanction: Civil fines of USD 100,000 per entity for violating FATCA registration requirements; U.K. prosecution for offshore tax evasion penalties of up to 200% of tax due; Bermuda assist authorities via exchange of information causing automatic loss of tax-exempt status

Director personal liability: Directors resident in the U.K. may be prosecuted extraterritorially under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 for failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion; imprisonment up to 7 years; worldwide asset freezes; personal liability for all unpaid foreign taxes
