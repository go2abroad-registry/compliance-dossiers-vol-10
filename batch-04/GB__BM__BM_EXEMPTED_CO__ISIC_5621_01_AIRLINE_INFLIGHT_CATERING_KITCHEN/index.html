Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner, Bermuda Government (online portal: https://portal.taxcommissioner.gov.bm); Registrar of Companies, Bermuda Ministry of Finance (https://www.roc.gov.bm); Bermuda Customs Service, Ministry of Finance (ASYCUDA World electronic single-window). HMRC has no direct taxing jurisdiction over Bermuda; the territory exercises full fiscal autonomy within the British Overseas Territory framework.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate as a Bermuda Exempted Company under the Companies Act 1981 through the Registrar of Companies; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation (primary corporate identifier). Step 2: Register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner for Payroll Tax and obtain an Employer Account Number within 14 days of engaging the first employee; registration is mandatory with no minimum turnover. Step 3: Register as an importer with Bermuda Customs Service via ASYCUDA World and obtain a Customs Identification Number before importing kitchen equipment, raw foodstuffs, or bonded inventory. Step 4: File the annual Economic Substance Return (or nil/negative declaration) with the Registrar of Companies. Step 5: Foreign entities require no separate UK TIN/VAT ID for Bermuda operations; the Certificate of Incorporation plus Payroll Tax Account Number constitutes the statutory tax identity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — Bermuda exempted companies are classified under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 as undertakings protected from all taxes on profits, income, dividends, capital gains, and appreciation until 31 March 2035; the territory levies no entity-level income tax and no withholding tax, and taxation is purely territorial/source-based.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Bermuda. An Exemption Certificate election is lodged with the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 to secure statutory tax protection to 2035. Under the Economic Substance (Substance Requirements) (Compliance) Regulations 2023, the company must file an annual return electing among relevant activities or a nil/negative declaration; a US federal check-the-box election may be made separately for US tax classification, and employers may elect payroll-tax instalment bases under the Payroll Tax Act 1995. No election is available to adopt UK VAT grouping or UK corporate tax residence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax in Bermuda; indirect taxation consists of customs duties under the Customs Tariff Act 1971 (aircraft stores and inflight catering supplies are duty-relieved for international flights), employer payroll tax under the Payroll Tax Act 1995, and stamp duties under the Stamp Duty Act 1971.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration: not applicable (no VAT regime; threshold is nil). Payroll Tax: registration is mandatory for every Bermuda employer upon engaging the first employee, without a turnover threshold. Customs: any entity importing goods into Bermuda must register with Bermuda Customs Service irrespective of value; a bonded inflight catering kitchen requires a Customs Aircraft Stores/Bonded Warehouse licence to receive duty-suspended foodstuffs and serviceware.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating is inapplicable because Bermuda imposes no VAT. The functional equivalent is customs duty relief for aircraft stores under the Customs Tariff Act 1971: meals, beverages, disposable serviceware, consumables, and kitchen supplies produced in a Customs-approved bonded inflight catering kitchen and delivered directly to an aircraft departing Bermuda on an international sector qualify for duty suspension/relief. Eligibility requires (i) a valid bonded inflight catering licence; (ii) direct delivery and physical loading on the named international flight; (iii) no diversion to domestic consumption; and (iv) an executed stores certificate from the catering officer or carrier. Invoicing of the foreign airline for offshore catering services is entirely outside Bermuda's indirect tax base and is not taxed.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Export Entry filed through ASYCUDA World
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or certified aircraft-stores airway consignment note)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice evidencing receipt of payment from the foreign airline
  • Bonded Catering Stores Transfer Manifest with customs seal and receiving store signature
  • Aircraft Stores Certificate attesting loading on an international flight
  • Supplier purchase invoices for duty-suspended raw materials (reconciliation of relief)
  • End-Use / End-User Exemption Certificate countersigned by the airline's inflight catering manager

Bermuda does not impose real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based tax clearance mandates. All customs declarations are filed electronically through ASYCUDA World; movements of bonded catering inventory into aircraft are recorded via electronic manifests and stores-transfer entries. Commercial invoices are unrestricted and no tax-invoice validation exists since no VAT is levied.

Bermuda has no VAT, so no VAT clawback mechanism arises; however, Bermuda Customs rigorously audits all duty-relieved aircraft stores. Diversion of bonded catering inventory to local consumption triggers immediate assessment of full customs duty at prevailing tariff rates, a delinquency surcharge up to ten times the unpaid duty, forfeiture of goods and conveying vehicles, and prosecution under the Customs Act 1971. Falsified Aircraft Stores Certificates, false export declarations, or misdeclared flight destinations can lead to cancellation of the bonded licence, fines up to BSD 25,000 per offence, imprisonment up to five years, and revocation by the Registrar of Companies of the Exemption Certificate protection. Bermuda imposes no exchange controls; the 180-day repatriation window is recorded as contractual/best-practice treasury discipline rather than a statutory penalty trigger.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar; current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible, with no exchange-control approval required for profit remittance by a non-resident-designated Exempted Company. Dividends, intra-group loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds are fully repatriable. Residual hurdles are limited to maintaining the BMA non-resident designation and filing Economic Substance Act 2018 evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil for this structure. A Bermuda Exempted Company holding non-resident designation is exempt from the surrender and mandatory-conversion regime of the Exchange Control Act 1972; export/business receipts may be held indefinitely in USD, BMD, GBP, or any other currency at an authorised dealer with no statutory conversion ratio or timeline.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) - sole foreign exchange administration and central bank of Bermuda; Bank of England (BoE) ancillary for GBP sterling clearing legs

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low rating. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex) are not BMA-licensed, cannot accept BMD deposits, and lack depositor-protection status under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No local file or master file for stand-alone exempted companies; CITA 2023 transfer pricing documentation (Country-by-Country Report, Master File, Local File) is mandatory only for groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750M (approximately BMD 750M; BMD pegged 1:1 to USD); an annual Economic Substance Declaration is mandatory for all exempted companies engaged in relevant activities, including 'distribution and service centre' scope, which applies to airline inflight catering procurement and logistics.

Economic substance scope: Under the Economic Substance (Requirements and Compliance) Regulations 2018, the ISIC 5621 airline inflight catering activity constitutes a 'distribution and service centre' relevant activity; the exempted company must demonstrate adequate physical presence, qualified employees, expenditure, and core income-generating activities in Bermuda; annual ESR reporting to the Registrar of Companies is compulsory for the entity even where taxable profits are nil.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Fine up to BDA 250,000; daily penalty BDA 10,000; criminal conviction for false statutory declaration; additional CITA administrative penalties up to USD 25,000 plus interest

Director personal liability: Civil restitution of misrepresented profits; travel bans; imprisonment up to 5 years for officers executing false declarations; director disqualification orders.
Shell Company / ESR Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty BDA 100,000 for failure to meet the economic substance requirement; continuing default BDA 10,000 per day; compulsory strike-off and dissolution; loss of exempted status

Director personal liability: Each director or officer in default liable to a fine of up to BDA 10,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; personal liability for outstanding penalties where the company is struck off.
AML / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2018: fines up to BDA 250,000 for regulated entities; asset freeze orders; Companies Act penalty of BDA 250 per day for beneficial ownership register failures

Director personal liability: Directors' criminal liability for money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997: imprisonment up to 14 years; confiscation orders; travel restrictions and disqualification orders.
CIT Registration / Filing Failure (Qualifying MNE Groups Only)

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties up to USD 10,000 per entity plus USD 1,000 per day of continuing failure; interest at the statutory rate; the 15% top-up tax liability remains fully payable

Director personal liability: Senior accounting officer attestation failures; officers may be assessed for penalties where default results from willful neglect.
