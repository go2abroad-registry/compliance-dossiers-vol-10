Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet Unique of the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, transmitted to the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the department of the registered office; final entry in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-27 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); Decree No. 2023-1107 (ordinary and simplified forms); Articles L.210-2 and L.210-6 (constitution formalities); Decree No. 2021-300 governing the Guichet Unique filing portal.

Formation / constitutional filings

Dossier de constitution (single-form incorporation file) comprising the signed Statuts (Articles of Association), capital deposit certificate, legal-notice attestation, presidential declarations and the necessary registers annexes.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 50 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital for an SAS (may be incorporated with €1). The full subscribed nominal capital must be deposited before registration and placed in a blocked bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the certificate of deposit (certificat de dépôt des fonds) is required in the filing. Any in-kind contribution exceeding a stake of 50% or where a general meeting so decides requires a Commissaire aux Apports report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety certification and evacuation compliance per Code du travail Articles R.4216-1 to R.4216-34, including tested alarm systems, emergency exits, portable extinguishers and evacuation drills; for establishments open to the public, the conformity declaration issued by the Prefectoral Commission de Sécurité is required. Structural-load and occupancy clearance is evidenced by the office lease confirming the building's intended use and load capacity, together with a verification of electrical installations by an approved technical inspector. Prior to any hiring, the company must register with the occupational-health service (SPSTI), obtain the medical-fitness visiting plan and post the internal regulations in a place accessible to all posted and local employees.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum capital for an SAS (may be incorporated with €1). The full subscribed nominal capital must be deposited before registration and placed in a blocked bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the certificate of deposit (certificat de dépôt des fonds) is required in the filing. Any in-kind contribution exceeding a stake of 50% or where a general meeting so decides requires a Commissaire aux Apports report.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), operating under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; centralized digital portal: impots.gouv.fr and the dedicated EU VAT/OSS portal 'Le Suivi du formulaire' via the 'Pro' professional space, with the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) hosted on Chorus Pro for e-invoicing.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a French SAS obtains a SIREN (unique business identifier) and SIRET (establishment identifier) upon registration with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). Following successful incorporation, the SAS must file the 'Déclaration d'existence' with DGFiP and apply for a French VAT number ('Numéro de TVA intracommunautaire') on Form 3557-SD via the impots.gouv.fr professional portal. For foreign non-established businesses supplying taxable services in France without a permanent establishment, a direct French VAT registration is obtained by filing Form 3557-SD (with annexes) with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE), or, for distance sales and certain cross-border B2C services, registration under the European Union One-Stop-Shop (IOSS/OSS) may be used instead of a full French VAT ID; in all cases, third-party written authorization and a domestic fiscal representative may be required for non-EU entities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the standard rate of 25% on its worldwide profits insofar as they are attributable to French operations, regardless of distribution; by election a newly created SAS may be taxed at the income-tax level (IR) for the first five fiscal years or indefinitely in certain holding-company situations, but the default statutory regime is entity-level corporate taxation, denial of the check-the-box pass-through treatment except for the civil partnership (SCI) option; territorial scope: France applies a narrowly construed territoriality principle for corporate tax, taxing only profits attributable to French establishments and French-source income. — A foreign entity establishing a French SAS obtains a SIREN (unique business identifier) and SIRET (establishment identifier) upon registration with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). Following successful incorporation, the SAS must file the 'Déclaration d'existence' with DGFiP and apply for a French VAT number ('Numéro de TVA intracommunautaire') on Form 3557-SD via the impots.gouv.fr professional portal. For foreign non-established businesses supplying taxable services in France without a permanent establishment, a direct French VAT registration is obtained by filing Form 3557-SD (with annexes) with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE), or, for distance sales and certain cross-border B2C services, registration under the European Union One-Stop-Shop (IOSS/OSS) may be used instead of a full French VAT ID; in all cases, third-party written authorization and a domestic fiscal representative may be required for non-EU entities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the standard rate of 25% on its worldwide profits insofar as they are attributable to French operations, regardless of distribution; by election a newly created SAS may be taxed at the income-tax level (IR) for the first five fiscal years or indefinitely in certain holding-company situations, but the default statutory regime is entity-level corporate taxation, denial of the check-the-box pass-through treatment except for the civil partnership (SCI) option; territorial scope: France applies a narrowly construed territoriality principle for corporate tax, taxing only profits attributable to French establishments and French-source income.

Available election forms

Under the CGI, the SAS may: (i) irrevocably elect the 'option pour l'impôt sur les sociétés' if structured as an SELAS into an entity taxed as an IR partnership (Article 239 bis, 239 ter CGI) or, for newly created SMEs, elect IR taxation on the first five fiscal years (Article 239 quater CGI); (ii) elect for French tax consolidation under Article 223 A CGI ('régime d'intégration fiscale') provided the parent holds at least 95% of the subsidiary; (iii) elect for the payment of VAT by the 'régime réel normal' versus the simplified 'régime réel simplifié'; (iv) elect for the franchise en base de TVA (small-business VAT exemption) if under statutory thresholds, subject to a five-year renewal election; and (v) file a hybrid-mismatch declaration (Déclaration 2258-SD) to align with the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) rules on hybrid mismatches; there is no analogous US-style Check-the-Box regime, but a hybrid entity's tax transparency is governed by Articles 8, 1655 ter and the 1998 OECD partnership report, and by administrative doctrine BOI-IS-10-10-20.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For service-rendering businesses (including EOR and staffing payroll services), the registration threshold is the 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption: the general limit of EUR 36,800 of annual taxable turnover (2024/2025 reference; increased to EUR 40,000 as of 2025 for certain qualifying SMEs per art. 293 B CGI) for providers of services; however, once the turnover exceeds this threshold, VAT registration is compulsory on the first euro of income with no further exemption; for non-resident supply of B2B services, the reverse-charge mechanism (Article 283-2 CGI) deems the French recipient liable and no voluntary registration threshold applies to non-established suppliers except under specific simplified regimes, so full French VAT registration is required at inception where the supplier has a French establishment or where the place of supply is France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT is available for exports of goods definitively leaving the EU, and for certain services directly linked to exported goods or to intangible supplies made to non-French taxable persons (Article 262-I CGI, Article 262-II CGI); an EOR or staffing agency may zero-rate only those services where the recipient is a business established outside the EU and the service is effectively consumed outside France per the general place-of-supply rule in Article 259-1 CGI, or where the services are directly related to the sale and transport of exported goods (freight inspection, customs brokerage) — payroll facilitation and workforce management services are generally not export-zero-rated unless the place of supply is the recipient's country under the B2B 'place of supply' rule, in which case the invoice must carry the French intra-community VAT number and a valid reverse-charge reference; strict documentary proof under Article 275 CGI and BOI-TVA-CHA-40 requires the exporter to hold evidence that goods left the EU within 90 days of despatch, including the export declaration ('l'exportation') dated by customs, the proof of physical removal from the EU, and any commercial contract identifying the French supplier.

  • Customs Export Declaration — the 'Déclaration d'Exportation' (DAE, IE-507 XML format) lodged electronically via the DELTA-X system or a customs broker, containing the 8-digit export code 18101000 for temporary staffing services or the appropriate EC code, and validated with an MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading — the air waybill (AWB / LTA), ocean bill of lading, CMR or international transport document issued by the carrier, stating the shipper, consignee, nature of goods and the definitive destination outside the EU, compatible with the attached export declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — a bank attestation of the foreign buyer's SWIFT MT103 payment or equivalent proof of provenance of funds evidencing a non-French counterparty and matching in amount and descriptive reference the export invoice and customs record

Under the 'Réforme de la facturation électronique' (Law 2023-1322 and DGFiP specifications), as of 1 September 2026 all VAT-registered entities established in France must receive electronic invoices and must issue e-invoices in a structured XML format (Factur-X / UBL / CII) transmitted via the Portail Public de Facturation (Chorus Pro) or through an accredited Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP), with mandatory e-invoicing for large and mid-size enterprises by that date and for SMEs by 1 September 2027; real-time fiscal 'clearance' is achieved through the transmission of transaction-level data to DGFiP for VAT reporting (e-reporting) not through real-time per-transaction tax stamping, as in Italy or Latin America; API-only clearance applies to PDP and to selected large taxpayers and requires the OpenAPI v2 specification, SAML2/OpenID Connect authentication, and cryptographic signature per the Chorus Pro security model.

Export zero-rating claims are scrutinized by the DGFiP's national audit directorate (DVNI) via documentary reconstruction tests; where the 0% rate is applied without the mandatory proof of exportation or where fraud is detected (faux export, fictitious freight, or chain circular invoices), the French tax administration imposes a 40% deliberate-understatement penalty (une majoration de 40% pour manquement délibéré) or an 80% penalty for organized fraud ('manœuvres frauduleuses', Article 1729 CGI, para. c), plus late-payment interest at the monthly rate of EURIBOR+5% (Articles 1727 and 1731 CGI), and a criminal fine of up to EUR 75,000 and imprisonment of five years under Articles 1741 CGI (fraude fiscale), and the notified assessment may be escalated to the French courts; the DGFiP may refuse the VAT credit on such exports and retrospectively claw back all zero-rated output VAT as if it were payable at 20%, with no mitigation for good faith absent compliance with the documented export file under Article 275 CGI.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Euro operates under full capital and current account convertibility per EU single market rules. No prior approval required for profit remittances to non-resident shareholders. Hurdles limited to post-transaction beneficial ownership verification, economic substance attestation, and AMF reporting thresholds (€10,000 cash transactions).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds. Receipts credited directly to corporate bank accounts. FX conversion permitted only for commercial purposes; any transaction exceeding €10,000 must be reported to AMF with supporting documentation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: 50,000,000 EUR (group turnover threshold triggering Local File and Master File requirements)

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions including fines up to 500,000 EUR or imprisonment up to 5 years, plus payment of evaded taxes with interest

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability, disqualification from corporate offices, and potential travel bans
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Fines up to 10% of annual turnover, potential dissolution of the entity, and back taxation with penalties

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil leading to personal liability for debts and taxes; possible imprisonment for fraudulent management
AML and KYC Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to 1 million EUR or 5% of annual turnover (whichever higher), plus criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Personal fines, imprisonment for willful non-compliance, and disqualification from management positions
