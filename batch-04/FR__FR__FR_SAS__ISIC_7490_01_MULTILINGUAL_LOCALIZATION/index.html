UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: French AML/CFT obligations are principally governed by Code monétaire et financier Articles L.561-1 to L.561-50 and related regulatory provisions, including Articles R.561-1 to R.561-63. Beneficial ownership is governed principally by Articles L.561-2-2, R.561-1 and R.561-55 to R.561-63. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a declared registered office under Code civil Article 1835, Code de commerce Article L.123-11 and related provisions. A commercial domiciliation company may be used if it is duly authorized and the parties execute a compliant domiciliation agreement. A home address may be used subject to statutory and lease restrictions. A virtual office is generally acceptable for a localization agency with no regulated activity, provided that the company can receive official correspondence, maintain required records, identify its actual management and operating locations, and demonstrate genuine business activity. A mailbox-only arrangement, accommodation address without authorization or address inconsistent with actual management creates tax, banking, AML and enforcement risk.

Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation certificate or automatic 30-day reservation period. Conduct clearance through INPI, RNE, RCS, trademark, trade-name and domain searches before signing the incorporation documents. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital for a SAS; the articles of association fix the share capital and the terms of release of subscribed shares. Cash contributions must be deposited into a specially opened blocked bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until registration, and a certificate of deposit of funds (attestation de dépôt des fonds) must accompany the incorporation filing. The blocked funds are released to the legal representative only after the RNE registration is obtained.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises require standard fire safety compliance and a fire certificate/attestation from the landlord, ensuring two escape routes and appropriate detection; structural load clearance is provided by the building owner for the leased area. The company must complete the workplace health and safety self-assessment under the Labour Code, register with CARSAT if applicable, and carry employer liability insurance before any staff are employed.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum capital for a SAS; the articles of association fix the share capital and the terms of release of subscribed shares. Cash contributions must be deposited into a specially opened blocked bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until registration, and a certificate of deposit of funds (attestation de dépôt des fonds) must accompany the incorporation filing. The blocked funds are released to the legal representative only after the RNE registration is obtained.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — portal: impots.gouv.fr; business formalities via the Guichet Unique (guichet-entreprises.fr / formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr)

E-government filing portal

For an SAS with foreign shareholders: (1) file incorporation documents with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce through the Guichet Unique; (2) DGFiP automatically assigns SIREN (9-digit identifier) and SIRET following RCS registration; (3) obtain the French intra-Community VAT number (n° de TVA intracommunautaire) by registering with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) via form P0 (imprimé 3517-SD); (4) apply for the EU EORI number through the customs authority (Douanes) if goods are imported/exported.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For an SAS with foreign shareholders: (1) file incorporation documents with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce through the Guichet Unique; (2) DGFiP automatically assigns SIREN (9-digit identifier) and SIRET following RCS registration; (3) obtain the French intra-Community VAT number (n° de TVA intracommunautaire) by registering with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) via form P0 (imprimé 3517-SD); (4) apply for the EU EORI number through the customs authority (Douanes) if goods are imported/exported.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not use US-style check-the-box. For an SAS, corporate income tax (IS) is the default. Available elections include: (i) temporary pass-through taxation under IR for new SMEs (art. 239 bis AA CGI), (ii) French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) for 95%+ subsidiaries (art. 223 A CGI), (iii) VAT grouping (assujettis uniques) for fiscal unity, and (iv) parent-subsidiary participation exemption election (régime mère-fille).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for established taxable persons; VAT registration is mandatory upon starting a taxable activity in France. Small businesses may benefit from the 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption (no VAT chargeable) only if annual turnover remains below €36,800 for qualifying services (translation/localization being a service) or €91,900 for goods (2025 thresholds); once exceeded, VAT registration and invoicing become mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exports of goods: 0% VAT applies if the goods physically leave the EU and evidence is retained within 90 days (CGI art. 262). For B2B localization, translation, and dubbing services supplied to a non-French/non-EU business customer (e.g., a US licensee), the place of supply is outside France under CGI art. 259 (customer's country), so the service is outside the scope of French VAT / effectively zero-rated. To claim this, the supplier must hold the customer's VAT ID or equivalent tax identification registered in the customer's country and a written contract evidencing the cross-border nature of the service. B2C services to non-EU consumers may be zero-rated only if intellectual or marketing services are electronically supplied or where specific exclusion applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation / DAE) with exit confirmation from the EU customs clearance system (Delta D)
  • Bill of Lading (maritime) or Air Waybill (air) or CMR/rail waybill evidencing physical export to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (proof of FX repatriation and payment conversion through a French or EU-authorized bank, compliant with the French Monetary and Financial Code)

France is phasing in mandatory B2B e-invoicing and B2C e-reporting via a centralized legal scheme. Large companies must issue e-invoices from 1 September 2026; ETIs and SMEs from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be submitted to a Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) connected to the public 'Annuaire Central'; no digital stamping, but real-time clearance of invoice data to tax authorities is required through the PDP network.

French customs and tax authorities apply strict substantive review of export zero-rating. Missing or inconsistent proof of exit or payment triggers retroactive VAT assessment and refusal of the 0% rate. Civil penalties include the 40/25/20% scale based on good or bad faith, escalating to 80% for 'manoeuvres frauduleuses' and 100% for abusive schemes, plus 0.20% per month late-payment interest. Criminal prosecution for tax fraud (fraude fiscale) can impose a €75,000 fine and five years' imprisonment. Specific export invoice fraud may also lead to outright corporation and VAT clawbacks for up to six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Codes of Liberalisation of Capital Movements and Current Invisible Operations. France applies no prior exchange-control authorization to EUR/USD/GBP trade payments, trade settlements, or profit repatriation. An SAS may remit dividends and repay shareholder loans without administrative license provided the distribution is supported by distributable reserves, a valid shareholder resolution, and compliant tax documentation. French withholding tax may apply on outbound dividends or interest under domestic law, subject to applicable double tax treaty relief; the paying bank will require a corporate authorization and may request tax residence certificates before execution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds denominated in foreign currency. Resident exporters may retain foreign currency balances on authorized multicurrency accounts and convert at freely negotiated commercial rates. Cross-border monetary flows are subject to Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical reporting, normally fulfilled by the instructing credit institution; selected direct investment, real estate, and financing transactions above statutory thresholds may trigger direct reporting obligations by the enterprise.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) exercising delegated authority over foreign exchange administration, balance-of-payments reporting, and cross-border capital movement monitoring.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility as an ancillary cross-border payments rail for client collections, freelancer settlements, and low-value vendor disbursements, but not as a substitute for a licensed French credit institution. Wise B2B and Payoneer operate in France through EU e-money institution passports or partner banking arrangements under PSD2, providing multicurrency accounts, virtual IBANs, and retail FX conversion. Legal constraints include the absence of credit institution status, no French deposit-guarantee cover, restricted eligibility for statutory capital attestation or escrow, and dependency on partner banks for clearing. such EMIs are suitable for operational receipts and disbursements after the primary French banking relationship is established; enhanced due diligence is still required on underlying invoices and beneficiary identity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from an EU shareholder in EUR should be routed by SEPA Credit Transfer; non-EUR or non-EEA capital injections should be routed by SWIFT MT103 through the shareholder’s bank. The remittance information must clearly identify the transaction as a capital injection, using an explicit reference such as 'SHARE CAPITAL INCREASE – SAS EQUITY CONTRIBUTION' and, where supported, the EPC purpose code 'EQUI'. The receiving French bank will confirm the credit against the SAS IBAN and may require the capital deposit certificate, shareholder resolution, source-of-wealth documentation, and UBO identification before funds are released for operational use.
