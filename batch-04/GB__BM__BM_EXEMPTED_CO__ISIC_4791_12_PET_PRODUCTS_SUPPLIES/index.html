Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda has no unitary revenue agency. Tax administration is split among: (i) the Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC) for Payroll Tax and, from 1 January 2025, the 15% Corporate Income Tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CITA); (ii) the Registrar of Companies for incorporation and the Companies Act 1981 register; and (iii) the Bermuda Customs Service (Department of Customs) for customs/import duties under the Customs Tariff Act 1971 and the Customs Act 1981. Primary portals: OTC e-services (otc.bm), Registrar of Companies (roc.gov.bm), and the Bermuda Customs electronic manifest/entry system.

E-government filing portal

An exempted company is formed by a licensed Bermuda Corporate Service Provider and obtains only a Companies Act registration number; Bermuda does not issue a general income-tax TIN because out-of-scope entities pay no corporate income tax. An employer must register with the OTC for Payroll Tax administration; a constituent entity of an MNE group with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more must file a CITA registration notice with the Tax Commissioner and obtain a CIT TIN. This electronic-retail entity, being an out-of-scope low-risk exempted company, normally requires no tax ID unless it employs Bermuda payroll staff, imports dutiable goods, or joins an in-scope MNE group.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda law has no check-the-box or flow-through election; an exempted company is always entity-level and tax-exempt unless CITA 2023 applies. For in-scope MNE groups only, CITA provides limited elections: accounting-standard designation (IFRS/US GAAP), substance-based income exclusion (SBIE), and transitional loss-pooling elections. US federal classification of a Bermuda exempted company (disregarded entity or partnership) is a US Treasury check-the-box election under Treas. Reg. Section 301.7701-3 (Form 8832), independent of Bermuda law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax applies in Bermuda; the only indirect-type levy is customs/import duty at the border (plus specific excise taxes on fuel, tobacco, alcohol, and motor vehicles).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: Bermuda has no VAT/GST regime, so no BMD turnover threshold triggers an indirect-tax registration or ID. Importers must file customs entries in BMD with Bermuda Customs. Destination-country rules govern the entity's e-commerce sales into the UK/EU: UK Import One Stop Shop (IOSS) at 20% VAT applies to consignment imports with goods value not exceeding GBP 135; EU IOSS applies at member-state rates for consignments not exceeding EUR 150.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable at the Bermuda level: because Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST, exports from Bermuda are outside the scope of any Bermuda consumption tax and there is no export zero-rating claim, refund, or crediting mechanism. For the entity's B2C electronic retail of pet supplies into the UK/EU, the remote supply is taxed at destination under the IOSS regimes, or, where goods are held in UK/EU stock before sale, the local UK/EU VAT registration governs; zero-rating would arise only under UK/EU export rules (e.g., export sale from UK/EU to Bermuda as a third-country supply), not under Bermuda law.

  • Bermuda Customs outbound manifest/export goods declaration (where goods physically depart Bermuda)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list evidencing the cross-border B2C sale and final destination
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export consignment (if freight is used)
  • Inbound remittance / payment-gateway settlement record evidencing receipt of export proceeds in BMD/USD

None: Bermuda mandates no real-time fiscal clearance, no e-invoicing API, and no digital stamping. The only digitized customs obligation is the Bermuda Customs electronic pre-arrival manifest/entry filing for goods arriving in Bermuda. If the entity registers for UK IOSS/VAT, UK Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires digital record-keeping and software filing, but this is a UK regime, not a Bermuda clearance mandate.

No VAT clawback is possible in Bermuda. Penalty exposure arises under: (i) the Customs Act 1981 - false or fraudulent declarations render goods liable to forfeiture and expose the declarant to fines up to three times the duty value; (ii) the Companies Act 1981 - false filings to the Registrar carry fines up to BMD 50,000 and director disqualification; and (iii) CITA 2023 for in-scope groups - BMD 250 daily default penalties plus up to 100% of the additional CIT under-assessed. For UK VAT, IOSS non-compliance or unregistered remote sales expose the entity to HMRC inaccuracy penalties up to 100% of the VAT, default surcharge, and retrospective assessments for up to eight years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged to the US Dollar (USD) at a fixed 1:1 exchange rate, and USD is legal tender in Bermuda. Bermuda maintains a liberalised exchange control regime: exempted companies holding 'non-resident' status (automatically granted to qualifying exempted companies) are outside the scope of the Exchange Control Act 1972. Accordingly, there are no restrictions on capital account convertibility, current account convertibility, or profit repatriation. Dividends, interest, royalties, and liquidation proceeds may be freely remitted to any jurisdiction in any major currency without prior approval. The only practical hurdle is compliance with the BMA's AML/ATF regulations and the provision of accurate economic substance and beneficial ownership documentation to the appointed bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender requirements, timeline, or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds in Bermuda for non-resident exempted companies. Export receipts from e-commerce sales (including pet supplies) may be retained fully in any currency, including foreign currency accounts held with Bermuda commercial banks or offshore banks. For resident companies (not applicable here), the Exchange Control Act permits the BMA to impose surrender directions, but no such directions are in force for non-resident exempted entities.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) acting as the monetary authority, financial regulator, and de facto central bank; no independent central bank exists. The BMA oversees the Bermuda dollar, banking, and foreign exchange operations under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut, Wise, and Airwallex are not licensed banks in Bermuda and cannot be used as the primary treasury vehicle. The BMA does not recognise EMI status from other jurisdictions for the purpose of accepting client deposits in Bermuda; a Bermudian exempted company may open and maintain an EMI account for payment routing and currency conversion, but this must be supplementary to a fully licensed Bermuda commercial banking relationship. Legal constraints include: (i) EMIs are not covered by the Bermuda Deposit Insurance scheme; (ii) BMA's AML/ATF guidance cautions regulated financial institutions against dealing with unregulated intermediaries; and (iii) settlement finality through EMIs may not satisfy the non-resident treasury substance test. Best practice is to use a licensed Tier-1 bank as the anchor account and utilise EMIs solely for low-value collection and disbursement automation.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: High-volume micro-transactions from Stripe/PayPal with no prior banking history in Bermuda

Defense evidence: Provide six months of processed transaction reports from payment processors, a detailed revenue reconciliation ledger, e-commerce customer refund policy, and a signed statutory declaration confirming the absence of unlicensed money transmission activity. Include platform statements (Stripe/PayPal) evidencing that settlement flows directly from the platform to the Bermuda operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the UK parent to the Bermuda exempted company must be executed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer using a correspondent bank (typically Chase or HSBC in USD). Recommended routing: UK parent's UK bank → correspondent bank → Bermuda Tier-1 bank (e.g., HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield) → beneficiary Bermuda company's operational account. The wire must include the beneficiary company's legal name, account number, and a purpose code 'General Capital Injection' or 'GIF – Unrequited Transfers' (IMF BOP code). To prevent freezing by the receiving bank, pre-notify the Bermuda relationship manager with a board resolution authorising the injection and a capital contribution letter. For amounts exceeding USD 250,000, the bank may require an expedited AML review of the UK parent, so the parent should provide its annual accounts and a recent DBS check. No BMA prior approval is required for non-resident exempted companies; post-receipt, the bank can issue a capital receipt certificate for audit purposes.Trade disbursements for supplier payments (pet product purchases) should utilise a dual-lane clearing architecture: (1) Operational payments below USD 25,000: use SWIFT MT103 through the Bermuda bank's online electronic banking portal. For USD payments to suppliers in China or the U.S., settlement occurs via the Fedwire/CHIPS network through the bank's New York correspondent. (2) Large trade settlements above USD 25,000 or letters of credit: open a commercial irrevocable documentary letter of credit (LC) at the Bermuda bank, with the seller advised via the seller's bank. The LC should be confirmed by a top-tier U.S. or European bank to mitigate settlement risk. Payment to suppliers in invoiced EU/GBP currencies can be made directly from multi-currency sub-accounts without forced conversion. All outbound transfers must include a descriptive purpose code (e.g., '1000' for goods trade) and an invoice reference; this ensures smooth downstream reconciliation and satisfies the BMA's transaction monitoring expectations.Outright forward FX contracts (deliverable) with licensed Bermuda banks or their overseas parent desks for BMD/USD, USD/GBP, and USD/EUR up to 12 months tenor, subject to the bank's ISDA/CSA documentation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies not fully convertible or where settlement in the domestic currency is impractical (e.g., CNY/CNH NDFs) – permitted only via the Bermudian bank's offshore booking entity and require an underlying trade exposure.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps) between USD and GBP/EUR for matching cash flows from the UK parent and supplier invoices; permitted for hedging forecasted working capital exposures.Plain vanilla FX options (zero-cost collars) for hedging large one-off procurement exposures in non-USD currencies, permitted for non-resident exempted companies regardless of the absence of a Bermuda finance licence.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Bermuda levies no corporation tax, capital gains tax, or undistributed profits tax on an Exempted Company; taxable profits are nil by statute, subject to the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 and the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (deferral to 1 January 2035).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an Exempted Company to any shareholder, whether resident, non-resident, or beneficial owner. There is also no dividend tax or any other distribution tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation benchmark. Bermuda does not impose corporate income tax and does not mandate Local File or Master File preparation for exempted companies. For purposes of the economic substance test, the threshold is BMD 0 — every Bermuda Exempted Company carrying on a 'relevant activity' (including the e-commerce retail distribution of pet supplies) must maintain adequate physical substance and keep contemporaneous records showing the location of core income-generating activities. The de minimis pure holding exemption does not apply to distribution and service centre activities.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2016 and the Economic Substance Regulations 2018 apply. 'Plumbing supply e-commerce distribution' is a relevant activity (Distribution and Service Centre) and requires the exempted company to demonstrate that: (1) core income-generating activities in Bermuda are directed and managed by a sufficient number of employees; (2) the company has an adequate physical presence (premises) in Bermuda; and (3) the company incurs an adequate level of operating expenditure in Bermuda. The CIGA list includes purchasing and disposing of goods, taking and managing decisions, and undertaking the main distribution functions. A purely passive shell without local substance is non-compliant; the company must file an annual ESG-01 and may be audited by the Registrar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies Act 1981 (s. 177), false statements attract a fine of up to BMD 10,000 and up to 6 years' imprisonment. Where the conduct constitutes tax evasion under foreign law, the UK or other competent authority may impose substantial penalties on the foreign parent, and Bermuda may suspend the entity's exemption certificate. Asset-seizure orders may be issued by the Supreme Court.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for restitution of any improperly avoided tax plus interest; the Court may issue a travel ban preventing departure from Bermuda; directors may be disqualified for up to 10 years and are exposed to criminal imprisonment of up to 6 years.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Act 2016 (s. 8 and s. 15), non-compliance attracts a financial penalty of BMD 10,000; continuing non-compliance for more than 6 months invokes a BMD 100,000 penalty. The Registrar may strike the company off the register; the Financial Sanctions Committee may also issue a direction to pay enforcement costs.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the entity's non-payment of penalties; they may also be personally disqualified from holding directorships and subjected to a travel ban pending restoration of compliance. Individual directors face fines up to BMD 10,000 per offence.
Failure to Register Beneficial Ownership / AML Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Regulations 2017, contravention carries a fine of up to BMD 100,000 and, for continuing failure, a daily fine. The Office of the Director of Corporate Tax and the Financial Intelligence Agency may impose further AML penalties under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, including asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally liable for the daily fines; the Supreme Court may issue a travel ban and an order disqualifying the director from managing any company registered in Bermuda. Individuals may be imprisoned for up to 2 years for knowingly failing to provide information.
Money Laundering / Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 (POCA) and the AML/ATF Code of Practice 2015, money laundering carries a maximum penalty of up to 14 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine. Civil forfeiture of the electronic payment assets and online storefront may be ordered. The company's payment-processor merchant account may be terminated and reported to the Financial Action Task Force.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly failed to prevent laundering incur personal criminal liability and may be imprisoned for up to 14 years. Personal wallets and overseas assets can be subjected to confiscation; a travel ban is automatic pending investigation, and extradition to the United Kingdom is permissible under the mutual legal assistance treaty.
False Economic Substance Declaration

Statutory sanction: Knowingly or recklessly making a false substance declaration constitutes a criminal offence under the Economic Substance Act 2016 (s. 15(2)), with fines up to BMD 250,000, and a civil penalty of up to BMD 50,000 for each year of non-compliance. The entity may be struck off the register, and its Exempted Undertaking Tax Protection status may be withdrawn retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the BMD 250,000 fine if the false declaration was signed or authorised with intent; a restitution order may be issued to recapture foreign taxes that the company avoided. Directors are subject to a 10-year travel ban and up to 7 years' imprisonment for fraud by false representation under the Criminal Code Act 2015.
