Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Registrar of Companies (Companies Act 1981 registry, Economic Development Department, Ministry of Finance) for incorporation and corporate identifiers; Office of the Tax Commissioner (Tax Commissioner appointed under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, administered via the Bermuda Government portal www.gov.bm) for any Bermuda corporate income tax (CIT) registration, filing and assessment obligations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Bermuda Exempted Company by submitting the Memorandum of Association and required declarations to the Registrar of Companies, obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation and a unique company registration number. Step 2: No general income-tax TIN is issued because Bermuda imposes no income tax on out-of-scope entities; the company registration number is the standard statutory identifier used for banking, customs and regulatory filings. Step 3: Only where the entity is part of an in-scope multinational group (consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more per the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023), register with the Tax Commissioner within 30 days of becoming in-scope; the Tax Commissioner issues a CIT reference number for annual CIT returns and economic substance declarations. Step 4: Every exempted company must file an annual economic substance declaration with the Registrar of Companies through the COMPASS/online portal regardless of tax scope.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch classification regime exists in Bermuda. A Bermuda Exempted Company is, by default, resident for exchange-control and regulatory purposes but is not subject to Bermudian income, corporation, capital gains, withholding or distribution taxes unless brought within the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 scope (large multinational groups only). Eligible entities may apply for a Tax Assurance Certificate, historically issued under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, giving statutory assurance against the future imposition of taxes on income, capital, gains and distributions for a specified period; however, this assurance does not shield in-scope groups from the 15% CIT enacted to implement OECD Pillar Two. For in-scope groups, the Corporate Income Tax Act provides limited elections, including designation of a parent entity for consolidated filing, intra-group loss surrender elections, and transitional loss-utilisation elections, all subject to Tax Commissioner approval.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – Bermuda levies no VAT, GST, sales or use tax on any goods or services; the sole substantive indirect levy is customs import duty on imported goods under the Customs Tariff Act 1973, together with excise-type duties on a limited list of goods.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – there is no VAT/GST registration threshold because no general indirect tax is imposed on supplies of goods or services in Bermuda; importers of dutiable goods must obtain a customs declaration registration with the Bermuda Customs and Revenue Department irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – since no VAT/GST is levied in Bermuda, there is no export zero-rating mechanism, no input VAT credit, and no refund procedure to claim. Goods and services exported from Bermuda by an Exempted Company are wholly outside the scope of Bermuda indirect taxation; the only local fiscal event is customs duty on the importation of goods into Bermuda, and no duty is assessed on goods leaving the island. For groups within Bermuda CIT scope, export revenue is reportable on the annual CIT return and must be supported by contemporaneous documentation demonstrating that the economic activity and income sourcing are properly attributed under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023.

  • Electronic export declaration lodged via Bermuda Customs ASYCUDA World portal (export entry with customs reference number)
  • Commercial invoice / pro-forma invoice evidencing the export transaction
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods out of Bermuda
  • Packing list detailing shipped items
  • Certificate of Origin (requested by the destination jurisdiction or importer, where applicable)
  • Proof of payment / settlement records (card acquirer statements, PayPal or Stripe payout records) retained for anti-money-laundering and CIT substance audits

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, e-invoicing or API-based tax reporting regime exists in Bermuda for sales activities. Import and export declarations are processed electronically through Bermuda Customs using the ASYCUDA World single-window system, which requires electronic lodgement of the declaration, commercial invoice, transport documents, and any required permits or licences prior to physical clearance. B2B and B2C e-invoicing between Bermuda companies and overseas counterparties remains entirely unregulated and may follow commercial practice.

There is no VAT zero-rating fraud exposure in Bermuda because no VAT exists. Enforcement risk centres on: (i) inaccurate customs declarations – the Bermuda Customs and Revenue Department may impose forfeiture of goods and monetary penalties for false or fraudulent declarations under the Customs Tariff Act 1973; (ii) economic substance non-compliance – the Registrar of Companies may strike off, fine, or revoke the certificate of incorporation of an exempted company that fails to file its annual declaration, with fines for each day of non-compliance; (iii) for CIT-in-scope groups, late or incorrect filings attract fixed and daily penalties plus interest, with criminal exposure for fraudulent misstatement; and (iv) anti-money-laundering obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008 require retention of underlying export and settlement records for at least five years, and breaches carry significant fines and director disqualification. No retrospective VAT clawback can arise.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar (USD) under a Currency Board-like arrangement supervised by the BMA. Capital account and current account transactions for exempted companies that have been designated as 'non-resident' for exchange control purposes are, for practical purposes, fully convertible. Exempted companies may hold, transfer, and remit funds in any major global currency without BMA exchange control approval, provided the entity is owned exclusively by non-residents and transacts outside Bermuda. Dividends, distributions, and profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders are freely permitted; there are no capital controls, no withholding taxes on dividends, and no statutory remittance limits. The absence of capital transfer restrictions, together with the fixed USD peg, makes Bermuda a highly stable jurisdiction for cross-border treasury operations. However, entities that either transact with Bermuda-resident persons or maintain Bermuda-source revenue may be required to comply with the standard exchange control regime applicable to resident accounts, which can impose prior approval requirements on certain capital transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, any person resident in Bermuda who is entitled to sell or obtain foreign currency must (unless granted a general or specific exemption) offer that foreign currency to an authorised dealer (licensed bank) within one month of receipt or acquisition, and the dealer will convert it into Bermuda Dollars at the pegged rate. There is no fixed statutory percentage of export proceeds that must be surrendered; the surrender obligation applies to the full amount of foreign currency unless an exemption is granted. For a Bermuda Exempted Company designated as 'non-resident', this mandatory surrender obligation is disapplied: the company may retain export sales proceeds in USD, EUR, GBP, or other designated foreign currency in its offshore bank accounts without conversion to BMD. To rely on this exemption, the exempted company must maintain a 'non-resident' designation letter issued by the BMA and must not engage in local Bermuda business. All export-related foreign currency receipts should be invoiced in accordance with the company's permitted trade structure to preserve non-resident status and avoid unintended surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) — acting as the central bank and financial regulatory authority, administering the Exchange Control Act 1972 and Exchange Control Regulations 1973 in conjunction with the Bermuda Government; the Bank of England retains residual oversight as the UK overseas territory monetary authority, but day-to-day regulation is exercised solely by the BMA.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW. Bermuda does not have a separate fintech or EMI licensing regime under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999; any entity that accepts deposits or holds client funds must be licensed as a bank or deposit-taking company. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK-licensed electronic money institutions) generally cannot open a domestic Bermuda bank account for the purpose of directly holding Bermudian customer funds or making local BMD settlements. A Bermuda Exempted Company may, however, use an offshore EMI for cross-border treasury operations if the EMI is duly licensed in a reputable jurisdiction (UK, EU) and supports international wire transfers, but such EMI settlement accounts are typically held with partner banks in other jurisdictions and do not constitute a Bermuda clearing relationship. Local Tier 1 banks will not normally open sub-accounts or technical integration arrangements for regtech or online payment platforms unless the platform itself holds a BMA banking licence. Practical constraint: most global payment gateways (Stripe, PayPal) are treated as third-party payment aggregators, not as substitute bank accounts, and the exempted company must maintain at least one regulated Bermuda or international bank account for corporate treasury and capital compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions to the Bermuda Exempted Company must be made via a regulated financial institution wire transfer using SWIFT MT103. The recommended routing is from the UK parent entity's bank (e.g., HSBC UK, Barclays) to the Bermuda bank's for-correspondent account, typically through HSBC Bank USA or Citibank New York as an intermediary bank, ultimately credited to the Bermuda account. The wire must include a purpose code where required by the originating bank; in the UK we advise using 'REM' (remittance) or the BOP code specific to the originating transaction, but for a share-capital injection the remittance field must clearly state 'Capital injection / subscription for ordinary shares of Bermuda Exempted Company'. The remitter must provide a Board Resolution and an updated register of members evidencing the share allotment. For entities within the same corporate group, an Intercompany Loan Agreement or Share Subscription Agreement must be included in the onboarding KYC package. No BMA exchange control approval is needed for a non-resident exempted company if the capital is in a convertible currency and the ultimate shareholders are non-resident. The beneficiary bank will require the beneficiary account number, swift code (e.g., BNTBBMHM for Butterfield), and a clear narrative: 'Investment in exempted company — not a trading transaction'. If the amount exceeds the bank's AML threshold (typically USD 100,000), enhanced due diligence is triggered, and the bank may request the supporting capital documents prior to crediting the funds.For ongoing outbound trade settlements to suppliers, manufacturers, and logistics partners, the primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 international wire transfer. This rail is appropriate for commercial payments for inventory (e.g., purchasing timepieces, jewelry components, diamonds) and for operating expenses. For large, repeat, or high-value transactions, banks may require an underlying commercial invoice and may batch payments via MT101 or a multi-bank file format, but standard practice in Bermuda is individual MT103 per transaction. If the business enters into significant bulk import contracts (e.g., 100+ timepieces per annual cycle), a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) can be used, with the Bermuda bank as issuing bank and the supplier's bank as advising/confirming bank. The LC must comply with UCP 600; common forms include a sight LC for immediate payment against shipping documents or a deferred-payment LC (up to 90 days). For e-commerce payments via payment gateways (Stripe/PayPal), settlement funds are generally paid into the Bermuda bank account via a single ACH or wire from the gateway's bank; from there, the company disburses to suppliers via MT103. All outbound wires must include a purpose code (e.g., 'Goods trade' / 'Import payment') and an accurate deferred-fee description: 'Payment for retail inventory — not for goods to be resold in Bermuda'. The clearing time for MT103 via correspondent banking is typically 1–3 business days; SWIFT gpi is recommended to ensure real-time tracking and to facilitate compliance queries.Outright FX Forward contracts (deliverable) on GBP/USD, EUR/USD, and GBP/EUR, where the counterparty is the Bermuda bank or an international prime broker, used to fix the exchange rate for future supplier payments or customer settlements up to 12 months.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on restricted or less-liquid currencies (e.g., emerging-market currencies, crypto-affected or sanctioned-adjacent currencies) if any supplier requires settlement in such a currency; these are permitted only via regulated FX swaps broker or the bank's trading desk and are not available for all currencies.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) for large multi-period debt or financing arrangements to exchange GBP/EUR/USD payment streams into the functional currency of the Bermuda entity, subject to a demonstrated underlying commercial exposure and an internal hedging policy approved by the board.FX options (plain vanilla, over-the-counter) for a premium-limited hedging strategy to protect against adverse GBP/EUR movements while retaining upside; these are permissible for a Treasury-approved hedge and require the execution of an ISDA Master Agreement with the counterparty bank.Spot FX transactions with T+2 settlement for immediate treasury alignment, utilising the Bermuda bank's trading desk at the pegged BMD/USD rate plus a small spread; no third-party unregulated forex brokers are permitted for corporate treasury hedging.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW. Bermuda does not have a separate fintech or EMI licensing regime under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999; any entity that accepts deposits or holds client funds must be licensed as a bank or deposit-taking company. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK-licensed electronic money institutions) generally cannot open a domestic Bermuda bank account for the purpose of directly holding Bermudian customer funds or making local BMD settlements. A Bermuda Exempted Company may, however, use an offshore EMI for cross-border treasury operations if the EMI is duly licensed in a reputable jurisdiction (UK, EU) and supports international wire transfers, but such EMI settlement accounts are typically held with partner banks in other jurisdictions and do not constitute a Bermuda clearing relationship. Local Tier 1 banks will not normally open sub-accounts or technical integration arrangements for regtech or online payment platforms unless the platform itself holds a BMA banking licence. Practical constraint: most global payment gateways (Stripe, PayPal) are treated as third-party payment aggregators, not as substitute bank accounts, and the exempted company must maintain at least one regulated Bermuda or international bank account for corporate treasury and capital compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda TP threshold (BMD 0); Bermuda does not assess corporate income tax, so no Local File or Master File is required domestically. However, for UK-owned groups, the UK's OECD TP rules apply at consolidated revenue exceeding £20 million for Local File and £50 million for Master File; controlled transaction values exceeding £1 million in intercompany services and £5 million for goods require contemporaneous TP documentation in the UK. For a Bermudian seller of watches/jewelry, each intercompany transactions with UK related parties must be mapped to the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda's Economic Substance Regulations 2018 apply only to 'relevant activities' – banking, insurance, fund management, financing, leasing, headquarters, shipping, holding (pure equity holding), intellectual property, and distribution and service centres. Online retail of fashion jewellery and fine timepieces is not a relevant activity; accordingly, the company need only file an annual Economic Substance Declaration confirming no relevant activity is performed. The company must nonetheless observe the Anti-Money Laundering (AML) registration requirement and maintain a physical registered agent in Bermuda. If the entity later becomes a 'holding company' holding IP rights or Swiss watches inventory, it may transition into a relevant activity and will then be required to demonstrate economic substance in Bermuda.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / General Anti-Abuse (UK GAAR)

Statutory sanction: The UK's anti-abuse regime will reattribute all profits to the UK, impose Corporation Tax at 25%, late payment interest, and penalties of up to 100% of the tax understated; the Bermuda Registrar may strike off the company if its registered agent files a notice of non-compliance. A failure to report the offshore structure under UK DOTAS can trigger a £5,000 per-day penalty for continued non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Bermudian company who are also UK residents are exposed to personal liability for underpaid UK taxes if HMRC obtains a director liability order under Finance Act 2024; travel restrictions and asset freezing orders may be issued in the High Court, and the director may be disqualified for up to 15 years under the Company Directors Disqualification Act.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: For non-relevant activities, the strongest local sanction is a BMD 500,000 fine for a misleading or false Economic Substance Declaration under Bermuda's Economic Substance Amendment Act 2022. In the UK, HMRC can force an attribution of Group income via the transfer pricing legislation, applying a 20% surcharge on the excessive value of retained profits.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly file a false substance declaration face Bermuda criminal liability, personal fines of BMD 250,000 and imprisonment of up to 8 years. A UK court may also hold directors jointly and severally liable if the company is found to be an empty shell and the director has extracted assets without distributing dividends, triggering the unlawful return of capital regime.
AML/CFT Breach (Banking / Payment Processing)

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing (AML/ATF) Act 2022 applies to 'relevant financial business' and to any business accepting specified payments above €10,000 in cash. Online retailers accepting payment cards are not caught as principals, but if the business cryptographically operates a payments gateway or issues prepaid cards, it must licences. Failure to file a SAR (Suspicious Activity Report) can trigger a fine of up to BMD 1.5 million and the forfeiture of the goods in Bermuda; at the UK level, HMRC can issue a penalty for lack of AML registration where UK customers exist, with a maximum fine of £8,000.

Director personal liability: Corporate directors are liable for failing to train staff or appoint a Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO); UK FCA has the power to ban individuals from any senior management role if they are found 'not fit and proper' under the FIT test. Travel bans and extradition risk arise when the jewellery supplier is linked to sanctions violations involving luxury goods destined to embargoed jurisdictions; directors can be personally subject to worldwide freezing orders and imprisonment upon conviction in Bermuda or the UK.
Prohibited Domestic Business / Licence Violation

Statutory sanction: The Minister of Finance may impose a BMD 250,000 penalty and order the winding up of the company under the Companies Act; the directors' resolution authorising the trade in onshore territory is void ab initio; the Registrar will record a strike-off for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Any director who knowingly permitted prohibited domestic trading is jointly and severally liable for losses suffered by third parties. A shareholder in Bermuda can petition to wind up the company; the director may be disqualified from the board of any Bermuda company for 7 years and could be barred from re-entry to Bermuda under immigration and customs powers.
