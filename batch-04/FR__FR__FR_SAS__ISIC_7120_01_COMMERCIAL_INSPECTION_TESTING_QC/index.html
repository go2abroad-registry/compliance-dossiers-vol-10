Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; single-window business formalities: Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises, operated by INPI, at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; customs and excise authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a French-established SAS: incorporate via the INPI Guichet unique; INSEE automatically issues SIREN/SIRET, and the DGFiP service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) opens the corporate tax account and allocates the intra-Community VAT number (format 'FR' + 2-digit key + SIREN) without a separate application. The President must ensure the activity declaration and beneficial-ownership data are filed, and thereafter file monthly or quarterly VAT returns and annual corporate income tax returns (liasse fiscale) through impots.gouv.fr. For a non-resident entity without a French fixed establishment: file CERFA n° 15798*01 with the DGFiP 'unité des non-résidents' (Montreuil) for French VAT registration; non-EU businesses generally must appoint an accredited tax representative (représentant fiscal) unless a waiver applies; alternatively, non-established sellers of imported goods may register under IOSS, and EU distance sellers under OSS (with the €10,000 annual distance-sales threshold applying for intra-EU supplies).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SAS is a corporation subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level on profits derived from enterprises operated in France under the territoriality rule of CGI art. 209; it is not eligible for pass-through taxation.

Available election forms

French law has no US-style check-the-box election; classification follows legal form and substance. Available elections and mechanisms: (i) fiscal integration (intégration fiscale, CGI art. 223 A) for a French parent holding at least 95% of eligible French subsidiaries; (ii) single VAT taxable person grouping (assujetti unique, CGI art. 256 C quater, effective from 2023) for financially, economically and organisationally integrated French entities; (iii) option to waive the small-business VAT exemption franchise (CGI art. 293 B) in order to invoice VAT to B2B clients; (iv) French hybrid-mismatch rules transposing ATAD (CGI arts. 209 B and 212 bis) neutralising deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction outcomes; (v) option for the simplified VAT payment scheme and payment-basis VAT accounting where eligible (CGI art. 269-2-c).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Euro operates under full capital and current account convertibility per EU and French FX liberalization. Profit remittances and service fee receipts face no convertibility barriers for EU-resident or qualifying non-resident entities. Hurdles limited to standard tax withholding (e.g., 12.8% on dividends) and beneficial ownership verification; no capital controls apply to routine corporate inflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export-related receipts required under French law. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted at spot rates per commercial contract terms. Voluntary surrender facilities available through Banque de France for statistical purposes only.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for cross-border liquidity via regulated EMI providers such as Wise and Revolut Business. Legal constraints confined to French AML/CFT obligations, TRACFIN reporting, and e-CBI tax filing; no prohibition on offshore digital EMIs for EU-established SAS entities.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000. Mandatory French Local File and Master File obligations arise where the French entity's annual turnover or balance-sheet total reaches EUR 400 million, or where a controlling or controlled foreign entity has annual turnover or balance-sheet total of EUR 400 million or more. Country-by-Country Reporting mandatory at EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. Instead, French tax law requires genuine substance for entity-level tax benefits through LPF article L64 (abus de droit), Article 57 CGI controlled-entity rules, and OECD/EU beneficial-ownership requirements. For the QC and pre-shipment inspection sector, a SAS must demonstrate physical premises, qualified employees, performance of actual inspection protocols, insurance coverage, decision-making power in France, and bank accounts with a transparent business purpose. Shell entities cannot obtain treaty relief or retain deductibility of intercompany charges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: CGI article 1741: up to 5 years' imprisonment and fine of EUR 75,000; aggravated circumstances, such as use of foreign accounts or concealment through an entity organised outside France, increase the maximum sentence to 7 years' imprisonment and EUR 500,000 fine. Administrative penalties of 40%-80% apply, plus monthly late-payment interest at 0.20%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct the false return are personally exposed to criminal conviction, imprisonment, travel bans, and disqualification from managing a French company. French courts may pierce the corporate veil in cases of fraudulent concealment, rendering the director civilly liable for restitution of the evaded corporate income tax.
