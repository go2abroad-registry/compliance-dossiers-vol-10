Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Office of the Tax Commissioner, Government of Bermuda, Hamilton (online portal: https://www.gov.bm/taxtools); Registrar of Companies, Ministry of Finance for corporate registration

        
E-government filing portal
A Bermuda Exempted Company is incorporated via the Registrar of Companies and receives a Company Registration Number (CRN), which serves as its statutory tax identifier. No income tax TIN is issued because Bermuda does not impose corporate income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, or VAT. If the exempted company employs local staff or makes payroll payments in Bermuda, the employer must separately register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner for Payroll Tax and Social Insurance accounts, obtaining a Payroll Tax ID for those specific obligations. Foreign-owned companies with no local payroll, no property, and no physical presence in Bermuda are not required to apply for any direct tax registration. The CRN is accepted for bank account opening, economic substance reporting, and international tax documentation (e.g., W-8BEN-E or CRS forms).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone

        
Available election forms
Not applicable. The exempted company enjoys a statutory guarantee of immunity from all local taxation (including income, withholding, capital, and corporate taxes) for 50 years, per the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966. No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are available because there is no Bermuda income tax to elect into or out of. The company may elect to be classified as resident or non-resident for exchange control purposes, and may apply for Category 1 or Category 2 tax-exempt status under the same Act, but this election has no corporate tax consequence in Bermuda.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
None; Bermuda levies no VAT or GST. Indirect taxation is limited to import customs duty (and excise duty on certain goods such as fuel, liquor, tobacco, and vehicles) administered by the Bermuda Customs Department.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration regime exists in Bermuda. Importers/exporters may require a customs broker license and must obtain a Commercial Importer/Exporter code with the Customs Department for all sea/air cargo clearances.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Since Bermuda has no VAT/GST, exports are entirely outside the scope of Bermuda indirect taxation (not merely zero-rated). No refund claim is possible or necessary because no tax is charged on the sale. Under the Customs Tariff Act, goods exported from Bermuda within a defined time (generally 30 days) may be eligible for drawback of any import duty paid on raw materials or components, if the goods are re-exported in the same or a substantially transformed condition. Dehydrated vegetables and spices exported from Bermuda would not be subject to any Bermuda indirect tax regardless of destination.

No real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate for exports. Bermuda operates an electronic customs declaration and cargo manifest system; all import and export entries must be transmitted electronically through the Bermuda Customs Department's approved software or its official online portal. No digital stamping, API-based authentication, or continuous transaction controls are required for VAT purposes.

      
No VAT zero-rating or export VAT fraud exists in Bermuda because there is no VAT/GST. However, false export declarations or failure to produce supporting documents for a duty drawback claim are subject to penalties under the Bermuda Customs Act 1963, including forfeiture of goods, fines up to BD$5,000 (or three times the value of the goods), and imprisonment for up to two years for deliberate evasion. The customs authority can conduct retrospective audits and claw back any drawback paid if the goods are not exported or the supporting documentation is defective. Given the absence of VAT, no clawback of output VAT can arise.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged by statute at a fixed 1:1 parity with the United States Dollar (USD); both currencies circulate interchangeably in Bermuda, and BMD is freely convertible to USD at par without BMA approval. For a Bermuda Exempted Company holding a Non-Resident (Exchange Control) endorsement, current-account transactions (trade settlements, commissions, fees) and capital-account transactions (share capital injections, intercompany loan repayments, dividends) are permitted without per-transaction BMA consent, provided the company does not transact with Bermuda residents in BMD and holds no Bermudian domestic real property. Profit remittance and dividend distributions are unrestricted and tax-free at source, as Bermuda imposes no income, corporation, capital-gains, or withholding taxes on exempted companies. The residual hurdles are procedural rather than fiscal: the bank requires up-to-date beneficial-ownership filings, board-resolved distribution authority, and confirmation that the company maintains valid Non-Resident exchange-control designation with no Bermuda economic-substance notification requirement for pure foreign trading activity.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No mandatory surrender or conversion ratio applies. A Non-Resident designated Bermuda Exempted Company may retain export proceeds, including USD receipts from bulk dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper, indefinitely in BMA-licensed bank accounts or external foreign-currency accounts without BMA consent under the Exchange Control Act 1972. Statutory timelines requiring Bermuda-resident businesses to offer foreign-currency receipts to an authorised dealer are expressly disapplied for non-resident exempted companies. Governance requirement: maintain a complete export-file audit trail comprising commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, certificates of origin, third-party quality certificates (e.g., SGS or Bureau Veritas), and SWIFT payment confirmations for at least five years to withstand AML, sanctions, and trade-compliance review.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) - Bermuda's central-bank-equivalent and integrated financial regulator, established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, administering exchange control (Exchange Control Act 1972), banking licensing (Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999), and AML/ATF supervision (Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2018). As Bermuda is a British Overseas Territory, UK financial sanctions administered by HM Treasury (OFSI) extend to Bermuda, and FATF international standards apply via the BMA. The Bank of England performs no direct banking supervision in Bermuda but coordinates on crisis management, sanctions policy, and AML cooperation.

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility: Low as a primary banking substitute; Medium as a supplementary settlement overlay. Bermuda has no dedicated EMI licensing class; deposit-taking and financial-services activity in or from Bermuda requires BMA licensing under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, and digital-asset activities are separately regulated under the Digital Asset Business Act 2018. Offshore digital EMIs (Airwallex, Currencycloud, Wise Business, Dukascopy) are legally usable only as non-Bermuda settlement tools where the EMI is authorised in a FATF-compliant jurisdiction (FCA UK, MFSA, ASIC, MAS), does not solicit Bermuda domestic banking business, and the exempted company maintains its principal regulated banking relationship with a BMA-licensed institution. Recommended positioning: deploy offshore EMIs for high-velocity, small-ticket supplier payments in the China/India/Turkey dehydrated-spice corridors while retaining HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield for LC issuance, large wire traffic, escrow, and multi-currency sweeping.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Beneficial-ownership opacity: nominee shareholders, bearer shares, discretionary trust layers, or a multi-tier offshore holding chain obscuring the ultimate natural-person UBO.
        
Root cause: Beneficial-ownership opacity: nominee shareholders, bearer shares, discretionary trust layers, or a multi-tier offshore holding chain obscuring the ultimate natural-person UBO.

        
Defense evidence: Beneficial Ownership Declaration filed under the Companies and Limited Liability Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Act 2017; certified Register of Members and Register of Directors; full legal-entity structure chart descending to ultimate natural persons with passport copies; statutory declarations signed by each UBO; confirmation letter from the BMA-licensed registered agent verifying the BOA filing and ongoing six-month update obligations.

      

      
      

        Unverified source of initial capital or share premium: the opening equity injection (typically USD 1,000 to 500,000 for a spice-trading exempted company) lacks a corroborated paper trail.
        
Root cause: Unverified source of initial capital or share premium: the opening equity injection (typically USD 1,000 to 500,000 for a spice-trading exempted company) lacks a corroborated paper trail.

        
Defense evidence: Six-month bank statements of the funding shareholder; executed share subscription agreement and board resolution; notarised statement of source of wealth and source of funds (accumulated trade receivables, asset disposal proceeds, or salary and bonus with supporting payslips and tax assessments); funds-flow memo tracing each wire leg from originator to beneficiary; share certificates issued to evidence capital.

      

      
      

        High-risk or sanctioned-corridor counterparties in the spice supply chain, including suppliers or buyers in FATF-grey-listed or OFSI-sanctioned jurisdictions, or freight carriers requiring enhanced screening.
        
Root cause: High-risk or sanctioned-corridor counterparties in the spice supply chain, including suppliers or buyers in FATF-grey-listed or OFSI-sanctioned jurisdictions, or freight carriers requiring enhanced screening.

        
Defense evidence: Named-party sanctions screening results covering all counterparties, beneficial owners, shipping lines, freight forwarders, and intermediary banks (OFSI, EU, UN, OFAC); signed supplier and buyer due-diligence questionnaires; certificates of origin, phytosanitary certificates, and HACCP/ISO 22000 quality certifications; customs declarations and applicable export/import licences; written transaction-flow narrative confirming no sanctioned vessels, entities, or flag states are involved.

      

      
      

        Transaction-profile mismatch: declared wholesale business (ISIC 4638 - dehydrated vegetables and spices) contradicted by round-dollar amounts, rapid fund on-passing, or invoice values clearly divergent from prevailing international commodity prices.
        
Root cause: Transaction-profile mismatch: declared wholesale business (ISIC 4638 - dehydrated vegetables and spices) contradicted by round-dollar amounts, rapid fund on-passing, or invoice values clearly divergent from prevailing international commodity prices.

        
Defense evidence: Executed purchase orders, proforma invoices, and master supply agreements specifying Incoterms and product specifications; transport evidence comprising bills of lading, airway bills, and freight-forwarder CMRs; third-party inspection and quality certificates (SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek) covering weight, grade, moisture, aflatoxin content, and value; customs entry records and warehouse receipts; five-year document-retention matrix with monthly trade-finance reconciliation evidencing that every receipt or payment maps to an underlying physical trade.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold in Bermuda (local currency: BMD). Under Economic Substance Regulations 2018, an exempted company carrying on a relevant activity must maintain adequate substance records with no local currency threshold triggering documentation; the entity must file an annual Economic Substance Declaration (ES Declaration) through the Registrar's portal. Additional OECD Pillar Two / GloBE qualifying documentation may be required if the multinational group has annual group revenue exceeding €750 million, but this is an OECD-level documentation obligation addressed by the Bermuda Economic Substance (GloBE) regime.

      
Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations 2018 (as amended) require every exempted company that carries on a relevant activity in Bermuda to demonstrate adequate economic substance in Bermuda commensurate with the relevant activity. A pure equity holding entity or pure treasury entity is subject to reduced substance requirements. For a dehydrates and spices trading company, 'distribution and service business' and 'headquarters business' are relevant activities only where conducted from Bermuda; a pure foreign trading company that does not conduct relevant activity in Bermuda must still file the ES Declaration but is not required to maintain substance in Bermuda. Where the entity is UK tax resident by central management and control, UK economic substance rules also apply.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / UK CFC & Tax Residency Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: HMRC may raise a UK corporation tax assessment for the full tax year, apply interest on unpaid tax, and impose penalties of up to 100% of the tax understated for deliberate and concealed inaccuracies. The entity would also lose Bermuda tax-exemption protection because the activity is not undertaken in Bermuda except as a foreign-incorporated taxable UK entity. Penalties for failure to notify chargeability can be up to 100% of the unpaid tax plus daily penalties.

        
Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for UK taxes under the Finance Act 2016 and associated provisions if they are knowingly concerned in the fraudulent evasion of corporation tax. The directors may be disqualified under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986 for up to 15 years, subject to personal asset freezes and confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002, and senior officers may face imprisonment for up to 7 years for fraudulent evasion of income tax or VAT under the Taxes Management Act 1970 (conspiracy to defraud HMRC can carry an unlimited fine and up to 14 years' imprisonment).

      

      
      

        Economic Substance Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations 2018, the Registrar may levy a penalty of up to 250,000 Bermudian dollars plus a further daily penalty of up to 10,000 Bermudian dollars for continued non-compliance. The Registrar may also strike off the company, and the Minister of Finance may petition the Supreme Court for a winding-up order or an order that the company ceases its relevant activity.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally liable for knowingly providing false or misleading information in an economic substance declaration, with fines and potential criminal prosecution under the Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations and the general criminal law. Personal liability extends to nominees and local corporate service providers who knowingly facilitate false filings, and one director may also be personally named in civil recovery proceedings by the Bermuda Government for the company's unpaid penalties, including travel bans or restriction orders in an appropriate case.

      

      
      

        AML / Beneficial Ownership Irregularity
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Act 2017 and the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering) Regulations, the company is liable to a maximum fine of 200,000 Bermudian dollars for an offence of failing to provide accurate beneficial ownership information, and continued offences may lead to a daily default fine. Financial institutions may be directed to freeze transactional flows where AML verification is absent. Suspicious transactions report obligations apply to the relevant financial service providers. The company may be struck off or subjected to a compliance order by the Supreme Court.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit or assist in the concealment of beneficial ownership face personal criminal liability with imprisonment of up to 2 years. Reckless or fraudulent involvement in money laundering attracts a maximum of 14 years' imprisonment under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 (Bermuda), alongside confiscation orders and director disqualification. Individual directors may be banned from serving as officers of any Bermuda company for a specified period.

      

      
      

        Foreign Tax Evasion / Penalties for Facilitating Offshore Tax Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 (UK) and the Bermuda Criminal Code, a body corporate that fails to prevent the facilitation of foreign tax evasion is subject to an unlimited fine, and where relevant, HMRC can issue an accelerated payment notice and a follower notice requiring early payment of disputed tax. There is no cap on civil financial penalties for deliberate offshore non-compliance; UK penalties for deliberate errors on offshore matters can be up to 200% of the tax. The entity may be subject to a disclosure of tax avoidance scheme (DOTAS) reference number and public CFC attribution.

        
Director personal liability: Senior managers who consent or connive in the facilitation of overseas tax evasion commit a criminal offence and can be imprisoned for up to 2 years. UK assets of the directors can be frozen by a worldwide freezing order, and directors can be pursued for equitable compensation for breach of fiduciary duty in respect of exposure to foreign tax liabilities. Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the exempted company is a mere facade concealing the director's actual business; resulting liability includes restitution of diverted profits, damages, and disqualification as a director for up to 15 years.
