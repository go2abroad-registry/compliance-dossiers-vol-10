Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Registrar of Companies (RoC), Ministry of Finance, Bermuda (portal: https://www.roc.gov.bm) - responsible for incorporation, annual filings and Economic Substance Declarations; Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Bermuda (portal: https://www.gov.bm) - administers payroll tax, stamp duty, land tax and hotel occupancy tax; Bermuda Customs Department (portal: https://www.gov.bm) - administers customs duties and import/export clearance. Bermuda has no corporate income tax authority: an exempted company holds a Tax Assurance Certificate freezing all future Bermudian taxes on income, profits, dividends and capital gains until 2035 under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate under the Companies Act 1981 by delivering the Memorandum of Association, declaration of compliance and registered-office particulars to the RoC; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and unique Company Number, which serves as the statutory identifier for all Bermudian filings. Step 2 - Apply for a Tax Assurance Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966. Step 3 - Appoint a Bermuda-resident agent and secretary and maintain a registered office in Bermuda; pay annual government fees to remain in good standing. Step 4 - Register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner for Payroll Tax only if Bermuda employees are engaged (within 15 days of first remuneration payment); this is not an income tax registration. Step 5 - Register with Bermuda Customs for an Importer/Exporter Code only when goods physically transit Bermuda; offshore drop-shipping of dried goji berries does not trigger local customs registration. Step 6 - File the annual Economic Substance Declaration on the RoC portal, including the business-activity classification under the Economic Substance Act 2018. Step 7 (UK nexus only) - If the entity makes taxable supplies in the UK or establishes a UK fixed establishment, apply to HMRC on Form VAT1 for a UK VAT registration number as a non-established taxable person before the first UK supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - A Bermuda Exempted Company is entirely outside the Bermuda income-tax net: no corporation tax, capital gains tax, dividend tax, withholding tax or VAT; profits from offshore trading (bulk exports of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals) are tax-free regardless of shareholder residence. Non-resident ownership is unrestricted and no minimum capital is required. Subject to the Economic Substance Act 2018 (relevant activities include distribution and service centre or pure holding entity) and to the Corporate Income Tax and Global Minimum Tax Act 2023 for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Available election forms

(1) Tax Assurance Certificate election under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 - a statutory election freezing any future Bermudian tax on income, profits or capital for the legislative protection period. (2) No check-the-box election exists in Bermuda: an exempted company is a non-transparent body corporate and cannot elect pass-through treatment; hybrid transparency is achievable only through an exempted partnership or trust wrapper. (3) Economic Substance Act 2018 - no election, but the entity may be designated a pure equity holding entity with simplified substance obligations. (4) Corporate Income Tax and Global Minimum Tax Act 2023 - in-scope constituents may elect the Bermuda Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax (QDMTT) safe harbour and apply the substance-based income exclusion formula; the Minister of Finance may designate the CIT commencement date by order. (5) UK CTA 2010 Part 6A - no effective hybrid-mismatch election exists for a Bermuda company (non-transparent), but UK-parented groups must manage imported mismatch and reverse hybrid risks.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST or sales-and-use tax in Bermuda. Indirect taxation comprises (i) customs duties under the Customs Tariff Act 1968 on goods entering Bermuda, (ii) stamp duty under the Stamp Act 1971 on specified instruments, (iii) hotel occupancy tax (10%) and payroll tax as selective consumption and labour taxes. Exports and transshipments are entirely outside the Bermudian indirect-tax base. For UK/EU market entry, the operative regimes are UK VAT (VATA 1994) and EU VAT (Directive 2006/112/EC) applied at destination.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - Bermuda imposes no turnover-based indirect-tax registration threshold; Customs Importer/Exporter Code registration is transaction-based with no monetary threshold. UK nexus: if the Bermuda entity is a UK taxable person, HMRC registration becomes compulsory when taxable turnover exceeds GBP 90,000 in 12 months (VATA 1994 Sch. 1); non-established taxable persons must register before the first UK supply; voluntary registration is available for zero-rated exporters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda: exports are inherently outside the Bermudian indirect-tax base - no export duty, no VAT chain and no refundable input tax. UK VAT (if registered): exports of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals to third-country customers are zero-rated under VATA 1994 s.30 and SI 1995/2518 regs. 129-134, provided the goods are physically exported from the UK within the statutory time limit (normally 3 months) and the supplier holds prescribed evidence; services connected with offshore wholesale (brokerage, commissionaire or marketing) are generally outside the scope of UK VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rules (Art. 44 VAT Directive).

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from the UK/EU or transit port to the destination market

No real-time fiscal clearance, API fiscalisation or digital stamping mandate exists in Bermuda; the Customs Department operates an electronic single-window declaration system, and ordinary commercial invoices, bills of lading and packing lists are accepted evidence. The UK does not mandate continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing for B2B exports; where EU counterparties are involved, national e-invoicing mandates (e.g., Italy SdI, France 2026-2027) and PEPPOL BIS 3.0 may apply by contract or local law.

Bermuda: false customs declarations attract fines up to BMD 10,000 or three times the duty evaded, forfeiture of goods and imprisonment under the Customs Tariff Act 1968; Economic Substance Act 2018 non-compliance attracts administrative penalties up to BMD 250,000 plus daily default penalties of BMD 5,000 per month and, for directors, imprisonment up to 14 years; GloBE/CIT Act 2023 non-filing penalties reach BMD 1,000,000. UK clawback: false UK zero-rating claims are assessed retrospectively under VATA 1994 s.73 (up to 20 years where dishonesty is involved), with interest and penalties up to 100% of the tax (FA 2007 Sch. 24), and criminal prosecution for dishonest evasion under CEMA 1979 s.170 (up to 7 years' imprisonment; joint and several liability for facilitators).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at par (1:1) to the United States Dollar (USD), and both currencies circulate interchangeably. Bermuda operates a fully convertible capital account for non-resident designated entities; a Bermuda Exempted Company that is designated as 'non-resident' for exchange-control purposes may hold, remit, and receive foreign currency without prior BMA approval and may repatriate dividends, liquidation proceeds, and capital without consent. Resident-status entities, in contrast, require BMA permissions for certain capital transactions. Profit remittance is structurally unobstructed: with no Bermuda corporate income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, or exchange-control gate, the only material constraint is the availability of cleared USD liquidity at commercial banks and the completion of standard AML/KYC source-of-funds verification for dividends and intercompany repayments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and BMA regulations, Bermuda residents (including resident-status companies) must, within one month of receipt, offer any foreign currency to an authorised dealer for conversion into BMD, with limited retention thresholds for business working capital. A Bermuda Exempted Company that has obtained 'non-resident' designated status under the Act is exempt from this statutory surrender requirement and may maintain unrestricted foreign-currency accounts abroad and in Bermuda; therefore no mandatory conversion ratio, no export-license precondition, and no compulsory remittance timeline applies to the target entity's inward export proceeds, provided the designated non-resident designation is filed and maintained with the BMA through the licensed registered agent.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) acting under the Exchange Control Act 1972 (Bermuda) and the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999; monetary policy autonomy rests with the BMA while the British Crown retains ultimate sovereignty through the UK Government's Governor-appointment process.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility as an ancillary receivable-collection channel via UK-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and EU/UK payment service providers offering SWIFT, SEPA, and multi-currency IBAN virtual accounts; Low viability as the primary treasury rail because Bermuda's BMA mandates that deposit-taking and settlement of corporate operating funds be conducted through a BMA-licensed bank under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, and the Digital Asset Business Act 2018 does not authorise fiat e-money issuance or merchant acquiring. Offshore EMIs may therefore onboard the Exempted Company for expedited client collections and card payments, but all clearing, FX execution, and capital flows must ultimately transit a licensed Bermuda commercial bank to preserve regulatory substance and audit integrity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Bermuda prescribes no statutory local currency threshold for local or master transfer pricing documentation because it has no corporate income tax and no formal transfer pricing rules. The relevant threshold arises under the UK’s parent jurisdiction and the OECD BEPS framework: UK Transfer Pricing documentation is required where the UK-connected party is a large multinational or where transactions exceed the HMRC territorial thresholds for master file (group revenue above EUR 750 million with UK entities) and local file (intercompany transactions exceeding GBP 1 million for low-margin services or GBP 5 million for goods and other supplies). Bermuda exempted companies should nevertheless maintain contemporaneous arm’s length evidence for all connected-party transactions in order to support economic substance filings and to mitigate UK CFC, US GILTI, and global minimum tax exposures.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda’s Economic Substance Regulations 2018 (ESR), issued under the Economic Substance Act 2018, apply to exempted companies carrying on relevant activities including distribution and service centre businesses. A company deriving income from international bulk export distribution of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals may be classified as a pure equity holding entity or a distribution and service centre business if it holds intangible property and earns income from facilitating the sale of goods to related parties. The company must file an annual Economic Substance Return, report its level of core income-generating activities (CIGAs), and demonstrate that it is managed and directed in Bermuda, has adequate employees, premises, and expenditure in Bermuda proportionate to its activity, and performs its CIGAs in Bermuda. Where the entity is a pure equity holding company, a reduced substance test applies, requiring compliance with statutory obligations and adequate human and physical resources. Failure to satisfy ESR results in penalties up to BMD 250,000 per relevant activity and potential automatic exchange of information with the UK tax authority. The UK’s global minimum tax (Pillar Two) income inclusion rule may apply to Bermuda subsidiaries of UK-parented multinationals with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million, notwithstanding the Multinational Operations (Tax) Act 2024, and requires Bermuda entities to file a top-up tax return.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Illegal Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: HM Revenue & Customs may invoke CFC anti-avoidance provisions under Taxation (International and Other Provisions) Act 2010, assess the UK parent on apportioned Bermuda profits, impose penalties equal to up to 200% of the tax owing under the penalty regimes in Schedule 24 FA 2007, and issue cross-border information notices. In Bermuda, the Economic Substance Act requires the Registrar to impose a penalty of BMD 250,000 per relevant activity and a further BMD 10,000 per day for continued failure after the compliance notice period.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for UK tax debts where they knowingly participate in fraudulent evasion of income tax, contrary to Section 144 FA 2000, punishable by imprisonment for up to 7 years and unlimited fines. The Bermuda court can order disqualification of directors and restitution of misappropriated assets. Persons managing a company that fails to meet economic substance obligations may be prosecuted under Bermuda law; directors face fines of up to BMD 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 2 years.
Shell Company Abuse / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Under the Beneficial Ownership (Companies) Amendment Act 2018, failure to maintain and file beneficial ownership information is an offence subject to fines up to BMD 25,000 on the company and potentially BMD 50,000 on the corporate service provider. The Registrar of Companies may strike off the company and the Tax Commissioner may withdraw tax protection. In the UK, the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 enables the registrar to strike out overseas entities and impose financial penalties, and the Unexplained Wealth Orders regime can seize assets associated with shell-structure concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the company to fail in beneficial ownership disclosure are liable to imprisonment for up to 6 months in Bermuda (2 years on indictment) and fines of up to BMD 25,000. UK directors who fail to prevent facilitation of tax evasion under Section 45 CTA 2010 face unlimited fines; the court may order director disqualification orders for up to 15 years under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986. Travel bans may be imposed at the Bermuda Magistrates’ Court upon an application in connection with a serious default or fraud investigation.
Anti-Money Laundering / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Bermuda’s Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008, as amended, require regulated corporate service providers to conduct CDD and report suspicious activity to the Financial Intelligence Agency. Non-compliance attracts fines up to BMD 1,000,000 for the company. UK enforcement under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 provides criminal custody for up to 7 years for wilful breach of financial sanctions, and the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation can impose monetary penalties for up to 50% of the value of the breach.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers can be prosecuted for money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 (Bermuda), with penalties of imprisonment up to 14 years and fines at the court’s discretion. The UK Serious Fraud Office can impose a Serious Crime Prevention Order and confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002, including freezing personal assets and travel bans; directors are personally liable for failure to prevent money laundering where there has been deliberate disregard of suspicious transaction controls.
HMRC Registration and VAT Fraud

Statutory sanction: HMRC may register the Bermuda company compulsorily, issue a VAT assessment for past supplies with a 20-year assessment window in cases of deliberate conduct, impose a penalty of up to 100% of the VAT liability under Schedule 24 FA 2007, and charge default interest. Breach of the VAT accountability rules under the Finance Act 1994 for direct sales to UK consumers via fulfilment houses triggers customs penalties under the Customs and Excise Management Act 1979.

Director personal liability: Directors of a VAT-registered company can be made personally liable for VAT debts where the company avoids payment and the director knowingly enabled the avoidance, under Section 39 VATA 1994, including a personal liability notice. For fraudulent evasion of VAT, directors can be imprisoned for up to 7 years and receive fines of any amount; on conviction in the Crown Court, confiscation orders can require disgorgement of personal wealth.
