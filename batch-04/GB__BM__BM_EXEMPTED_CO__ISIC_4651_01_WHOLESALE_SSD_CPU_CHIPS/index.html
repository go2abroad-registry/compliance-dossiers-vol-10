VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax regime. Bermuda levies only import Customs Duty (Customs Tariff Act 1973, enforced by HM Customs) and limited Stamp Duty (Stamp Duty Act 1976); no indirect tax is chargeable on supplies of goods or services.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Bermuda imposes no turnover-based indirect tax and therefore no registration threshold; no VAT number or fiscal registration can be obtained. Customs registration (Customs Broker/Importer Account) is required only for physical import/export entries.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - the absence of VAT means no 0% export rating or refund mechanism exists. Bermuda customs duty does not apply to goods exported from the territory; the typical drop-shipped wholesale model (SSD/CPU chips moving directly from overseas manufacturers to global buyers without Bermuda landing) triggers no Bermuda tax event.

  • Bermuda Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World) - required only where goods are physically exported from Bermuda; waived for drop-shipment flow-through
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Air Waybill or Ocean Bill of Lading (transport evidence)
  • Tax Exemption Certificate and Economic Substance Return acknowledgment (substantiates zero Bermuda income-tax exposure)

None. No real-time fiscal clearance, CTC (continuous transaction control), or digital stamping regime exists for B2B invoices. Import/export declarations must be filed electronically through ASYCUDA World (Bermuda Customs single-window); no API e-invoicing clearance obligation applies to wholesale transactions.

No VAT clawback is applicable. Scrutiny focuses on (a) Economic Substance Act 2018 compliance for the wholesale/trading relevant activity - failure to file or false filings attract escalation penalties up to BD$250,000 for companies, daily default penalties, and imprisonment for officers; (b) Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 accuracy for in-scope groups - understatement penalties and retrospective adjustment of tax payable plus interest; (c) Customs export misdeclaration penalties and forfeiture under the Customs Tariff Act 1973; (d) anti-money-laundering record-keeping under the Proceeds of Crime (AML/ATF) regime for cross-border wires.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at par (1:1) to the US Dollar, and USD is legal tender and circulates freely in Bermuda. For a Bermuda Exempted Company designated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes, both current-account items (trade receivables, brokerage commissions, service fees) and capital-account items (paid-up equity, shareholder loans, capital repatriation, profit distributions) are fully convertible with no BMA prior-approval requirement. There are no exchange-control permits, no surrender obligations, and no withholding taxes on dividends, interest, royalties, or capital gains. The residual constraints are bank-level: all accounts must be held with a BMA-licensed depositary, and transaction-level sanctions/AML screening under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2018 applies to every wire.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender regime exists in Bermuda. A non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company may retain export receipts from SSD and CPU wholesale brokerage in any convertible currency, hold them in offshore or on-island accounts, and redeploy them freely without any mandatory conversion ratio or statutory remittance timeline. The only documentary obligation is prudential: the BMA-licensed receiving bank may request the underlying commercial invoice, shipping documents, and an export entry to evidence the transaction for AML and economic-substance record-keeping, but no conversion, surrender, or BMA licensing is triggered.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, with delegated exchange-control and currency-management powers under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, as amended through the 2009 liberalisation; monetary and banking supervision is exercised by the BMA under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. The BMA does not issue e-money-institution (EMI) licences, and no Bermuda-domiciled EMI can act as a primary settlement bank. A UK-authorised FCA EMI or an OECD-regulated digital bank may serve as an ancillary collection rail for the UK digital-trade gateway, receiving EUR/GBP receipts from EU and UK buyers and providing UK IBANs. However, the Bermuda treasury backbone must remain at a BMA-licensed depositary because: (i) BMA-regulated banks will not accept an unlicensed EMI's account statement as sole verification of corporate substance; (ii) mainstream EMIs typically decline the Medium_Risk_Enhanced semiconductor wholesale profile absent a hard-banked Bermuda relationship; and (iii) Escrow_Inspection_Wire settlements require a regulated bank escrow agreement. Legal deployment: use a UK EMI solely as a low-value collection conduit with daily treasury sweeping to the Bermuda operating account, supported by an intercompany treasury services agreement and a parent guarantee.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-loan funding is routed by Swift MT103 from the UK gateway account or any OECD jurisdiction to the Bermuda operating account at a BMA-licensed bank. Payment-purpose reference must state 'Equity injection / paid-up share capital' or 'Subordinated shareholder loan' matching the corporate records; no BMA exchange-control approval is required for a non-resident exempted company. The verification bundle comprises: board resolution approving the capital call or loan; subscription agreement and share allotment document; executed shareholder loan agreement; source-of-funds declaration from the lender; updated register of members; and a reference to the economic-substance archival file. The bank will apply standard AML verification prior to crediting, and funds should remain unencumbered for at least two business days to satisfy source-of-funds checks.Operative clearing rails are Swift_TT and Escrow_Inspection_Wire. Supplier settlements are executed via Swift MT103 (and MT103 STP single-customer-credit-transfer) from the Bermuda operating account; for large batch purchases exceeding USD 250,000, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit, preferably confirmed by a UK or EEA bank, is issued at the request of the Bermuda buyer with the seller's complying presentation against shipping and inspection documents. Escrow-Inspection-Wire releases are made by MT103 between the segregated escrow sub-account and the beneficiary account only upon presentation of the independent inspection certificate and final invoice, with the goods-description narrative embedded in field 70 of the message for customs, sanctions, and AML traceability. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% general corporate income tax; no capital gains tax; no branch profits tax. A 15% Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax (DMTT) applies under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CITA 2023) solely to constituent entities of in-scope MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Entity identification Constituent entity legal name, Bermuda Registrar (BR) number, tax identification number, registered office, compliance representative and electronic contact details. Non-resident shareholders are not filers; the Bermuda entity files under its own BR number and must disclose its ultimate parent entity and intermediate holding chain. Late filing attracts a statutory fixed penalty plus a daily surcharge for each day the return remains outstanding; interest accrues on any unpaid DMTT at the statutory rate. Accuracy-related penalties of up to 25% of the understated tax apply to negligent understatement; willfully false declarations expose the entity and its officers to fines and imprisonment under CITA Part XI.
