Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda does not operate a domestic check-the-box regime or hybrid mismatch election. The principal statutory election available is an application under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 for a certificate of assurance that the exempted company will not be subjected to future Bermuda taxes (other than taxes on land, payroll, customs/import duties, and social insurance) until the applicable protected date, typically 2035 or a later date as specified in the certificate. For U.S. federal tax purposes only, a Bermuda entity may separately file IRS Form 8832 to elect classification as a disregarded entity or partnership, but this is not a Bermuda tax election and does not alter Bermuda's tax-exempt status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — No Bermuda VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. Bermuda levies only customs duties on imports, stamp duty on specific instruments, and payroll-related contributions where employment nexus exists.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

BMD 0 / Not applicable — Bermuda has no indirect tax regime and therefore no turnover threshold that triggers fiscal registration for VAT/GST/sales tax. A business physically importing goods into Bermuda may require customs clearance and duty payment through the Bermuda Customs Department, but this is not a fiscal turnover registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Bermuda imposes no VAT, GST, or sales tax, so there is no export zero-rating mechanism and no input VAT refund regime. The exempted company's cross-border online sales of maternity and infant care commodities are not subject to Bermuda indirect tax. If goods physically pass through Bermuda exclusively in transit, customs duty is not normally chargeable, but any local importation giving rise to consumption in Bermuda will be subject to customs duty at the applicable tariff rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is officially pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar. Convertibility is unrestricted for most current-account transactions, including trade in maternity and infant-care commodities, through authorised dealers. Profit remittances and dividend payments are freely executable in either BMD or USD provided the distribution meets Bermuda's solvency test, an exchange-control declaration is submitted through the bank, and the underlying source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership records are compliant with the BMA AML/ATF Code. Capital-account transactions such as share subscriptions, loan injections, repayments, share redemptions, and liquidation distributions are permitted for non-resident owners but require either prior BMA exchange-control approval or processing through an authorised dealer under delegated authority. No sectoral restriction applies to online maternity/baby e-commerce, but the entity must maintain an up-to-date corporate register, beneficial-ownership register, and any economic substance filing required by the Bermuda Economic Substance Amendment Act 2019.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda exchange-control rules impose prompt repatriation and offering of export proceeds to an authorised dealer unless a specific retention permission is granted by the BMA. The accepted statutory/normative timeline is that proceeds must be offered to the authorised dealer within one month of receipt or as directed by the BMA. Because BMD is pegged to USD at 1:1, there is effectively no mandatory conversion ratio for USD-denominated export receipts: the exporter may retain USD in an authorised foreign-currency account without conversion. Non-USD export receipts must either be converted into BMD/USD through the authorised dealer or be held under a BMA retention permission.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), functioning as the integrated financial-services regulator, monetary authority, and Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, with day-to-day foreign exchange authorizations delegated to licensed authorised dealers (Bermudian banks).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a supplementary collection and disbursement rail, but not as the sole treasury venue. Bermuda-incorporated exempted companies may onboard to leading offshore digital EMI/neobank platforms (for example Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, or Payoneer) for USD/GBP/EUR collections and supplier disbursements. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a Bermuda bank and cannot hold a BMD clearing account or provide BMA-supervised local banking services; (ii) the EMI's payment infrastructure is generally licensed in the UK, EU, US, Singapore, or Hong Kong and does not extend Bermuda banking rights; (iii) the BMA expects a locally regulated bank to perform exchange-control reporting and sanction screening for material cross-border fund movements, and the EMI may not be able to satisfy BMA exchange-control filing; (iv) opaque beneficial-ownership structures and Caribbean/non-resident ownership may trigger EMI de-risking and sudden account closure. The optimal blueprint is a Bermuda BMA-licensed bank account as the anchor for capital, FX, and settlement, with one or two EMI rails used for marketplace payouts and small-ticket supplier payments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Local File or Master File is required under Bermuda's standard zero-rate exempted company regime. Mandatory OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation arises only for a Bermuda CIT Act 2023 relevant Bermuda entity forming part of an MNE group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million, equivalent to BMD 750 million because the Bermudian dollar is pegged 1:1 to the US dollar. In that scenario, contemporaneous documentation must substantiate pricing of cross-border intercompany transactions in the e-commerce supply chain, including procurement, logistics, marketing, and intellectual property royalties.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda applies the Economic Substance Act 2018 and associated Economic Substance Regulations. An exempted company carrying on a relevant activity, which may include distribution and service business in the context of online maternity and infant commodity sales with a Bermuda warehouse or Bermuda employees, must demonstrate: management and control exercised in Bermuda; adequate physical presence and full-time employees; sufficient operating expenditure; and core income-generating activities performed in Bermuda. An annual Economic Substance Return is filed with the Registrar even if the company has no corporate income tax filing obligation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Bermuda CIT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda CIT Act 2023: assessment to 15% on adjusted taxable profits, interest at the statutory corporate rate, civil penalty of up to 100% of the understated tax for deliberate concealment, revocation of Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection relief, and strike-off of the company by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the exempted company is a bare nominee or sham. Directors and beneficial owners may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, subjected to civil restitution orders, hit with travel bans, disqualified from acting as directors for up to 10 years, and imprisoned for fraudulent evasion or false declarations.
Economic Substance Failure / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Act 2018 and Regulations: first-offence financial penalty of up to BMD 250,000, second-offence penalty of up to BMD 500,000, daily default penalties for continuing breaches, potential publication of the company's non-compliant status, cancellation of exempted status, and strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false economic substance declarations are personally liable when the company cannot pay the penalty. Civil restitution is enforceable against personal assets; travel bans may be imposed pending investigation; repeated breaches lead to director disqualification and imprisonment of up to 5 years for knowingly making false statements.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) Act 1997, Beneficial Ownership Act 2017, and Bermuda Monetary Authority AML/ATF Regulations: unlimited financial penalties, confiscation of laundered funds, fines of up to BMD 1,000,000 for AML control deficiencies, revocation of the company's exempted status, and referral to the Financial Investigation Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, MLROs and officers who knew or suspected money laundering and failed to file a suspicious activity report are personally criminally liable. The corporate veil is pierced for sham arrangements; courts may impose confiscation orders, freezing orders, travel bans, extradition measures, and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering offenses.
