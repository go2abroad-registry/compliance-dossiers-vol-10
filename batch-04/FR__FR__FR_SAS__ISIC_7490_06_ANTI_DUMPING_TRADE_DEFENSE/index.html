Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the official portal impots.gouv.fr; corporate registrations and SIREN/SIRET allocation are effectuated through the Guichet Unique operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

For a Societe par Actions Simplifiee (SAS), the entity must complete the Guichet Unique electronic filing (M0 form) attaching the bylaws, registered office proof, and director identification; DGFiP then assigns a SIREN (9-digit), SIRET (14-digit), and an intra-Community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) for all EU taxable persons. A non-resident foreign enterprise without a French establishment that performs taxable services in France under articles 259, 259-A, and 283-2 of the French General Tax Code (CGI) must apply directly to the DGFiP Service des Impôts des Entreprises Etrangères (SIEE) for a French VAT number, appointing a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal accrédité) when not established in the EU. The client's Trade Remedy Defense Consulting scope requires a valid French VAT ID for any B2B or B2C supply invoiced in France, while the statutory professional license (legal representation) must be evidenced before the le gal practice authorization is granted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation, Territorial_Scope: The SAS is by default subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) under article 206(1) CGI on all worldwide profits unless the permanent establishment exception applies; no tax-exempt zone relief applies to this ISIC 7490 service activity. The registered office and place of effective management in France render the SAS fully resident and within the full tax liability (territorialité mondiale with foreign tax credit mechanism).

Available election forms

Under article 239 bis A CGI, a newly created SAS (less than 5 years old, fewer than 50 employees, turnover below EUR 10 million) may elect de transparence, i.e., to be taxed as a partnership (régime des sociétés de personnes), subjecting profits to income tax at shareholder level; this election is optional, must be filed with the local tax office within the first fiscal year, and lapses mandatorily after 5 years. France has enacted no separate 'check-the-box' hybrid classification; however, under article 210-0 A CGI, the SAS may elect for the tax integration regime (régime d'intégration fiscale) when it holds at least 95% of qualifying subsidiaries, and hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD 1/2 (article 212 bis CGI, and the BEPS Action 2 transposition) disallow mismatched deduction outcomes unless the counterparty's treatment is aligned.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold applies to a large-business B2B consulting SAS; a French-established entity must register for VAT from its first taxable transaction regardless of turnover. For cross-border distance sales and electronically supplied services to French consumers by an EU vendor, the intra-Union distance sales threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year; for a non-EU vendor supplying intangible/consulting digital services to a French non-taxable person, registration is mandatory from the first euro. The simplified small-business exemption (article 293 B CGI) applies only to French-established businesses with turnover not exceeding EUR 85,800 (goods) or EUR 37,500 (services), and is not available to the ISIC 7490 anti-dumping defense practice given the absence of low-turnover status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under article 262 CGI, supplies of goods dispatched/transported outside the European Union are strictly exempt with credit (zero-rated) provided the exporter proves a 'véritable exportation' (genuine export); export evidence must be secured within 90 days of dispatch. Services ancillary to an export (freight, handling, trade-remedy legal defense directly connected to exported goods) qualify under article 262 CGI only if the service is directly linked to the physical export and invoiced to the exporter; B2B anti-dumping defense services provided to a foreign client normally fall outside French VAT (article 259-1 CGI), being taxable in the customer's country under the general B2B place-of-supply rule, not as zero-rated in France. For an SAS established in France, zero-rating applies to: (a) the supply itself when goods are shipped to a non-EU destination with transport documentation; (b) intra-Community supplies under article 262 ter CGI with the customer's valid EU VAT ID and recapitulative statement (EC Sales List); and (c) certain services directly linked to cross-border trade, subject to precise Evidenz documentation.

Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing will enter into force on 1 September 2026 (large taxpayers) and 1 September 2027 (SMEs), pursuant to the French e-invoicing reform (loi de finances 2022), requiring issuance and transmission through the public Portal Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Partenaire de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) using Factur-X/UBL/CII formats; e-invoicing data must be transmitted within 1 working day of invoice issuance. The reform does not impose real-time pre-clearance stamping as in Latin America; instead, automated e-reporting of transaction and status data is pushed to DGFiP, with invoices facing audit timestamps. For export transactions, the exporter must also file the electronic customs export declaration (DAU) via the DELTA/EBE system (Sonar) in real time before dispatch; the invoice itself stays a regular VAT document until the 2026 mandate applies.

French VAT law does not impose a 180-day statutory repatriation deadline per se; a 180-day reference period is retained in this dossier as the DGFiP documentary benchmark for export proceeds to be received in the exporter's French bank account, beyond which the fiscal administration may question the economic reality of the export and impose regular fines under article 1788 A CGI (5% penalty per quarter on unpaid VAT) and article 1728 CGI (10% late-payment penalty, escalating to 40% for deliberate misdeclaration under article 1729 CGI and 80% for manoeuvres frauduleuses or abuse of right under articles 1729 b and L. 64 LPF). Fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger retrospective clawback of the VAT exempted, interest (1.2% per month), 40%/80% penalties, and criminal exposure under the Douane Code (article 414) with up to 5 years' imprisonment and confiscation of goods; the Ministry of Economy may also ban the exporter from export operations. For trade-remedy defense consulting, DGFiP rigorously scrutinizes that the service is physically rendered from France to a non-EU beneficiary and not resold or deemed rendered to a French entity under the mid-distance rules of article 259-1 CGI.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a full convertibility regime for both current and capital account transactions within the EU/EEA under Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Non-EU/EEA cross-border flows are unrestricted but are subject to statistical Balance of Payments (BOP) declarations to the Banque de France, with an overriding 150,000 EUR threshold for direct investment declarations. Capital injections and profit remittances are freely convertible from EUR to any foreign currency, provided that the underlying funds are of documented legitimate origin. Dividend and profit distributions are freely remittable to non-resident shareholders after satisfaction of French corporate tax and withholding tax rules, including participation-exemption relief under Article 145 of the French General Tax Code and protection under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. As a euro-area resident under the euro single currency, a French SAS may retain export revenues in EUR or any foreign currency, and there is no obligation to liquidate foreign-currency balances into EUR. Day-to-day receipts from trade-defense services are recorded via normal invoicing, and any exceptionally large cross-border receipts are reported by the receiving bank under the Banque de France BOP reporting regime for statistical monitoring only.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting as the National Central Bank; supervised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking compliance, with FX and Balance of Payments surveillance coordinated with the European Central Bank and the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. French-authorized electronic money institutions (EMIs) under the PSD2 regime are permissible for collecting trade-defense consultancy fees in EUR and, where passported, in EU currencies; however, EMIs are prohibited from granting commercial credit or providing corporate treasury concentration services and cannot serve as the sole capital-injection rail. Non-EU/EEA digital EMIs are generally not viable because French laws require either a full credit institution license or a properly passported EMI registration with the ACPR, leading to practical account-opening refusals for a foreign-licensed EMI without a French branch.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections by non-resident shareholders must be routed through the SWIFT network using MT103 (customer transfer) or MT202 (financial institution transfer), with the OECD/unified European BOP classification code IRED (investment in equity capital of a French resident enterprise) or the bank-specific equivalent. The initiating bank must obtain the Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) of the French SAS and pass a beneficiary-verification directive. Settlement in EUR can occur via the Banque de France T2/Target2 euro system; for cross-currency injections (e.g., USD/EUR), banks execute a spot FX conversion at client's cost with confirmation of commercial-origin purpose. Where the investor is domiciled outside the EU/EEA, the French receiving bank will require a declaration to the DG Trésor for statistical/direct-investment monitoring if the cumulative injection exceeds the applicable threshold (generally 150,000 EUR) and will ensure the funds are recorded on the BOP direct-investment surveillant statement.Trade-defense consulting disbursements (expert review fees, customs-lawyer retainers, local-counsel advances, translator costs) are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for non-EU counterparties and via the SEPA Instant Credit Transfer or SEPA Credit Transfer scheme for EU/EEA beneficiaries. For high-value disbursements linked to litigation, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) is generally not used because the services are non-goods-based; instead, escrowed disbursement undertakings and unconditional bank-guarantee payments are deployed. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium viability. French-authorized electronic money institutions (EMIs) under the PSD2 regime are permissible for collecting trade-defense consultancy fees in EUR and, where passported, in EU currencies; however, EMIs are prohibited from granting commercial credit or providing corporate treasury concentration services and cannot serve as the sole capital-injection rail.
