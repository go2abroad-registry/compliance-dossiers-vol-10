Name reservation / lock period

France does not operate a pre-registration name reservation or extension mechanism for SAS entities: the RNE checks the requested dénomination sociale at the moment of filing the incorporation dossier through the Guichet Unique. In case of rejection, the applicant receives a reasoned refusal notice; the appropriate remediation is to amend the draft articles and re-file a corrected dossier. Where rejection arises from a third-party trademark or passing-off objection, the applicant may institute infringement or interim measures proceedings before the competent Tribunal de commerce (summary référé) or, before formation is complete, request INPI's mediation support. No administrative appeal body handles company-name refusals, and no filing fee is refunded upon name-driven rejection.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS; capital may be set at EUR 1 or a higher amount. Contributions in kind generally require an auditor unless a statutory exemption is validly elected.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – French General Directorate of Public Finances; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; professional portal: pro.impots.gouv.fr; sole incorporation filing gateway: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique operated by INPI).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – File the SAS incorporation dossier (statuts, capital proof, non-resident director documentation) with the Guichet Unique via INPI. Step 2 – The French Trade and Companies Registry (Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce) issues within the dossier authorization: (i) SIREN (9-digit national identifier), (ii) SIRET site-specific code, and (iii) INSEE APE code matching ISIC 7320/NAICS 541910. Step 3 – DGFiP automatically allocates the intra-community VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire) formatted as FR plus a 2-digit control key plus the SIREN, available in the company's DGFiP online tax account. Step 4 – For non-EU-resident parent shareholders, the SAS must appoint a resident tax representative (représentant fiscal) acceptable to DGFiP to receive tax correspondence, unless a French permanent establishment already exists. Step 5 – Validate French tax registration by obtaining the attestation d'immatriculation TVA from the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE).

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France applies entity-level corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) with no US-style check-the-box pass-through election. Available statutory election mechanisms: (i) Option for tax-consolidated group (régime d'intégration fiscale) under CGI Article 223A, permitting French subsidiaries controlled at least 95% maternally to pool taxable results (irrevocable five-year commitment, elected annually by the parent); (ii) Election to constitute a single taxable person for VAT (assujetti unique / groupe TVA) under CGI Article 256 C, effective since 1 January 2023; (iii) Election for the Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) or non-EU IOSS regime for distance sales and B2C e-services; (iv) Tax-favorable options on IS amortization/depreciation (e.g., declining-balance depreciation, déduction des brevets) and the option for SARL's Irish-investment-type annuity regime which is not applicable to SAS; (v) Hybrid mismatch mandatory alignments under France's ATAD implementation (CGI Article 212 bis and Decree 2019-1558) require self-correction rather than elective neutralization, and no protective check-the-box exists for hybrid entities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is unconditionally and fully convertible in France for both current-account and capital-account transactions. No prior administrative authorisation or exchange-control permit is required for conversion of EUR into any foreign currency, for cross-border payment execution, or for repatriation of dividends, service fees, or liquidation proceeds. Current-account operations — including cross-border SaaS or market-research subscriptions, B2B survey-platform receivables, and mass disbursements to consumer panelists — are entirely free and may be settled in EUR or foreign currency without restriction. Capital-account operations are likewise liberalised. The only residual constraints are: (i) mandatory statistical balance-of-payments declarations to Banque de France, via its online reporting portal and pursuant to CMF Article L141-8 and DG Trésor implementing instructions, where aggregated cross-border settlements exceed EUR 750,000 per calendar month per counterparty country; and (ii) the EU/French foreign-direct-investment screening regime under CMF Articles L151-3 et seq., which applies only to investments into enumerated sensitive sectors (defence, dual-use, critical infrastructure, cybersecurity, and certain data/AI/health activities of strategic national interest) and is not engaged by a consumer-polling and market-research SaaS platform unless its data infrastructure is separately classified as critical. Profit remittance from the French SAS to non-resident shareholders is free of FX restriction. The sole tax hurdle is the statutory dividend withholding tax under Article 119 bis, 2 of the Code Général des Impôts: standard rate 25% for outbound dividends to non-French corporate shareholders, reducible or eliminable under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 5% participation and 24-month holding period) or under an applicable double-tax treaty; relief is obtained via the standard 'réclamation' procedure or reduced-withholding authorisation. Payments to foreign panel participants (survey incentives) are current-account transactions subject to zero FX-control barriers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no export-proceeds surrender regime whatsoever. There is no statutory requirement to repatriate, convert, or deposit foreign-currency export or service receipts into a French account, and no mandatory conversion ratio exists for EUR or foreign-currency earnings. The French SAS may lawfully retain survey-platform receivables in EUR or in foreign currency, indefinitely, in accounts held with a French credit institution or with a duly authorised electronic-money institution. The only enduring obligation is statistical: French residents must report cross-border settlements to Banque de France for balance-of-payments purposes (CMF Article L141-8, implementing EU statistical requirements). The reporting threshold is triggered where cumulative monthly settlements with non-residents exceed EUR 750,000 per counterparty country; the platform's service exports (ISIC 7320 consumer polling) must also be reflected in the annual 'liaison financière' declarations. No licence, permit, or prior approval attaches to the export of market-research services, and no conversion obligation applies to foreign-currency panel-payout funding.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting as national central bank, balance-of-payments reporting authority, and operator of the TARGET2 component in France, in conjunction with the European Central Bank (ECB) for Eurosystem monetary policy. External financial relations and exchange-control policy are administered by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF), principally Articles L141-1 et seq., L151-1 et seq., and L561-1 et seq. Supervisory and enforcement authority for banking and payment institutions is allocated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution), and financial-market conduct relating to FX and hedging instruments is supervised by the AMF (Autorité des Marchés Financiers). France, as a Eurozone Member State, applies no national exchange-control licensing system; capital and payment flows are governed by EU free-movement rules under TFEU Articles 63-66 supplemented by statistical declaration obligations enforced by Banque de France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

High operational feasibility as a settlement and mass-payout layer, subject to defined legal constraints. Wise (Wise Payments SA, licensed as an electronic-money institution in Belgium by the NBB and passported into France) and Qonto (French digital bank, ACPR-licensed) are lawful options for daily treasury, supplier payments, panel rewards, and multi-currency disbursements. Feasibility rating: High for operational settlement and mass micro-payout automation (Wise Batch/API, PayPal Mass Payout funding); Low-Medium for capital escrow or as sole treasury bank. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI such as Wise is not a credit institution under Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 and may neither extend credit nor offer overdraft facilities; (ii) client funds held with Wise are safeguarded in segregated accounts at custodial banks, but are not covered by the French statutory deposit-guarantee scheme (FGDR) — the platform should therefore cap balances at operational-cycle needs; (iii) a fully licensed French digital bank such as Qonto may offer credit products, but core liquidity and borrowing lines (Bpifrance co-financing, commercial overdraft) are more advantageously priced at a Tier-1 bank; (iv) Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical declarations are executed through tier-1 credit institutions, not EMIs, requiring the SAS to retain at least one licensed bank account for compliance reporting; (v) capital injections and equity contributions must flow through a licensed credit institution under wire-traceability rules, never through an EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for share capital, equity contributions, and strategic financing: (a) EU/EEA shareholder wires — SEPA Credit Transfer using ISO 20022 pain.001 messages with purpose code 'GMTO' in the <PURP> tag and a 70-char remittance reference 'AUGMENTATION DE CAPITAL FR_SAS'; funds must be credited to a dedicated blocked account (compte bloqué) at a French credit institution or notary, which will issue the statutory attestation de dépôt des fonds for registration of the capital increase with the RCS (greffe du tribunal de commerce). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. Eligible small and medium-sized companies may qualify for a 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to the statutory share-capital, ownership, and turnover conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic withholding rate on dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident is generally 25%, subject to treaty relief, applicable European Union rules, and statutory exemptions. A qualifying EU parent may obtain an exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary regime, subject to ownership, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse requirements.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the statutory deadline is the second working day following 1 May, subject to the DGFiP electronic-filing calendar and any officially announced extension. The corporate income tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month after year-end; for a calendar-year company, by 15 May. Installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, subject to statutory exceptions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: French full transfer-pricing documentation under Article L.13 AA of the Tax Procedures Code generally applies where the French entity's annual turnover excluding tax or gross assets is at least EUR 150 million, or where it belongs to a group meeting the statutory threshold through another qualifying entity. A separate annual transfer-pricing declaration (Form 2257-SD) generally applies at the EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, including specified group relationships. Thresholds and scope should be checked against current law and the entity's group facts.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. French substance remains relevant under transfer-pricing rules, permanent-establishment analysis, beneficial-ownership requirements, anti-abuse doctrines, controlled foreign company rules, and treaty principal-purpose tests. The SAS should maintain personnel, decision-making, functions, assets, and risk-control evidence consistent with its claimed income and contractual arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Incorrect corporate income tax return or omission

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late interest, and surcharges that may reach 40% for deliberate breach or 80% for fraud or specified abusive conduct; criminal penalties may apply to tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability for personal participation in tax fraud and, in qualifying circumstances, personal tax-debt liability under statutory procedures. Corporate limited liability is not a defense to an individual's own offense.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Income adjustments, tax, interest, and applicable penalties. Failure to provide required documentation may trigger a penalty of the greater of EUR 10,000 or 5% of transfer-pricing adjustments, subject to statutory conditions and caps.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal criminal or civil exposure for intentional misconduct, false records, or participation in fraud; ordinary company-level reassessments do not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Unlawful dividend or withholding-tax treatment

Statutory sanction: Recovery of withholding tax, late interest, and applicable surcharges; unlawful distributions may also be subject to restitution and company-law remedies.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable where statutory conditions for personal liability are met, including serious and repeated tax non-compliance or intentional participation. A dividend recipient may also be required to repay an unlawful distribution.
False invoicing, fictitious services, or tax fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, substantial tax penalties, and potential criminal prosecution. Tax fraud may be punished by imprisonment and fines under the French Tax Code and Penal Code, with higher sanctions for aggravated circumstances.

Director personal liability: Individuals who direct, authorize, or knowingly assist fraud may face personal criminal fines and imprisonment. Asset recovery or confiscation may apply where legally authorized.
Fictitious residence, shell arrangements, or treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or statutory relief, reassessment, interest, and penalties under domestic anti-abuse rules and applicable treaty provisions.

Director personal liability: Personal exposure depends on participation, intent, and statutory grounds. A foreign shareholder's ownership alone does not establish director liability or pierce the SAS's limited liability.
Money laundering or sanctions breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal and administrative sanctions may include substantial fines, imprisonment, asset seizure, and restrictions on business activity, depending on the offense and applicable sanctions regime.

Director personal liability: Directors and other individuals who knowingly participate may be prosecuted personally. Travel restrictions or asset freezes may be imposed only under applicable legal procedures; they are not automatic consequences of a tax filing breach.
