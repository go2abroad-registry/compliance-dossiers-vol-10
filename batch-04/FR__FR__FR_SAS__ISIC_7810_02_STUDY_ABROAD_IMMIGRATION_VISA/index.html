Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. France honours IMF Article VIII obligations and applies the EU capital-movement framework (TFEU Articles 63-66), which permits unrestricted dividend distributions, profit repatriation, intercompany loans, equity capital contributions, and liquidation proceeds without prior central-bank authorization. The only operative gates are (i) balance-of-payments statistical declaration to the Banque de France for cross-border flows above the regulatory reporting thresholds, including the direct-investment declaration duty for transactions exceeding EUR 75,000; (ii) annual foreign-asset reporting on French tax form 3916 for any bank account held outside France; and (iii) EU/UN/OFAC financial sanctions screening enforced by the French banking system. Capital-account restrictions are limited to prior approval for foreign direct investment in sensitive defence, public-security, and critical-technology sectors under Article R151-3 of the FDI decree, which does not apply to an ISIC 7810 student-placement and visa-advisory operation. Profit remittance hurdles are purely tax-driven: dividends to non-resident shareholders are subject to 25% withholding tax, reducible under applicable double tax treaties, with no foreign-exchange limitation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists under French law. Export receipts, including service fees collected from non-resident international students and partner agencies, may be retained indefinitely in EUR or any foreign currency in French or foreign bank accounts; there is no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory timeline for repatriation. The only compliance duties are (i) accurate bookkeeping and VAT treatment of zero-rated service exports, (ii) disclosure of any foreign-held bank accounts on annual tax form 3916, and (iii) balance-of-payments statistical reporting for cross-border flows, handled by the French relationship bank as resident reporting intermediary.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as the national central bank within the Eurosystem, with the European Central Bank (ECB) exercising exclusive competence over euro monetary policy and foreign-exchange reserve operations for the euro area. Exchange-control supervision and balance-of-payments statistical administration are carried out by the Banque de France under Articles L151-1 to L165-1 of the Code monétaire et financier (Monetary and Financial Code), while EU/UN sanctions screening and asset-freeze administration are coordinated with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational treasury purposes; Low for regulated escrow custody. French-licensed Electronic Money Institutions such as Qonto, Shine, and Treezor can open a corporate IBAN rapidly, provide payment cards, invoicing tools, and FX conversion; however, an EMI is not a credit institution, cannot hold client monies in a legal 'séquestre' escrow, cannot grant credit, and must safeguard funds through a partner credit institution. Offshore non-EEA EMIs, including UK and US digital banks, are effectively non-viable as primary treasury rails because the PSD2 passport regime requires an EEA authorisation; a non-EEA EMI would need a French-licensed branch or agent to operate lawfully. Given the Medium_Risk_Enhanced classification of the immigration/visa advisory business, the primary operating account must remain at a French credit institution, with the EMI channel restricted to day-to-day expense payments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax on taxable profits. A qualifying small enterprise may access a 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of profit, generally subject to annual turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up share capital, and at least 75% ownership by individuals or qualifying entities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable tax treaties. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption may apply to a qualifying EU or EEA corporate parent, commonly requiring a qualifying ownership interest, minimum holding conditions, and anti-abuse compliance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the return is generally due by the second business day after 1 May of the following year. For a financial year ending on a date other than 31 December, the return is generally due within three months after the year-end. The final corporate income tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, subject to the statutory calendar applicable to the taxpayer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets generally triggers the full transfer pricing documentation regime, including Local File and Master File requirements under French law. A simplified transfer pricing declaration may apply at a lower EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, or where the taxpayer belongs to a group meeting the statutory threshold. Documentation may also be required below these thresholds where the tax administration requests it or where the transactions are material and controlled.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a separate regime formally named Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless enforced through the French general anti-abuse rule, abuse-of-law doctrine, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled foreign company rules, permanent-establishment principles, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, withholding tax anti-conduit principles, and EU principal-purpose-test concepts. The SAS should maintain real French management, personnel, premises, operational decision-making, books and records, banking, customer-service capacity, and commercial risk consistent with its study-abroad and immigration-consulting activities. A paper intermediary lacking personnel, decision-making, contractual control, or commercial risk may be denied deductions, treaty relief, or beneficial-owner status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, 80% fraud surcharge in appropriate cases, late-payment interest, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that may include imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with higher penalties in aggravated or organized circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, personal tax liability for fraudulent or repeated non-compliance, civil damages, management bans, and asset-seizure measures. Travel restrictions or imprisonment may arise only under applicable judicial or criminal procedures and are not automatic.
VAT fraud and improper treatment of client funds

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, interest, penalties generally ranging from 10% to 80% depending on conduct, possible criminal prosecution for fraudulent concealment, and restrictions on business activity in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Managers may be pursued for deliberate or repeated tax non-payment, fraudulent accounting, misuse of corporate assets, or personal guarantees. Criminal imprisonment and fines may apply where the facts constitute tax fraud or related offenses.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment, secondary adjustment or constructive distribution treatment, withholding tax, interest, documentation penalties, and a possible 40% or 80% penalty for deliberate or fraudulent conduct. Failure to provide required documentation may attract a statutory penalty linked to the reassessed profits and the statutory minimum.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for deliberate tax evasion, fraudulent management, or knowingly approving unsupported related-party arrangements. The corporate veil is not ordinarily pierced solely because an adjustment is made.
Beneficial ownership and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or directive relief, withholding tax reassessment, interest, anti-abuse penalties, and possible 40% or 80% penalties where intent or fraud is established.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve sham or conduit arrangements may face civil restitution, tax-fraud prosecution, management bans, and confiscation measures. Imprisonment is fact-dependent and requires judicial proceedings.
Unlicensed immigration, legal, or regulated advisory activity

Statutory sanction: Administrative closure, professional sanctions, invalidation of applications, civil damages, criminal prosecution for unauthorized practice, fraud, forgery, or use of forged documents, with fines and imprisonment depending on the offense.

Director personal liability: Directors and consultants may be personally liable for client restitution, fraud, forgery, unauthorized practice, and breaches of professional or consumer law. Limited liability does not protect a person from personal criminal conduct.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanctions, regulatory reprimands, monetary penalties, suspension or prohibition of activity, confiscation, and criminal penalties for laundering or terrorist-financing participation. Penalties depend on the regulated status and specific offense.

Director personal liability: Managers may be personally sanctioned for intentional participation, facilitation, concealment, or serious governance failures. Civil restitution, asset freezing, professional bans, and imprisonment may apply in criminal cases.
Bribery and influence peddling

Statutory sanction: French anti-corruption offenses may result in imprisonment and substantial fines, confiscation, exclusion from public procurement, corporate compliance orders, and prosecution of both the company and individuals. Legal entities may face fines up to five times the amount applicable to individuals under relevant criminal provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors, employees, and intermediaries may be personally prosecuted for active or passive bribery and influence peddling. Criminal records, asset confiscation, management bans, and imprisonment may result.
Fraud, forgery, and consumer-protection violations

Statutory sanction: Contract rescission, customer restitution, administrative fines, civil damages, injunctions, and criminal penalties for fraud, forgery, deceptive commercial practices, or misuse of personal data.

Director personal liability: Individuals who direct, authorize, or knowingly participate in deceptive or forged-document schemes may be personally liable for restitution and criminal sanctions, including fines and imprisonment.
Corporate asset misuse and unlawful profit extraction

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax and withholding tax reassessment, denial of deductions, interest, penalties, repayment of improperly distributed assets, and potential abuse of corporate assets prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to restore corporate assets, compensate creditors, bear insolvency-related liabilities, and face criminal sanctions for misuse of company property or unlawful distributions.
Insolvency, creditor prejudice, and late filing

Statutory sanction: Clawback of transactions, insolvency proceedings, director contribution to insufficient assets, prohibition from managing companies, civil liability, and possible bankruptcy-related criminal penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution, personal contribution to the shortfall, management bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment for fraudulent bankruptcy or asset dissipation. Travel restrictions may be ordered only where legally justified by judicial proceedings.
