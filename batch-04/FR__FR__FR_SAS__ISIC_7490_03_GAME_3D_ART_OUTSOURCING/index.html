Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS; founders define the capital freely in the statuts. Cash contributions must be fully or partially paid up at incorporation depending on statutory provisions (may follow the SA rule of at least 50% paid-up at formation with the balance within 5 years, unless the statuts provide full up-front payment). All cash contributions are first deposited into a blocked escrow account (dépôt des fonds) at a bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the deposit certificate is required for registration; funds are released only upon issuance of the Kbis extract.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: The building lease must certify a 'bureaux' use class; install fire extinguishers, evacuation signs, and emergency lighting per article R.4227-1 et seq. of the Labour Code; present the works file to the local Commission de sécurité when inspection is mandated by the building height or capacity. Occupational clearance: complete the single workplace risk assessment document (Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques) under Labour Code Article L.4121-1, affiliate the company with a service de prévention et de santé au travail (occupational health service), and register workstation conformity (display screens, ergonomics). Electrical installations must conform to NF C 15-100 standards, and no structural load certification applies to floor-based office furniture; any warehouse mezzanine for assets would need an engineer's conformity note.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Non-established businesses generally deal with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). The principal online tax portal is impots.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the business formalities single window (Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, operated by INPI). Its registration data are transmitted to the tax administration, which assigns a SIREN and SIRET; the SIREN is the principal business identifier, not a VAT number. For VAT-taxable activity, apply to the competent SIE for a French intra-Community VAT identification number (FR followed by a two-character key and the SIREN). A foreign taxable business with no French establishment generally applies to the SIEE before commencing taxable French transactions. A non-EU business may be required to appoint a French fiscal representative unless an applicable legal exemption or reciprocity arrangement applies. VAT registration does not itself establish a permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying young company meeting statutory ownership, activity, size, and age conditions may elect temporary taxation under the income-tax partnership regime for up to five fiscal years. This is not a US-style check-the-box election. France applies anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implementing ATAD; no general election allows a SAS to choose a pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a France-established business supplying services, the ordinary franchise en base threshold is EUR 37,500 annual turnover, with a EUR 41,250 tolerance threshold under the applicable rules. A SAS carrying on taxable services must monitor the thresholds and register or begin charging VAT when the applicable conditions require it. Different thresholds apply to goods and certain activities. The small-business franchise generally does not provide a turnover threshold for a non-established business making transactions for which it is liable to French VAT; specific cross-border SME-scheme rules may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be exempt with the right to deduct input VAT, provided the supplier establishes the export and satisfies documentary requirements. Goods supplied to customers in another EU Member State are generally treated under intra-Community supply rules, not export zero-rating. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: B2B place-of-supply rules commonly place the service where the business customer is established, while B2C services and statutory exceptions require separate analysis. Game-art outsourcing supplied to a non-French business customer may therefore be outside French VAT under the applicable place-of-supply rule rather than a zero-rated export.

  • French or EU export customs declaration and its Movement Reference Number (MRN), together with customs confirmation of exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, and destination
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR, or carrier delivery records
  • Customer order, contract, and records linking the exported goods to the customs and transport evidence
  • For services, contract, customer business-status evidence, and records supporting the place-of-supply treatment; customs export documents do not ordinarily substantiate a services transaction

France is introducing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction/payment e-reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all in-scope businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; issuance obligations begin on that date for large and intermediate-sized businesses and on 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-businesses. In-scope invoices must be transmitted through a public or approved private platform, with required structured data reported to the tax administration. This is not a general real-time pre-clearance or digital-stamping system. Cross-border transactions and transactions with consumers are generally subject to e-reporting rather than the domestic B2B e-invoicing issuance mandate.

France imposes no general 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The supplier must retain reliable evidence supporting export exemption and input VAT deduction. Unsupported or fraudulent exemption can result in assessment of unpaid VAT, interest for late payment, and tax penalties; intentional fraud may attract substantially higher penalties and criminal sanctions. Input VAT deductions or refunds may be denied or reclaimed where the statutory conditions or substantiation requirements are not met.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in France on both current and capital account under Articles 63-66 TFEU and the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF); France imposes no exchange controls on payments, profit distributions, or capital movements. Profit remittance by the SAS is unrestricted at central-bank level but requires formal corporate approval of audited annual accounts and robust source-of-funds documentation for bank AML review; a French dividend withholding regime (generally 25%/30%, reduced by EU and tax treaties) may apply unless treaty-relief documents are filed with the French tax administration. Capital inflows for non-sensitive sectors are free and subject only to statistical reporting to the Banque de France; investments in sensitive sectors by non-EU investors require prior authorisation under Article L151-3 CMF. The 3D art outsourcing activity (ISIC 7490) is non-sensitive, so convertibility is effectively full for both operating and treasury transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; all export invoices may be denominated in EUR or any freely convertible currency, and the SAS may hold foreign-currency balances in French or foreign bank accounts. The only binding requirements are accurate invoicing with retention of underlying contracts and invoices for six years, the balance-of-payments statistical declaration to the Banque de France for large cross-border flows, and compliance with EU financial-sanctions and capital-movement reporting rules. There is no deadline for repatriation of export revenues into France.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France); foreign-exchange and capital-movement policy is administered by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy and Finance), with prudential banking supervision exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM overall. French-licensed e-money institutions (Qonto, Shine, Manager.one) and EU-passporting EMIs (Revolut Business, Wise, Airwallex) may lawfully provide IBANs, SEPA/SWIFT rails, FX conversion, and PayPal-adjacent treasury tools under CMF Article L521-1 and PSD2; they are highly viable for day-to-day collections and disbursements. However, EMIs are not credit institutions: they cannot grant overdrafts, issue commercial letters of credit, or act as counterparty for OTC derivatives, and they must hold client funds in safeguarded accounts at a credit institution. The SAS should therefore use EMIs as settlement front-ends only and retain a tier-1 French bank as the core treasury and credit counterparty.

The bank file must include the notarised board or shareholder resolution approving the capital increase, the attestation de dépôt des fonds, the updated Kbis extract, and each shareholder's identity and source-of-funds evidence. If the contributor is a non-EU investor, confirm first that the activity does not trigger the L151-3 CMF sensitive-sector prior-authorisation rule (3D art outsourcing does not); in all cases, the transaction requires the standard cross-border statistical declaration to the Banque de France. The capital movement should never be mixed with operational revenue; keep the escrow account separate until registration of the capital increase is completed.For euro disbursements inside the Single Euro Payments Area, use SEPA Credit Transfer (instant SCT available for urgent sub-100k settlements); for USD or extra-EEA corridors, use SWIFT MT103 with beneficiary BIC + IBAN/ABA and the appropriate BPM6 purpose code for international professional or computer services. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM overall. French-licensed e-money institutions (Qonto, Shine, Manager.one) and EU-passporting EMIs (Revolut Business, Wise, Airwallex) may lawfully provide IBANs, SEPA/SWIFT rails, FX conversion, and PayPal-adjacent treasury tools under CMF Article L521-1 and PSD2; they are highly viable for day-to-day collections and disbursements. However, EMIs are not credit institutions: they cannot grant overdrafts, issue commercial letters of credit, or act as counterparty for OTC derivatives, and they must hold client funds in safeguarded accounts at a credit institution. The SAS should therefore use EMIs as settlement front-ends only and retain a tier-1 French bank as the core treasury and credit counterparty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File are mandatory when the French entity's annual turnover (HT) exceeds EUR 400 million or its gross balance-sheet total exceeds EUR 400 million, or when the entity is at least 50% owned or owns at least 50% of such a threshold entity. Country-by-country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Additionally, a simplified annual transfer-pricing statement (Form 2257-SD) must be filed by all companies if intercompany transaction flows exceed EUR 50,000.

Economic substance scope: France has no formal Economic Substance Regulations in the UAE/BCI sense, but applying an economic substance doctrine through the general anti-abuse principle (abus de droit), DAC 6 mandatory disclosure, anti-hybrid rules, and BEPS substance requirements. Treaty relief and reduced WHT rates require genuine staff, physical facilities, real functions and risk assumption; artificial shell structures lacking substance are recharacterized and penalized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 of the French Tax Code (CGI), penalties of up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine (aggravated fraud: seven years and EUR 3,000,000). In addition, the reassessed tax is subject to an 80% fraud penalty plus late interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in falsification of accounts are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, penalties and interest. Criminal conviction may trigger a ban on managing any French commercial enterprise and a travel ban while under judicial investigation.
Abusive Shell Arrangement (Abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: Article L64 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales (LPF) allows recharacterization of the structure and denial of claimed benefits. The resulting tax deficiency is levied with an 80% penalty for misreporting, plus late interest. The shell company is disregarded and profits are re-attributed to the beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: De jure directors are personally exposed to civil liability under the 'action en insuffisance d'actif' for compensating losses arising from gross managerial fault. In egregious cases, the DTA claim is rejected, extradition and travel bans may be enforced, and criminal conviction for fraud can lead to imprisonment.
AML / UBO Violation (Monetary and Financial Code)

Statutory sanction: Up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine for money laundering; failure to identify UBOs carries administrative sanctions up to EUR 1,000,000 and potential blocking of bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge of the illegal origin of funds face criminal money-laundering liability and can be subject to EU travel bans, extradition and attachment of personal assets. A judicial prohibition on managing French companies can also be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The French tax authority recalculates the arm's-length price, charging additional corporate income tax with a 40% deliberate understatement penalty and 0.20% monthly late interest. In chronic non-cooperation, the penalty rises to 80% and the case is referred to the tax-offence commission for criminal prosecution under Articles L228 to L234 LPF.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally approve distorted transfer-pricing documentation may be held liable for gross negligence, resulting in personal civil damages and disqualification from management. If fraudulent misreporting is proved, imprisonment and travel bans can be ordered.
