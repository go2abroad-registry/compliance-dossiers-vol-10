VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax — Bermuda relies exclusively on customs import duties, stamp duties on land transactions, and employer payroll taxes. There is no consumption tax on goods or services including wholesale fertilizer transactions.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no indirect tax registration threshold exists. Customs import duty registration is required only for goods physically imported into Bermuda, with rates typically 10%–25% on dutiable imports; fertilizer wholesale offshore transactions do not trigger any VAT threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Bermuda imposes no VAT and therefore no 0% export VAT rate. Bermuda exempted companies conducting offshore wholesale of prilled urea and potassium chloride are outside any consumption tax net. Export trades from Bermuda are not subject to customs export duties under the Bermuda Customs Tariff Act, provided goods do not enter the local Bermudian market. Where physical transshipment occurs through Bermuda, customs transit declarations (B-CAMES) are required, but no VAT zero-rating claim or refund mechanism is triggered.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate exists in Bermuda. The Bermuda Customs Department operates an electronic Single Administrative Document (SAD) filing portal (Bermuda Customs Automated Managed Entry System, B-CAMES) for import/export declarations. Invoices need not be digitally cleared; standard commercial invoicing and record retention apply.

No VAT fraud clawback regime exists in Bermuda given the absence of VAT. However, penalties apply for customs declaration inaccuracies: fines up to 100% of evaded duty, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment up to 2 years for fraudulent misdeclaration under the Customs Tariff Act 1971 and Customs (Control and Management) Act 2015. False statements to the Registrar of Companies or OTC attract fines up to BMD 50,000 and director disqualification. No tax clawback applies because no income or export tax liability is imposed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at parity (1 BMD = 1 USD) and circulates interchangeably with USD. Bermuda operates a bifurcated exchange-control system under the Exchange Control Act 1972 (as extended to Bermuda) and the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969: (i) current-account transactions of 'resident' persons require authorized-dealer approval but are practically unrestricted; (ii) capital-account transactions of residents require BMA permit, while non-resident-designated persons, including a Bermuda Exempted Company that obtains 'non-resident' exchange-control status, are entirely outside the permit regime. The subject entity can therefore receive equity injections, shareholder loans, trade receivables, and profit remittances in any freely convertible currency without BMA approval, and can distribute dividends or repay intercompany debt to the UK parent with zero withholding taxes, no capital controls, and no remittance moratorium. Practical hurdles are limited to banking-form related documentary filings (BMA statistical forms ECB-1/ECB-2 for inward/outward transfers of USD 25,000 or more, filed by the bank, not the company) and to the UK parent's obligation to comply with HM Revenue & Customs cross-border dividend and debt/equity reporting rules. There is no exchange-control restriction on converting GBP into USD for investment into Bermuda, and no restriction on the subsequent liquidation of that investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory export-proceeds surrender ratio or statutory conversion timeline for Bermuda Exempted Companies designated as non-resident under the Exchange Control Act 1972. Export receipts in USD and other foreign currencies may be retained indefinitely in external foreign-currency accounts with any BMA-authorized bank. In the event the company were ever deemed resident (e.g., central management and control exercised in Bermuda by a local director), the Exchange Control Notice A/1972 would require all foreign-currency proceeds to be surrendered to an authorized dealer within 30 days or converted into BMD at the 1:1 peg, unless a 'retention of foreign currency' certificate is granted by the BMA. Current corporate-booking practice will preserve non-resident status by chartering, invoicing, and treasury-managing the commodity trades outside the island, thereby avoiding the surrender regime and retaining full USD-operating freedom.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, with statutory responsibility for currency issue, exchange control administration, and the licensing and supervision of all authorized banks in Bermuda. Her Majesty's Treasury (HMT) retains ultimate sovereignty over monetary affairs of the British Overseas Territory, but day-to-day FX regulation and delegated licensing authority rest exclusively with the BMA.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (conditional). Bermuda does not maintain a distinct e-money institution (EMI) licensing category; a digital wallet or payment firm operating locally must be licensed either under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 (if taking deposits) or the Digital Asset Business Act 2018 (if executing virtual-asset payments). An offshore digital EMI, such as a UK FCA-authorised e-money institution or a passported European Economic Area EMI, may open a multicurrency corporate account for the Bermuda Exempted Company without a Bermuda banking licence because the legal relationship is a safeguarded e-money contract, not a deposit-taking relationship. Legal constraints are material: (i) leading UK EMIs classify 'physical commodity trading', 'fertilizer distribution', and 'agrochemical chartering' as restricted high-risk sectors and impose per-transaction caps of approximately GBP 25,000-50,000 with 30-60 day clearing settlements, which is incompatible with million-dollar LC or charter payments; (ii) safeguarded e-money is not covered by the Bermuda Deposit Compensation Scheme or the UK Financial Services Compensation Scheme, creating unacceptable counterparty risk for treasury-grade settlements; (iii) inbound USD wires from commodity buyers are routed via the EMI's US partner bank, and the intermediary bank will apply enhanced KYC because Bermuda entities subject to UK Economic Crime (Transparency and Enforcement) Act provisions may be mis-coded as opaque structures; (iv) EMIs cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit and cannot clear Fedwire USD on a same-day basis. The EMI is thus viable only as a sub-limit disbursement rail for payroll, small vendor expenses, or as a fallback during the primary banking onboarding phase, with complete prohibition on its use for trade settlement or LC issuance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant structure for initial and subsequent capital injections from the UK parent to the Bermuda Exempted Company: (1) Equity route: UK parent transfers GBP via CHAPS to its London bank, which executes a spot GBP/USD conversion and sends an MT103 SWIFT message to Butterfield/Clarien in Bermuda with field 72 free-format code '/REC/EXB-ECB1/' or 'INVEST / SUBSCRIPTION GB__BM__BM_EXEMPTED_CO', attaching the board resolution and share subscription agreement as cover documents; the Bermuda bank books the USD credit to the Capital Escrow Account. The UK side requires no regulatory approval for outward direct investment, though HMRC may require BEPS Pillar 2 and Country-by-Country notifications; (2) Shareholder-loan route: an MT103 with purpose code 'LOAN' plus an executed intercompany loan agreement and subordination deed to avoid thin-capitalization recharacterization; the Bermuda bank applies its 'échange control statistical form ECB-1' for any credit above USD 25,000 equivalent; (3) Trade-credit route: if the UK parent prepays for a future cargo, the incoming MT103 must carry the supplier-invoice reference and a warehouse receipt; the counterparty bank will treat it as trade settlement rather than an injection, avoiding any 'investment' flag. All routes require that the beneficiary account be pre-nominated in the account-opening mandate and that the UBO's UK proof of address be current within three months.Outbound settlement rails are bifurcated: (a) Cash payments: SWIFT MT103 (field 71A 'OUR') for cash-against-documents purchases, freight payments to vessel owners, and discharge-port disbursements, routed from Butterfield via its New York correspondent bank (Citibank/BNY Mellon) over Fedwire; the GPI Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR) is mandatory for same-day confirmation; (b) Documentary trade: SWIFT MT700 for Commercial Letters of Credit with 30-day usance terms subject to UCP600 and ISBP 745, issued by Clarien Bank or Bermuda Commercial Bank and confirmed by a first-class London confirming bank (HSBC UK/Barclays/Lloyds); mandated documents must include full set of clean-on-board bills of lading made out to order of the issuing bank freight prepaid, a marine insurance certificate, and the SGS quality certificate confirming urea nitrogen content of not less than 46.2% (or potash potassium oxide content of 60% minimum). For charter payments specifically, the MT103 must reference the vessel's IMO number and the charter-party liquidation statement. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium (conditional). Bermuda does not maintain a distinct e-money institution (EMI) licensing category; a digital wallet or payment firm operating locally must be licensed either under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 (if taking deposits) or the Digital Asset Business Act 2018 (if executing virtual-asset payments). An offshore digital EMI, such as a UK FCA-authorised e-money institution or a passported European Economic Area EMI, may open a multicurrency corporate account for the Bermuda Exempted Company without a Bermuda banking licence because the legal relationship is a safeguarded e-money contract, not a deposit-taking relationship. Legal constraints are material: (i) leading UK EMIs classify 'physical commodity trading', 'fertilizer distribution', and 'agrochemical chartering' as restricted high-risk sectors and impose per-transaction caps of approximately GBP 25,000-50,000 with 30-60 day clearing settlements, which is incompatible with million-dollar LC or charter payments; (ii) safeguarded e-money is not covered by the Bermuda Deposit Compensation Scheme or the UK Financial Services Compensation Scheme, creating unacceptable counterparty risk for treasury-grade settlements; (iii) inbound USD wires from commodity buyers are routed via the EMI's US partner bank, and the intermediary bank will apply enhanced KYC because Bermuda entities subject to UK Economic Crime (Transparency and Enforcement) Act provisions may be mis-coded as opaque structures; (iv) EMIs cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit and cannot clear Fedwire USD on a same-day basis. The EMI is thus viable only as a sub-limit disbursement rail for payroll, small vendor expenses, or as a fallback during the primary banking onboarding phase, with complete prohibition on its use for trade settlement or LC issuance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% - Bermuda imposes no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, no value-added tax, and no dividend withholding tax. A Bermuda Exempted Company also holds a statutory protection against future Bermuda taxation under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, effective through 2035.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an Exempted Company to any shareholder, whether resident or non-resident.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Local File or Master File statutory threshold is prescribed in Bermuda because no corporate income tax is levied. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for Bermuda-headquartered MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more (approximately BMD 850 million), requiring registration and filing through the BMA RAPID portal.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations apply because wholesale distribution of urea and potash is a 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity. The Exempted Company must be directed and managed in Bermuda, convene board meetings at adequate frequency in Bermuda, maintain board minutes and accounting records in Bermuda, and perform its core income-generating activities in Bermuda through adequate physical premises, full-time employees, and operating expenditure. A shell or letterbox company with no local substance will fail the economic substance test and become subject to penalties and enforcement action.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Facilitation of Foreign Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: In Bermuda, criminal fraud and money laundering offenses carry imprisonment and significant fines. Where the company has sufficient UK nexus, the Criminal Finances Act 2017 may impose unlimited fines on the company and criminal liability on directors for failure to prevent the facilitation of foreign tax evasion. Foreign tax authorities can issue protective assessments and penalties, and Bermuda will cooperate through its Tax Information Exchange Agreement with the UK.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve sham invoicing or misrepresent corporate activity can be prosecuted for conspiracy to defraud, face confiscation and restitution orders, and be subjected to exit bans or passport surrender while under investigation. The corporate veil can be pierced where the company is used as a façade for tax evasion.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Initial ESR penalty of BMD 10,000 plus BMD 2,000 per day of continuing default; the BMA may issue a formal substance failure notice; the Registrar may strike off the company or seek a court order to wind it up. Countries receiving CbCR/ESR information may recharacterize profits and impose transfer pricing adjustments with penalties.

Director personal liability: Bermuda resident directors who sign false substance declarations may incur financial penalties and imprisonment; courts may disqualify directors for up to several years and order restitution of management fees or dividends paid from companies lacking substance.
AML / CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Violations of Bermuda's Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2018 may result in civil penalties, regulatory fines, suspension of the exempted company's registration, and criminal prosecution. International sanctions exposure can lead to asset freezes, clawback of proceeds, and refusal or revocation of correspondent banking relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the appointed MLRO may be personally prosecuted for failure to submit suspicious activity reports or for consenting to prohibited transactions. Individuals risk imprisonment, fines, travel restrictions, and civil asset recovery. Foreign regulators may seek mutual legal assistance and extradition.
Fraudulent Trading, Wrongful Dividends, and Piercing the Corporate Veil

Statutory sanction: Under Bermuda law, fraudulent trading and misrepresentation in corporate filings can result in court-ordered winding up, civil restitution to creditors, financial penalties for the company, and criminal liability for individuals involved in the fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required personally to contribute to the company's assets on winding up, barred from acting as directors for a defined period, and ordered to repay unlawful distributions. Bermuda courts may also impose freezing orders, seizure of travel documents, and imprisonment for contempt in connection with attempts to dissipate assets.
