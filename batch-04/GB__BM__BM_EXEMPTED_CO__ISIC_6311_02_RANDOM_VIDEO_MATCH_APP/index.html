Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity seeking to operate through a Bermuda Exempted Company incorporates under the Companies Act 1981 through a licensed local registered agent. The incorporation process yields a Certificate of Incorporation and, upon filing of the exempted materials, an annual Certificate of Exemption identifying the entity as a Bermuda exempted company. No statutory income tax ID or VAT ID is issued because Bermuda levies no corporate income tax and no VAT. For any Bermuda-source payroll activity, the entity must separately register with the Payroll Tax Office for a payroll tax account only if it has Bermuda-based employees; for a pure foreign-owned video-match social app with no Bermuda payroll, no payroll-tax registration is required.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar (USD) and is fully convertible in law and market practice. Bermuda maintains a residual Exchange Control Act regime administered by the BMA, but Bermuda exempted companies (BM_EXEMPTED_CO) are typically designated as 'non-resident' for exchange control purposes, thereby enjoying unimpeded current account and capital account convertibility, including unrestricted opening of foreign currency bank accounts, retention and deployment of export proceeds, and dividend/profit remittance to shareholders, provided the company's business is conducted wholly outside Bermuda and the requisite 'non-resident' designation is endorsed by the BMA. Profit remittance operates via the provisions of the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973; no material exchange control hurdles are expected for the described corporate structure. The BMA maintains jurisdiction over balance-of-payments reporting and foreign currency transaction monitoring, but does not impose approval hurdles for non-resident exempted companies making legitimate distribution remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a non-resident Bermuda exempted company, no export proceeds surrender requirement exists under the Exchange Control Act 1972, the Exchange Control Regulations 1973 or the Central Bank of Bermuda Act 1969. The obligor has zero mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory deadline for converting export receipts into BMD. This exempted treatment is predicated on the BMA's designation of the company as non-resident; should the company conduct local business within Bermuda (which this entity does not), a conversion obligation would apply within 28 days and 100% of export receipts would need to be surrendered through an authorised dealer. For the present scope, all export proceeds may be retained, deployed and invested in USD, BMD or other permitted foreign currencies without surrender.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW / RESTRICTED. Bermuda's Payment Systems (Electronic Payments) Act 2019 obliges any electronic money institution or payment service provider operating in Bermuda to obtain a licence from the BMA. A non-resident exempted company may not lawfully maintain a virtual EMI account offshore unless the EMI is a BMA-licensed enterprise or operates under a recognised passporting framework; however, UK/EU EMIs commonly reject Bermuda-licensed corporate clients due to enhanced correspondent banking de-risking and evolving high-risk jurisdiction classification nuances. Digital cross-border EMIs such as Wise (UK) or Revolut Business generally require a physical underlying bank settlement; given the BMA licensing requirement and the electronic-money-issuer-of-record controls, the practical viability of offshore EMI accounts is low. Direct account relationships with the four Bermuda commercial banks constitute the most reliable route. A hybrid approach of using an EU/UK EMI as a disbursement tool with a Bermuda operational account as the anchor is legally permissible provided the EMI does not maintain a branch, market into Bermuda, or solicit Bermudian residents.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing should be structured as: (1) a SWIFT MT103 originating from the shareholder's bank to the Bermudian operational account; payment must be denominated in USD or BMD and must be split between a capital contribution for equity or a shareholder loan for treasury funding; (2) the remittance advice must state the standard purpose code applicable to foreign direct investment into Bermuda, namely IMF Balance of Payments Manual 7 (BPM7) category Other Equity — for non-FDI inward investment use Other Investment Liability — Currency and Deposits; (3) for equity injections in an exempted company the authorised share capital mechanics under the Companies Act 1981 (as amended) require issuance of shares and filing of a Memorandum of Increase of Authorised Share Capital if the cap is exceeded; (4) the bank will require a copy of the board resolution approving the capital call and share allotment, a structure chart showing the beneficial owner, and the bank's own Capital/Equity Investment questionnaire; (5) all verification documentation must be transmitted in advance of the wire under the bank's pre-approval regime; for amounts above $100,000 the mandatory reporting threshold to the BMA under the Financial Intelligence (Bermuda) Regulations 2021 is notification rather than consent, but any remittance above $6 million will trigger the BMA's Foreign Currency and Deposit Reporting requirements to the central bank within 48 hours.Preferred clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) SWIFT MT103 for high-value or USD disbursements to merchant partners (Paymentwall, Apple) and content-distribution networks, using the priority field 'profit remittance' or 'business service fee'; Wire transfers to low-tax or offshore prepaid-card providers are discouraged; SWIFT USD correspondent routing via the New York fed account of a Bermuda commercial bank is the compliant corridor. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (nil) for ordinary Bermuda Exempted Companies; 15% CIT only for in-scope MNE groups under the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by a Bermuda Exempted Company to shareholders, whether resident or non-resident.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 0 for ordinary exempted companies (no Bermuda TP filing requirement). For in-scope MNE groups under the CIT Act, mandatory Bermuda Local File and Master File documentation applies when the group consolidated revenue is at least EUR 750 million (approximately BMD 750 million), with detailed records of cross-border related-party financing, IP licensing, and intercompany service charges.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Economic Substance Act requires any exempted company carrying on a relevant activity to demonstrate economic presence proportionate to the business. ISIC 6311 (data hosting and online platform operations) is treated as an IP business when revenue is derived from proprietary video-matching algorithms, user data, or licensing arrangements. The company must show core income-generating activities in Bermuda: active board management, strategic decisions, adequate local employees, premises, and operating expenditure. Merely holding contracts and bookings outside Bermuda will fail the substance test and expose the entity to penalty and striking-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Misuse of Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Under Bermuda Proceeds of Crime and tax legislation, the company is liable for underpaid tax (if in-scope) plus interest at 8% per annum and penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded; criminal conviction can yield fines up to BMD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are personally liable for civil restitution if they knowingly approve false returns; courts may issue travel bans, freezing orders, and disqualification orders, and in serious cases order imprisonment.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Breach

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike off the company, revoke its exempted certificate, and issue a penalty of BMD 10,000 plus BMD 100 per day for continued non-compliance; information is exchanged with foreign tax authorities under OECD TIEAs.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false economic substance declarations face personal fines, disqualification from holding board positions in Bermuda, and civil restitution for funds mischaracterized as substance-based income.
AML, Sanctions, and Payment Rail Integrity

Statutory sanction: Bermuda's Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Code imposes entity-level fines, license revocation, and a criminal penalty of up to 14 years' imprisonment for money laundering; unlicensed money service business activity triggers separate penalties under the Banks and Trust Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are criminally liable if the company operates without appropriate AML systems; personal confiscation orders, asset forfeiture, travel restrictions, and debarment from regulated sectors apply.
Transfer Pricing and UK CFC / GAAR Counteraction

Statutory sanction: HMRC can apply UK CFC rules to apportion profits to UK taxpayers at a 25% charge plus penalties of up to 200% of the additional tax; Bermuda authorities may also adjust CIT computations for in-scope MNE groups. Civil penalties and accelerated payment notices apply.

Director personal liability: UK-resident directors and shadow directors may incur personal liability under CFC attribution where they arrange a Bermuda structure for tax avoidance; HMRC can issue director penalty notices, travel bans, and proceedings for bankruptcy or imprisonment in cases of fraudulent evasion.
