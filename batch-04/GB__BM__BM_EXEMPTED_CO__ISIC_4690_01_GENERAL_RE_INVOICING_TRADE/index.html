Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Ministry of Finance – Corporate Income Tax Agency (CITA) and Office of the Tax Commissioner (CIT administration); Registrar of Companies (entity formation and annual compliance); Customs & Excise Department (trade declarations). Digital portals: CIT Portal (https://cit.gov.bm) for corporate income tax registration and filing; Companies e-Registry for annual corporate filings; ASYCUDA World for customs declarations.

E-government filing portal

A foreign beneficial owner incorporating a Bermuda Exempted Company must retain a licensed registered agent (corporate service provider) in Bermuda. The agent files the certificate of incorporation with the Registrar of Companies; the Registrar issues a Certificate of Incorporation and a unique company registration number (CRN). Bermuda does not issue a VAT ID because no VAT or GST exists. If the Exempted Company is a constituent entity of an in-scope multinational group (consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million / BMD 750 million), it must register on the CIT Portal within 30 days of becoming in-scope and will receive a Bermuda Tax Identification Number (BTIN). Out-of-scope entities are not required to obtain a BTIN; payroll tax registration applies only if the company employs Bermuda-resident staff. Annual government fees and the filing of an annual return with the Registrar of Companies remain mandatory for all exempted companies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – The Bermuda Exempted Company is historically exempt from Bermuda income tax, capital gains tax, dividend withholding tax, and all indirect taxes. Effective 1 January 2025, the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 imposes a 15% minimum top-up tax (GloBE rules: IIR, UTPR, QDMTT) on constituent entities of in-scope multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. An Exempted Company engaged solely in offshore re-invoicing and not belonging to an in-scope group remains entirely outside Bermuda income tax; if it is part of an in-scope group, it may qualify for simplified treatment as a pure equity holding entity or benefit from the substance-based income exclusion.

Available election forms

(i) Election under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 for treatment as a Pure Equity Holding Entity (PEHE), reducing GloBE reporting and substance obligations for entities whose income derives mainly from dividends, equity participations, and incidental income; (ii) Election to apply the Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax (QDMTT) Safe Harbour; (iii) Election to apply the Transitional Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) Safe Harbour during the transitional period; (iv) Election to use simplified substance-based income exclusion calculation methods; (v) Group consolidation elections for GloBE purposes. The US-style check-the-box classification regime is not available under Bermuda law, and no pass-through tax election exists; Bermuda exempted companies are always treated as separate taxable persons under Bermuda law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – Bermuda does not operate a VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax system; the only border charge is the Bermuda Customs Tariff (import duties) levied on the CIF value of imported goods. Offshore re-invoicing trade and intermediary services are entirely outside the scope of Bermuda indirect taxation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – There is no indirect tax registration regime, economic turnover threshold, or VAT registration obligation in Bermuda. Customs import duties apply per the Bermuda Tariff Schedule (rates generally range from 0% to 33.25% on CIF value, with most commercial goods dutiable at 0%–25%). No Bermuda duty is levied on exportation or on offshore re-invoicing service income.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Bermuda levies no VAT, so there is no 0% export VAT rate, no VAT refund mechanism, and no zero-rating claim procedure in Bermuda. The Bermuda Exempted Company's re-invoicing margin is not subject to any Bermuda indirect tax. If the same entity were to establish a UK branch or permanent establishment making taxable supplies in the UK, UK VAT registration would be required once UK taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold (currently GBP 90,000 per rolling 12 months); UK exports of goods from the UK could then be zero-rated subject to HMRC export evidence requirements. However, a pure Bermuda re-invoicing entity without UK establishment remains a non-established, non-UK-registered legal person outside the UK VAT net.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World export declaration or UK SAD Form C88 where UK VAT zero-rating is claimed)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / CMR consignment note)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank confirmation of export proceeds

No real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists in Bermuda for commercial or re-invoicing transactions. Bermuda Customs & Excise requires electronic import/export declarations through the ASYCUDA World customs clearance system. For re-invoicing trade, the Bermuda entity must retain commercial invoices, bills of lading, contracts, and banking remittance records to demonstrate commercial substance and, if applicable, to support economic substance filings under the Economic Substance Regulations.

Bermuda has no indirect tax clawback regime because no VAT/GST exists. Under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, fraudulent or negligent CIT filings attract penalties: failure-to-notify penalties up to BMD 250,000, late-filing penalties, evasion surcharges up to 200% of unpaid tax, and potential criminal sanctions; the CITA may conduct targeted audits of in-scope entities. If the Bermuda entity is also UK VAT-registered and falsely zero-rates exports, HMRC is entitled to retrospectively claw back VAT improperly reclaimed or not accounted for, and may impose: (i) deliberate inaccuracy penalties up to 100% of the VAT liability (FA 2007 Schedule 24); (ii) failure-to-notify penalties (FA 2007 Schedule 41); (iii) default interest; and (iv) seizure of business records. UK case law (including R (Euro Tyre) Ltd v HMRC, C-33/16) requires objective documentary evidence of the physical removal of goods from the UK as a condition of valid zero-rating; a mere re-invoicing arrangement without actual cross-border movement is subject to full VAT assessment, penalties, and possible civil fraud proceedings.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is fully convertible and fixed at a 1:1 peg to the US dollar, with USD accepted as de facto legal tender for settlement. For a Bermuda exempted company satisfying non-resident designation criteria, current-account payments are unrestricted; capital-account operations (share capital injections, shareholder loans, dividend and interest repatriation) require no prior exchange control consent and carry no statutory withholding or conversion burden. The only practical friction is the commercial banks' AML/source-of-funds diligence rather than any balance-of-payments control; accordingly, a re-invoicing hub may freely route remittances in any major currency, subject to FATF-compliant record-keeping under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2018.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export proceeds in Bermuda. Exporters and re-invoicing intermediaries are not required to convert foreign currency receipts into BMD, need not surrender earnings to the BMA or any authorised dealer, and may aggregate funds indefinitely in USD, EUR, GBP or other convertible currencies. The only applicable obligation is the general transaction documentation and record-keeping duty under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2018.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, acting as the central monetary authority and the designated exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972; banking licensing and supervision are exercised under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High as a supplementary collection and disbursement rail only. Regulated UK/EU electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) and cross-border payment platforms such as Payoneer/XTransfer may be used to receive buyer remittances in local currencies and to pay small/mid-ticket suppliers, with funds safeguarded at partner banks. Legal constraints: Bermuda has no domestic EMI licensing regime; the BMA does not authorise non-deposit-taking trade clearing as an EMI activity, and the exempted company's offshore position does not require a local EMI licence. Foreign-based platforms holding funds at offshore partner banks are outside Bermuda's exchange control remit and do not trigger a Bermuda licence, but they cannot issue or confirm Letters of Credit, cannot provide Bermuda clearing, and do not satisfy creditor settlement expectations for primary trade flows. The EMI/platform rail must therefore be reconciled daily to the primary BMA-licensed bank account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Direct-shipment re-invoicing pattern: goods move from the overseas supplier directly to the end customer while payment flows through the Bermuda entity, constituting a trade-based money-laundering red flag.

Root cause: Direct-shipment re-invoicing pattern: goods move from the overseas supplier directly to the end customer while payment flows through the Bermuda entity, constituting a trade-based money-laundering red flag.

Defense evidence: Provide a complete transactional documentation set for each pipeline: purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, customs entry, proof of payment, a diagram of the commercial chain, board minutes describing the Bermuda entity's economic substance (staffing, risk assumption, margin retention), and a rolling 12-month trade-flow and working-capital model.
In-and-out movement: buyer funds received in the Bermuda account are disbursed to suppliers within 24-48 hours without demonstrable value addition or credit risk.

Root cause: In-and-out movement: buyer funds received in the Bermuda account are disbursed to suppliers within 24-48 hours without demonstrable value addition or credit risk.

Defense evidence: Deliver a bank-approved operating procedure covering receipt-to-payment timing, a board-approved treasury policy, evidence of credit and currency risk assumption by the Bermuda entity (e.g., intercompany agreements with margin allocation), invoice-level payment schedules, and the first three months of reconciliation reports to the relationship manager.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections and shareholder loans should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the UK, EU, or Hong Kong parent or group treasury to the Bermuda operating account. The remitting bank must state in field 70 (remittance information) the purpose, e.g., 'B040 - Capital contribution to Bermuda subsidiary' for paid-in capital or 'B010 - Trade working capital loan' for a shareholder advance, aligning with OECD/BoP purpose classifications where the outward remitting jurisdiction requires a code. For USD transactions clearing through New York, the MT103 is supplemented by Fedwire, CHIPS, or SWIFT GPI. The Bermuda receiving bank will require, and the file must be pre-positioned to contain: a board resolution approving the injection; an executed shareholder loan agreement where applicable; group audited financial statements or management accounts evidencing the source of funds; a UBO source-of-wealth statement where funds originate from individuals; the certificate of incorporation and register of members; and confirmation of tax-exempt status where the bank's risk rating requires it. Subsequent dividend or interest repatriations are processed against the same standing file.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfer (TT) settlement of supplier invoices and intercompany re-invoicing balances; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) issued by HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield through their correspondent networks (HSBC London/New York and Citibank or JPMorgan New York for Butterfield) for high-value, inventory-backed transactions; (iii) documentary collections (MT400/MT420) for lower-risk, repeat flows; and (iv) domestic Bermuda clearing for BMD/USD payments to local beneficiaries. The 'XT_Transfer' rail, comprising cross-border instant or express transfer platforms such as XTransfer, Payoneer, or Wise Business, is acceptable only for low-value, high-frequency buyer collections and supplier disbursements, and must be reconciled daily to the primary BMA-licensed account; Letters of Credit and major settlements cannot ride on XT rails. All payment instructions must reference the related purchase order and invoice to satisfy the bank's transaction-monitoring filters.Outright FX forward contracts (USD/EUR, USD/GBP, EUR/GBP) executed with an authorised dealer or the relationship bank under an ISDA or ISDA-style master agreement; no BMA licensing or exchange-control approval is required for a non-resident designated exempted company.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts permitting USD-settled hedging of invoice exposures in restricted or non-convertible currencies (e.g., CNY, INR) without requiring physical delivery of the restricted currency.Cross-currency swaps (coupon-only or principal exchange) between USD and EUR/GBP executed under an ISDA Master Agreement with a licensed international bank; these are permitted under Bermuda law and treated as ordinary treasury products outside the scope of the Exchange Control Act 1972.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium-High as a supplementary collection and disbursement rail only. Regulated UK/EU electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) and cross-border payment platforms such as Payoneer/XTransfer may be used to receive buyer remittances in local currencies and to pay small/mid-ticket suppliers, with funds safeguarded at partner banks. Legal constraints: Bermuda has no domestic EMI licensing regime; the BMA does not authorise non-deposit-taking trade clearing as an EMI activity, and the exempted company's offshore position does not require a local EMI licence. Foreign-based platforms holding funds at offshore partner banks are outside Bermuda's exchange control remit and do not trigger a Bermuda licence, but they cannot issue or confirm Letters of Credit, cannot provide Bermuda clearing, and do not satisfy creditor settlement expectations for primary trade flows. The EMI/platform rail must therefore be reconciled daily to the primary BMA-licensed bank account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0.000% - Bermuda levies no corporate income tax on Bermuda Exempted Companies; the statutory rate is nil and the Income Tax Act 1961 does not apply to exempted undertakings. The Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 guarantees no future introduction of income, capital gains, or dividend taxes.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0.000% - Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends and no income tax on distributions received by shareholders. Dividend remittances from the Bermuda Exempted Company to the UK parent are made free of any Bermudian deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 0 - Bermuda has no statutory Local File/Master File filing requirement and no income tax adjustment mechanism. Arm's-length documentation becomes commercially and practically mandatory where UK intra-group re-invoicing turnover exceeds GBP 1,000,000 or where the multinational group meets the OECD/Large Business threshold of EUR 50 million consolidated revenue, as HMRC will demand a UK Local File in any inquiry. The contemporaneous benchmark must be retained in the UK parent group.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations 2018 apply in full: a general re-invoicing and trade intermediary is a 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity. The entity must demonstrate adequate physical premises, a proportionate employee headcount, and annual operating expenditure in Bermuda in line with the core income-generating activities performed there. For a re-invoicing hub, the central CIG functions are contract negotiation, invoicing, logistics coordination, and risk-taking decisions, all of which must be directed and managed from Bermuda. Failure to satisfy the ESR exposes the entity to penalties of BMD 10,000 plus BMD 1,000 per day, rising to BMD 60,000 for non-compliance with notices and BMD 250,000 for deliberate evasion, with parallel HMRC scrutiny under UK CTA 2010 Part 9A and the Diverted Profits Tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / UK Diverted Profits Tax and CFC Erosion

Statutory sanction: UK Diverted Profits Tax at 6% of diverted profits with an additional 30% premium where HMRC issues a DPT notice after a two-year investigation window; CFC apportionment under CTA 2010 Part 9A at the full UK corporation tax rate; penalties for failure to notify up to 100% of the assessed tax and interest running from the date the tax should have been paid.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable where they knowingly directed the artificial structures; the UK GAAR counteraction adds a 60% penalty. Travel bans, asset freezing orders, and extradition proceedings can be pursued under the UK-Bermuda Mutual Legal Assistance framework.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Bermuda ESR civil penalties of BMD 10,000 escalating by BMD 1,000 per day, BMD 60,000 for failure to answer a corrective notice, and BMD 250,000 for evasion or misrepresentation. The Registrar may strike off the company, and the UK HMRC will treat the structure as a DPT or CFC target with an automatic information exchange under the UK-Bermuda EOI arrangement.

Director personal liability: Every director at the date of the default is jointly and severally liable for unpaid ESR penalties; court-imposed management bans of up to 5 years may be issued by the Supreme Court of Bermuda, and UK directors face disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986.
AML / Sanctions Breach in Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Bermuda AML/ATF Regulations 2020 and the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997: criminal fines on the company of up to BMD 1,000,000 plus confiscation of proceeds. UK OFSI sanctions penalties of up to 50% of the transaction value apply where a UK-connected bank or counterparty is used.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal prosecution, travel bans, asset freezing orders, and imprisonment for up to 15 years for illicit money transmission; director disqualification follows any UK insolvency or OFSI enforcement action.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: HMRC primary adjustment followed by penalties of 35% to 100% of the tax understated; where the UK TP documentation is absent, further daily penalties of GBP 300 per day apply. Bermuda itself imposes no TP penalty, but all TP evidence is examinable in a UK inquiry under CTA 2010 Part 4.

Director personal liability: An officer who signs a deliberately false TP certificate may be prosecuted for fraudulent evasion of tax under UK TMA 1970; civil restitution can be claimed against directors who caused the misstatement, and personal liability for the diverted tax arises under the DPT anti-avoidance provisions.
Fraudulent Trade Re-Invoicing and Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent trading liability under Bermuda Companies Act 1981 (including s. 148) and UK Insolvency Act 1986 s. 213: court-ordered contribution of the full deficit. The offence is indictable and punishable by confiscation orders, fines, and imprisonment for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Civil courts order full personal restitution of company assets lost through the scheme; directors are disqualified for up to 15 years; the Supreme Court of Bermuda and UK courts impose travel bans and surrendered passports pending trial, and imprisonment follows conviction for fraud.
