Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Ministry of Finance / Office of the Tax Commissioner (administers payroll tax and other direct levies); Bermuda Customs Service (administers customs duties and trade controls); Registrar of Companies (company formation and exempted-status certificates); Government portal: www.govt.bm (online services via the Bermuda Tax & Customs digital portals).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishes a Bermuda Exempted Company under the Companies Act 1981 by filing incorporation documents through a licensed local registered agent with the Registrar of Companies. Upon incorporation, the company receives a Certificate of Incorporation and an assigned registration number. Because Bermuda imposes no corporate income tax, VAT, or capital gains tax, the company is not required to procure a CIT TIN or VAT registration number. Where the company employs staff, it must register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner for Payroll Tax and obtain a Payroll Tax Account Number. For import/export activity, it must register with the Bermuda Customs Service (via customs broker) and obtain a Customs Business Registration / Declarant account for filing declarations on the Bermuda Customs Single Window.

Default tax classification

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Customs Duties (no VAT/GST system in Bermuda; no domestic indirect tax on wholesale distribution services)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold applicable. Customs registration is mandatory for any person importing or exporting goods into or out of Bermuda irrespective of economic value; a customs broker or declarant account must be registered. Payroll tax registration applies to every employer, with no minimum turnover threshold, if the company has Bermuda-resident employees; employer registration is triggered upon first hiring a locally based employee.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST. Goods exported from Bermuda are outside the scope of customs duty; no local VAT zero-rating claim or refund mechanism exists. For customs purposes, the physical export of acetate eyewear frames must be evidenced to satisfy Bermuda Customs that the goods are not imported for domestic consumption; export documentation must identify the consignee abroad, the export trade reason, and the outward conveyance.

  • Bermuda Customs Outward Manifest / Export Declaration
  • Commercial Invoice describing acetate ophthalmic eyewear frames (HS 9003.11 / 9003.19 nomenclature)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from Bermuda
  • Packing List and freight documentation supporting the outward movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange inflow evidence where export proceeds are credited to a Bermuda bank account

No real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime is mandated for B2B goods invoices. Customs declarations must be lodged electronically through the Bermuda Customs Automated Processing System / Bermuda Trade Single Window portal; API- or portal-based submission of import/export cargo manifests and customs declarations is required. Regular invoices may be issued freely without government fiscal clearance.

Although no VAT zero-rating refund is at risk, Bermuda Customs and the Financial Authority may audit export transactions to prevent duty evasion and trade-based money laundering. False export declarations or misdescribed goods attract penalties under the Customs Tariff Act 2017 (including penalties up to the value of the goods or ten times the duty avoided), forfeiture of goods, and criminal sanctions. Misdeclarations can trigger retrospective re-classification, seizure, and revocation of trader customs privileges; payroll tax non-compliance attracts assessments, interest, and penalties up to 10% of unpaid tax plus daily fines. Exempted status under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 may also be revoked for fraud or serious compliance breaches.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at parity (1:1) to the United States Dollar (USD), creating a de facto hard-currency zone with minimal convertibility friction. Current-account payments, including trade settlements, logistics costs, insurance, royalties, and profit remittances, are freely convertible without prior BMA approval. Individual transfers equal to or exceeding BMD 500,000 (or the USD equivalent) must be reported by the receiving bank to the BMA for balance-of-payments statistics, but this is an ex-post notification obligation, not a pre-authorisation hurdle. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity subscription and outbound dividend declarations, are lawful for exempted companies under the BMA General Permission No. 1 framework; however, material capital movements must be accompanied by a complete beneficial-ownership pack, bank-source verification, and the company's valid Economic Substance Certificate. Bermuda imposes 0% dividend withholding tax and 0% capital gains tax, so profit repatriation to the United Kingdom parent is entirely tax-neutral at the Bermuda layer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for trade receipts. The Bermuda Exempted Company may retain export proceeds in any permissible foreign currency (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, CNY) and redeploy them directly against import disbursements, avoiding FX conversion drag. Nevertheless, any single export-credit entry equal to or exceeding BMD 500,000 must be logged by the receiving bank into the BMA statistical report, and the client must maintain a verifiable audit trail of the underlying sales contracts, commercial invoices, packing lists, and customs export declarations in the KYC/CDD file.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the central bank and exchange-control administrator under the Exchange Control Act 1972 (as amended by the Exchange Control (Amendment) Act 2020), the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, and the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low feasibility. Bermuda does not recognise UK-authorised EMI passporting, and offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, etc.) are not automatically permitted to clear BMD or to act as primary settlement banks for a Bermuda-exempted company. Such platforms may only be used as payment-instrument facades routed through a BMA-licensed clearing sponsor or through the United Kingdom parent's GBP account when the flow is entirely UK-side. The statutory alternative is to hold a full Payment Services Licence under the Bermuda Payment Services Act 2018, which requires a local trust or service-provider footprint and materially higher compliance cost; therefore the optimal blueprint places BMA-licensed tier-1 banks at the core and confines EMIs to low-value, high-frequency operational disbursements (e.g., courier, sample and ancillary platform fees) that settle at the licensed bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for Bermuda exempted companies outside the scope of the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023; 15% for constituent entities of in-scope multinational groups meeting the EUR 750,000,000 consolidated revenue threshold under the OECD Pillar Two / GloBE rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% Bermuda statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Bermuda exempted company to non-resident shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 1,000,000 for related-party cross-border transactions triggering mandatory Local File documentation for Bermuda CIT in-scope entities; Master File is expected for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding BMD 100,000,000. For ordinary exempted companies outside the CIT scope, no transfer pricing documentation is statutorily required, but contemporaneous arm's-length records should be kept to substantiate economic substance and resist recharacterization.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations (Amended) apply to any Bermuda exempted company carrying on a relevant activity, including 'distribution and service centre'. The wholesale of ophthalmic eyewear frames falls squarely within that category. The company must demonstrate that core income-generating activities are managed and directed in Bermuda, maintain adequate employees and premises in Bermuda, incur sufficient real operating expenditure, and submit the annual Economic Substance Return electronically. The Tax Exemption Certificate does not waive the ES Return requirement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations permit default assessments, strike-off, financial penalties up to BMD 250,000 per offence, and, on conviction, fines up to BMD 500,000; the foreign revenue authority may recharacterize the arrangement as artificial and impose tax, interest, and transfer-pricing penalties.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where directors know of the sham; directors can be personally liable for unpaid taxes, disqualified from acting as directors in Bermuda, subjected to travel bans and immigration detention for non-cooperation, and imprisoned for criminal evasion or false statements.
Beneficial Ownership and AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Beneficial Ownership (Companies) Act 2017 and the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, penalties include regulatory fines up to BMD 250,000, unlimited fines for money laundering, asset freezing and forfeiture, and imprisonment up to 14 years for laundering proceeds or 5 years for record-keeping violations.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable if they knowingly or negligently allow the company to process unlawful payments; personal asset recovery orders, banning from financial services, travel restriction, and custodial sentences can all be imposed in respect of AML failures.
Transfer Pricing and Misinvoicing of Related-Party Trade

Statutory sanction: If Bermuda CIT applies, the Bermuda Tax Administration can adjust the profit, disallow expense deductions, impose interest, and levy penalties up to 100% of the underpaid top-up tax. In foreign jurisdictions, the same facts trigger secondary adjustments, withholding taxes on deemed dividends, and penalties under anti-avoidance legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing certificates or direct invoice manipulation can be held jointly and severally liable for the tax result, banned from future trade, have their passports surrendered, and may be sentenced to imprisonment for tax evasion as directors of a fraudulent conduit.
Money Laundering via Cross-Border Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Bermuda's Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Act, Monetary Authority of Bermuda directives, and the Proceeds of Crime Act apply to financial activity; penalties include monetary penalties up to BMD 1 million per contravention for firms, unlimited fines on natural persons, confiscation of proceeds, and imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize unstructured fund movements are personally liable under civil restitution, may be banned from holding senior positions, subjected to travel bans and surrender of passports, face seizure of personal assets, and risk custodial penalties for conspiracy to launder proceeds.
Civil Tax Evasion and Obstructing Foreign Tax Authorities

Statutory sanction: Bermuda will enforce exchange of information requests; the UK may apply corporate criminal offences, penalties for deliberate failure, interest, and a potential 100% supplement; in Bermuda, obstructing the Tax Information Exchange regime can lead to BMD 100,000 fines and dissolution of the exempted company.

Director personal liability: Director travel bans may be issued pending interrogation or production of records; directors can be made personally liable for the foreign tax plus penalties if the entity is insolvent and the court finds the offshore vehicle was a mere facade; imprisonment for false declarations and perjury is available in serious cases.
