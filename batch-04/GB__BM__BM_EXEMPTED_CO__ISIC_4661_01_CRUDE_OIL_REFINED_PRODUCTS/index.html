Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Department of Taxation (Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance), portal https://www.gov.bm/taxes; Corporate Income Tax Agency (CITA administration); Bermuda Customs Department (ASYCUDA World single window). The UK's HMRC has no direct jurisdiction: Bermuda is a fiscally autonomous British Overseas Territory with a separate tax identity.

E-government filing portal

Incorporation under the Companies Act 1981 yields the Certificate of Incorporation, a Companies Register number, and automatic 'exempted undertaking' status. No income-tax TIN exists. The standard 5-step procurement for a foreign-owned exempted company: (1) obtain Bermuda Monetary Authority exchange-control approval as 'non-resident' through the registered agent; (2) register for employer payroll tax with the Department of Taxation if Bermuda employees are remunerated (payroll tax number issued); (3) register as a Financial Institution for CRS/FATCA and obtain a GIIN/reporting number; (4) if the MNE group has revenue >= EUR 750 million, obtain CITA registration for the 15% corporate income tax/QDMTT (fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025); (5) obtain a Customs importer/exporter code only if physical goods move through Bermuda. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Bermuda exempted companies are taxed on a nil territorial base: no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, wealth tax, withholding tax, or inheritance tax on offshore trading income; the only local imposts are payroll tax (graduated employer rates, generally 5%-10% on Bermuda-sourced remuneration), land tax, customs duty, and stamp duty. Exception: CITA 2023 imposes a 15% top-up tax (IIR/UTPR/QDMTT) on constituent entities of in-scope MNE groups, overriding the tax-exempt classification for those groups from fiscal year 2025.

Available election forms

No US check-the-box or UK hybrid-mismatch election is available because the entity is not subject to a domestic income tax on its ordinary profits. Tax identity elections available: (i) Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Certificate election under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (as amended 2020), guaranteeing no income tax or capital levy until 30 June 2050; (ii) CITA 2023 GloBE elections - QDMTT parity/safe-harbour elections, currency and aggregate elections under the OECD Model Rules; (iii) Economic Substance Act 2018 relevant-activity notification and substance-test elections, filed through the Companies Register; (iv) exchange-control 'non-resident' designation elected at incorporation through the BMA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Bermuda customs duty regime (Customs Tariff Act 1972, as amended); no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax exists in Bermuda.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold is prescribed in Bermuda. A Customs importer/exporter code under the Customs Tariff Act 1972 is mandatory only for physical importation/exportation through Bermuda, with ad valorem duties generally 0%-35%; offshore wholesale brokerage (ISIC 4661) of crude oil and refined products that never enters Bermuda creates no indirect-tax nexus or registration requirement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - Bermuda has no VAT, hence no zero-rating claim or export-refund mechanism exists. The exempted company's offshore brokerage income is outside the Bermuda tax base ab initio: title and risk in the marine fuel pass at non-Bermudian ports (typically FOB Rotterdam/Singapore), goods never enter Bermuda, and no Bermuda output tax is charged. To sustain the tax-exempt treatment the company relies on: (i) a valid Tax Protection Certificate (protection to 2050); (ii) Economic Substance Act 2018 compliance where a 'distribution and service centre' relevant activity exists (management and control in Bermuda, adequate employees, expenditure, premises), with the annual ES return filed within 6 months of year-end; (iii) CRS/FATCA filing; and (iv) CITA 2023 returns if the group exceeds the EUR 750 million GloBE threshold. Although Bermuda imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline, trade-finance providers and UK-linked AML reviews apply a 180-day inward-remittance benchmark for proceeds substantiation.

  • No Bermuda customs export declaration, duty stamp, or VAT certificate is required for cargo that never transits Bermuda; the mandatory commercial and audit bundle for the offshore marine-fuel wholesale brokerage comprises:
  • Customs Export Declaration of the loading jurisdiction (e.g., Singapore Customs, Dutch Customs or UAE Customs export approval)
  • Bill of Lading evidencing clean title transfer (FOB/CIF terms)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/LC settlement advice evidencing proceeds receipt
  • Standby Letter of Credit or documentary Letter of Credit plus presentation documents
  • Certificate of quality/quantity (SGS or independent surveyor report) and Certificate of Origin where destination rules require
  • FATCA/CRS registration record and Bermuda exchange-control non-resident certificate

No continuous-transaction-control, fiscal e-invoicing clearance, or digital-stamping mandate applies to B2B invoices in Bermuda; offshore trade invoices require no Bermudian fiscal approval. Customs processing is fully electronic via the UNCTAD ASYCUDA World platform, requiring digital lodgment of import/export declarations, electronic duty computation, and online payment, with automated 'green lane' release for low-risk declarations.

Bermuda levies no VAT, so no VAT clawback is possible; the operative risks are: Economic Substance Act 2018 penalties up to BMD 250,000 (and up to BMD 100,000 on directing officers), strike-off and revocation of exempted status; CITA 2023 assessment of 15% top-up tax, late-filing and understatement penalties, and retrospective adjustment from 1 January 2025 notwithstanding tax protection certificates; payroll tax default penalties (10% surcharge plus interest) for concealed Bermuda remuneration; customs penalties up to three times duty evaded and forfeiture for misdeclaration; and, for fraudulent trade finance documentation (counterfeit bills of lading, circular LC chains, fictitious sales), penalties under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 (up to 14 years' imprisonment), civil recovery orders, and cancellation of BMA permissions, with HMRC/UK OFSI coordination given the UK sovereign nexus.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the United States Dollar (USD), which effectively removes USD exchange risk for the marine-fuel brokerage treasury. Bermuda operates an open capital and current account system; for a non-resident exempted company, there is no meaningful distinction between capital and current account items — both are freely convertible and fully repatriable. Profit remittance to the UK parent by way of dividends or management fees does not require BMA consent, provided the entity retains its non-resident exchange-control status and its statutory filing records are current. The only real hurdles are banking-layer AML scrutiny, not statutory convertibility limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio. There is no deadline for repatriation and no obligation to convert marine-fuel-brokerage export receipts from USD, EUR, or any other freely convertible currency into BMD. Non-resident exempted companies may retain export proceeds indefinitely in offshore or onshore clearing accounts, subject to bank-level source-of-funds documentation and a commercially reasonable transactional rationale.

Central bank supervision: The Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, serves as the statutory monetary authority and prudential supervisor, exercising monetary and exchange-control functions jointly under the Exchange Control Act 1972. It licenses banks under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, administers AML/ATF obligations under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008, and issues exchange-control permissions to 'non-resident' entities such as Bermuda Exempted Companies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-Low feasibility. A UK FCA-authorized EMI cannot lawfully provide Bermuda domestic clearing or hold BMD without a BMA banking or money-service-business license. Such EMIs typically exclude ISIC 4661 crude-oil and refined-products brokerage on sanctions and reputational grounds. EMIs are acceptable only as supplementary low-value collection channels for commissions smaller than USD 50,000; all Standby LC, escrow, and major trade settlements must flow through BMA-supervised licensed banks. The entity should maintain Butterfield or HSBC Bank Bermuda as the primary clearing firewall and treat EMIs as API-connected convenience rails, never as treasury counterparties.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax (CIT) for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025, enacted under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 as amended by the CIT (Amendment) Act 2024, and applying only to Bermuda exempted companies that are members of multinational enterprise groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000 (OECD Pillar Two scope). All other Bermuda Exempted Companies remain subject to the historical 0% corporate income tax regime and pay only annual Listed Government Fees.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda does not impose withholding tax, income tax, or capital gains tax on dividends paid by a Bermuda Exempted Company to any shareholder, resident or non-resident.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda statutory local-currency Local File or Master File threshold has been enacted. The operative threshold is the OECD Pillar Two scoping rule: multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more must file Country-by-Country data and maintain contemporaneous OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines documentation through the Bermuda Tax Administration. Out-of-scope exempted companies must nonetheless retain pricing documentation (intercompany brokerage commissions, cost-plus computations on trading operations, treasury and SBLC fee allocations) to demonstrate economic substance and to rebut any characterization as a letterbox or booking shell.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018 (as amended) subjects every Bermuda Exempted Company carrying on a relevant activity — including distribution and service centre, shipping, financing/finance and leasing, and headquarters activities all implicated by marine-fuel wholesale brokerage, SBLC credit-line treasury, and SWIFT-settled commodity trading — to a dual test: (i) direction and management in Bermuda (regular board meetings, local quorum, strategic decisions taken in the Islands) and (ii) core income-generating activities performed in Bermuda (contract negotiation, commodity procurement, trade booking, treasury and trade-line administration), supported by adequate employees, physical premises, and proportionate expenditure. Pure holding-entity relief is unavailable where active trading income exists; failure to satisfy the test is a 'systemic failure' attracting escalating sanctions, and the Registrar coordinates ESR findings with the CIT administration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 150% of the under-declared tax in cases of deliberate inaccuracy, plus fixed and daily filing penalties, statutory interest at 8% per annum, disallowance of QRTC claims, and criminal prosecution under the Criminal Code and CIT Act 2023 with confiscation orders; compelled disclosure to foreign revenue authorities under TIEAs and CbC exchange arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors, senior officers, and beneficial owners face personal civil restitution, substantial personal fines, travel bans, director disqualification orders, and imprisonment (up to 14 years where money-laundering or fraud statutes are engaged); courts will pierce the exempted-company veil where the entity is a mere façade or undercapitalized conduit for upstream group tax evasion.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR sanctions escalate from fixed penalties (up to BD$25,000 for late or non-filing) to a maximum penalty of BD$250,000 for a single economic-substance failure and BD$1,000,000 for a second consecutive failure; the Registrar may strike off the company, report the entity to foreign tax authorities, and impose officer-level fines.

Director personal liability: Officers are subject to statutory officer liability for ESR defaults, personal fines, travel restriction orders, disqualification, and continued personal liability after strike-off where assets were stripped; group directors may additionally be named in adverse findings exchanged under the international substance review process.
AML, Terrorist Financing, Sanctions and Frauds on Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fines, confiscation and forfeiture orders, and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997; loss of the statutory commodity-trading license for ISIC 4661/NAICS 424720 activity; and exclusion from correspondent banking and target payment rails (Standby Letters of Credit and SWIFT wires).

Director personal liability: Directors, MLROs, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face personal criminal prosecution, asset freezes, travel bans, extradition exposure, and mandatory disqualification; the corporate veil will be disregarded where the exempted company was used as the instrument of financial crime, exposing principals to joint and several civil and criminal liability.
