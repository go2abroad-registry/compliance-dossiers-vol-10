Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Conditionally permitted for a low-risk ESG carbon advisory and audit SAS. A French SAS may use a licensed domiciliation company or a managed business centre as its registered office, provided the arrangement reflects genuine commercial substance and the company maintains actual operational documents in France. Because the business is a professional service activity without a statutory license, a clearly documented virtual-office arrangement is acceptable at incorporation stage.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre National des Entreprises maintained by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), with commercial registration processed through the competent local commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 et seq. governing the Société par Actions Simplifiée; Articles L.123-1 et seq. governing registration and the Registre National des Entreprises; Articles L.210-2 and R.210-3 et seq. governing corporate formation and filings.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic application for registration through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, including the signed SAS articles of association, beneficial-owner declaration, and supporting formation documents.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. For cash contributions, at least 50% of each subscribed cash contribution must be paid upon incorporation; the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Contributions in kind are generally fully contributed at incorporation, subject to applicable auditor and valuation rules. No statutory escrow account is required, but paid-in cash must be deposited with an authorized bank or notary before registration and released after evidence of incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: No manufacturing-specific civil-defense or workplace operating clearance is required for a standard office. The premises must satisfy applicable French fire-safety, emergency-access, electrical, accessibility, and occupational-health requirements. An établissement recevant du public may require approval by the local safety commission; leased offices should have compliant fire systems, evacuation procedures, workplace risk assessment, and structural-load verification where the building owner or use change requires it.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing board is ordinarily applicable to office-based ESG advisory, carbon accounting, or non-physical brokerage activities. For any physical installation or regulated environmental activity, the relevant authority is the Préfet of the department, acting through the Direction régionale de l'environnement, de l'aménagement et du logement (DREAL), under the Code de l'environnement.

Category: No general manufacturing or industrial operating permit is required for the stated advisory activity. A separate authorization, registration, or declaration under the Installations Classées pour la Protection de l'Environnement (ICPE) regime may be required if the company later operates qualifying storage, combustion, waste, chemical, or industrial facilities.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; official portals: https://www.impots.gouv.fr (professional taxpayer portal) and https://portail-public-facturation.fr (mandated e-invoicing and e-reporting public portal); customs and export-control functions are executed by Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) through the DELTA customs clearance system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Assess establishment: a foreign entity either (i) incorporates a French SAS through the Guichet Unique (INPI), obtaining SIREN/SIRET from INSEE and triggering assignment by the DGFiP of a French VAT registration number (format FR + key + 9 digits) and a corporate income tax (IS) file, or (ii) operates without a French permanent establishment. Step 2 - Where no French PE exists, the foreign supplier must register for French VAT before the first taxable transaction in France: EU-established entities file Cerfa form 3519-SD; non-EU entities file Cerfa form 3520-SD and must appoint an accredited tax representative (Article 289 A CGI) who is jointly and severally liable for VAT. Step 3 - The completed registration request is submitted at the competent SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) through the impots.gouv.fr professional portal. Step 4 - Upon validation, the DGFiP issues the French VAT number, opens the IS file, and enrols the entity for the mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting regime (PPF/PDP). Standard turnaround is 2 to 6 weeks after receipt of a complete dossier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - A French SAS is a separate taxable legal person and is not a pass-through vehicle by default; it is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on French-source and allocable profits, standard rate 25%, with a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of annual profit for qualifying SMEs with turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million. The SAS falls within the territorial scope of French taxation for its French activities, is a distinct VAT taxable person, and does not benefit from any tax-exempt zone status.

Available election forms

France does not apply a US-style check-the-box election, but the following statutory elections are available to an SAS: (1) the option for transparent partnership taxation under Article 239 bis AA of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI), available for up to five consecutive fiscal years to a small unlisted SAS (up to 50 employees and annual turnover or balance-sheet total not exceeding EUR 10 million) upon unanimous shareholder consent and notification to the DGFiP; (2) the fiscal-integration election ('intégration fiscale') under Article 223 A CGI allowing a French parent holding at least 95% of the SAS to consolidate its taxable results; (3) the VAT-grouping election ('assujetti unique') under Article 267 bis CGI allowing related French entities to be treated as a single taxable person for VAT; and (4) hybrid-mismatch and transfer-pricing notification elections implemented under ATAD (Article 212 bis B CGI).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established foreign suppliers have no exemption threshold: registration is compulsory from the first taxable B2B transaction in France. For French-established small enterprises, the 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption applies up to annual taxable turnover of EUR 91,900 for supplies of goods (tolerance EUR 101,000) or EUR 36,800 for services and liberal activities, such as ISIC 7020 ESG carbon audit and advisory services (tolerance EUR 39,100); crossing the tolerance triggers VAT liability and compulsory registration. For EU established suppliers of B2C e-commerce distance sales, the EUR 10,000 pan-Union OSS threshold applies, above which French VAT is chargeable unless the OSS scheme is used.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (1) exports of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the seller or by a third party acting on the seller's behalf, under Article 262-I-1° CGI; (2) intra-Community supplies of goods to a customer holding a valid EU VAT identification number, under Article 262 ter CGI; and (3) supplies of services treated as taking place outside France under Articles 259 and 259 A CGI when the B2B customer is established outside France, which covers cross-border ESG carbon auditing, Scope 1-3 carbon footprint verification, and offset brokerage services supplied to non-French clients. The seller must retain full documentary proof of physical export or of the offshore place-of-supply rule within the statutory records period, and for intra-EU goods the customer's VAT number must be validated via VIES.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU - Déclaration Administrativo-Union) processed and validated through the DGDDI DELTA export clearance system
  • Commercial Invoice issued to the foreign buyer or offshore client
  • Packing List (colisage) where applicable
  • Bill of Lading (connaissement maritime), Air Waybill (lettre de transport aérien) or CMR road consignment note evidencing physical dispatch outside the EU or intra-Community movement
  • VIES validation certificate of the EU customer's VAT number for intra-Community zero-rated supplies
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing effective cross-border payment of the export proceeds
  • Customs export proof report extracted from the DELTA system and available via the DGDDI 'Infocentre' portal

France operates a continuous transaction control (CTC) model via mandatory structured e-invoicing and e-reporting, without a digital fiscal stamping or prior real-time clearance mechanism. Under the Finance Act 2022, as postponed by the Finance Act 2024: from 1 September 2026 all companies must be able to receive structured electronic invoices, and large enterprises and mid-caps (ETI) must issue e-invoices and transmit e-reporting; from 1 September 2027 all small and medium-sized enterprises must issue e-invoices and transmit e-reporting for B2C and cross-border flows. Mandated formats: Factur-X (hybrid PDF/XML), Peppol BIS 3.0 or UBL, conforming to the EN 16931 semantic standard. Invoices and transaction data are submitted via a certified PDP (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire) or through the DGFiP-operated Portail Public de Facturation (PPF), which provides the national interoperability directory and API-based data transmission; no separate digital stamp is required.

The DGFiP and the DGDDI jointly cross-check DELTA export declarations with VAT returns (CA3 and annex 3310-A) supporting export zero-rating and refund claims. Missing, incomplete or false export evidence leads to reassessment of French VAT at the 20% standard rate, the statutory monthly late-payment interest (intérêt de retard), and an additional penalty of 40% for intentional deficiencies under Article 1729 CGI, rising to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law; refunded VAT credits are subject to retrospective clawback (répétition de l'indu) within the ordinary 3-year assessment period, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or undisclosed activity under Article L 169 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales. Directors of an SAS participating in fictitious export flows or sham carbon-offset brokerage additionally face criminal tax-fraud exposure under Article 1741 CGI, with a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and five years' imprisonment, plus possible disqualification and debarment from the PPF e-invoicing system.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under a fully liberalized foreign exchange regime within the Eurozone. Both capital and current accounts are convertible without prior authorization. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted subject only to standard corporate governance documentation, beneficial ownership verification, and anti-money laundering compliance. Primary hurdles include source-of-funds attestation and alignment with French tax residency rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Service exports such as carbon footprint auditing and ESG advisory do not trigger mandatory surrender of proceeds under French law. Repatriation is recommended within 90 days via banking channels but is not statutorily mandated. Any goods-related offset brokerage components require surrender to a designated bank account within 15 days per customs regulations.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory dividend withholding tax rate: 30% for non-resident corporate shareholders; 12.8% for non-resident individual shareholders. A 0% rate applies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where an EU parent company holds at least 10% of the French entity's share capital for a minimum of 24 consecutive months.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal years ending on 31 December, the return is due by 3 May (or the second business day after 1 May if 3 May is non-working) of the following calendar year. For other fiscal year-ends, the return is due within three months of the close of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 (annual turnover or total assets) triggers the mandatory preparation and filing of French transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File). Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the form applied in offshore centres. Instead, the General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article L64 of the French Book of Tax Procedures), the abuse-of-law doctrine, and the OECD Principal Purpose Test (via the MLI) require genuine economic substance, local decision-making, contractual relevance and operational staff. For ESG carbon accounting or offset brokerage, the entity must evidence real premises, employees, and commercial activity in France to support treaty eligibility and deductibility of intra-group charges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; aggravated or organised commission increases penalties to EUR 3,000,000 and 7 years. For legal persons, the fine is multiplied up to five-fold and additional sanctions include judicial supervision or dissolution.

Director personal liability: Directors or statutory officers who knowingly participate in or authorise evasion are jointly liable for the tax underpaid plus penalties and interest. Criminal conviction triggers personal asset seizure, travel bans, prohibition from managing a business, and imprisonment.
Artificial Shell Company / Abuse of Law (Abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment with a penalty of 80% of the reassessed amount under the abuse-of-law procedure, plus late-payment interest of 0.20% per month. If the scheme invokes treaty benefits, the DTA relief is denied and fraud penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who are deemed the decisive authors of the artificial arrangement may be held personally liable for the additional assessments and monetary penalties; criminal sanctions for tax evasion can include imprisonment and travel bans.
AML Non-Compliance / Money Laundering (Blanchiment)

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties of up to EUR 500,000 or 10% of annual turnover for eligible entities imposed by the ACPR or the Commission Nationale des Sanctions; criminal penalties for money laundering include fines up to EUR 500,000 for individuals and confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability where non-reporting is intentional or negligent and laundered transactions flow through the entity. Consequences include custodial sentences up to 5 years, fines, asset forfeiture, and travel prohibitions.
