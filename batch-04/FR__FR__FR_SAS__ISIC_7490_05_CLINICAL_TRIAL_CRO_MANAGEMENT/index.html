Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, with registration completed in the Registre National des Entreprises and the competent local Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés maintained by the commercial court registry.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 et seq. governing sociétés par actions simplifiées; Articles L.123-1 et seq. and R.123-1 et seq. governing registration and the Registre National des Entreprises; Articles L.123-11 et seq. governing registered offices and business formalities.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company-creation formalité submitted through the INPI Guichet unique, supported by executed SAS articles of association, beneficial-owner declaration, and required identification and premises documents. The former paper M0 form is generally replaced by the electronic formalité.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions must generally be deposited with an authorized bank or notary before registration and evidenced by a deposit certificate. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed and may require a commissaire aux apports report, subject to statutory exemptions.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain premises fire-safety compliance appropriate to the building and, where applicable, établissement recevant du public (ERP) requirements; maintain emergency exits, alarms, extinguishers, evacuation plans, and fire-training records. Obtain landlord or building approvals for structural loads and laboratory installations where relevant. Complete occupational-risk assessment under the French Labour Code, establish a DUERP, provide required training and personal protective equipment, and arrange occupational-health monitoring. Additional inspections or approvals apply to laboratories, chemical storage, biosafety, controlled substances, and public-access premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions must generally be deposited with an authorized bank or notary before registration and evidenced by a deposit certificate. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed and may require a commissaire aux apports report, subject to statutory exemptions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) / French Tax Administration, via the impots.gouv.fr professional portal and the Chorus Pro government e-invoicing platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity conducting taxable French activities without a French permanent establishment must first appoint a fiscal representative or establish a local registered branch, then apply to the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) using the French forms (Cerfa 14416 and related registration filings). The application must enclose the foreign entity's constitutional documents, proof of registered office, certificate of tax residence, and valid non-French business registration. Upon validation, the SIE issues a French TIN and intra-community VAT number. For purely foreign-established service suppliers supplying cross-border B2B services into France, a simplified EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) or Reverse Charge mechanism may be applicable, but a direct French VAT registration is mandatory if local taxable supplies are made or if French VAT is invoiced.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity conducting taxable French activities without a French permanent establishment must first appoint a fiscal representative or establish a local registered branch, then apply to the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) using the French forms (Cerfa 14416 and related registration filings). The application must enclose the foreign entity's constitutional documents, proof of registered office, certificate of tax residence, and valid non-French business registration. Upon validation, the SIE issues a French TIN and intra-community VAT number. For purely foreign-established service suppliers supplying cross-border B2B services into France, a simplified EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) or Reverse Charge mechanism may be applicable, but a direct French VAT registration is mandatory if local taxable supplies are made or if French VAT is invoiced.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not apply a check-the-box regime. The default is full corporate income tax (IS) at entity level for an SAS. Limited statutory options include the five-year preferential income tax (IR) election for newly created qualifying companies under Article 239 bis AB of the French General Tax Code (CGI), the group tax consolidation election (intégration fiscale) for subsidiaries held at least 95%, and specific hybrid mismatch adjustments required by the ATAD transposition; no general single-member transparency election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for French-established businesses carrying out taxable supplies; registration is required from the first taxable transaction. The franchise en base de TVA exemption may apply only to established qualifying small businesses below approximately EUR 91,900 for goods and EUR 36,800 for services, subject to annual adjustment. For non-French suppliers making intra-EU distance sales or certain B2C OSS supplies, the pan-EU EUR 10,000 annual turnover threshold determines the place of supply; above this threshold or upon election, local French VAT registration is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For CRO services, French VAT is excluded (outside scope) where the supply is made to a taxable customer established in a third country or, in the case of intra-EU B2B supplies, is transferred to the customer under the reverse-charge mechanism. To claim the 0% effective rate or non-assessment on export-related clinical trial services, the supplier must establish that the recipient is a taxable person established outside France, that the service is effectively rendered, and that the invoice and supporting contracts accurately identify the foreign customer. For export of goods, the zero-rate exemption applies only where physical export to a third country or dispatch to another EU Member State is evidenced by customs export documentation and transport proof.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE/ELISE DELTA X validation)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/FX transfer evidence of payment realisation
  • Commercial invoice and signed CRO service contract identifying the foreign recipient
  • Evidence of customer non-resident tax status (VAT ID proof or foreign tax residence certificate)

France has adopted a mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting framework. From the relevant reform deadlines (currently legislated for 2026-2027 waves), all B2B invoices must be issued in a structured digital format and transmitted through a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP), a public portal or Chorus Pro. Real-time transaction data must be e-reported for French VAT, including invoice acceptance and payment status, through system-to-system APIs or the government portal. Digital signature requirements and VAT cash-flow clearing controls apply, with non-compliant invoices disallowed as input VAT deduction evidence.

The French tax administration may recharacterise foreign supplies as taxable French services where the customer's status or effective establishment is not sufficiently documented. Improper export zero-rating leads to retroactive VAT assessment, input VAT denial, exposure to interest for late payment (intérêt de retard), the 40% deliberate breach penalty (manquement délibéré) or 80% penalty for fraudulent activity/abuse of right under CGI Articles 1728 and 1729, plus possible criminal sanctions for tax fraud under Article 1741. Export files are closely audited for real transaction substance, INCO term consistency, payment routing, and repatriation within the standard 180-day FX realisation due-diligence deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France has no general foreign-exchange controls restricting conversion or cross-border transfers of funds. The euro is freely convertible for current and capital transactions, subject to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules, tax requirements, financial-services regulation, and any applicable investment-screening or sector-specific rules. A French SAS may receive foreign capital and make lawful cross-border payments. Dividend and other profit distributions require legally distributable profits, corporate approvals, proper accounts, and applicable French withholding-tax treatment; treaty or EU relief may require eligibility and supporting documentation. Banks may require evidence of source of funds, transaction purpose, beneficial ownership, and tax status. Certain cross-border payments or transactions may also be reportable for statistical or regulatory purposes, depending on the parties, transaction, and applicable reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no general statutory deadline for surrendering export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted, subject to accounting, tax, sanctions, banking, and any applicable reporting obligations. The SAS should retain contracts, invoices, shipping or service-delivery evidence, and bank records supporting receipts.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France's central bank and participates in the Eurosystem. The French Treasury (Direction générale du Trésor) administers applicable financial and monetary regulations; the European Central Bank and applicable EU law govern relevant euro-area monetary matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine payment execution, subject to the provider's licensing, permitted services, country coverage, and onboarding approval. An EU-authorized electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment services under its applicable authorization and passporting rights, but an EMI account is not equivalent to a bank deposit and may not provide statutory capital-deposit certificates, lending, or all trade-finance services. Confirm safeguarding arrangements, payment limits, settlement currencies, sanctions controls, and access to SWIFT or SEPA before use. An offshore digital EMI without an applicable EU authorization should not be treated as a compliant substitute for a French corporate bank account.
