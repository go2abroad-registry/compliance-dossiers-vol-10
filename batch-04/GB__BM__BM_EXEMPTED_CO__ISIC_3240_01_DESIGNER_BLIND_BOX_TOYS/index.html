Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is pegged at a statutory 1:1 parity with the United States dollar and is freely exchangeable into USD, GBP and EUR. Bermuda exempted companies face no exchange-control licensing, no discriminatory reinvestment norms and no ministerial approval for dividend distributions, interest, management fees, liquidation proceeds or share redemption payments. The UK layer operates without discretionary foreign-exchange controls for B2C trade receipts, subject only to FCA safeguarding rules for payment-service users and HM Treasury reporting obligations on high-value movements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Bermuda exempted companies are not subject to any statutory timeline for repatriation, mandatory conversion ratio or surrender requirement for export receipts. Foreign-currency proceeds may be retained indefinitely in any BMA-licensed bank account in the currency elected by the seller. The UK imposes no export-proceeds surrender regime for resident companies or their Offshore Special Territory subsidiaries.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) under the Exchange Control Act 1972, supported by the Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) and the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) for the UK operational settlement layer under the Payment Services Regulations 2017 and the HM Treasury Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Regulations 2024.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rated HIGH with structural conditions. Bermuda does not maintain a local electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime; the EMI must hold a valid FCA e-money authorisation or an EEA equivalent to lawfully serve the UK digital-trade layer. Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex and Ebury are all viable for merchant FX conversion, supplier payouts, multi-currency wallet balances and API-driven payment automation. The principal legal constraints are: (i) an EMI cannot replicate the correspondent-banking anchor of a Bermuda licensed bank for high-value M2M instructions; (ii) the EMI's AML engine will require a genuine UK/EU commercial nexus, a physical operating address, UK VAT registration where applicable, a working website and full beneficial-ownership disclosure; and (iii) certain EMIs decline entities incorporated in Offshore Special Territories unless the entity can evidence substantive trading beyond passive asset holding. The recommended treasury architecture is therefore a dual-rail model: Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda for statutory banking, liquidity concentration and high-value settlement; and a single FCA-licensed EMI for high-velocity B2C collections, multi-currency conversions and automated supplier payout streams.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 into the Bermuda concentration account. Message discipline: Field 52A identifies the ordering bank as the shareholder's regulated financial institution; Field 57A identifies Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda as the receiving bank; Field 59 names the exempted company and account; and Field 70 must recite 'EQUITY CAPITAL INJECTION / SHARE SUBSCRIPTION / [shareholder legal name]'. No Bermuda exchange-control approval is required for the injection, but a BMA balance-of-payments statistical return is due where cumulative quarterly inflows exceed BMD 100,000. For injections originating from the UK, HM Treasury sanctions screening applies and the UK remitting bank will typically require a certified board resolution approving the allotment and an updated Register of Members. The proceeds may be received in BMD or USD and, where GBP is the operating currency, converted on a spot basis into the UK settlement account under a standing FX instruction.Trade payables in the designer-blind-box supply chain are settled through two approved rails. First, SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for standard inventory and logistics payments up to USD 1.5 million or equivalent, with Field 70 citing the supplier invoice number, contract reference and customs HS code 9503.00.10. Second, an irrevocable sight Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Bermuda concentration bank and confirmed by a top-tier UK or US confirming bank for purchase orders exceeding USD 1.5 million or where an Asian contract manufacturer requires a bank undertaking. For multi-currency liquidity, surplus GBP balances are auto-swept into a USD transit account via a standing FX agreement executed by the Bermuda treasury desk, thereby reducing ticket costs and settlement risk. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda statutory TP documentation threshold exists for Exempted Companies outside the CIT Act 2023. In-scope MNE groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more (approximately BD$1.06 billion) must observe OECD arm's length principles and produce Master File, Local File and CbCR data to the Bermuda Tax Administration Agency (BTAA) on demand; no de-minimis exemption applies once in scope.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2018 (as amended) require every Exempted Company engaged in a 'relevant activity' - banking, insurance, fund management, financing & leasing, headquarters, shipping, distribution & service centre, IP business, or pure holding entity - to maintain substantial economic presence in Bermuda. The designer blind-box toy manufacturing, rotocasting injection, and airbrush finishing operation is normally a non-relevant, non-resident activity; the company may declare 'non-resident' and file accordingly. If the company is the registered holder or licensor of the toy IP and derives territorial or notional income from that IP, it constitutes an IP business requiring enhanced substance: core income-generating activities in Bermuda, adequate local premises, full-time qualified employees, and local expenditure proportionate to the IP income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Perjury

Statutory sanction: Under Bermuda's CIT Act 2023, knowingly providing false information is an offence punishable on summary conviction by a fine of up to BD$250,000 or imprisonment up to 2 years, and on indictment by a fine of up to BD$500,000 or imprisonment up to 5 years. UK exposure: the Criminal Finances Act 2017 creates a corporate offence of failure to prevent the facilitation of UK tax evasion, with an unlimited fine; UK HMRC may assess UK corporation tax on a Bermuda company managed and controlled in the UK at 25% plus interest and penalties of up to 100% of the tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns without exercising reasonable diligence are personally liable: civil restitution of diverted profits, disqualification as director for up to 15 years, confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, asset-freezing orders, travel bans with passport surrender, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for fraudulent evasion of UK income tax (Taxes Management Act 1970, s.106A).
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations 2018: the Registrar may impose a penalty of up to BD$250,000 for the initial default, BD$10,000 per day for continued default, and may default the company to the Competent Authority for automatic exchange of information with UK HMRC. HMRC may then re-characterise the company as UK tax resident, open a UK permanent establishment assessment, and levy UK corporation tax, interest, and penalties. The Registrar may also strike the company off under s.168 of the Exempted Companies Act 1981.

Director personal liability: Each director is personally liable for fines up to BD$50,000 under ESR; UK Finance Act 2024 Personal Liability Notices (PLNs) can be issued to directors and shadow directors of offshore entities for the company's unpaid UK tax where an avoidance arrangement fails; travel bans and freezing orders apply under UK Finance Act 2019, s.93 as a protective measure.
AML / CFT and Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act 1997: failure to disclose suspicious activity is punishable by imprisonment of up to 5 years and an unlimited fine on conviction; the Beneficial Ownership Act 2017 imposes a fine of up to BD$25,000 and imprisonment of up to 8 years for non-provision of beneficial ownership information; the Bermuda Monetary Authority may also impose administrative fines and suspend or revoke the entity's registration or banking access.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to maintain the beneficial ownership register face personal fines and imprisonment of up to 2 years; civil asset forfeiture and confiscation orders may reach personal assets where proceeds have been diverted; travel bans and bail conditions restricting exit from Bermuda or the UK are available in the court's inherent jurisdiction; sanctions violations additionally trigger OFSI/UN penalties and custodial sentences.
Failure to Register and File under the CIT Act 2023

Statutory sanction: CIT Act 2023: failure to register attracts a strict liability fine of BD$25,000 plus BD$10,000 per month of non-compliance; failure to file Form CIT-1 attracts BD$25,000 plus BD$10,000 per month; late payment of top-up tax accrues interest at the statutory rate and a penalty up to 100% of the unpaid top-up tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly and wilfully cause the company to omit registration or filing commit an offence: on summary conviction, a fine of BD$100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 1 year; on indictment, a fine of BD$500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; the court may pierce the corporate veil and order personal liability for the company's unpaid top-up tax where profits were diverted into personal accounts, alongside civil restitution and travel bans.
