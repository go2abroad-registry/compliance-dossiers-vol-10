Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 (SAS), L.210-1 to L.210-10 and R.210-1 et seq., as amended; Decree No. 2023-873 implementing the electronic Guichet Unique.

Formation / constitutional filings

Digital formation dossier of a Société par Actions Simplifiée comprising the signed statuts (articles of association), declaration of incorporation, capital deposit certificate, legal publication certificate and beneficial owner declaration filed through the Guichet Unique.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 21 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

SAS has no legal minimum share capital; the capital is freely set in the articles. The entirety of the share capital must be subscribed. At least 50% of cash par value must be paid up at formation (balance within five years); in-kind contributions must be fully paid up upon filing. The paid-up funds must be deposited with a bank, notary or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and evidenced by a capital deposit certificate.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office must comply with French workplace safety regulations (Code du travail), including on-site access, installation, fire-safety provisions, electrical inspections, and ergonomic conditions; a simple declaration of occupancy and a certificate of compliance from the fire-safety inspector or the landlord is required for office premises. For service-only offices, no specific civil-defence permit is required.

Paid-in capital rule: SAS has no legal minimum share capital; the capital is freely set in the articles. The entirety of the share capital must be subscribed. At least 50% of cash par value must be paid up at formation (balance within five years); in-kind contributions must be fully paid up upon filing. The paid-up funds must be deposited with a bank, notary or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and evidenced by a capital deposit certificate.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty. Digital portal: impots.gouv.fr for tax filing; formal entity and VAT registration via the official single window at guichet-entreprises.fr (operated by the Réseau des Chambres de Commerce et d'Industrie).

E-government filing portal

The non-resident shareholder establishes a French SAS by filing the incorporation dossier through guichet-entreprises.fr. Upon successful registration, the company automatically receives a SIREN number (9-digit INSEE identifier) and a SIRET establishment number. The French tax authority (DGFIP) then automatically assigns the intra-Community VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire, format 'FR' + 2-digit control key + 11-digit SIREN). The VAT number can be verified and extracted via the French VAT number portal or the EU VIES system. No separate application form is required for a standard SAS, but for a non-resident-managed SAS the agent (président) must submit identity, registered office proof, and a declaration of beneficial ownership to the Companies Registry (RCS) as part of the dossier. If a French establishment (fixed base) is opened, a separate SIRET for that establishment is generated automatically; for a purely non-resident-managed SAS with no French fixed establishment, the single SIREN and VAT number suffice for tax compliance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SAS is treated as a French tax-resident corporation subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) on its worldwide profits. There is no pass-through or transparent election available under French domestic law; a French SAS cannot be treated as a partnership for French tax purposes. The standard IS headline rate is 25% (20% for small-business reduced rate is not applicable to the SAS default regime unless the entity meets the statutory SME thresholds, but the default classification remains Entity_Level_Taxation). The tax domicile of the SAS is in France (place of effective management is presumed to be the registered office unless proven otherwise). Territoriality: under domestic law, the SAS is taxed on French-source income and foreign income is potentially taxed subject to treaty relief; however, as a French incorporated entity it is fully resident and taxable on worldwide profits under the global taxation principle, with foreign tax credit relief under the relevant tax treaties. The reduced 15% IS rate for qualifying SMEs (up to €42,000 profit) is available only for companies satisfying independent SME criteria and is not part of the default classification; it is an elective/qualifying regime. — The non-resident shareholder establishes a French SAS by filing the incorporation dossier through guichet-entreprises.fr. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SAS is treated as a French tax-resident corporation subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) on its worldwide profits. There is no pass-through or transparent election available under French domestic law; a French SAS cannot be treated as a partnership for French tax purposes. The standard IS headline rate is 25% (20% for small-business reduced rate is not applicable to the SAS default regime unless the entity meets the statutory SME thresholds, but the default classification remains Entity_Level_Taxation). The tax domicile of the SAS is in France (place of effective management is presumed to be the registered office unless proven otherwise). Territoriality: under domestic law, the SAS is taxed on French-source income and foreign income is potentially taxed subject to treaty relief; however, as a French incorporated entity it is fully resident and taxable on worldwide profits under the global taxation principle, with foreign tax credit relief under the relevant tax treaties. The reduced 15% IS rate for qualifying SMEs (up to €42,000 profit) is available only for companies satisfying independent SME criteria and is not part of the default classification; it is an elective/qualifying regime.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists in France. The SAS cannot elect pass-through treatment. Available statutory elections for a French SAS include: (i) option for the tax-integrated regime (régime d'intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A of the French General Tax Code (CGI), allowing a parent SAS with at least 95% ownership to consolidate French subsidiaries' results, effective for a 5-year renewable term; (ii) election for VAT liability on rental real estate (option for VAT on let bare premises) under Article 260 CGI; (iii) option for the simplified VAT settlement scheme (régime simplifié) where the annual turnover threshold is met, electing semi-annual VAT payments instead of monthly; (iv) election to use the foreign tax credit (crédit d'impôt) or deduction method for foreign income under treaty provisions; (v) for hybrid mismatch purposes, France applies the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) rules under Articles 212 bis, 212 ter and 237 septies CGI, and any hybrid loan or branch instrument must be declared on the annual tax return (form 2758-SD for transfer pricing and hybrid documentation). A non-resident SAS parent may elect to be treated as a fiscal integration head if it has a French permanent establishment, which is subject to specific administrative approval; otherwise the SAS is taxed standalone.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.2%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 10,000 (annual EU-wide distance sales of goods or cross-border remote/digital B2C telecoms, broadcasting and electronic services threshold under Article 258 and 259 CGI and EU OSS regime). For the French SAS providing B2B services, there is NO registration threshold — the supplier must self-assess French VAT on the reverse charge basis when the B2B customer is French VAT-registered. For B2C digital/ASO/SEO localization services provided by the French SAS to non-VAT-registered customers located in the EU, the relevant threshold for applying the destination-country VAT via the OSS scheme remains EUR 10,000 (EU-wide cross-border supply); below this threshold, home-country (French) VAT applies to such EU B2C supplies. For domestic French B2C supplies, there is no registration threshold once the SAS performs any taxable transaction in France. For non-resident suppliers of ASO/SEO services into France, the B2C threshold is EUR 0 (no registration minimum), and the non-resident supplier must register for French VAT or use the non-union OSS scheme for electronic services where the global EU B2C revenue is below EUR 10,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the French SAS providing ASO/SEO localization services (ISIC 7310) to foreign customers, the applicable tax treatment is NOT an 'export zero-rating' but the extraterritoriality rule for B2B services under Article 259-1 CGI and Article 43 of the EU VAT Regulation 282/2011. Where the recipient is a taxable person (i.e. a VAT-registered business) established outside the EU or in another EU state, the supply of services is deemed to take place at the customer's place of establishment; consequently, the French SAS must NOT charge French VAT on the invoice, and the supply falls outside the scope of French VAT. This B2B rule has no formal threshold, and there is no requirement to obtain prior approval. For services where the foreign customer is a private individual / non-business consumer (B2C), the services are deemed supplied in France (because the supplier is established in France) and are therefore subject to French VAT at 20% unless the service is physically performed outside the EU (e.g. on-site SEO work in the USA may qualify for extraterritoriality under Article 259-2 CGI only if the service relates to immovable property located abroad or is physically performed abroad and enjoyed outside the EU). For goods exports (if the SAS ships promotional merchandise or physical localization kits), zero-rating applies under the legal export exemption of Article 262-1 CGI, which requires the goods to leave the EU within 90 days of supply. The B2B foreign customer VAT number must be validated via the VIES database for EU counterparties; for non-EU customers, a declaration of business status (attestation) is required. The service contract must have clear economic substance: no sham provision, no shell-company recipient, and the service must be genuinely consumed/benefited from outside France.

  • For services (B2B): signed service contract specifying the customer's VAT identification number (VIES-validated for EU clients), the nature and scope of ASO/SEO localization deliverables, the place of performance and jurisdiction clauses; supplier invoice with the mention 'Exonération de TVA — Article 259-1 CGI — Client assujetti établi hors France' (or reverse-charge mention for EU B2B clients: 'TVA due par le preneur, article 283-1 CGI'); bank transfer or SWIFT payment record from the foreign customer's account evidencing actual cross-border remuneration; beneficial-ownership declaration of the foreign customer for non-EU entities (attestation from the customer); any supporting delivery evidence such as geo-IP server logs, ASO store listing screenshots showing the target country, and email/communication records from the foreign customer.
  • For goods exports (if merchandise is physically shipped out of the EU): customs export declaration (EX-Document / CAU — Carte Administrative Unique) with the export date and customs office stamp; Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill (AWB); proof of departure from the EU within 90 days; commercial invoice containing the export exemption mention under Article 262-1 CGI; inward remittance certificate (Certificat de rapatriement) from the bank acknowledging the foreign-sourced payment.
  • For B2C digital services supplied to non-EU private consumers: optional extraterritoriality declaration and the customer's non-EU address/IP-address evidence (geo-location logs of the App Store / Google Play user, national identity card or passport copy, IP-country log from the ASO campaign dashboard).

France has enacted a phased real-time e-invoicing mandate for all VAT-taxable persons established in France. The legal framework is set in Articles 1929 and 289 bis CGI and the 2024-2039 Finance Act implementation schedule. Developer timeline: (i) from 1 September 2026, all taxable persons established in France must be able to receive all invoices electronically in a structured format via the Public Invoicing Portal (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF) using an approved Dematerialisation Partner Platform (PDP) or a licensed operator; (ii) from 1 September 2026, large enterprises must issue e-invoices; (iii) from 1 September 2027, SMEs and micro-enterprises must issue e-invoices. Mandatory technical standards: structured electronic invoice format (Factur-X, UBL, CII) or the hybrid mixed format; transmission via the PPF with a mandatory unique identifier (Numéro d'identification de facturation) for each invoice; audit file (Fichier des Écritures Comptables, FEC) submission to DGFIP on request. Real-time transaction clearance (e-invoice clearances) is executed by the PPF/PDP platforms: each invoice must be transmitted with metadata (supplier VAT number, customer VAT number, transaction type, VAT amount, payment due date, and a cryptographic signature where required). No physical tax stamp or digital stamping is required; instead, the PPF performs automated cross-validation against the VAT return and EC Sales List. Non-compliance penalties: 5% per invoice of the total invoice amount (minimum EUR 15, maximum EUR 375 per invoice) plus a separate EUR 15 per transaction for data transmission failures; anti-fraud fine of up to EUR 1,500 for missing e-invoice data (EUR 7,500 for aggravating circumvention).

The French tax administration (Direction Nationale d'Enquêtes Fiscales, DNE) and the National Customs and Indirect Taxation Office (Direction Nationale du Renseignement et des Enquêtes Douanières, DNRED) conduct targeted audits on agencies claiming B2B extraterritoriality for digital services. On audit, the taxpayer bears the burden of proving the customer's business status, the real economic substance of the cross-border supply, and the absence of a French taxable nexus. Absent adequate documentation, the supply is recharacterised as taxable in France, giving rise to: (i) retrospective clawback of the VAT not declared, plus late-payment interest (intérêts de retard) at 0.20% per month from the original due date; (ii) a 40% additional penalty for 'absence de bonne foi' (good-faith deficiency) under Article 1728 CGI, or 80% for 'manœuvres frauduleuses' or recharacterisation as an artificial arrangement under Article 1729-1 CGI; (iii) a further 5% penalty for failing to file the recapitulative statement (EC Sales List) or insufficient data under Article 1788 CGI; (iv) where hybrid mismatch or sham arrangements are detected (e.g. a shell company intermediary taking the income to avoid French VAT), the anti-abuse provision of Article 2325 CGI allows fiscal recharacterisation and joint and several liability for the intermediary; (v) criminal penalties for VAT fraud (Article 1741 CGI) can reach EUR 750,000 fines and 7 years' imprisonment where the under-declared VAT exceeds the criminal threshold and involves false documentation. Additionally, for goods exports, any mismatch between the customs declaration and the physical shipment or a false inward remittance certificate automatically triggers a full clawback of the zero-rated VAT and the application of the export forfeiture fine (amende forfaitaire) of up to 10% of the transaction value under Article 413 bis of the French Customs Code.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro has full de jure capital and current-account convertibility in France. No government exchange-control permit is required for cross-border payments, profit repatriation, dividend distributions, intercompany loans, or liquidation distributions to non-resident shareholders, save for transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions or designated persons under EU/UN/OFAC lists. An SAS may freely pay dividends after annual-account approval; the standard dividend withholding tax is 25% for corporate shareholders (12.8% income tax plus 17.2% social levies for natural persons), reducible under tax treaties or eliminated at 0% for an eligible EU corporate shareholder holding at least 10% of the capital for two years (or a 24-month holding commitment) under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU). The true residual constraints are tax anti-avoidance rules—interest and royalty payments must demonstrate economic substance to avoid recharacterization under Article 212 bis of the French General Tax Code, the EU ATAD interest-limitation rule (Article 212 quinquies), and DAC-6 cross-border arrangement disclosure—not currency convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France applies no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for service export receipts in euros; the CMF imposes only declarative reporting, not repatriation or conversion. Any enterprise that executes cross-border settlements exceeding €750,000 in aggregate per calendar month must file a Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration (OPR e-declaration). For the ASO/SEO localization agency, export proceeds are services invoiced to non-resident clients classified under BOP service purpose codes 204 (advertising and market research) or 211 (telecommunications, computer, and information services). Failure to file triggers penalties of between €1,500 and €3,000 per omission plus interest under CMF R. 165-1 et seq. Proceeds may remain in the EUR treasury account, be converted at market rates, or be applied to operational disbursements without prior approval.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) as the national monetary authority, exercising exchange-control reporting and balance-of-payments oversight under the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF) in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) and the European Systemic Risk Board. The Direction Générale du Trésor at the French Ministry of Economy administers financial sanctions policy, while the BdF Balance-of-Payments Directorate receives mandatory statistical declarations under EU Regulation 1849/2005, EU Regulation 2015/847 on transfers of funds, and the free-movement-of-capital framework of Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

HIGH for the target payment rails. Wise Europe SA (licensed as an Electronic Money Institution by the National Bank of Belgium, passported into France under PSD2) and Payoneer Financial Services Ireland Ltd (EMI licensed by the Central Bank of Ireland, passported into France) may lawfully open remote corporate accounts and execute cross-border payments on behalf of the SAS. Under CMF Articles L. 525-1 to L. 525-3, EMIs may not take deposits or grant commercial credit; client funds must be safeguarded (segregated) with a credit institution. Offshore (non-EU) EMIs without an EU-licensed establishment—such as a UK-only EMI after Brexit—have no direct French passport and may not hold the SAS's treasury balances; the SAS must therefore channel the bulk of its liquidity through an EU-passported EMI or a tier-1 French bank. The SAS should maintain a tier-1 bank account as the bank-relationship anchor for substance, credit references, and potential financing, while using Wise_B2B and Payoneer as cost-effective receiving and vendor-disbursement rails for non-resident clients and independent contractors.

Payoneer Fit: High

HIGH for the target payment rails. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 of consolidated annual turnover or total balance-sheet assets. A French entity or group exceeding this threshold must maintain Master File and Local File documentation. A French ultimate parent of an MNEs group with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000 must file a country-by-country report (form 2258-SD). Below these thresholds, mandatory documentation is not triggered, but the taxpayer must still demonstrate arm's-length pricing upon audit.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulation filing regime comparable to low-tax jurisdictions. Nevertheless, economic substance is tested through the general anti-abuse doctrine (abus de droit), controlled-foreign-company rules, anti-treaty-shopping provisions, and the OECD BEPS tests. A French SAS acting as an ASO/SEO localization agency must have real staff, premises, contracts, decision-making and profit-allocating functions. A shell structure with no genuine operational substance in France risks reclassification, loss of treaty benefits, and 80% fraud penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under article 1741 CGI; aggravated fraud involving concealment of tax base may raise the fine to EUR 3,000,000 and imprisonment to 7 years; civil tax adjustment carries a 40% bad-faith penalty plus 0.20% monthly late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: The president and director-generals may be criminally prosecuted personally. Through article L.651-2 of the French Commercial Code, directors may be ordered to cover corporate tax debts for mismanagement. Travel bans, asset freezing, and confiscation of personal assets may be imposed.
Non-Filing or Materially False Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Automatic 10% late-filing increase; 40% penalty for deliberate inaccuracy; 80% penalty for fraudulent manoeuvre; late-payment interest accrues at 0.20% per month; failure to comply with repeated notices may trigger enforced tax collection and additional fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or knowingly approve false declarations may be personally liable for penalties and interest, subject to criminal sanctions under articles 1742 and 1743 CGI, including imprisonment and fines; civil liability may cover the entire tax shortfall.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: DGFiP may re-determine French taxable income at arm's-length level and impose additional corporate income tax plus 0.20% monthly interest; deliberate mispricing attracts a 40% penalty; artificial avoidance arrangements attract an 80% penalty under article 1729 CG.

Director personal liability: Directors who initiate or approve fictitious pricing arrangements may be held personally liable under civil mismanagement rules; where the arrangement is fraudulent, criminal tax evasion charges and disqualification from management may follow.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: The arrangement may be disregarded as an abuse of law under article L.64 of the French Tax Procedures Book; French tax is re-established against the true beneficial owner; the 80% abuse penalty is applied in addition to interest; treaty protections may be revoked.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors and beneficial owners may be jointly and severally liable for corporate tax and penalties; courts may prohibit the directors from managing a commercial enterprise and impose travel restrictions in criminal abuse cases.
Beneficial Ownership / AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with RBE disclosure carries criminal penalties, including fines up to EUR 15,000 and imprisonment of up to 6 months; separate anti-money-laundering breaches may result in account freezing, financial penalties by the ACPR, and transaction blockage by PSP providers.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally exposed to criminal fines and imprisonment; concealment of substantial beneficial ownership may lead to piercing of the corporate veil and personal seizure of bank accounts and assets where the company is used as a laundering vehicle.
