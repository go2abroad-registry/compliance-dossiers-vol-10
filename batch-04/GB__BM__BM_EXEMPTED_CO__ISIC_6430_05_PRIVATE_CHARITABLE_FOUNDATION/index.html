Available election forms

Bermuda does not maintain a domestic check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime because it levies no income tax. Relevant elections: (1) exempted-company status election under section 114 Companies Act 1981, conferring statutory tax protection under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (extended to 31 March 2035); (2) charitable registration election under the Charities Act 2014, mandatory for charities above the statutory income threshold; and (3) US federal Form 8832 check-the-box classification elected by foreign owners for US tax transparency only, with no Bermuda tax consequence. No hybrid mismatch elections are available or required.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Bermuda has no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. The indirect tax regime comprises customs import duty (Customs Tariff Act 1978), payroll tax (Payroll Tax Act 1995), and stamp duty (Stamp Duties Act 1976). UK VAT (20%) does not extend to Bermuda.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. No turnover-based VAT or sales-tax registration threshold exists in Bermuda. Importers must hold customs registration and utilise ASYCUDA World before clearing goods; employers must register for payroll tax effective from the first payment of Bermuda-sourced remuneration; charities must register with the Registrar of Charities once gross annual income exceeds the statutory threshold under the Charities Act 2014.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. No VAT is imposed in Bermuda; therefore no export zero-rating claim or refund mechanism exists. Outward shipments are not subject to Bermuda indirect tax. Goods imported into Bermuda and subsequently re-exported or transshipped may be relieved from customs duty under the re-export and transshipment procedures of the Customs Act 1978.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar is pegged at par to the U.S. dollar under the Currency Act 1972 (1 BMD = 1 USD), and USD circulates freely as de facto legal tender. The Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973 delegate exchange-control administration to the BMA. A Bermuda Exempted Company operating in a Tax-Exempt-Zone capacity is designated non-resident for exchange-control purposes, which confers full current-account and capital-account convertibility: endowment capital may enter, be held in USD/EUR/GBP multi-currency accounts, and be remitted abroad as grants without BMA conversion consent or surrender obligations. Resident domestic-company restrictions (current-account approval above a notional BMD 5,000 threshold and capital-account approval through an authorized dealer) do not apply to non-resident exempted companies. Profit and grant remittance is structurally unrestricted: no withholding tax, no dividend tax, and no exchange-control cash-surrender applies by virtue of the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 guarantee (tax protection to 31 March 2035). The only practical hurdles are bank-side compliance documentation, namely a deed of endowment, a grant-policy file, and annual audited financial statements evidencing the non-trading charitable status of the foundation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the ordinary endowment-management model, no export or trade receipts arise; however, where the foundation earns foreign-currency income from research licensing, patent monetisation, or incidental commercial activity, the statutory regime applicable to a Bermuda-resident entity would require all foreign-currency export proceeds to be offered to an authorized dealer within 30 days of receipt, with a mandatory 100% conversion ratio into BMD, subject only to a BMA general or specific retention permit. As a non-resident-designated Exempted Company, the foundation falls outside the resident surrender net and may retain 100% of such receipts in a USD non-resident account with no BMA statutory timeline. To protect against inadvertent reclassification as resident, the foundation must: (i) avoid carrying on business with a Bermudian-source character; (ii) maintain the registered office solely as a statutory address; and (iii) file the annual exchange-control declaration through its licensed corporate service provider confirming the non-resident designation.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as agent of the Bermuda Minister of Finance under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Currency Act 1972

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Low as a sole treasury rail; Medium solely as a satellite disbursement account. Legal constraints: Bermuda does not license standalone e-money institutions, and the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1991, read with the BMA AML/ATF Code of Practice and the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008, requires any entity holding client funds on deposit to be a licensed bank or deposit company. A UK/EEA-authorised fintech EMI (e.g., Wise Business or Revolut Business) may open a multi-currency account for the Exempted Company, but it cannot lawfully hold the Bermuda endowment's fiduciary capital as substantive custodian, and most EMIs categorise charitable foundations as high-risk associations requiring full charitable-registration evidence, grantee-level sanctions screening, and a written purpose declaration for every outbound grant. The blueprint therefore confines any EMI to auxiliary low-value grant payments below USD 25,000 per transaction, while capital custody and statutory treasury rails remain exclusively at Butterfield and HSBC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound rail is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the UK donor, trustee, or group treasury to the foundation's HSBC Bank Bermuda USD core account (SWIFT: HSBCBMHM) or Butterfield operational account (SWIFT: BNTBBMHM), Hamilton branch, with the beneficiary field exactly matching the registered exempted-company name. Remittance information (field 70) must state 'ENDOWMENT CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - NON-RESIDENT EXEMPTED CO - CHARITABLE FOUNDATION' to preclude trade or money-service misclassification. The UK remitting bank will apply the Bank of England and ONS FDI survey classification (F.51 shares and other equity or D.8 other capital transfers) and may require a payment-purpose declaration for amounts above GBP 10,000 equivalent; the Bermudian receiving bank applies the internal code 'NRT' (non-resident transfer). The supporting chain-of-funds packet comprises the executed deed of endowment, foundation charter, certificate of incorporation, tax-exemption certificate, and a director source-of-wealth questionnaire. No BMA filing, Form A/TDC, or conversion approval is triggered because the beneficiary is a non-resident entity.Outbound grant flows settle over the SWIFT MT103 rail from Butterfield or HSBC in USD, or through a EUR/GBP sub-account, with field 70 reading 'GRANT DISBURSEMENT - [grantee name] - [programme year] - [grant agreement reference]'. Otherwise, MT103 plus MT199 evidence-copy packages are the market-standard, lowest-cost clearing protocol. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Bermuda levies no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, no VAT, and no withholding tax on profits, dividends, interest, or royalties.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda law imposes no withholding tax on dividends, distributions, or other payments made by an exempted company to non-resident shareholders, grant beneficiaries, or founders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable. Bermuda maintains no general transfer pricing documentation regime and prescribes no local file or master file thresholds in BMD. For multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more, Bermuda implements Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) under its Taxation (Country-by-Country Reporting) Regulations; constituent entities must notify the Registrar of the group's reporting entity. Separate and independent of transfer pricing, the Economic Substance Regulations 2018 require a Bermuda entity conducting a relevant activity to demonstrate adequate substance proportionate to its core income-generating activity, irrespective of turnover; the practical threshold is the existence of a registered office, local board minutes, accounting records retained in Bermuda, and (where non-holding activity applies) local employees and premises.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018 and accompanying Regulations apply to any 'relevant entity' carrying a 'relevant activity'. A private charitable foundation holding endowment assets generally falls inside the 'holding entity' relevant activity if its primary function is the acquisition and holding of equity interests; it must satisfy a reduced substance test: (i) compliance with the Companies Act 1981, (ii) a registered office in Bermuda, and (iii) board minutes and accounting records kept in Bermuda. The 'fund management' relevant activity applies only to licensed collective investment vehicles managing third-party investor capital; a charitable grant-making endowment not offering fund management services to the public is outside that head. The foundation should nonetheless file the annual ESR declaration and, if it engages in grant management or investment administration in Bermuda, maintain local personnel and premises proportionate to its activities to avoid reclassification as a non-compliant shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filings

Statutory sanction: Companies Act 1981 and Economic Substance Act 2018 impose a fine of up to BMD 10,000 per false statement; the registrar may strike off the company, revoke its exempted status, and the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act guarantee ceases to apply to tainted profits; Bermuda Revenue may impose full tax replication plus interest at 2% monthly.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause a false filing face personal criminal fines, imprisonment of up to two years, disqualification from office, and personal liability for the resulting tax, penalties, and the foundation's debts where fraudulent trading is established under section 84 of the Companies Act.
Shell Company / Inadequate Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Act 2018: fines up to BMD 10,000 per default, aggregate financial penalties of up to BMD 250,000 for serious non-compliance, and mandatory dissolution or strike-off; the entity may also be blacklisted in the EU and OECD, triggering cross-border anti-abuse measures.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for ESR penalties if they knowingly allow the entity to be used as a shell; courts may impose personal fines, freezing orders over personal assets, travel bans under the Foreign Tax Compliance frameworks, and imprisonment for contempt where the entity continues to operate in defiance of a compliance notice.
Charitable Misuse and Grant Fraud

Statutory sanction: Charities Act 2014 and the Charities (Registration) Regulations empower the Bermuda Regulator of Charities to suspend or remove trustees, recover misapplied property, impose fine of up to BMD 25,000, and issue restriction orders; wilful abuse may be prosecuted as fraud or theft under the Bermudian Criminal Code, carrying imprisonment of up to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors and trustees are jointly and severally liable for restitution of misdirected endowment assets; personal liability extends to unpaid grants and creditors' claims where the charitable veil is pierced; deliberate diversion of charitable funds results in disgorgement, disqualification from serving on charities, and imprisonment.
AML/CFT and Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Monetary Authority may impose civil penalties of up to BMD 1,000,000 per contravention; the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 criminalises money laundering with fines and imprisonment of up to 14 years; sanctions violations attract asset freezing, confiscation orders, and international enforcement action.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, imprisonment, confiscation of personal assets, and travel bans where they knowingly or negligently permit the charitable vehicle to be used for laundering terrorist finance or sanctions evasion; the BMA and Director of Public Prosecutions may impose director debarment and personal civil liability for the full value of the funds involved.
