Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Ministry of Finance (tax policy) and Office of the Tax Commissioner (administration of the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 and payroll tax); Registrar of Companies (maintenance of exempted company register and annual government fees). Official portals: www.gov.bm (Ministry of Finance / Tax Commissioner) and www.roc.gov.bm (Registrar of Companies).

E-government filing portal

For a Bermuda Exempted Company, no general income-tax TIN or VAT ID is required because Bermuda imposes no corporate income tax, VAT, or GST on non-resident-owned exempted companies. Standard procedure: (1) incorporate under the Companies Act 1981 via the Registrar of Companies using an approved registered agent; (2) obtain Certificate of Incorporation, which carries the Bermuda Company Number (BN) used as the entity identifier for all filings; (3) file a Declaration of Compliance and, if applicable, an Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 application to obtain a tax-exemption undertaking; (4) if the entity employs Bermuda-resident staff, register with the Payroll Tax Office and obtain an employer payroll account number; (5) if the entity is a constituent entity of a multinational group with global revenue at or above €750 million, register for Corporate Income Tax (CIT) under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 (effective fiscal years beginning 1 January 2025) with the Office of the Tax Commissioner; a CIT registration number will be issued. No VAT registration is possible or necessary because no VAT regime exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone. Bermuda Exempted Companies are generally non-resident for Bermudian tax purposes and enjoy statutory tax exemption under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (typically until 2035, with ministerial undertaking extension possible to 2055, subject to current policy). No corporate income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, or VAT applies to a pure offshore exempted company earning foreign-source income and engaged in permitted business outside Bermuda. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. Bermuda Exempted Companies are generally non-resident for Bermudian tax purposes and enjoy statutory tax exemption under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (typically until 2035, with ministerial undertaking extension possible to 2055, subject to current policy). No corporate income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, or VAT applies to a pure offshore exempted company earning foreign-source income and engaged in permitted business outside Bermuda. Residual Bermuda taxes that may apply only if local nexus arises: employer payroll tax (rates 5%–20% on Bermuda payroll), employee payroll tax, social security contributions, customs import duties on physical goods entering Bermuda, stamp duty on Bermuda-situated assets or instruments, and annual Government Fee payable to the Registrar of Companies (based on authorised share capital). The new 15% CIT applies exclusively to in-scope multinational groups exceeding the €750 million revenue threshold; a small resin statue manufacturing entity owned outside Bermuda and selling directly to export markets will not ordinarily come within scope unless part of such a group.

Available election forms

Bermuda does not operate a check-the-box entity classification election and has no local law equivalent of U.S. Check-the-Box or hybrid mismatch elections. The Exempted Company structure itself operates as a de facto tax-transparent/flow-through vehicle only in the sense that no Bermudian entity-level tax is imposed; foreign owners determine home-jurisdiction treatment. Elections available under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 (for in-scope MNE groups) include: (i) election for the standard 15% CIT rate on constituent entities, (ii) choice to be subject to Qualifying Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (QDMTT), (iii) consolidated group filing/designation of a reporting entity, and (iv) transitional safe-harbour and substance-based income exclusion elections consistent with Pillar Two. For pure exempted companies outside CIT scope, no tax elections are required; the principal protective filing is the annual Economic Substance Declaration or exemption certificate, not a hybrid-mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT and no general sales/use tax. Bermuda operates a customs import duty regime under the Customs Tariff Act 2008 and Customs Act 2014, supplemented by payroll tax (on Bermuda employment) and stamp duty (limited instruments). For purposes of this dossier, the applicable indirect tax system is classified as: N/A — no VAT/GST/SUT in Bermuda.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Bermuda and no VAT registration exists. An entity importing consigned goods into Bermuda for local customs clearance must register with the Bermuda Customs Service (a Customs Number/Commercial Importer Code is required for each importer of record) and, if applicable, appoint a licensed customs broker. No economic turnover threshold governs indirect tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda has no VAT, therefore no VAT zero-rating or export refund mechanism exists. Exports from Bermuda are generally outside the scope of Bermuda customs duty; duty is imposed at importation into Bermuda, not on exportation. If goods are imported into Bermuda and subsequently re-exported without being consumed locally, relief may be available under temporary importation, warehousing, or duty drawback provisions of the Customs Act 2014/Customs Tariff Act 2008. The immediate business context (UK/EU consumer sales) requires the seller to assess the customer’s import jurisdiction: exports of resin statue garage kits into the United Kingdom may be subject to UK import VAT (currently 20% standard rate) collected by UK HMRC at entry; if the Bermuda company sells on an anti-DDP (unpaid import duty) basis, the UK/EU buyer bears import VAT and duty. For EU distance sales of such goods supplied into the UK, a Bermuda supplier has no UK VAT registration obligation unless it is treated as an “overseas person” making taxable supplies in the UK (e.g., B2B domestic supply or storage in the UK under a UK fulfilment arrangement), in which case UK VAT registration and accounting may be required.

  • Bermuda Customs Export Declaration (electronic entry filed via Bermuda Customs Electronic System, with the exporter name, description of goods, HS code, value, and import destination)
  • Commercial Invoice (detailed product description, unit values, HS classification, currency, Incoterm, and consignee information)
  • Packing List (carton contents, weights, dimensions, and quantity reconciliation)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (evidence of physical export from Bermuda to the destination market)
  • Certificate of Origin (where requested by the importing jurisdiction, e.g., for UK/EU preferential or origin verification purposes)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/transaction records evidencing receipt of export proceeds into the Bermuda bank account

No real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API integration, or digital stamping mandate applies in Bermuda. Bermuda Customs Service operates the Bermuda Customs Electronic System (BCS) as the official electronic declaration and clearance portal for import/export documentation. Commercial invoices and export declarations must be filed electronically through BCS or via an authorised customs broker. There is no continuous transaction control (CTC) reporting to any tax authority, no national e-invoicing platform, and no fiscal device requirement.

Bermuda has no VAT zero-rating, so no VAT clawback penalty regime exists. However, customs violations under the Customs Act 2014 are subject to strict penalties: false or fraudulent export declarations, misdeclaration of goods, or evasion of customs duty can result in monetary fines (up to three times the value of the goods), forfeiture and seizure of goods and conveyance, and, in serious cases, imprisonment. Under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024, any in-scope MNE that fraudulently underreports CIT or supports an export-related scheme can face penalties of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, late-payment interest, and prosecution. For the underlying export sales, the primary clawback risk arises in the destination jurisdiction: if a Bermuda exporter incorrectly zero-rates UK supplies that are in fact domestic UK supplies, HMRC may assess underdeclared UK VAT with penalties up to 100% of the tax (culpable conduct), plus interest, and may pursue director/legal-representative liability for the overseas entity. The claimed 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation period is not a statutory Bermuda deadline (Bermuda has no exchange controls); it reflects standard banking documentation requirements for export proceeds and should be met to preserve clean audit evidence of genuine cross-border trade.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar and freely interchangeable at authorized Bermuda banks. A Bermuda Exempted Company with non-resident status enjoys unrestricted convertibility for both current account transactions (trade settlement, royalties, service fees, e-commerce flows) and capital account transactions (equity injection, intercompany loans, profit repatriation, liquidation proceeds). No prior BMA approval is triggered where the transaction is denominated in USD or another foreign currency and involves no Bermuda-resident counterparty. Profit remittances are not subject to withholding tax or exchange-control hurdles; the primary compliance duty is the annual economic substance declaration and maintenance of an adequate accounting and governance trail.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement applies to a non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company. Export receivables may be retained in USD or any convertible foreign currency without mandatory conversion into BMD, and there is no regulatory timeline for conversion or repatriation. The exemption applies provided the company is not carrying on local business in Bermuda and has received non-resident status from the BMA. A resident Bermuda business would be required to offer foreign currency receipts to an authorized dealer within a specified period, but that regime is inapplicable to the present exempted-structure manufacturing and e-commerce model.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) — acting as Bermuda's central bank, securities regulator, and exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Digital Asset Business Act 2018.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity injection, the shareholder transmits a SWIFT MT103 from its current bank to the Bermuda company's operational or escrow account held at Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda. The wire must include the beneficiary's BMD/USD account number, SWIFT/BIC, and the field-70 remittance information '//INVCAP// Share capital contribution per board resolution dated [date]'. The remitter must provide a board resolution, share subscription agreement, and source-of-funds statement to satisfy correspondent-bank screening. Because BMD/USD parity eliminates conversion cost, funds are credited directly in USD. For e-commerce inflows, Stripe/PayPal settle in USD to the same Bermuda account; each settlement carries the merchant reference, card-network authorization, and settlement report, providing a complete verification trail for AML and audit purposes.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for supplier payments, raw-material purchases, and logistics costs under USD-denominated commercial contracts. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH with legal constraints. UK FCA-regulated Electronic Money Institutions and global fintech treasury platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Currencycloud, Airwallex, Capabult) may accept a Bermuda Exempted Company, but only after demonstrating a genuine UK/EEA commercial nexus, such as a UK-based distribution agent or a confirmed e-commerce marketplace contract. The primary legal constraint is that EMIs cannot rely on Bermuda's Digital Asset Business Act licence for fiat payment accounts; the EMI must hold a valid European/UK payment institution licence. The structure is viable where Stripe and PayPal settlements are routed into a USD-denominated EMI or corporate bank account, followed by systematic treasury sweeps into the operational account at Butterfield or HSBC.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 0 - no local transfer-pricing documentation threshold is prescribed for a standard exempted company; there are no mandatory Local File or Master File requirements under Bermuda law. If the entity falls within the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 as an in-scope MNE constituent entity, controlled transactions must comply with the arm's length standard and supporting OECD TP documentation must be prepared and maintained.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda International Business (Economic Substance) Act 2018 applies to relevant activities such as banking, insurance, fund management, financing, leasing, headquarters, shipping, distribution and service centres, IP holding and holding entities. Manufacture and direct-to-consumer online sale of resin statues is generally not a listed relevant activity. However, if the company operates as a related-party distribution and service centre, it must demonstrate economic substance in Bermuda with local premises, local employees, board meetings in Bermuda and core income-generating activities performed locally. An annual ESR declaration is required even for entities with no relevant activity to confirm nil status; failure to file can trigger investigations and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

CIT Act 2023 Non-Compliance / Fraudulent Use of Nil Rate

Statutory sanction: BMD 250,000 fixed penalty for failure to file plus BMD 10,000 daily penalty for continued non-compliance; compound interest on unpaid CIT; assessment by the Tax Commissioner; potential strike-off from the Bermuda Register.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly cause the non-filing may be assessed personally for unpaid tax and penalties; Bermuda courts may pierce the corporate veil in cases of sham or abuse, giving rise to civil restitution, travel bans under international tax enforcement orders, and imprisonment for fraudulent evasion of Bermuda CIT.
Economic Substance / Shell Company Abuses

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the International Business (Economic Substance) Act 2018 of up to BMD 250,000 per breach, daily penalties for continued default, exchange of information to foreign revenue authorities, suspension of the company's tax-protection certificate, and in extreme cases deregistration and winding-up.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be made personally responsible for deficits caused by artificial substance arrangements; the corporate veil can be pierced where the exempted company is a mere facade, exposing directors to civil restitution, travel bans pending inquiry, and disqualification orders.
