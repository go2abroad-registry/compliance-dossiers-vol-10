Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for an Exempted Company; capital may be issued at par in any currency and denomination adopted by the Bye-laws. No escrow account is mandated. However, annual Government fees and share capital duty levies are assessed against authorised share capital, and immigration/quarantine file approvals may require a minimum issued capital of BMD 12,000 for resident director eligibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at a fixed 1:1 rate to the United States Dollar (USD). Bermuda maintains a formal exchange control regime, but a Bermuda Exempted Company that is designated as non-resident for exchange control purposes is generally free to conduct capital and current account transactions in foreign currency without individual prior authorization. Current account transactions, including payment for green coffee imports, trade-related fees, and operating expenses, are fully convertible for non-resident businesses. Profit remittance is unrestricted by the exchange control regime: dividends, returns of capital, and liquidation distributions may be paid to non-resident shareholders free of Bermuda withholding tax, provided the company has satisfied its obligations under the Companies Act 1981, the Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act 2018, and BMA monetary reporting requirements. The practical hurdles are primarily documentary rather than regulatory: banks require valid board resolutions, certified constitutional documents, and evidence of beneficial ownership before processing any capital or dividend remittance. There is no separate exchange control approval for an exempted company operating wholly outside Bermuda if it maintains the non-resident designation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no mandatory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds for a Bermuda Exempted Company. Under the Exchange Control Regulations, entities designated as non-resident are permitted to retain export receipts, trade receivables, and all foreign currency earnings in offshore or external accounts without converting those proceeds into BMD. There is no statutory timeline obligating the repatriation of export proceeds to Bermuda; the company may use foreign currency revenue directly for trade settlement, intercompany payments, or treasury operations. Resident companies, by contrast, are subject to exchange control oversight, but the exempted structure, particularly one engaged in international specialty green coffee distribution, is outside those surrender requirements. The absence of a surrender rule does not eliminate record-keeping obligations: the BMA and the appointed bank expect complete supporting documentation for each cross-border transaction, including contracts, invoices, bills of lading, and SWIFT payment references, to demonstrate the non-resident trade pattern.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) acting as the central banking and financial services regulator under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, with statutory powers over exchange control delegated under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and Exchange Control Regulations 1973.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low direct viability for Bermuda-regulated banking purposes. Offshore Digital EMI providers such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, or MultiPass are not licensed by the Bermuda Monetary Authority and cannot serve as the primary bank for a Bermuda Exempted Company under BMA supervision. They may be used as supplementary GBP- or EUR-denominated operating accounts routed through UK electronic money institutions, but such services present significant legal and compliance constraints: unreconciled third-party intermediary relationships, sanctions risk across coffee origin jurisdictions, and reluctance by Bermuda commercial banks to accept EMI-sourced funds as cleared corporate capital. A medium-to-low feasibility rating is assigned for any reliance on offshore digital EMIs as principal treasury rails. The recommended structure is a Bermuda-licensed bank as the primary USD/BMD settlement account, with a UK EMI used only for small administrative expenses if fully documented and disclosed to the primary bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Insufficient source-of-wealth documentation for the Bermuda Exempted Company's shareholder or ultimate beneficial owner injecting capital.

Root cause: Insufficient source-of-wealth documentation for the Bermuda Exempted Company's shareholder or ultimate beneficial owner injecting capital.

Defense evidence: Provide a documented source-of-wealth memorandum, three years of audited or certified financial statements for the parent entity, personal bank reference letters, UK HMRC tax filings, certified passport pages, proof of residential address, and the full corporate chain from the UK parent to the Bermuda exempted company, supported by a board resolution authorizing the capital injection.
Lack of commercial rationale for procurement and payment flows in the specialty green coffee trade, including mismatched invoices, payment dates, or shipping routes.

Root cause: Lack of commercial rationale for procurement and payment flows in the specialty green coffee trade, including mismatched invoices, payment dates, or shipping routes.

Defense evidence: Compile a complete trade file per shipment: signed green coffee purchase contract, pro forma commercial invoice, final invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, phytosanitary certificate, cupping or quality analysis report, shipment insurance certificate, and all correspondence with the supplier. Include the payment authorization matrix and, where relevant, the documentary credit terms to align each MT103 remittance with the underlying physical trade.
Sanctions, adverse media, or geopolitical risk flags associated with counterparties, clearing banks, or the coffee origin country.

Root cause: Sanctions, adverse media, or geopolitical risk flags associated with counterparties, clearing banks, or the coffee origin country.

Defense evidence: Prepare a compliance-grade adverse media check and sanctions screening report for all counterparties, beneficial owners, and intermediate banks; obtain a legal entity chart, certificate of incorporation, and director identity documentation for the supplier; provide a jurisdictional risk assessment showing the specific coffee origin is not subject to UK, UN, or US OFAC comprehensive sanctions; and include a written compliance opinion from the company's MLRO or a recognized AML consultancy.
Unexpected use of a UK-based electronic money institution, third-party payment processor, or cryptocurrency intermediary in the trade settlement chain.

Root cause: Unexpected use of a UK-based electronic money institution, third-party payment processor, or cryptocurrency intermediary in the trade settlement chain.

Defense evidence: Furnish a detailed payment-rail justification memorandum, a copy of the EMI's FCA permission or passporting certificate, the electronic agreement between the Bermuda company and the EMI, bank account statements in the EMI showing source of funds, and an assurance that the EMI's payment routes are not using unregulated cash transfer businesses. Demonstrate that no Bermuda exchange-control restriction is circumvented by the intermediary and that the primary settlement remains within a BMA licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital for the Bermuda Exempted Company should be routed by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the UK parent's corporate account to the Bermuda bank's USD or BMD account, using a beneficiary reference aligned with the share allotment resolution. The wire instruction should include purpose code NTR or equivalent for a capital contribution, with the narrative field stating 'Share Capital Contribution to Bermuda Exempted Company, Board Resolution dated [date]' or 'Intercompany Loan under Loan Agreement dated [date]' if the funding is structured as debt. Supporting verification at the receiving bank must include a recent board resolution, a copy of the registered certificate of incorporation and memorandum of association, evidence of the exempted company's non-resident exchange control designation, and, if the capital exceeds an internal threshold, an audited or certified source-of-funds report. All inbound funds should first be credited to a segregated capital escrow account established at the bank, with release to the operational account only after the bank's compliance team confirms the corporate registry filings and economic substance declarations are current.Outbound trade disbursements for specialty green coffee purchases should primarily clear through SWIFT MT103 for open-account or advance-payment transactions, and through a Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit (LC at sight or with a tenor) for larger or first-time supplier relationships. The Bermuda bank will transmit MT103 payments through its New York or UK correspondent clearing network, with the payment narrative including the supplier invoice number, contract reference, and ISIC trade description 'Green coffee beans, not roasted, not decaffeinated, for repackaging' to avoid sanctions screening mismatches. For documentary credit settlements, the Bermuda bank issues the LC via SWIFT MT700, with the confirming bank in the supplier's country negotiating the documents; the required clearing documents include the original bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, phytosanitary certificate, and weight/quality certificate. The company should maintain a liquidity buffer in USD on the operational account, a committed overdraft facility for documentary LCs, and a reporting line to the bank for any payments exceeding the local currency control threshold. All outbound payments must be matched to an executed trade contract to preserve clear audit evidence for the BMA and the AML/KYC framework.FX Forwards – vanilla forward contracts up to 12 months for USD/BMD or USD/EUR settlement of coffee trade payments.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) – permissible where the local currency of the coffee supplier is restricted or non-deliverable, settled in USD via the Bermuda bank's treasury desk.Cross-Currency Swaps – permitted for long-term mismatch management between GBP-denominated funding from the UK parent and USD-denominated coffee trade settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda statutory local file/master file threshold in Bermuda dollars. However, the Economic Substance Act 2018 requires a distribution and service entity, including green coffee trading companies, to document arm's length pricing to demonstrate that profits are commensurate with functions, assets, and risks in Bermuda. For UK parent companies with turnover above £200 million, UK transfer pricing documentation under TIOPA may require a Master File; those documents should be retained and made available to Bermuda authorities on request.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 and Economic Substance Regulations apply to all exempted companies carrying on a 'relevant activity'. 'Distribution and service business' is a relevant activity, meaning an exempted company that physically receives, stores, or distributes green coffee through Bermuda must satisfy the economic substance test: management and direction in Bermuda, adequate employees, premises, and core income-generating activities in Bermuda. Pure foreign-source trading without any Bermuda presence may report no relevant activity, but shell-company avoidance is statutorily targeted; IP-rich or captive-distribution structures attract stricter inspection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Fine up to BMD 10,000 per offence and up to BMD 10,000 per day for continuing failure; forfeiture of legal status via strike-off; director disqualification up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution of any government fees avoided; courts may impose a travel ban and prison up to 5 years for knowingly making a false declaration; veil-piercing is available where the entity is a facade for UK tax avoidance.
Shell Company / Inadequate Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda ESR enforcement can impose penalty up to BMD 10,000, then BMD 10,000 per day, and a notice of failure can lead to compulsory dissolution of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for fines; HMRC/UK CFC rules can attribute undistributed profits to UK shareholders; travel bans or extradition exposure arise if UK tax evasion is prosecuted.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Counter-Terrorist Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties from the Financial Intelligence Agency up to BMD 250,000 per breach; criminal fine and imprisonment up to 10 years on conviction.

Director personal liability: Individual officers who wilfully fail to appoint a compliance officer or file a SAR can face personal fines and imprisonment; confiscation of assets and a travel ban are available.
UK CFC / GAAR Attribution

Statutory sanction: UK HMRC may assess UK corporation tax on the Bermudian profits under CFC chargeable profits legislation, plus interest and penalties of up to 100% of the tax under Fraud or Misrepresentation in a Return (FA 2007 Schedule 24).

Director personal liability: For non-compliance with a CFC information notice, UK directors face up to £20,000 fine and imprisonment up to 2 years; GPS tag and travel bans are possible in serious tax evasion investigations.
