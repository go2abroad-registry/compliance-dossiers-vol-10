Governing statute

Companies Act 1981 (Bermuda), as amended – ss. 4 (mode of incorporation), 7 (memorandum of association), 14 (exempted company), 18 (certificate of incorporation), 108B (register of directors); supplemented by the Companies (Registration Fees) Regulations 2014 and the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or capital deposit requirement. The Memorandum must state the authorized share capital (commonly USD 12,000) and at least one share must be allotted and issued to a subscriber; capital may remain unpaid.

Funding & unlock path: A Fire Safety Certificate under the Fire Safety and Protection Act 2016 is required only where a physical workspace is occupied, subject to inspection by the Bermuda Fire and Rescue Service. The Workplace Standards Division requires employers to provide a safe working environment under the National Occupational Safety and Health Policy; where the company has no Bermudian employees and no physical premises, no workplace clearance certificate is applicable.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or capital deposit requirement. The Memorandum must state the authorized share capital (commonly USD 12,000) and at least one share must be allotted and issued to a subscriber; capital may remain unpaid.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the Bermuda Exempted Company is an opaque limited-liability company exempt from Bermuda income tax, profits tax, capital-gains tax, withholding tax, and capital-transfer taxes. It is not a pass-through entity for Bermudian tax purposes. It remains separately liable for Bermuda payroll tax, customs duty, social insurance contributions, and property-related duties. For UK tax purposes, it is treated as a non-UK resident company unless central management and control is exercised in the UK under CTA 2009 s.14; no UK transparency or pass-through election is available for a Bermudian incorporated limited company. Under OECD/GloBE Pillar Two, a Bermuda entity that is part of an MNE group with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million may become subject to the Bermudian domestic top-up tax enacted for such groups; standalone and smaller licensing entities retain tax-exempt status.

Available election forms

Bermuda has no check-the-box election because the entity itself is not subject to Bermuda income tax. For US federal income tax purposes, a Bermuda Exempted Company with limited-liability shareholders generally defaults to an association taxable as a corporation; if the entity is an eligible entity (not a per se corporation), an IRS Form 8832 election may be made to treat it as a partnership, and a single-member arrangement may be eligible for disregarded-entity treatment. For UK tax purposes, the Bermudian company is opaque by default; UK hybrid mismatch rules in TIOPA 2010 Part 6B and CTA 2010 Part 21 may recharacterise an arrangement only where differing legal characterisations inside and outside the UK produce double non-taxation. The Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 requires substantial activity in Bermuda for relevant IP-holding or licensing businesses, but confers no tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Bermuda has no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax; the applicable indirect-type charges are customs duty under the Customs Tariff Act 1972 and employer-paid payroll tax under the Payroll Tax Act 1995. UK VAT under the Value Added Tax Act 1994 can become relevant only if the Bermuda entity makes taxable supplies in the UK, supplies digital film licensing services to UK consumers, or acquires goods or services in circumstances giving rise to a UK reverse charge.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Bermuda: no VAT/GST registration or turnover threshold exists; a Customs account is required for import/export activity of any value and payroll-tax employer registration begins with the first employee. In the UK, a non-established business must register for VAT if its taxable UK supplies exceed £90,000 in the previous 12 months or are expected to exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days; a non-UK supplier without UK establishment is generally outside UK VAT registration when supplying film-licencing services to UK business customers, because the UK customer accounts for the reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No Bermudian export VAT zero-rating exists because Bermuda does not charge VAT. Under UK VAT, if the Bermuda Exempted Company becomes VAT-registered, zero-rating is limited to direct exports or enumerated goods: the goods must be exported from the UK by or on behalf of the supplier within 3 months of supply, and the supplier must retain statutory evidence including customs documentation, shipping records, and proof of physical removal. Independent-film licensing involves intangible intellectual-property rights rather than goods, so royalty and licence fees are not subject to goods zero-rating; a supply of film rights to a customer outside the UK is outside the scope of UK VAT under place-of-supply rules, while a UK business customer must reverse-charge. Digital delivery by streaming or download link is treated as an electronic service and cannot be zero-rated as an export of physical goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK CDS electronic declaration or Bermuda ASYCUDA Export Declaration) for physical film media or promotional goods
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight, Air Waybill for air freight, or CMR/CIM document for road/rail movement evidencing dispatch and foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit evidence matching the invoiced licensing fee or royalty, together with the executed film licensing agreement, royalty statement, and invoice to the foreign licensee

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing regime exists in Bermuda. Bermuda Customs uses ASYCUDA World for all import/export declarations, electronic risk assessment, and clearance by authorised brokers or declarants. In the UK, Making Tax Digital for VAT requires VAT-registered businesses to maintain digital records and submit VAT returns through HMRC-approved software using a functional API; the UK does not require state clearance or cryptographic stamping of individual invoices, although commercially agreed XML or PEPPOL e-invoicing may be used.

HMRC and Bermuda Customs may conduct post-clearance verification. If export zero-rating was claimed without satisfying the goods-export test, HMRC will withdraw relief, assess the unpaid VAT under VATA 1994 s.73, charge interest under VATA 1994, and claw back related input VAT previously claimed. Civil penalties under FA 2007 Sch.24 range from 30% for careless inaccuracy to 70% for deliberate inaccuracy and up to 100% for deliberate and concealed conduct, with reductions for unprompted disclosure. Fraudulent evasion of UK VAT is a criminal offence under VATA 1994 s.72, carrying an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 7 years; false customs documents are also prosecutable under CEMA 1979. Retrospective clawback may reach 4 years for ordinary errors, extended to 20 years for deliberate or concealed conduct. In Bermuda, because no VAT is collected, the equivalent exposure is customs-duty reassessment and administrative penalties; there is no VAT clawback risk.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 and fully backed by the United States Dollar (USD). Current account transactions, including film licensing royalty inflows, copyright distribution fees, and operational expense outflows, are freely convertible without BMA approval for exempted companies designated as 'non-resident' for exchange control purposes. Capital account transactions (share capital injections, shareholder loans, dividend repatriation, and liquidation distributions) are generally unrestricted for such non-resident-designated entities, subject to BMA General Permission if the transaction exceeds the stated thresholds. A standard Bermuda Exempted Company (non-resident designation) may hold multicurrency bank accounts, freely remit profits, and is not subject to withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties. No profit remittance hurdles exist provided the entity maintains valid Economic Substance Act 2018 filings and submits annual exchange control declarations where required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies for a Bermuda Exempted Company designated as 'non-resident' for exchange control purposes. All export proceeds (film licensing fees, royalty advances, sub-distribution income) may be retained in the original foreign currency without any obligation to convert into BMD or surrender to the BMA. Resident-designated entities previously faced surrender requirements but the modern regulatory practice grants general permission to retain all export-related foreign currency earnings in approved accounts. The BMD/USD peg effectively eliminates any exchange-rate conversion burden for USD-denominated trade proceeds; non-USD receipts (EUR, GBP) may be kept in designated foreign currency accounts or converted via licensed Bermuda banks without BMA pre-approval.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the island's central bank and exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972, the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, and the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal constraints. A Bermuda Exempted Company may procure virtual IBANs and multicurrency settlement accounts from UK FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) or EU-licensed EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex) provided the EMI's onboarding policy expressly accepts Bermuda corporate clients; many EMIs restrict or reject offshore-island holding structures. Key legal limitation: EMIs are not 'banks' under Bermuda law and cannot act as a local clearing member, provide BMD accounts, or issue Bermuda-recognised letters of credit. Such EMIs are best used as a supplementary collection or disbursement rail, with a Bermuda-licensed commercial bank retained as the primary treasury and escrow counterparty. The BMA does not license fiat EMIs; however, inbound SWIFT transfers from an EMI to a Bermuda bank account are fully acceptable if the underlying payments correspond to genuine film licensing activities and the source of funds passes sanctions/AML screening.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant SWIFT wire structure: MT103 from the shareholder's home-country bank to the Bermuda Exempted Company's USD or BMD account at a Bermuda-licensed bank. Recommended purpose/message field 70: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – SHARE SUBSCRIPTION', with field 72 advising 'BMA GENERAL PERMISSION REF' if applicable. The BMA's General Permission No. 1 allows non-resident entities to hold and deal in foreign currency without per-transaction consent; however, deposits exceeding BMD 1,000,000 individually or BMD 5,000,000 in aggregate within a 12-month rolling period may trigger a Funds Source Detail Report (FSDR) under the Proceeds of Crime Regulations. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with legal constraints. A Bermuda Exempted Company may procure virtual IBANs and multicurrency settlement accounts from UK FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) or EU-licensed EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex) provided the EMI's onboarding policy expressly accepts Bermuda corporate clients; many EMIs restrict or reject offshore-island holding structures. Key legal limitation: EMIs are not 'banks' under Bermuda law and cannot act as a local clearing member, provide BMD accounts, or issue Bermuda-recognised letters of credit. Such EMIs are best used as a supplementary collection or disbursement rail, with a Bermuda-licensed commercial bank retained as the primary treasury and escrow counterparty. The BMA does not license fiat EMIs; however, inbound SWIFT transfers from an EMI to a Bermuda bank account are fully acceptable if the underlying payments correspond to genuine film licensing activities and the source of funds passes sanctions/AML screening.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a non-CIT-scope Bermuda Exempted Company. Alternative headline rate of 15% applies exclusively to in-scope multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue equal to or above EUR 750 million under Bermuda's Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Bermuda does not impose any withholding tax on dividends, whether paid to resident or non-resident shareholders. The Bermuda exemption from income and withholding taxes is contained in the Certificate of Exemption issued to every exempted company.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

CIT-R due within 15 months after the end of the relevant fiscal year for the first filing period and within 11 months after fiscal year-end for subsequent years. Quarterly estimated CIT instalments are due 15 days after the end of each calendar quarter.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on or before 31 January of each year for companies incorporated on or before 31 August; otherwise, on or before 31 January of the year following incorporation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Bermuda transfer pricing documentation threshold applies to CIT-exempt exempted companies. For CIT-scope multinational enterprise groups, transfer pricing documentation (master file, local file, and country-by-country report) is mandatory where the group's consolidated revenue is equal to or above EUR 750 million and the Bermudian entity has intra-group transactions with a cumulative value exceeding BMD 5 million in a fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Amendment Act 2018 applies directly to a Bermuda exempted company engaged in independent film copyright licensing. 'Intellectual Property Business' is a relevant activity, requiring the company to pass the economic substance test in Bermuda: (i) core income-generating activities (negotiation, drafting, and enforcement of licensing agreements; management of royalty collections; and risk management) performed in Bermuda; (ii) an adequate physical presence with premises and full-time employees in Bermuda proportionate to the licensing activity; (iii) a minimum quantum of annual expenditure and board meetings in Bermuda. A pure holding company exemption applies only to entities holding equity interests without IP exploitation. Annual Economic Substance Declaration compliance is mandatory; failure results in penalties, exchange of information with UK HMRC, and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and sham corporate residence

Statutory sanction: UK HMRC may assert UK tax residence and charge corporation tax at up to 25% on worldwide profits, impose penalties up to 200% of tax under the Follower Notice and Enablers regime, levy interest on unpaid tax, and issue a Personal Liability Notice on the foreign director; Bermuda may revoke the Certificate of Exemption.

Director personal liability: Directors become jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid UK tax and penalties where they knowingly enabled tax evasion. Civil restitution, disqualification as director in the UK for up to 15 years, travel bans imposed by the Bermuda court, and custodial sentences of up to 14 years under UK tax fraud statutes.
Economic substance failure (shell company)

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Registrar and Tax Commissioner impose a penalty of BMD 10,000 for failure to file the Economic Substance Declaration, a further BMD 100,000 penalty for failure to satisfy the economic substance test, BMD 250,000 for providing false information on a declaration, and BMD 1,000,000 for continued significant failure; information is automatically exchanged with UK HMRC.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are criminally liable with fines up to BMD 250,000 and imprisonment of up to five years. Repeat violations trigger disqualification from serving as directors of any Bermuda company, private enforcement of restitution by creditors, prohibition on travel to the UK under extradition arrangements, and the court may pierce the corporate veil to hold directors personally accountable for licensing liabilities.
AML and film finance money laundering

Statutory sanction: Financial Sanctions (Implementation of International Sanctions) Regulations require freezing of funds and penalties up to the greater of BMD 250,000 or twice the value of the underlying transaction; the Bermuda Monetary Authority may revoke the company's banking licence or impose fines under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997; individuals may be imprisoned for up to 14 years for money laundering offences.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal liability, seizure of personal bank accounts, travel bans, and extradition under UK-Bermuda mutual legal assistance arrangements. Directors who knowingly permit the non-registration of a beneficial owner commit a strict liability offence punishable by a fine of BMD 100,000 and imprisonment of five years. The court may order civil restitution equal to the laundered amount pressed against personal assets of the directors.
Late or non-filing of annual company returns

Statutory sanction: Automatic strike-off and dissolution under section 237 of the Companies Act 1981; all assets and contracts of the film licensing business vest in the Crown and may be forfeited; unlawful trading after strike-off constitutes an offence punishable by a fine of BMD 25,000; late fees accrue at 10% per annum of the unpaid government fee.

Director personal liability: Directors become personally liable for all debts and liabilities of the company incurred after the date of dissolution, including outstanding licensing obligations and penalties. The Bermuda court may issue a travel ban preventing the director from leaving the jurisdiction; reinstatement of the company requires a court order and payment of all arrears by the director personally. Directors who continue to operate the Amazon film distribution business post-strike-off additionally face criminal prosecution and imprisonment of up to two years.
