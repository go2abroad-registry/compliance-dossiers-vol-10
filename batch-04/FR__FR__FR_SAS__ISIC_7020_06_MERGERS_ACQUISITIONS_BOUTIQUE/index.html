A French SAS must have a registered office in France under French Commercial Code Articles L.123-11 and following. Domiciliation with a licensed domiciliation provider is generally permissible if the provider is authorised and the domiciliation agreement, availability of documents, and identification requirements are satisfied. A home address may be used subject to statutory and contractual restrictions. For an M&A advisory boutique, a virtual office is not an adequate substitute for operational substance where the business performs regulated investment services, client due diligence, transaction monitoring, advisory execution, or employs local personnel. Determine whether the activities constitute investment services, financial analysis, placement, investment advice, or regulated portfolio-management activity. Relevant activities may require AMF authorisation, registration as an investment-services provider, ORIAS registration, or use of an appropriately authorised professional. Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The National Institute of Industrial Property (Institut national de la propriété industrielle, INPI) operates the mandatory Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises. The competent commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce) records the SAS in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS); the company is also entered in the Registre national des entreprises (RNE).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, including Articles L.210-1 et seq. (commercial companies), L.227-1 to L.227-20 (SAS), R.123-53 et seq. (registration), and Articles L.561-46 et seq. (beneficial ownership information). The French Civil Code applies to contracts and corporate matters not specifically governed by the Commercial Code.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company-creation formalities filing (formulaire de création d’entreprise, formerly M0) submitted through the Guichet unique, accompanied by executed SAS articles of association and supporting documents.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 28 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital above EUR 1. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at formation; the balance is payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions are deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations pending registration. Contributions in kind are fully contributed at incorporation and may require a commissaire aux apports, subject to statutory exemptions. The stated USD fee is an approximate conversion of standard registry and beneficial-owner filing charges; the amount varies with official tariffs and exchange rates and excludes publication and professional costs.

Funding & unlock path: No manufacturing-site clearance is ordinarily required. The registered office must comply with applicable building-use, fire-safety, accessibility, and occupational-health requirements. If premises qualify as an établissement recevant du public, applicable ERP fire and accessibility rules and approvals apply; employers must assess workplace risks and maintain the required DUERP.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the impots.gouv.fr professional tax portal and the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Corporate formation and identification data are initially filed through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is incorporated through the INPI Guichet unique. Following registration, INSEE issues the SIREN and SIRET identifiers, and the DGFiP establishes the corporate tax account. The French VAT identification number is issued by the competent SIE and generally has the format FR plus a two-digit control key and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity making taxable transactions in France must submit the applicable VAT registration or identification forms to the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères or the competent SIE before, or promptly upon, commencing taxable activity. An EU-established business generally registers directly without appointing a French fiscal representative. A non-EU-established business generally must appoint a French fiscal representative for French VAT compliance unless an applicable legal exemption or mutual-assistance arrangement applies. Supporting documents normally include constitutional documents, foreign tax registration evidence, proof of business activity, identity and authority documents for representatives, French bank or commercial information where requested, and evidence of taxable transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). There is no United States-style check-the-box election. A qualifying SAS may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime for up to five financial years if statutory conditions are met, including qualifying ownership, activity, size, age and shareholder requirements. The election must be notified within the statutory deadline and is generally irrevocable after the applicable period. A French SAS may also be subject to special tax consolidation, parent-subsidiary, merger, participation-exemption and group contribution regimes where their statutory conditions are satisfied. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance framework may deny deductions or impose inclusion where hybrid arrangements produce a deduction-without-inclusion or double deduction outcome.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established SAS, the principal French domestic franchise-in-base thresholds for services are EUR 37,500 of prior-year turnover and EUR 41,250 of current-year turnover, subject to statutory transition and sector rules. These thresholds are exemption thresholds rather than a general safe harbor for all VAT obligations. A non-established business generally has no French turnover threshold when it performs transactions for which it is the person liable to French VAT; registration may be required from the first taxable transaction. The franchise regime does not generally eliminate registration obligations for intra-EU transactions, imports, certain reverse-charge transactions or transactions for which the customer is not liable. Cross-border B2B advisory services are generally located where the business customer is established and may be subject to customer reverse charge rather than French VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT if the supplier can substantiate exportation and exit from the EU customs territory. The supplier must retain the customs export declaration and reliable evidence of physical exit. Intra-EU supplies of goods may qualify for exemption if the customer has a valid VAT number in another Member State and the supplier proves dispatch or transport and satisfies recapitulative statement requirements. Services supplied to a business customer established outside France are generally governed by the B2B place-of-supply rule and may be outside the scope of French VAT or subject to customer reverse charge, rather than technically being zero-rated exports. M&A and corporate advisory services are generally taxable advisory services; the financial-services exemption does not ordinarily cover independent transaction, valuation or strategic advisory services. VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices, a direct business connection, proper invoice particulars and absence of an applicable deduction restriction.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and its Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Evidence of acceptance and release for export by French customs
  • Evidence of exit from the European Union customs territory, such as the Export Accompanying Document exit result or equivalent customs confirmation
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms and applicable VAT treatment
  • Transport evidence, including bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note or courier documentation, as applicable
  • Packing list and purchase order or sales contract where relevant
  • Proof of delivery or import clearance in the destination jurisdiction where required to corroborate exit
  • For intra-EU supplies, customer VAT-number validation, transport evidence and the required European Sales List or recapitulative statement
  • Inward remittance evidence only where relevant to an internal control, contractual requirement or specific evidentiary request; France does not generally require an Inward Remittance Certificate as a statutory condition for ordinary export VAT exemption

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through approved Partner Dematerialization Platforms (Plateformes de dématérialisation partenaires, or PDPs). From 1 September 2026, all French-established businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices, and large companies and intermediate-sized enterprises must issue them; SMEs and micro-enterprises must issue them from 1 September 2027. The phased regime also requires electronic reporting of certain B2C and cross-border transactions. The French model is not a universal real-time invoice-clearance system: invoices are transmitted through a PDP and required data are reported to the tax administration under the prescribed technical standards. Public-sector invoices continue to use the Chorus Pro framework. Invoices must contain mandatory French and EU VAT particulars, including supplier and customer identification, VAT numbers where applicable, taxable amount, VAT rate, VAT amount, transaction date, invoice number and applicable reverse-charge or exemption wording.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement for export VAT exemption or ordinary commercial receipts. Export VAT exemption may be denied or clawed back if customs exit, transport, customer status or transaction substance is not substantiated. Incorrectly exempted output VAT may become payable retrospectively, with recovery of improperly deducted input VAT, late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month, and tax penalties depending on conduct. A 10% surcharge may apply for certain late or incomplete filings; penalties may increase to 40% for deliberate non-compliance, 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or concealed activity, and potentially 80% for abusive legal arrangements, subject to statutory rules and procedural safeguards. Customs violations may trigger recovery of duties and taxes, administrative fines, seizure or other customs sanctions. Deliberate false documentation can create criminal exposure. The taxpayer must retain invoices, customs records, transport evidence, accounting records and supporting correspondence for the applicable French tax and customs limitation periods, commonly three years for VAT assessment purposes, subject to extensions and special rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains a fully convertible currency regime for both current account and capital account transactions in line with OECD and IMF standards. Resident SAS entities may freely remit profits, dividends, and advisory fees without prior approval or surrender obligations. All FX transactions are subject solely to standard statistical reporting to the Banque de France.

Export proceeds surrender rules

French residents face no mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Receipts may be collected in foreign currency and converted at prevailing market rates. All transactions exceeding the €10,000 reporting threshold must be declared electronically to the Banque de France for statistical purposes. No forced conversion ratios or prescribed timelines apply.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs encounter substantial legal and regulatory constraints in France owing to stringent AML/CFT obligations and the absence of a full cross-border EMI license. Local banks or regulated payment institutions remain the preferred channel. Services such as Wise may support specific corridors but require linkage to a French operational account for compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 80% of evaded tax (if intentional), plus 40% for serious misdeclaration; criminal fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if found to have committed fraud; travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment possible
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, 100% penalty on unreported income; dissolution of entity

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution, disqualification from management, and criminal charges for abuse of corporate form
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1 million (or 10% of turnover); criminal penalties up to €750,000 and 5 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML breaches; travel bans, asset seizure, and imprisonment for willful non-compliance
