Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Ministry of Finance — Office of the Tax Commissioner (corporate income tax/DMTT and payroll tax administration) and Registrar of Companies (company registration, economic substance filings). Customs matters are administered by the Comptroller of Customs, Bermuda Customs Department. Official portals: www.gov.bm, www.roc.gov.bm (Registrar of Companies), customs ASYCUDA World portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Bermuda Exempted Company (BEC) with the Registrar of Companies via the roc.gov.bm portal; the certificate of incorporation and 8-digit company registration number constitute the primary statutory identifier (also posted on the public register). Step 2: Submit the standard Exemption Certificate application to the Minister of Finance under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (EUTPA), securing statutory immunity from Bermuda income, capital, profit, and withholding taxes until 28 March 2035. Step 3: Register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner for payroll tax only if the BEC employs Bermuda-resident staff; a BEC with no Bermuda payroll has no payroll tax filing obligation. Step 4: Register as an importer/exporter with the Bermuda Customs Department (ASYCUDA World) to obtain a customs identifier for export declarations. Step 5: If the BEC is a constituent entity of an in-scope MNE group (consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million), register for the Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (DMTT) under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 and the Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax Act 2024, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025. There is no VAT/GST registration in Bermuda.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — a Bermuda Exempted Company is a separate legal entity with limited liability and no Bermuda corporate income tax, capital gains tax, accumulated earnings tax, or withholding tax, pursuant to the EUTPA Exemption Certificate. The entity remains subject to the Economic Substance Act 2018 for 'relevant activities', including 'distribution and service centre' activities, if it is tax-resident in Bermuda; in-scope MNE groups are subject to the 15% DMTT and to the Income Inclusion Rule/UTPR under the OECD Pillar Two framework. Alternatively, a BEC may certify non-Bermuda tax residence (where it is centrally managed and controlled in another jurisdiction), thereby becoming excluded from Bermuda economic substance requirements and leaving profits to the residence jurisdiction's CFC or principal-tax rules. A BEC may not carry on local Bermuda business with Bermudian residents without a special ministerial licence, reinforcing its offshore distribution identity.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity-classification election exists under Bermuda law; classification follows the Companies Act 1981. Available elective mechanisms: (i) EUTPA Exemption Certificate — the default election granting zero-tax protection through 2035; (ii) under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 and Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax Act 2024, in-scope groups may elect to apply the GloBE model rules with a qualified domestic minimum top-up tax (QDMTT) safe harbour, ensuring that Bermudian excess profits are taxed locally at 15% rather than by a foreign jurisdiction; (iii) hybrid mismatch elections are not locally codified — Bermuda relies on OECD anti-hybrid architecture embedded in the DMTT for QDMTT integrity, so hybrid instruments are not recharacterised at Bermuda level; (iv) for US federal tax purposes only, the BEC may file IRS Form 8832 to elect classification as a corporation, partnership, or disregarded entity, commonly electing disregarded-entity status where the Bermuda vehicle is a pure distribution intermediary owned by a foreign parent, although that election has no effect on Bermuda domestic classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Bermuda imposes no VAT, GST, sales tax, or use tax. The only indirect tax is Bermuda Customs Duty on imported goods under the Customs Tariff Act 1971; no export duty exists. Exports of goods from Bermuda are outside the scope of any Bermuda indirect tax, and imported goods subsequently re-exported may qualify for duty drawback or temporary-admission relief under the Customs Act 1941.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST regime or registration threshold exists in Bermuda because no general consumption tax is in force. For customs purposes, every commercial importer/exporter must hold a Bermuda Customs registration (ASYCUDA World importer/exporter code) without any de minimis threshold, and a licensed customs broker must process each entry unless the entity maintains a valid customs clearance licence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in a VAT sense — Bermuda has no zero-rating mechanism because exports are outside the scope of any Bermuda consumption tax. The export of pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot soup base bricks from Bermuda, or a drop-shipment in which the goods never physically land in Bermuda, is not taxed and requires no refund claim. The only domestic relief is the customs re-export/drawback regime: where the packaged goods or their inputs are imported into Bermuda and re-exported within the statutory period, the BEC may apply for remission or drawback of customs duty. Where the BEC is named as importer of record at destination, UK import VAT (20%) or EU import VAT will apply at the border, and the BEC may need an overseas-person VAT registration in the destination member state to recover that import VAT, but such treatment is governed by the destination jurisdiction and is not a Bermuda tax identity matter.

Bermuda imposes no domestic FX controls or statutory repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark reflects the China SAFE export-proceeds collection period applicable where the Bermudian distribution profits are on-forwarded to the Chinese operating entity. Penalties for abuse: (1) Customs — false export declarations, undervaluation, or misuse of the re-export drawback procedure trigger forfeiture of goods, a fine of up to treble the value of the goods or BMD 25,000, and imprisonment for up to five years under the Customs Act 1941. (2) Economic Substance — a Bermuda-tax-resident BEC conducting 'distribution and service centre' activities must prove core income-generating activities in Bermuda (logistics management, inventory and warehousing decisions, supply-chain and pricing decisions) with adequate physical premises, a locally employed senior manager, and proportionate operating expenditure; breach attracts court-imposed penalties up to BMD 50,000, daily default penalties, strike-off, and director liability. (3) DMTT/Pillar Two — incorrect returns or understated QDMTT exposure attract surcharges up to 10%, interest, civil penalties up to 100% of understated tax, and criminal liability for deliberate evasion. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar under a currency board system. Exchange controls are effectively absent: both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible, and there are no statutory restrictions on profit repatriation for Bermuda Exempted Companies. However, capital account inflows and outflows must be recorded and supported by appropriate corporate documentation, and the entity must maintain satisfactory economic substance under the Economic Substance Act 2018 to retain tax-exempt status and continued unrestricted distribution capacity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender or compulsory conversion ratio. A Bermuda Exempted Company may hold, convert, and disburse export and trading receipts in BMD, USD, GBP, EUR, or other permissible settlement currencies at negotiated commercial rates. The BMA requires transaction-level AML/ATF monitoring and may require evidence of underlying trade flows for large or rapid movements.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the currency board authority, monetary authority, and financial services regulator for Bermuda; HM Treasury and Bank of England have no direct exchange control or monetary jurisdiction over Bermuda as a separate non-sovereign special member state territory.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Marginal. Bermuda does not maintain a distinct e-money institution (EMI) licence category under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999; any entity taking deposits or issuing payment instruments on a systemic basis must be BMA licensed. An offshore digital EMI, including a UK-authorised EMI or European e-money institution, may not lawfully provide primary payment accounts to a Bermuda-based entity without a BMA licence. They may only be used as auxiliary settlement channels if the underlying funds are cleared via a fully licensed BMA-regulated bank. Legal and operational friction is substantial.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the corporate shareholder account to the Bermuda Exempted Company's designated capital escrow or operational account at a licensed BMA bank. The wire instruction must include the mandatory purpose code (e.g., 'capital contribution', 'inward direct investment', or 'shareholder loan') in the bank's required format, together with a clear reference to the share subscription or intercompany loan agreement. The Bermuda bank will verify the identity of the originating account and the underlying corporate authorisation prior to acceptance. Because USD clearing ultimately passes through a US correspondent bank, the remitter must ensure the beneficiary account is unrestricted and not subject to OFAC or UK sanctions screening delays.For trade settlement, the Bermuda Exempted Company may use (a) SWIFT MT103 for cash telegraphic transfer under letters of credit or open-account TT terms, and (b) commercial letters of credit issued through the Bermuda bank or its confirming bank, with advisement via SWIFT MT700/MT720. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No standalone Bermuda Local File or Master File filing threshold exists for a small exempted company trading in tangible goods because Bermuda has no general transfer pricing legislation outside the Pillar Two/CIT sphere. An in-scope MNE group (consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million, approximately BMD 1.47 billion) may be required to maintain OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation and Country-by-Country Reporting in support of the 15% minimum tax, together with contemporaneous economic substance records evidencing the distribution entity's arm's length margin.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018, as amended by the Economic Substance Amendment Act 2024, applies full substance requirements to a Bermuda Exempted Company carrying on a Distribution and Service Centre business for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025. The entity must evidence in Bermuda: local governance and board control; core income-generating activities including purchasing decisions, stock control, pricing, contract negotiation, invoice administration, and logistics management; adequate full-time employees; physical premises; and operating expenditure proportionate to the level of income. The substance assertion is tested annually through the ESR return, and the BTA may exchange findings with HMRC and other competent authorities under the UK-Bermuda Tax Information Exchange Agreement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic Substance Avoidance / Shell Company Recharacterisation

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty of up to BMD 250,000 under the Economic Substance Act, plus daily default penalties for failure to respond to compliance notices; registry strike-off; the BTA will exchange the entity's profile with HMRC and other CRS partners, and the foreign tax administration may reallocate profits under its CFC or permanent establishment rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent who knowingly sign false substance declarations are individually guilty of an offence; the court may impose fines on directors, disqualify them from serving on exempted companies, and, for fraudulent misdeclaration, impose imprisonment of up to 5 years. Directors of a struck-off company are personally jointly liable for debts contracted during the period of default.
Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Exemption Claims

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines of up to BMD 100,000 on the entity; civil penalties of up to 200% of evaded or recharacterised tax if the group crosses the Pillar Two threshold; the BTA may refer the matter to the Director of Public Prosecutions; violations of the Companies Act 1981 expose the entity to restoration conditions and loss of limited liability.

Director personal liability: Directors acting in bad faith are personally liable for unpaid penalties and may face asset-recovery proceedings through the UK-Bermuda TIEA; fraudulent trading attracts personal civil liability for all company debts, travel restrictions pending inquiry, and custodial sentences of up to 10 years under the Theft (Amendment) Act 2019 for dishonesty offences.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Transparency Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalties of up to BMD 25,000 for registry offences plus continuing daily fines; the entity may be suspended from the Beneficial Ownership portal and flagged to the Bermuda Monetary Authority; correspondent banks may freeze funds under AML red-flag protocols.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Money-Laundering Reporting Officer are personally liable where wilful non-disclosure is proven; imprisonment of up to 2 years and personal fines apply; professional directors found to have aided concealment may be disqualified by the Registrar.
Trade Sanctions / Export Licence Violation

Statutory sanction: Violations of the UK Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 and OFAC equivalents can trigger asset freezes, cargo seizures, prohibition from government procurement, and fines of up to the greater of GBP 1 million or 50% of the transaction value; the Bermuda entity may lose its Exemption Certificate for conduct damaging Bermuda's international standing.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability, travel bans, and international arrest warrants are realistic where sanctions evasion is proven; directors of the exempted company may face extradition to the UK or Bermuda and disgorgement orders for profits generated by the unauthorised exports.
Treaty Abuse / Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Foreign anti-avoidance reassessment under the UK GAAR, ATAD, or DTA limitation-of-benefits clauses, with penalties of up to 100% of the additional foreign tax; on the Bermuda side, an in-scope group under CITA faces a 15% minimum top-up tax and disallowance of intercompany deductions that lack arm's length substance.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise artificial arrangements may be assessed for restitution of tax lost by counterparty jurisdictions, banned from acting as directors under Bermuda's Insolvency Act, and exposed to contempt sanctions for refusing to provide cross-border information.
