Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda (portal: https://www.taxcommissioner.gov.bm); Registrar of Companies, Ministry of Finance (portal: https://www.gov.bm/registrar-companies via Bermuda Online System); Bermuda Customs Department, Ministry of Finance (ASYCUDA World single window). HM Revenue & Customs (UK) has no fiscal jurisdiction over Bermuda, a British Overseas Territory with statutory fiscal autonomy.

E-government filing portal

Bermuda does not issue a universal income-tax TIN. A foreign-owned Bermuda Exempted Company is formed through a licensed Bermuda registered agent: (1) reserve the company name with the Registrar of Companies; (2) file the Memorandum of Association and bye-laws; (3) receive the Certificate of Incorporation carrying the statutory Company Number, which functions as the de facto tax identifier; (4) apply for the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 Tax Protection Certificate; (5) register with the Tax Commissioner for Payroll Tax only if employing Bermuda-resident staff and for Foreign Currency Purchase Tax compliance; (6) obtain a Bermuda Customs declarant/importer number if importing goods; (7) file Beneficial Ownership particulars. The non-resident parent company files no Bermuda income-tax registration. No minimum capital requirement applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. A Bermuda Exempted Company is a non-resident, internationally-held limited company not subject to Bermuda income, capital gains, withholding, or estate taxes on worldwide profits, with a statutory moratorium under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966. Exception: the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 imposes a 15% OECD Pillar Two top-up tax only on constituent entities of MNE groups with consolidated turnover equal to or above EUR 750m, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025; a standalone clinker wholesale Exempted Company below that threshold has no Bermuda income-tax liability.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; classification follows the Bermuda Companies Act 1981 and domestic juridical status. Available elections: (a) designation as non-resident for Exchange Control Act purposes; (b) EUTPA Tax Protection Certificate election securing the statutory tax moratorium; (c) for in-scope CIT groups, designation of the Bermuda filing constituent entity and any qualified domestic top-up elections under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023; (d) no hybrid-mismatch elections are administered by Bermuda, and mismatch outcomes are governed by the counterparty jurisdiction rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None. Bermuda levies no VAT, GST, or general sales tax. The only indirect-type taxes are customs duties on imports under the Customs Act 1941 (generally 15 to 33.25 percent ad valorem), the Foreign Currency Purchase Tax (0.75 percent on foreign-currency conversion at Bermudian banks), Hotel Occupancy Tax (4 percent), and passenger fees. Wholesale supplies of Portland clinker by a Bermuda Exempted Company to foreign buyers fall entirely outside any Bermuda indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. No turnover threshold triggers a VAT or GST registration in Bermuda. Employers must register for Payroll Tax on Bermuda-resident employees irrespective of turnover; importers must hold a Bermuda Customs declarant number for each ASYCUDA declaration; the Foreign Currency Purchase Tax is collected at the bank at the point of conversion without entity registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Bermuda has no VAT or GST, and therefore no 0 percent export VAT claim procedure and no export tax refund mechanism exist. Exports of Portland clinker by sea attract no Bermuda tax. Imported clinker entered for warehousing, transshipment, or vessel-to-vessel transfer may be declared to Bermuda Customs under transit or bond procedure to defer or avoid customs duty; physical export from Bermuda does not trigger any tax clawback.

  • Bermuda Customs transit or export entry (ASYCUDA World electronic declaration or in-transit authorization)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading with port of loading or transshipment point outside Bermuda
  • Commercial invoice governing the SWIFT LC or TT wire sale
  • Vessel manifest and packing list endorsed by the shipping agent
  • Evidence of the payment chain (SWIFT MT103 or LC document set) where the Bermudian bank requires Foreign Currency Purchase Tax documentation on any local currency conversion

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping regime exists for commercial invoices in Bermuda. Import and export declarations are filed electronically through the Bermuda Customs ASYCUDA World single window; sea export and transit cargo manifests are transmitted electronically by the vessel agent. Commercial invoices supporting SWIFT LC and TT wire payments remain paper or PDF based without state clearance.

No export-VAT clawback exists because no zero-rating system exists. Bermuda Customs may verify transit and bond documentation on export: false declarations or removal of bonded goods without lawful entry attract forfeiture, fines up to three times the dutiable value, and imprisonment under the Customs Act 1941. For in-scope CIT groups, understating Bermuda taxable profits attracts interest and penalties under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, including default fines up to BMD 10,000, per-day penalties, and 10 percent interest. Fraudulent representations to the Tax Commissioner or Customs may void the EUTPA protection and trigger recovery with treble tax-equivalent amounts. The 180-day benchmark reflects a commercial credit term for open-account ocean-freight transactions rather than a Bermuda statutory repatriation deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar operates under a currency-board style arrangement pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar, with unrestricted free convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, intercompany loan repayment, and liquidation surplus repatriation are not subject to exchange-control approval, but each outward payment is subject to the Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing (AML/ATF) regime administered by the BMA, including sanctions screening and source-of-funds verification. No withholding tax exists in Bermuda on dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, or capital gains, and no additional exchange-control licensing fee applies to corporate treasury flows. The absence of exchange-control barriers is conditional on the exempted company maintaining its certificate of exemption, correct beneficial-ownership filings under the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Regulations 2021, and compliance with Financial Action Task Force (FATF) transparency standards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios in Bermuda. A Bermuda Exempted Company engaged in bulk Portland clinker supply and ocean vessel freight may retain export receipts in USD, BMD, EUR, or GBP indefinitely; may net receipts against supplier and freight payables; and may convert at market rates through any licensed Bermuda bank. Supporting commercial documentation, including sales contracts, invoices, bills of lading, letters of credit, and freight statements, must be retained for a minimum of five years to satisfy BMA record-keeping obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the monetary authority and central financial regulator of Bermuda for the Bermuda dollar (BMD), with regulatory powers under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, the Exchange Control Act 1972, the Banking Act 1969, and the Companies Act 1981.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility rating. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) may be used only as ancillary payment channels, not as the primary treasury bank, because the Bermuda Banking Act 1969 requires a BMA license for deposit-taking and the provision of payment account services to Bermuda-domiciled entities. An unlicensed UK or EU EMI cannot lawfully hold fiat deposits on behalf of the Bermuda company nor present itself as a Bermuda banking presence; however, a UK Financial Conduct Authority regulated EMI licensed under the UK Electronic Money Regulations 2011 can execute high-frequency, low-value supplier disbursements in EUR and GBP provided the account is structured as an electronic money account, not a deposit account, and the EMI performs independent AML/ATF screening over the underlying cement trade flows. A BMA-licensed Digital Asset Business is not required because the trade operates exclusively in fiat currencies through SWIFT and correspondent banking rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections are routed by SWIFT MT103 from the UK parent or international investor bank to the Bermuda entity's Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda USD account, with wire field 70 populated using the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (equity investment) and a narrative referencing the board resolution and share issuance. USD funds are cleared same-day through the banks' New York correspondent relationships; EUR and GBP injections are converted at the value date at the prevailing market rate with no BMA exchange-control approval required. The receiving bank verifies the capital payment against the certified share register, the registered agent's beneficial-ownership filing, and BMA sanctions lists; inward remittances above USD 50,000 are reported by the bank in the BMA's balance-of-payments statistical framework. The company should maintain a capital escrow ledger separate from operational trade balances until post-issuance verification by the corporate services provider is completed.Outbound trade disbursements use three clearing rails: (1) confirmed at-sight commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) for purchases of Portland clinker from cement producers, payable against presentation of a clean bill of lading, commercial invoice, certificate of quality, and marine insurance policy; (2) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for freight disbursements to shipowners and chartering agents, using ISO 20022 purpose code 'TRPT' or 'SUPP' with invoice and vessel references in field 70; and (3) negotiable documents against payment in the case of high-value urgent cargo payments. USD liquidity is centralized in the Butterfield USD account with HSBC Bermuda as the fallback clearing bank; same-day netting is applied between inbound trade receipts and outbound freight payables to minimise foreign exchange exposure.Plain-vanilla deliverable foreign exchange forwards in USD/EUR, USD/GBP, and USD/BMD under a zero-cost collar policy, with a maximum tenor of twelve months and counterparty limits approved by the Bermuda board of directors.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) denominated in USD against emerging-market currencies (e.g., USD/CNY, USD/MXN, USD/ZAR) used for port-side procurement exposures in cement-importing jurisdictions where onshore deliverable FX markets are restricted.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) executed between USD and EUR or GBP to hedge long-dated supplier financing receivables and vessel charter payment streams, subject to BMA supervisory guidance on non-bank derivatives and IFRS 9 hedge accounting documentation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermudian transfer-pricing documentation threshold exists for exempted companies outside the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023. Covered multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750m, approximately BMD 750,000,000, are expected to maintain OECD-compliant Master File, Local File and Country-by-Country-reportable data for the Bermuda constituent entity.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 and its Regulations apply to relevant entities carrying on relevant activities, including distribution and service-centre businesses and shipping or sale of goods. A wholesale cement and portland clinker distribution operation, together with ocean vessel freight management, must demonstrate in Bermuda a physical local office, appropriate managerial presence, senior management decision-making, employees sufficient in number and qualification, and operating expenditure proportional to the business. Filing an economic substance declaration of active substance without actual support is treated as a criminal false statement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

CIT Evasion and GloBE Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Default tax assessment for the full top-up tax, statutory interest and administrative penalties; conviction for tax evasion may result in fines and forfeiture orders in addition to unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and managers whose fraud or gross negligence caused the understatement are jointly and severally liable; civil restitution and director disqualification orders are available, and serious cases carry imprisonment.
Economic Substance Deficit / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Regulatory penalties imposed by the Bermuda Registrar, possible striking-off or dissolution of the company, and seizure or forfeiture of assets under enforcement procedures; false declarations are criminal offences.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false declaration are personally exposed to criminal fines and imprisonment; the corporate veil may be pierced and directors may face restitution and travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and UK CFC Exposure

Statutory sanction: UK HMRC may recharacterise the arrangements under transfer-pricing rules and, where a controlled foreign company is identified, charge UK corporation tax on apportioned profits at up to 25%; penalties may reach 100% of the underpaid tax for deliberate errors.

Director personal liability: Both the UK parent directors and Bermuda entity directors who knowingly implemented the mispricing can be penalised; dishonest exploitation of the Bermuda zero rate can lead to civil restitution and criminal tax-fraud proceedings.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties by the Bermuda Registrar under the Beneficial Ownership (Companies) Regulations; money-laundering offence exposure under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 for regulated financial or company-service businesses; continued breach leads to strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failing to provide required ownership information, including fines and imprisonment; beneficial owners may be prohibited from dealing with the company and are subject to asset freezes and travel bans.
