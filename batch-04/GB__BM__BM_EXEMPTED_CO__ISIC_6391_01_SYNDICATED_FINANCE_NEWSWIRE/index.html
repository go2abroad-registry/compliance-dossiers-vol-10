Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC), Government of Bermuda, with company-law registration administered by the Registrar of Companies at the Registry General; primary digital portal: Government of Bermuda e-Services at www.gov.bm.

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda exempted company is incorporated through the Registrar of Companies and obtains a Bermuda Company Register Number. Bermuda does not impose general income tax, capital gains tax, or VAT, so no routine TIN is issued for a purely non-resident-owned exempted company. For in-scope MNE groups under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (Pillar Two top-up tax), the constituent entity must register with the OTC on the Bermuda CIT/safe-harbour portal, provide the group's CBCR information, and receive an OTC-issued tax identification number. Foreign owners may also require a Tax Exemption Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966; this certificate is filed with the Registrar of Companies but is not an income-tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' classification election exists in Bermuda law. The equivalent statutory election regime is the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 election to be treated as a tax-exempted undertaking and hold a Tax Exemption Certificate. For MNE groups subject to the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, the OTC permits elective application of GloBE safe harbours (transitional CBCR safe harbour, substance-based income exclusion, and qualified domestic minimum top-up tax) as prescribed by the Bermuda CIT regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

N/A - Bermuda has no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax; the only broad-based levy with payroll characteristics is the Bermuda Payroll Tax, which is not a consumption tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. No VAT/GST/consumption-tax registration threshold exists in Bermuda. Payroll-tax registration is required for all employers irrespective of turnover, and customs duty is collected on physical imports without a general trader registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No Bermuda VAT/GST regime yields a zero-rating claim; the concept of export zero-rating is inapplicable. A Bermuda exempted company deriving offshore service revenue from newswire syndication, API data feeds, or syndicated finance information is outside Bermuda's income and payroll tax net and does not charge Bermuda indirect tax. If a taxable presence is later created in the UK or another buyer jurisdiction, that jurisdiction's export or place-of-supply rules would independently determine VAT applicability.

  • Not applicable - no Bermuda Customs export declaration is required for electronic/API-delivered financial newswire services.
  • No bill of lading is required because the supply is an offshore service, not physical goods.
  • No inward remittance certificate is statutorily required for tax substantiation in Bermuda.

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital tax-invoice mandate applies to Bermuda business-to-business services. Bermuda Customs operates an electronic import declaration interface for physical goods, but it is not a VAT/e-invoice clearance system and is inapplicable to syndicated-finance newswire data services.

There is no fraudulent export zero-rating penalty because no export VAT refund exists. Bermuda abuse-of-exemption scrutiny focuses on misrepresented tax-exempt status: OTC may impose late-filing and inaccuracy penalties plus daily default penalties under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023; the Attorney-General may revoke an Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act certificate on material non-disclosure or fraud; and a foreign tax authority, including HMRC, may assess secondary adjustments or withholding taxes where substance is mischaracterised. A Bermuda CIT top-up tax underpayment is retrospectively collected with interest from the first in-scope accounting period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is fixed at 1:1 to the U.S. dollar (USD) under the ECA framework. Bermuda maintains administered exchange control rather than full formal convertibility, but capital and current account flows are freely convertible in practice through authorized dealers. Profit remittances from a Bermuda Exempted Company to non-resident shareholders are permitted without case-by-case approval provided the company holds 'non-resident' designation from the BMA; foreign-currency funds may be held and transmitted without Bermuda-dollar conversion. The ISIC 6391 subscription/API newswire business will settle predominantly in USD, which structurally bypasses Bermuda-dollar conversion friction and renders the ECA approval path administrative rather than substantive.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export-proceeds surrender applies to a Bermuda Exempted Company formally designated as non-resident under the Exchange Control Act 1972; such entities may retain foreign-currency export receipts indefinitely without any conversion ratio or surrender timeline. For BMA-resident entities, proceeds from export of services must be offered to an authorized dealer within 30 days of receipt (or such extended period as the BMA directs) and converted unless a general or specific permission permits retention; the mandatory conversion ratio is 100% for BMD-denominated proceeds. The Exempted Company must secure a formal BMA Non-Resident Designation letter and open USD-denominated accounts to eliminate all surrender obligations; ISIC 6391 newswire subscription revenue is classified as export of services and is freely receivable through SWIFT MT103 rails.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), operating under the Bermuda Exchange Control Act 1972 (ECA), with ultimate sovereign supervisory responsibility retained by the Bank of England for Bermuda as a British Overseas Territory

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility for regulated UK-based electronic money institutions (EMIs) including Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex and Payoneer, supported by the Low_Risk_Standard AML tier of the digital newswire business. Legal constraints: (i) most UK EMIs require a demonstrable UK commercial nexus and may reject a standalone Bermuda Exempted Company without the UK parent as transactional counterparty; (ii) EMIs are not BMA-licensed institutions and cannot provide Bermuda-licensed escrow or segregated client-money protection under Bermuda law; (iii) multi-currency IBAN issuance is operationally available but inbound corporate payments above certain thresholds (typically GBP/USD equivalent) trigger enhanced due diligence on tax-exempt status and must be backed by the Bermuda Certificate of Exemption; (iv) U.S. domiciled digital banks generally decline offshore-territory entities; (v) EMI virtual IBANs are not settlement-grade for syndicated-finance ISO 20022 rails and should be reserved for non-critical operational collections rather than capital escrow or regulatory submissions. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (i) the UK parent executes a SWIFT MT103 GPI credit transfer from a London clearing bank (HSBC UK, Barclays, or Standard Chartered) to HSBC Bermuda (BIC HSBCBMHD) or Butterfield (BIC BNTBBMHD); (ii) SWIFT field 70 must carry the unambiguous purpose code and narrative 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION' and where local bank codes require it, the UK purpose narrative 'NTEQ'; (iii) the credit is lodged to a segregated USD Capital Escrow Account inscribed as 'Non-Resident Exempted Co — BMA Exchange Control Permission not required'; (iv) for GBP funding, conversion to USD may be executed at the London wholesale interbank market before transmission to avoid any Bermuda-dollar step; for BMD-denominated needs, an authorised dealer converts at the statutory 1:1 parity and files BMA Form S1 (transaction report); (v) the remitting bank must obtain the beneficiary bank's BMA non-resident designation confirmation and maintain the GPI end-to-end tracking receipt; (vi) the Exempted Company files the inward remittance declaration with its Bermuda registered agent for the BMA annual return. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility for regulated UK-based electronic money institutions (EMIs) including Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex and Payoneer, supported by the Low_Risk_Standard AML tier of the digital newswire business. Legal constraints: (i) most UK EMIs require a demonstrable UK commercial nexus and may reject a standalone Bermuda Exempted Company without the UK parent as transactional counterparty; (ii) EMIs are not BMA-licensed institutions and cannot provide Bermuda-licensed escrow or segregated client-money protection under Bermuda law; (iii) multi-currency IBAN issuance is operationally available but inbound corporate payments above certain thresholds (typically GBP/USD equivalent) trigger enhanced due diligence on tax-exempt status and must be backed by the Bermuda Certificate of Exemption; (iv) U.S. domiciled digital banks generally decline offshore-territory entities; (v) EMI virtual IBANs are not settlement-grade for syndicated-finance ISO 20022 rails and should be reserved for non-critical operational collections rather than capital escrow or regulatory submissions. BMA guidance imposes no prohibition on holding EMI accounts, but all foreign-currency accounts must be declared on the BMA annual compliance return and any BMD deposits remain restricted to BMA-licensed institutions.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no corporate income tax on Exempted Companies. The Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CITA) imposes a 15% top-up tax only for in-scope multinational enterprise groups with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an Exempted Company, irrespective of the residence of the recipient shareholder, and no branch remittance tax on foreign-owned profits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold applies to out-of-scope exempted companies. For in-scope MNE groups under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, OECD Pillar Two-aligned documentation (Master File, Local File and Country-by-Country Report) is mandatory when the group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750 million, approximately BMD 815 million at the pegged exchange rate.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Requirements for Relevant Activities) Regulations 2018 apply to Bermuda exempted companies undertaking relevant activities, including intellectual property holding, financing and leasing, and distribution and service centres. Although a financial newswire and syndication API business is not automatically a specified relevant activity, entities holding proprietary data, algorithms or IP licences will be scrutinised under the IP regime. Satisfying the doctrine requires locally-directed management in Bermuda, sufficient full-time employees, adequate operating expenditure, and a physical Bermuda presence, all certified annually in Form ESR-1. Non-compliance triggers fines, information exchange with foreign authorities, and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Filing under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent evasion penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded, fixed penalties ranging from BMD 500 to BMD 100,000, confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, and criminal prosecution of the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who sign false returns are individually prosecutable; conviction exposes them to imprisonment of up to 5 years, personal civil restitution for unpaid tax, travel bans imposed by court order, and disqualification from acting as directors for up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Regulations Breach

Statutory sanction: Initial penalty of BMD 10,000 escalating at BMD 10,000 per month of continued non-compliance to a maximum of BMD 250,000; compulsory exchange of information with foreign fiscal authorities; strike-off of the exempted company from the register; loss of the 2035/2055 tax protection covenant.

Director personal liability: Officers face personal fines up to BMD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for non-compliance with enforcement notices; directors are jointly and severally liable for outstanding corporate penalties, subject to court-ordered travel bans restricting cross-border movement, and disqualification from company management.
Anti-Money Laundering / MLRO Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Regulations 2008: fines up to BMD 500,000 per breach, suspension or revocation of the entity's AML registration, freezing of corporate assets by the Financial Intelligence Agency, and criminal indictment of the corporate body.

Director personal liability: Money laundering facilitation is an indictable offence carrying imprisonment of up to 14 years; senior managers and the Money Laundering Reporting Officer face personal civil fines where AML controls are demonstrably inadequate, investigative travel bans may be imposed, and personal asset forfeiture can follow conviction.
