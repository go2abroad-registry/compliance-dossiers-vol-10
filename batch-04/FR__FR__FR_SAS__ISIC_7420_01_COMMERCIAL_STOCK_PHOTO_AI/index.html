Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) for the mandatory Guichet unique des formalités d’entreprises; the competent commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce) records the company in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L. 210-1 et seq. (commercial companies) and L. 227-1 to L. 227-20-1 (SAS); applicable formation and registration provisions include Articles R. 123-1 et seq. and R. 123-53 et seq.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company-formation formalité submitted through the Guichet unique, supported by the signed articles of association (statuts), with the required declaration of beneficial owners (déclaration des bénéficiaires effectifs).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A SAS has no statutory minimum share capital; capital may be set in the articles. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Cash contributions must be deposited before filing with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; deposited funds are released after presentation of the RCS registration certificate (extrait Kbis). The USD filing-fee figure is an approximate conversion of standard registry and beneficial-owner filing charges; fees are set in euros and may change.

Funding & unlock path: No manufacturing-site clearance is ordinarily required for a digital-only business. For leased or occupied premises, comply with applicable building and fire-safety rules; premises open to the public may require ERP authorization and fire-safety review. Employers must meet French occupational health and safety duties, including risk assessment in the document unique d’évaluation des risques professionnels (DUERP), and obtain any building-specific structural approvals required for the premises.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), through the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official impots.gouv.fr portal. French VAT identification and tax-account procedures are administered by the competent SIE; non-established businesses generally deal with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) where applicable.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS obtains its SIREN and SIRET identifiers through registration with the competent business-registration authority and the Registre national des entreprises, with information transmitted to the DGFiP. The company must file the applicable tax-registration information, identify its principal establishment, business activities, accounting period, beneficial ownership and bank details, and request VAT activation or a French VAT number from the competent SIE. The French VAT number is generally formatted as FR plus a two-character key and the nine-digit SIREN. A non-resident entity carrying out taxable transactions in France must assess whether French VAT registration is required, whether it may use the VAT One Stop Shop, and whether it must appoint a French tax representative. Businesses established in another EU Member State generally do not require a French fiscal representative solely because they are VAT-registered in France; businesses established outside the European Union may require an accredited French fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement or special rule applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Subject to statutory eligibility conditions, including limits on age, workforce, turnover or balance-sheet size, and ownership requirements, certain SAS entities may elect temporary taxation under personal income tax (impôt sur le revenu), generally for a maximum period of five fiscal years. This is an entity-level statutory election and not a United States-style check-the-box election. No general French check-the-box election is available. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion where arrangements produce deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes, but they do not constitute an elective entity-classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France applies a VAT franchise regime rather than a single universal registration threshold. For domestic businesses principally supplying services, the ordinary franchise threshold is generally EUR 37,500 of annual turnover, with a tolerance threshold generally of EUR 41,250, subject to the applicable year and legislative changes. For trading, accommodation and comparable goods activities, the principal threshold is generally EUR 85,000, with a tolerance threshold generally of EUR 93,500. Special thresholds apply to certain regulated professions and activities. The threshold does not protect a business from VAT obligations arising from reverse charge, intra-Community acquisitions, imports, distance sales, platform rules, or transactions for which French VAT is otherwise due. A foreign business without a French establishment may require French VAT registration from its first taxable transaction where no exemption, reverse charge, One Stop Shop or other simplification applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union may be supplied free of French VAT where the exporter can substantiate exportation and comply with customs and invoicing requirements. The principal evidence is the electronic customs export record and confirmation that the goods exited the European Union. Intra-Community supplies of goods may generally be exempt from French VAT where the customer is identified for VAT in another Member State, the supplier reports the transaction correctly, and transport to that Member State is evidenced. Services supplied to a business customer established outside France are generally taxed according to the place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of French VAT rather than technically zero-rated; evidence of the customer's business status, establishment and contractual delivery location is required. Digital stock-media, licensing and generative-asset services require separate analysis because B2B and B2C place-of-supply, electronically supplied service, marketplace-intermediary and platform-deemed-supplier rules may differ.

  • Electronic customs export declaration containing the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Electronic confirmation of exit from the European Union or equivalent customs exit evidence
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, currency, Incoterms and export destination
  • Transport documentation, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note or courier proof of delivery
  • Packing list and, where relevant, import-clearance or delivery evidence in the destination country
  • Proof of the customer's location and VAT status for intra-Community transactions, where applicable
  • Contract, order, delivery and payment records supporting the commercial substance of the transaction
  • Bank remittance evidence where relevant to refund or audit substantiation; France does not generally require an inward-remittance certificate as a universal export-VAT condition

France does not currently operate a universal real-time invoice-clearance system for all private-sector transactions. Mandatory domestic business-to-business electronic invoicing is scheduled to apply in phases: all businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; large and mid-sized businesses must issue them from 1 September 2026; and small and micro-enterprises must issue them from 1 September 2027, subject to legislative or administrative changes. Invoices must generally pass through an approved private platform or the designated public infrastructure under the applicable reform. Electronic transaction reporting (e-reporting) will apply to specified business-to-consumer and cross-border transactions. Public-sector invoicing is generally handled through the Chorus Pro system. France does not impose a universal digital tax stamp or real-time fiscal clearance requirement for ordinary commercial invoices, although electronic-format, authenticity, integrity, archiving and audit-trail requirements apply.

France has no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline requiring export proceeds to be brought into France within 180 days. Export proceeds may generally be received through ordinary banking channels subject to anti-money-laundering, customs, accounting and payment-reporting rules. Incorrectly applying an exemption or zero rate can result in assessment of the unpaid French VAT, statutory late-payment interest, and tax penalties. The standard penalty for failure to pay or late payment is generally 5% of the tax due, with late-payment interest generally accruing at the statutory rate, subject to applicable procedural rules. In cases involving deliberate misstatement, concealment or fraudulent conduct, increased penalties may apply, potentially reaching 40% for deliberate default and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abusive conduct, in addition to recovery of VAT, interest, and possible criminal exposure for serious tax fraud. A VAT refund may be denied or clawed back where export proof is incomplete, inconsistent, fabricated or not retained for the statutory record-retention period. Customs penalties, import-export adjustment, platform reporting consequences and joint-liability rules may also apply where the transaction involves an intermediary or deemed supplier.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no general French exchange-control approval for dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, loan repayments, capital contributions, or liquidation proceeds paid to non-residents. Transfers remain subject to corporate-law distribution rules, applicable withholding tax, transfer-pricing and beneficial-ownership requirements, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, and documentary evidence of the underlying transaction. A non-resident shareholder may subscribe for SAS shares and pay share capital or share premium through a French bank, provided source of funds, ownership, tax residence, and transaction-purpose information are satisfactory. Certain foreign investments in protected French sectors may require prior filing or authorization under French foreign-investment-control rules; a stock photography and generative visual-assets marketplace is not ordinarily a protected strategic sector solely by reason of its business description, but technology, data, defense, or critical-infrastructure features must be separately assessed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a domestic bank, convert foreign-currency export receipts into euros, or repatriate them within a prescribed period. Export receipts may be maintained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to the bank's account terms, accounting records, sanctions controls, and tax compliance. Cross-border payment reporting may be performed by banks or payment service providers for statistical, regulatory, and anti-money-laundering purposes. Exporters must retain commercial invoices, contracts, transport or delivery evidence, customs records where applicable, and payment reconciliation records. Cash or equivalent monetary instruments of EUR 10,000 or more entering or leaving the European Union must be declared to customs. No mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank. Applicable foreign-exchange, payment-services, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering rules also derive from European Union law and French legislation administered by competent authorities including the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, the Autorité des marchés financiers, the Direction générale du Trésor, and TRACFIN.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route share capital and share-premium funding from the subscriber's own verified bank account to the French bank's dedicated capital-escrow account before incorporation or capital increase. Use a clear payment reference such as 'subscription for shares in [SAS name]—share capital' or 'share premium contribution' and identify the subscriber and corporate registration process. Provide the bank with the signed articles, subscription form, shareholder resolution, ownership chart, source-of-funds evidence, and expected amount. After incorporation or the capital increase, deliver the Kbis and required corporate documents so the depositary bank can release funds to the operational account. Shareholder loans and ordinary intercompany funding should be sent to the operational account under a documented loan or cash-pooling arrangement, with arm's-length terms and accounting treatment. SEPA Credit Transfer is generally preferred for euro-denominated EEA transfers; SWIFT cross-border wires should include the correct beneficiary legal name, IBAN, BIC, registered address, purpose narrative, and any bank-requested regulatory information. France does not use a universal statutory purpose-code system for ordinary corporate wires, so the commercial purpose and supporting documents must be accurate and consistent. Banks may report statistical or regulatory data and may suspend or reject incomplete, sanctioned, or unexplained transfers.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area, subject to bank limits and sanctions screening. Use SWIFT MT103 terminology for cross-border customer credit transfers, recognizing that ISO 20022 messages are increasingly used in bank infrastructures; include complete originator, beneficiary, invoice, currency, amount, and purpose information. For documentary trade, use a commercial letter of credit under UCP 600, preferably confirmed where counterparty or country risk justifies confirmation, and require compliant documents such as commercial invoice, transport document, insurance evidence, packing list, and certificate of origin where applicable. Documentary collections may be processed under URC 522. Maintain segregation between supplier payments, shareholder distributions, payroll, taxes, refunds, and platform settlements. Screen all counterparties and banks against applicable EU and UN sanctions and retain invoices, contracts, proof of delivery or service performance, customs records where applicable, and payment reconciliations. For platform payouts, use PSP settlement accounts only under documented merchant agreements and reconcile gross receipts, fees, refunds, chargebacks, reserves, and net settlement to the French ledger.Deliverable euro/foreign-currency forwards entered into with an authorized bank or investment firm for identified forecast or committed exposures, documented under an appropriate master agreement and supported by a treasury policy.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies subject to local convertibility or settlement restrictions, provided the counterparty is authorized, the transaction is used for genuine commercial hedging, and applicable local-market rules, reporting, and net-settlement requirements are satisfied.Cross-currency swaps or currency swaps for identified shareholder loans, bank debt, lease liabilities, or recurring operating exposures, with maturity, notional, valuation, collateral, and termination terms documented.Foreign-exchange options and collars may be used for hedging where the cost, premium, accounting treatment, suitability, and counterparty documentation are approved; avoid speculative or leveraged structures unrelated to the SAS's underlying exposures.Spot foreign-exchange conversions through the operating bank or authorized payment institution for ordinary settlement needs, with competitive-rate controls, dual authorization, and transaction-level reconciliation.Apply EMIR-related requirements where applicable, including legal entity identification, trade confirmation, portfolio reconciliation, risk-mitigation procedures, collateral arrangements, and clearing or reporting analysis. Maintain evidence that derivatives are risk-reducing and proportionate to forecast revenue, supplier, debt, or dividend exposures.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to High for operating receipts, card acquiring, SEPA payments, and multi-currency treasury if the provider is an EU-authorized payment institution or electronic-money institution passporting into France. Low for statutory share-capital escrow, primary corporate-banking functions, cash-intensive activity, or transactions involving elevated sanctions and source-of-funds risk. Offshore digital EMIs without EU authorization, passporting, or a French or EEA regulated partner should not be used for regulated payment services to the SAS. Stripe and PayPal may support collections and payouts subject to their merchant underwriting, reserve, prohibited-business, chargeback, and beneficial-owner policies; they should not be treated as substitutes for a regulated corporate bank account. Customer funds held by an EMI are generally safeguarded rather than covered by the French deposit-guarantee scheme applicable to eligible bank deposits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Master File/Local File documentation is mandatory when French annual turnover or total balance-sheet assets exceed EUR 400 million; annual TP information statement on form 2257-SD is required once annual turnover exceeds EUR 50 million

Economic substance scope: France uses the arm's-length principle under Article 57 CGI and the general anti-abuse rule under Article L64 LPF. There is no standalone offshore ESR regime, but an SAS is expected to have real staff, premises, decision-making, assets and commercial risk in France. Absence of substance indicates sham arrangements, leading to reattribution of income, denial of DTA relief under the Principal Purpose Test and potential CFC inclusion for the foreign shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to EUR 500,000 and five years' imprisonment under Article 1741 CGI; increased to EUR 1,000,000 and seven years for organised fraud; administrative fraud penalty of 40% or 80% of omitted tax base plus 0.20% monthly late-payment interest

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal conviction, confiscation of assets, prohibition to manage, French travel ban and civil liability for restoring hidden profits; the corporate veil may be pierced under Article L267 LPF to recover entity tax debts from directors acting in deliberate bad faith
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: EUR 15,000 penalty for omitted TP statement; reassessment under Article 57 CGI for hidden profit transfers; 5% increase on tax deficit for unavailable documentation; 40% penalty for gross negligence and 0.2% monthly interest thereafter

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structured non-real service fees are jointly liable for the tax deficiency; travel bans and exclusion from public contracts may be ordered in serious cases; criminal fraud charges may apply if TP documentation is forged
Shell company / sham structure

Statutory sanction: Application of Article L64 LPF (abus de droit) to disregard the sham and reassess shareholder-level distributions; 80% abusive arrangement penalty; refusal of treaty relief under the Principal Purpose Test; nullification of advantageous tax positions

Director personal liability: De facto managers may be held personally liable for French taxes, penalties and interest; the court may impose a travel ban, order civil restitution of distributed profits and permit a criminal fraud indictment when the sham was the instrument of the offence
Money laundering of commercial receipts

Statutory sanction: Article 324-1 French Penal Code: five years' imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine, increased to ten years and EUR 750,000 when habitual; corrective tax reassessment plus 80% penalty; asset freezing and confiscation orders

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal conviction, travel bans and asset seizure; the corporate veil is disregarded for the laundering instrument, and shareholders must demonstrate legitimate origin of distributed funds or restitution
