Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required under the Bermuda Companies Act 1981 for an exempted company. The company must have at least one share issued, and the memorandum must state the authorised share capital and the currency of shares. Any issued shares may be issued for such consideration as the board determines, subject to solvency requirements. No escrow account or paid-up capital deposit rule applies unless bank account opening requirements or specific regulated activity legislation (e.g. Investment Funds Act 2006, Digital Asset Business Act 2018) impose one; this entity is not so regulated.

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Bermuda Exempted Companies obtain a company registry number from the Registrar of Companies at incorporation. If the entity is a constituent entity of a multinational group with EUR 750 million or more consolidated revenue in at least two of the four preceding fiscal years, it must apply to the Office of the Tax Commissioner for a Bermuda Tax Identification Number (TIN) using Form CIT-R1 within 30 days of entering the CIT net. Bermuda does not issue VAT/GST registration IDs because no VAT/GST is levied. For non-CIT entities, the company registry number serves as the functional tax identifier for payroll tax, customs duty, and economic substance filings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar and is freely convertible for current account transactions under the Exchange Control Act 1972. Capital account transfers, including share capital injections and intra-group loans, require exchange control approval unless the exempted company is designated as 'non-resident' — a status commonly granted to exempted entities upon incorporation. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and loan repayments by a non-resident designated exempted company are unrestricted and exempt from BMA conversion mandates, provided the company maintains segregated foreign currency ledgers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1973 (as amended), resident entities must offer all exported goods and services proceeds in foreign currency to an authorized dealer (a BMA-licensed bank) within one month of receipt. A Bermuda Exempted Company with 'non-resident' designation is excluded from this surrender obligation and may retain export receivables, including software subscription revenue from the cloud platform, in any designated foreign currency without mandatory conversion to BMD. For non-designated entities, a BMA 'Commercial Exemption' can extend the surrender window to six months.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969; the UK Privy Council and HM Treasury retain ultimate sovereignty oversight for the British Overseas Territory, but the BMA is the sole statutory central bank, currency board, and exchange control administrator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. UK FCA-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Revolut, Wise) may provide GBP/USD/EUR multi-currency settlement accounts for outbound disbursements from the UK parent, but they are not permitted to open BMD-denominated or BMA-supervised clearing accounts absent a BMA deposit-taking license. No EU/UK EMI passporting arrangement exists for Bermuda; cross-border EMI services are legally limited to non-Bermuda traffic, and local merchant settlement in BMD requires a BMA-licensed bank sponsorship. Vertical SaaS must therefore maintain an underlying full commercial bank account in Bermuda for currency clearance, with EMIs used solely as a supplementary treasury sweep rail.

Statutory sanction: Under the U.K. Criminal Finances Act 2017, a corporate offence for failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion carries unlimited fines. Bermuda may also revoke exempted status, impose civil penalties, and deregister the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or facilitate tax evasion may be personally liable, subject to travel bans, seizure of assets, and imprisonment for up to 14 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Bermuda's Economic Substance Regulations, fines apply for non-compliance; persistent breach can lead to strike-off and liquidation of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified from acting as directors and held personally liable for penalties if they signed false declarations of compliance.
AML/CFT Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Bermuda's Proceeds of Crime Act and Anti-Money Laundering Regulations, regulatory fines can be imposed; criminal prosecution may occur for wilful blindness or facilitation.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers may be criminally liable for money laundering offences, with penalties including fines, forfeiture of assets, and imprisonment of up to 14 years.
