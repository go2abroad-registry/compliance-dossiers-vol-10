Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC) — administering payroll tax, land tax, and the 15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax (BCIT) under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023; Registrar of Companies (RoC) for incorporation of Exempted Companies; Bermuda Government e-services portal (www.gov.bm) for tax filings; Bermuda Customs Department for customs duty and ASYCUDA World clearance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Exempted Company with the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 1981 (no minimum capital, 100% foreign ownership permitted). Step 2: Obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and, where applicable, a Tax Assurance Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (confirms zero tax on income, capital gains, and dividends until 31 March 2035, subject to BCIT override). Step 3: If the entity is a constituent entity of an MNE group with annual consolidated turnover of at least €750M in two of the four preceding fiscal years, register for BCIT with the Tax Commissioner and obtain a Bermuda Tax Identification Number (BTIN) within 30 days of the trigger; otherwise no income-tax registration is required. Step 4: Register for payroll tax with the OTC if the Bermuda company has any Bermuda-resident employees or directors; register for customs duty account with the Bermuda Customs Department if importing goods. Step 5: File the annual Economic Substance Declaration and annual return with the RoC via the Companies eService portal.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Step 1: Incorporate the Exempted Company with the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 1981 (no minimum capital, 100% foreign ownership permitted). Step 2: Obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and, where applicable, a Tax Assurance Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (confirms zero tax on income, capital gains, and dividends until 31 March 2035, subject to BCIT override). Step 3: If the entity is a constituent entity of an MNE group with annual consolidated turnover of at least €750M in two of the four preceding fiscal years, register for BCIT with the Tax Commissioner and obtain a Bermuda Tax Identification Number (BTIN) within 30 days of the trigger; otherwise no income-tax registration is required. Step 4: Register for payroll tax with the OTC if the Bermuda company has any Bermuda-resident employees or directors; register for customs duty account with the Bermuda Customs Department if importing goods. Step 5: File the annual Economic Substance Declaration and annual return with the RoC via the Companies eService portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Bermuda; entity classification is fixed by Bermuda corporate law. Under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, in-scope MNE groups may elect: (a) to apply a domestic minimum top-up tax on Bermuda constituent entities (OECD Pillar Two transitional safe harbour); (b) to approve a particular financial accounting standard (IFRS vs US GAAP) for BCIT profit computation; (c) to treat a local entity as a qualified resident entity for CIT group filing; and (d) to exclude a notional entity via the qualified income test. The UK hybrid mismatch rules do not apply to a standalone Bermuda entity absent a UK connection.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None for Bermuda — no VAT, GST, or sales/use tax is levied in the jurisdiction. Bermuda imposes customs import duties, payroll tax, and stamp duty; the UK VAT (20%) is not in force in Bermuda and a Bermuda Exempted Company has no UK indirect tax nexus absent a UK fixed establishment or UK suppliers/customers.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Bermuda. The UK VAT registration threshold (currently GBP 90,000, subject to periodic adjustment) is relevant only if the Bermuda company carries on a taxable business in the UK through a fixed establishment, makes taxable supplies of services with a UK business recipient, or acquires goods physically present in the UK.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST, so no export VAT zero-rating or refund mechanism is required for Bermuda-originated supplies; exports are outside the scope of any indirect tax. If the Bermuda Exempted Company later registers for UK VAT (e.g., via a UK branch), zero-rating may apply to: (i) supply of goods exported to a non-UK destination within a prescribed period; (ii) chartering or leasing of ships used for international commercial transport; and (iii) B2B supplies of offshore marine services to non-UK customers falling outside the UK place-of-supply rules. All zero-rating claims require valid commercial evidence retained for at least 6 years.

  • Bermuda: no export customs declaration is required (non-VAT territory)
  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / National Export System) — required only for UK-registered supplies leaving the UK
  • Bill of Lading or duly executed charter party / vessel lease agreement evidencing international use
  • Commercial invoice and offshore supply contract (ISIC 5222)
  • Proof of departure: port terminal log, export consignment record, or navigation evidence for the vessel
  • Inward remittance certificate (SWIFT MT103) or bank statement evidencing settlement
  • Insurance certificate and classification society records supporting international voyage (best practice)

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping requirement exists in Bermuda's indirect tax system. Import declarations are filed electronically through the Bermuda Customs Department's ASYCUDA World customs clearance platform. If UK VAT registration arises separately, the company must comply with UK Making Tax Digital (MTD) requirements for VAT — including functional-compatible software and digital record-keeping — but no UK e-invoicing clearance is mandated for standard VAT returns.

In the UK context (if UK VAT zero-rating is claimed), false or unsupported zero-rating gives rise to interest, clawback of output tax, and a penalty of up to 100% of the VAT evaded (reducible to 30% for careless error), with retrospective assessments of 4 years (extended to 20 years for deliberate concealment) and potential criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion. In Bermuda, misdeclaration to the Customs Department is an offence under the Customs Act 1968, leading to seizure/forfeiture of goods and penalties of up to three times the duty evaded; in-scope MNE groups making false BCIT filings face a penalty not exceeding 150% of the tax understated, plus statutory late-filing penalties, and directors may be personally liable for knowingly assisting evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar, providing de facto full convertibility for trade and treasury flows. Bermuda's Exchange Control Act 1972 differentiates between resident and non-resident entities for exchange control purposes. A Bermuda Exempted Company, when beneficially owned by non-residents and not carrying on business in Bermuda, is generally classified as non-resident, thereby allowing unrestricted holding of foreign currency accounts, unhindered capital account transfers, and free profit or dividend remittance to overseas shareholders. Convertibility on the current account is effectively absolute; on the capital account, the BMA retains oversight but does not impose routine approval for exempted companies. The only practical hurdles are documentary compliance under anti-money laundering rules and the requirement to demonstrate that the underlying vessel-leasing transactions are genuinely offshore in character.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for a Bermuda Exempted Company. Charter hire income invoiced and received in US dollars may be retained in US dollar accounts at Bermuda banks or any external correspondent bank without obligation to convert into Bermuda dollars. The BMA's exchange control framework requires only statistical reporting for individual foreign currency transactions above BM$1,000,000; this is a monitoring requirement, not a surrender or rationing obligation. Accordingly, the offshore supply vessel leasing treasury can operate with full US dollar pass-through unfettered by local remittance restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, subject to legal and operational constraints. A Bermuda Exempted Company may engage UK or US-regulated fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) for initiating and receiving cross-border payments, provided the EMI does not maintain a physical place of business or solicit deposits in Bermuda, which would trigger the BMA Banking Act licensing requirement. EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Payoneer can be used for low-value charter-hire collections and vendor payments; however, they are not acceptable as the primary clearing rail for medium-risk maritime leasing under Bermuda's Proceeds of Crime Regulations. The BMA-regulated settlement banks will generally reject an EMI as a correspondent banking intermediary unless the EMI holds segregated client funds at a top-tier global bank and provides full transaction auditability. Furthermore, the shipping sector triggers enhanced sanctions screening; many EMIs automatically decline maritime-chartering merchants. Therefore, the EMI channel should be limited to ancillary disbursements while maintaining the BMA-regulated bank account as the main treasury spine.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder loan capital must be routed via SWIFT wire transfer (MT103) from the parent or investor's corporate bank account to the Bermuda Exempted Company's USD operating account maintained at a BMA-regulated bank. The payment instruction must state a clear commercial purpose in Field 70, e.g., 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN DISBURSEMENT', and reference the executed capital injection agreement. For amounts exceeding US$500,000, the receiving Bermuda bank will require an advance notification package comprising a board resolution, an updated beneficial ownership register, source-of-wealth evidence, and confirmation that the contribution will be booked as paid-in capital or intercompany loan in the entity's financial statements. No BMA pre-approval is required, but the bank will file a currency transaction report for any transfer above BM$1,000,000. Clearing is typically executed through the bank's New York correspondent via Fedwire or CHIPS; for UK-sourced funds, FPS or CHAPS to London then onward SWIFT to Bermuda is acceptable.Outbound disbursements for vessel operations — including bunker fuel, crew payroll, port fees, insurance premiums, and repair services — should use the SWIFT MT103 message type to the payee's bank, cleared via CHIPS or Fedwire in US dollars. For larger supply contracts and equipment procurement, the standard clearing rail is an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 inline with the underlying purchase order. Charter hire received from international oil majors may arrive as SWIFT MT103 credits to the operating account; the bank may sweep these into an offshore maritime escrow account for consecutive disbursement authority. To minimise friction, the Bermuda treasury should maintain a controlled disbursement account with same-day overdraft and a real-time balance API. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a Bermuda Exempted Company outside Pillar Two scope; 15% Corporate Income Tax (CIT) under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025, for MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million, applied via the Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (QDMTT) and Income Inclusion Rule (IIR).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an exempted company to non-resident shareholders, regardless of shareholding percentage or share class.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Mandatory only for in-scope MNE groups: the CIT return must be filed within 15 months after the end of the first fiscal year subject to CIT and within 12 months after the end of each subsequent fiscal year; fiscal year equals the entity's accounting period reflected in audited financial statements.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due no later than 31 January for companies with a 31 December fiscal year end; for other accounting periods, within six months after the fiscal year end, as prescribed under the Companies Act 1981.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 5,000,000 per annum of related-party revenue or expense triggering the Local File requirement (BMD is pegged 1:1 USD); Master File required when consolidated group revenue reaches BMD 1,000,000,000; Country-by-Country Report mandated when the MNE group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Out-of-scope exempted companies are not CIT-transfer-pricing mandated but must still contemporaneously evidence arm's length charter rates for any tax authority enquiry, maritime escrow due diligence, or UK CFC investigation.

Economic substance scope: The International Tax Compliance (Economic Substance) Act 2018 applies to exempted companies carrying on 'shipping' as a relevant activity, which expressly includes chartering and operating offshore platform support vessels. The entity must demonstrate in Bermuda: (i) core income-generating activities comprising vessel charter negotiation, fleet deployment, voyage direction, crewing, and marine superintendent oversight; (ii) suitable full-time employees physically present in Bermuda with maritime qualifications; (iii) adequate operating expenditure on Bermudian premises and equipment; and (iv) board meetings held physically in Bermuda with a majority of directors present and substantive minutes recorded. An annual Economic Substance Return must be filed. Failure results in exchange-of-information reporting to the UK and EU and potential loss of the Tax Exemption Certificate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic Substance Failure / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: First offence: financial penalty of BMD 250,000 and an official ESR non-compliance determination; continuing default attracts a further BMD 250,000 per year plus BMD 10,000 monthly uplifts; second offence within five years: penalty up to BMD 500,000; the Registrar must exchange the non-compliance file with the UK Revenue to expose the group to Pillar Two top-up tax in the parent jurisdiction; the Tax Exemption Certificate is suspended or revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers face pecuniary penalties up to BMD 25,000 each and daily default fines of BMD 2,000; knowingly falsifying the ESR return constitutes an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years; travel bans and asset freeze orders may issue where non-compliance is linked to profit stripping into a zero-tax jurisdiction.
CIT Evasion and Fraudulent Non-Filing (In-Scope MNE Groups)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 200% of the evaded CIT; interest at 1.5% per month; the BTA may impose a compliance penalty of BMD 250,000, obtain court orders for production of books, and recharacterise unsubstantiated management fees as deemed dividends; the recharacterised distribution remains tax-free in Bermuda but generates unreported foreign-source income in the UK shareholder's hands.

Director personal liability: Officers who approve the false CIT return are jointly and severally liable for the evaded top-up tax; criminal conviction on indictment carries imprisonment up to 7 years and an unlimited fine; the UK parent remains exposed under the GloBE Income Inclusion Rule and UK CFC rules, creating secondary corporate liability for the Bermuda subsidiary's shifted profits.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Amendment) Act 2022, conviction on indictment attracts a fine of BMD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; failure to maintain the Beneficial Ownership Register triggers civil penalties of BMD 50,000; the Bermuda Monetary Authority may direct the freezing of bank accounts; maritime escrow is terminated and the vessel becomes liable to arrest in foreign jurisdictions.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for knowing AML defaults, regulatory disqualification orders barring service as a director for up to 10 years, and travel restriction notices during investigation; personal restitution orders require the unwinding of vessel sale-and-leaseback proceeds traceable to director accounts.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Defeat

Statutory sanction: The BTA may adjust charter consideration by reference to third-party fixtures; upward adjustments for in-scope CIT years attract a 25% documentation penalty plus interest; secondary adjustments recharacterise the shifted margin as a constructive dividend, which, while untaxed in Bermuda, exposes the UK shareholder to an unreported distribution; reciprocal enforcement is available under the UK-Bermuda TIEA.

Director personal liability: Directors signing the CIT return without verifying transfer pricing documentation face a BMD 100,000 administrative fine; persistent absence of documentation supports a UK general anti-abuse finding and a CFC charge at up to 13% additional UK corporation tax on Bermuda profits; directors may face cross-border civil proceedings in the UK High Court for restitution of the tax shortfall.
