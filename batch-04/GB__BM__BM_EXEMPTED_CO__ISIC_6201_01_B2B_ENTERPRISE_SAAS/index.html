Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the United States Dollar (USD) and is fully backed by US Dollar reserves; current account transactions (trade in services, SaaS licences, royalty payments) are freely convertible for entities granted non-resident status. Capital account transactions (share capital injections, intercompany loans, distribution of dividends on liquidation) require prior 'specific permission' under the Exchange Control Act, but Bermuda exempted companies with non-resident status routinely receive broad general permission (Category 1 or 2 advantage) allowing unimpeded conversion and remittance of profits and capital in USD. For SaaS revenue collected in non-USD currencies (GBP/EUR), the exemption permits conversion through an authorised bank at the prevailing market rate with no surrender obligation; the practical hurdle is limited to submitting a compliance form and maintaining an Authorised Dealer relationship with a Bermuda-licensed bank or the Crown Agents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory default under the Exchange Control Act 1972 requires all export proceeds to be converted into BMD within three months of the date of export and to be surrendered to an Authorised Dealer; however, a Bermuda exempted company that has obtained non-resident status from the BMA is generally exempt from mandatory surrender and may retain export receipts in USD, GBP, or any approved foreign currency provided the funds are credited to a Foreign Currency Account (FCA) held at an Authorised Dealer. The company should file a Declaration of Non-Residence (Form ECE/1) and, in practice, the BMA provides a routine blanket permission for SaaS/technology businesses allowing open retention of overseas receivables, subject to quarterly reporting to the authorised bank in the form of a 'Statement of Export Proceeds'. Failure to obtain the non-resident declaration could trigger a surrender obligation, so the legal entity must execute the declaratory certificate at incorporation and include it in the bank's jurisdiction file.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) acting under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1957 (as amended); the BMA is the sole licensing and supervisory body for banks, trust companies, and investment businesses in Bermuda, and administers exchange control as 'Exchange Control' authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH with restrictions. The Bermuda Exempted Company has no in-country EMI (Electronic Money Institution) license requirement to hold accounts at foreign-licensed fintechs, and the BMA does not block offshore EMI onboarding; however, Bermuda exchange control applied to resident entities would require an Authorised Dealer in the loop, unless non-resident status is granted. In practice, the most viable route is opening a UK FCA-authorised EMI account (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, or ClearBank via banking-as-a-service) and layering it beneath the Bermuda entity's non-resident account at Butterfield or HSBC; the EMI should be used strictly for inbound SaaS subscription collections in GBP/EUR with same-day conversion to USD and onward sweep to the Bermuda bank. Legal constraint: UK EMIs are subject to the UK's 'payment account access' rules and the FCA's 'safeguarding' requirements; the Bermuda entity must ensure the EMI agreement is structured as an 'e-money account' and not a 'credit facility' (which would trigger BMA banking licensing), and the EMI's safeguarding arrangements must be evidenced by an annual attestation letter from the EMI´s auditor to the Bermuda board on a best-efforts basis.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership opacity: Bermuda Exempted Company with nominee shareholders or a trust/SPV upstream holding layer without a transparent waterfall report, which triggers enhanced due diligence (EDD) and potential bank rejection under the BMA's AML/ATF Code of Practice section 2.3.

Defense evidence: Provide (1) an organisational chart from the ultimate individual beneficial owner through all intermediate entities, each annotated with entity type, jurisdiction, and shareholding percentage; (2) a certified copy of the register of members filed at the Bermuda Registrar of Companies; (3) a full trust deed or declaration of trust (if any) naming the settlor, trustee, and protectors, with a legal opinion from a Bermuda counsel confirming the trust is governed by Bermuda law and does not frustrate the identification of a 'beneficial owner' under the BMA's Beneficial Ownership (Companies) Regulations 2021; (4) a source-of-wealth certificate executed by each individual with 25% or greater ownership, detailing the provenance of funds used in any prior capital injection; and (5) a corporate authorisation resolution from all intermediate entities confirming that the ultimate controller is the sole signatory power for the Bermuda entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol uses the SWIFT MT103 via Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda with the following purpose codes: (1) share capital subscription — 'STRO', narrative 'BVI/UK US$ XX,XXX equity injection of common shares of the Bermuda Exempted Company' with a certified board resolution attached; (2) shareholder loan — 'LOAN', narrative 'Intercompany loan subordination acknowledgement' and use cash collateral arrangement with a 12-month term, documented by a loan agreement executed by both parties; (3) SaaS licence fee remittance received from the UK operating company as pass-through of B2B end-customer subscriptions — 'ICT' (services) / 'SOFT' (software), using the customer-specific invoice number in field 72 (Remittance Information). To verify, the bank will require (at opening) a 'Standard Instruction Letter' naming the shareholder, the remitter bank, and the AML classification of the incoming wire, plus a commitment that any inbound wire above USD 100,000 will be accompanied by the underlying SaaS contract and customer order form at the first occurrence.Preferred clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for vendor payments, contractor salaries, and intercompany dividend remittances, with copy of invoice and tax clearance (for dividends only — BMA does not require withholding clearance since Bermuda has zero withholding tax, but the bank will request a solvency certificate from directors); (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C at sight, issued through Butterfield's correspondent network, commonly drawee in London/New York) for material capital expenditure (e.g., hardware co-location or HPC procurement) — always use an irrevocable, confirmed L/C subject to UCP 600 with a 5% margin deposited as collateral; (3) intra-group sweeps are executed via SWIFT MT101/MT103 with netting only if a master netting agreement (ISDA/ISDA Credit Support Annex) is in place, otherwise each intercompany invoice must be individually settled; (4) for high-frequency low-value disbursements to UK contractors, the same-day GBP payment using FPS/CHAPS via the UK partner account at HSBC UK (HSBC Bank Bermuda has a UK branch and can sweep same-day if instructed before 12:00 UTC). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (standard Bermuda exempted company); 15% for in-scope multinational enterprise groups under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CITA) for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025 where consolidated group revenue is at least EUR 750 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Bermuda does not impose a withholding tax on dividends paid by an exempted company, and no Bermuda capital duty or documentary tax applies to the declaration or payment of a dividend.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda domestic transfer pricing legislation applies to standard exempted companies; therefore no local-file or master-file threshold exists in BMD. For in-scope MNE groups under CITA 2023 (consolidated revenue of BMD 1,200 million, being approximately EUR 750 million), the Bermuda taxpayer must maintain contemporaneous documentation equivalent to the OECD TP Guidelines, including a group master file, local file, and country-by-country report, and must provide these records within 30 days of a Tax Commissioner request. For all exempted companies, the economic substance review requires a functional and risk analysis of the Bermuda entity; a formal transfer pricing study is mandatory where the entity books intercompany revenue or cost-sharing arrangements.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Requirements for Attribution of Profits) Act 2018 and the related Economic Substance Regulations impose a substantive nexus test on Bermuda exempted companies deriving income from 'relevant activities'. The listed relevant activities are: banking, insurance, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, shipping, distribution and service centre, intellectual property holding, and pure equity holding. A B2B enterprise SaaS company that merely bills third-party customers through Bermuda is not automatically a relevant activity; however, a SaaS operator that exploits proprietary software code, trademark, or other IP rights from Bermuda will be classified as an 'IP business', requiring (i) local economic substance (employees, premises, and expenditure adequate to the IP exploitation), (ii) core income-generating activities performed in Bermuda (research and development, strategic IP maintenance, and the active conduct of IP protection and enforcement), and (iii) a modified-nexus profit attribution that limits the IP-derived income eligible for Bermuda's zero-rate regime. The company must file an Economic Substance Declaration annually; a failure to satisfy the substance test results in information exchange with the UK, EU member states, and the OECD, and recharacterisation of the Bermuda entity as a shell with consequent disallowance of the zero-rate treatment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Act 2018: initial penalty BMD 2,500; continuing default BMD 500 per day to BMD 50,000; false declaration BMD 5,000; strike-off by the Registrar and dissolution of the company; information exchange with foreign tax authorities under the EU and OECD mechanisms.

Director personal liability: Director personal liability extends to civil restitution of penalties, disgorgement of benefits derived from the shell structure, a 5-year director disqualification order, a travel ban issued by the Supreme Court of Bermuda, and imprisonment of directors up to 12 months for knowingly providing false statutory declarations.
Tax Evasion (CITA 2023 and Foreign Tax Offences)

Statutory sanction: CITA 2023: civil fraud penalty of 100% of understated tax, interest at 8% per annum, a fine of up to BMD 50,000, and imprisonment up to 5 years. Under the UK Criminal Finances Act 2017, a UK parent or partner that fails to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion by the Bermuda entity may face an unlimited fine, confinement of corporate assets, and a prohibition on distributed payments.

Director personal liability: Directors acting with knowledge or reckless indifference are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, penalties, and interest; they may be subject to a worldwide Mareva injunction, UK HMRC civil tax-geared penalties above the Bermuda amounts, a passport suspension and travel ban, and extradition proceedings under the Bermuda-US Extradition Treaty.
AML and Proceeds of Crime Violations

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act 1997: money laundering carries up to 14 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine; failure to file a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) carries up to 5 years' imprisonment on the responsible officer; the company's bank accounts may be frozen and the Financial Intelligence Agency may issue a freezing order; the corporate entity can be subjected to confiscation and civil recovery of the entire property laundered.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Compliance Officer are criminally liable for 'tipping off' when warning a suspected client; directors face personal liability for the company's debts under the Companies Act where the AML breach constitutes fraudulent trading, and they may be detained under the Bermuda Emergencies and Police Act with a travel ban, surrender of passports, and restraint orders over Bermudian and overseas trust assets.
Fraudulent Trading and Breach of Directors' Duties

Statutory sanction: Companies Act 1981 (as applied to exempted companies): a court may declare that the persons knowingly party to the fraudulent trading are liable to make such contribution to the company's assets as the court thinks proper; a disqualification order of up to 15 years may be made; the company's certificate of incorporation may be revoked on the grounds of fraud, removing the shield of limited liability.

Director personal liability: Civil liability: restitution to the company for the full amount dissipated, compensation to creditors, and interest; criminal liability: imprisonment up to 10 years and a fine of BMD 50,000; personal routes include a summary attachment of Bermudian real property, a travel ban, and the director's removal from all Bermuda companies in which they hold an interest.
