Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Office of the Tax Commissioner, Bermuda (taxpayer services and payroll tax); Registrar of Companies, Bermuda (corporate registry); Bermuda Customs Department, Ministry of Finance (customs and trade duties); all filings transacted via Bermuda Business Portal (bizportal.gov.bm).

        
E-government filing portal
A Bermuda Exempted Company is incorporated via the Registrar of Companies on the Bermuda Business Portal, obtaining a unique Bermuda Company Number; this number serves as the base corporate identifier. Tax-specific registration: (1) apply to the Office of the Tax Commissioner for a Bermuda Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) at incorporation or immediately thereafter, required for payroll tax, stamp duty, and customs account purposes; (2) upon hiring the first employee, register as an employer for payroll tax within 14 days via the Bermuda Business Portal (Payroll Tax Rate 10% to 14.75% of remuneration bands, non-owner managers up to 27.75%); (3) register as an importer of record with the Bermuda Customs Department if importing clinical diagnostic ELISA assay reagents (HS 3822 / HS 3002), obtaining a Customs Duty Account number; (4) no VAT, sales tax, or corporate income tax registration applies at entity level, except that Bermuda's Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CITA) imposes registration and annual filing obligations on a Bermuda member of an in-scope multinational group whose consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million, effective for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2025; (5) for invoiced exports of goods exceeding BMD 50,000 in value, obtain exchange control approval from the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) under the Exchange Control Act 1972, given the company's non-resident owned status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone

        
Available election forms
A Bermuda Exempted Company may obtain a Tax Exemption Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, guaranteeing statutory exemption from all Bermuda taxes (corporate income tax, capital gains tax, VAT, and withholding taxes) until 31 March 2035. Under CITA 2023, in-scope multinational groups may elect transitional Pillar Two safe harbour rules, elect the simplified calculation method, and designate the ultimate parent entity as the responsible registration and filing party; no US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Bermuda law. Companies may elect Bermuda dollar (BMD) or foreign functional currency reporting for payroll tax and CITA purposes, subject to BMA approval.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
None (Bermuda levies no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax); applicable indirect instruments are Customs Duties on imports (13.25% standard, 25% on certain goods, with duty-free or reduced rates for medical diagnostic reagents under the Customs Tariff for laboratory consumables), Payroll Tax (10% to 27.75% on employer remuneration), and Stamp Duty on specified legal instruments.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not Applicable - Bermuda prescribes no turnover threshold for VAT/GST because no such tax exists. Customs Duty registration is mandatory per transaction for importers; Payroll Tax registration is triggered upon hiring the first Bermuda-based employee; CITA registration applies only to multinational groups with consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 750 million.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Not applicable - Bermuda operates no VAT/GST system and therefore no export zero-rating mechanism or input VAT refund regime exists. Exports of clinical diagnostic ELISA assays and reagents from Bermuda attract zero Bermuda indirect taxation by default. Substantiation for exemption from Bermuda customs duty (where goods are temporarily imported and re-exported) requires a Temporary Importation Entry and Re-export Certificate; for the destination jurisdiction (e.g., United Kingdom, EU, or other), the Bermuda exporter must supply standard commercial documentation supporting the importer's VAT deferral or import VAT zero-rating claim, including commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and certificate of origin.

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate applies in Bermuda. The Bermuda Business Portal (bizportal.gov.bm) is the sole approved digital filing platform for corporate, payroll tax, customs, and stamp duty submissions; import customs declarations are lodged electronically via the Bermuda Customs Automated System (BCAS); no real-time clearance is imposed on exports or on B2B invoicing for clinical diagnostic reagents.

      
As no VAT zero-rating applies, no fraudulent zero-rating clawback exists in Bermuda. Relevant penalties: (1) Customs duty evasion under the Customs Tariff Act 1970 attracts fines of up to three times the evaded duty, forfeiture of goods, and potential criminal prosecution; (2) Payroll tax mis-declarations attract fines up to BMD 5,000 plus a 10% surcharge on unpaid tax; (3) CITA 2023 under-reporting by an in-scope multinational attracts penalties up to 115% of the tax undercharged plus statutory interest; (4) Exchange control violations under the Exchange Control Act 1972 are punishable by fines and imprisonment, and failure to repatriate export proceeds within the approved period may result in BMA enforcement, revocation of exchange control approval, and monetary penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar (USD) and is freely interchangeable via authorised dealers. Bermuda maintains formal capital controls, but a Bermuda Exempted Company designated as non-resident under the Exchange Control Act 1972 achieves full current-account and capital-account convertibility without BMA transaction approval. Profit repatriation, dividend distribution, and intercompany loan repayments are permitted without delay for designated entities, subject only to standard bank documentary review (board resolution, statutory financial statements, and remittance instruction). Non-designated resident entities face case-by-case BMA consent for outward transfers; therefore, maintaining non-resident designation is mandatory for frictionless clearing of reagent-distribution receipts.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Baseline rule under the Exchange Control Regulations 1973: resident exporters must offer all foreign-currency export proceeds to an authorised dealer in Bermuda within six months of receipt and any non-BMD balances must be converted to BMD unless an exemption is granted. Mandatory conversion ratio for resident entities: 100% of export proceeds. For a Bermuda Exempted Company designated as non-resident and holding external accounts: surrender obligation is disapplied, the conversion ratio is 0%, and receipts in USD, EUR, or GBP may be retained offshore or swept to the UK parent without BMA filing. Export documentation (commercial invoice, airway bill, packing list) must be retained for five years for bank compliance audits.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969; administers exchange controls under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, and supervises licensed banks under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold in Bermuda; no income tax or transfer pricing adjustment regime applies to exempted companies. However, a Bermuda-resident entity that is a member of a multinational group must comply with the OECD Pillar Two GloBE rules, which may require domestic top-up tax reporting if the group's revenue exceeds EUR 750 million; related-party transactions must be priced at arm's length for purposes of determining GloBE profits, and the Bermuda Economic Substance (ESR) regime requires that any 'relevant activity' be undertaken with adequate substance in Bermuda, including senior management presence and physical premises.

      
Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Amendment Regulations 2018 and the Economic Substance (Requirements and Compliance) Regulations apply to financial and distribution activities. A Bermuda exempted company engaged in wholesale distribution of clinical diagnostic ELISA assays and reagents that claims 'tax resident' status in another jurisdiction must be able to demonstrate that the core income-generating activities (negotiation of contracts, management of logistics, and assumption of distribution risk) are performed in Bermuda, unless it is a pure holding vehicle or is tax resident and taxed in the UK under the UK's territorial rules. The company must file an annual ESR return, and non-compliance triggers BD$250,000 penalty (approximately GBP 250,000) plus a potential seven-year imprisonment for officers for a willful default.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Civil recovery of unpaid levy plus interest at 1.5% monthly, and a fine of BD$25,000 (approximately GBP 25,000) for a false declaration; the Bermuda Registrar of Companies may strike off the entity and prohibit re-registration for two years.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be subject to personal liability for the levy debt if they caused the company to trade after insolvency or knowingly signed false declarations; criminal prosecution may result in a prison sentence of up to five years, a fine of up to BD$50,000 (approximately GBP 50,000), and a Bermuda court order banning the director from acting as a director for any Bermuda-registered company for ten years. A travel ban may be imposed where outstanding fiscal penalties exceed BD$5,000.

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure
        
Statutory sanction: Immediate ESR enforcement action: penalty of BD$250,000 (approximately GBP 250,000) for first breach, yearly penalty of BD$500,000 (approximately GBP 500,000) for continued non-compliance, potential strike-off, and exchange of information to the UK HMRC under the Bermuda-UK Tax Information Exchange Agreement.

        
Director personal liability: A director who knowingly fails to file the ESR return or makes a false substance declaration is personally liable to a fine of BD$10,000 (approximately GBP 10,000) and imprisonment for up to seven years; the Supreme Court of Bermuda may also order disgorgement of profits, a five-year directorship ban, and a travel restriction pending the resolution of the enforcement case.

      

      
      

        AML/ATF and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Amendment (Supervision and Enforcement) Act 2020, penalties include a fine of up to BD$1,000,000 (approximately GBP 1,000,000) and confiscation of the proceeds of the transaction; the beneficial ownership register may be publicly struck or the entity dissolved.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face criminal prosecution under the Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Code; imprisonment of up to ten years and an unlimited fine; the director's personal assets may be subject to civil recovery where the corporate veil is pierced for fraud, and a travel ban is automatic while an arrest warrant or extradition request is outstanding.

      

      
      

        Regulatory Licensing and Product Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: In Bermuda, the Department of Health and the Customs Department may seize the products and impose a fine of BD$50,000 per batch; the Bermuda court may issue a prohibition order against the company; if the activities constitute the unlicensed practice of medical device distribution, criminal penalties up to BD$100,000 apply.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable in negligence for harm caused by defective diagnostic reagents under Bermuda tort law; the court may pierce the veil because the company was a mere facade of the distributor, leading to an order for personal damages, a five-year directorship disqualification, and, in cases involving consumer harm, imprisonment of up to two years.
