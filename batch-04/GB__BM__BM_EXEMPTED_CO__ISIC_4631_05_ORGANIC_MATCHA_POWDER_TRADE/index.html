E-government filing portal

Bermuda imposes no corporate income tax, therefore no national tax TIN or VAT ID is procured for a Bermuda Exempted Company. On incorporation the Registrar of Companies issues a Certificate of Incorporation and the company automatically qualifies for tax-exempt status. For formal assurance, the company files an application under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 with the Minister of Finance to obtain a Tax Exemption Certificate (Tax Assurance). Payroll tax registration (with an employer registration number) is required only if the company employs staff in Bermuda; otherwise no statutory tax identifier is issued. Non-resident owners need no additional fiscal registration for ordinary shareholding or distribution activities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda's legal tender is the Bermudian dollar (BMD), pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar (USD). The Exchange Control Act 1972 and subsidiary regulations impose a regime of administrative exchange control, but a Bermuda Exempted Company that has been granted 'non-resident' designation by the BMA is treated as resident outside the Sterling Area for exchange control purposes. As such, it may freely maintain, receive, and disburse foreign currency accounts, execute capital account transactions including equity injections and shareholder loans, and remit dividends, profits, and loan repayments without being subject to conversion limits or prior approval requirements. Current account transactions (trade settlements, service fees, commissions) are fully convertible in BMD/USD and in any major world currency. The only de facto hurdle is ensuring all transfers move through a BMA-licensed authorized dealer (typically the commercial bank), and that required inspection/declaration forms are filed where applicable. Profit remittance is unrestricted for exempted companies, and no withholding taxes are imposed by Bermuda law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a Bermuda Exempted Company with non-resident designation, there is no statutory surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio. Foreign currency export receipts may be held in offshore or onshore foreign currency accounts in any denomination, and may be used directly to settle trade payables or converted to BMD/USD at will. For comparison, a Bermuda resident company (not designated non-resident) is generally obliged to offer any foreign currency received to an authorized dealer within a specified period, normally 90 days, unless a general or specific exemption is obtained from the BMA. The subject entity, being an Exempted Company and presumed designated non-resident for the purposes of its certificate, falls outside this resident requirement; therefore no surrender timelines apply to its matcha powder export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., UK-licensed EMIs, EEA payment institutions, or neobank platforms). Legal constraints: Bermuda's banking regulatory framework requires the BMA to license deposit-taking business; offshore EMIs are not automatically recognized as 'banks' under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 and typically cannot provide the escrow-grade corporate banking services required for an exempted company. Further, Bermuda's AML/CFT regime imposes serious restrictions on maintaining accounts with unregistered or remotely supervised payment intermediaries; a Bermuda Exempted Company that opens an operational account at a foreign EMI without a physical relationship may trigger enhanced scrutiny by the BMA and may jeopardize its non-resident designation. A more viable alternative is a BMA-regulated 'digital asset business' or class-E licensee with an explicit sandbox permission to accept deposits, but such institutions are not yet standard Tier 1 clearing venues. Therefore, the corporate treasury blueprint should treat offshore EMIs only as a supplementary liquidity pass-through, never as the primary clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital injection into the Bermuda Exempted Company: (i) the beneficial owner sends funds from a bank in their home jurisdiction via SWIFT MT103 to the bank account of the exempted company at a Tier 1 Bermudian bank; (ii) the wire must reference the purpose code for equity injection or shareholder loan — in standard SWIFT practice this is transmitted in field 70 (Remittance Information) as 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN – CAPITAL INJECTION'; (iii) where the receiving bank is Butterfield or HSBC, an advance electronic notice (e.g., SWIFT MT199/MT195) may be required for amounts above USD 250,000 to allow pre-screening of sanctions and source-of-funds; (iv) the exempted company's board must pass a resolution either accepting the capital injection as share premium or recording it as a formal shareholder loan with agreed interest terms; (v) BMA reporting is generally not required for non-resident designated exempted companies, but a Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) and Common Reporting Standard (CRS) self-certification must be on file. All wires must originate from a bank account beneficially owned by the shareholder or from a recognized financial institution; intragroup transfers from a third-party payment processor will trigger enhanced due diligence and are not recommended.Clearing rails for outbound trade settlements, specifically the purchase of organic matcha powder from producers and export to global distributors: (a) SWIFT MT103 — the primary rail for settlement of open-account trade invoices; the instruction must include the 11-character SWIFT/BIC of the receiving bank, IBAN or account number of beneficiary, purpose code such as 'GDS - goods purchased' or '1301 - general merchandise trade', and complete product description; (b) Commercial documentary letter of credit (LC) — used when a matcha producer or trading counterparty requires guaranteed payment; the Bermudian bank issues an irrevocable LC via SWIFT MT700, often confirmed by a Japanese correspondent bank for matcha producers; (c) Documentary collections (drafts / Bills for Collection via SWIFT MT400) are acceptable for smaller consignments; and (d) Escrow-based trade disbursement through HSBC or Butterfield's trade finance desk for large contract lots, with inspections being verified by SGS or comparable certificate. All outbound payments must be supported by a purchase order, commercial invoice, and shipping documentation; sanctions screening of the beneficiary bank and counterparty is performed automatically but the treasury team should maintain a surety checklist in the ERP.Plain vanilla deliverable FX forwards (BBD/USD, USD/JPY, USD/KRW, and other freely traded pairs) via a Tier 1 Bermudian bank or its correspondent trading desk; non-resident designated exempted companies are exempt from central bank approval for these.FX options (European-style plain vanilla call/put options) negotiated with the commercial bank's derivatives desk, used to cap currency depreciation exposure on Japanese yen (JPY) or Korean won (KRW) procurement costs.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between USD and BMD are technically available given the 1:1 peg, but are not necessary; more relevant are JPY/USD cross-currency interest rate swaps applied when financing working capital trade lines in yen.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on illiquid or restricted currencies (e.g., for settlement in certain emerging market matcha resale markets) may be cleared through international bank desks in London or New York, but only where the underlying receipts are not in BMD; NDFs are not prohibited by the BMA for exempted companies, provided they are documented under ISDA and the account bank's risk committee approves the hedge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Vanuatu-Style Shell Abatement

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance Authority may impose a financial penalty of up to BMD 250,000 plus BMD 10,000 per day for ongoing non-compliance; UK HMRC may impose corporation tax at 19%-25% on worldwide profits, plus interest and penalties up to 100% of tax due, and potential criminal investigation under UK Finance Act 2019

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for civil restitution equal to taxes evaded; UK travel bans may be issued under HMRC civil recovery powers; imprisonment of up to 7 years under UK Criminal Finances Act 2017 for failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion
Money Laundering / AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Bermuda AML/ATF Act 2012 penalties up to BMD 1,000,000 for corporate entities; breach of Proceeds of Crime Act may result in forfeiture of assets and license revocation of the registered agent; UK Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 extraterritorial enforcement

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal liability with imprisonment up to 14 years for knowingly laundering proceeds; directors may be banned from holding corporate office for 10 years; personal monetary fines of up to BMD 500,000
Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to BMD 10,000 per offence, continuing daily fines of BMD 1,000, and potential striking-off of the exempted company under the Beneficial Ownership Act 2021

False Accounting / Material Misstatement
