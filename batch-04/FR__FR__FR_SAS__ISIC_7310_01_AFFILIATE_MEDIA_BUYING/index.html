Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title II – Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-30 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); general company provisions of Articles L. 210-1 et seq.; implementing Decree No. 2022-1297 on the digital single filing portal.

Formation / constitutional filings

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital applies to an SAS. The articles of association fix the share capital. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed; at least 50% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation, with the balance payable according to the articles (maximum five years). Funds are deposited in a blocked bank account, with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or with a notary until the RCS registration certificate is issued.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office must comply with the French Labor Code health and safety requirements for office premises. A fire safety certificate from the building owner or any applicable SDIS clearance is required for shared or tertiary buildings. Electrical installations must be verified in accordance with the Code du travail. If staff are employed, the company must register with the registered workplace health service (service de prévention et de santé au travail) and arrange periodic occupational health checks.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum capital applies to an SAS. The articles of association fix the share capital. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed; at least 50% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation, with the balance payable according to the articles (maximum five years). Funds are deposited in a blocked bank account, with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or with a notary until the RCS registration certificate is issued.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); online portal – https://www.impots.gouv.fr/ (business registration via https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr)

E-government filing portal

A domestic SAS obtains its SIREN/SIRET automatically from INSEE through the one-stop shop (Guichet unique) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr and receives a French intra-community VAT number upon process completion. A non-resident entity must file Form Cerfa 15163 (or the foreign-operator procedure) with DGFiP, providing constitutional documents, proof of foreign registration, director identification and powers of attorney, and, where required, appointing a fiscal representative ('représentant fiscal') responsible for French VAT and tax obligations. A provisional French TIN/VAT ID is issued within approximately 10 business days and a definitive VAT number is allocated after substance and activity verification.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A domestic SAS obtains its SIREN/SIRET automatically from INSEE through the one-stop shop (Guichet unique) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr and receives a French intra-community VAT number upon process completion. A non-resident entity must file Form Cerfa 15163 (or the foreign-operator procedure) with DGFiP, providing constitutional documents, proof of foreign registration, director identification and powers of attorney, and, where required, appointing a fiscal representative ('représentant fiscal') responsible for French VAT and tax obligations. A provisional French TIN/VAT ID is issued within approximately 10 business days and a definitive VAT number is allocated after substance and activity verification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists for the SAS; it is mandatorily subject to impôt sur les sociétés (IS). Available elective regimes include French tax consolidation ('intégration fiscale') for groups, VAT grouping for fiscal unity, and participation-exemption elections for French Sociétés Mères. Hybrid mismatch provisions under the ATAD-inspired French tax law (LOI n° 2018-898) operate automatically and offer no elective mechanism for Article 239 quater transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For domestic established businesses: €85,000 for supply of goods and €37,500 for supply of services (franchise en base thresholds) — exceeding either threshold triggers mandatory VAT registration. For non-established foreign suppliers, no minimum threshold applies; any taxable supply in France creates a VAT registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations are exempt with credit (effective 0% VAT) under CGI art. 262-I-1°, provided the goods leave France/EU by the seller or on its behalf, supported by customs export status and transport documents. For services (including affiliate media buying/brokerage) where the customer is a non-EU taxable person, the place of supply is outside France pursuant to CGI art. 259B, so no French VAT is chargeable; where the customer is EU VAT-registered, the B2B supply is zero-rated/reverse-charged in the customer's EU member state if a valid EU VAT ID is obtained. For services to non-taxable persons outside the EU, local consumption rules may still exempt French VAT if effectively used and enjoyed in the non-EU customer's country.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/Déclaration d'exportation via DELTA G or ECS)
  • Bill of Lading (LTA/CMR/airway bill) evidencing transport out of France/EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (certificat de domiciliation devise / bank payment receipt)

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting under the French 2026 reform: all established taxable persons must issue and receive structured e-invoices through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) using EN 16931-compliant formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII). Invoices must contain SIREN, SIRET, French VAT ID, a unique invoice identifier, and an electronic rating. Transaction data is transmitted near-real-time to DGFiP via secured APIs; there is no physical fiscal stamp requirement for standard exports, but customs export clearance in DELTA G / ECS (European Export Control System) must be completed digitally, and certain state or prepaid supplies require a prior digital authorization.

Under CGI art. 1727–1729 and Code des douanes art. 414, the export zero-rating can be retroactively clawed back where export documentation is fraudulent or the deadline is missed. DGFiP may issue rectification within the statutory six-year period (extended to ten years for concealed fraud). Penalties escalate from 10% (minor inaccuracy) to 40% (deliberate omission) and 80% for manœuvres frauduleuses, plus monthly late-payment interest of 0.20%. Customs offences may incur criminal penalties up to three years’ imprisonment and a fine of two times the evaded VAT; the person claiming zero-rating is personally liable for restitution of VAT along with penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no general French exchange-control regime requiring prior approval for ordinary commercial payments, dividend distributions, intercompany settlements, or foreign investment funding. Capital movements remain subject to sanctions compliance, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing controls, beneficial-owner verification, tax reporting, statistical reporting, and applicable French foreign-direct-investment screening. Outbound dividends and other profit remittances generally require properly approved corporate accounts, evidence of distributable profits, withholding-tax analysis, and completion of applicable tax formalities. Bank transfer delays may arise where the payment involves sanctioned countries, high-risk jurisdictions, complex ownership, unusual affiliate pricing, regulated advertising sectors, or insufficient evidence of the underlying transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to repatriate, surrender, or convert export proceeds within a prescribed period, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export receipts may generally be retained in euro or foreign-currency accounts and converted at the company's discretion, subject to contractual bank terms, accounting requirements, sanctions controls, anti-money-laundering controls, and accurate tax and statistical reporting. Where payments relate to regulated goods, sanctioned counterparties, controlled jurisdictions, or documentary trade finance, additional evidence and reporting may be required. The company should maintain invoices, contracts, shipping or service-delivery evidence, customer and beneficial-owner information, and payment-reconciliation records.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. Foreign-exchange restrictions are principally governed by European Union law, French financial and monetary legislation, and anti-money-laundering rules administered and supervised by the relevant French authorities, including the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution and the Direction générale du Trésor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for routine EUR collections and payments, subject to provider eligibility and risk acceptance. A French SAS may use a European Union or European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution where the provider onboards French legal entities and provides the required account or payment services. Wise Business may be available for eligible French companies, but availability, account features, currencies, limits, and local receiving details must be confirmed directly with the provider. Mercury is a United States-focused financial-services platform and its availability to a French SAS, including ACH functionality, is not assured; ACH is a United States clearing rail and is not a substitute for SEPA. An EMI safeguarding account is not a bank deposit account and may not provide lending, deposit-guarantee protection, or all bank services. Offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully be used to evade French or EU AML, sanctions, tax, beneficial-owner, payment-services, or transaction-reporting requirements. A regulated EMI may passport services within the EEA, but the precise service, local establishment, and supervisory status must be verified in the relevant regulator register.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Fund the SAS through a bank account or regulated payment account held in the SAS's legal name. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro funding from the Single Euro Payments Area and SWIFT cross-border transfer for non-SEPA currencies or banks. The remittance narrative should identify the transaction as share capital, share-premium funding, shareholder loan, or intercompany funding, as applicable; the legal characterization must match the corporate resolutions and accounting entries. For incorporation, deposit subscribed capital into a designated capital-deposit account and obtain the bank certificate required for registration. For post-incorporation funding, route funds to the operational account and retain the funding agreement, board or shareholder approval, source-of-funds evidence, and bank confirmation. Banks may request a purpose, invoice, contract, ownership chart, tax information, and proof of economic rationale before crediting or releasing funds. Do not split transfers to avoid monitoring thresholds, use third-party remitters without approval, or describe debt funding as equity. Reconcile the incoming transfer to the ledger and preserve the SWIFT or SEPA confirmation, value date, ordering customer, beneficiary, currency, and amount.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA scheme and bank-supported limits. Use SWIFT cross-border payments for non-SEPA currencies, non-EEA counterparties, or banking corridors that require correspondent banking. A traditional SWIFT MT103 remains a payment-message reference for applicable bank processing, but banks are migrating to ISO 20022 formats, including pacs.008; the bank's current messaging standard should be confirmed. For documentary trade settlements, use a commercial documentary letter of credit, standby letter of credit, documentary collection, or open-account payment according to the counterparty and risk profile. The bank may require a purchase order, commercial invoice, contract, transport or service-delivery evidence, beneficiary ownership information, sanctions screening, and approved purpose. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% is the principal domestic withholding rate on dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate recipient, subject to exemptions, treaty reductions, and specific recipient classifications. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive may eliminate withholding where the qualifying ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, beneficial ownership, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. A 12.8% domestic rate generally applies to certain individual recipients rather than non-resident corporate shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million of French turnover or gross assets, or membership in a group meeting the EUR 400 million threshold, generally triggers full Local File and Master File documentation obligations under French transfer-pricing rules. A French transfer-pricing summary declaration on Form 2257-SD generally applies at a lower EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, or where the relevant group threshold is met. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France has no single stand-alone economic-substance regulation equivalent to a comprehensive offshore ESR regime. Substance is enforced through the French anti-abuse doctrine, abuse-of-law rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, treaty principal-purpose tests, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, and general arm's-length principles. A French SAS must maintain real business functions, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational control, decision-making evidence, commercially justified contracts, accounting records, and risk-bearing capacity. An affiliate media-buying company should demonstrate genuine campaign management, platform relationships, lead-generation activity, pricing control, fraud monitoring, and performance risk rather than acting as a conduit for foreign profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Concealment

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties of up to 80% of the avoided tax in aggravated cases, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that may include imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000. Aggravated or organized conduct may attract imprisonment of up to seven years and a fine of up to EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability and personal tax liability in cases of fraudulent maneuvering or serious and repeated non-compliance. Civil veil piercing is exceptional and is not automatic. Courts may impose confiscation, professional prohibitions, asset restraints, and travel restrictions where legally ordered; imprisonment is possible for qualifying criminal conduct.
Fictitious Services and Shell-Company Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Expense denial, taxable-profit reassessment, withholding-tax assessment, abuse-of-law penalties, interest, and potentially fraud or false-accounting prosecution. A foreign company is not unlawful merely because it is low-substance; the risk arises from fictitious, abusive, or misrepresented transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted where they knowingly approve fictitious transactions, false books, or fraudulent tax structures. Asset confiscation, management bans, and imprisonment may apply under the underlying offense; travel restrictions require a specific judicial or administrative basis.
Transfer-Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under the arm's-length principle, denial of deductions, withholding-tax consequences, interest, and documentation penalties. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation can generally trigger the higher of EUR 10,000 or 0.5% of the transactions lacking documentation, subject to the applicable statutory cap and rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal exposure for deliberate falsification or fraudulent concealment, but ordinary transfer-pricing disagreement normally creates corporate tax liability rather than automatic director imprisonment.
Value Added Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, recovery of improperly claimed credits, late-payment interest, penalties generally ranging from 40% for serious non-compliance to 80% for fraudulent or concealed conduct, and possible criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers may be prosecuted for intentional VAT fraud or knowingly false records. Corporate fines, confiscation, professional bans, and imprisonment may apply under the relevant criminal provisions.
Withholding Tax and Beneficial-Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, interest, penalties, denial of treaty benefits, and possible joint or secondary liability under applicable tax rules.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise where directors knowingly authorize non-compliant payments or fraudulent treaty claims. Good-faith reliance on properly documented treaty relief does not eliminate the need for reasonable verification.
Anti-Money Laundering and Payment-Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Suspicious-transaction reporting, account restriction or closure, asset freezing, forfeiture, and money-laundering prosecution. Money laundering may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 375,000; aggravated cases may carry imprisonment of up to ten years and a fine of up to EUR 750,000, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and beneficial owners may be personally prosecuted for knowing participation, facilitation, or concealment. Corporate criminal fines may be up to five times the individual fine where applicable, with confiscation and management prohibitions possible.
False Accounting and Corporate Records

Statutory sanction: Accounting corrections, tax reassessment, corporate-law penalties, civil damages, and criminal sanctions for document fraud, false records, or distribution of fictitious dividends, depending on the conduct.

Director personal liability: The president, directors, de facto managers, accountants, and approving officers may incur individual civil and criminal liability. Corporate limited liability does not protect a person from liability for personal fraud or intentional misconduct.
Unlawful Profit Distribution and Insolvency Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, shareholder and director claims, insolvency clawback, asset recovery, management liability, and possible criminal sanctions for fictitious dividends or bankruptcy-related misconduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to contribute to insolvency shortfalls where management faults caused or increased the deficiency and may face management bans, asset confiscation, or imprisonment for qualifying insolvency offenses.
Affiliate and Consumer-Protection Advertising Violations

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines, consumer compensation, platform clawbacks, contract termination, administrative sanctions, and potential GDPR penalties of up to EUR 20 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for intentional deceptive practices, obstruction, or criminal data offenses. Corporate limited liability does not shield individuals from their own unlawful conduct.
Failure to Maintain Corporate and Tax Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, reallocation of profits, treaty-benefit denial, permanent-establishment disputes, tax reassessment, interest, penalties, and possible abuse-of-law treatment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for deliberate misrepresentation or fraudulent arrangements. A lack of substance alone does not automatically create imprisonment or veil piercing, but it materially increases audit, reassessment, and anti-abuse exposure.
