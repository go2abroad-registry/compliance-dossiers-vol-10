Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS; the Articles may set the capital at any amount, including EUR 1. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid on incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed and may require a commissaire aux apports report, subject to statutory exemptions. Capital must be deposited in a qualifying bank or notarial account before registration and released after registration evidence is issued. The amount shown is an approximate USD equivalent of standard commercial registration and beneficial-owner filing charges; legal-publication fees, bank charges, and applicable taxes are excluded.

Funding & unlock path: No general pre-opening occupational licence applies to an ordinary office or remote-work model. The premises must satisfy applicable fire-safety, evacuation, electrical, building, accessibility, and occupational-health requirements. If the site is an établissement recevant du public, relevant ERP fire and accessibility rules and municipal or departmental approvals apply. The employer must maintain the single occupational-risk assessment document (document unique d'évaluation des risques professionnels), provide workstation and emergency procedures, and arrange required occupational-health monitoring. Structural-load certification is required only where building works, heavy equipment, or the landlord or authority requires it.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; official portals: impots.gouv.fr, portailpro.impots.gouv.fr, formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique/INPI), and the public e-invoicing directory (Annuaire/PPF) operated under DGFiP oversight.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS: (1) incorporate via the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; (2) INSEE automatically issues the 9-digit SIREN TIN and SIRET establishment numbers; (3) the French intra-EU VAT ID is 'FR' + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN, allocated by the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and confirmed on Form Cerfa n° 14472*04 (welcome file) or automatically at registration; (4) for non-resident ownership, no fiscal representative is required for the SAS itself because the SAS is a French resident taxpayer, but a foreign parent establishing a branch would require one; (5) apply for an EORI number (FR + SIREN) through DGFiP for any goods cross-border flows; timeline: SIREN within 2 working days, VAT number confirmation within 1 to 2 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the SAS is by default subject to French corporate income tax (IS, CGI art. 206(1)) on its worldwide profits at the 25% standard rate (15% reduced rate on the first 42,500 EUR of taxable profit for eligible small enterprises under 10M EUR revenue). No territorial exemption applies to active BPO service income; dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders attract 25% withholding tax subject to treaty relief. — For a French SAS: (1) incorporate via the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; (2) INSEE automatically issues the 9-digit SIREN TIN and SIRET establishment numbers; (3) the French intra-EU VAT ID is 'FR' + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN, allocated by the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and confirmed on Form Cerfa n° 14472*04 (welcome file) or automatically at registration; (4) for non-resident ownership, no fiscal representative is required for the SAS itself because the SAS is a French resident taxpayer, but a foreign parent establishing a branch would require one; (5) apply for an EORI number (FR + SIREN) through DGFiP for any goods cross-border flows; timeline: SIREN within 2 working days, VAT number confirmation within 1 to 2 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the SAS is by default subject to French corporate income tax (IS, CGI art. 206(1)) on its worldwide profits at the 25% standard rate (15% reduced rate on the first 42,500 EUR of taxable profit for eligible small enterprises under 10M EUR revenue). No territorial exemption applies to active BPO service income; dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders attract 25% withholding tax subject to treaty relief.

Available election forms

Available check-the-box equivalents: (a) option for partnership-like pass-through taxation under CGI art. 239 bis AB for small qualifying SAS (fewer than 50 shareholders, unlisted, predominantly natural-person ownership, revenue below 10M EUR); (b) French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under CGI art. 223 A for a 95%-owned group of French subsidiaries; (c) ATAD-aligned hybrid mismatch neutralizing rules (Law n° 2019-1479). A SAS cannot elect to be disregarded as a separate French taxpayer; it remains subject to IS unless the art. 239 bis AB option is validly made within the statutory deadline.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established taxable person, annual turnover of 36,800 EUR for service activities (BPO/helpdesk) triggers mandatory VAT registration under the 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption regime (CGI art. 293 B I; tolerance threshold 39,100 EUR in the first excess year); the threshold for trading/goods activities is 91,900 EUR (tolerance 101,000 EUR). Non-EU-established suppliers providing remote B2C services taxable in France have zero threshold and must register from the first euro; B2B services to a French recipient are reverse-charged by the recipient under CGI art. 283-2, generally requiring no supplier registration.

France applies a continuous-transaction-control-style reform (Ordonnances n° 2022-1087 and n° 2024-934; décrets n° 2023-374/375/376): mandatory structured e-invoicing for all domestic B2B invoices using Factur-X 1.09.03, UBL 2.1, or UN/CEFACT CII, routed through certified Partner Dematerialisation Platforms (PDPs). Issuance obligation is phased by taxpayer size: large and mid-size enterprises from 1 September 2026; SME and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027. Reception capability becomes mandatory for all VAT-registered companies from 1 September 2026. No real-time prior fiscal clearance or digital stamping is required; instead, PDPs transmit e-invoice and transaction data (e-reporting) to DGFiP within statutory deadlines and validate counterparties via the interoperable Annuaire directory. Cross-border B2B invoices remain non-structured but are subject to e-reporting obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility in accordance with EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and OECD Capital Movement Liberalisation Codes. The euro is freely convertible; there is no prior exchange-control authorisation for ordinary business operations. Profit remittance and dividend distributions from a French SAS are legally unrestricted but are subject to (i) Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting for cross-border transactions exceeding thresholds, (ii) EU sanctions and anti-money-laundering screening, and (iii) either a 30% final dividend withholding tax or reduced treaty/source rates, often 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying corporate shareholders or 5%-15% under double-tax treaties. Capital inflows for equity contributions and shareholder advances are permissible without exchange-control approval, but direct foreign investments may require prior declaration to the DG Trésor for sensitive sectors or non-EEA investors; remote BPO call-center services are not a sensitive French strategic sector.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in France to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or to convert foreign currency into euros. French exporters may retain export receivables in foreign currency bank accounts opened through an authorised bank or payment institution. No mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline applies. The only obligations are statistical: cross-border transfers and export/import invoicing in France must be reported to the Banque de France by resident banks for balance-of-payments purposes, and certain cash flows to non-cooperative jurisdictions must satisfy transparency and sanctions checks. For all practical purposes, a French SAS operating a remote BPO call centre is free to invoice its intercompany or third-party call-center services in EUR, USD or GBP and to accumulate export proceeds in a foreign-currency business account.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, in coordination with the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty (DG Trésor) and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), acting as the French foreign exchange administration and prudential banking regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal caveats. A French SAS may lawfully hold corporate transaction accounts with EU-licensed electronic-money institutions and payment institutions (e.g. Wise, Revolut Business, Qonto, or other ACPR-supervised EMIs) under the EU passporting regime. These EMIs can provide IBANs, SEPA credit transfers, SWIFT MT103 sending/receiving, and simpler FX execution, making them acceptable for low-value cross-border BPO receivables and operational expense payments. However, EMIs are not credit institutions: they cannot grant credit, invest client funds, pay interest, or provide full treasury/investment services. Offshore EMIs domiciled outside the EEA cannot conduct payment services in France without an ACPR licence or an authorised local partner; using such offshore providers for operational treasury creates a heightened regulatory and sanctions risk. The practical recommendation is to maintain a Tier-1 commercial bank as the principal account and use a licensed fintech EMI only as a secondary, capped liquidity rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital injection to a French SAS should be structured as a SWIFT MT103 credit transfer from an authorised foreign bank to the beneficiary account held at a French Tier-1 bank. The wire instructions must include: (i) full legal name, address and account number of the originator and beneficiary; (ii) a precise purpose narrative quoting 'apport en numéraire au capital social' where the injection is equity, or 'avance en compte courant d'associé' if it is a shareholder loan; (iii) the originator's VAT/tax number or national identifier and the French SIREN/SIRET of the SAS; (iv) inclusion of ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' or 'CACC' as accepted by the corresponding bank, although a plain-text purpose statement is often mandatory; and (v) charge option 'OUR' so net proceeds reach the beneficiary intact. For non-EU inbound flows, additional verification includes EU and OFAC sanctions screening of all parties, confirmation that no French exchange-control authorisation is required, and automatic Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting through the receiving bank. In all cases the bank will require a completed KYC file, evidence of the underlying capital transaction, and a French corporate resolution before crediting.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements and BPO service payments should primarily use SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR cross-border transfers and SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR disbursements within the European Economic Area. Commercial letters of credit are permissible and advisable for procurement of hardware, telecom equipment, or large IT infrastructure related to call-centre delivery, with an irrevocable documentary credit payable against transport and commercial invoices in compliance with UCP 600; but for ongoing subscription or call-centre service fees, direct MT103/SCT transfers with an underlying invoice trail are the standard clearing rail. Each outbound payment must include the beneficiary's bank BIC/IBAN, a purpose-of-payment code where required, the underlying commercial invoice number, and (for services) a French customs/statistical declaration only if the flow exceeds the balance-of-payments reporting threshold. To maintain liquidity and auditability, treasury should sweep EUR balances into a money-market account and use real-time gross settlement (TARGET2) rails for time-critical disbursements through the servicing bank.Vanilla outward-forward and forward-exchange contracts in EUR/USD and EUR/GBP for trade receivables and supplier liabilities, entered into with a French credit institution under ACPR rules.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for hedging proceeds in illiquid or non-fully-convertible currencies not supported by a real deliverable euro market; standard corporate NDFs in liquid hard currencies are ordinarily unavailable at French banks because deliverable forwards dominate.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps and interest-rate swaps) between EUR/USD/GBP for longer-dated balance-sheet hedging of intercompany receivables or foreign-currency share capital, subject to EMIR clearing thresholds and mandatory margining for non-financial counterparties.FX options (call and put structures) purchased by the French SAS without speculative intent, allowed under French prudential regulation and generally booked under an ISDA/FXA documentation framework.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with legal caveats. A French SAS may lawfully hold corporate transaction accounts with EU-licensed electronic-money institutions and payment institutions (e.g. Wise, Revolut Business, Qonto, or other ACPR-supervised EMIs) under the EU passporting regime. These EMIs can provide IBANs, SEPA credit transfers, SWIFT MT103 sending/receiving, and simpler FX execution, making them acceptable for low-value cross-border BPO receivables and operational expense payments. However, EMIs are not credit institutions: they cannot grant credit, invest client funds, pay interest, or provide full treasury/investment services. Offshore EMIs domiciled outside the EEA cannot conduct payment services in France without an ACPR licence or an authorised local partner; using such offshore providers for operational treasury creates a heightened regulatory and sanctions risk. The practical recommendation is to maintain a Tier-1 commercial bank as the principal account and use a licensed fintech EMI only as a secondary, capped liquidity rail.
