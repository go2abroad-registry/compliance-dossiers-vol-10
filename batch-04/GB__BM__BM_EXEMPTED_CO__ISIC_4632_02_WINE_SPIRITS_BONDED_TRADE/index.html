Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Department of Tax, Government of Bermuda (Ministry of Finance), eServices portal: https://tax.bm; Bermuda Customs Department (for customs and bonded warehouse licensing) under the Ministry of Finance

E-government filing portal

For a Bermuda Exempted Company, the statutory TIN is the Company Registration Number obtained automatically at incorporation from the Registrar of Companies. Where the entity becomes subject to Bermuda Corporate Income Tax (CIT, Pillar Two for multinational groups with consolidated revenue ≥ €750 million), registration with the Department of Tax must be completed within 30 days of falling within scope via the Bermuda Tax eServices portal. For customs purposes, the company must obtain a Customs Importer/Broker Code from the Bermuda Customs Department, and separate licensing as a bonded warehouse operator is required for wine/spirits storage.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda exempted companies are outside the domestic tax net; no corporate income tax on ordinary profits, no capital gains tax, no withholding tax, no VAT, and no wealth tax on non-resident owners)

Available election forms

Bermuda does not operate a check-the-box or entity-classification election system. The exempted company is inherently a non-resident-exempt entity. For CIT purposes, transitional rules permit an election to apply the OECD GloBE transitional safe harbour relief instead of full top-up tax calculations. Bermuda has no hybrid mismatch election mechanisms because its tax system lacks an income tax base for most entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST. Bermuda imposes import and excise duties (including excise on wine and spirits) administered by Bermuda Customs; bonded warehouse operations are a duty-suspension arrangement, not a VAT system.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. Customs registration is mandatory for all importers/exporter activities. A bonded warehouse operator licence and excise bond are required for the physical storage of wine/spirits; minimum capital is not statutorily required but customs financial guarantees are assessed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods held in a Bermuda bonded warehouse and subsequently exported outside Bermuda are treated as not entered into Bermuda's customs territory and are therefore outside the scope of import excise duties. A true zero-rating (exemption with credit/refund) applies only to the customs duty already paid if goods are re-exported within 12 months. Offshore services provided to non-residents are not subject to any Bermuda indirect tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA export entry)
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill for air cargo)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX proof evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds
  • Bonded Warehouse Removal Order / Duty Suspension Transfer Document
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List

Bermuda Customs uses ASYCUDA World for electronic declaration processing. Real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping are not required for standard business-to-business invoices. Bonded warehouse movements must be recorded and cleared electronically through ASYCUDA, with inventory reports filed to Customs on a periodic basis. Digital excise stamps apply on bottles destined for the local Bermuda market; exports move under electronic export entries without stamps.

Bermuda Customs conducts post-clearance audits of bonded warehouses. If the required export documentation is not produced within 180 days or if goods are diverted into the local market without duty payment, Customs will immediately claw back all suspended duties plus a 100% mark-up as penalty, impose a fine of up to BMD $5,000 per offence and/or custodial sentence, and the operator's licence may be revoked. Under Bermuda's CIT and anti-avoidance rules, deliberate false export substantiation can also trigger penalties of up to BMD $50,000 plus interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates an open exchange-control regime under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and successive Exchange Control Regulations. The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar. A Bermuda Exempted Company classified as non-resident is exempt from prior exchange-control approval for current-account and capital-account movements, enabling unrestricted share-capital injection, intercompany lending, dividend distribution, and profit remittance. No withholding tax, capital gains tax, or remittance tax applies under Bermuda law. Practical convertibility friction is limited to bank-level documentary verification of source of wealth and beneficial ownership, plus HMRC customs and excise verification for physical wine/spirits movements into the United Kingdom. The bonded-warehouse trade requires proof of duty deferment or duty payment status but does not alter currency-transfer freedom.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Neither the BMA nor HM Treasury imposes any statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio for Bermuda Exempted Companies. Export receipts from bonded wine/spirit sales may be retained indefinitely in USD, GBP, EUR, or other freely convertible currencies. The only de facto obligation is AML/CFT record-keeping requiring banks to retain underlying invoices, excise certificates, customs declarations, and shipment evidence for transactions above USD 10,000, and UK HMRC may require excise-records for imported goods, but no portion of proceeds must be surrendered or converted.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as the statutory central monetary authority and foreign exchange administration for Bermuda; Bank of England (BoE) and HM Treasury as the United Kingdom monetary authority and fiscal regulator governing GBP clearing, UK sanctions, and HMRC excise controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Regulated UK and EEA fintech e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, and Currencies Direct can provide ancillary services including low-value FX conversion, virtual IBAN management, and operational payment sweeps. They are not viable as the primary treasury, capital-escrow, or bonded-warehouse clearing rail because BMA does not license independent EMIs to take deposits or provide escrow and trade-finance intermediation under Bermuda banking law. Furthermore, non-HMRC-recognised payment institutions cannot evidence excise duty suspension or warehouse transfer under UK customs rules. The recommended treasury architecture uses a BMA-licensed tier-1 bank account as the core settlement rail and an EMI solely as a disbursement overlay for small operational FX payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections are routed as SWIFT MT103/MT202 transfers from the beneficial owner's bank to the Bermuda bank's nostro account using BMD, USD, or GBP. SWIFT field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'Capital Injection / Share Subscription - Bonded Wine and Spirits Trading Company'. For UK regulatory statistical reporting, use capital-transfer purpose code K1 or ECB-style P01 for eurozone remitters. No BMA approval or exchange-control licence is required for a non-resident exempted company. Funds are made available after compliance verification comprising board resolution authorising share issuance, subscription agreement, completed BMA beneficial-ownership form, and source-of-wealth declaration for the remitting shareholder.
