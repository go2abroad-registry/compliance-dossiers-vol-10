Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Bermuda exempted company; at least one share must be issued for value, commonly one or more ordinary shares of US$0.01 or US$1.00 par value; no escrow deposit or paid-up capital escrow rule is imposed. The directors must determine that the company is solvent and able to pay its liabilities as they fall due.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a fully virtual digital magazine portal with no employees, inventory, or premises in Bermuda. If the company later establishes a Bermuda office, it must obtain an Occupancy Certificate from the Department of Planning and a Fire Safety Certificate from the Bermuda Fire and Rescue Service, and must comply with the Occupational Health and Safety Act 2001 and any applicable workplace registration requirements.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC), Ministry of Finance, acting jointly with the Registrar of Companies (Bermuda Corporate Registry); official portal www.gov.bm and the OTC CIT Online Filing System. UK HMRC does not administer Bermuda tax law; the island is a self-governing British Overseas Territory with a separate fiscal regime.

E-government filing portal

A foreign beneficial owner forms the Bermuda Exempted Company through a licensed Corporate Service Provider (CSP) under the Companies Act 1981. The Registrar of Companies issues a Certificate of Incorporation and a unique corporate registration number. Thereafter, the entity files an Employer Registration application with the OTC to obtain an Employer Registrar Number for payroll-tax compliance. If the ultimate group has consolidated annual revenue above EUR 750 million, the Bermuda entity and all Bermuda group members must register under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, appoint a CIT Group Representative, and file annual CIT returns through the OTC portal. No VAT or general income-tax identification number exists because Bermuda imposes no VAT, GST, capital gains tax, or general corporate income tax (other than the 2023 top-up tax regime applicable solely to in-scope MNE groups).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda does not apply US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity elections. Available legal elections include: (1) the CIT Group Representative's statutory option to file a consolidated Bermuda CIT group return under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023; (2) the election to apply the qualified domestic minimum top-up tax computational adjustments and asset/deferred-tax transition rules permitted by the CIT Act and CIT Regulations; (3) the election under the Economic Substance Amendment Regulations 2018 to discharge substance obligations by demonstrating tax residence in a recognized foreign jurisdiction; and (4) an exempted company's standing statutory election (through its permit register) to remain outside Bermuda-sourced trade and to hold non-Bermudian business activity exclusively. Exempted-company status itself is the core classification election: no Bermuda-resident ownership requirement, no minimum paid-up capital, and tax exemption on all non-Bermuda-sourced income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

N/A – No VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax (Bermuda levies customs duty on physical imports only)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – Bermuda maintains no indirect-tax registration threshold, no VAT ID, and no turnover-based fiscal registration. Customs-duty registration is consignment-based and applies exclusively to the importation of physical goods under the Bermuda Customs Tariff; digital subscriptions, sponsored media placements, and remote services are outside the customs net.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Technically inapplicable because Bermuda imposes no VAT or GST on outgoing digital services; the digital magazine portal's subscription and sponsored-media revenue is therefore charged at an effective 0% without any zero-rating claim. The operative export-related conditions are: (i) preserve the entity's non-Bermuda territorial status so revenue is not recharacterized as Bermuda-source; (ii) avoid the creation of a UK permanent establishment or UK VAT registration nexus through the UK place-of-supply rules for B2C digital services; (iii) maintain management and control in Bermuda with adequate board governance and CIGA (core income generating activity) evidence to satisfy the Economic Substance Amendment Regulations 2018 if the entity falls within a relevant-activity category; and (iv) where the EUR 750 million MNE threshold is exceeded, comply with the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 for top-up taxation, recognizing that revenue from a digital publishing business remains fully exempt from independent Bermudian income tax otherwise.

N/A – no real-time fiscal clearance, no e-invoicing mandate, no digital stamping requirement, and no continuous transaction controls are imposed on the digital magazine portal. For physical imports entering Bermuda, filing and clearance occur through the Bermuda Customs online entry system (eCustoms/ASYCUDA-equivalent portal), but this does not apply to the provision of digital content or cross-border advertising services.

No VAT zero-rating clawback or export-refund penalty exists in Bermuda for digital services, because no indirect tax is levied. The material exposure is structural: (1) Economic Substance non-compliance — BD$100,000 initial penalty and BD$250,000 for continuing default, plus mandatory information-exchange escalation and potential strike-off; (2) CIT Act 2023 failures — late-filing and underpayment penalties of up to 10% of the top-up tax due, interest on overdue amounts, and criminal sanctions for false filings; (3) Customs Act 1947 — for physical goods, misdescription or undervaluation triggers forfeiture, a fine of up to ten times the evaded duty, and/or imprisonment; and (4) retrospective assessments may be raised for up to six years (unlimited in cases of fraud or deliberate non-declaration). The 180-day benchmark is retained as an internal substance and documentation control for FX remittance evidence, not as a statutory Bermuda repatriation deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at par with the US Dollar. Current-account transactions are fully convertible for exempted companies designated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes, and profit remittance is free from BMA approval. Capital-account inflows and outflows by exempted entities are generally unrestricted, subject to BMA notification and statutory reporting thresholds for certain inward investments and loans.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda-resident persons offering foreign currency export proceeds must surrender such proceeds to an authorised bank within one month of receipt, with mandatory conversion to BMD where applicable. However, a Bermuda Exempted Company holding non-resident status under the Exchange Control Act is exempt from the surrender obligation and may retain export receipts in USD or other foreign currency without conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as delegated exchange-control authority under the Bermuda Exchange Control Act 1972; ultimate sovereign supervisory responsibility rests with the UK Crown.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low for primary treasury banking. Offshore digital EMIs and UK-authorised e-money institutions cannot accept Bermuda domestic deposits or provide BMD clearing without separate BMA licensing under the Banking Act 1969 or the Digital Asset Business Act 2018. They may be used only as supplementary payment rails for Stripe and AdSense Payout withdrawals, provided the account is structured as an inbound collection corridor and not as the treasury concentration account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Bermuda’s Economic Substance (ES) Act 2018 and Economic Substance Regulations require an exempted company carrying on a 'relevant activity' to maintain substantial economic presence in Bermuda. Digital magazine publishing with in-house content creation, editorial staff, and digital sales infrastructure may constitute 'distribution and service business' or 'intellectual property' activity if core IP is owned. The company must annually file an ES declaration, have a physical office or demonstrate outsourced core activities, employ sufficient qualified personnel, and incur operating expenditure proportionate to the business.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Financial Transparency

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies Act 1981 and Beneficial Ownership (BRE) Rules, non-disclosure attracts asset forfeiture, fines up to BSD 10,000, and deregistration. The Bermuda Tax Authority may issue a default assessment of BSD 1,000 per day for ES declaration violations.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fines and contempt proceedings. The Registrar may obtain a court order freezing personal assets if the company is used for tax evasion in a foreign jurisdiction; travel bans can be imposed under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997. Imprisonment of up to 2 years is possible for knowingly making a false declaration.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Economic Substance (E.S.) Declaration will be marked 'excluded' or 'non-compliant', leading to a fine of BSD 10,000 for material inaccuracy, a further daily penalty, and strike-off of the company from the Register. The company may also lose its Exempted status and become subject to exchange of information measures.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for penalties if they failed to exercise reasonable diligence to maintain economic substance. They may be barred from acting as directors in Bermuda for up to 5 years. In severe cases, the court may pierce the corporate veil and render shareholders liable for the company’s debts.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Bermuda Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Act 2018, the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, and the FIU's Directions, a registered entity that fails to appoint an MLRO/CO and implement a risk-based compliance program may face fines up to BSD 250,000. Repeat offences lead to revocation of business license; banks may freeze accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally liable for money laundering, with imprisonment up to 14 years and unlimited fines. Personal bank accounts connected to the offence can be seized. The Financial Investigation Agency may impose travel bans pending criminal investigation.
Director Fiduciary Breach

Statutory sanction: The Bermuda Companies Act 1981 imposes civil penalties for misfeasance and breach of directors’ duties. The court may order restitution of misappropriated amounts, surcharge interest, and award costs against the director. The company’s identity may be 'pierced' and shareholders treated as principals.

Director personal liability: Directors can be ordered to repay all profits plus interest. A director who knowingly facilitates tax evasion in a foreign jurisdiction may be extradited. Personal assets, including real estate and foreign bank accounts, are subject to freezing orders. Imprisonment for serious breach is up to 2 years.
