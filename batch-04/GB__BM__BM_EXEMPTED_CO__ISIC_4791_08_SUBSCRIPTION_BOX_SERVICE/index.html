Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Government of Bermuda — Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance (official tax portal: tax.gov.bm); Registrar of Companies (corporate registry portal: roc.gov.bm); Bermuda Customs Department (customs clearance portal: ASYCUDA World); Bermuda Monetary Authority (Exchange Control Permit issuance).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Bermuda Exempted Company obtains its statutory tax identity through a four-step registration sequence: (1) incorporation with the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 1981 / Exempted Companies Act — the Certificate of Incorporation and registered company number comprise the de facto statutory tax identifier because Bermuda imposes no income tax and issues no general TIN; (2) Exchange Control Permit from the Bermuda Monetary Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972, designating the company as non-resident and freeing its foreign-currency operations from Bermuda exchange-control restrictions; (3) registrations triggered only by local substance: payroll tax and social insurance registration with the Office of the Tax Commissioner if Bermuda-based employees are engaged, and importer registration with Bermuda Customs Department (customs code and deferred-duty account) if goods are physically imported into Bermuda; (4) no VAT registration is possible or required because Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST. For a subscription-box merchandiser with foreign owners and non-Bermuda fulfilment, no minimum capital is required and no economic-substance registration is triggered solely by offshore trading.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A Bermuda Exempted Company is outside the scope of Bermuda income tax, capital gains tax, corporation tax (except the 15% OECD Pillar Two top-up taxes under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 for MNE groups with consolidated global revenue exceeding EUR 750 million, which do not apply to a typical SME subscription-box operator), withholding tax, dividend tax, and VAT/GST. Residual Bermuda tax exposures are limited to: (i) employer payroll tax at progressive rates between approximately 4.5% and 15.5% on remuneration of Bermuda-based employees; (ii) customs import duties on goods imported into Bermuda; (iii) stamp duties on specified instruments; and (iv) obligations under the Economic Substance Amendment Act 2018 if the company carries on a 'relevant activity' (e.g., distribution and service centre) with substantial activity actually in Bermuda. — A foreign-owned Bermuda Exempted Company obtains its statutory tax identity through a four-step registration sequence: (1) incorporation with the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 1981 / Exempted Companies Act — the Certificate of Incorporation and registered company number comprise the de facto statutory tax identifier because Bermuda imposes no income tax and issues no general TIN; (2) Exchange Control Permit from the Bermuda Monetary Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972, designating the company as non-resident and freeing its foreign-currency operations from Bermuda exchange-control restrictions; (3) registrations triggered only by local substance: payroll tax and social insurance registration with the Office of the Tax Commissioner if Bermuda-based employees are engaged, and importer registration with Bermuda Customs Department (customs code and deferred-duty account) if goods are physically imported into Bermuda; (4) no VAT registration is possible or required because Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — A Bermuda Exempted Company is outside the scope of Bermuda income tax, capital gains tax, corporation tax (except the 15% OECD Pillar Two top-up taxes under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 for MNE groups with consolidated global revenue exceeding EUR 750 million, which do not apply to a typical SME subscription-box operator), withholding tax, dividend tax, and VAT/GST. Residual Bermuda tax exposures are limited to: (i) employer payroll tax at progressive rates between approximately 4.5% and 15.5% on remuneration of Bermuda-based employees; (ii) customs import duties on goods imported into Bermuda; (iii) stamp duties on specified instruments; and (iv) obligations under the Economic Substance Amendment Act 2018 if the company carries on a 'relevant activity' (e.g., distribution and service centre) with substantial activity actually in Bermuda.

Available election forms

No US 'check-the-box' election is available for the Bermuda Exempted Company itself: Bermuda incorporated limited companies are per se corporations under US Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-2(b)(8)(i)(B) and cannot elect disregarded-entity or partnership status. Valid elections and exclusion mechanisms locally include: (1) Exchange Control non-resident Permit election before the Bermuda Monetary Authority — the foundational classification election for a 100% foreign-owned entity; (2) foreign tax-residence exclusion under the Economic Substance Amendment Act 2018 — the company files a declaration supported by a foreign tax-residence certificate that it is tax-resident elsewhere, thereby excluding itself from Bermuda economic-substance requirements for relevant activities; (3) Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 Pillar Two elections (QDMTT safe-harbour, substance-based income exclusion) available only to in-scope MNE groups; (4) migration/continuation out of Bermuda under the Companies Act 1981 as a full redomiciliation election; and (5) no hybrid-mismatch elections are available or necessary because Bermuda levies no income tax and has no hybrid mismatch legislative framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales tax applies in Bermuda. The applicable indirect-tax framework comprises: (i) customs import duties under the Customs Tariff Act 2001 and Customs Act 1941 (ad valorem rates commonly 0%–25% depending on tariff classification); (ii) stamp duties under the Stamp Duties Act 1976; and (iii) employer-paid payroll tax as an indirect employment cost. For subscription-box e-commerce, Bermuda indirect-tax nexus is limited to customs duties on goods imported into Bermuda; outbound fulfilment of subscription boxes to non-Bermuda subscribers is entirely outside Bermuda's indirect-tax net.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT or GST registration threshold exists. For Bermuda import activity, any commercial importer must register for a customs code with the Bermuda Customs Department through ASYCUDA World and may open a deferred-duty account or standing deposit; there is no minimum economic-turnover threshold and even a single commercial import requires an electronic declaration. No EU-style distance-selling or OSS turnover threshold exists in Bermuda.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Bermuda imposes no VAT on any supply, export sales of subscription boxes and offshore services are inherently outside the scope of Bermuda taxation at an effective rate of 0%; there is no statutory zero-rating claim to file and no VAT refund mechanism to invoke. To substantiate genuine export status for (i) Bermuda customs records, (ii) destination-country VAT treatment of the subscription supply (e.g., the UK 20% VAT distance-selling or Import One-Stop Shop obligation), and (iii) economic-substance and transfer-pricing documentation, the Bermuda Exempted Company must demonstrate that goods are conclusively consigned to foreign subscribers' addresses with evidenced transfer of title and risk, and that all order-taking, payment processing, warehousing and fulfilment functions are performed by a related non-Bermuda service provider. A mere invoicing pass-through of subscription revenue must not create a Bermuda 'distribution and service centre' relevant activity; the company should maintain its foreign tax-residence exclusion filing to preserve its Tax_Exempt_Zone status.

  • Bermuda Customs Export Declaration lodged electronically in ASYCUDA World (or the transit/re-export entry where goods were first imported into Bermuda; for low-value e-commerce consignments shipped directly air-freight or post, the airport/postal export manifest entry is accepted)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the Bermuda exporter, ultimate foreign subscriber consignee, HS tariff classification of each subscription-box SKU, unit values, quantities, and delivery terms (Incoterms)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Bermuda and arrival in the destination country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (supported by SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice) showing foreign-currency sales proceeds repatriated through a licensed authorized bank — required only for resident-status companies without a BMA non-resident Permit; a Permit-holding Exempted Company is entirely outside Bermuda exchange control
  • Certificates of Origin and destination-import records (customs entry, EORI/OSS references) retained to demonstrate the final market and to support the destination-country VAT treatment of each supply

Bermuda does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, continuous transaction controls, fiscal clearance, API integration, digital stamping, or government clearance of commercial invoices or exports. The only mandatory electronic clearance is customs: every import and export declaration must be lodged electronically through the ASYCUDA World customs management system, with submission before movement of goods. Commercial invoices are contract-determined and remain private; any e-invoicing format (e.g., PEPPOL/UBL or destination-country fiscal requirements) is driven by the buyer's jurisdiction and not by Bermuda law.

No Bermuda VAT zero-rating clawback exists because there is no VAT. However, misrepresentation of export or non-resident status attracts the following sanctions: (i) under the Customs Act 1941 and Customs Tariff Act 2001, false declarations or undervaluation of export/import entries expose the company to forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties up to three times the duty-foregone, fines, and imprisonment of responsible officers upon conviction; (ii) under the Tax Administration Act 2022 and the Economic Substance Amendment Act 2018, failure to file or the filing of false compliance declarations attracts fixed penalties plus daily continuing penalties, reassessments for up to six years, penalties of up to BMD 10,000 per offence and up to BMD 250,000 for economic-substance non-compliance, and potential strike-off of the company; (iii) abuse of the foreign tax-residence exclusion (claiming residence in a jurisdiction with no genuine economic link) results in retrospective re-imposition of Bermuda economic-substance requirements, revocation of the BMA non-resident Permit, and director personal liability for unpaid payroll or customs obligations; and (iv) for resident-status companies, failure to repatriate export proceeds by day 180 empowers the Bermuda Monetary Authority to compel conversion of earnings on the local market, impose fines, revoke exchange-control permissions, and treat subsequent foreign receipts as regulated transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is fully convertible at a fixed 1:1 peg to the US dollar (USD), which is the de facto settlement currency for Bermuda exempted companies. Both current account and capital account transactions are unrestricted for exempted companies. Profit remittance and dividend distributions require no BMA approval, but are subject to the Bermuda Companies Act 1981 solvency test and standard corporate governance authorisations. Remittance hurdles are minimal: a board resolution, supported by management or audited accounts, and a standard outward payment application to the bank are the only substantive evidentiary requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement in Bermuda for non-resident owned exempted companies. Export receipts may be retained in external accounts or repatriated in full, with no mandatory conversion into BMD. Statutory surrender timeline: N/A. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. The BMA imposes no foreign-exchange control or price-differential restrictions, and the 1:1 BMD/USD arrangement is a reserve currency board stabilisation mechanism, not a surrender regime.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary operating account and Medium as a disbursement-only auxiliary rail. The BMA requires any bank or deposit-taking institution holding Bermuda accounts to be licensed as a local bank and maintain a physical presence on-island. Offshore digital EMIs generally cannot obtain a BMA banking licence and are legally barred from opening a Bermuda-resident operational account. They may be used for non-Bermuda supplier payouts in jurisdictions that authorise their EMI licences, subject to that jurisdiction's own AML/CFT supervision, but they must not serve as the principal inbound capital collection or statutory treasury clearing account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol requires a SWIFT MT103 (Corporate Credit Transfer) via a reputable correspondent bank, denominated in USD or BMD, referencing the beneficiary's Bermuda account. The recommended purpose code is 'Foreign Direct Investment in Bermuda' or the source-bank's equivalent 'Capital Contribution' / 'Equity Injection' code; the remitter's name must match the registered shareholder, and the exempted company name and Bermuda registration number must be included as the beneficiary reference. Verification requires that the source bank runs standard CDD on the remitter and that the Bermuda bank confirms the beneficial ownership control number against the BMA register. Funds arrive as USD or BMD at 1:1, with no FX cost; sanitised clearing is via the US Fedwire/CHIPS network through the Bermudian bank's New York correspondent.Clearing rails for trade settlements include SWIFT MT103 single payment transfers for recurring supplier remittances, and SWIFT MT700 Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit for high-value consignment-backed orders. Where the supplier is invoiced in USD, disbusements settle over Fedwire/CHIPS through the Bermudian bank's US correspondent. For non-USD suppliers (GBP/EUR/CNH), the Bermudian bank converts at its dealing desk under the BMD/USD peg; for sub-USD 10,000 disbursements, an overseas EMI in the supplier's jurisdiction can serve as the pay-out rail, but it cannot hold or receive the Bermuda treasury balance.Forward outright USD/BMD trades, though economically redundant given the fixed 1:1 pegCross-currency swaps between USD and GBP/EUR/CNH to hedge subscription-box input cost exposureNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted currencies (e.g., CNH/RMB) to hedge supplier costs without requiring BMD deliverabilityCurrency options and collars on USD against major trade currencies, permitted under BMA derivative licensing thresholds for the non-resident exempted company

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BDA 0 (no Bermuda transfer-pricing statute or documentation threshold exists). If the entity is managed and controlled from the UK or is connected to UK taxed entities, HMRC's transfer-pricing rules and master file/local file documentation thresholds can apply extraterritorially; separate UK documentation may be required once group revenue or controlled transaction value crosses the HMRC thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda's Economic Substance Regulations 2018 mandate that any exempted company performing a relevant activity must have substantive economic presence in Bermuda: physical premises, qualified employees, inward expenditure, conduct of core income-generating activities, and periodic board meetings held in Bermuda. A distribution and service centre activity is relevant where goods are purchased from or marketed on behalf of a related party; a direct-to-consumer retail subscription box operation not involving related-party goods or services is outside the relevant-activity net, but still must file an annual ESD confirming its non-relevant or out-of-scope status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: HMRC can assess UK corporation tax on the Bermuda company's permanent establishment under UK CTA 2010 Part 24, apply the Diverted Profits Tax at 25%, impose penalties up to 100% of the tax and compound interest. Bermuda regulators can separately fine the entity BDA 250,000 and imprison responsible persons for up to five years for knowingly submitting false declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable as shadow directors; the corporate veil can be pierced for fraud, dishonest assistance, and facilitation of UK tax evasion. UK law may personally assess penalties on senior accounting officers and directors who fail to prevent tax evasion, and confiscation orders, travel bans and director disqualification can follow.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike the company off and dissolve it. Under the Economic Substance Regulations 2018, failures attract BDA 10,000 for first non-compliance, BDA 50,000 for continuing non-compliance, and BDA 250,000 for false ESR statements, with imprisonment up to five years; failure to provide accurate financial records adds a further fine of BDA 5,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for ESR penalties and may be imprisoned; a corporate director's shadow directors or ultimate beneficial owner can be required to make civil restitution, be disqualified from acting as directors, and be subject to asset freezing and travel restrictions.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Violations

Statutory sanction: Bermuda's AML/ATF regime imposes unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; beneficial ownership non-compliance carries initial penalties of BDA 10,000 and repeated penalties of BDA 25,000 with up to two years imprisonment; financial sanctions violations can lead to asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors, money laundering reporting officers, and compliance officers face criminal prosecution, personal fines, confiscation and restraint orders, travel bans, and disqualification. The corporate veil is disregarded where fraud perpetrators use the exempted company as a fraudulent conduit.
Permanent Establishment / Payroll and UK Attribution

Statutory sanction: HMRC can issue discovery assessments for unpaid corporation tax and VAT, allocate the full attributable profit to the UK permanent establishment, impose late filing penalties, daily penalties of up to GBP 100, and penalty dishonesty assessments of up to 100% of tax; PAYE unpaid amounts carry employer liability and interest.

Director personal liability: Senior accounting officers under UK FA 2009 Schedule 46 may be personally liable for GBP 5,000 penalties for inadequate tax accounting arrangements; directors who fail to account for PAYE and NIC can have personal liability under employment tax avoidance rules, together with disqualification and potential extradition or travel restrictions in serious cases.
