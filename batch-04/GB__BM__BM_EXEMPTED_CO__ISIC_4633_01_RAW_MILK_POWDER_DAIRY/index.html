Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda is a separate fiscal territory (British Overseas Territory) and imposes no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or withholding tax. The competent administrative bodies are: (1) Registrar of Companies, Ministry of Finance - incorporation, annual returns, company government fees, and Economic Substance for Companies Act 2018 (ES Act) filings via the Bermuda Corporate Registry e-Services portal (eservices.gov.bm); (2) Bermuda Customs Department (Ministry of Finance) - customs tariff administration on the ASYCUDA World electronic declaration platform; (3) Office of the Tax Commissioner / Department of Tax Compliance - exclusively for employer payroll tax (Payroll Tax Act 1995), land tax, and stamp duty. HM Revenue and Customs (UK) has no tax authority over Bermuda; the United Kingdom VAT Act 1994 and UK corporation tax legislation do not extend to Bermuda. The GB sovereign layer applies only in constitutional, sanctions/AML, and financial passporting contexts.

E-government filing portal

No income tax or VAT registration exists in Bermuda. A foreign-owned dairy commodity trader must: (1) incorporate a Bermuda Exempted Company under the Companies Act 1981 through a licensed Corporate Service Provider in Hamilton, receiving a Certificate of Incorporation and a permanent Companies File Number from the Registrar of Companies - no minimum capital is required and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted; (2) appoint a registered office and resident secretary in Bermuda and pay the annual government fee (currently approximately BMD 1,560 to BMD 27,830 depending on authorised share capital); (3) make a one-time application for a Tax Assurance Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 confirming statutory exemption from any future Bermuda tax on income, capital gains, dividends, or interest imposed before 31 March 2035; (4) obtain the non-resident designation under the Exchange Control Act 1972 to remove currency control restrictions and enable SWIFT LC/TT settlement for exported whole and skimmed milk powder; (5) if goods physically pass through Bermuda or staff are employed locally, register for a Customs Importer/Exporter Account with Bermuda Customs and for employer payroll tax with the Office of the Tax Commissioner; and (6) file the mandatory annual Economic Substance Declaration (ESD) with the Registrar of Companies. An exempted company trading exclusively outside Bermuda is not required to hold a Bermuda Business Licence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election and no EU/UK hybrid-mismatch election is available in Bermuda; the zero-tax exempted status applies automatically. Recognised elections and mechanisms include: (1) the one-time election for a Tax Assurance Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 guaranteeing tax immunity until 31 March 2035; (2) the non-resident designation election under the Exchange Control Act 1972, which confers unrestricted foreign-currency dealings and no FX repatriation constraints for export proceeds; (3) structural elections under the Companies Act 1981, including multi-class share structures and redomiciliation (continuation) into Bermuda; and (4) a written Economic Substance self-assessment election when completing the annual ESD to classify the exempted company's relevant activity (e.g., distribution and service centre or pure equity holding) and thereby determine any substance reporting obligations. UK hybrid mismatch rules under TIOPA 2010 affect UK-resident investors rather than the Bermuda company itself.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_VAT_No_GST_No_Sales_Tax: Bermuda operates a customs-duty-only indirect tax regime (Customs Act 1964 and Customs Tariff Act 1970). Exports, re-exports, and transhipments of goods from Bermuda, including whole and skimmed milk powder (ISIC 4633 / NAICS 424430), attract nil export duty and are outside the scope of UK VAT.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST regime, hence no economic-turnover threshold. Any business importing or exporting cargo through Bermuda must maintain a free Customs Importer/Exporter Account with the Bermuda Customs Department via ASYCUDA World; no turnover, capital, or residency condition attaches. Import customs duty is assessed ad valorem under the Customs Tariff Act 1970 at the rate applicable to the tariff classification of the goods (dairy products are generally dutiable on import; duty-free reliefs apply for re-export, bonded warehousing, and transhipment).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

There is no VAT system in Bermuda, so the concept of 0% export VAT applies only by analogy as outside the scope. Bermuda law imposes no export duty and no indirect tax on the sale of goods to a non-Bermudian purchaser for delivery outside Bermuda, provided the goods are physically exported within a commercially reasonable period and documented as such. If the milk powder had been imported into Bermuda and is subsequently exported, duty suspension under bonded warehouse procedures or a duty drawback claim under the Customs Act 1964 (perfecting export with proof of export documentation) constitutes the sole indirect-tax refund path. No Bermuda statute imposes a 180-day FX repatriation deadline; the non-resident designation under the Exchange Control Act 1972 permits open currency settlement for SWIFT LC and TT wire proceeds, although standard banking/AML practice may request an inward remittance certificate or final bank statement showing receipt of export proceeds. For deliveries into the UK/EU, import VAT is collected at destination by the importing member state and is not a Bermuda obligation.

No real-time tax-clearance invoice regime, no fiscal e-invoicing API, and no digital stamping or serialisation requirement exists in Bermuda for B2B dairy commodity transactions. Commercial invoices and packing lists are exchanged bilaterally in paper/PDF or via trade-platform integration. The only electronic clearance mandate is customs-related: import/export/re-export declarations must be lodged in ASYCUDA World, the customs single-window platform, with supporting Bill of Lading/Airway Bill, Certificate of Origin (where preferential), and insurance documents. A broader National Single Window for trade facilitation is under phased implementation by the Ministry of Finance, but it imposes no additional tax-invoice clearance obligation.

No VAT clawback or export zero-rating fraud penalty exists in Bermuda because no VAT is chargeable. The material enforcement and clawback risks are: (1) Customs breach - false declarations, undervaluation or mis-description of goods, or diversion of transhipped/duty-drawback dairy cargo into Bermudian home consumption triggers forfeiture of goods, penalties of up to three times the duty evaded, and criminal sanction (including imprisonment) for smuggling under the Customs Act 1964; (2) Economic substance failure - if the ES Act 2018 classifies the trading activity as a relevant activity (e.g., distribution and service centre or holding business) and the entity fails the economic substance test, the Registrar may levy a penalty of up to BMD 250,000 and daily penalties for non-filing of the annual ESD, with possible strike-off and automatic exchange of information with HMRC and EU authorities; (3) Exchange-control or AML review - inadequate evidence of non-resident status or of the commercial substance of SWIFT LC/TT flows may attract AML regulatory scrutiny under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2018 and prospective exclusion from correspondent banking; and (4) EUTPA assurance protection can be retrospectively challenged if the company engages in domestic Bermuda trade or is determined not to be a qualifying exempted undertaking, thereby exposing future profits without the 2035 tax-protection shield.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 with the US dollar (USD). Current account transactions, including trade payments, operational expenses, interest and dividends, are freely convertible and transferable for a Bermuda exempted company that maintains an Authorised Dealer relationship and complies with standard exchange control reporting. Capital account injections (equity subscription) are permitted in foreign currency without an upfront BMA permit, subject to evidenced source of funds and CRS/FATCA documentation. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted except for any applicable domestic fees; Bermuda does not impose income tax, withholding tax, or capital gains tax on exempted companies. There is no mandatory conversion or surrender obligation for receipts denominated in foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda does not impose export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratios. Export receipts may be retained in the invoicing currency (typically USD or EUR) in external or on-island multi-currency accounts. Where BMD is received, funds are credited at the fixed 1:1 peg, but there is no statutory timeline for repatriation and no fixed percentage conversion to local currency. The exempted company must maintain underlying trade documentation, including commercial invoices, bills of lading and inspection certificates, to support exchange control reporting by the Authorised Dealer.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), the integrated regulator and central monetary authority for Bermuda, administering the Exchange Control Act 1972, the Companies Act 1981, and the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering) Regulations 2008.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Low as a primary banking channel, Medium only for ancillary treasury visibility. Bermuda's Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 makes it unlawful to accept Bermuda-currency or regulated deposits without a BMA banking licence. EU and UK EMIs, such as Wise Business, Revolut Business or Railsr, are not licensed in Bermuda and cannot lawfully provide true Bermudian account numbers or deposit acceptance. They may support low-value payment initiation and FX conversion outside Bermuda but cannot issue documentary letters of credit, act as an Authorised Dealer, or provide the regulatory anchoring required for a dairy commodity trading treasury. A conventional Tier-1 Bermudian bank remains mandatory.

Compliant protocol for inbound capital injection: instruct the remitting bank to send a SWIFT MT103 with Level 1 and Level 2 correspondent details, using the beneficiary account at HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield with a clear remittance reference of Capital Subscription per the Memorandum and Articles of Association. In UK outbound wires, use HMRC purpose of payment code T100 General Capital Transfer or T106 Other Transfer of Capital. For USD inbound, the funds typically clear via CHIPS or Fedwire to the Bermudian bank's US correspondent; for GBP/EUR, use CHAPS or TARGET2 with the appropriate BIC. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (Bermuda levies no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no tax on profits or income of an exempted company)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Bermuda imposes no withholding tax or dividend tax on distributions paid by an exempted company to any shareholder, resident or non-resident).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None in Bermuda: no domestic transfer-pricing statute, no contemporaneous documentation threshold and no CbC filing obligation for Bermuda entities. Group-level OECD BEPS Action 13 rules may nevertheless apply via the ultimate parent in the UK/GB when consolidated turnover exceeds GBP 200 million or EUR 750 million, triggering UK CbC reporting under Schedule 19A FA 2016; the Bermuda entity must then pass accounting data to the group and respond to UK HMRC information requests under the UK-Bermuda TIEA.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda's International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act 2018 requires an exempted company carrying on a 'relevant activity' to satisfy a dual test in Bermuda: (i) governance - board meetings held in Bermuda, adequate quorum, board minutes confirming strategic direction, and directors possessing necessary expertise; (ii) operations - adequate physical premises, full-time employees, local expenditure and CIGA actually performed locally. A milk-powder wholesale trader generating distribution income is classified under 'Distribution and Service Business'; if purchasing, contract execution, shipping control or logistics are instead managed from London, the entity fails Bermudian substance and simultaneously becomes subject to UK corporation tax on a permanent establishment and potentially UK diverted profits tax at 25% of diverted profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Artificial avoidance of UK tax (CFC / Diverted Profits Tax)

Statutory sanction: UK CFC charge under TIOPA 2010 Part 9A, apportioning up to 100% of the Bermudian company's chargeable profits to UK shareholders; Diverted Profits Tax under FA 2015 s.77 at 25% of adjusted diverted profits; possible HMRC penalty of up to 100% of the tax plus interest, and reference to the HMRC Enablers of Avoidance regime with penalties of up to 50% of fees charged to the entity.

Director personal liability: In egregious cases, officers can be personally assessed to a penalty of GBP 5,000 per tax-avoidance arrangement under FA 2015 s.95; deliberate misrepresentation may result in director disqualification, UK travel restrictions pending criminal investigation, and civil restitution proceedings for the unremitted tax.
Shell company with no economic substance (Bermuda ESR failure)

Statutory sanction: Under the International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act 2018: default penalty of BD$10,000 plus BD$10,000 for each day of continuing failure; the Registrar may apply to court for an order requiring the company to provide information, and the Minister may strike the entity off the register; EU/UK listing as a non-cooperative jurisdiction may remove the existing 2035 tax-protection guarantee.

Director personal liability: Each director or senior officer who knowingly permits non-compliance commits an offence punishable by a fine of up to BD$10,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; directors remain personally liable for outstanding annual government fees and penalties where the company is struck off and dissolved.
AML / anti-money-laundering non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2021: unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 7 years for laundering; civil penalty of up to BD$1 million per breach imposed by the Bermuda Financial Intelligence Agency; freezing of corporate bank accounts and exclusion from SWIFT, LC financing and correspondent banking.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be indicted as 'consenting officers' under the Proceeds of Crime Act, face personal financial penalties, disqualification from acting as a director, orders freezing personal assets, and international travel bans during criminal proceedings.
False filings and beneficial-ownership concealment

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: a fine of up to BD$5,000 plus BD$500 per day of continuing default under s.378 of the Companies Act 1981; the Registrar may strike off the exempted company, causing the loss of limited liability and exposing assets to creditors; an inaccurate or false statement may support a court order for restoration on unfavourable terms.

Director personal liability: Signing officers face civil restitution to creditors, disqualification orders under the Directors and Officers Disqualification Act, and personal liability for the company's debts if the company is wound up without proper accounting records, pursuant to s.375 of the Companies Act 1981.
