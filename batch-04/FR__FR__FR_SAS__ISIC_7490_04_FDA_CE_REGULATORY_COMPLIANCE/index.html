Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises administered by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; RCS registration executed by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered office department.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title II, Chapter VIII: Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-30 and R. 227-1 to R. 227-11 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); general company provisions of the Civil Code Articles 1832 to 1844-17; Decree No. 67-236 of 23 March 1967 on the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de Constitution (online dynamic dossier of the Guichet Unique, equivalent to Cerfa Form No. 15702) submitted with the signed Statuts (Articles of Association).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum share capital for an SAS (may be set as low as EUR 1); however, where cash capital is stated, at least 50% of the subscribed cash capital must be paid up at incorporation and the balance within five years. Funds must be deposited with a French licensed bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the attestation de dépôt de fonds must accompany the filing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), operating through the national portal impots.gouv.fr and the decentralized Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) assigned to the SAS registered office; non-resident and cross-border matters are handled by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) and the Direction des Résidents à l'Étranger et des Services Généraux (DRESG).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the SAS via the Guichet des Formalités Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) to receive SIREN/SIRET from INSEE; 2) the SIRET is the domestic tax account identifier and the SIREN forms the base of the EU VAT number (FR + 2-digit control key + SIREN), automatically allocated by the SIE upon registration (Article 286 CGI); 3) for a non-established foreign entity operating with or without a permanent establishment in France, registration for VAT is made with the SIEE using CERFA Form No. 14080 (Demande d'immatriculation), naming a fiscal representative with joint and several liability (Article 289 A CGI) when the entity is established outside the EU; 4) corporate income tax registration is completed via Form No. 1000 (Extrait d'immatriculation) filed within 30 days of creating a French permanent establishment; 5) a foreign SAS shareholder is not required to obtain a French tax ID for purely passive investment, but the SAS must file the annual beneficial ownership declaration (RBE) under Article 1649 C CGI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: An SAS is by default fully subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) under Article 206-1 CGI on its worldwide profits, with a reduced 15% rate on the first €42,500 of taxable income (for taxpayers with turnover ≤ €10 million) and the standard 25% rate above; foreign branch income is exempt under the French territoriality principle (Article 209 CGI); Tax-Exempt Zone: no general tax-free zone applies to a commercial SAS registered in metropolitan France, although overseas-department specific regimes (e.g., LODEOM) exist but are not available to a mainland-registered consulting entity.

Available election forms

1) Pass-through election under Article 239 bis AA CGI: a qualifying small SAS (created by natural persons, 50-100% of capital held by individuals who are also manager-directors, turnover ≤ €10 million, exercising a commercial/liberal activity) may elect to be taxed as a conduit under Impôt sur le Revenu (IR) for up to 5 fiscal years, with irrevocable waiver permitted; 2) Young Innovative Company (JEI) regime under Article 44 sexies CGI for SAS with qualifying R&D expenditure ≥ 15%, giving a 3-year full corporate income tax exemption; 3) VAT franchise en base de TVA exemption under Article 293 B CGI is automatic below the thresholds but the SAS may positively elect to waive it; 4) Corporate income tax group consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI requires ≥ 95% direct/indirect ownership and is effective for at least 5 tax years; 5) No US-style check-the-box exists in France; hybrid entity classification is denied and subject to OECD BEPS Action 2 / Articles 205-212 quinquies CGI anti-hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€36,800 annual turnover (dominant services and liberal professions, 2024-2025 threshold) and €91,900 (dominant supply of goods) for the franchise en base de TVA exemption under Article 293 B CGI; the following-year threshold is exceeded at €39,100 / €101,000 respectively; a newly created SAS must register from its first taxable transaction if it does not qualify for the franchise, whereas a non-established foreign entity supplying taxable services in France is automatically in scope without any minimum threshold and must use reverse-charge, OSS, or direct registration mechanisms.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the EU are zero-rated (0% TVA) under Articles 262-I-1 and 262-I-2 CGI if the physical export is completed and evidenced within a 90-day period from the tax becoming chargeable; for the core FDA 510(k) / CE Mark regulatory consulting services, French VAT is displaced by the place-of-supply rule in Article 259-1 CGI when the client is established outside France (B2B intra-EU supplies to taxable persons follow reverse-charge under Article 259 A CGI, while B2B supplies to non-EU clients are territorially excluded under Article 259 B CGI and bear no French VAT); eligibility is conditioned on obtaining and preserving the client's EU VAT number (VIES validated) or, for non-EU clients, a certification of establishment outside the EU, the contract, and correspondence evidencing the supply of consultancy and the absence of any French establishment of the recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Document Administratif Unique / DAU) filed in the DELTA-X electronic system for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing departure from EU territory within 90 days
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, and Incoterms clause demonstrating transfer of risk and possession
  • Client EU VAT registration listing (VIES printout) or non-EU establishment certificate (e.g., overseas company registry excerpt)
  • Bank wire confirmation or Inward Remittance Certificate proving settlement from the non-EU or cross-border client
  • Supporting performance dossier for the consulting engagement (scope of work, FDA/CE submission tracking, correspondence) to substantiate the genuineness of the offshore service supply

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing under Ordonnance n° 2022-1290, phased from 1 September 2026 (all enterprises must receive; large and mid-size enterprises must issue) and 1 September 2027 (SMEs/micro-enterprises must issue); structured PDF/A3-Factur-X, UBL, or CII formats must be transmitted through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or certified Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires (PDP) with mandatory semantic controls; e-reporting of B2C transaction and payment data is transmitted to the DGFiP by the 10th day of the month following the transaction; France does not operate an Italian-style real-time fiscal stamping or per-invoice authorization clearance, but the PPF/PDP API architecture performs real-time schema validation, blockchain-grade traceability, and cross-referencing with VIES, enabling immediate audit flags on VAT ID mismatches and zero-rating claims.

France imposes no statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation rule; the 180-day parameter serves as the prudent evidentiary benchmark because the DGFiP, under Articles L. 13 AA and L. 13 B LPF, presumes the non-existence of an export operation where payment is not received within reasonable commercial terms and may reclassify the entire turnover as domestic taxable supplies at 20% TVA; penalties then apply cumulatively: intérêts de retard at 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI), a 40% bad-faith penalty (Article 1729 CGI) or 80% penalty for organised fraud (Article 1730 CGI), a 5% late-filing penalty (Article 1731 bis CGI), and a 100% clawback of improperly zero-rated VAT under Article 1788 A CGI for missing intra-EU removals; criminal tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI (€500,000 fine / 5 years' imprisonment, increased to €3 million / 7 years when committed by an organised gang) may be charged, and the SAS president may be held personally and jointly liable under Article L. 267 LPF where fraudulent manoeuvres rendered the corporate collection impossible.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions within the European Union framework. France generally has no exchange-control approval requirement for foreign ownership, capital contributions, intercompany loans, dividend payments, service fees, royalty payments, or repatriation of lawful profits. Transactions remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, and financial-reporting rules. French banks may require evidence of the transaction purpose, source of funds, beneficial ownership, tax residence, contractual basis, and corporate approvals before execution. Dividend distributions by an SAS require distributable profits or reserves, approved financial statements, and applicable withholding-tax analysis. French domestic dividend withholding is generally 25%, subject to treaty relief, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions, statutory exemptions, and anti-abuse requirements. Interest, royalties, and service payments require separate withholding-tax, treaty, VAT, transfer-pricing, and beneficial-ownership analysis. Non-EU investment-screening, sanctions, and foreign-subsidy rules may apply to transactions involving sensitive activities or restricted counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no general statutory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or deposited with a French bank. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be received through other lawful EU or international banking channels. The company must retain commercial, customs, invoice, transport, and settlement records and comply with applicable accounting, VAT, Intrastat, customs, statistical, tax, and anti-money-laundering obligations. Cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more entering or leaving the European Union must be declared to customs, and intra-EU cash transport is subject to applicable French reporting rules. Banks may impose contractual transaction-monitoring, documentary, and reporting requirements even though no currency-surrender obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, as the French central bank within the Eurosystem; the European Central Bank is responsible for euro monetary policy and relevant Eurosystem payment-system oversight. The Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution supervises French credit institutions and certain payment institutions, while the Autorité des marchés financiers supervises investment services and financial markets.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment operations and low to medium for full treasury banking. An EU-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, SEPA transfers, card acquiring, foreign-exchange conversion, and certain cross-border services under passporting or local authorisation. An EMI payment account is not a bank deposit, normally does not provide deposit insurance, and may not be suitable for statutory capital deposits, overdrafts, complex trade finance, custody of restricted funds, or high-value treasury concentration. Offshore non-EU digital EMIs cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to French customers merely by operating online; they require an applicable EU authorisation, passport, French establishment or lawful cross-border basis, and compliance with French and EU AML, sanctions, safeguarding, outsourcing, data-protection, and consumer rules. The company should maintain a regulated French or EU bank as the primary account and use an EMI only as a documented secondary rail after confirming safeguarding, settlement-bank exposure, account-closure rights, geographic coverage, and permitted business activity.

Route shareholder equity through a French or EU regulated bank to the SAS capital-deposit account before incorporation or to the operational account after incorporation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment operations and low to medium for full treasury banking. An EU-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, SEPA transfers, card acquiring, foreign-exchange conversion, and certain cross-border services under passporting or local authorisation. An EMI payment account is not a bank deposit, normally does not provide deposit insurance, and may not be suitable for statutory capital deposits, overdrafts, complex trade finance, custody of restricted funds, or high-value treasury concentration. Offshore non-EU digital EMIs cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to French customers merely by operating online; they require an applicable EU authorisation, passport, French establishment or lawful cross-border basis, and compliance with French and EU AML, sanctions, safeguarding, outsourcing, data-protection, and consumer rules. The company should maintain a regulated French or EU bank as the primary account and use an EMI only as a documented secondary rail after confirming safeguarding, settlement-bank exposure, account-closure rights, geographic coverage, and permitted business activity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400 million (turnover or total assets) for full documentation (Master File and Local File); simplified documentation for entities with turnover or assets between €50 million and €400 million.

Economic substance scope: France applies OECD BEPS principles and has a general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) under Article L.64 of the Tax Procedure Code. While no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist, substance is required to avoid challenges to the corporate veil and to benefit from tax treaties or EU directives.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (Article 1741 of the French General Tax Code). Civil penalties: 40% to 80% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they have committed fraud or serious misconduct. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 40% penalty for abuse of law (Article L.64).

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for the company's tax debts if the corporate veil is pierced due to artificiality.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 10% of turnover for legal entities (Monetary and Financial Code). Criminal penalties for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned up to 5 years for money laundering offenses (Article 324-1 of the Penal Code).
