Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum statutory capital is imposed for an SAS; the Articles of Association determine the issued capital. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and deposited in a blocked bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations before registration. Funds are released only upon presentation of the Kbis. Where the Articles permit partial release, the unpaid balance must be called within five years. In-kind contributions are subject to a report from a commissaire aux apports unless the applicable de minimis exemption applies.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office must comply with the fire-safety and accessibility requirements of the Code de la construction et de l'habitation for établissements recevant du public where the premises are open to clients. The employer must prepare and maintain the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP), provide adequate emergency exits and fire extinguishers, and subscribe to mandatory workplace accident and employer liability insurance. Structural load clearance is not applicable because no industrial equipment is installed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

French Directorate General of Public Finances (Direction générale des finances publiques, DGFiP), administered through the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Primary online infrastructure: impots.gouv.fr professional portal and the business formalities one-stop shop operated through the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI) Guichet unique. French VAT identification numbers are issued and administered by the DGFiP.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is incorporated and reported through the INPI Guichet unique, which transmits formation data to the commercial registry, INSEE and the DGFiP. The entity receives a SIREN number for the legal entity and a SIRET number for each establishment. The competent SIE assigns a French intra-Community VAT number, generally in the format FR plus a two-character control key and the nine-digit SIREN, after validating the entity and its taxable activities. The company must activate its professional account on impots.gouv.fr, register for corporate income tax and VAT obligations, identify the applicable VAT regime, and provide bank, registered-office, activity and beneficial-owner information where requested. A foreign entity without a French establishment must request French VAT registration from the competent SIE when it conducts French-taxable transactions requiring registration. An EU-established taxable person generally does not require a French fiscal representative; a non-EU-established taxable person carrying out French VAT-taxable transactions generally must appoint an accredited French tax representative, subject to applicable mutual-assistance exceptions. VAT registration does not by itself create a permanent establishment for corporate income tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying SAS may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu, IR), generally for a maximum of five financial years, if statutory conditions concerning age of the company, business activity, ownership, employee numbers and turnover or balance-sheet size are satisfied. The election is subject to prescribed timing and notification requirements and is not a general United States-style check-the-box election. France has no general entity-classification election permitting a SAS to select partnership treatment without satisfying the statutory IR option. Anti-hybrid-mismatch rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance framework may deny deductions or require inclusion for arrangements producing deduction-without-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes; they do not constitute a routine entity election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France has no general VAT registration threshold for a business that is liable to French VAT outside the franchise regime. For French-established businesses eligible for the VAT franchise, the principal annual turnover thresholds are EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods, sales for consumption on premises and accommodation, and EUR 37,500 for services and non-commercial activities. Tolerance thresholds are generally EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250 respectively. Exceeding the applicable threshold can cause VAT liability from the statutory date and requires timely registration or regime change. Cross-border services, intra-Community acquisitions, imports, exports, and transactions subject to reverse charge can create registration, reporting or identification obligations independently of domestic turnover thresholds. For intra-Community distance sales and certain electronically supplied consumer services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold and the One Stop Shop rules may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT when the statutory export conditions are satisfied and the exporter or qualifying intermediary can substantiate exit from the EU customs territory through accepted customs records. The exporter must retain a customs export declaration and evidence of actual exit, with the exporter of record and transaction chain correctly identified. Services supplied to a business customer established outside France are generally taxed according to the B2B place-of-supply rule and are outside the scope of French VAT in France rather than technically zero-rated, subject to customer-status and use-and-enjoyment rules. Services supplied to an EU business customer are generally subject to the reverse charge in the customer's Member State. Services supplied to consumers require separate place-of-supply analysis. A creator-MCN agency must distinguish agency services, advertising or marketing services, rights licensing, platform fees, digital services and creator payouts, because characterization and customer status can change the VAT treatment. Input VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices and a direct economic link to taxable transactions, including qualifying exports.

  • Electronic customs export declaration containing the correct exporter, declarant, consignee, commodity, value and procedure data
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent electronic evidence that the goods left the European Union customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, transport documentation and packing or delivery records sufficient to reconcile the goods, parties and export transaction
  • Contract, purchase order and accounting records establishing the export sale and the applicable VAT treatment
  • Proof of customer identity and non-EU destination where relevant
  • Bank payment evidence may support commercial substantiation but is not a universal French statutory substitute for customs exit evidence
  • An inward remittance certificate is not a generally mandatory French export-VAT document and France has no universal requirement to collect export proceeds through a prescribed banking certificate

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting under the 2024 Finance Law timetable. From 1 September 2026, all French-established businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices; large and intermediate-sized businesses must generally issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from that date. Small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises must generally issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from 1 September 2027. Transactions with consumers and certain cross-border transactions are generally subject to electronic transaction reporting rather than domestic B2B invoice transmission. Invoices are transmitted through an approved private platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire, PDP), with the public portal retaining directory and data-exchange functions under the implementation model. France does not operate a universal pre-clearance or real-time digital-stamping system for every invoice comparable to a mandatory continuous transaction control regime. Electronic invoices must contain required French VAT data, structured or hybrid machine-readable information, customer identification, VAT treatment, payment and transaction data, and must satisfy authenticity, integrity and legibility requirements. Sector-specific public-procurement e-invoicing rules may also apply.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds and no universal 180-day repatriation rule for claiming export VAT treatment. The DGFiP may deny the VAT exemption or reverse a refund where export status, customer status, place of supply, customs exit, transaction reality or invoice data is not substantiated. Unpaid or improperly refunded VAT may be assessed retrospectively, together with late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month and tax penalties that may include 40% for deliberate failure and 80% for fraudulent activity or fraudulent manoeuvres, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards. Customs duties, import VAT, customs interest and customs penalties may apply separately where customs declarations or proof of exit are inaccurate. Serious intentional VAT fraud may also generate criminal exposure under French tax and criminal law. Records, invoices, contracts, platform settlement reports, creator payment schedules, customer-location evidence, customs data and banking records should be retained for the applicable French statutory audit and limitation periods.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has no general exchange-control regime restricting ordinary current-account payments, including payment for services, royalties, imports, dividends, interest, and other legitimate commercial obligations. The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, customs, beneficial-ownership, and documentation requirements. A French SAS may generally receive foreign capital, retain foreign-currency balances, convert funds, and remit dividends or other distributions to non-resident shareholders after satisfaction of corporate-law, accounting, solvency, withholding-tax, transfer-pricing, and beneficial-ownership requirements. Dividend distributions require distributable profits and proper corporate approval; interim dividends require statutory conditions. Interest, royalties, management fees, and intra-group service payments must be commercially justified, arm's-length, properly documented, and reviewed for French withholding tax, treaty relief, VAT, and transfer-pricing consequences. Banks may delay, reject, or report transactions that lack a clear economic purpose or adequate source-of-funds evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a central bank and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export and cross-border service proceeds may generally be retained in euros or foreign currencies and held in France or abroad, subject to accounting, tax, customs, statistical, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering obligations. Payment-service providers and banks may request invoices, contracts, proof of delivery or service performance, counterpart information, and transaction-purpose evidence. Large or unusual international payments may be subject to regulatory reporting or enhanced due diligence. Cash movements and certain payment transactions are subject to applicable European Union and French reporting rules.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the oversight of the European Central Bank; the French Treasury and the Directorate General of Customs and Indirect Taxes also administer relevant external-payment, statistical, and trade-reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for transactional payments and foreign-exchange operations, but not a complete substitute for a French relationship bank. A regulated European Union electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, SEPA payments, cards, and foreign-exchange services, subject to its licence, passporting status, product terms, and risk policy. Wise Europe SA is a Belgian authorised payment institution and its business service is not equivalent to a French bank deposit account; availability, local account details, inbound funds, safeguarding, payment limits, and supported currencies are product-specific. Offshore or non-European digital EMIs create higher onboarding, settlement, sanctions, and account-closure risk and may not provide a French IBAN or capital-deposit certificate. They should not be used as the sole rail for French share-capital formation, payroll, taxes, regulated client-money flows, or material TikTok platform receipts without written confirmation of account functionality and compliance acceptance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity contributions and shareholder loans from an account held in the name of the identified shareholder or lender to the SAS corporate account through SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments or SWIFT cross-border transfer for non-euro payments. Use an unambiguous reference such as 'share capital contribution', 'share premium contribution', or 'shareholder loan under agreement dated [date]'; do not combine capital with unrelated operating receipts. For a newly formed SAS, obtain the bank or notary capital-deposit certificate, complete incorporation and registration, then release the funds to the operating account. Maintain the subscription decision, contribution agreement, source-of-funds evidence, bank confirmations, accounting entries, and beneficial-owner information. For foreign-currency transfers, retain the original currency amount, exchange-rate evidence, converted euro amount, fees, and reconciliation. The receiving bank may request the purpose, funding source, ownership chain, tax residence, and supporting agreements. Avoid third-party funding, cash deposits, payment splitting, or unexplained round-dollar transfers. Shareholder loans and related-party funding must be documented, booked, priced on an arm's-length basis, and reviewed for French interest-deduction, withholding-tax, thin-capitalisation, transfer-pricing, and hybrid-mismatch rules.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area, subject to bank limits and beneficiary validation. Use SWIFT MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent for non-SEPA or non-euro payments, with complete beneficiary, intermediary-bank, purpose, invoice, and sanctions-screening data. Use commercial documentary letters of credit, including SWIFT MT700 issuance and related amendment, presentation, and settlement messages, only where transaction value and counterparty risk justify documentary control. For ordinary creator, platform, advertising, software, and professional-service payments, standard bank transfer with contract and invoice support is generally more appropriate than a commercial LC. Maintain purchase orders or contracts, invoices, delivery or performance evidence, beneficiary tax and registration details, VAT treatment, withholding-tax analysis, and approval records. Screen beneficiaries, banks, countries, and vessels or goods where relevant. Do not route creator proceeds through personal accounts or make payments to unrelated third parties without a documented assignment or agency basis. Reconcile bank statements to the general ledger and creator-level sub-ledgers, and preserve payment and FX records for accounting, tax, audit, and AML purposes.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with an authorised bank or regulated investment firm, supported by documented forecast or committed commercial exposure and governed by an ISDA or equivalent treasury agreement where appropriate.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies or jurisdictions where physical delivery is restricted or unavailable, subject to counterparty eligibility, applicable local rules, EMIR reporting and risk-mitigation requirements, and documented underlying exposure.Foreign-exchange swaps for short-term liquidity and currency-mismatch management, subject to bank documentation, collateral terms, accounting treatment, and exposure limits.Currency options and zero-cost option structures for identified commercial exposures, subject to suitability, premium, termination, valuation, and governance controls.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps where required to hedge a documented currency and interest-rate exposure associated with qualifying financing.Treasury policy controls requiring board-approved limits, authorised counterparties, segregation of dealing and settlement duties, confirmation matching, valuation, collateral monitoring, hedge-accounting assessment, and prohibition of speculative trading.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for transactional payments and foreign-exchange operations, but not a complete substitute for a French relationship bank. A regulated European Union electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, SEPA payments, cards, and foreign-exchange services, subject to its licence, passporting status, product terms, and risk policy. Wise Europe SA is a Belgian authorised payment institution and its business service is not equivalent to a French bank deposit account; availability, local account details, inbound funds, safeguarding, payment limits, and supported currencies are product-specific. Offshore or non-European digital EMIs create higher onboarding, settlement, sanctions, and account-closure risk and may not provide a French IBAN or capital-deposit certificate. They should not be used as the sole rail for French share-capital formation, payroll, taxes, regulated client-money flows, or material TikTok platform receipts without written confirmation of account functionality and compliance acceptance.
