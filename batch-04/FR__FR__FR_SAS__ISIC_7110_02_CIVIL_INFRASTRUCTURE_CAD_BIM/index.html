Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) where applicable. Official portals: https://www.impots.gouv.fr and https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is incorporated and registered through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). The registration filing generates the French SIREN and SIRET identifiers. The entity then registers for corporate income tax, VAT, payroll taxes, and other applicable taxes with the competent SIE through the business tax account on impots.gouv.fr. The French VAT identification number is issued by the DGFiP and generally consists of the prefix FR, a two-digit control key, and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity without a French establishment must apply to the SIEE where French VAT registration is required; an EU-established business may in specified circumstances use the OSS or reverse-charge mechanisms instead of obtaining a French VAT number. Registration documentation generally includes constitutional documents, proof of registered office, directors' identification, business activity details, bank details, expected turnover, and evidence of the French taxable transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) by default. Eligible SAS entities may elect temporarily for partnership-style taxation under the French income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu, IR), generally for a maximum period of five fiscal years, subject to statutory conditions including size, age, ownership, activity, and shareholder requirements. The election is not an automatic check-the-box election and must be notified to the tax authority within the prescribed deadline. No general US-style entity-classification election exists. France applies anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive, which can deny deductions or impose income inclusion where applicable; these rules are not a taxpayer-selectable hybrid election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the French VAT franchise regime applicable to services, the principal historical thresholds are EUR 37,500 of prior-year turnover and EUR 41,250 where the current-year turnover exceeds the preceding threshold. Thresholds are subject to statutory amendment and special rules. VAT registration may nevertheless be required without reliance on the domestic threshold, including for intra-Community acquisitions, imports, certain cross-border supplies, transactions subject to reverse charge, and taxable transactions connected with a French establishment. For B2B engineering, CAD, and BIM services supplied to a business customer established outside France, the place-of-supply and reverse-charge rules must be assessed separately; the transaction may not require French output VAT but may still require reporting or registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT with right to deduction if the supplier can demonstrate that the goods left the EU customs territory and that the statutory export conditions were satisfied. The supplier must retain the customs export declaration, Movement Reference Number (MRN), electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs evidence, commercial invoice, transport documentation, and transaction records. Services require a separate analysis. B2B engineering, CAD, and BIM services are generally taxed according to the customer-location place-of-supply rule; services supplied to a business customer established outside France are generally outside the scope of French VAT, normally invoiced without French VAT with an appropriate place-of-supply and reverse-charge reference where applicable. This is not technically a French zero-rated export of goods. Services connected with French immovable property, including certain site-specific architectural or engineering services, may instead be taxable where the property is located.

France does not currently operate a general real-time invoice-clearance system for all domestic transactions. Mandatory electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions established in France is being phased in through approved Partner Dematerialization Platforms (Plateformes de dématérialisation partenaires, PDPs): receipt capability for all businesses from 1 September 2026; issuance capability for large and mid-sized businesses from 1 September 2026; and issuance capability for small and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027, subject to legally permitted adjustments. B2B invoices must contain mandatory VAT and transaction data and be transmitted through the prescribed platform architecture. Separate electronic reporting of transaction and payment data applies to transactions outside the domestic B2B e-invoicing scope, including certain exports, intra-Community transactions, and B2C transactions. There is no general French requirement for a digital tax stamp on each invoice.

France does not impose a general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Unsupported or incorrectly applied VAT exemption may be reassessed as French VAT, together with late-payment interest generally calculated at the statutory monthly rate and tax penalties. A 40% penalty may apply for deliberate failure or deliberate inaccuracy, and an 80% penalty may apply for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, subject to statutory conditions. Incorrect customs declarations may trigger customs duties, import VAT reassessment, customs penalties, seizure, or loss of customs simplifications. Serious VAT fraud may also create criminal exposure. Export documentation must be retained for the applicable French accounting and tax limitation period, generally at least six years for tax records, with longer retention potentially required under commercial, customs, or accounting rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility under EU law (Article 63 TFEU). Current-account payments for services, fees and royalties are freely transferable without prior exchange-control authorisation. Capital-account operations, including foreign direct investment, portfolio investment and intercompany loans, are also liberalised, subject only to (i) prior authorisation for direct investments in sensitive/strategic sectors (e.g. defence, artificial intelligence, critical infrastructure) under the French FDI screening framework and (ii) mandatory beneficial-ownership and anti-money-laundering declarations. Profit remittance is unrestricted in foreign-exchange terms, but attracts a domestic dividend withholding tax of 25% unless reduced by an applicable double-tax treaty or eliminated by the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU parent companies. No exchange-control licensing is required for converting EUR into foreign currency or repatriating profits, provided the transfer is executed through an ACPR-authorised credit institution and is declared for balance-of-payments statistical purposes, typically on a currency-by-currency basis in stable economic monetary policy conditions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose a mandatory surrender or fixed mandatory conversion ratio of export proceeds into EUR. Exporters may lawfully retain foreign-currency receipts in a foreign-currency account opened with a French credit institution. The statutory repatriation/collection timeline for trade and services receivables from non-residents is governed by the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier) and commercial payment rules, generally requiring that export receivables be collected and made available in France within 180 days from the due date or from service completion, unless the exporter's credit institution grants a contractual extension. The mandatory conversion ratio is therefore 0% (no statutory obligation to convert export receipts into euros). All cross-border payments in excess of EUR 10,000 per calendar month or per transaction must be reported to Banque de France in the framework of the balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical declaration, regardless of the currency nominated.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting under the regulatory and supervisory framework of the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) and the Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR - Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for EU/EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passported into France under PSD2, such as Qonto, Wise, N26 or Revolut, for operational payment flows and low-value disbursements. Low for offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EU/EEA. Legal constraints: an offshore (non-EU/EEA) EMI without a French ACPR licence or EU passport cannot provide a French-issued IBAN, cannot act as a formal custodian of French registered capital, cannot issue the statutory certificate of deposit (certificat de dépositaire de fonds) required during incorporation of a SAS, and is not accepted by French tax or treasury authorities for balance-of-payments reporting. Even EU-licensed EMIs are not admitted for capital escrow accounts or as the primary treasury bank for large-value cross-border settlements; a fully licensed French credit institution or a French branch of a European credit institution remains mandatory for statutory corporate treasury functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be completed by bank wire transfer using ISO 20022 SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001) for EUR payments or SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR currencies. The receiving bank must be an ACPR-authorised credit institution in France holding the capital contribution either on a blocked account in the name of the SAS in formation or on a dedicated capital escrow account. The exact payment purpose code must be indicated: use Banque de France/European BOP purpose group 'CAPA' (capital payments) or 'INVE' for direct investment equity contributions; where the shareholder is a foreign direct investor, the bank will map the instruction to the French BOP item 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital'. Field 70 must explicitly contain 'APPORT CAPITAL SOCIAL' and a reference to the acte de constitution. Verification: the depositary bank issues a non-negotiable certificate of deposit ('certificat de dépôt des fonds') listing the subscribed capital amount, the account holder, and the release conditions upon registration of the SAS; release of funds to the permanent operating account is permitted only after production of the K-bis extract and the registrar's approval decision.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements and subcontractor payments should follow the following clearing rails: EUR payments inside the European Union must be executed via SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001) in TARGET2 for large-value RTGS payments; non-EUR payments must be dispatched via SWIFT MT103 with strict field discipline, including correct beneficiary bank BIC, correspondent routing, and mandatory purpose code. For engineering/CAD services, pure service disbursements are settled by bank transfer; commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) are appropriate only where the invoice bundles hardware deliverables, certified infrastructure equipment or a construction phase requiring documentary evidence of dispatch. All outbound payments must be supported by the underlying commercial invoice, contract, and, where applicable, a summary of services rendered; payments exceeding EUR 10,000 per month or per transaction trigger Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration. If an escrow arrangement is required for large infrastructure contracts, the escrow account must be maintained at a licensed French or EU credit institution and governed by a French-law escrow agreement.Plain vanilla FX forward (change à terme) executed with a licensed French or EU credit institution, documented as a non-speculative hedge under French accounting principlesFX swap (swap de change) for short-term liquidity and rolling settlement of cross-currency obligations, subject to EMIR clearing/reporting thresholdsCross-currency interest rate swap (CCIRS) used for hedging multi-year infrastructure contracts or project finance debt denominated in foreign currencyNon-deliverable forward (NDF) with cash settlement, used only when the underlying currency is not deliverable in the French market due to settlement restrictions; must be executed with a regulated bank, subject to the same hedge documentation obligations as deliverable forwards

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for EU/EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) passported into France under PSD2, such as Qonto, Wise, N26 or Revolut, for operational payment flows and low-value disbursements. Low for offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EU/EEA. Legal constraints: an offshore (non-EU/EEA) EMI without a French ACPR licence or EU passport cannot provide a French-issued IBAN, cannot act as a formal custodian of French registered capital, cannot issue the statutory certificate of deposit (certificat de dépositaire de fonds) required during incorporation of a SAS, and is not accepted by French tax or treasury authorities for balance-of-payments reporting. Even EU-licensed EMIs are not admitted for capital escrow accounts or as the primary treasury bank for large-value cross-border settlements; a fully licensed French credit institution or a French branch of a European credit institution remains mandatory for statutory corporate treasury functions.
