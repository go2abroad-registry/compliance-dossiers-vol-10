E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company is incorporated under the Companies Act 1981 by the Registrar of Companies; the company registration number serves as the statutory identifier and no general corporate TIN is issued because exempted companies are outside the Bermuda corporate income tax net. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: For Bermuda tax purposes an exempted company is non-resident without ordinary residence; its income, gains, and distributions are fully exempt from Bermuda taxation. Residual local levies (payroll tax, social insurance, customs duty, stamp duty) may apply upon specific in-island triggers. If the entity is a constituent entity of a multinational group with global consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more, the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 (15% top-up tax) applies to fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025; otherwise, no corporate income tax applies.

Available election forms

(i) EUTPA Tax Exemption Certificate: guarantees no future Bermuda tax on income, profits, dividends, or capital gains until at least 31 March 2035, subject to the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 for in-scope MNE groups; (ii) US check-the-box election under IRC S.7701 / Treas. Reg. 301.7701-3 (IRS Form 8832): a Bermuda entity is not a per se corporation, so the Exempted Company may elect corporation, partnership, or disregarded-entity status for US federal tax purposes; (iii) UK hybrid and other mismatch rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A: assessable when the Bermuda entity is involved in hybrid financing structures, with no standalone Bermuda-level filing election; (iv) OECD Pillar Two transitional safe harbours and top-up tax filing elections available under the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 for constituent entities of in-scope MNE groups.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / No GST / No Sales-and-Use Tax: Bermuda imposes no value-added or consumption tax. Indirect taxation comprises Customs Duty on imports under the Customs Tariff Act 2001 and Stamp Duty on specified instruments/transactions; exports of goods and offshore services are entirely outside the Bermudian indirect tax base.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A (no VAT/GST regime exists; no economic turnover threshold). Any entity importing into Bermuda must obtain an Importer/Exporter Code with Bermuda Customs irrespective of transaction value or turnover; all declarations are processed in Bermuda Dollars (BMD), pegged 1:1 to the USD, through ASYCUDA World with a licensed customs broker.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT concept: Bermuda has no VAT, therefore no zero-rating claim, export VAT credit, or refund mechanism exists. Export sales of assembled bionic companion robotic animals are outside the scope of any Bermudian indirect tax and incur zero Bermuda export duty. Imported components subsequently re-exported (including temporary importation for assembly, repair, or finishing) may qualify for customs duty drawback or relief under the Customs Act 1972 and Customs Tariff Act 2001, provided the goods are entered on import, re-exported within the statutory period, and evidenced through ASYCUDA World declarations, bills of lading or airway bills, and physical inspections.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank SWIFT/MT103 evidence of export proceeds for trade compliance review)

Electronic customs clearance is mandatory through ASYCUDA World (Bermuda Customs Automated System) for all import and export declarations. There is no mandatory real-time fiscal invoice clearance, no continuous transaction controls, no API-based tax clearance, and no digital stamping under Bermuda law; commercial invoices, packing lists, and transport documents are lodged as supporting records attached to each ASYCUDA declaration.

Bermuda Customs may retrospectively audit export and drawback claims under the Customs Act 1972. False declarations, diversion of goods to local consumption, or non-repatriation of proceeds trigger clawback of all duty relieved, forfeiture of goods, fines up to BMD 10,000, imprisonment for up to 2 years, and deactivation of the Importer/Exporter Code. Additional penalties under the Economic Substance Act 2018 (fines up to BMD 250,000, restoration orders, and possible winding-up) apply for false economic substance certifications. Bermuda does not maintain statutory exchange controls; the 180-day export-proceeds window is a standard bank and OECD trade-compliance benchmark and should be contractually embedded in the sales terms governing Stripe and credit-card settlement flows.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged 1:1 to the United States Dollar (USD) and both currencies circulate freely in the local economy. For a Bermuda Exempted Company designated as non-resident for exchange control purposes, there are no material restrictions on capital account or current account transactions. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, shareholder loan repayments, and liquidation distributions may be effected without prior BMA approval, although completing a BMA Exchange Control declaration may still be required to maintain formal non-resident status. Currency convertibility is deemed full in practice, with no surrender obligations or discriminatory exchange rate barriers. Transactions in USD are cleared through U.S. correspondent banks, while GBP/EUR transactions are routed through London-European clearing corridors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or statutory conversion timeline applies to export proceeds in Bermuda. The Exchange Control Act 1972 does not impose a general obligation on Exempted Companies to convert export receivables into BMD, and the BMA permits retention of export earnings in foreign currency accounts. Non-resident designated entities are fully exempt from any surrender or repatriation quota. Documentary filing of the BMA Exchange Control declaration and bank-level receipt confirmation are recommended to evidence compliance, but there is no obligation to liquidate USD, GBP, or EUR balances within a specified horizon.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), operating under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969 and the Exchange Control Act 1972.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Limited as a principal banking venue. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) are not licensed under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 in Bermuda and cannot provide a Bermuda-registered IBAN or serve as the primary bank for a Bermuda Exempted Company. Use of such EMIs is legally permissible only for ancillary inbound/outbound GBP/EUR collections and disbursements, and only if the EMI has a compliant EU/UK e-money licence. The BMA does not formally recognise EMIs as deposit-taking institutions for Bermuda corporate treasury purposes, and major Bermuda banks will generally require the Exempted Company to maintain its principal operating account with a BMA-licensed bank. Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary flows, Low as primary clearing rail. Regulatory constraint: no BMA passported EMI regime exists, so offshore EMIs must be treated as unregulated counterparties with heightened credit and sanctions risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital injection must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the remitting shareholder's bank to the Bermuda Exempted Company's USD-denominated account at a BMA-licensed commercial bank, with a purpose code clearly stating 'Capital Subscription' or 'Shareholder Loan'. In the OBI/Remittance Information field, the remitter must reference the company's registration number and the board resolution authorising the injection. Where inbound funds are remitted in GBP or EUR, payment rails may be CHAPS/TARGET2 respectively, with conversion into USD/BMD at the receiving bank. The beneficiary bank will apply sanctions screening to the injection, and the company must be prepared to produce a notarised board resolution and updated shareholder register. No BMA exchange control approval is required for a non-resident Exempted Company; if the entity has inadvertently been treated as resident, the non-resident designation must first be restored using the BMA Form EC/1. For USD wires originating from the United States, the State routing number and correspondent intermediary SWIFT code of the Bermuda bank must be used; all wires must pass through OFAC-compliant U.S. correspondent banks to settle in USD.Disbursements for trade payments use layered rails: (a) supplier deposits and component purchases under USD 250,000 are settled by SWIFT MT103 via U.S. correspondent clearing (Fedwire/CHIPS); (b) larger import contracts are documented with an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700), paid against presentation of ocean/air waybills and commercial invoice; (c) GBP disbursements are cleared over CHAPS through a UK correspondent; EUR payments use TARGET2 via the bank's EU correspondent. Outbound wires must include primary purpose codes such as 'Goods/Intra-Company purchase' or 'Inventory settlement' and be pre-matched to the underlying trade contract and supplier onboarding file. For transactions above the bank's threshold (typically USD 100,000), a compliance officer may request the full trade document package prior to execution. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Limited as a principal banking venue. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) are not licensed under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 in Bermuda and cannot provide a Bermuda-registered IBAN or serve as the primary bank for a Bermuda Exempted Company. Use of such EMIs is legally permissible only for ancillary inbound/outbound GBP/EUR collections and disbursements, and only if the EMI has a compliant EU/UK e-money licence. The BMA does not formally recognise EMIs as deposit-taking institutions for Bermuda corporate treasury purposes, and major Bermuda banks will generally require the Exempted Company to maintain its principal operating account with a BMA-licensed bank. Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary flows, Low as primary clearing rail. Regulatory constraint: no BMA passported EMI regime exists, so offshore EMIs must be treated as unregulated counterparties with heightened credit and sanctions risk.
