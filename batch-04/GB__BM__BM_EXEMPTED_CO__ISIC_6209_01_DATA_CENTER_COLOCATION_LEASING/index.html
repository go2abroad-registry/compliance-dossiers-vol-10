E-government filing portal

A foreign entity acquiring a Bermuda Exempted Company is incorporated under the Companies Act 1981 by filing Memorandum and Articles of Association through a licensed local service provider; the Registrar of Companies issues a Certificate of Incorporation bearing a company registry number, which functions as the base entity identifier. There is no VAT ID in Bermuda. For the new Corporate Income Tax (CIT) introduced by the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025), in-scope constituent entities must register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner via the Bermuda Corporate Taxpayer registration portal using an authorised group representative, obtain a Bermuda Tax Reference Number, and file annual CIT returns. Entities outside the CIT scope are not required to obtain a tax ID solely for corporates; employers separately register for payroll tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – a Bermuda Exempted Company historically benefits from the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (exemption from future Bermudian taxes until 2035, except payroll tax, land tax, stamp duties, customs duties and user taxes); international groups satisfying the OECD Pillar Two nexus may become subject to the 15% Corporate Income Tax.

Available election forms

Bermuda domestic law does not provide a check-the-box election; an exempted company is a body corporate. A Bermuda eligible entity can, for U.S. federal income tax purposes, file IRS Form 8832 to elect classification as a corporation or a disregarded entity, subject to sole-owner limitations. The CIT regime permits group-wide elections, including the appointment of a filing constituent entity and, where applicable, a qualified domestic minimum top-up tax election; standard foreign tax credit and substance-based income exclusion elections are available for CIT return computation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / sales-and-use tax; Bermuda levies customs duties, payroll tax, stamp duty, and land tax instead.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Bermuda has no general VAT/GST registration threshold. Customs registration is required for importers of goods (BMD 50+ consignment); payroll tax registration is required for all employing businesses; CIT registration applies only to in-scope multinational groups under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023.

Services such as data-centre colocation and bare-metal server hosting are outside the scope of Bermudian indirect taxation. Any tax protection certificate or CIT assessment, if any, is governed by the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 and not by an export zero-rating mechanism.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the United States Dollar (USD), and USD is accepted as legal tender in Bermuda alongside BMD. There are no exchange controls restricting capital account convertibility or current account transactions for a Bermuda exempted company, provided transactions are routed through an authorised dealer or custodian bank. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation are freely permitted without BMA approval, subject to the annual Bermuda business exemption filing and any applicable Economic Substance (ES) reporting. The practical hurdle is not currency conversion but documentary proof of beneficial ownership and source of funds during bank onboarding, plus the demonstration of adequate substance in Bermuda to avoid adverse UK tax residence recharacterisation under the UK–Bermuda TIEA framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda does not impose any statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline for export proceeds or service revenue earned by an exempted company. Receipts derived from data center colocation leasing and bare-metal server hosting (ISIC 6209) may be retained in USD, BMD, GBP, or any other convertible currency in the entity's multi-currency account. The sole compliance condition is that all such receipts must pass through a BMA-licensed authorised dealer, and any transaction involving a non-resident must be recorded in the bank's statutory exchange control reporting.

Central bank supervision: The Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) is the central banking and financial services regulatory authority of Bermuda, a British Overseas Territory under the sovereignty of the United Kingdom. The BMA administers the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1981, exercises prudential supervision over licensed banks, and issues monetary policy consistent with the fixed BMD/USD peg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low–Medium. The BMA does not issue standalone Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licences; non-bank payment activity requires a Money Service Business authorisation under the Money Service Business (Prohibition) Act 2022, which the legislature enacted to curb unlicensed MSBs. A UK FCA-authorised EMI (e.g., a dedicated B2B platform) may be used as a supplementary collection channel for GBP-denominated payments from UK colocation clients, but it cannot substitute for a full BMA-licensed bank account. The exempted company must maintain its primary operating account and clearing relationship with HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield; EMI rails are acceptable only for pre-cleared, low-value, repetitive client collections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the UK parent are executed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the Bermuda entity's operational USD or BMD account at HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield. The remittance must state a precise purpose code: 'EQUITY' for subscription of issued share capital or 'LOAN' for shareholder debt evidenced by a formal admitted loan agreement. Supporting documents include the board resolution of both the UK parent and the Bermuda exempted company, a group organisational chart, and a funds-received instruction letter to the receiving bank. For any remittance exceeding USD 100,000 or its BMD equivalent, the receiving bank will require a counterparty bank reference and a source-of-funds declaration; settling the payment in USD and allowing the bank to convert into BMD at the fixed peg will minimise friction. The funds may be credited to a capital escrow account before release to the operational account to satisfy the bank's post-closing verification procedures.Recurring lease settlement from the Bermuda exempted company to UK data center clients and infrastructure subcontractors is executed by clean SWIFT MT103 payment for USD-denominated obligations and by CHAPS via MT103 for GBP-denominated obligations. Documentary credits (commercial LCs) are reserved for one-off capital equipment purchases (server racks, cooling units, power distribution hardware) exceeding USD 250,000, issued by Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda under their standard trade finance framework. The preferred clearing rail for recurring monthly colocation billing is direct MT103/CHAPS rather than LC, because it preserves settlement speed, reduces documentary compliance costs, and avoids adding a trade-product risk profile to an otherwise low-risk service revenue stream. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda transfer pricing documentation threshold applies to ordinary exempted companies because there is no Bermuda corporate income tax baseline. For CITA in-scope MNE groups only, the EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold (approximately BMD 760 million) triggers OECD Pillar Two-aligned Master File and Local File documentation obligations.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018 (as amended) applies where the colocation leasing business is classified as a 'relevant activity' (financing and leasing). The entity must then demonstrate: (i) economic substance in Bermuda, (ii) management and control exercised in Bermuda, (iii) adequate employees, premises, and expenditure commensurate with the leasing activity, and (iv) that core income-generating activities (CIGA) for lease of rack space, power, cooling, and network services are performed in Bermuda. Where the entity acts purely as a passive owner of overseas data center assets, it is not a relevant activity; however, if material income flows via the exempted company, the authorities may reclassify it as an IP-holding vehicle and require enhanced substance. Failure to satisfy ESR triggers penalties, strike-off, and automatic exchange of information with the UK via the TIEA/OECD framework.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Bermuda: ESR strike-off, financial penalties, and information exchange under the UK-Bermuda TIEA; UK: HMRC may treat the company as UK tax-resident, impose corporation tax at 25%, apply Diverted Profits Tax at 25%, and levy penalties of up to 200% of the tax understated under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: civil restitution orders, director disqualification, travel bans under the Proceeds of Crime framework, and imprisonment up to 7 years for cheating the public revenue; personal liability for fraudulent trading under Bermuda Companies Act 1981 s.225 and UK Insolvency Act 1986 s.213.
Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance (Amendment) Act 2019: fixed penalty BMD 5,000 per failed declaration, additional penalty up to BMD 10,000, daily default surcharge BMD 200, compulsory strike-off, and automatic exchange of information with foreign tax authorities; criminal false-declaration penalty up to BMD 50,000 and/or 5 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers in default are jointly and severally liable for unpaid penalties; the Supreme Court may order disqualification, a 5-year travel bar, and seizure of personal assets where the shell structure was used to facilitate tax evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering / Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Bermuda: criminal fines up to BMD 250,000 or three times the benefit (whichever is greater), imprisonment up to 10 years, and revocation of any corresponding banking or EMI relationships; UK: failure to disclose offences under POCA 2002 carry up to 5 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines; cross-border asset freezes may apply.

Director personal liability: Individual liability attaches to directors who knowingly consent or are reckless; civil confiscation orders under the Bermuda Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 and UK POCA 2002, travel bans, and Interpol red notices; personal assets of directors may be frozen or confiscated.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / CITA Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Bermuda: 15% CITA on re-determined allocable profits, statutory interest on the deficiency, and penalty of 25% of understated tax (75% for deliberate concealment); UK: HMRC transfer pricing adjustments under TIOPA 2010 Part 4, CFC charges, and diversion of profits tax at 25% on the shifted margin.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false CITA returns are jointly and severally liable for the group's CIT deficiency; fraudulent transfer pricing may trigger UK Finance Act 2015 penalties of 100% of the diverted tax; criminal proceedings may result in imprisonment up to 7 years and international travel bans.
