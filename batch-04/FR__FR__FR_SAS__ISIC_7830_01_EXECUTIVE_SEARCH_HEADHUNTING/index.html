Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty — taxpayer portal: www.impots.gouv.fr (espace professionnel and EDI). Business registration single-window: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Customs and indirect export controls: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) — www.douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

INSEE automatically attributes the statutory identification numbers: SIREN (9 digits) and SIRET (14 digits). 3. The same filing automatically opens the corporate income tax (IS) and VAT files with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE), which attributes the intra-Community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) in the format FR + 2-digit checksum + 9-digit SIREN. 4. Collect the Kbis extract as conclusive evidence of legal registration. 5. Register a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) in view of the compulsory e-invoicing and e-reporting obligations from September 2027. 7. If goods are ever exported or imported, obtain an EORI number (FR + SIREN) from the DGDDI; non-resident shareholders do not require French tax representation in the case of a French-established SAS.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — an operating French SAS is an opaque, French-tax-resident legal entity subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, article 205 CGI) on its worldwide profits. Profits are taxed at the standard rate of 25%, with a reduced rate of 15% applicable to the first €42,500 of taxable profit where annual turnover is below €10 million (article 219-IV-b CGI). France applies territoriality only to the participation-exemption regime (régime mère-fille) and to foreign permanent-establishment profits under treaty conditions; headhunting and executive-search fee income is fully within the scope of French entity-level taxation. No tax-exempt zone or statutory pass-through classification is available to an operating SAS.

Available election forms

France has no US-style check-the-box or general pass-through election for business companies; an operating executive-search SAS cannot elect transparent taxation. Available statutory elections include: (i) option under article 239 ter CGI for an eligible pure-holding SAS (holding of securities or real estate only, without industrial or commercial activity) to be taxed as a société de personnes — not available to a headhunting agency; (ii) option under article 293 B CGI to waive the VAT franchise en base and become a full VAT taxpayer, valid for two years; (iii) VAT-group election (assujettissement unique) under article 256 C-II CGI for closely linked eligible entities established in France; (iv) French fiscal integration (régime d'intégration fiscale) under article 223 A CGI where the SAS holds at least 95% of a French subsidiary group; (v) no hybrid-mismatch elective relief exists — French transposition of ATAD statutorily denies deductions for hybrid payments and structures; (vi) automatic application of the reduced 15% small-business corporate rate and simplified filing regimes where statutory turnover thresholds are met.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 group-wide annual turnover or balance-sheet total for mandatory Master File and Local File; EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue for Country-by-Country Reporting. French entities with turnover below these thresholds must still maintain simplified transfer-pricing documentation when intra-group transactions are present, as the tax administration may request it on audit.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, French case law and statutory rules enforce substance-over-form, abuse of law (abus de droit), abnormal acts of management (acte anormal de gestion), and the OECD principal purpose test. A shell entity lacking genuine activity, premises, personnel or decision-making functions may be disregarded, and income may be re-attributed under Articles 57 CGI and 155 A CGI.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion & Fictitious Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 80% of the reassessed tax for fraudulent manipulation, 40% for bad-faith understatement; late-payment interest at 0.2% per month; criminal fine for tax evasion up to EUR 500,000 for individuals and EUR 1,000,000 for the corporation; imprisonment up to five years for responsible natural persons.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the entity's tax debts under Article 1745 CGI; travel bans, restriction on directly or indirectly managing legal entities, and imprisonment are available for serious fraud; DGFiP may seek civil restitution of taxes, penalties and interest.
Shell Company & Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization under Article 155 A CGI and abuse-of-law proceedings allowing the tax administration to disregard the shell; reassessment of French-source income over the full limitation period of six or seven years under abuse of law; 80% penalty for artificial arrangements; denial of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is possible where the shell is used to evade French tax; directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and penalties, subject to travel bans and imprisonment for complicity in tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment under Article 57 CGI with latent redistribution of taxable profits; late-payment interest; penalty for inadequate documentation equal to up to 5% of the reassessed amount if documents not provided within thirty days; up to 80% penalty if manipulation is demonstrated.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or conceal non-arm's-length arrangements may be held personally liable for the resulting tax adjustments under managed-company fault rules and also face criminal sanctions if the scheme is fraudulent.
AML & Money-Laundering Red Flags in Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under the French Monetary and Financial Code (L. 561-1 et seq.), legal persons face fines up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for breach of AML/CTF obligations; natural persons face imprisonment up to ten years and fines up to EUR 750,000 for money-laundering; assets may be frozen and seized.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for AML compliance failures; if they know or should know of laundering through retainer payments, they risk travel bans, criminal convictions, disgorgement of criminal assets and imprisonment.
