Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda has no general income tax authority. Fiscal administration is split: (i) Office of the Tax Commissioner (payroll tax and stamp duty filings via the Bermuda Government e-filing portal at www.gov.bm/taxes); (ii) Bermuda Customs Department (customs duties); (iii) Office of the Corporate Income Tax Administrator (CIT Act 2023, applying only to in-scope constituent entities of multinational groups with global revenue of at least EUR 750 million, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025). HMRC is the competent authority only in the contingent scenario where the Bermuda Exempted Company establishes a UK permanent establishment or is centrally managed and controlled from the UK.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate under the Companies Act 1981; the Registrar of Companies issues a Certificate of Incorporation, and the company file number operates as the de facto corporate identifier (Bermuda issues no general TIN). Step 2: Register as an employer with the Office of the Tax Commissioner before making any local payroll payments and obtain a payroll tax registration ID. Step 3: Register with Bermuda Customs only when importing physical goods, obtaining a customs processing ID. Step 4: If the entity becomes a constituent entity of an MNE group meeting the EUR 750 million global revenue threshold, register for Bermuda Corporate Income Tax within 30 days of entering the CIT scope. Step 5: Bermuda has no VAT or VAT ID; a UK VAT registration with HMRC is required only if the entity acquires a UK establishment and makes taxable supplies exceeding the UK VAT registration threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms: (1) tax protection assurance under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, guaranteeing exemption from future Bermuda direct taxes until 31 March 2035 and extendable to 2050; (2) under the CIT Act 2023, in-scope groups may elect simplified transitional operational rules for the 2025 to 2027 fiscal periods, together with qualified domestic minimum top-up tax safe-harbour consistent computational elections; (3) payroll tax payment elections allowing annual or quarterly instalment schedules; (4) in the UK nexus scenario, UK residence follows the actual central management and control test rather than any formal entity election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Bermuda has no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. Indirect levies are limited to transactional Bermuda Customs Duty on imported physical goods (digital goods and offshore software services are outside the scope) and employer-side Bermuda Payroll Tax on local wages. A separate UK VAT at 20% is contingent and applies only if a UK establishment exists and taxable supplies are made in the UK.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Bermuda imposes no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold; customs duty is triggered transactionally on each import, and payroll tax registration is mandatory for every employer from the first BMD 1 of taxable wages. In the contingent UK scenario, HMRC registration is required when taxable turnover exceeds GBP 90,000 on a rolling 12-month basis. Bermuda dollar is pegged 1:1 to the US dollar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Bermuda for VAT because no VAT or GST exists; cross-border supplies of gaming anti-cheat middleware, SaaS, and offshore software licences are outside the scope of Bermuda indirect taxation, and there is no input VAT to recover. For physical goods exported from Bermuda, zero customs duty is available when the export is completed and the Bermuda Customs Export Entry is validated. In the contingent UK scenario, exports of goods to non-UK destinations are zero-rated under VATA 1994 Section 7 and HMRC Notice 703, while B2B digital services supplied to non-UK businesses are outside the scope of UK VAT because the place of supply is outside the UK; the supplier must retain full evidence in the VAT account to support the treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bermuda Customs Export Entry)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill proving physical movement of exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Swift MT103 credit advice proving cross-border receipt
  • Commercial invoice, export sales contract, and digital delivery or licence log evidencing the underlying supply

Bermuda has no real-time fiscal clearance regime, no continuous transaction controls, no API-based government clearance, and no digital stamping or e-invoicing mandate. Electronic filing of payroll tax returns is mandatory through the Bermuda Government e-filing portal. Making Tax Digital compliant reporting would apply only in the contingent UK VAT registration scenario, in which VAT returns are submitted electronically via HMRC-recognised software without digital stamping.

Bermuda statutory exposure is limited because no export VAT is charged. Fraudulent customs documentation attracts forfeiture and fines under the Customs Act 1955; payroll tax late filing attracts penalties up to 10% of the tax due plus interest, and false returns carry civil penalties and criminal sanctions. Under the CIT Act 2023, inaccurate returns and information trigger interest, civil penalties, and retrospective reassessment; deliberate misrepresentation used to maintain tax-exempt or reduced status while failing economic substance requirements can result in clawback of the exempted status. In the contingent UK scenario, dishonest evasion of export zero-rating attracts penalties up to 100% of the VAT at risk under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007, with retrospective assessments extendable to 20 years for deliberate and concealed conduct, and clawback of zero-rating applies where export evidence is not retained to HMRC standards.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda has no exchange-control legislation. The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar (USD) and is fully convertible; USD circulates freely as de facto legal tender. Capital account contributions, current account payments, profit remittances and dividend distributions to non-residents are unrestricted and require no BMA consent. No Bermudian withholding tax, capital gains tax or income tax applies to an exempted company; the only operative constraints are bank-level AML/KYC source-of-funds verification and the entity's good-standing filings under the Companies Act 1981.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Bermuda imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio and no surrender obligation for export receipts. Software licence and anti-cheat middleware subscription proceeds may be retained in any currency (BMD, USD, EUR, GBP) in local or foreign accounts. Intra-group royalties and licence fees must nonetheless be documented at arm's length to satisfy OECD BEPS transfer-pricing expectations and the Bermuda economic substance regime for pure IP holding where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), operating under the Companies Act 1981 and the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, with supervisory powers for banking, anti-money laundering and anti-terrorist financing under the Proceeds of Crime (AML/ATF) Regulations 2020 and supporting BMA AML/ATF Guidance Notes.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. Bermuda does not maintain a domestic e-money institution (EMI) licensing class, and the BMA does not regulate foreign EMIs operating remotely. A regulated UK or EU EMI (FCA/e-money licensed) can be engaged as a collections rail for the anti-cheat engine and will legally serve a Bermuda exempted company; however Bermudian clearing banks treat unlicensed digital EMI accounts as non-correspondent-grade and will not settle to them for capital or operational flows. Recommended structure: use a regulated UK EMI for card-based SDK licence collections, then sweep balances via SWIFT MT103 to the Bermudian tier-1 operating account. Overall feasibility: Medium.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable — Bermuda has no corporate income tax and no domestic transfer pricing documentation legislation. However, if the exempted company is a relevant entity under the Economic Substance Regulations 2018, it must maintain contemporaneous evidence of economic presence, including records of employees, premises, expenditure, board meetings, and core income-generating activities.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda’s Economic Substance Amendment Act 2018 and Economic Substance Regulations 2018 apply to exempted companies carrying on relevant activities. An anti-cheat gaming middleware business that licenses software, algorithms, or other intellectual property may be classified as an intellectual property business or, if it only holds shares and earns dividends or capital gains, a pure equity holding business. Such entities must satisfy location, management, staff, and expenditure requirements in Bermuda, file an annual ESR declaration, and be prepared for automatic exchange of substance information with the UK HMRC.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Artificial UK Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda has no corporate income tax, so the primary exposure arises from false ESR declarations and misrepresentation under the Companies Act 1981 and the Economic Substance Regulations 2018, carrying financial penalties up to BMD 250,000, daily default penalties up to BMD 10,000, and strike-off. The UK may also apply the General Anti-Abuse Rule, CFC rules, transfer pricing adjustments, and HMRC penalties for deliberate avoidance.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders can be treated as earning UK taxable income in substance; HMRC can pierce the corporate veil, issue personal assessments, freeze assets, impose travel bans, and pursue criminal prosecution for tax evasion where deliberate concealment is proven.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The Registrar of Companies can impose a penalty of up to BMD 250,000 and BMD 10,000 for each day of continued non-compliance; the company may be struck off the register, lose legal capacity, and have its information exchanged with HMRC under the tax information exchange framework.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit or cause the company to fail the economic substance test are exposed to fines, disqualification orders, travel bans, and personal liability for debts incurred after the company has been struck off.
AML / ATF and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda’s Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, AML/ATF Code, Beneficial Ownership legislation, and sanctions regulations impose substantial fines, suspension or revocation of registration, public censure, and custodial penalties; money laundering offences can attract imprisonment of up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners can be personally prosecuted, subjected to asset-freezing orders and travel bans, disqualified from holding directorships, and extradited where cross-border money laundering is established.
Annual Return and Government Fee Default

Statutory sanction: The Registrar imposes a late filing penalty of BMD 500 and, for continued default, additional penalties and eventual strike-off from the register. A struck-off company loses the limited-liability protection and the right to sue or enforce contracts; restoration requires a court order and payment of arrears.

Director personal liability: Directors who continue to trade or sign contracts after strike-off become personally liable for the company’s debts; they may also be subject to director disqualification, travel restrictions, and monetary penalties in Bermuda.
