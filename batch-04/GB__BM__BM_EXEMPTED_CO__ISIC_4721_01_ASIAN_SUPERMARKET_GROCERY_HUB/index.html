Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC), Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda; electronic portal: OneGov Bermuda (www.gov.bm) and OTC e-services (taxcommissioner.gov.bm). HMRC (United Kingdom) retains sovereign oversight for information exchange under the Bermuda–UK Tax Information Exchange Agreement but does not administer Bermuda national taxes.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the licensed Bermudian resident agent (Corporate Service Provider) files the company's TIN registration with the Office of the Tax Commissioner under the Tax Information Exchange Act 2016; the Bermuda company registration number serves as the entity TIN for CRS/TIEA purposes. Required filings include the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, registered office address, director/officer/member registers, and beneficial-ownership particulars. No TIN fee applies; the entity thereafter pays an annual exempted-undertaking government fee by authorized-share-capital band (currently up to approximately BMD 28,315) to the Registrar of Companies. The company is not required to register with HMRC solely because Bermuda is a British Overseas Territory; UK tax filings arise only if a UK permanent establishment or UK VAT-able trade is established.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the Bermuda Exempted Company is tax-exempt by default. The Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (guarantee extended to 2035) ensures no Bermuda tax on income, profits, distributions, dividends, or capital gains. Residual impositions: customs import duties on grocery inventory, payroll tax on Bermuda-resident employees under the Payroll Tax Act 1995, land tax if freehold property is held, and annual business/trade licence fees if retail activity is conducted locally.

Available election forms

Bermuda offers no native check-the-box election as it imposes no entity-level income tax. Available mechanisms: (i) non-resident/exchange-control status election for exempted companies operating wholly offshore, which excludes non-Bermudian employee remuneration from Bermuda payroll tax; (ii) US check-the-box election under IRC Section 7701 via IRS Form 8832 for US owners to treat the entity as corporation, partnership, or disregarded entity; (iii) Economic Substance Act 2018 non-residence certification, supported by foreign tax-residence evidence, for entities engaged in Bermuda relevant activities; (iv) hybrid-mismatch and BEPS outcomes are addressed solely at the foreign parent jurisdiction because Bermuda levies no income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax. Bermuda raises indirect revenue through customs import duties (Customs Act 1964 and Tariff), payroll tax, and business licence fees. Imported Asian condiments and vegetables attract customs duty at the border; basic staple groceries are duty-free under the tariff, while processed and packaged food items may be dutiable up to the general 24.75% commercial rate. Domestic retail sales by the Asian supermarket are not subject to any transaction tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no turnover threshold triggers a consumption-tax registration because no VAT/GST regime exists. Instead, the grocery importer must register a Customs Import Account with the Bermuda Customs Department (ASYCUDA World) before first import, without a monetary minimum. The retail operation must also obtain a Retail Trade Licence under the Business Licence (Tax) Act 1970, with an annual fee graduated by gross-receipts bands.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable locally: Bermuda has no VAT/GST mechanism to zero-rate exports. The relevant reliefs are: (1) Bermuda re-export duty relief — imported goods re-exported unchanged within 12 months may obtain a duty refund under the Customs Act 1964, and goods transshipped through Bermudian ports are duty-free; (2) UK export VAT — where grocery stock is supplied from the UK to Bermuda, the supply qualifies for UK zero-rating under VATA 1994 s.30 only if the goods physically leave the UK, the exporter holds acceptable export evidence (customs entries, shipping documents, commercial invoices) within 3 months of the supply, and the goods are not substantially processed in the UK; (3) exchange control — Bermuda exempted companies operate free of FX restrictions, and the 180-day benchmark reflects commercial/UK trade-credit norms rather than a Bermudian statutory repatriation deadline.

Customs import/export declarations are filed electronically through ASYCUDA World via accredited declarants. Point-of-sale receipts remain ordinary commercial documents with standard record-keeping requirements; no government fiscal-stamping obligation applies to retail grocery transactions.

Under the Customs Act 1964, false declarations, duty evasion, and fraudulent drawback claims render goods forfeitable and expose the holder to fines and imprisonment (commonly cited maxima: fines in the thousands of BMD and imprisonment up to 5 years), with unpaid or wrongly refunded duty recoverable with interest. The Economic Substance Act 2018 imposes fines up to BMD 250,000 for misleading filings and potential director imprisonment up to 2 years. On the UK side, invalid export zero-rating triggers VAT recovery up to 100% of the tax plus interest and penalties under the Finance Act 2007/2008 regime. Enhanced Bermuda-UK TIEA exchanges and automatic CRS/CbC reporting materially increase audit traceability; the grocery retailer should maintain shipment-level evidence independently to resist clawback during HMRC or OTC post-clearance audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar is freely convertible with the US Dollar at a fixed 1:1 parity under a currency board-like arrangement. Current account transactions for trade and operational payments are effectively unrestricted and may be settled in USD or BMD. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, loan disbursements, and intercompany funding, require exchange control notification or approval where the counterparty is a Bermuda resident. A Bermuda Exempted Company classified as non-resident for exchange control purposes may maintain external foreign currency accounts and receive capital contributions via USD wire without material conversion hurdles. Dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted in the currency of account; Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, or profits. The primary practical hurdle is documentation: board resolutions, source of funds declarations, and exchange control filing evidence must be presented to the clearing bank before high-value inbound wires are credited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in Bermuda compelling an Exempted Company to surrender export proceeds or mandatorily convert foreign currency receipts into Bermuda Dollars. Export receivables and supermarket import resale proceeds denominated in USD may be retained in USD clearing accounts inside or outside Bermuda. Bermuda-resident domestic trading companies remain subject to exchange control permission if they maintain foreign currency accounts. For Exempted Companies designated as non-resident, the foreign currency retention right is broader. Nevertheless, each bank will require a written exchange control classification certificate or declaration, the company's certificate of incorporation, and a valid BMA permit or non-resident designation letter to allow unfettered USD accumulation and onward disbursement.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the integrated financial services regulator, monetary authority, and administrator of the Exchange Control Act 1972, with issuance and management of the Bermuda Dollar (BMD) at statutory parity with the United States Dollar (USD).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — subject to strict boundary limitations. A UK-authorized Electronic Money Institution (EMI) such as a UK FCA-regulated payment firm may provide payment accounts and card acquiring services to a Bermuda Exempted Company without obtaining a Bermuda banking licence, provided the EMI does not establish a physical place of business in Bermuda, does not solicit Bermuda domestic clients, and does not present itself as a Bermuda deposit-taking institution. For this Asian supermarket grocery hub, a UK EMI can be used as an ancillary payment rail for card acquiring settlement and small-value supplier payments, but the primary corporate treasury account should be maintained with a licensed Bermuda clearing bank to ensure proper exchange control classification, SWIFT correspondent access, and LC issuing capacity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and subordinated loan capital should be routed exclusively by SWIFT MT103 from a regulated UK, US, or Hong Kong corporate bank account into the Bermuda clearing bank's USD correspondent account. The wire beneficiary should be the Bermuda Exempted Company's USD account at the licensed Bermuda bank. The SWIFT remittance field must clearly state the purpose, e.g., 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION – SHARE CAPITAL INJECTION – BM EXEMPTED COMPANY' or 'INTERCOMPANY LOAN – GROCERY HUB WORKING CAPITAL'. The remitter must include the Bermuda bank account number and, if required, the exchange control permit reference. The receiving bank will request the board resolution, updated registered shareholder schedule, source-of-funds documentation, and a confirmation that the Bermuda company is classified as non-resident for exchange control. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Bermuda does not impose a general corporate income tax on the exempted company; a 15% Bermuda CIT under the CIT Act 2023 can apply only to a constituent entity of a large MNE group with consolidated annual revenue of at least €750m in two of the prior four years, a status not engaged by the standalone grocery retail hub.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — no Bermuda withholding tax is levied on dividends paid by an exempted company to a UK or non-Bermudian shareholder; the exempted company may remit dividends in BMD or USD freely.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The exempted company’s annual government fee and return must be completed by the statutory anniversary/assessed billing cycle prescribed by the Companies Act 1981; late completion triggers strike-off proceedings. For a CIT-in-scope MNE constituent entity, the separate Bermuda CIT return (CIT-1 / Tax Administration ePortal) is due no later than 15 months after the fiscal year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The CIT return is due not later than 15 months after the end of the fiscal year; registration with the Bermuda Tax Administration is due within 30 days of becoming an in-scope entity. Payments are due in four quarterly instalments during the fiscal year and final settlement by the return due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda Local File or Master File threshold applies to a non-CIT exempted company because Bermuda levies no general corporate income tax. For CIT-in-scope multinational groups, Bermuda aligns with the OECD Pillar Two GloBE rules and transfer pricing principles; the controlling threshold is an MNE group consolidated revenue of at least €750m (equivalent to approximately BMD 780m at peg) in at least two of the four preceding fiscal years. No local-currency TP documentation threshold exists for this standalone supermarket hub.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2018 and the Companies Act 1981 impose substance requirements only upon entities carrying on a 'relevant activity'. Physical grocery retailing of imported Asian condiments and vegetables (ISIC 4721) is not a relevant activity, so no ESR economic substance test, local payroll or expenditure ratios, or 'directed and managed in Bermuda' test applies. If the entity later adds intra-group procurement, distribution centre, financing, or IP activities, it would become subject to ESR: it must maintain physical presence, sufficient employees, core income-generating activities in Bermuda, and file an annual ESR return; failure risks penalties, strike-off, and recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Offshore Tax Exemption Misuse / Sham

Statutory sanction: HMRC may reclassify the company as UK tax resident or as having a UK PE, assess corporation tax on all trading profits (19%-25% depending on profit band), add penalties for deliberate errors up to 100% of tax, and impose daily uncertainty penalties. Bermuda may also strike off the company if the registered office is nominal or the resident representative is not maintained.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil where the company is a mere façade; directors can be personally liable for UK tax, civil restitution, and costs. Fraudulent evasion of UK income tax or corporation tax is a criminal offence carrying imprisonment up to 7 years, uncapped fines, travel bans, and confiscation orders.
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Non-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Registrar may impose monetary penalties, daily default penalties, strike the company off the register, and render the company unable to contract. Beneficial ownership filing default carries separate court-imposed fines; false statements attract imprisonment under Bermuda Companies Act and BOO legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for penalties if the company trades after being struck off; they may be disqualified and face criminal liability for knowingly submitting false beneficial ownership filings, with imprisonment and travel restrictions.
AML/CFT and Banking Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 and AML/ATF Regulations impose heavy financial penalties, licence suspension, account termination, and criminal conviction for money laundering; the bank may also freeze the settlement accounts and terminate EMI/interchange facilities.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly or recklessly permit unregistered money service business activity or fail to report suspicion are subject to criminal imprisonment, personal fines, and surrender of proceeds; banking counterparties will obtain personal undertakings and may invoke personal guarantee clauses.
Unlicensed Retail / Food Safety Contravention

Statutory sanction: Bermuda authorities may issue prohibition orders, close the store, levy monetary penalties per day of unlicensed trading, seize non-compliant food products, cancel import permissions, and disqualify the company from future licensing.

Director personal liability: Registered managers and directors face joint and several liability for unpaid licence fees and fines; serious public-health violations involving contaminated goods are punishable as criminal offences with imprisonment, personal civil liability for injury and damages, and exclusion orders.
Transfer Pricing / CIT Documentation Failure (MNE Top-Up Scenario)

Statutory sanction: Bermuda CIT Act 2023 provides administrative penalties for late registration and filing, daily amounts for continuing default, interest on underpaid CIT, and penalty adjustments of up to 100% of the understated 15% CIT for intentional inaccuracy.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause or knowingly permit the default can be personally assessed for penalties; false statements on the CIT return can trigger criminal prosecutions, confiscation of benefits, disqualification, and, in clear abuse cases, lifting of the corporate veil with civil restitution obligations.
