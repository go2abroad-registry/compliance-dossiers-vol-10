Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Office of the Registrar of Companies (ROC) within the Ministry of Finance; Tax Administration is managed by the Bermuda Tax Administration (BTA) under the Ministry of Finance, with the Government of Bermuda Tax Portal (www.gov.bm/taxation)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities seeking to establish a Bermuda Exempted Company must file incorporation documents with the Registrar of Companies. Although Bermuda does not levy income tax, VAT, or capital gains tax, a Bermuda Tax Identification Number (BTIN) or Company Reference Number (CRN) is automatically assigned upon incorporation. For registration purposes, the exempted company must obtain a Certificate of Exemption and apply for a tax compliance certificate through the Bermuda Tax Administration portal if required for banking or licensing purposes. No separate VAT ID is issued because Bermuda has no VAT/GST regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda operates no check-the-box entity classification regime. An exempted company is by default classified as a non-resident tax-exempt entity eligible for a specific exemption certificate that guarantees no income tax, corporate tax, or capital gains tax will be imposed for a defined period (typically until 2035 under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023). A hybrid mismatch election is not applicable, but entities may elect to be treated as a resident for certain exchange control or licensing purposes under the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) regime. The Minister of Finance may issue a specific exemption under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 endorsing the tax-exempt status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - Bermuda does not levy VAT/GST; therefore no export VAT zero-rating mechanism exists. International air freight forwarding and aircraft charter brokerage services provided by a Bermuda exempted company are outside the scope of domestic indirect taxation. Customs duty zero-rating may apply to imported aircraft, aircraft engines, and spare parts under the Tariff Classification schedule if the goods are used exclusively in international air commerce, subject to the Customs Department relief regulations.

  • Bermuda Customs Import Declaration (C1) or Export Declaration where applicable
  • Aircraft registration certificate confirming the vessel is a Bermuda-registered aircraft
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing international carriage of cargo
  • Charter party agreement or Brokerage Contract evidencing the commercial transaction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds within the applicable period
  • Customs duty exemption certificate or duty relief authorization from the Bermuda Customs Department

Bermuda has no electronic invoicing real-time fiscal clearance requirement. No API-based tax clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control exists for indirect tax purposes. Customs duties apply to imported goods under the Customs Act 2010 and are settled via the Bermuda Customs Electronic Single Document (ESD) platform; however aircraft parts and equipment for international air cargo charter operations may be eligible for bonded or conditional duty relief.

Bermuda does not administer export VAT zero-rating refunds. However, customs duty evasion or fraudulent claims for duty relief on aircraft or spares are subject to penalties under the Customs Act 2010, including forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties up to three times the duty evaded, and criminal prosecution. Exempted companies must maintain accurate records to substantiate any customs duty exemption; failure to do so may result in retrospective duty assessment, interest, and cancellation of the certificate of exemption. Anti-money laundering and economic substance filings are scrutinized by the BMA and the Tax Commissioner; incorrect tax compliance certificates may lead to cancellation of the tax exemption certificate and exposure to penalties under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 when applicable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda's currency is the Bermudian Dollar (BMD), pegged at a fixed 1:1 parity with the US Dollar (USD). The Exchange Control Act 1972, administered by the Bermuda Monetary Authority, classifies Exempted Companies as non-resident for exchange control purposes. This designation grants Exempted Companies free convertibility on both current and capital account transactions in foreign currencies, with no requirement to route transactions through the domestic banking system or obtain BMA approval for ordinary trade settlements, profit remittances, intercompany loans, or shareholder distributions. Current account payments (cargo charter fees, fuel payments, handling charges, brokerage commissions) are freely remittable without restriction. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections and outbound loan repatriation, are also unrestricted for non-resident Exempted Companies, provided the beneficial ownership, source of funds, and economic substance documentation meet licensed bank underwriting standards. The only friction arises where a bank chooses to process in BMD; given the 1:1 peg, banks typically transact in USD for air-cargo charter operations to avoid any residual conversion risk and maintain corridor settlement velocity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1972 (as amended) and the Exchange Control Regulations, resident persons in Bermuda must offer any foreign currency received for exports or services to an authorised dealer within one month of receipt, with a mandatory conversion ratio of 100% into BMD at the official parity rate. However, a Bermuda Exempted Company operating as a non-resident entity is expressly exempt from these surrender obligations. Export proceeds and trade receipts derived from air cargo charter brokering may be retained indefinitely in USD or any freely convertible foreign currency in offshore or onshore settlement accounts without mandatory conversion, so long as the entity maintains its non-resident exchange control status and files the required declarations with the BMA when opening bank accounts. Where the Exempted Company intermediates freight payments on behalf of UK or third-country principals, it must ensure that such pass-through settlement does not create a Bermuda resident economic nexus that would reclassify the entity as resident and trigger the surrender provisions.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Cross-border digital EMI/neobank onboarding for a Bermuda Exempted Company is assessed as Low-Medium feasibility. Bermuda's Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 requires a full banking licence for deposit-taking activity, and no standalone EMI passporting regime exists in Bermuda; UK-authorised EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise) generally do not onboard Bermuda-incorporated entities owing to correspondent banking de-risking, the absence of a UK-EMI overseas permission for Bermuda, and heightened jurisdictional scrutiny of British Overseas Territory corporates. A Bermuda Exempted Company may, however, use a UK-licensed EMI for payment corridors denominated in GBP and EUR, provided the EMI agrees to accept a foreign-incorporated non-resident entity, the underlying beneficial owners pass UK KYC, and the EMI's restricted payment business rules do not prohibit air-cargo charter brokerage. Legal constraints include the EMI's inability to provide true escrow or trade-credit services, the lack of Fedwire access for USD clearing, and the requirement that the company maintain a primary bank account with a Bermuda-licensed bank to satisfy the BMA's exchange-control declaration and substance record keeping.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection into the Bermuda Exempted Company is executed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the company's primary USD settlement account at a Bermuda-licensed authorised dealer. The wire must be tagged with the correct purpose code, including 'CAPT' or 'Capital Contribution' for inward equity, 'INTR' for intercompany loans, or 'BSSS' for shareholders' subordinated loans, with reference made to the company's account number and the board resolution approving the injection. Foreign exchange controls require the receiving Bermudian bank to record the transaction on the BMA's Exchange Control Form EC-1 (or electronic equivalent) to preserve the non-resident exempt status. Verification procedures include the reconciliation of the SWIFT field 50K remitter with the registered shareholder register, confirmation that the remitter is not on the OFAC/UN sanctions lists, and a source-of-funds declaration for any amount exceeding USD 100,000. For injections routed from the United Kingdom, the remitting bank may require the Bermuda company to evidence its exempted status via a Certificate of Exempted Company and the BMA's non-resident designation before releasing the wire.Outbound trade settlements for air cargo charter brokerage are cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103 for supplier payments, aviation fuel payments, ground handling fees, navigation charges, and commission disbursements to sub-agents. For large charter payments exceeding USD 250,000, SWIFT MT202 COVER messages are used for interbank reimbursement through the Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication network. Commercial letters of credit (documentary credits) are issued via SWIFT MT700 from the Bermuda authorised dealer on behalf of the Exempted Company to sellers of aircraft charter services and cargo capacity, with a 'confirmed, irrevocable' tenor requiring presentation of an air waybill, certificate of origin, insurance certificate, and broker's receipt. Settlement of USD-denominated obligations is effected through correspondent clearing at Federal Reserve Banks in New York via Fedwire, with the Bermuda bank maintaining nostro accounts at major US money-centre banks. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% standard Bermuda corporate income tax (no income, capital gains, or withholding tax for exempted companies); a 15% rate under Bermuda's Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 applies only to multinational enterprise groups with consolidated global revenue of EUR 750 million or more, effective for financial years commencing on or after 1 January 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no dividend withholding tax. Dividends and capital distributions may be paid gross to non-resident shareholders, subject solely to the Companies Act 1981 solvency test (the board must certify that the company remains solvent immediately after the distribution). No exchange control approval is required; payments are free of Bermudian levy.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Nil local threshold — Bermuda imposes no domestic transfer-pricing documentation, Local File, or Master File requirement for standard exempted companies. For in-scope multinational groups (consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million), the Bermuda CIT Act 2024 and OECD Pillar Two rules may require jurisdictional data and risk assessments; a prudent operational threshold for preparing contemporaneous arm's length documentation is any related-party transaction exceeding BMD 10 million per annum.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2018 (as amended) apply to exempted companies carrying on relevant activities: banking, insurance, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, shipping, distribution and service centre, IP business, and pure equity holding. A third-party air cargo charter brokerage is not categorically a relevant activity, but intra-group charter procurement or administrative/back-office support to related parties can trigger 'distribution and service centre' or 'headquarters' classification. Such in-scope companies must be directed and managed in Bermuda, maintain physical premises, adequate full-time employees, adequate operating expenditure, and file an annual ESR return. Non-compliance exposes the company to the statutory ESR penalties and internationally coordinated exchange of information.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion / Foreign Tax Abuse

Statutory sanction: UK Finance Act 2019 corporate criminal offence of facilitating tax evasion; HMRC assessment for the full diverted profit at 25% (Diverted Profits Tax) plus interest and penalties of up to 200% of the understated tax; simultaneous Bermuda sanctions include Registrar strike-off and BMD 10,000 compliance penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors face civil restitution, UK disqualification orders, international travel bans under the Criminal Justice (International Cooperation) regime, and imprisonment of up to 7 years for fraudulent evasion; insolvency officeholders may pursue wrongful trading recoveries.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Act 2018: BMD 10,000 fine for late or non-filing (first offence), BMD 1,000 per day continuing, up to BMD 100,000 for serious violations; false substance declaration voids the company's good standing and may lead to automatic strike-off and communication of adverse information to the UK HMRC under exchange-of-information arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for fines of up to BMD 10,000; personal travel bans and asset freezing orders are available where the company is used to mislead foreign revenue authorities; criminal fraud liability may attach in the UK.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering in Air Cargo

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Act 2017: corporate fines up to BMD 100,000 per contravention, supervisory enforcement by the Bermuda Monetary Authority including licence suspension, asset freezing and confiscation orders; UK failure-to-prevent and money laundering statutes similarly activate for UK-connected management.

Director personal liability: Directors personally exposed to fines up to BMD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years; proceeds of crime confiscation against personal estates; travel bans, extradition proceedings, and disqualification from UK or Bermuda directorships are consequential liabilities.
False Filing and Misrepresentation to the Registrar

Statutory sanction: Companies Act 1981 false statement offences: fines up to BMD 10,000, revocation of the exempted status, and cancellation of the Certificate of Incorporation; the Registrar may also refer matters to the Director of Public Prosecutions.

Director personal liability: Signing directors are jointly and severally liable for restitution of waived fees and for civil damages to creditors; criminal conviction carries up to 2 years' imprisonment in Bermuda and disqualification from acting as directors in the UK under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986.
Unlawful Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Companies Act 1981 distributions compliance: unlawful distributions are void to the extent of the deficiency; the Registrar and creditors may compel clawback with interest; the company may be compulsorily wound up.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised unlawful distributions are personally liable to repay the distributed amount plus interest; in insolvency, liability for wrongful trading and misfeasance proceedings; UK-connected directors may be made subject to bankruptcy restriction orders.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: UK CFC (TIOPA 2010 Part 9A) rules can attribute the Bermuda profit to the UK controlling interest; Diverted Profits Tax applies at 25% plus up to 200% penalty potential; HMRC may issue a payment notice on the Bermuda company requiring UK tax payment through a UK representative.

Director personal liability: Senior accounting officers in UK companies are personally liable to penalties for failure to certify appropriate tax accounting arrangements; UK directors who participate in contrived arrangements may be disqualified for unfit conduct and exposed to civil recovery actions.
