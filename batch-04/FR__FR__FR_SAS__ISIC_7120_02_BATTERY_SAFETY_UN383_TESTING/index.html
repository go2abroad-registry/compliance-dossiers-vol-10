Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés) acting through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) Guichet Unique online portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce): Articles L.227-1 to L.227-30 (Société par Actions Simplifiée - SAS); Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (general company law); Articles R.123-30 to R.123-80 (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés - RCS); single-business filing window (Guichet Unique) implementing rules under Order No. 2021-300 of 19 March 2021.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de constitution de société (Company Formation Declaration) filed electronically on the Guichet Unique, accompanied by the signed Statuts (Articles of Association), the attestation de dépôt de fonds and the attestation de parution (legal publication certificate).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 11 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS (Article L.227-2). Where the Articles fix a capital, at least 50% of the nominal value of cash subscriptions must be paid up at incorporation and held in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) at a licensed bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; funds are released only upon production of the Kbis registration extract. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up and, unless the statutory de minimis exemption applies, certified by a commissaire aux apports (Article L.225-8, R.225-7).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) for non-established businesses; official portal: impots.gouv.fr. Company formation and statutory business registration are completed through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), with SIREN/SIRET allocation by INSEE.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the SAS through the INPI Guichet unique and obtain its registration extract, SIREN and SIRET numbers. 2. File the required tax registration information with the competent SIE through the business creation process and provide the articles of association, registration extract, registered-office evidence, directors' details, business activity description, bank details and expected taxable transactions. 3. The DGFiP issues the French corporate tax account identifiers and, where applicable, the intracommunity VAT identification number in the format FR plus a two-digit key and the SIREN. 4. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the SIEE for French VAT registration when it makes taxable supplies in France that are not fully subject to the domestic reverse charge or another exemption. The application generally requires proof of foreign incorporation, foreign tax identification, certificate of tax residence, constitutional documents, identity and authority documents for the representative, description of French activities, contracts or invoices, and banking information. 5. EU-established applicants may generally use the applicable electronic or administrative channel of their home tax authority; non-EU entities may need a French fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement or statutory exception removes that requirement. 6. Validate the VAT number through the European Commission VIES system where relevant. VAT registration does not by itself replace customs EORI registration, which is required for customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). An eligible SAS may elect temporarily for income-tax treatment under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code, generally for a maximum period of five financial years, provided statutory conditions are met, including limits relating to headcount, turnover or balance-sheet total, age of the company, ownership and management. The election is not a general check-the-box regime and must be exercised within the statutory time limit. An SAS may not generally elect the partnership-style permanent pass-through treatment available to certain other French entities. France has implemented anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework; these are anti-avoidance provisions, not a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the French VAT franchise regime, the principal turnover threshold for supplies of services is EUR 37,500, with a tolerance threshold generally of EUR 41,250, subject to the applicable tax year and statutory updates. Different thresholds apply to supplies of goods and certain regulated activities. Exceeding the relevant threshold or making taxable transactions outside the franchise regime can require immediate VAT registration. The threshold does not eliminate registration obligations arising from specific cross-border, import, export, distance-selling, or taxable-establishment rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to a destination outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT with the right to deduct input VAT where the goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU by the seller or by an authorized intermediary and the exporter can substantiate the export and exit from the EU customs territory. The seller must preserve the customs export declaration and reliable evidence of exit. Intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-identified customer in another EU Member State may be exempt with the right to deduct input VAT if the customer has a valid VAT number, the goods are transported to another Member State, the supplier retains transport evidence, and the required recapitulative statement and reporting are completed. For laboratory testing and certification services, a B2B service supplied to a business customer established outside France is generally outside the scope of French VAT under the place-of-supply rule, rather than a French zero-rated export. B2B services supplied to an EU or non-EU business customer generally follow the customer's establishment and may require reverse-charge wording. Special rules apply to services connected with movable tangible property, B2C customers, and services effectively used or enjoyed in France.

  • Electronic customs export declaration containing the applicable customs procedure and Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs exit certification or equivalent electronic evidence that the goods physically exited the European Union
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, packing list and transport documentation
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note or other carrier evidence, as applicable
  • Proof of transport and delivery to the non-EU destination
  • Exporter, declarant and customs representative records, including EORI details
  • For intra-Community supplies: customer VAT identification number, transport evidence and recapitulative statement

  • Payment records and accounting records supporting the transaction; France does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a condition for export VAT exemption

France is introducing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all French-established businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices; issuance obligations initially apply to large companies and in-scope intermediate-sized companies, followed generally by small and medium-sized businesses from 1 September 2027. Domestic business-to-business invoices will be transmitted through the public invoicing portal or an approved private Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP). Cross-border and certain business-to-consumer transactions are generally subject to electronic transaction reporting rather than domestic invoice transmission. France does not operate a universal real-time pre-clearance model for all invoices. Invoices must contain legally required identification, VAT, payment, date, numbering and transaction information, and must be retained in an auditable electronic format.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for establishing export VAT exemption or service place of supply. Export evidence must nevertheless be complete, consistent and retained for the applicable statutory audit period. If zero-rating or exemption is denied, unpaid French VAT may be assessed retrospectively, together with late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month and tax penalties. A 40% penalty may apply for deliberate failure or deliberate inaccuracies, and an 80% penalty may apply in cases involving fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law, subject to statutory conditions. False, incomplete or manipulated customs declarations may trigger customs duties, VAT, customs penalties and other sanctions. Serious fraudulent conduct may create criminal tax-fraud exposure. Input VAT refunds can be reduced, denied or clawed back where export, transport, customer-status, invoicing or accounting substantiation is inadequate.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully convertible currency regime for both current and capital accounts in full compliance with EU and IMF standards. Capital account transactions, including profit remittances to non-resident shareholders, are unrestricted subject to standard tax clearance, beneficial ownership verification, and anti-money laundering documentation. No capital controls or convertibility hurdles apply to established EU-based entities conducting cross-border trade or laboratory services.

Export proceeds surrender rules

French exporters must declare all export receipts to customs authorities for statistical purposes. Repatriation of foreign currency proceeds is strongly recommended within six months of shipment or per contractual terms. Authorized banks facilitate conversion to EUR at the official rate; non-repatriation may trigger enhanced reporting to the Banque de France and potential tax penalties.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France
