Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda (portal: https://www.gov.bm/taxes); H.M. Customs Service (Bermuda Customs) administers customs duty, import/export control and customs-broker licensing through the ASYCUDA World e-clearance portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Bermuda Exempted Company under the Companies Act 1981 with the Registrar of Companies (minimum capital not required; non-resident ownership permitted). Step 2: As an 'exempted undertaking', apply to the Ministry of Finance under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 for a Certificate of Exemption guaranteeing no future Bermuda taxes up to 31 March 2035 (tax-exempt status). Step 3: Register as an employer with the Office of the Tax Commissioner under the Payroll Tax Act 1995, obtaining a Payroll Tax account reference number, if staffing Bermuda-resident employees. Step 4: Apply to H.M. Customs Service for a Customs Broker licence under the Customs Management Act 2015, obtaining a Broker Identification Number and ASYCUDA World user credentials; register as importer/exporter for any own-account transactions. Step 5: Open a foreign-currency account and obtain Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) exchange-control 'non-resident' designation for free flow of funds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Exempted Company with Certificate of Exemption: zero corporate income tax, zero capital gains tax, zero withholding tax and no dividend taxation; no entity-level taxation on worldwide income; territorial scope is nil — Bermuda taxes neither resident nor non-resident income, only employer payroll tax on Bermuda-source remuneration, customs duty on imports, and stamp duties).

Available election forms

(1) Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 election — a written election entitles the undertaking to an Exemption Certificate capping all future Bermuda tax liabilities at nil until 31 March 2035; (2) No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Bermuda law, Bermuda having no income tax; (3) Payroll Tax Act 1995 employer election — an employer may elect between the standard payroll tax computation or the alternative apportionment basis where employees bear a share; (4) Companies Act 1981 continuation election — permits an exempted company to continue as a foreign-incorporated entity in another jurisdiction while preserving its Bermuda legal identity, relevant for downstream restructuring.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Customs Duty Regime only — Bermuda levies no VAT, no GST and no sales tax; indirect taxation is composed of import customs duty under the Customs Tariff Act 1970, the Customs Management Act 2015 and associated service/brokerage licensing fees.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — with no VAT/GST no registration threshold exists. A customs brokerage business must be licensed under section 22 of the Customs Management Act 2015 as a precondition to operating, and any employer paying Bermuda remuneration must register for Payroll Tax with the Office of the Tax Commissioner regardless of a de minimis turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT concept — Bermuda has no VAT, therefore no VAT zero-rating or export refund mechanism exists. Under the Customs Management Act 2015, exported goods leave Bermuda free of customs duty; goods imported for re-export may qualify for duty-free bonded warehousing, temporary admission or outward-processing relief provided they are exported within the prescribed ward period; customs brokers must substantiate the export with an ASYCUDA World export declaration and proof of physical departure to avoid re-assessment of import duty.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged via ASYCUDA World (automated export message with broker reference)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical export movement from Bermuda
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the declared goods and Incoterms
  • Evidence of outward remittance and BMA exchange-control approval where foreign currency proceeds are received

Mandatory e-customs clearance: all import, export and transit declarations must be lodged electronically through H.M. Customs Service's ASYCUDA World single-window platform using API/user credentials; declarations require digital acceptance and risk-based selectivity prior to physical release of goods; duty payments are settled electronically via ACH or wire transfer. There is no VAT e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping obligation in Bermuda, as no VAT regime is in force; brokers must, however, retain ASYCUDA declaration records for audit.

Under the Customs Management Act 2015 and the Customs Tariff Act 1970, goods fraudulently entered for export or falsely declared are liable to forfeiture and the offender is liable on summary conviction to a fine of up to BD$50,000 and/or imprisonment; H.M. Customs may assess a civil penalty of up to 100% of the duty evaded or, where the goods cannot be recovered, a sum equal to the goods' value. A wrongly 'exported' or transit-relief shipment that remains in Bermuda is retrospectively clawed back as an importation chargeable to duty at the prevailing rate plus penalty and statutory interest. Customs brokers risk licence suspension or revocation under section 22 of the Customs Management Act 2015, disqualification orders, and enhanced audit scrutiny where consecutive faulty declarations are detected.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The BMD is fully convertible and statutorily pegged at 1:1 with the US Dollar (USD). Bermuda operates a liberalized exchange-control regime: current-account transactions (brokerage service fees, customs-duties disbursements, freight payments) are freely permitted for non-resident-designated entities, while capital-account transactions (equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend remittances) are covered by standing BMA General Permissions, eliminating case-by-case approval for standard exempted-company flows. The practical convertibility hurdles are documentary: proof of non-resident exchange-control status, maintenance of the Beneficial Ownership Register under the Companies and Limited Liability Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Act 2018, and satisfaction of bank-level enhanced due diligence. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted and free of Bermuda withholding tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, resident persons must offer all foreign-currency proceeds to an authorized dealer within 3 months of receipt. A Bermuda Exempted Company designated non-resident for exchange-control purposes is expressly exempt from the surrender obligation; the mandatory conversion ratio is therefore 0%. All export and brokerage proceeds may be retained in USD or any freely convertible foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts without BMA restriction, provided the non-resident designation is documented and maintained.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969; sole issuer of the Bermudian dollar (BMD), regulator of all domestic deposit-taking institutions as authorized dealers, and administrator of the Exchange Control Act 1972 and Exchange Control Regulations 1973.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, ancillary use only. UK FCA-authorized electronic money institutions and global fintech platforms (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, WorldFirst) generally accept Bermuda Exempted Companies on a risk-acceptance basis, but impose enhanced UBO disclosure, minimum trading history, and transaction-velocity caps. Legal constraints: BMA exchange-control rules require all BMD-denominated transactions with Bermuda residents to route through authorized dealers; additionally, major correspondent banks periodically restrict EMI access to USD and GBP clearing rails, creating settlement fragility for statutory-bounded customs-duty payments. EMIs are viable for low-value disbursements and secondary FX conversion, but the primary treasury relationship must remain with a Bermuda-licensed commercial bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% historic Bermuda income tax rate; 15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Act 2024 applies only to in-scope MNE groups with consolidated revenue equal to or exceeding EUR 750 million for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2025. A Bermuda Exempted Company engaged solely in customs brokerage is ordinarily outside CIT scope.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (no Bermuda withholding tax on dividends). A Bermuda Exempted Company may distribute profits freely to foreign shareholders without deduction of Bermuda tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Bermuda has no local-currency transfer pricing documentation threshold under general law. Mandatory Master File, Local File, and Country-by-Country Report obligations arise when the exempted company belongs to an MNE group with consolidated revenue at or above EUR 750 million (approximately BMD 750 million) and the Bermuda entity is in scope of the CIT Act 2024. Below that scope, contemporaneous pricing records are recommended but not statutorily required.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2018 apply to 'relevant activities', which include banking, insurance, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, shipping, distribution and service centres, holding entities, and intellectual property businesses. A pure customs brokerage house (ISIC 5229) is not a designated relevant activity, so the exempted company files a nil economic substance notification only. If the Bermuda entity also performs intra-group distribution or service-centre functions, it must demonstrate management and control in Bermuda, adequate staff and physical presence, and performance of CIGA in Bermuda; failure triggers information exchange with HMRC and potential re-characterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent CIT Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of BMD 10,000 plus BMD 250 per day of continued default; interest on underpaid CIT at the prescribed rate; on conviction, fines and imprisonment for fraudulent evasion. The Registrar may also strike off the company and revoke the exemption certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who approve false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid CIT and may face civil recovery proceedings. Bermuda courts may impose director disqualification for up to five years and issue travel bans where assets are at risk of dissipation.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties of BMD 10,000 per notification failure plus BMD 10,000 per month of continued default; in aggravated cases, penalties up to BMD 250,000 for IP-related substance failures; the Registrar may exchange information with HMRC and foreign competent authorities, triggering overseas reassessment of profits.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly submit false ESR declarations are personally liable for fines up to BMD 10,000, may be disqualified from acting as directors, and may face extradition or mutual legal assistance proceedings where foreign tax evasion is alleged.
AML, Customs Fraud, and Unlicensed Brokerage

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008, fines up to BMD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years. Under the Customs Act, goods are forfeited and civil penalties up to double or triple the duty evaded apply; the customs broker licence is revoked for repeat violations.

Director personal liability: Any director or senior manager who consents to or connives in the offence is criminally liable. Civil confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 may attach personal assets, and travel restrictions can be imposed during confiscation investigations.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and BEPS Structures

Statutory sanction: In-scope groups suffer Bermuda CIT top-up reassessments. The UK and EU tax authorities may impose transfer pricing adjustments, interest, and penalties of up to 100% of under-declared tax under UK TTPA 2018 and corresponding EU rules. BEPS Action 6, 7, and 8-10 countermeasures and CFC rules re-attribute profits to the source jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors of the UK/EU affiliate who cause incorrect returns to be filed may be jointly liable for penalties and restitution of diverted profits. Director disqualification and cross-border arrest warrants are possible where the arrangement is found to be a fraudulent evasion of tax.
