Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance (administers Corporate Income Tax, Payroll Tax and Land Tax; portal: Bermuda Tax & Payroll Portal at www.gov.bm/tax). Bermuda Customs Service, Ministry of Finance (administers import/export declarations via the Bermuda Customs Automated Business System, B-CABS). Registrar of Companies, Ministry of Finance (incorporation and annual exempted company fee). No national VAT authority exists because Bermuda levies no VAT, GST or general sales tax.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporation: the licensed registered agent files the Memorandum and Articles of Association with the Registrar of Companies and obtains the Certificate of Incorporation and Company Number. 2) Payroll Tax: within 15 days of becoming an employer, register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner to obtain a Payroll Tax Account Number. 3) Corporate Income Tax: if the ultimate parent MNE group has consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million (approx. BMD 890 million), the Bermuda constituent entity must register on the Bermuda Tax Portal and obtain a Bermudian CIT Tax Reference Number. 4) Customs: apply to the Bermuda Customs Service for an Importer/Exporter Code and B-CABS account before the first shipment; no VAT ID exists in Bermuda. 5) Where eligible, apply to the Minister of Finance for a tax stability certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Bermuda Exempted Company is non-resident for exchange control purposes and statutorily exempt from Bermuda income tax, corporation tax, capital gains tax and withholding tax; it is subject only to annual government fees and, for in-scope large multinational groups, the 15% Corporate Income Tax imposed by the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Available election forms

Bermuda law provides no check-the-box classification election; entity classification is statutory. Available elections include: (i) for U.S. federal income tax purposes, the entity may file IRS Form 8832 to elect treatment as a corporation, partnership or disregarded entity; (ii) election under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 to obtain a tax stability guarantee until 2035; (iii) under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, elections governing the Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax mechanics, GloBE Income Inclusion Rule and Undertaxed Profits Rule ordering, and the transitional country-by-country reporting safe harbour; (iv) payroll tax combined-employer election; and (v) USD filing currency election for CIT returns. Bermuda has no comprehensive double tax treaties and no jurisdiction-specific hybrid mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Customs Duty (import tariff) and Stamp Duties - Bermuda has no VAT, GST, sales tax or other general consumption tax; customs duty is levied under the Customs Tariff Act 1973 on the CIF value of imported goods, and stamp duty applies to specified instruments and land transactions.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold because no VAT/GST exists. Customs: importer/exporter code required before first shipment with no monetary threshold; duty is assessed per consignment, with a standard general tariff rate of 22.25% on many goods. Payroll Tax: BMD 0 threshold - any employer with Bermuda payroll must register. Corporate Income Tax: EUR 750 million (approx. BMD 890 million) consolidated revenue threshold of the ultimate parent MNE group.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in the VAT sense: Bermuda has no VAT/GST, so exports of goods and services (including cross-border factoring and reverse-factoring fees) are outside the scope of any output tax and no zero-rating claim is required. For customs purposes, goods are not subject to duty upon export; duty-paid goods that are re-exported within 12 months of import may qualify for a duty drawback under section 70 of the Customs Tariff Act 1973, provided the export declaration is lodged and the goods are identified in B-CABS.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls (CTC), API-based reporting mandate, e-invoicing obligation or digital stamping regime. Electronic filing is mandatory only for customs: import entries, export declarations, manifest submissions and duty payments are processed through B-CABS. Payroll Tax and CIT returns are e-filed on the Bermuda Tax Portal. Trade finance and factoring invoices are issued on a commercial basis without government fiscal clearance or approval.

No export VAT zero-rating fraud or clawback regime exists because Bermuda has no VAT. Customs enforcement: false export declarations, undervaluation, misdescription or smuggling expose the entity to forfeiture of goods, treble-duty assessments, civil penalties up to USD 500,000, importer licence revocation and imprisonment under the Customs Tariff Act 1973 and Customs Management Act. Corporate Income Tax enforcement: late filing penalty of BMD 500 plus BMD 500 per month of continuing default; negligent understatement penalty of 50% of the deficient tax; wilful evasion penalty of 150% of the deficient tax plus criminal prosecution. Payroll Tax enforcement: 10% late-payment surcharge plus monthly interest of 1%. Bermuda maintains no exchange controls, so the 180-day benchmark is an evidentiary/documentary standard rather than a statutory repatriation deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

BMD is pegged at 1:1 to USD and both are legal tender; full convertibility applies to current and capital accounts. No exchange control approvals, remittance taxes, or repatriation restrictions apply to a Bermuda Exempted Company designated 'non-resident' under the Exchange Control Act 1972. Dividends, factoring income, interest, and loan repayments may be remitted freely to non-residents. Practical hurdles are limited to bank-level source-of-funds verification and annual beneficial-ownership filing with BMA.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Bermuda imposes no surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no retention requirement for export or factoring proceeds in any currency. Collections may be held indefinitely in BMD, USD, GBP, or EUR. Statutory reporting obligations are limited to suspicious transaction reports filed with the Financial Intelligence Agency within 5 business days of suspicion, plus 5-year record retention. No FX surrender regime exists.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969. Responsibilities include issuance of the Bermudian dollar (BMD), administration of the Exchange Control Act 1972, and AML/ATF supervision under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008. As Bermuda is a British Overseas Territory, UK HM Treasury (OFSI) sanctions extend to the jurisdiction, and BMA guidance notes are binding on financial business entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM-HIGH for cross-currency collections. UK FCA-regulated EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may open GBP/EUR accounts for a Bermuda Exempted Company with a complete KYC pack, but no EMI supports BMD or Bermudian local clearing. Legal constraints: (i) the factoring entity is a regulated 'financial business' under Bermuda AML law, so client monies must not be parked in an EMI float; (ii) EMI accounts must be zero-balance concentration accounts with daily sweep to a BMA-licensed bank; (iii) BMA expects correspondent-banked clearing as the principal rail, limiting EMIs to treasury sub-ledgers and not for factoring disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share capital and shareholder loans are remitted via SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire from UK/US banks to the Bermuda account with full remitter identification. Payment narrative must include: (i) 'BM EXEMPTED CO CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION'; (ii) BMA financial-business registration number; (iii) tax-exemption certificate number. No Bermuda purpose-code is prescribed, but a source-of-funds confirmation (bank statement, loan agreement, dividend evidence) is required for transactions above USD 100,000. The receiving bank verifies the board resolution authorising the injection and tags the remittance as 'equity capital' to avoid trading-income misclassification; BMA pre-approval is not required for non-resident designated companies.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments, debited from the Bermuda account and credited through UK/New York correspondents; interbank cover via MT202. For reverse-factoring undertakings: Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 (MT700), confirmed by a London or New York bank to guarantee supplier payment. Bulk receivable-purchase settlements use MT101; GBP clears via CHAPS; EUR via TARGET2. Local BMD/USD clearing is same-day through the Bermuda Automated Settlement System. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory local file or master file for standard exempted companies outside the CIT scope. For CIT in-scope groups with global revenue exceeding €750 million, maintain OECD-aligned master file and local file where aggregate related-party transactions exceed BMD 100 million in the fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The Economic Substance Act 2018 and Economic Substance Regulations require relevant entities carrying on 'financing and leasing' (including factoring / trade finance) to demonstrate adequate people, premises, and core income-generating activities in Bermuda. Exempted companies cannot rely solely on zero-tax status; they must satisfy substance requirements for each relevant activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (CIT Act for in-scope groups)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to BMD 100,000 per offence; imprisonment up to 10 years; additional assessment of 100% of the evaded tax and interest at 8% per annum.

Director personal liability: Each director who approved the filing is jointly and severally liable for civil restitution; courts may issue travel bans, disqualification orders, and personal seizure of assets for restitution.
Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Registrar may impose penalties up to BMD 250,000 for three years of non-compliance then BMD 1,000,000 thereafter; continued default results in strike-off of the company and notification to foreign regulators.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false economic substance declarations face personal fines up to BMD 500,000 and a three-year ban from acting as directors in Bermuda.
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act 1997: imprisonment up to 14 years and unlimited fines for money laundering; property confiscation orders; company dissolution and cancellation of registration.

Director personal liability: Directors who operate the shell can be extradited, subjected to travel prohibition, and ordered to repatriate unlawfully derived proceeds; criminal restitution orders may extend to assets beneficially owned by the director.
AML / Financial Services Licence Breach

Statutory sanction: The Bermuda Monetary Authority may impose fines up to BMD 1,000,000, revoke the licence, issue cease-and-desist orders, and require clawback of fees and disgorgement of profits.

Director personal liability: Senior management may be personally fined up to BMD 250,000 and made subject to restorative compensation orders; directors' assets may be traced where negligent supervision caused measurable client loss.
