Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Office of the Tax Commissioner (within the Ministry of Finance) and the Bermuda Registrar of Companies; online access via the Bermuda Government portal at www.gov.bm.

E-government filing portal

Bermuda does not issue a general income-tax TIN because it levies no income or corporation tax. A Bermuda Exempted Company is established by filing incorporation documents with the Registrar of Companies and obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation. If the company carries on an employment activity in Bermuda, it must register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner for payroll tax and obtain an Employer Registration Number. If it imports goods, it must obtain a customs broker/importer account with the Bermuda Customs Department. No VAT ID is required.

Available election forms

Bermuda has no fiscal check-the-box election. For US federal tax purposes, the Bermudian exempted company, being a foreign eligible entity, may make a check-the-box election on IRS Form 8832 to be classified as a corporation, partnership, or disregarded entity. No hybrid mismatch or special statutory elective tax regimes apply under Bermuda law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Sales and Use Tax (not applicable; Bermuda does not levy VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax and instead relies on customs duties and selective consumption taxes)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST registration threshold. Bermuda payroll tax registration is generally required by any employer with Bermuda-based employees once employment obligations arise.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable for VAT/GST because Bermuda does not impose VAT/GST. For customs duty purposes, goods are not subject to duty if exported from Bermuda; relief from duty on imports subsequently re-exported may be claimed under the Bermuda Customs Act and customs tariff rules, subject to documentary evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration or out-of-bond movement record
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill evidencing physical departure from Bermuda
  • Commercial invoice
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment evidence confirming foreign exchange settlement

No VAT electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate exists. Customs declarations and duty filings are submitted electronically through the Bermuda Customs automated processing system; real-time fiscal stamping and API clearance mandates do not apply.

No VAT zero-rating fraud enforcement exists in Bermuda because no VAT/GST is levied. Customs duty evasion and export-relief misdeclarations are subject to the Bermuda Customs Act penalties, including monetary penalties, forfeiture of goods, seizure, and potential prosecution; retrospective clawback of duty relief is available where export evidence is falsified or goods do not leave Bermuda.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged at a fixed rate of 1:1 to the United States Dollar (USD), and both currencies circulate interchangeably within the local economy. Bermuda operates a substantially liberalised exchange control regime for offshore exempted companies: under the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, the BMA has granted general permission for non-resident-owned Bermuda Exempted Companies to transact freely in any currency; to maintain foreign currency bank accounts in Bermuda and abroad; to borrow, lend and invest offshore; and to remit dividends, interest, royalties, management fees and liquidation proceeds without prior individual exchange control approval. Current account and capital account transactions are therefore both fully convertible for such entities, and profit remittance is subject only to standard bank-level completion of the remittance instruction. The only residual administrative hurdle is the obligation to maintain a current Certificate of Exemption and, where a relevant activity is undertaken, to file an annual economic substance declaration under the Economic Substance Act 2018 and the International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act 2018.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no time-bound repatriation timeline. A Bermuda Exempted Company engaged in the export of payroll software services and software licences may retain 100% of its foreign currency export receipts in any permitted currency and may use those receipts directly for offshore disbursements. Licensed dealer banks in Bermuda are authorised under the general permission to accept such receipts, credit the entity's foreign currency account, and execute onward SWIFT MT103 payments without reference to the BMA for each transaction. The entity's statutory obligations are limited to accurate bookkeeping and, where applicable, the filing of the annual economic substance declaration. At the banking level, export invoices, digital platform order confirmations and relevant subscription contracts must be retained as underlying documentation for audit and AML transaction-monitoring purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), the central bank and integrated financial regulatory and exchange control authority administering the Exchange Control Act 1972, the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 (as amended), the Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Guidance Notes, and the Digital Asset Business Act 2018.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW to MEDIUM. Bermuda does not have a general electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime under local law; the Digital Asset Business Act 2018 regulates digital asset-related payment services but does not create a direct EMI passport. Offshore EMIs regulated by the UK FCA, the Central Bank of Ireland, or EU national authorities are legally able to support payment processing for Bermudian entities, but local Bermudian banks treat such EMIs cautiously and ordinarily require the primary treasury, payroll settlement and capital escrow account to be held at a locally licensed bank. Consequently, an offshore EMI (e.g., a UK FCA-authorised direct debit provider, Wise Platform, or Payoneer) may be engaged as a secondary disbursement agent for BACS Direct Debit, UK Faster Payments, and global SWIFT liquidity, but it must not hold the entity's core deposits. The principal legal constraints are: (i) the BMA's expectation that payment intermediaries are regulated in a FATF-equivalent jurisdiction; (ii) the need for a DABA licence or exemption if the EMI engages in digital asset activity; and (iii) Enhanced Due Diligence approval by the local deposit-taking bank before such an EMI is integrated.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for the initial capital injection and future top-ups is via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank chains) from the UK parent or subscriber, settled over CHAPS, Fedwire or CHIPS through the bank's New York or London clearing agent, credited to the entity's BMD/USD settlement account in Bermuda. The payment should be annotated in SWIFT Field 70 as 'CAPITAL INJECTION / SUBSCRIPTION FOR SHARES' to enable the bank's Straight-Through Processing and correct balance-of-payments classification. The authenticated wire details must be confirmed by the entity's board resolution and dual-signatory authorisation. Under the general permission, no individual BMA exchange control approval is required for the capital injection; the bank will record the transaction on the account's transaction monitoring profile. For smaller GBP-denominated top-ups below GBP 25,000, a standard SWIFT transfer remains the only recommended inbound rail; Direct Debit ACH is not appropriate as an inbound capital injection rail because it does not create an irrevocable cleared funds position in the required account.Outbound disbursements in the payroll automation software business follow a dual-rail strategy: (i) settlement of salaries, contractor fees, and statutory national insurance or pension remittances to the UK and EEA uses SWIFT MT103 wires or, for periodic payroll, Direct Debit BACS settlement via a UK FCA-authorised third-party provider or the bank's UK clearing correspondent; (ii) larger cross-border trade payments for software licences, enterprise subscriptions, and international tax contributions are executed by SWIFT MT103 over the bank's correspondent network and, where performance guarantees or goods are involved, by irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 via MT700. The target payment rails for this treasury are therefore Direct Debit ACH for recurring UK/EEA salary runs and SWIFT Wire for all non-domestic, bulk, and irrevocable settlement instructions. The entity will maintain SWIFT purpose codes aligned with Bermuda's balance-of-payments reporting categories and will retain payroll manifests and statutory payment schedules for audit and AML review.USD/GBP deliverable FX forwards executed with licensed Bermudian banks (HSBC Bank Bermuda or Butterfield) or UK FCA-authorised counterparties, with tenors up to 24 months, documented under an ISDA Master Agreement or the bank's standard FX Master Agreement.Cross-currency interest rate swaps converting USD/BMD-denominated intercompany borrowings into GBP or EUR tranches to match long-term payroll liabilities, subject to board approval and economic substance compliance.FX swaps and same-day spot transactions for intra-day liquidity management between payroll account currencies and the base USD/BMD account; the fixed BMD/USD 1:1 peg and free USD interchangeability inherently reduce basis risk.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for third-country emerging-market currencies applicable to certain payroll destinations where convertibility restrictions exist, permitted under the licensed dealer's global markets authorisation only upon evidence of an underlying payroll exposure in the restricted currency.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable. Bermuda has no statutory transfer pricing regime. For ESR record-keeping, the applicable threshold is not a monetary amount; any Exempted Company engaged in a relevant activity with Bermuda gross income (or foreign-source income effectively connected to Bermuda) must maintain local accounts, contracts, and minutes supporting arm's-length remuneration.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2018 fully apply. A Bermuda Exempted Company performing a 'relevant activity'—which may include Headquarters, Distribution and Service Centre, or Intellectual Property Business for a payroll software platform—must demonstrate: (i) management and control exercised in Bermuda, (ii) core income-generating activities performed in Bermuda, (iii) adequate physical presence, qualified employees, and operating expenditure in Bermuda, and (iv) compliance with local record-keeping and reporting. A company that is tax resident elsewhere must prove that presence in the other jurisdiction to avoid the ESR penalty framework.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty of up to BMD 100,000; daily default penalty of BMD 1,000; automatic strike-off of the company from the Bermuda Register. The Registrar may also require a sworn declaration of compliance before registration is reinstated.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly cause or permit a false ESR filing are subject to imprisonment for up to 5 years and personal financial restitution for any penalties levied. A director may also be disqualified from acting as a director of any Bermuda company and be issued with a travel ban.
Beneficial Ownership (BO) Register Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to BMD 10,000 for each violation, plus a daily default fine of BMD 500 for continued failure. The Registrar can take immediate corrective action, including freezing the company's file and preventing any corporate transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, disqualification from corporate management for a period up to 5 years, asset seizure orders, and imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Proceeds of Crime Act. Travel bans may be imposed pending completion of investigations.
AML/ATF Framework Violations (Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, Anti-Terrorism Act and FCPA-like exposure)

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines imposed by the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) up to BMD 1,000,000 per contravention; revocation of any required money service provider licence (if the SaaS platform integrates payment rails); transaction blocking; mandatory surrender of records.

Director personal liability: Directors and money laundering reporting officers may be held criminally liable if they knowingly permit money laundering through payroll disbursements. Penalties include imprisonment up to 14 years, personal asset forfeiture, and lifetime travel bans. Civil restitution may be ordered in tandem with a UK or US sanctions enforcement action.
Misleading Foreign Tax or Transfer Pricing Statements

Statutory sanction: Bermuda low-level penalties are limited; however, foreign revenue authorities (HMRC, IRS, EU Member State) may reassess profits under CFC and transfer pricing rules, impose penalties of up to 200% of the tax underpaid, and exchange information via the Bermuda-UK TIEA. The Bermuda entity may be struck off for filing false declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors may be pursued personally under foreign 'personal liability for transfer pricing' provisions (e.g., UK Finance Act 2024 measures) if they approve invoices knowing local income was under-priced. Directors risk extradition from Bermuda, global asset freezes, and imprisonment for tax evasion/complicity in tax fraud.
