Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital for an SAS; capital is freely fixed in the articles. For cash subscription, at least 50% of the nominal value of cash shares must be paid up upon incorporation, and the balance may be called within five years. Cash contributions must be deposited into a temporarily blocked bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and are released only upon RCS registration. In-kind contributions are delivered immediately; a contribution auditor (commissaire aux apports) is required unless a statutory exemption is established, and is prudently retained where an in-kind asset exceeds EUR 30,000 or represents the main part of the capital.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office must maintain a fire-safety attestation for the applicable premises category if open to clients, and an up-to-date occupational risk assessment (document unique d'évaluation des risques - DUERP) under the Labour Code. No separate structural load inspection is required for ordinary administrative use; any structural certificate is a landlord obligation under applicable urban planning regulations.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum capital for an SAS; capital is freely fixed in the articles. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the official portal impots.gouv.fr; corporate registration and SIREN/TAX ID issuance coordinated through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS, incorporation with the Guichet Unique automatically generates a SIREN number and, upon verification of economic activity, the intra-community VAT identification number (n° d'identification TVA intracommunautaire) with format FRXXYYYYYYYZZ (two-digit key + SIREN) issued by the DGFiP. A foreign entity without French incorporation but liable for French VAT (e.g., importing goods, holding stock, or supplying taxable services subject to French place-of-supply rules) must file a pre-registration questionnaire (form No. 2172-SD equivalent) with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) covering local establishment or direct VAT registration, and may be required to appoint an accredited tax representative (représentant fiscal) if not established in the EU; processing typically takes 2–4 weeks and requires proof of identity, articles of association, and evidence of the first taxable transaction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists under French law; an SAS is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, article 206 CGI). Available elections include: (i) option for a single VAT taxable person / VAT grouping under article 256 C III CGI for related entities; (ii) option to waive the small-business VAT exemption (article 293 B CGI) to voluntarily become a VAT payer; (iii) option for the reduced corporate income tax rate of 15% for taxable profits up to €42,500 per tax year under article 219 CGI; (iv) election under the parent-subsidiary regime (régime mères-filles); and (v) hybrid mismatch counter-rules under articles 209 B, 209 C and 212 bis CGI (ATAD 2 transposition) restrict but do not create elective deductions — no check-the-box equivalent exists for entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France applies no general monetary registration threshold for taxable persons established in France; French-resident businesses must register from their first taxable euro, subject to the small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA, article 293 B CGI) below €37,500 annual turnover for services and €85,000 for trade in goods (reduced ceilings of €34,600 and €81,500 with incremental tolerance). For non-French/EU suppliers providing digital services or intra-EU distance sales of goods, the €10,000 annual EU-wide threshold applies before French VAT registration is triggered. Foreign suppliers with no EU establishment are liable to register for French VAT on the first euro of taxable French supplies unless exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération à l'exportation) applies to the sale and physical dispatch of movable goods from France to a destination outside the EU provided proof of export is obtained within the statutory documentation window under articles 262-I-1° CGI. Services directly linked to exported goods (transport, handling, inspection) may also be zero-rated under article 262-I-2° CGI. Pure supply-chain audit consulting services (ISIC 7020) sold B2B to a non-EU customer are not technically zero-rated as exports: the place of supply is the recipient's country of establishment under article 259-1 CGI, rendering the French supply out of scope of French VAT (no VAT, no refund). If the same service is sold to a French VAT-registered client, domestic 20% VAT applies; if sold to an EU client, the reverse charge place-of-supply rule under article 259-1 CGI applies. To benefit from zero-rating on goods, the exporter must retain documentary proof of physical departure from the EU within 90 days of the invoice date (administratively accepted, not a strict statutory deadline) and obtain customs clearance validation through the DELTA export system.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/EXA) lodged electronically via the DELTA/Système de dédouanement, including the Export MRN (Movement Reference Number) and customs release certificate
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill or CMR/road consignment note evidencing physical departure from the EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 proof of settlement from the foreign buyer
  • Commercial Invoice (original) matching the customs declaration value and description
  • Packing List
  • Proof of VAT registration status of the customer for B2B services (when applying reverse charge or non-EU out-of-scope treatment)

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is phased in under the French e-invoicing reform: from 1 September 2026 all taxable persons must be able to receive structured e-invoices and all large and mid-size enterprises must issue them; from 1 September 2027 all small enterprises must issue structured e-invoices. Invoices must be transmitted through an accredited Dematerialization Partner Platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire — PDP) or the public portal Portail Public de Facturation (PPF), which forwards structured transaction data (including VAT summary and payment data) to DGFiP. This is a transmission/clearance model (not an Italian-style real-time fiscal stamping) but the formats must conform to EN 16931 (Factur-X, UBL, CII), and e-reporting of domestic B2C cross-border transaction data is transmitted to DGFiP near-real-time via the PPF. From 2026, non-compliant invoicing can trigger penalties of €15 per invoice up to €50,000 per entity per year.

The French tax administration may retrospectively claw back zero-rated VAT (recouvrement de la TVA exonérée) if the export proof is missing, incomplete, or fraudulent, with the right of recovery extended to 10 years under article L.176 of the Livre des procédures fiscales where the absence of proof, bad faith, or fraudulent maneuvers is established (standard 3-year right for good-faith taxpayers). Penalties escalate under article 1729 CGI: 40% of the VAT in issue for deliberate non-disclosure (mauvaise foi), 80% for fraud with organized maneuvers (manœuvres frauduleuses); late-payment interest accrues at 0.20% per month under article 1727 CGI. Criminal tax fraud under article 1741 CGI carries fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years, with additional customs penalties under the Code des douanes for false export declarations including confiscation of goods and fines equal to up to one time the value of the goods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full member of the euro area and the European Economic and Monetary Union; the euro (EUR) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no material restrictions on profit remittances or dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders, provided the source funds are legally repatriated earnings, corporate formalities (e.g., shareholder approval, tax clearance, and beneficial ownership documentation) are satisfied, and anti-money-laundering / know-your-customer verification is completed. For transactions with non-EU jurisdictions, EU capital movement provisions and French financial sanctions screening apply, but no general pre-approval is required except for specific regulated sectors or countries subject to EU restrictive measures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose any mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds into euros. Exporters and entities performing cross-border services are free to retain foreign currency receipts in designated bank accounts; however, all cross-border payments above €10,000 must be reported to the French Customs and Indirect Taxation Administration (Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects, DGDDI) or the Banque de France under the monthly balance-of-payments reporting regime (reporting circuit via a resident intermediary bank). The applicable statutory timeline is T+1 business day for reporting over the counter / postal transactions and T+5 business days for declaration amendments; the reporting threshold is €10,000 per transaction. No conversion ratio is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) and Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) acting under the Foreign Exchange and Foreign Trade Regulations (Code monétaire et financier), in coordination with the European Central Bank for euro-area monetary policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for inbound EUR receipt and outbound EUR disbursement, and Low for serving as the sole treasury rail for a French SAS that needs to evidence statutory statutory capital deposits, given French bank account verification requirements for corporate registration. Offshore digital EMI institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business) are legally viable as add-on payment rails for settlement, provided (i) they hold an EU/EEA e-money institution license passported into France or an equivalent license recognized under the second Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC), (ii) they comply with the French AMF/FEA framework and register with the ACPR, and (iii) the SAS maintains a primary euro account with a prudentially supervised French credit institution. An offshore EMI not authorized in France cannot be used as the exclusive banking infrastructure for operational clearing or for satisfying the statutory deposit of share capital before incorporation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS), the initial shareholder capital injection must be routed as a compliant international wire transfer to a French bank account opened in the name of the 'société en formation'. The wire must include a clear remittance info / purpose code: 'Capital Subscription – Incorporation of SAS' (French: 'Apport en capital social – Constitution de SAS'). The sending bank must use SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA CT if within SEPA zone) and the beneficiary must be the company account with the pre-registered RIB. French banks require the deposit certificate (attestation de dépôt des fonds) issued by the receiving bank (Caisse des Dépôts or any French credit institution) for the notary / registration. For euro-area transfers, the purpose code shall be 'REMU' or 'SUBS' with settlement method 'CRED' and reference 'A000'. For transfers from third countries, the transfer must be accompanied by the underlying shareholders' resolution, certified ID passports of contributing shareholders, and a completed 'MTC' (Moyen de Télécompensation) declaration if the amount exceeds €10,000. The capital remains blocked on an escrow-like account (compte de dépôt) until legal incorporation is completed and the RCS registration is filed; upon issuance of the RCS receipt, the funds are converted to an operational account.For outbound trade-related disbursements (payments to suppliers, contractors, logistics providers, and subcontractors in the AEO supply-chain audit business), the primary clearing rails are (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) for euro-denominated payments within the EEA, governed by SEPA Regulation (EU) No 260/2012, and (ii) SWIFT MT103 (Customer Credit Transfer) for non-EEA or non-euro disbursements. Where the underlying transaction is a B2B trade settlement for goods or services, a Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary credit under UCP 600) may be used with the French bank as issuing or confirming bank, but for a service-oriented AEO audit (ISIC 7020) wire transfers are standard; no documentary credit is statutory mandatory. All outbound payments require a valid purpose code: for services, use 'SERV' (services) or the harmonized French reporting code 'SF' for balance-of-payments data; for contracts exceeding €100,000, supporting contracts and invoices must be retained for five years. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for inbound EUR receipt and outbound EUR disbursement, and Low for serving as the sole treasury rail for a French SAS that needs to evidence statutory statutory capital deposits, given French bank account verification requirements for corporate registration. Offshore digital EMI institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business) are legally viable as add-on payment rails for settlement, provided (i) they hold an EU/EEA e-money institution license passported into France or an equivalent license recognized under the second Electronic Money Directive (2009/110/EC), (ii) they comply with the French AMF/FEA framework and register with the ACPR, and (iii) the SAS maintains a primary euro account with a prudentially supervised French credit institution. An offshore EMI not authorized in France cannot be used as the exclusive banking infrastructure for operational clearing or for satisfying the statutory deposit of share capital before incorporation.
