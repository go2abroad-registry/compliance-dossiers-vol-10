Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - the Bermuda exempted company is outside the scope of Bermuda income tax, capital gains tax, and withholding tax; its Bermuda fiscal burden is confined to annual company fees, customs duty on dutiable imports, payroll tax on Bermuda remuneration, and the 15% Pillar Two Corporate Income Tax applicable only to constituent entities of MNE groups with group revenue of at least EUR 750 million. For UK purposes the company is non-UK resident absent UK central management and control.

Available election forms

Bermuda has no check-the-box or pass-through entity election; an exempted company is always a body corporate. Relevant elections under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 include designation of a filing group representative for consolidated CIT filing, election of the accounting period to align with the ultimate parent's fiscal year, and transitional elections for loss carry-forward and foreign tax credits as prescribed by the Tax Commissioner. No hybrid-mismatch or CFC elections exist under Bermuda law; UK hybrid-mismatch treatment is irrelevant unless a UK taxable presence exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales/use tax. The only broad-based Bermuda indirect tax is Import Duty under the Customs Tariff Act 1995, charged on the C.I.F. customs value (including ocean freight and insurance) of goods imported into Bermuda at a standard general rate of 22.25%, with tariff lines ranging from 0% on essentials to 75% on tobacco and luxury goods. NVOCC ocean freight forwarding services, container brokerage, and documentation fees are outside the charge to Bermuda indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold: import duty attaches from the first importation, and every importer or customs broker must hold a customs registration and file an electronic declaration for each shipment. Payroll tax registration applies from the first Bermuda employee, with a reduced rate for qualifying small businesses. CIT registration applies only to Bermuda entities within MNE groups having consolidated global revenue of at least EUR 750 million (approx. BMD 810 million); the test is group-wide and is not a Bermuda turnover threshold. No VAT/GST registration threshold exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable: Bermuda levies no VAT/GST, so there is no 0% export VAT rate, no input VAT credit, and no VAT refund mechanism for NVOCC services. Goods exported from Bermuda are not subject to Bermuda customs duty, which attaches only at importation. Duty drawback is available when duty-paid goods are re-exported in the same condition (e.g., NVOCC transshipment cargo); the drawback claim must be filed with Bermuda Customs within the statutory claim period and substantiated by duty payment records and export documentation. Bermuda abolished exchange controls in 2003 and imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure operates as a contractual/audit benchmark for freight collections and inward remittances.

  • Electronic cargo manifest (inward/outward) filed pre-arrival via the Bermuda Customs single-window
  • Master bill of lading and house bill of lading / sea waybill
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Customs import declaration and duty payment receipt for duty-paid goods destined for re-export
  • Customs duty drawback claim form with proof of re-exportation
  • NVOCC shipping instructions, container booking records, and freight invoice
  • Bermuda trade and business licence or freight forwarding licence, where local licensure applies

Bermuda Customs operates an electronic single-window requiring pre-arrival electronic cargo manifests and electronic import declarations/clearance for ocean freight. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous e-invoicing/audit-control mandate, and no digital stamping regime. CIT, payroll tax, and customs filings are submitted via official Bermuda Government e-services, and supporting records (bills of lading, manifests, commercial invoices, duty receipts) must be retained for six years for customs/CIT audit purposes.

There is no VAT zero-rating clawback in Bermuda. Equivalent risks are: (i) customs drawback fraud, attracting forfeiture of goods, statutory fines, and criminal prosecution under the Customs Tariff Act 1995; (ii) CIT non-compliance where in scope, exposing the company to Tax Commissioner assessments, interest, and late-filing/late-payment penalties of up to 10% of the tax shown due plus registration/default fines; (iii) Payroll Tax default, where deliberate underpayment may trigger additional tax of up to 100% and director liability; and (iv) UK-side HMRC challenge if it asserts a Bermuda PE, transfer-pricing or diverted-profits adjustments, or CFC attribution where UK-connected management and control exists, producing retrospective tax and penalty exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is legal tender at a fixed 1:1 peg to the US Dollar. Current-account transactions are effectively freely convertible via licensed authorised dealers. Capital-account transactions require Exchange Control permission; however, a Bermuda Exempted Company granted non-resident designation (non-resident ownership, business conducted wholly outside Bermuda, and a Certificate of Designation issued under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966) enjoys broad general permissions to maintain USD and multicurrency accounts and to effect capital conversions without per-transaction licensing. Profit and dividend remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted under the Companies Act 1981, subject only to BMA anti-money-laundering and sanctions screening of the payment trail. The practical remittance hurdle is documentary rather than regulatory: the paying bank requires a board resolution, solvency statement, and evidence of the entity's non-resident tax-exempt status.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1972, a resident exporter must offer export proceeds to an authorised dealer within six months of accrual and comply with prescribed conversion instructions. A non-resident-designated Exempted Company is exempt from mandatory surrender and may retain ocean freight receivables in USD or other foreign currencies in its offshore account tier without any compulsory conversion ratio to BMD. For NVOCC settlement efficiency, funds may remain in USD to match carrier payables, provided the account is flagged as non-resident in the bank's Exchange Control reporting and periodic returns to the BMA.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), constituted under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969 and functioning as Bermuda's central bank, currency board, and integrated financial services regulator. Exchange Control powers are exercised by the BMA under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, administered through locally licensed authorised dealers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Bermuda Exchange Control requires any entity receiving deposits or conducting money-service activity in or from the Territory to hold a BMA licence under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 or the Money Services Business regime. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., a UK-authorised electronic money institution) enjoys no passport into Bermuda and cannot clear Bermuda-resident settlement flows without local licensing. For a non-resident Exempted Company, the practical expedient is to use a UK or EU EMI at the UK treasury layer for GBP/EUR disbursements while retaining the Bermuda bank account as the primary USD clearing node. Legal constraint: a UK EMI naming the Bermuda entity as account holder is permissible where the entity conducts no regulated activity inside the UK; NVOCC receivables routed into Bermuda must nonetheless be settled through a BMA-authorised dealer for Exchange Control reporting. Fintech rails are therefore ancillary, not a substitute, for Bermuda clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the UK parent or non-resident shareholders must arrive via SWIFT MT103 to the Bermuda account referencing the corporate name and an explicit purpose code, e.g., 'Capital Contribution under Companies Act 1981' or 'Shareholder Subordinated Loan'. The remittance must be pre-notified to the BMA-authorised dealer so the inflow is tagged as non-resident capital and excluded from resident Exchange Control returns. Verification bundle: board resolution authorising share issuance, updated share register stamped by the Bermuda registered agent, and a copy of the Certificate of Designation confirming non-resident tax-exempt status. For loans, a signed term sheet or loan agreement and an intra-group interest-rate benchmark (SOFR reference) must accompany the first drawdown.Carrier freight charges, container depot fees, customs duties, and port-handling costs are settled via SWIFT MT103 corporate-to-corporate wires with strict reference to the freight invoice and bill-of-lading number. Commercial Letters of Credit are deployed only where a sub-contracted ocean carrier or foreign warehouse operator requires documentary undertakings, such as ILUs or charter-party hire exceeding 90-day tenors. For high-volume low-value operational disbursements, the bank's multicurrency clearing facility enables USD ACH/Direct or local-currency batch transfers to the UK account via correspondent rail, provided each disbursement carries a purpose code consistent with Bermuda Exchange Control general permissions. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for Bermuda Exempted Companies under statutory tax protection until 31 March 2035; 15% domestic minimum top-up tax applies under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 to in-scope multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Bermuda does not impose dividend withholding tax on distributions made by an Exempted Company to foreign shareholders, whether in cash or in specie.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on the 15th day of the 15th month after the end of the fiscal year for in-scope MNE groups under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023. Quarterly estimated top-up tax instalments may apply when domestic top-up tax is anticipated.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual Return must be delivered within thirty days of the anniversary of incorporation. The ESR Return must be filed within twelve months after the end of the accounting period. Both filings are compulsory even when the exempted company has no CIT liability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No general transfer pricing documentation regime applies to Exempted Companies outside the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023. For in-scope MNE groups, mandatory Master File and Local File obligations are triggered when consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000 in at least two of the four preceding fiscal years; in Bermuda local currency this threshold is BMD 750,000,000 because the Bermuda dollar is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply to entities conducting relevant activities. Pure NVOCC ocean freight forwarding under ISIC 5229 is generally not a 'relevant activity' unless the entity also owns, operates, or charters vessels, which would invoke the 'shipping' category. If relevant activity is triggered, the entity must demonstrate a physical Bermuda office, qualified full-time employees, board meetings held and minuted in Bermuda, and sufficient operating expenditure proportionate to core income-generating activities. Even for non-relevant activities, an annual ESR declaration confirming 'not relevant' must be filed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misuse of Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Under the Bermuda Tax Administration Act 2018 and Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, penalties may reach 120% of the tax understated, plus interest at 6.5% per annum. Fines up to BMD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to five years may be imposed for fraudulent evasion. Repeat misuse can lead to revocation of tax protection and compulsory liquidation.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false tax or annual declarations face civil recovery of concealed profits, travel bans, disqualification from acting as directors, and imprisonment up to five years. Non-resident directors are subject to international extradition through mutual legal assistance between the United Kingdom, Bermuda, and third-party states.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda ESR Act 2018 imposes annual fines between BMD 10,000 and BMD 100,000 for an entity failing to satisfy substance requirements. Persistent non-compliance triggers compulsory strike-off, loss of the exemption certificate, and consequent inability to hold assets or enter into contracts in Bermuda.

Director personal liability: Directors of shell companies are jointly and severally liable for the substance penalties up to BMD 100,000, may be subjected to freezing orders over corporate and personal assets, and can be barred from leaving Bermuda while an ESR enforcement investigation is pending.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Act 2018, the company is liable to unlimited fines on conviction, seizure of all assets derived from the offending logistics contracts, and mandatory suspension or cancellation of the required freight-forwarding / NVOCC licence.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal imprisonment of up to twenty years, confiscation orders for the value of laundered funds, and global asset freeze. They are also disqualified from holding corporate office for up to ten years and may be tried in absentia with subsequent international arrest warrants.
Non-Filing / Annual Return Strict Liability

Statutory sanction: The Registrar of Companies may impose a late filing penalty of BMD 300 plus a daily surcharge of BMD 25. ESR non-filing carries a statutory penalty up to BMD 100,000. The company may be dissolved ex parte, with all Bermuda assets and banking mandates automatically forfeited to the Crown.

Director personal liability: Each director and the Bermuda resident secretary are personally liable for the unpaid penalties and daily defaults. Directors who continue to trade after strike-off are personally liable for all corporate debts incurred during the post-strike-off period.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / PE Misallocation

Statutory sanction: Under the Bermuda CIT framework and OECD Pillar Two alignment, Bermuda may adjust the GloBE top-up tax computation, impose recharacterization penalties of 5% of the transaction value, and levy daily default penalties for incomplete transfer pricing documentation. The maximum aggregate penalty can exceed BMD 250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify CIT returns knowingly containing false international profit allocations are personally liable for restitution to the tax administration and criminal fines up to BMD 250,000. They may also be prohibited from leaving Bermuda pending final determination of the adjustment.
