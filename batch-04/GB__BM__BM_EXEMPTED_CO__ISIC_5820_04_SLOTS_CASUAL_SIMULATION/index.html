Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist in Bermuda because the jurisdiction imposes no direct income tax. Available statutory elections include: (i) application for a Tax Assurance Certificate; (ii) election to file as an economic substance reporting entity under the International Business Companies framework; (iii) for Pillar Two-qualifying groups only, election of the domestic minimum top-up tax and income inclusion rule mechanisms under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024; (iv) standard elections as to share capital structure, dividend approval mechanics, and redemption rights under the Companies Act 1981.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT and no GST regime. Bermuda indirect imposts are limited to customs duty on physical imports, payroll tax on Bermuda employment, and land tax on Bermuda real property. Electronically supplied simulated social casino software, virtual slots content, and in-app purchases delivered through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are entirely outside the Bermuda indirect tax base.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Bermuda has no VAT/GST registration threshold in Bermudian dollars (BMD). A Bermuda Customs Service importer registration is required only for physical importation of goods into Bermuda; pure digital software publishing with no tangible goods crossing the customs frontier triggers no indirect tax registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST and therefore provides no export zero-rating mechanism or reclaimable input VAT. Revenues from simulated slots games sold via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are outside the Bermuda indirect tax base and are neither taxable nor zero-rated. If the Bermuda Exempted Company were ever separately VAT-registered in the United Kingdom through a UK permanent establishment, B2B supplies of digital gaming services would be outside the scope of UK VAT, while B2C supplies would be taxed at the UK customer's place of residence; however, the subject entity has no such UK nexus.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate exists in Bermuda. Invoicing and revenue records must comply with common-law evidentiary retention rules, the Companies Act 1981 annual filing requirements, and the Anti-Money Laundering Regulations 2018, including FATCA and CRS reporting obligations administered by the Office of the Tax Commissioner.

Export zero-rating fraud and VAT clawback penalties are not cognisable in Bermuda because no export VAT regime exists. General enforcement consequences include: (i) late or non-filing of annual company returns with the Registrar of Companies triggers fixed penalty fees, restoration costs, and company strike-off or dissolution; (ii) payroll tax non-compliance attracts civil penalties of up to 50% of the tax due plus 8% annual interest and director liability in serious cases; (iii) knowingly false statements to the Registrar or the Tax Commissioner are prosecutable under the Bermuda Criminal Code (Theft Act 1950) and the Companies Act 1981; (iv) AML/CFT deficiencies expose the entity and its service providers to fines up to BMD 250,000, licence suspension, and payment-rail termination by Apple and Google; (v) for groups exceeding the EUR 750 million revenue threshold, the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 permits retrospective assessments, interest, and penalties of up to 100% of the top-up tax due for wilful misreporting. No mandatory FX repatriation deadline or exchange-control clawback applies in Bermuda; however, FATCA/CRS records must evidence the intra-group profit distribution trail.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar (USD), giving the currency de facto convertibility in international transactions. The Exchange Control Act 1972 classifies persons and entities as resident or non-resident; a Bermuda exempted company that is owned by non-residents and does not carry on business in Bermuda is ordinarily designated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes. Such non-resident designated entities enjoy full capital and current account convertibility: foreign-currency receipts may be retained, no prior BMA approval is required for bona fide external remittances, and profit distributions such as dividends, shareholder loan repayments, royalty payments and liquidation proceeds may flow out freely. No stamp duty, withholding tax or exchange-control levy is imposed on outward profit remittance. If the entity maintains BMD accounts with a licensed Bermuda bank, the BMA retains oversight of transactions involving Bermuda-dollar lending or domestic residents; however, for a wholly non-resident exempted company, the convertibility regime is unrestricted and profit remittance is not obstructed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement on non-resident designated exempted companies. Export receipts may be retained in BMD, USD or any other freely usable currency in a licensed Bermuda bank account. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory timeline requiring repatriation, conversion or surrender of export proceeds. Licensed banks may apply internal documentary checks for large outward payments, including source-of-funds verification and purpose-code declarations, but the BMA does not require a non-resident entity to convert export receipts into BMD. This position applies because the company is designated non-resident; a resident Bermudian company would ordinarily require BMA permission for specified capital transfers, but that classification does not apply to the exempted structure used here.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as Bermuda's central bank, currency issuer and exchange-control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate feasibility for ancillary collections but not recommended as the primary treasury clearing rail. Bermuda does not maintain a separate e-money institution (EMI) licensing category; any entity holding funds for customers or issuing stored value must generally be a licensed bank or a licensed financial institution under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, or rely on a foreign-authorised EMI. Offshoredigital EMIs, including UK-authorised e-money institutions, may offer multicurrency IBANs and remote onboarding, but they frequently decline social-casino and gaming-related merchants under merchant-category risk policies. A foreign EMI also creates settlement exposure outside Bermuda, complicating local books-and-records expectations and BMA oversight. Therefore, use an offshore EMI only as a supplementary collection route, with the primary operating and liquidity accounts held at a BMA-licensed Bermuda commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 wires to the beneficiary bank in Bermuda, using the company's full legal name and the accurate BMD or USD account number. Bermuda does not use IBAN; the beneficiary bank BIC/SWIFT code must be provided, and US-dollar wires are typically cleared through the bank's US correspondent network (Fedwire/CHIPS). The remitter must use an ISO 20022 remittance purpose code of 'CAPT' (capital contribution) or 'INVT' (investment) on the MT103; when the remitter is a UK bank using CHAPS, the transfer should be reported as an investment/capital cross-border payment. The beneficiary bank will verify the exact corporate name match, UBO information, source of funds and a board resolution authorising the account and the incoming transfer. For amounts above USD 100,000, provide a signed subscription letter and a board resolution in advance to pre-empt OFAC sanctions screening and correspondent-bank due diligence.Outbound trade settlements should use SWIFT MT103 or, for high-value structured transactions, a commercial letter of credit (documentary credit) issued by the Bermuda bank and advised to the beneficiary's bank. For recurring intercompany and royalty payments, MT103 with SWIFT GPI is recommended for end-to-end tracking. USD disbursements require no FX conversion because BMD is pegged to USD; GBP and EUR disbursements are converted at the bank's treasury desk at market rate.
