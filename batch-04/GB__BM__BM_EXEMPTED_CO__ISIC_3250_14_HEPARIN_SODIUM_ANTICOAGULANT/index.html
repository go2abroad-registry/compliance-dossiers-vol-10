Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC), Bermuda; official government portal: gov.bm with OTC electronic filing modules accessed via https://www.gov.bm (Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 administration); Bermuda Customs Service (Ministry of Finance) for customs duty, import/export declarations and the ASYCUDA World single-window platform; Registrar of Companies (Ministry of Finance) for incorporation and issuance of the Bermuda Company Registration Number.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Bermuda Exempted Company with the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 1981 and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and Company Registration Number, which is the primary statutory identifier (no minimum capital requirement, non-resident ownership permitted). Step 2: If the entity is a constituent entity of an MNE group with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more, register for Corporate Income Tax (CIT) with the Office of the Tax Commissioner within the window prescribed by OTC registration guidance; the registration is submitted through the OTC e-filing portal and results in issuance of a CIT Registration/Tax Identification Number. Step 3: Register with Bermuda Customs Service as an importer/exporter via the ASYCUDA World electronic single window to obtain a Customs Client Code; no VAT registration exists because Bermuda levies no VAT/GST. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda historically imposes no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or withholding tax on dividends, interest or royalties; in-scope MNE groups became subject to the 15% Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 top-up regime for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025; payroll tax, customs duty and stamp duty apply only where employees, imports or specified instruments exist).

Available election forms

Under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, in-scope constituent entities may elect: (i) to act as the designated filing entity for the Bermuda group; (ii) to apply the Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (QDMTT) safe harbour; (iii) to adopt a designated accounting period with Tax Commissioner approval; (iv) to apply transitional loss carry-forward and GloBE loss election mechanics consistent with the OECD Pillar Two Model Rules; and (v) to use simplified effective tax rate calculations where the jurisdictional GloBE ETR exceeds the transitional rate. The Minister of Finance may issue certificates under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 granting an undertaking against future Bermuda income/capital taxes until 31 March 2035, subject to CITA carve-outs for in-scope MNE groups. No domestic check-the-box election exists in Bermuda; however, a Bermuda Exempted Company may file IRS Form 8832 for US federal tax classification (disregarded entity vs. corporation), and CITA/GloBE hybrid mismatch rules operate to deny benefits from deduction/non-inclusion mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Bermuda has no VAT or GST; the relevant indirect tax levies are Bermuda Customs Duty (import tariffs under the Customs Tariff Act 1971), stamp duty and the foreign currency purchase tax; the UK VAT system (Value Added Tax Act 1994) applies only within the UK VAT territory and can touch the entity only through a UK taxable presence or UK importation of goods.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Bermuda because no VAT/GST is levied; all commercial importers must register as customs clients with Bermuda Customs regardless of turnover. CIT registration under the CITA 2023 applies to Bermuda constituent entities of MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more (approximately BMD 810,000,000 at the Bermudian dollar/US dollar 1:1 peg). The UK VAT registration threshold of GBP 90,000 taxable turnover is relevant only if the group establishes a UK taxable presence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda levies no VAT/GST, so no domestic export zero-rating claim or refund mechanism exists; exports from Bermuda are outside the scope of Bermuda indirect taxation and no material export duty applies to pharmaceutical products under the Customs Tariff Act 1971. Where the group exports heparin sodium through a UK VAT-registered branch or from Great Britain to Bermuda, UK VAT zero-rating under VATA 1994 applies if: (i) the goods are exported to a destination outside the UK VAT territory; (ii) removal occurs within 3 months of the time of supply; (iii) the supplier retains official export evidence (UK Customs Declaration Service movement reference number) and commercial evidence (contract, invoice, bill of lading/airway bill, payment evidence); and (iv) the goods are shipped by or on behalf of the supplier. Pharmaceutical export income remains within the Bermuda CITA 2023/GloBE computation where the group is in scope; export zero-rating does not exempt CIT top-up exposure.

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government e-invoicing clearance mandate applies in Bermuda; customs declarations are processed through the ASYCUDA World single-window system, and CIT/payroll returns are filed electronically through the OTC portal. If the entity becomes UK VAT registered, it must operate under Making Tax Digital (MTD) using API-compatible software for digital VAT returns; the UK does not require government pre-clearance of individual e-invoices.

Bermuda imposes no exchange controls, so there is no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied contractually under the letter-of-credit/SWIFT trade-finance evidence bundle and by the group transfer pricing documentation policy, and failure to evidence inward remittance within 180 days may invalidate UK VAT zero-rating evidence and trigger a deemed-supply assessment. Under UK VAT, HMRC may deny export zero-rating retrospectively where evidence is incomplete, assessing tax and interest under the standard 4-year time limit (extended to 20 years for deliberate error or fraud) with penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 of up to 100% of potential lost revenue, reduced only for timely disclosure; criminal liability under the Customs and Excise Management Act 1979 may arise for fraudulent evasion. Under Bermuda's CITA 2023, inaccurate returns, non-registration, or failure to maintain transfer pricing documentation expose the entity to penalties up to 100% of the understated tax plus interest, monetary penalties imposed by the Tax Commissioner, and a QDMTT top-up recomputation; fraudulent arrangements do not benefit from Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 certificates, and retrospective adjustment windows extend to 6 years for non-fraud and up to 20 years where fraud or concealment is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is externally pegged at 1:1 to the U.S. dollar (USD). BMA administers a formal exchange control regime under the Exchange Control Act 1972. For an exempted company designated as non-resident, current account transactions, including trade settlement, service fees, and dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders, are generally freely permitted; however, all such payments still require an authorised dealer or BMA approval where the transaction is denominated in BMD or funded through a local bank account. Capital account transactions, comprising initial share capital injection, quasi-equity loans, bond issuances, liquidation proceeds, and substantial intra-group cross-border funding, are subject to prior BMA consent, notification, or applicable exemption orders. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal because Bermuda imposes no income tax, withholding tax, or capital gains tax; nevertheless, the company must maintain valid economic substance records, file the required annual corporate returns, and present audited financial statements or director confirmation to the commercial bank and BMA when process remittance evidence is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda does not impose a statutory percentage-based surrender obligation on export proceeds for exempted companies designated as non-resident. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in an approved external account or an authorised-dealer foreign currency account without mandatory conversion into Bermuda dollars. Receipts must be credited to the company's licensed banks within standard banking timelines, and supporting documentation comprising invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations, and sanitary certificates must be retained to evidence the trade transaction. Where the company maintains a Bermuda-dollar operational account, conversion of export proceeds into BMD is permitted only through a BMA-authorised dealer at the prevailing market rate, and any conversion exceeding prescribed limits or presenting unusual patterns may require BMA exchange control declaration.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), operating under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969 and the Exchange Control Act 1972 as the delegated Exchange Control Authority for the British Overseas Territory of Bermuda.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Bermuda does not license standalone non-bank electronic money institutions (EMIs), and offshore digital EMIs are not recognised by the BMA as authorised dealers for local clearing or settlement. Such entities may facilitate non-BMD payment corridors if they are properly licensed in their home jurisdiction and pass BMA-equivalent AML/CFT scrutiny, but they cannot provide the domestic authorised-dealer relationship required for Bermudian exchange control, statutory filings, or BMD liquidity. The treasury blueprint should therefore rely on licensed Bermuda banks as primary rails and treat EMIs only as auxiliary collection channels outside Bermuda.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through reputable correspondent banks to the account of the Bermuda exempted company held at a BMA-authorised dealer. The payment narrative should state 'share capital subscription' or 'capital contribution' and reference the company name, registration number, and, where required, the purpose code cited by the remitting jurisdiction. The compliance verification pack must include board resolution approving the allotment, subscriber certificate, updated register of members, source-of-funds evidence, a legal opinion confirming the exempted company's non-resident exchange-control status, and confirmation of the applicable BMA exemption or notification. Funds may be credited to a capital escrow account before drawdown to operational accounts only after the bank's onboarding and AML refresh procedures are satisfied.Outbound trade settlements for heparin sodium anticoagulant raw materials should be executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 transfers for standard open-account payments and confirmed irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) for large supplier commitments, especially where the supplier requires documentary security. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low. Bermuda does not license standalone non-bank electronic money institutions (EMIs), and offshore digital EMIs are not recognised by the BMA as authorised dealers for local clearing or settlement. Such entities may facilitate non-BMD payment corridors if they are properly licensed in their home jurisdiction and pass BMA-equivalent AML/CFT scrutiny, but they cannot provide the domestic authorised-dealer relationship required for Bermudian exchange control, statutory filings, or BMD liquidity. The treasury blueprint should therefore rely on licensed Bermuda banks as primary rails and treat EMIs only as auxiliary collection channels outside Bermuda.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018 applies to Bermuda relevant entities carrying on relevant activities. Heparin fractionation is likely outside the listed relevant activities unless the entity also operates as an IP holding, headquarters, or distribution and service centre. If a listed activity is present, the entity must pass the economic substance test by demonstrating effective management, sufficient employees, physical premises, expenditure and core income-generating activities in Bermuda. A tax-exempt shell with no substance will be treated as non-compliant.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Offshore Avoidance

Statutory sanction: In the UK, HMRC may impose follower notices, penalties equal to up to 100% of the understated tax, and criminal fines up to GBP 10,000 or imprisonment. In Bermuda, false statements to the Registrar attract fines up to BMD 250,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years; the company may be struck off.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or permit tax evasion or false declarations are jointly and severally liable for restitution and penalties, subject to director disqualification, travel bans, asset seizure and custodial sentences.
Economic Substance Non-Compliance and Shell Company Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may issue a non-compliance determination and impose penalties of up to BMD 500,000 plus continuing daily penalties, and may order winding-up or strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may become personally liable for the company's non-compliance costs; the Bermuda court can issue compliance orders, personal fines, travel restrictions and disqualification from managing Bermuda companies.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Filing Failure

Statutory sanction: Companies Act and AML/ATF regime sanctions include fines up to BMD 500,000, confiscation orders and imprisonment for up to 5 years for natural persons; suspension of the entity and freezing of bank accounts may follow.

Director personal liability: Nominated directors and officers with actual knowledge face personal criminal liability, civil restitution, travel bans and prohibition from operating financial accounts in Bermuda.
False or Misleading Tax and ESR Declaration

Statutory sanction: On conviction, a fine of up to BMD 250,000 or imprisonment for up to 5 years, or both; the company may lose its exempted status and tax protection certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors who ratify or sign a false declaration are personally liable for penalties, equitable compensation, restitution and costs; the Court may impose disqualification and travel ban orders.
