Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Office of the Tax Commissioner (Corporate Income Tax Division) and the Bermuda Registrar of Companies; official portal: gov.bm (tax administration: tax.gov.bm)

E-government filing portal

The Bermuda Exempted Company is incorporated under the Companies Act 1981 through the Registrar of Companies and obtains a Bermuda Registration Number (BRN). No TIN is automatically issued for tax-exempt operations. Under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CITA), an in-scope multinational group (global annual revenue exceeding USD 750 million) must register for Bermuda CIT with the Tax Commissioner within 90 days after the end of its first reporting period and will receive a Bermuda Tax Reference Number (BTRN). Out-of-scope exempted companies file a one-time status notification and maintain only the BRN for customs and statutory compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act (EUTPA) election: upon payment of the annual government fee, the Registrar issues a Tax Protection Certificate immunizing the company from future Bermuda income, capital gains, and withholding taxes until 31 March 2035. Under CITA, in-scope groups may elect a qualifying parent entity for group filing, elect to apply the qualified refundable tax credit mechanism, and must align entity treatment with OECD GloBE hybrid mismatch rules; the US check-the-box regime is not recognised statutorily in Bermuda.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No broad-based VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax applies in Bermuda; the sole indirect levy is customs duty under the Customs Tariff Act 1973, supplemented by excise duties on fuel, alcohol, and tobacco.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST registration threshold; customs duty is assessed per import transaction, and importers must hold a valid Bermuda Customs consignee code in ASYCUDA World.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Bermuda levies no VAT/GST; exported goods are outside the scope of Bermuda customs duty and no VAT zero-rating claim or refund mechanism exists. For in-scope CIT groups, export revenue is included in tangible asset-related income and assessed under Bermuda's economic substance rules, subject to the 15% CIT only where the group exceeds the global revenue threshold.

  • Bermuda Customs Export Entry (ASYCUDA World submission)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)

Bermuda Customs requires electronic filing of all import and export entries through the ASYCUDA World (UNCTAD) platform. No continuous transaction controls, fiscal e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping regime applies to invoices.

No VAT/export zero-rating clawback exists as no such regime operates in Bermuda. Customs duty fraud triggers penalties under the Customs Act 1973, including forfeiture or seizure of goods and monetary penalties up to three times the goods' value, plus potential criminal prosecution. For in-scope CITA entities, inaccurate CIT returns attract a penalty of up to 25% of the additional tax and statutory interest; fraudulent or wilfully negligent filing attracts a penalty up to 100% of the unpaid tax and referral to the Revenue Appeals Tribunal. The EUTPA certificate does not shield against customs duties or social insurance contributions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda runs a liberalised exchange control regime. The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the United States Dollar. Current account transactions, including profit remittances, dividends and management fees, are freely permitted for exempted companies; capital account inflows and outflows are substantially unrestricted for non-resident-owned entities. Exempted companies qualify as 'achieving the equivalent of non-resident status' under BMA exchange control guidelines, meaning no prior BMA approval is required for routine trade payments, service disbursements or dividend distributions. Approval is only triggered for the issue or transfer of capital securities, the establishment of trusts, or transactions exceeding threshold reporting amounts, which must be submitted to the BMA via an authorised bank. Profit repatriation to the UK parent is unencumbered, subject to standard tax residency and transfer pricing documentation; no withholding taxes apply since Bermuda levies no income or dividend withholding tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation applies to Bermuda exempted companies. Export receipts may be retained in any freely convertible currency (USD, EUR, GBP, etc.) without mandatory conversion into BMD. However, all export proceeds exceeding BMD 100,000 (or equivalent) per transaction or per connected series must be reported to the Bermuda Monetary Authority through the resident authorised bank on an electronic Foreign Exchange Transaction Reporting Form. No conversion ratio is prescribed. The company may direct incoming remittances to a USD-denominated account, and the exchange control status of the entity should be declared on the bank's annual compliance certificate.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) acting under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Bermuda Companies Act 1981

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. Bermuda exempted companies may use offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud for auxiliary payment flows, e.g., collecting platform payouts or disbursing to third-party suppliers. However, the BMA expects a licensed Bermuda bank to remain the principal treasury anchor. Foreign EMIs cannot legally offer deposit-taking or clearing services within Bermuda without a Bermudian electronic business licence or money service business licence; using such services solely as a pass-through payment processor is permissible if the EMI is regulated in the EEA, UK, or Singapore and does not establish a physical Bermuda presence. In practice, Bermuda banks may question accounts where large turnover moves through an unlicensed foreign EMI, so the entity should maintain only a thin 'working capital float' there and run same-day sweeps into the HSBC or Butterfield operational account. Routine regulatory reporting of cross-border transfers above BMD 100,000 remains the responsibility of the authorised bank, thus creating a compliance preference for routing material trade flows through the tier-1 local banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the UK parent must be executed as a SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) from a sanctioned bank to a licensed Bermuda bank. The remittance must clearly state the beneficiary account name, quote the beneficiary's SEPA/IBAN or Bermuda account number, and reflect the purpose code 'EQUI' (Equity Injection) with narrative: 'Share subscription in favour of [Bermuda Exempted Company], reference Board Resolution dated [date]'. A bank-approved escrow account or segregated share premium account should be designated to receive the funds. The Bermuda bank will verify the company's registration certificate, the corporate register of members, the allotment resolution of the board of directors (or shareholders), and the signing authorities. If the amount exceeds USD 1,000,000, an enhanced due-diligence questionnaire focusing on the source of funds of the UK parent must accompany the transfer. BMA reporting will be handled by the receiving bank, but the company must file a copy of the MT103 advice and the share allotment certificate in its statutory records. Under the Bermuda Economic Substance framework, a capital injection classified as finance and leasing may require additional evidence that the company is managed and directed from Bermuda.Trade disbursements for silicone ingots, moulds and packaging are executed via SWIFT MT103 for direct payments (ocean freight, platform fees, and small value purchases). For larger supplier agreements exceeding USD 250,000, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (LC) issued by Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda is recommended; the LC should be confirmed by an international bank in the seller's country. The LC must comply with UCP 600, and the underlying invoice should reference HS Code 3910 (silicones in primary forms) or ISIC 3240. Payment aggregators (Stripe, PayPal, Amazon payouts) are used for the e-commerce side; these external settlement rails are acceptable but the company must effect a regular 'sweep' into the primary operating account to maintain an independent audit trail. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% under the ordinary Bermuda income/profit tax regime; 15% only for a constituent entity of an in-scope multinational group under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025, where the group's consolidated revenue is EUR 750 million or more in at least two of the previous four fiscal years.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 0 (no domestic CIT-based transfer pricing documentation threshold exists for ordinary exempted companies); a parent group becomes subject to OECD-aligned CbC/Master File/Local File discipline when it is an in-scope MNE group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750m, approximately BMD 1.2 billion at current exchange, in at least two of the preceding four fiscal years. Material intercompany transactions must nonetheless be priced on an arm's-length basis in the CIT/QDMTT computation.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018, as amended, applies where the exempted company carries on a relevant activity in Bermuda. Manufacturing compression-molded silicone fidgets alone is not a listed relevant activity; however, if the company also runs a distribution and service centre, financing or leasing, IP-holding, headquarters, or pure-equity-holding function in Bermuda, it must maintain adequate employees, premises, expenditure, board management, and core income-generating activities in Bermuda. A pure shell company with no CIGA activity may remain validly registered but will be a high-risk red flag for foreign tax residence, CFC attribution, and AML/beneficial-ownership scrutiny.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic substance evasion / shell company abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Act 2018: initial financial penalty, continuing daily penalties for non-compliance, possible public notice of default, and deregistration or strike-off in egregious cases; false statements may be prosecuted as criminal offences. In a CIT/QDMTT context, the Tax Commissioner may make an adjusted assessment, deny substance-based carve-outs, and charge interest.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the Bermuda resident representative may be personally prosecuted if the offence was committed with their consent, connivance, or neglect; the corporate veil may be pierced in liquidation or court enforcement, with personal civil restitution, asset freezing orders, travel bans, disqualification from managing companies in Bermuda, and imprisonment for fraud or false declarations.
Tax evasion and false representation

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Bermuda criminal and tax law: fines at the discretion of the court, confiscation of the proceeds of crime, additional tax up to 100% of tax evaded, interest, deregistration, and exclusion from doing business in Bermuda; if the in-scope CIT return is false, penalties may be combined with late-filing penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false returns, orchestrated sham CIGA activities, or caused the company to evade foreign tax have personal liability in criminal proceedings, civil restitution to the Crown or creditors, and can be disqualified, restrained from leaving Bermuda, and sentenced to imprisonment.
AML / beneficial ownership breach

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Bermuda Companies Act, the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, and the AML/ATF regime: civil fines, daily default penalties, adverse public filing notices, and regulatory suspension; where funds are diverted as disguised returns, the company may lose its exempted status.

Director personal liability: Officers can be held personally liable for fines for allowing the company to be used as a vehicle for money laundering or tax evasion; courts may issue compensation orders, worldwide-asset freezes, travel bans, and imprisonment for directors who knowingly enable concealment.
