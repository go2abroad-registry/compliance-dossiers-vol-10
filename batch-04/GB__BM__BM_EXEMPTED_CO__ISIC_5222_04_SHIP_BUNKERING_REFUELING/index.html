Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC), Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda - statutory authority for Corporate Income Tax and Payroll Tax, administered through the Government of Bermuda e-Tax portal; Bermuda Customs Service (BCS), Ministry of Finance - competent authority for customs duty, bonded fuel controls and ships' stores clearance, operated through the ASYCUDA World single-window electronic platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Bermuda Exempted Company under the Companies Act 1981 with the Registrar of Companies and obtain the company registration number. Step 2: Determine CIT status under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 (CITA); if the entity is a constituent entity of an in-scope multinational group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million, register with the OTC within 30 days of the first day of the first applicable fiscal year and obtain a Bermuda corporate tax identification file. Step 3: Open a payroll tax account with OTC from the first date of Bermudian employment. Step 4: Open a customs account with BCS for the import, bonded storage and export/bunker supply of marine fuels, obtaining an ASYCUDA World importer/exporter code and a continuous bond. Step 5: Foreign-source and non-Bermuda profits of the Exempted Company are not subject to Bermuda income tax and require no general tax registration when the CITA threshold is not met.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Bermuda Exempted Companies enjoy statutory exemption from Bermuda income, corporation, withholding, capital gains and wealth taxes on foreign-source income and operations; however, from fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025, CITA applies a 15% GloBE-aligned corporate income tax exclusively to constituent entities of qualifying multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million, thereby displacing the tax-exempt zone default in that narrow multinational context.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or entity classification election exists under Bermuda law; an Exempted Company is always opaque for both Bermuda and United Kingdom tax purposes. Under CITA, in-scope groups may exercise specific GloBE-model elections, including the consolidated filing and data-location election, transitional Qualified Refundable Tax Credit elections, and deferred-tax recapture elections. No Bermuda hybrid-mismatch election is available; UK hybrid mismatch rules (CTA 2010 Part 6A) operate only at the UK group level to neutralise mismatches arising from differing tax treatment of the Bermudian entity or its instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Nil VAT/GST/Sales tax. Bermuda imposes customs import duty, excise-equivalent charges on specified goods, payroll tax and stamp duty; the regime relevant to ship bunkering is the Bermuda customs duty framework with relief for ships' stores supplied to international voyages, not a VAT or sales-and-use tax system.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST because no such tax is imposed. Customs duty registration is triggered at the first commercial import or bunker supply event without any de minimis turnover threshold: the supplier must register with Bermuda Customs Service in Bermudian dollars (BMD), obtain an ASYCUDA World importer/exporter code, and post a continuous customs bond for duty-suspended fuel. Any employer of Bermuda-resident staff must separately register for payroll tax with the Office of the Tax Commissioner from the first payroll period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Although Bermuda has no VAT zero-rating, bunker fuel supplied to ocean-going vessels may be relieved from Bermuda customs duty as ships' stores under the Customs Act 1959 and the Bermuda Tariff Schedule. Eligibility criteria: (i) the receiving vessel is engaged in an international voyage and is granted outward clearance by Bermuda Customs Service; (ii) the quantity supplied does not exceed the vessel's reasonable voyage requirement having regard to route, bunker capacity and consumption profile; (iii) the supplier holds a current BCS ships' stores approval or operates under a valid bonded status; and (iv) delivery occurs within the customs port limits under the prescribed declaration. Bunkers delivered to coastal-only vessels, fishing boats without international clearance, or vessels remaining in Bermudian territorial waters are ineligible and attract duty at the prevailing rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration - electronic ASYCUDA World export/ship-stores entry
  • Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) - ISO 8217-compliant, identifying product grade, quantity, IMO number and delivery location
  • Bill of Lading or Certificate of Clearance Outwards issued by Bermuda Customs Service/port authority
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 banking record evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds
  • Customs ships' stores application or standing approval referencing each named vessel
  • Vessel's stores book or independent bunker survey report evidencing quantity and tank soundings
  • Import/bond entry demonstrating duty-suspended procurement of the fuel prior to delivery

No mandatory real-time national e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping regime is in force in Bermuda. All customs movements, including outbound bunker fuel, must be declared electronically through the ASYCUDA World Bermuda Single Window and cleared before vessel departure. The Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) must be electronically generated or retrievable and must record the supplying entity, receiving vessel IMO number, port of departure, product grade, quantity and transaction value; a signed BDN plus supporting survey documents must be retained for customs audit. Government API-based fiscal clearance and digital code stamping are not applicable.

Bermuda Customs Service may conduct retrospective audits of duty-free ships' stores and bunker exports within six years of the delivery date. Misdeclaration, over-delivery, covert diversion into the domestic market, or forged supporting documents attract seizure and forfeiture of the fuel or conveying craft, monetary penalties of up to the greater of BMD 100,000 and three times the duty saved, interest, and criminal prosecution of responsible directors and officers. Corrupt or fraudulent practices also expose the supplier to cancellation of bonded status and ships' stores licence revocation, retrospective clawback of all relieved duty, and, for in-scope multinational groups, CITA non-compliance penalties by the Office of the Tax Commissioner. Systematic abuse may further lead to revocation of the Exempted Company certificate by the Registrar of Companies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda maintains a fixed exchange-rate regime with the Bermudian dollar (BMD) at 1:1 to the United States dollar. The current account is fully convertible for Bermuda exempted companies designated as non-resident for exchange control purposes. This designation permits unrestricted repatriation of dividends, capital, interest, and trade proceeds without any assessed tax. Resident-designated entities remain subject to exchange control approval for capital account transactions; however, a Bermuda Exempted Company organised outside the economic substance perimeter is routinely treated as non-resident provided no undertaking in Bermuda is owned or controlled and the company is not owned by persons resident in Bermuda. Profit remittance hurdles are therefore negligible for a properly designated exempted company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a Bermuda Exempted Company with valid non-resident designation, there are no mandatory surrender requirements for export receipts; the statutory conversion ratio and any surrender timeline are inapplicable. If the company were to lose non-resident designation and be treated as resident, the Exchange Control Act 1972 would require export proceeds to be offered for conversion to authorised banks within a prescribed period (usually on receipt) and any conversion would occur at the prevailing market rate as determined by the BMA-authorised dealer.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as the integrated central bank and supervisory authority; foreign exchange control is additionally administered under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a replacement for a licensed Bermuda bank and Medium only as a supplementary non-BMD collection vehicle. Foreign fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 or the Digital Assets Business Act 2018 for mainstream fiat clearing in Bermuda. An offshore EMI can provide euro or sterling receipt channels, but it cannot settle US dollars or Bermudian dollars through the local clearing house, cannot issue a commercial letter of credit, and will not satisfy BMA-oriented trade counterparty expectations for marine fuel bunkering. A Bermuda Exempted Company should therefore maintain a primary relationship with a BMA-licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company, inbound capital flows are effected by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the overseas parent or shareholder. The payment message should carry the explicit purpose code 'Capital contribution to Bermuda exempted company' together with the exempted company number. No BMA exchange control approval is required for the injection because the entity is designated non-resident. Verification by the receiving BMA-licensed bank comprises the source-of-funds statement, a board resolution authorising the share issuance, the bank reference from the remitting institution, and confirmation that no cash or bearer instrument is involved. Any escrow release must be supported by the underlying bunkering contract and a disbursement undertaking.The primary clearing rail for bunker fuel and lubricant settlements is SWIFT MT103 (high-value USD payment) executed through Chase, BOFA, Citi, or HSBC US clearing nodes. For high-value cargo purchases or refuelling contracts requiring guarantee-backed settlement, a documentary commercial letter of credit is issued as an SWIFT MT700, with receipt of documents through nominated banks and payment against presentation of the commercial invoice, bill of lading, and certificate of quality/quantity. A standby LC (SWIFT MT760) is used to guarantee demurrage or unpaid invoices. All USD payments ride the same-day Fedwire-linked correspondent network; BMD payments are settled at par with USD within Bermuda. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No domestic Bermuda transfer pricing threshold exists. For exempted companies within multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue exceeding EUR 750 million, documentation aligned with OECD Pillar Two and country-by-country reporting may be substantively relevant for filing in foreign jurisdictions; Bermuda itself does not require local file/master file registration in BMD.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018 requires any exempted company carrying on a relevant activity — expressly including shipping, distribution, and service activities in the marine fuel bunkering sector — to demonstrate substantial economic presence in Bermuda. The company must be directed and managed from Bermuda, incur meaningful local expenditure, maintain a physical office/port facility, and employ adequate full-time local staff. A company that is tax resident outside Bermuda may satisfy ESR by producing a foreign tax residence certification, but pure shell or letter-box operations in Bermuda are prohibited. Non-compliance results in monetary penalties, director disqualification, and possible deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic Substance / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Act 2018 penalties: USD 50,000 initial fine, USD 100,000 continuing fine, maximum USD 250,000; additional daily penalties for non-filing; company may be struck off the register and ordered to pay costs

Director personal liability: Directors and officers can be personally fined up to USD 10,000 and imprisoned for up to 7 years. Courts may pierce the corporate veil to impose civil restitution, issue travel bans, and disqualify directors from acting in any corporate capacity in Bermuda.
Shell Company / Corporate Abuse

Statutory sanction: Companies Act 1981 sanctions: forfeiture of corporate rights, automatic strike-off, restoration fee of USD 1,000, daily late filing penalties up to USD 500, and potential dissolution with asset forfeiture to the Crown

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit trading while the company is in forfeited status become personally liable for the company's debts and contractual liabilities. Directors may be disqualified for up to 5 years, subject to civil restitution orders, and personal assets may be attached through piercing of the corporate veil.
AML / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Proceeds of Crime Amendment Act 2023 and Financial Sanctions Act 2020: corporate fines up to USD 5 million per violation, confiscation of profits, suspension or revocation of bunkering licence, and freezing of assets; individuals face fines up to USD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners face individual criminal liability, asset forfeiture, and imprisonment. Courts may issue travel bans to ensure presence for prosecution. Additionally, directors may be held personally liable for civil restitution to affected counterparties, and the corporate veil is lifted where the company is found to be purely a facade for sanctions-dodging operations.
