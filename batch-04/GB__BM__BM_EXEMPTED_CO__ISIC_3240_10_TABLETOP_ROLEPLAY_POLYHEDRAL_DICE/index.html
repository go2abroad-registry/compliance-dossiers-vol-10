Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda corporate income tax, capital gains, withholding tax, and indirect tax; subject to the 15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax (CIT) only if the group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750 million (Corporate Income Tax Act 2023); the present low-revenue tabletop gaming dice-casting business falls outside the CIT charge.

Available election forms

No check-the-box mechanism exists in Bermuda. Available elections: (a) Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, granting a statutory tax-exemption guarantee until 31 March 2035; (b) in-scope MNE groups may elect CIT exclusion or equivalence treatment under the CIT Act 2023 transitional rules; (c) voluntary or mandatory UK VAT registration (NETP) to recover input VAT and zero-rate qualifying exports; (d) UK hybrid mismatch rules (CTA 2010 Part 6A) are inapplicable because the exempted company is a non-UK tax resident entity outside UK corporation tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Bermuda: no VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax; imports bear customs duty under the Customs Tariff Act 1947; exports are duty-free. UK VAT (20% headline rate) applies on an extra-territorial nexus basis where the Bermuda Exempted Company makes taxable supplies in the UK.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Bermuda: nil — no VAT registration threshold exists because no VAT regime is in force; Customs registration is turnover-independent. UK (ancillary): nil threshold for a non-established taxable person — HMRC registration is due from the first taxable supply in the UK; the £90,000 annual threshold applies only to UK-established suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda: export sales are outside any Bermudian tax charge, rendering zero-rating unnecessary. For UK VAT obligations (NETP or UK-established status): goods physically exported from the UK to a non-UK destination within three months of the time of supply qualify for zero-rating under VATA 1994, Sch 8, Grp 13, subject to strict evidential proof (export declaration, transport documents, destination evidence); ancillary services performed directly in connection with exported goods may also be zero-rated.

  • Bermuda Customs Export Entry (ASYCUDA World electronic declaration)
  • UK Customs Export Declaration (CDS / SAD C21) where the goods depart from the UK
  • Commercial invoice (Bermuda/UK issuing entity)
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Master/House Air Waybill evidencing dispatch to a non-UK destination
  • Proof of export (customs stamped copy, carrier certificate, port/airport departure seal)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT credit advice evidencing FX repatriation
  • Underlying sales contracts, Kickstarter campaign records, Stripe/PayPal transaction logs

Bermuda: no e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate; all customs transactions (import/export declarations) are lodged electronically through ASYCUDA World. UK: Making Tax Digital for VAT mandates API-based digital record-keeping and VAT return filing; HMRC has announced mandatory e-invoicing for VAT-registered businesses on a phased basis from 2027 onwards; no real-time fiscal clearance stamping is presently in force.

Bermuda: false customs/export declarations are penalised under the Customs Act 1948 (monetary penalties, forfeiture, imprisonment) and can lead to revocation of Exempted Undertaking tax protection under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966. UK: misclaimed export zero-rating is clawed back by HMRC assessment for 4 years (ordinary care), 6 years (careless), or 20 years (deliberate evasion), with up to 100% civil penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007, default interest, and criminal liability for fraudulent evasion of VAT under VATA 1994 s.72 (up to 7 years' imprisonment); officers of the Exempted Company may be personally liable for consenting or conniving in the offence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda maintains an open monetary regime with no systemic exchange controls. The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at parity to the US dollar (USD). Both current account and capital account transactions are freely convertible. Profit remittances, dividends, and distributions may be repatriated to non-resident shareholders without exchange control authorization or Bermuda withholding tax, subject only to standard AML/CFT vetting and bank formality.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or repatriation requirement applies in Bermuda. Export receipts from the sale of handmade sharp-edge epoxy resin liquid core RPG dice may be held in USD, BMD, or any freely convertible currency at commercial bank discretion. The BMA imposes no mandatory conversion ratio, no confirmation timeline, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) — the integrated regulatory, supervisory, and central monetary authority for Bermuda

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High as an ancillary collection and supplier payment rail, but Low as the primary treasury relationship. A non-Bermuda electronic money institution (EMI) may not lawfully accept deposits or provide regulated payment services to Bermuda entities without appropriate licensing or an approved waiver. A properly licensed EMI in the United Kingdom or European Economic Area can be used for low-value digital marketplace collections, while capital injection and trade settlements must remain on BMA-supervised domestic banking rails to preserve audit trail integrity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable - Bermuda has no standalone transfer pricing documentation regime under the Payroll Tax Act or Companies Act; in-scope MNE groups subject to the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 must apply the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines to determine arm's length pricing for transactions between constituent entities and must maintain contemporaneous documentation only where required by the ultimate parent jurisdiction (e.g., UK Master File and Local File under UK TTPA 2010 if the UK parent exceeds the GBP 50 million turnover or GBP 5 million related-party transaction thresholds)

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2018 (as amended) apply to Bermuda exempted companies conducting designated relevant activities such as banking, insurance, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, shipping, distribution and service centres, or IP business; tabletop dice casting is not a designated relevant activity, so no sectoral substance test applies; however, every exempted company, including a pure equity holding entity, must annually file the Economic Substance Declaration and maintain a reasonable degree of local presence (registered office, local corporate records, and compliance arrangements); an entity that merely holds IP or routing sales from a UK parent without local substance will be classified as an IP business or distribution and service centre respectively and will be required to pass the full economic substance test or face penalties and potential strike-off

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Payroll Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction: fine not exceeding BMD 100,000 plus an additional penalty of up to three times the tax evaded, and imprisonment for a term not exceeding 5 years; the Tax Commissioner may also issue assessments for the full unpaid payroll tax with 10% late charge and 2% monthly compounding interest

Director personal liability: Bermuda law allows the Tax Commissioner to collect as a civil debt against directors who knowingly signed or directed false returns; individual directors face personal restitution orders, travel bans pending tax prosecution, disqualification from acting as directors for up to 10 years, and imprisonment for fraud
Shell Company - Failure to Maintain Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of BMD 10,000 for late ESD filing plus BMD 5,000 per day of continued default; the Registrar may strike off the company and the Tax Commissioner may impose additional penalties of up to BMD 250,000 for false declarations; the company loses the benefit of the tax protection certificate and becomes liable to any future Bermuda tax legislation

Director personal liability: Directors who signed a false ESD are personally liable for the penalty amounts; the court may order civil restitution of any tax benefit obtained, issue travel bans where the director is about to leave Bermuda, impose director disqualification orders, and in severe cases impose imprisonment for perjury in statutory declarations
Money Laundering / AML Regulatory Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Act 2018 and the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, the company is liable to fines of up to BMD 250,000 per contravention; senior staff face fines of BMD 100,000; in a criminal money laundering prosecution, confiscation orders may seize all assets connected to the offence and imprisonment may extend to 10 years

Director personal liability: Directors and AML compliance officers owe a personal statutory duty under the AML/ATF regime; breach can result in personal criminal conviction, civil recovery and restitution of laundered amounts, freezing of personal assets, court-imposed travel bans, and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced where the director knowingly profited from the offence
UK Tax Avoidance - CFC and Offshore Diversion

Statutory sanction: UK corporation tax is charged at the 19%-25% rate on the diverted profits of the CFC; HMRC may impose transfer pricing adjustments with penalties up to 100% of underpaid tax, interest on overdue tax, and court costs; advance payment notices can require payment of the disputed tax before appeal proceedings conclude

Director personal liability: UK-resident directors who are involved in the offshore arrangement can be liable for penalties for facilitating the tax evasion under the Criminal Finances Act 2017, including unlimited fines and up to 10 years imprisonment; in parallel, HMRC may apply for director disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986 and travel restrictions, while Bermuda courts may separately enforce personal civil liability where the director caused the loss to the company
