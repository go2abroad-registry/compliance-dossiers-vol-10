Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Bermuda Government; operational portal at https://www.taxandcustoms.gov.bm (e-Tax and e-Customs filing system). Company formation and corporate registry functions are administered separately by the Registrar of Companies (Bermuda Registry of Companies, Ministry of Finance) via the Registry portal. For the majority of exempted companies, the Registry rather than the Tax Commissioner issues the primary corporate identifier.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a Bermuda Exempted Company under the Companies Act 1981 through an approved appointed agent; the Registry of Companies issues a Certificate of Incorporation containing the unique company registration number (this number functions as the de facto corporate/payroll/customs identifier). Step 2: Obtain a Certificate of Exemption from local tax liability by filing an application under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (EUTPA), which secures a statutory assurance against any newly imposed Bermudian tax on income, profits, capital gains, assets or appreciation for a period ending no earlier than 31 March 2035. Step 3: No general TIN or VAT ID is issued because Bermuda imposes no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax and no consumption tax on a micro-SaaS business; payroll tax registration with the Tax Commissioner is required only if the company directly employs Bermuda-resident staff. Step 4: If the company is part of a multinational enterprise group with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more in at least two of the preceding four fiscal years, it must register under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CIT Act) for the 15% jurisdictional CIT/QDMTT, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025; a micro-SaaS entity falling below the threshold has no filing obligation. Step 5: No additional import/export licence is required for digital cloud services; a Bermuda Customs registration is only triggered if physical goods are imported.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - A Bermuda Exempted Company is an offshore special-territory entity subject to no entity-level income tax, no capital gains tax, no withholding tax on dividends, interest or royalties, no inheritance/estate tax and no VAT/GST. The company is resident in Bermuda from an incorporation perspective but is treated as wholly tax-transparent for non-resident owners in practical terms; dividends and sale proceeds derived by non-resident shareholders are outside the Bermuda tax net. The only periodic government charges are annual exempted-company government fees and registered-office/filing fees, which are quasi-administrative rather than profit-based taxes. The new 15% corporate income tax under the CIT Act does not apply to this micro-SaaS structure because the consolidated group revenue threshold of EUR 750 million is not met, and the business is outside the scope of Bermuda's payroll tax and social insurance unless a Bermudian workforce is engaged.

Available election forms

Bermuda has no check-the-box entity classification regime and no hybrid-mismatch elections equivalent to US Subchapter S or UK transparency elections. The material statutory election/assurance mechanisms available are: (1) an application under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 for a tax assurance certificate (the primary 'election' engaged at incorporation to lock in tax-exempt status); (2) for in-scope multinational groups under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, elections relating to the Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (QDMTT) safe harbour, transitional country-by-country reporting safe harbour, and controlled-foreign-company income inclusion calculations; (3) an election to change the exempted company's share capital currency or to register as a 'non-resident insurance undertaking' where relevant; and (4) for customs purposes, an election to treat the business as a non-importer where no physical goods cross the Bermudian border. For a micro-SaaS business using Paddle or Lemon Squeezy as merchant of record, the only election practically material is the EUTPA assurance, which is obtained automatically by the appointed agent on formation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Bermuda imposes no VAT, no GST and no general retail sales and use tax. The only border-level indirect levy is customs import duty applied under the Customs Tariff Act 1974, which attaches to the importation of physical goods into Bermuda and is irrelevant to digital SaaS subscription services delivered remotely.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no indirect tax registration threshold in Bermuda because no VAT/GST system exists. A Bermudian customs import licence is required only where physical goods are imported, and the micro-SaaS service is intangible and delivered electronically, so no registration or remittance obligation arises. Where the entity sells to consumers in the UK or EU, the statutory VAT registration and collection obligation is discharged by the merchant-of-record platforms (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) under their own VAT registration numbers, not by the Bermuda supplier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Bermuda - there is no VAT/GST framework and therefore no statutory mechanism for claiming a 0% export rate or an input VAT refund. Export of SaaS/digital services from Bermuda is automatically and permanently outside the scope of any Bermudian indirect tax. The operative rule for the micro-SaaS company is the merchant-of-record model: Paddle and Lemon Squeezy collect and remit applicable VAT/GST in the consumer's jurisdiction, and the Bermuda exempted company invoices the merchant of record at a commercially agreed rate that is not subject to any Bermuda output tax. No specific Bermudian application, documents or approval are required to achieve effective zero-rating.

None. Bermuda has no real-time fiscal clearance regime, no mandatory electronic invoicing standard, no B2B continuous transaction control (CTC) reporting, no digital stamping, and no API-based fiscal data push to any Bermudian revenue authority. The e-Tax and e-Customs portal is used only for employer payroll tax returns, CIT returns (where applicable) and customs declarations for physical goods. The SaaS company remains free to issue commercial invoices through Paddle or Lemon Squeezy as merchant of record, with those platforms bearing responsibility for VAT compliance in destination jurisdictions.

No domestic Bermudian export zero-rating clawback can arise because no VAT/GST refund or zero-rating election exists. Scrutiny instead focuses on: (a) false statements in the EUTPA assurance application or annual exempted-company declarations, which may result in revocation of the tax undertaking, loss of exempted status, penalties under the Companies Act 1981 and potential prosecution for fraud; (b) deliberate misdeclaration to the Bermuda Customs Department in respect of any physical imports, punishable by fines up to BMD 5,000, forfeiture of goods and imprisonment under the Customs Tariff Act 1974; and (c) failure to register under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 where the group genuinely exceeds the EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold, exposing the company to late registration penalties, interest on underpaid top-up tax, and 10-year look-back audit adjustments. The 180-day repatriation benchmark is not a statutory deadline in Bermuda as the territory has no exchange-control legislation; however, where the merchant-of-record settlement is denominated in USD (pegged 1:1 to BMD), settlement delays beyond 180 days may trigger UK CFC or transfer-pricing scrutiny if the Bermuda entity is treated as a non-UK resident with UK-controlling ownership.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar is fixed at parity with the US dollar (BMD 1 = USD 1) and is fully convertible for non-resident designated entities; an Exempted Company is granted non-resident exchange-control status, allowing unrestricted holding and settlement in BMD, USD, GBP, EUR and other major currencies without prior Central Bank case-by-case approval. Current-account remittances (SaaS subscription, license fees, professional fees) are freely processed through Authorised Bank dealers. Capital-account movements — paid-in share capital, shareholder loans, and liquidation proceeds — are also free of exchange-control licensing provided the funds are traceable and entered through a Bermuda Authorised Bank; only transactions involving Bermuda residents or BMD-denominated domestic borrowings remain subject to BMA's resident exchange-control perimeter. Profit remittances take the form of dividends or management fees, require a board resolution and normal AML/beneficial-owner documentation, and are not subject to withholding tax or exchange-control surrender.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory surrender timeline: none — Bermuda law imposes no deadline for repatriating export proceeds. Mandatory conversion ratio: none — export receipts may be retained in original currency in an onshore foreign-currency account or converted at market rates through an Authorised Bank. The only administrative condition is the Exempted Company's annual confirmation of non-resident status with the BMA under Paragraph 1, First Schedule to the Exchange Control Regulations 1953 and the completion of ordinary customer due diligence at the receiving bank. Receipts from overseas SaaS sales are classified as exports of services and do not trigger any invoice-export or surrender certificate.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating for external (UK/EU) EMI accounts: Medium-High for merchant settlement only; Low for Bermuda-resident deposit-taking. Legal constraints: (i) Taking deposits or carrying on banking business in or from Bermuda requires a license under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, so an offshore EMI cannot lawfully hold Bermuda customers' deposits or offer Bermuda IBAN-denominated local banking products. (ii) For collection of worldwide SaaS revenue, an Exempted Company may freely instruct Paddle or Lemon Squeezy to sweep net subscription settlements to an EMI (e.g., UK or EU entity) and then onward-transfer to the Bermuda bank; this is treated as a contract for money remittance, not regulated banking activity in Bermuda. (iii) For core treasury operations — capital injection, dividend payment, FX conversion, payroll — only Bermuda Authorised Banks and federally chartered US/EU corresponding banks should be used, because BMA-exempted non-resident EMIs are not permitted to disburse Bermudian-dollar payments or issue cheque facilities in Bermuda.

Use SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank) from the UK parent or beneficial owner's bank to the Bermuda bank's USD/CHIPS correspondent account when BMD is requested. The instruction must include: (i) beneficiary account name exactly matching the Exempted Company registered name; (ii) company registration/account number; (iii) payment narrative 'Capital contribution – Bermuda Exempted Company' with reference to the Board Resolution; (iv) 'OUR' charges to ensure full test-key receipt; and (v) purpose code ECI (Equity Capital Investment) or a bank-specific non-resident/Article- (if applicable) code. International wire transfers into a non-resident account require no BMA prior licence; the bank will file the ordinary currency return to the BMA and may require KYC-verified source. For chain-of-title, all inward cross-border wires should use a segregated 'Capital Escrow Account' before recharacterization into paid-up share capital following a law firm's closing set.Clearing strategies: (i) SWIFT MT103 USD/BMD direct remittance for recurring supplier invoices, SaaS refunds, SaaS license reseller commissions, and outsourcing payments; (ii) commercial letter of credit (MT700/MT760) reserved for large software professional services or infrastructure procurement — though for a CO consumer SaaS, standby LC is usually unnecessary; (iii) USD clearing via CHIPS/Fedwire through the Bermuda bank's US or UK correspondent network for high-value transfers; (iv) card-payment merchant settling directly from Paddle/Lemon Squeezy to the operational account in a daily monthly sweep. All outbound disbursements should be tied to an auditable invoice, include a Statement of Purpose, and be processed only by authorised signatories on the bank's e-banking mandate; the BMA's exchange-control returns are prepared by the bank and do not require separate approval.BMD/USD outright forward with an Authorised Bermuda Bank (eliminates residual operational risk due to BMD peg)Short-dated FX forward in EUR/USD, GBP/USD for SaaS revenue translation of future subscription flowsCross-currency interest-rate swap (CCIRS) on longer-term shareholder debt, where materialFX option (European put/call) structured with the Authorised Bank for tail-risk protection on GBP/EUR collectionsNon-deliverable forward (NDF) only if a quoting dealer is outside Bermuda and the BMD is explicitly excluded — not required given BMD/USD parity

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating for external (UK/EU) EMI accounts: Medium-High for merchant settlement only; Low for Bermuda-resident deposit-taking. Legal constraints: (i) Taking deposits or carrying on banking business in or from Bermuda requires a license under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, so an offshore EMI cannot lawfully hold Bermuda customers' deposits or offer Bermuda IBAN-denominated local banking products. (ii) For collection of worldwide SaaS revenue, an Exempted Company may freely instruct Paddle or Lemon Squeezy to sweep net subscription settlements to an EMI (e.g., UK or EU entity) and then onward-transfer to the Bermuda bank; this is treated as a contract for money remittance, not regulated banking activity in Bermuda. (iii) For core treasury operations — capital injection, dividend payment, FX conversion, payroll — only Bermuda Authorised Banks and federally chartered US/EU corresponding banks should be used, because BMA-exempted non-resident EMIs are not permitted to disburse Bermudian-dollar payments or issue cheque facilities in Bermuda.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable in Bermuda - no statutory Local File or Master File requirement exists. However, if the Bermuda entity is a member of a UK-headed multinational group with consolidated revenue above GB£750 million, the group may be required to prepare UK CbCR and master/local file documentation under UK TFA 2016 Schedule 19A and OECD-aligned guidance. Bermudian ESR requirements impose an equivalent substance threshold: the entity must demonstrate adequate staffing, premises, and core income-generating activities in Bermuda.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 (and 2019 Amendment) applies. A Bermuda exempted company carrying on a relevant activity, including intellectual property business or pure equity holding, must satisfy the economic substance test: core income-generating activities must be directed and managed in Bermuda, with adequate physical presence, qualified employees, and operating expenditure proportionate to the activity. A consumer micro-SaaS deriving revenue from proprietary software IP is treated as an intellectual property business unless it qualifies as a pure equity holding entity. Non-compliant entities are reported to the United Kingdom under the OECD exchange of information framework and face escalating penalties and strike-off. Annual ESR return filing is mandatory regardless of tax liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to BD$5,000 per information notice; continuing default fine of BD$20,000; exchange of information to HMRC under the OECD framework; automatic strike-off and dissolution under the Companies Act 1981; loss of the Assurance of Exemption.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally fined up to BD$5,000 and imprisoned for up to 1 year for a false declaration. UK directors also face disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986 and HMRC senior accounting officer penalties under FA 2009 Schedule 46.
UK Tax Evasion / Offshore Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: HMRC assessments for UK corporation tax (up to 25%) or income tax (up to 45%), plus interest; penalties under TMA 1970 and FA 2007 ranging from 30% to 100% of potential lost revenue; additional offshore penalties of up to 200% under FA 2016 Schedule 21; criminal prosecution for cheating the public revenue.

Director personal liability: Director disqualification, civil restoration orders, account freezing orders, travel bans, and seizure of assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002; individual liability under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 for facilitating tax evasion, punishable by up to 7 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines.
AML / Financial Crime Red Flags at Payment Processor Level

Statutory sanction: Freezing of payment accounts, chargeback reversals, immediate termination of the merchant agreement, and filing of Suspicious Activity Reports to the UK FCA/NCA; referral by the Bermuda Financial Intelligence Agency to the police; penalties under the Bermuda Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability for money laundering under the UK Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 (up to 14 years' imprisonment) and under Bermuda AML legislation (up to 5 years' imprisonment); personal civil liability to creditors when the corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent or sham trading; director disqualification.
Late or Missing Statutory Filings

Statutory sanction: BD$500 late filing penalty for the Annual Return; ESR penalties of up to BD$5,000 plus continuing default fines of BD$20,000; automatic strike-off after repeated non-compliance; loss of the right to obtain certificates of good standing.

Director personal liability: Directors' names published in the Bermuda Gazette; personal service of court orders; travel bans imposed by Bermudian courts where directors ignore compliance orders; disqualification from acting as a director of any Bermuda company.
