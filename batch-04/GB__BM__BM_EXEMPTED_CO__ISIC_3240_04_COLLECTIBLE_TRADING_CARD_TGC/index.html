E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda has no U.S.-style check-the-box election. Statutory mechanisms applicable to a Bermuda Exempted Company include: (a) application under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 for a certificate shielding certain income-type taxes, subject to transitional provisions in the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 for in-scope MNE groups; (b) election under the Economic Substance (International Tax Compliance) Act 2018 to be treated as a pure equity holding entity where the company holds only passive investments (not relevant to active ISIC 3240 printing/exporting because manufacturing is not a relevant activity); (c) elections under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 for in-scope groups, including QDMTT safe harbour, substance-based income exclusion, and loss carry-back/forward elections; and (d) payroll tax arrangements governing employer withholding for any Bermuda-based staff.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax; Bermuda levies import customs duties under the Customs (Tariff) Act 1966 and has no export duty or general consumption tax on goods or services.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; Bermuda imposes no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold. Any person importing goods commercially must hold a Bermuda Customs importer code regardless of value, and an employer must register for Payroll Tax from the first payroll. Threshold is expressed as N/A in BMD/USD.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable: Bermuda has no VAT/GST system and therefore no 0% export VAT rate, no input VAT credit, and no export refund mechanism. Goods manufactured in Bermuda and exported (including security holographic cold-foil printed collectible trading cards) are not subject to Bermuda customs duty upon export, and the export profits of a Bermuda Exempted Company are not subject to Bermudian income tax. The UK VAT zero-rating regime does not extend to Bermuda because Bermuda is outside the UK VAT territory.

No real-time e-invoicing mandate, no fiscal clearance, API stamping or digital stamp requirement. Bermuda does not operate a continuous transaction controls or SAF-T system. Commercial invoices may be issued in electronic or paper form. Imports and exports are controlled by Bermuda Customs through electronic declarations filed by licensed customs brokers in the customs automated processing system, and shipments may be selected for physical examination and risk-based valuation.

No VAT zero-rating or export-refund regime exists in Bermuda, so no VAT clawback can arise. Bermuda has no exchange control and imposes no statutory deadline for foreign-currency repatriation; the value 0 reflects the absence of any mandatory repatriation rule. For customs purposes, false declarations or duty evasion are investigated by Bermuda Customs and are punishable under the Customs Act 1941 by fines, forfeiture of goods and imprisonment, and the importer/exporter registration may be suspended or revoked. If the exempted company ever becomes part of an MNE group in scope of the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, inaccurate tax returns may attract administrative penalties and interest; outside that scope, a Bermuda Exempted Company has no income-tax clawback exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged to the U.S. dollar at a fixed 1:1 rate. Bermuda maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the Exchange Control Act 1972; there are no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or invested capital. Exempted companies may hold and transact in any foreign currency. Profit remittance is not subject to withholding tax or prior approval, but all foreign exchange transactions must be conducted through authorised dealers and reported to the BMA as required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation of export receipts, and no prescribed percentage of foreign currency earnings must be converted into BMD. Exporters must, however, submit an annual Exchange Control Return to the BMA declaring aggregated foreign currency inflows and outflows.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex) can support cross-border receivables and vendor payments, but they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Bermuda. Legal constraints include: (i) the EMI must be authorised in its home state and may not solicit Bermuda-resident customers without a BMA licence under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999; (ii) the EMI's services must not involve the issuance of Bermuda-dollar electronic money; (iii) all transactions must comply with the BMA's anti-money laundering requirements, including the Travel Rule (client information sharing for wire transfers). Practical constraints: Bermudian counterparties often prefer local bank transfers; however, EMIs are viable for international settlement in USD, GBP, and EUR.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No general Bermuda transfer-pricing threshold applies due to the absence of a domestic corporate income tax for the standard exempted company. For an in-scope constituent entity under the CIT Act 2023, any material related-party transaction must be supported by an OECD-aligned local file and master file; no BMD de minimis threshold is provided in the draft CIT regulations.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Companies) Act 2018 applies where a Bermuda exempted company conducts one or more 'relevant activities': banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centres, holding entity, and IP holding. The physical printing and publishing of collectible trading cards is not a listed relevant activity. However, the company must demonstrate adequate management and control in Bermuda for any significant outsourced or internal functions, and if the company merely receives passive royalty or financing income, reduced substance requirements may still apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False CIT Declaration

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, plus interest at 1% per month; criminal indictment with fines up to BD$250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or cause the company to file a false CIT return may be held criminally liable and subject to personal fines, disgorgement of benefits, and UK-Bermuda extradition-based imprisonment. Travel bans may be imposed pending tax debt recovery.
Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: First offence: fixed penalty of BD$10,000. Continuing default: additional penalty of BD$10,000 per month up to BD$250,000. Three consecutive years of non-compliance triggers automatic strike-off and dissolution of the exempted company.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's economic substance penalties if they knowingly failed to procure sufficient substance. Directorial disqualification orders and personal prohibition from serving in Bermuda companies may be issued.
Shell Company Abuse / AML Control Failure

Statutory sanction: Bermuda AML/ATF Act penalties: fines up to BD$1,000,000 and revocation of the company's exempted status. The entity may be struck off and subject to asset-forfeiture proceedings under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined up to BD$250,000, banned from acting as directors for up to 10 years, and subjected to a court-imposed travel prohibition pending regulatory investigation.
Money Laundering / Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997: imprisonment up to 15 years, unlimited fines, and confiscation of all assets derived from the offending conduct. The Bermuda Monetary Authority may issue a closure notice and freeze the company's bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who aid, abet, or fail to report suspicious activity face the same criminal imprisonment term and fines, plus personal confiscation orders and a lifetime directorship ban. Travel bans are routinely imposed to secure jurisdiction.
Fraudulent Trading / Wrongful Solvency Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Section 200 of the Companies Act 1981, the Supreme Court may declare directors personally responsible for all outstanding debts and liabilities of the company. Civil recovery actions and disqualification orders are automatic.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to make unlimited civil restitution from personal assets, prohibited from leaving Bermuda without court consent, and imprisoned for up to 7 years for fraudulent trading. Piercing the corporate veil is permitted against dominant shareholders who act as shadow directors.
