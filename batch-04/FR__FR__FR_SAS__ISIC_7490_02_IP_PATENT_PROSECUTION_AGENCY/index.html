Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Limited. An SAS must have a registered office (siège social) located in France, which may be the manager's domicile, leased premises, or a licensed domiciliation company (société de domiciliation) for a maximum renewable contract of 5 years under Article L.123-11-2 Code de commerce. Therefore a commercial domiciliation address with a dedicated office floor-room and professional liability policy is permissible in the first year, but a physical law-firm presence must be in place by the first annual professional renewal.

The competent Registry of the Commercial Court (Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce), with filing submitted through the national one-stop business formalities portal operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS; capital may be EUR 1 or more. For cash contributions, at least 50% of subscribed share capital must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Cash contributions must be deposited in a qualifying bank or notarial escrow account before registration, and the deposit certificate must be filed. Contributions in kind require valuation documentation and, unless validly waived, a commissaire aux apports report.

Funding & unlock path: No general industrial clearance is required for a normal office. The premises must satisfy French Labour Code health and safety requirements, electrical and fire-safety obligations, emergency access, evacuation procedures, and occupational-risk documentation. If the premises qualify as an établissement recevant du public, municipal and fire-service requirements apply, including accessibility and fire-safety approval. Structural-load inspection is required only where triggered by the building, lease, works, equipment, or applicable safety rules.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS; capital may be EUR 1 or more. For cash contributions, at least 50% of subscribed share capital must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Cash contributions must be deposited in a qualifying bank or notarial escrow account before registration, and the deposit certificate must be filed. Contributions in kind require valuation documentation and, unless validly waived, a commissaire aux apports report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and, for non-established businesses, the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE); official portal: impots.gouv.fr. Commercial entity formation and allocation of the SIREN and SIRET identifiers are processed through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS must be incorporated through the INPI Guichet unique. The filing generates the SIREN and SIRET identifiers and registration with the Registre national des entreprises. The company then activates its professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr and registers for applicable taxes with the competent SIE. The French VAT identification number is issued by the DGFiP and normally consists of the prefix FR, a two-digit control key, and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity without a French establishment must apply to the SIEE before making taxable French supplies requiring registration, generally using the prescribed registration documentation, corporate certificates, tax identification evidence, activity and representative information, and bank details. An EU-established business may generally register directly; a non-EU business normally requires a French tax representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance exception applies. VAT registration does not replace customs EORI registration, which is required for relevant import and export activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). An eligible SAS may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code, generally for a maximum of five fiscal years, subject to statutory conditions including non-listing, qualifying ownership, employee and turnover or balance-sheet limits, and commencement of activity within the permitted period. The election is not a general check-the-box regime and must be exercised within the statutory deadline. There is no general United States-style entity classification election. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework can deny deductions or require inclusion for arrangements producing deduction/non-inclusion or duplicate deductions; they do not create a separate elective hybrid classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established service provider, French VAT liability generally arises from the first taxable transaction unless the business validly uses the franchise en base de TVA. For services, the principal franchise threshold is EUR 37,500 of annual turnover, with a tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250 under the applicable current rules. These thresholds are exemption thresholds, not a general safe harbor for a business that makes transactions subject to mandatory VAT registration, and special rules apply to foreign businesses, intra-EU transactions, imports, and distance sales. A patent prosecution agency must analyze customer status and place-of-supply rules transaction by transaction. B2B services are generally located where the customer is established and may be invoiced without French VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism; B2C services and services located in France may require French VAT registration and collection.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to a destination outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT, economically equivalent to zero-rating, where the supplier or an authorized intermediary can substantiate that the goods physically left the EU and that the transaction qualifies under the French VAT rules. Evidence normally includes the customs export record and corresponding exit confirmation, linked commercial documentation, transport evidence, and accounting records. Services require a separate place-of-supply analysis. A B2B patent prosecution or trademark registration service supplied to a customer established outside France is generally located at the customer's establishment under the general B2B rule and is invoiced without French VAT, usually with an appropriate place-of-supply and reverse-charge statement where applicable; this is not technically an export zero-rating of a French-located service. Services supplied to French customers remain subject to French VAT unless a specific exemption applies. Official patent-office fees paid as disclosed disbursements require separate analysis and must not be treated automatically as outside the VAT base.

  • Customs export declaration containing the export movement reference number (MRN)
  • Electronic customs exit confirmation or equivalent proof that the goods exited the European Union, including the export accompanying document or validated exit results where applicable
  • Commercial invoice and contract or order documentation identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, and destination
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, transport document, or other independent transport evidence
  • Packing list and delivery documentation where relevant
  • Accounting records linking the export transaction to the customs declaration and payment records
  • Import or destination-country evidence where reasonably available
  • Inward remittance evidence may support the audit file but is not a universal French statutory condition for VAT exemption, and a bank-issued inward remittance certificate is not generally mandatory

France does not currently operate a universal real-time invoice-clearance system for ordinary domestic business-to-business invoices. The statutory electronic invoicing reform requires businesses established in France to be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Issuing obligations generally begin on 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and tax groups and on 1 September 2027 for medium-sized enterprises, small enterprises, and micro-enterprises, subject to legislative or regulatory amendments. Invoices must generally be transmitted through an approved private platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire) or the public platform designated by law, with structured data reporting to the DGFiP. E-reporting of specified business-to-consumer and cross-border transactions is also phased in. The system is not equivalent to mandatory prior fiscal clearance. Invoices to French public-sector customers are subject to the Chorus Pro framework. Electronic invoices must satisfy French VAT invoice-content, authenticity, integrity, readability, archiving, and audit-trail requirements.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline applicable to export proceeds. Export proceeds should nevertheless be recorded accurately, collected through compliant payment channels, and supported by banking and accounting evidence consistent with French anti-money-laundering, customs, and tax requirements. If export VAT exemption is unsupported or falsely claimed, the DGFiP may assess the VAT that should have been charged, statutory late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month, and tax penalties. Penalties may include 10% for late or deficient payment, 40% for deliberate misconduct, 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, and up to 100% in cases involving opposition to a tax audit, subject to statutory conditions and judicial or administrative review. Input VAT refunds may be denied or clawed back, and previously exempt output transactions may be reassessed. Serious intentional VAT fraud may also trigger criminal proceedings, fines, and imprisonment under applicable French law. Customs inaccuracies can result in recovery of duties and taxes, customs penalties, seizure measures, and loss of simplified customs authorizations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and generally has full current-account and capital-account convertibility within the European Union and the Euro Area. A French SAS may receive foreign investment, convert currencies, pay foreign suppliers, distribute dividends, repay shareholder loans, and remit profits subject to corporate-law, accounting, tax, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, beneficial-ownership, and bank-documentation controls. There is no general French foreign-exchange approval for ordinary capital contributions or dividend remittances. Payments involving sanctioned persons, restricted jurisdictions, dual-use goods, or regulated financial activity may be blocked or require authorization. Dividend distributions require distributable profits or available reserves, approval by the competent corporate body, satisfaction of French company-law solvency and accounting requirements, and withholding-tax analysis. A non-resident shareholder may generally receive dividends without a French exchange-control restriction, but treaty relief or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief requires supporting tax-residence and beneficial-ownership documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no statutory minimum conversion ratio, and no universal statutory deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros or repatriated through a French bank. Export receipts may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be received through banks or payment institutions lawfully established in the European Economic Area, subject to contractual, tax, accounting, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering controls. The SAS must retain invoices, contracts, customs records where applicable, proof of service or delivery, bank records, and accounting evidence. Statistical or balance-of-payments reporting may be requested by Banque de France or the relevant financial institution; this reporting is not a surrender obligation. Cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more across the EU external border are subject to customs declaration rules, which do not apply to ordinary bank transfers.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the authority of the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange and external-financial regulatory policy is also administered through the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty, including the Directorate-General of the Treasury.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium for payments and treasury sub-accounts; Low as a substitute for a primary French relationship bank or regulated client-money account. A foreign electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide services in France through EU passporting, a French branch, or an authorized agent, but it must maintain the applicable safeguarding, conduct, reporting, and AML framework. An offshore provider without EU authorization cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to the SAS merely through an online platform. Wise or a comparable provider may be useful for supported currencies and international payments, but account availability, local receiving details, transaction limits, beneficiary verification, and prohibited-use rules are contractual. The company should retain a French or EEA bank account for tax payments, payroll, official patent-office payments, reserve liquidity, and continuity.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident shareholder, director, or ultimate beneficial owner, particularly where the ownership chain includes multiple holding companies or jurisdictions.

Root cause: Non-resident shareholder, director, or ultimate beneficial owner, particularly where the ownership chain includes multiple holding companies or jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Certified passports or national identity documents; proof of residential address; complete ownership chart to each natural-person beneficial owner; corporate registries and constitutional documents for every holding entity; director appointment evidence; signed beneficial-owner declaration; tax-residence certificates where relevant; and a concise commercial rationale for the structure.
Unclear source of capital, shareholder loans, or initial operating funds.

Root cause: Unclear source of capital, shareholder loans, or initial operating funds.

Defense evidence: Executed subscription or shareholder-loan agreement; board or shareholder approval; bank statements showing the remitting party and accumulation of funds; audited accounts or tax returns; sale, dividend, employment, or investment-income evidence; source-of-wealth narrative; and an explicit explanation reconciling the remitter, shareholder, and beneficiary.
Patent prosecution and international trademark activity involving multiple countries, official-fee payments, client funds, or third-party disbursements.

Root cause: Patent prosecution and international trademark activity involving multiple countries, official-fee payments, client funds, or third-party disbursements.

Defense evidence: Detailed service description; engagement letters; client-invoice and payment-flow map; representative client categories and jurisdictions; official-fee schedules; segregation policy for client money, if applicable; confirmation that regulated French industrial-property-attorney activities are performed by appropriately qualified professionals; sanctions-screening procedure; and sample invoices identifying professional fees versus reimbursed official charges.
Expected cross-border flows that are disproportionate to the declared business size or that rapidly transit through the account.

Root cause: Expected cross-border flows that are disproportionate to the declared business size or that rapidly transit through the account.

Defense evidence: Twelve-month cash-flow forecast; transaction-volume and currency matrix; principal customer and supplier list; executed contracts or purchase orders; expected countries and payment purposes; reconciliation controls; approval matrix; and explanation of seasonality, filing deadlines, or large official patent-office payments.
Use of personal accounts, virtual IBANs, offshore payment providers, or unrelated third-party remitters.

Root cause: Use of personal accounts, virtual IBANs, offshore payment providers, or unrelated third-party remitters.

Defense evidence: Written prohibition on personal-account use; designated corporate-account policy; provider licensing evidence; contractual explanation of virtual-IBAN arrangements; third-party payment authorization; invoice and agency documentation; beneficial-owner and sanctions screening; and reconciliation proving that funds relate to the SAS's documented business.
Sanctions, export-control, dual-use, or high-risk-country exposure in international patent or trademark work.

Root cause: Sanctions, export-control, dual-use, or high-risk-country exposure in international patent or trademark work.

Defense evidence: Customer and counterparty screening records; country-risk assessment; goods, technology, and end-use analysis where relevant; applicable EU and French authorization or legal opinion; blocked-party escalation procedure; evidence of rejected transactions; and written confirmation that the bank may suspend transactions pending review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity contributions through the SAS's designated French or EEA bank account, using the subscribing shareholder's account whenever practicable. For incorporation, use the temporary capital-deposit account or notarial deposit route and provide the bank with the draft articles, subscription schedule, shareholder identity evidence, source-of-funds evidence, and incorporation documents. After registration, provide the Kbis or equivalent registration extract, final articles, beneficial-owner filing evidence, and board or shareholder approval to release or reclassify the funds. For later capital increases, use a subscription agreement, corporate approval, updated capital documentation, and an identified transfer reference such as 'capital contribution' or 'share capital increase'. For shareholder loans, provide a signed loan agreement, arm's-length terms, maturity, interest provisions, repayment schedule, and transfer-pricing support where applicable. Use SEPA for euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT cross-border transfers for other currencies or jurisdictions. France does not impose a general foreign-exchange purpose-code regime for ordinary corporate transfers; the bank's payment-purpose field, transfer narrative, and any statistical reporting must accurately identify the transaction. Confirm beneficiary name, IBAN, BIC, currency, intermediary-bank requirements, sanctions status, and fee allocation before release.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA scheme, subject to bank limits and beneficiary validation. Use SWIFT customer-credit transfers, historically documented by MT103 and increasingly processed through ISO 20022 payment messages, for non-SEPA or non-euro payments. For official patent-office payments, pay directly from the corporate account to the official authority or authorized payment channel, retain the filing reference, fee schedule, receipt, and client-invoice reconciliation, and avoid unexplained third-party settlement. For higher-value or documentary-risk transactions, use a commercial documentary letter of credit under UCP 600, with banks confirming authenticity, sanctions clearance, document-compliance requirements, and applicable ISP98 or URDG 758 rules where relevant. Maintain contracts, purchase orders, invoices, proof of service or delivery, customs documents where applicable, beneficiary screening, and payment approvals. A French SAS should not use customer receipts or client funds for general corporate liquidity unless the applicable professional and safeguarding rules expressly permit it and the funds are properly segregated.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with an EU-authorized bank or investment firm, documented by a master agreement and used for identifiable commercial exposures.Non-deliverable forwards where the currency or jurisdiction is not freely deliverable, subject to bank eligibility, derivatives documentation, sanctions controls, and suitability or appropriateness requirements.Currency swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps for documented funding or recurring currency exposures, subject to counterparty credit limits, collateral terms, valuation, and ISDA or equivalent documentation.Foreign-exchange options or option collars for budget protection, subject to appropriate risk disclosures, authority limits, premium funding, and avoidance of speculative or disproportionate exposure.Natural hedging through matching foreign-currency receipts and payments, foreign-currency accounts, and contractual currency clauses; maintain an exposure register and prohibit hedging activity unrelated to documented operating or financing risk.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payments and treasury sub-accounts; Low as a substitute for a primary French relationship bank or regulated client-money account. A foreign electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide services in France through EU passporting, a French branch, or an authorized agent, but it must maintain the applicable safeguarding, conduct, reporting, and AML framework. An offshore provider without EU authorization cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to the SAS merely through an online platform. Wise or a comparable provider may be useful for supported currencies and international payments, but account availability, local receiving details, transaction limits, beneficiary verification, and prohibited-use rules are contractual. The company should retain a French or EEA bank account for tax payments, payroll, official patent-office payments, reserve liquidity, and continuity.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard rate for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2022; reduced rate of 15% applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for companies with annual turnover below EUR 10 million)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% (flat tax, prélèvement forfaitaire unique, on dividends distributed to non-resident individuals and legal entities, unless reduced by treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million (group consolidated turnover) for the obligation to prepare full transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File). For entities below this threshold, a simplified documentation requirement applies if the entity's turnover exceeds EUR 50 million or if the entity holds intra-group transactions exceeding EUR 100,000. The Local File must be filed with the annual tax return (Form 2257).

Economic substance scope: France does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE, but the concept of substance is embedded in anti-abuse rules, including the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the MLI, the anti-hybrid rules (ATAD), and the doctrine of 'abus de droit' (abuse of law). A French SAS must demonstrate real economic activity (employees, premises, decision-making) to benefit from treaty relief and to avoid being challenged as a shell company. The French tax administration may disregard artificial arrangements lacking commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: up to EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment (increased to EUR 3 million and 7 years for aggravated cases). Administrative penalties: 80% of the evaded tax (majoration pour manœuvres frauduleuses) plus interest of 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax debts if they have committed serious misconduct (e.g., fraudulent misrepresentation). Civil restitution of unpaid taxes, travel bans, and potential imprisonment (up to 5 years).
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 40% penalty for lack of substance (abus de droit). The company may be dissolved by court order if found to be a sham.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the company's tax liabilities if they knowingly participated in creating a shell structure. Travel bans and criminal prosecution for tax evasion may apply.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income by the tax authority, plus a 40% penalty for serious mispricing (or 80% if fraudulent). Interest on late payment applies. Failure to file Form 2257 triggers a penalty of EUR 15,000 per missing document.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they signed the tax return knowing the transfer pricing was incorrect. In severe cases, criminal charges for tax evasion may be pursued.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities. Criminal penalties for individuals: up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be held personally liable for AML breaches. Travel bans and prohibition from managing a company may be imposed.
