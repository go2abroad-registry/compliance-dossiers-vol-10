Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda Exempted Company treated as non-resident for Bermudian tax purposes; exempt from income, profits, capital and withholding taxation; eligible for Tax Assurance Certificate; digital B2B marketplace income sourced outside Bermuda excluded from domestic tax base).

Available election forms

Bermuda domestic law does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. For U.S. federal income tax purposes, a Bermuda Exempted Company is a per se corporation and is ineligible for a check-the-box pass-through election. For UK Pillar Two purposes, the group may rely on the Bermuda Tax Assurance Certificate and transitional safe-harbour relief; no UK CTA 2010 s.130 hybrid election applies to Bermuda entities; in the UK, the entity may elect to be a qualifying indirect shareholder for the anti-hybrid rules but no VAT grouping election is available unless the entity registers for UK VAT and obtains an HMRC VAT-grouping direction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No Bermuda VAT/GST/sales and use tax. Indirect tax exposure arises only in destination jurisdictions: UK VAT (and Northern Ireland protocol rules) for cross-border B2B digital marketplace services; potential local sales tax in the jurisdiction of the customer.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Bermuda (no domestic indirect tax). If UK VAT nexus is established: compulsory UK VAT registration when actual or expected taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in any 12-month period (from 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2026, standard threshold); for a non-established taxable person (non-UK entity) registration is mandatory for any taxable supply of digital services to UK non-business customers (no threshold) and for distance sales of goods into Great Britain / Northern Ireland above the GB/NI distance selling thresholds (currently £8,818 for supplies of goods subject to excise duty and £85,000 for distance sales of all other goods into Northern Ireland).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable for Bermuda domestic indirect taxation as no VAT/GST exists in Bermuda. For UK VAT purposes, exports of physical goods from Great Britain or Northern Ireland to a destination outside the UK may be zero-rated under VATA 1994 Sch. 8 Group 12 provided the supplier obtains and preserves valid evidence of export within statutory time limits (normally goods leaving the UK within three months of supply and within 12 months thereafter). Supplies of digital B2B platform services to a non-UK business customer are outside the scope of UK VAT (reverse charge applies to the customer) and do not require zero-rating substantiation; the supplier should retain the customer's VAT registration number or business evidence to prove B2B status.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is pegged to the US dollar at 1:1 under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act. Exchange control applies to residents, but a Bermuda Exempted Company may obtain formal 'non-resident' designation from the BMA, after which it may hold and freely operate foreign-currency bank accounts (USD, EUR, GBP, CNH) without conversion restrictions. Current-account transactions, including B2B trade settlement and supplier payments, are fully convertible and unrestricted for designated non-resident entities. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted with no withholding tax because Bermuda levies no income tax; however, a BMA exchange-control 'Form A' permit or notification is required for any transaction booked in BMD with Bermuda residents. Capital account transactions require prior BMA approval only where the counterparty or account is resident or BMD-denominated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1972, resident exporters must surrender convertible foreign currency proceeds to an authorised dealer in exchange for BMD within a statutory period of six months from shipment or receipt, as directed by the BMA. A Bermuda Exempted Company designated non-resident for exchange-control purposes is exempt from mandatory surrender of export proceeds for all receipts booked through designated non-resident foreign-currency accounts. To preserve this exemption, the B2B marketplace must route all cross-border receipts exclusively through foreign-currency settlement accounts at BMA-licensed banks and must not credit any Bermuda-resident BMD account. The company must file a BMA Exchange Control Application for non-resident designation and obtain written confirmation from the authorised dealer to evidence the surrender exemption.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, administering the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium. The Payment Services Act 2019 and the BMA Fintech Regulatory Sandbox require a licence for any person carrying on payment services 'in or from within Bermuda'; an unlicensed UK/FCA-authorised EMI (for example Wise, Airwallex or Currencycloud) cannot lawfully custody funds for a Bermuda company from a Bermuda book. However, for a non-resident Exempted Company, remote multi-currency EMI accounts provided by FCA-regulated, EU-licensed, or Hong Kong-stored-value-licensed institutions are practically viable for outbound supplier payments, provided the EMI's terms accept Bermuda-incorporated non-resident customers and the BMA does not treat the account as Bermuda business. The company must supply the EMI with the BMA Exchange Control non-resident designation letter, the certificate of incorporation, the beneficial-ownership register extract, and a pass-through attestation that all funds originate from verified B2B marketplace receivables.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% standard tax-exempt status; 15% for Bermuda taxable constituent entities of multinational enterprise groups with consolidated revenue equal to or exceeding EUR 750 million under Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, effective for fiscal periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends, irrespective of recipient residence; no advance corporation tax or dividend excise is chargeable on distributions by a Bermuda exempted company.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

If no Corporate Income Tax applies, no CIT return is due; the annual Economic Substance Return is due within six months after the financial year end. For an in-scope CIT constituent entity, the CIT return for a December-end period is due 15 months after the end of the financial year, with registration and preliminary information returns due at the date notified by BRA when the group first exceeds the EUR 750 million threshold.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No general local-currency threshold applies to a Bermuda tax-exempt company because there is no underlying income tax. For an in-scope Corporate Income Tax group, the mandatory threshold is group consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million, equivalent to approximately BMD 750 million under the Bermuda dollar fixed parity. For economic-substance proof, contemporaneous OECD transfer-pricing documentation should be prepared whenever cross-border related-party supply-chain, platform or royalty flows exceed BMD 1 million annually.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda Economic Substance Requirements apply to every exempted company that carries on a relevant activity. An electronic B2B wholesale platform engaged in sourcing, logistical intermediation, invoicing and payment routing is treated as a distribution and service centre; if the local entity holds proprietary trademarks, algorithms or platform IP and recognises IP income, an IP-business substance test with increased local ownership of intangible functions applies. The company must show economic presence through Bermuda-located senior management, physical office, proportionate employees and expenditure, and must file annually. The doctrine is fatal to ordinary offshore treaty shopping: mere registration without a real Bermuda operation will be considered a shell and will not attract the statutory tax-exemption protections.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and offshore concealment of UK permanent establishment

Statutory sanction: HMRC may determine UK tax residence under the UK statutory residence test; a UK permanent establishment assessment applies; unpaid corporation tax at 19% or 25% plus interest, penalties up to 100% of tax, and a potential diverted profits tax charge of 25% on the shifted platform profits; criminal prosecution under the Fraud Act 2006 or the Criminal Finances Act 2017 for failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for evasion-linked debts, disqualified as company directors, prohibited from acting as money-services signatories, and made subject to HMRC penalties for deliberate errors; serious fraudulent evasion can lead to imprisonment on indictment.
Shell company or sham economic substance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance Authority may impose a financial penalty of up to BMD 10,000 for the first failure and BMD 1,000 per day for a continuing offence, in addition to cancellation of the exempted undertaking protection certificate and strike-off from the Bermuda register under the Companies Act 1981; the company loses the jurisdictional protection against liquidation and tax-assurance relief.

Director personal liability: Bermuda courts may pierce the corporate veil where the exempted company is a mere facade; directors are liable for misrepresentation in the statutory declaration, disqualified by the Bermuda Registrar, barred from serving as significant persons in licensed institutions, and exposed to civil restitution claims by creditors and asset-freezing orders.
AML and beneficial-ownership failures in B2B payment flows

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008 and Bermuda Companies Act beneficial-ownership filing requirements attracts heavy civil penalties, blocking or deferred transaction monitoring, and criminal prosecution; the Registrar can strike off a non-compliant exempted company and impose daily default fines pending rectification.

Director personal liability: Relevant individuals are criminally liable where the failure is intentional; imprisonment terms apply on conviction, personal monetary penalties are imposed on directors and compliance officers, and the directors become subject to cross-border travel restrictions and freezing orders in relation to tainted marketplace proceeds.
Artificial transfer pricing and profit stripping through Bermuda platform IP

Statutory sanction: HMRC can apply transfer-pricing adjustments under TIOPA 2010, charge diverted profits tax at 25% on amounts treated as diverted from the UK, impose controlled-foreign-company income attribution on the Bermuda untaxed profit, and levy interest with daily accrual; failure-to-report penalties of up to 100% of the understated UK tax are also possible.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer-pricing documentation and those who deliberately cause the UK company to enter non-arm-length transactions may be assessed under HMRC penalties for deliberately withholding documents; the corporate veil may be pierced in the context of evasion, with directors jointly liable for restitution and subject to disqualification orders.
