Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy and Finance; online business-tax portal impots.gouv.fr and the Compte Fiscal des Professionnels (CFP) for corporate and VAT administration.

        
E-government filing portal
For a domestic French SAS, incorporation is effected through INPI's single Guichet Unique, which automatically delivers SIREN, SIRET, APE/NAF code, and legal-existence documents; DGFiP then opens tax accounts and issues the FR VAT identification number (n° TVA intracommunautaire) where taxable activities exist. A foreign entity without French establishment must register for French VAT directly with DGFiP's non-resident business service (SIEE/SIENR in Lyon), submitting identity, evidence of foreign tax registration, intended activities in France and estimated turnover, and appointing a VAT fiscal representative if the entity is not established in the EU; an explicit application is required before the first taxable supply where no turnover threshold applies.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a domestic French SAS, incorporation is effected through INPI's single Guichet Unique, which automatically delivers SIREN, SIRET, APE/NAF code, and legal-existence documents; DGFiP then opens tax accounts and issues the FR VAT identification number (n° TVA intracommunautaire) where taxable activities exist. A foreign entity without French establishment must register for French VAT directly with DGFiP's non-resident business service (SIEE/SIENR in Lyon), submitting identity, evidence of foreign tax registration, intended activities in France and estimated turnover, and appointing a VAT fiscal representative if the entity is not established in the EU; an explicit application is required before the first taxable supply where no turnover threshold applies.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
French domestic law does not provide a 'check-the-box' election. A SAS is by default a separate corporate income tax (IS) taxpayer. It may, however, elect for a time-limited five-fiscal-year partnership-type transparency regime under CGI art. 239 bis AB where it qualifies as a PME (headcount under 50 and turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million). No elective hybrid-mismatch override is available; France applies ATAD 2 anti-hybrid rules that recharacterize or deny deductions where another jurisdiction takes an inconsistent entity or instrument classification.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
20%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Domestic franchise en base de TVA exemption thresholds: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for principal supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for principal supplies of services (upper revocation limits EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,100). Non-established foreign suppliers have no de minimis threshold and must register for French VAT before the first taxable supply; EU/OSS distance-selling registration is triggered once cross-border B2C supplies to EU consumers exceed EUR 10,000 in a calendar year.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Physical exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union qualify for 0% French VAT where the exporter holds an accepted electronic customs export declaration (MRN) and proof that the goods left France/EU; indirect exports are also 0% rated when the statutory indirect-export conditions are met. For UI/UX design and digital subscription services supplied to a business customer established outside France, the place of supply is outside France (no French VAT) where the B2B recipient is a taxable person outside France; the supplier must retain the recipient's foreign VAT number or equivalent evidence, the service contract, bank/Stripe/Wise payment records and, if challenged, proof that the services are actually consumed outside France.

France requires structured electronic invoicing and e-reporting for domestic B2B supplies through an accredited Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the DGFiP Portail Public de Facturation (PPF). Invoices must be machine-readable (UBL, Factur-X/CNES or CII) and transmitted by API to the DGFiP via the platform; there is no per-invoice physical stamping or real-time pre-clearance, but a mandatory audit trail and invoice-data e-reporting apply. The mandate is phased, with the first compulsory issue/transmission obligations beginning 1 September 2026 and completion for all remaining business sizes by 1 September 2027; B2C e-commerce returns are filed electronically under the OSS/IOSS schemes.

      
DGFiP audits export substantiation and, if zero-rating or outside-scope treatment is not proved, retrospectively claws back the benefit and assesses VAT at 20% on the full proceeds. Late payment interest accrues at 0.20% per month; a 40% penalty applies for deliberate inaccuracy or understatement and 80% for fraudulent maneuvers or abuse of law (abus de droit). Criminal exposure under CGI art. 1741 includes fines and imprisonment for serious tax evasion, and the responsible officers or legal representatives may be held personally liable in the case of abusive schemes.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
France maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for both residents and non-residents through Articles L151-1 to L151-6 of the Code monétaire et financier and European TFEU Art. 63-65. The French franc/EUR is fully liberalized; there is no FX approval requirement for trade payments, service exports, dividend remittances, or equity injections. The SAS as a French resident may freely hold EUR or foreign-currency accounts both in France and abroad. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unconstrained by FX law, but financial intermediation data on every cross-border settlement with a value of at least EUR 15,000 must be reported to Banque de France through the receiving bank's balance-of-payments tele-transmission ('liaison financière'). Traveler/physical cash movement of EUR 10,000 or more must be declared to French customs under Art. R152-2 CMF. No prior authorization is needed for an inbound equity injection into a design-subscription SAS; outbound direct investment is regulated only in defense, dual-use, and the Art. L151-3 'strategic sectors' screening, none applicable to ISIC 7410.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None applicable. France abolished mandatory surrender and forced conversion of export/service receipts in 1989 with the full implementation of the EU capital-movement directive. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation and the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. Export proceeds from the UI/UX subscription activity may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency on a French-resident bank or EMI account. The sole residual obligation is statistical: each receipt from a non-resident counterparty equal to or above EUR 15,000 must be transmitted to Banque de France by the account-holding bank using the cross-border settlement reporting system; the corporate customer must supply the bank with an accurate payment motive code covering 'services exports' or proportional capital flows.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Banque de France; the Direction Générale du Trésor (Bilateral Affairs, Financing and Financial Services Directorate) owns the external financial relations regulation under the Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier).

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Wise
            Fit: High
          

          
High for the declared payment rails (Stripe_Billing and Wise) as complementary collection and disbursement engines, but not as the legal treasury anchor. A fintech EMI such as Wise is a Belgian-regulated e-money institution passporting into France under the EU single-licence regime; it can collect and disburse, execute spot FX, and technically aggregate client funds, but it cannot supply a statutory capital-escrow account ('compte bloqué') for SAS incorporation, cannot grant credit, does not offer a compliant account for statutory auditor confirmations, and does not automatically handle Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting to the same degree as a credit institution. Stripe_Billing is a payment aggregator, not a settlement institution. The legally efficient stack is: Tier-1 French bank for corporate account, escrow certificate, and Banque de France reporting; Stripe_Billing for recurring-design subscription collection; Wise for low-cost cross-border supplier disbursements and FX conversion; the French bank remains the beneficiary-of-funds ledger and the anchor for all treasury compliance evidence.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Non-resident director or ultimate beneficial owner without verifiable source of wealth.
        
Root cause: Non-resident director or ultimate beneficial owner without verifiable source of wealth.

        
Defense evidence: Certified passport (apostilled for non-EU); proof of address in residence country; personal/professional CV; reference letter from an established bank; source-of-wealth statement; six months of personal bank statements; Luxembourg/French notary-certified or apostilled copies of the SAS articles of association; statutory UBO declaration filed at the Registre National des Bénéficiaires Effectifs.

      

      
      

        Opacity in the shareholder chain (intermediate holding in a low-corporate-tax or offshore jurisdiction).
        
Root cause: Opacity in the shareholder chain (intermediate holding in a low-corporate-tax or offshore jurisdiction).

        
Defense evidence: Full corporate chain and shareholding tree up to ultimate individuals; Kbis/registry extracts of each vehicle; notarized ownership declarations; tax-residency certificates from relevant states; legal opinion confirming economic substance and commercial rationale; latest audited financial statements of the intermediate holding company.

      

      
      

        Recurring subscription digital-services model perceived as having chargeback/refund abuse risk.
        
Root cause: Recurring subscription digital-services model perceived as having chargeback/refund abuse risk.

        
Defense evidence: Signed design-subscription master agreements with representative clients; sample invoices and purchase orders; PCI-DSS attestation of compliance; refund/chargeback policy; public terms and privacy policy; breakdown of client counterparties and geographies; historical payment-rail exports and revenue reconciliation ledger.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Equity capital injections by a non-resident shareholder must be executed via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer/SEPA Instant for EUR) to the capital-escrow account ('compte bloqué') of the SAS opened prior to registration. The remittance information (field 70) must contain an explicit French-language purpose line such as 'APPORT EN CAPITAL – AUGMENTATION DE CAPITAL SAS [NAME]' together with the subscribing shareholder's corporate name, the deposit certificate reference, and the relevant notary or registry file number. The receiving bank maps the transaction to the Banque de France balance-of-payments 'code motif' family relating to capital participation in a French resident company, not to a current-services payment; the bank automatically tele-declares the operation (threshold EUR 15,000). At incorporation, the bank issues the 'certificat de dépôt des fonds' required by the greffe; after immatriculation the funds are released into the operational current account. For a later capital increase the same routing applies, supported by the shareholders' extraordinary resolution, the minutes, the updated UBO declaration, and, when applicable, the auditor's capital-increase report.The primary clearing rail for outbound settlements is SWIFT MT103 with fully populated fields 59 (beneficiary), 57A (beneficiary bank), 33B (currency amount), and 70 (payment purpose), executed through SWIFT gpi for end-to-end tracking. For intra-EEA supplier payments denominated in EUR, the SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant are the lowest-cost rails. For high-value outsourcing contracts and import-of-goods transactions, an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit is issued by the anchoring French bank and confirmed by the beneficiary's bank; disbursement requires presentation of compliant shipping or acceptance documents. Wise Business is recommended as a parallel rail for low-value freelance design-outsourcing disbursements in USD, GBP, or EUR, with the French bank account remaining the statutory ledger; each disbursement must be recorded with full VAT/reverse-charge documentation and the correct motive code for imports of services. Treasury calendar discipline—fixing FX cut-off times for intraday conversion—must be applied to avoid weekend settlement gaps.Deliverable FX Forward (Change à Terme) on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CHF, up to 24-month tenors, documented under a bank master agreement (ISDA or the bank's local Master Agreement), no underlying trade declaration required for French corporate hedging.FX Spot (Change au Comptant) for immediate conversion of subscription collections and supplier disbursements, standard T+2 settlement.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) for currencies with restricted deliverability or regulatory prohibition (e.g., INR, IDR, TRY, NGN, UYU) referenced to EUR/USD, permitted as a MiFID II OTC derivative where the local market does not allow physical delivery.Cross-Currency Swap (Swap de Devises) for medium-term intercompany-loan or refinancing exposure, combining initial notional exchange and re-exchange with periodic interest settlement legs.FX Option / Zero-Cost Collar (Tunnel de Change) for EUR/USD and EUR/GBP income protection with a premium-free cap-and-floor structure.Foreign-Currency Money-Market Deposit (EUR/USD/GBP term deposit at a regulated French or EU credit institution) as a permissible liquidity parking tool under full client-fund safeguarding rules.
