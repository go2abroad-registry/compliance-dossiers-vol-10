Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned bakery group forms a Bermuda Exempted Company through a licensed Corporate Service Provider (CSP) filing the Memorandum and Articles of Association with the Registrar of Companies. The Certificate of Incorporation and Bermuda Company Number (M-series) constitute the statutory identifier for tax, CRS, and FATCA purposes. For employing staff, a Payroll Tax account must be registered with the Office of the Tax Commissioner; for customs operations, an importer code is obtained from BCRA; for automatic exchange of information, the company must register on the Bermuda Ministry of Finance CRS/FATCA portal. There is no annual corporation-tax registration or corporation-tax return for the exempted company itself.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda Exempted Companies receive a statutory guarantee of exemption from income, capital gains, and withholding taxes until 28 March 2035 under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, with an optional Tax Protection Certificate confirmable at incorporation. Bermuda domestic law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; however, group-level classification elections remain relevant under US IRC 7701 (for US tax owners), UK CTA 2010 Part 6A hybrid-mismatch rules, and the Economic Substance (Bermuda) Act 2018, under which the company may elect to be treated as a pure equity holding entity only if it satisfies the associated filing conditions. A master-franchise central-kitchen operating a physical bakery chain is not a designated ES 'relevant activity', but any separate brand-IP holding must be ring-fenced to avoid IP-business economic-substance obligations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST; indirect taxation consists of Customs Import Duty (Customs Tariff Act 1995), employer Payroll Tax, Social Security contributions, and stamp duties on land and insurance policies

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST and prescribes no VAT registration threshold; payroll tax registration arises with the first employee; customs importer registration applies on the first importation of goods into Bermuda

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable under VAT law, as Bermuda has no VAT/GST. Goods exported from Bermuda may instead qualify for exemption from customs import duty, or for duty drawback/remission under the Customs Tariff Act 1995, subject to physical removal from Bermuda within a prescribed period and verifiable export documentation. Royalties, franchise fees, and central-kitchen supply income derived from overseas franchisees are foreign-source income that sits entirely outside Bermuda's tax net and requires no export zero-rating claim.

  • Customs export entry lodged through BCRA CEDS (Customs Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal of goods from Bermuda
  • Commercial export invoice and packing list
  • Bank credit advice or settlement record demonstrating receipt of export proceeds

Not applicable - Bermuda has no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping mandate; import and export declarations are filed through BCRA's Customs Electronic Declarations System (CEDS), and payroll tax returns are submitted quarterly through the Tax Commissioner's online portal

Bermuda maintains no statutory exchange-control repatriation mandate; the 180-day window reflects banking and master-franchise commercial best practice. Penalties for false customs declarations or duty evasion under the Customs Tariff Act 1995 reach three times the duty evaded, with forfeiture of goods and potential imprisonment. Duty drawback or remission is retrospectively clawed back if exported goods are re-imported into the Bermudian domestic market. Non-compliance with Economic Substance (Bermuda) Act 2018 filing obligations attracts penalties of up to BMD 250,000, plus director liability, even though the operating bakery business itself is not a designated relevant activity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar, ensuring narrow-band convertibility for trade and treasury purposes. For a non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company, current-account conversions for trade payments and profit remittances are effectively unrestricted. Capital-account inflows such as share subscription proceeds also qualify for automatic approval where the shareholder is outside the 'Scheduled Territories', subject to normal AML/CFT screening. No Bermuda withholding tax applies to dividends, royalties, interest, or branch profit remittances, so profit repatriation encounters no statutory fiscal impediment. Practical exchange-control friction remains limited to documentation, board authorisation, and transaction reporting with the BMA-authorised banking partner.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda's Exchange Control regime does not compel an Exempted Company to surrender export receipts to the central authority. Export proceeds from bakery goods, master-franchise royalties, central-kitchen product sales, and related trade settlements may be retained in BMD or convertible foreign currency accounts at BMA-authorised banks. There is no mandatory conversion ratio or stricted surrender timeline; the legal obligations are limited to full traceability, accurate booking, compliance with the receiving bank's AML/KYC policy, and availability of supporting contracts and invoices for six years following the transaction.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) acting as the delegated exchange-control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972 of Bermuda, with ultimate external-relations responsibility held by Her Majesty's Treasury via the Government of Bermuda.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium-High for payment initiation and clearance, but Low for deposit-taking. Bermuda does not issue a dedicated EMI licence akin to the UK's Electronic Money Regulations; non-bank payment activity falls under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, the Digital Asset Business Act 2018, and the BMA's Domestic Money Transmission rules. UK/EEA digital EMIs cannot passport into Bermuda, and offshore digital banks are generally prohibited from accepting BMD customer deposits. Nevertheless, an offshore EMI may be used as a non-deposit cross-border payment gateway for SWIFT MT103, SEPA, and stablecoin-flow treasury operations, provided the EMI is licensed in a recognised jurisdiction, maintains clean sanctions screening, and is used only for conduits of funds toward a fully licensed Bermuda bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's or lender's bank to a BMA-authorised bank in Bermuda, using the exact exempted-company name and registered number in the beneficiary field. Recommended payment purposes are 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — EXEMPTED COMPANY' or 'SHARE SUBSCRIPTION PROCEEDS', with reference to the board-approved capital plan and the relevant BMD/USD account. USD and BMD are at parity; therefore no conversion loss arises on the receiving side. The receiving bank will request an exchange-control approval reference where applicable, a notarised certified copy of the corporate resolutions, the UBO declaration, and confirmation that the incoming funds are free from embargoed or sanctioned sources. Verification should include a pre-transfer debit-advice test via SWIFT MT103, an electronic bank-guarantee arrangement where the remitter is a licensed bank, and same-day reconciliation against the capital call certificate.Outbound disbursements for master-royalty payments to the Chinese bakery franchisor, central kitchen equipment purchases, and bakery ingredient imports should clear via SWIFT MT103 for direct remittances and irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP600 with SWIFT MT700 issuance for bulk, documentary trade. Clearing rails should use the receiving bank's correspondent network in London or New York to effect USD or GBP settlement, then convert into the final settlement currency through the BMA-authorised bank. For recurring master-royalty wires, the payer should maintain a standing settlement instruction file, an auto-reconciliation report against royalty invoices, and a validation process for the MT103 narrative to include the franchise agreement number and royalty period. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A - Bermuda does not impose local corporate income tax, so no local transfer-pricing documentation threshold exists. For MNE groups whose ultimate parent meets the EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold, a Country-by-Country Report filing may be required from the Bermuda constituent entity; the Economic Substance test requires a relevant activity, but no separate transfer-pricing Local File is prescribed.

Economic substance scope: The International Tax Compliance (Economic Substance) Act 2018 and related Rules require every Exempted Company engaged in a 'relevant activity' (including banking, insurance, fund management, finance and leasing, headquarters, shipping, holding, IP, or distribution and service) to maintain real economic presence in Bermuda. For a bakery/franchise chain, the relevant categories are 'pure holding' or 'distribution and service business'. Full substance requires management and control to be exercised in Bermuda, adequate physical premises, commensurate employees, and minimum core-income expenditures in Bermuda. An annual Economic Substance Return must be filed even if the company has no taxable profit; tax exemption does not exempt substance compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Offshore Tax Abuse

Statutory sanction: In the UK: HMRC penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded, accelerated payment notices, asset freezing orders, and potential criminal prosecution under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 for failure to prevent facilitation of foreign tax evasion. In Bermuda: fines under the Companies Act 1981 and Economic Substance Act, plus the Bermuda Registrar can make an adverse finding and strike the company off the register if false declarations are made.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly run the company with no substance may be personally jointly liable for any UK/foreign tax evaded, receive travel bans through court-ordered restrictions pending criminal investigation, and face imprisonment of up to seven years for tax evasion conspiracy or cheating the public revenue.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance Regime: fixed fine of BD$10,000 for failure to file a correct return; daily penalty of BD$1,000 thereafter; if the Registrar determines that the entity carries a relevant activity without adequate substance, a fine of BD$430,000 may be imposed and the company's details may be publicly identified as non-compliant. The company may be struck off and lose the statutory exemption protection.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign a false substance declaration are individually liable to a penalty of up to BD$430,000 and imprisonment up to six months; for repeat or intentional violation, imprisonment may be extended to three years. The court may also issue an order banning a director from managing any Bermuda company for up to ten years.
AML/KYC Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 and the Anti-Money Laundering Regulations 2008, the company may be fined up to BD$1 million and its registration may be revoked; prohibited from engaging in financial services if the entity also holds a restricted EMI or banking licence. The Bermuda Monetary Authority can issue compliance orders, and the registrar may dissolve the company.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be sentenced to up to five years' imprisonment for knowingly facilitating money laundering; civil restitution of laundered funds is enforceable against personal assets, and court-imposed travel bans may be secured pending forfeiture proceedings.
Fraudulent Misrepresentation / Misleading the Registrar

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent trading and false statement offences under the Companies Act 1981: fines up to BD$50,000 and up to five years' imprisonment; directors may be disqualified from acting as a director for up to fifteen years; the company may be dissolved with retrospective liability for all debts incurred during fraudulent trading.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for all company debts contracted in reliance on the false annual return; civil restitution orders require payment of compensation to aggrieved franchisees and creditors; further travel bans may be imposed to guarantee presence during civil and criminal proceedings, and imprisonment will follow for egregious cases.
