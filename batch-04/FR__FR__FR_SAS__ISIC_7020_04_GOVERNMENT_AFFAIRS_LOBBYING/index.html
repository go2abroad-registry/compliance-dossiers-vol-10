Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office in France. A commercial domiciliation company may be used if it is authorized and the parties execute a compliant written domiciliation agreement; the domiciliation provider must maintain required records and verify the company's identity and effective management arrangements. Temporary domiciliation at the president's personal residence may be available under Article L.123-11-1 of the Commercial Code, subject to statutory conditions and applicable lease, condominium and local restrictions. A virtual office alone does not satisfy banking, tax-residence, employment, data-protection, lobbying-recordkeeping or operational-substance expectations. For government-affairs advisory and lobbying activity, maintain accessible records, qualified personnel, client files, compliance controls and a credible French operating presence. Registration as a representative of interests with the Haute Autorité pour la Transparence de la Vie Publique (HATVP) may be required under Law No. 2016-1691 of 9 December 2016, Articles 25 et seq., where statutory contact and influence thresholds are met; this is a transparency registration, not a general professional license.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. No exchange controls or restrictions on profit remittance, dividends, or repatriation of capital for non-resident investors, subject to standard anti-money laundering (AML) and tax compliance. The EU Capital Movements Directive (88/361/EEC) guarantees free movement of capital, with limited derogations for public policy or security. Profit remittance requires no prior authorization; only standard tax withholding (if applicable) and reporting to the tax authorities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds is required in France. Exporters may retain foreign currency in accounts or convert freely. However, all cross-border transactions exceeding €10,000 must be reported to the French Customs and Indirect Taxation Directorate (DGDDI) for balance-of-payments statistics. For invoicing in foreign currency, the exporter must ensure compliance with EU and French regulations on payment terms and currency usage, but no forced conversion ratio exists.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) and the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR) under the aegis of the European Central Bank (ECB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. French fintech EMIs (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Manager.one) offer fast onboarding and multi-currency accounts, but they are not full banks and may have limitations on large cross-border transactions, escrow services, or complex trade finance. They are viable for operational accounts and low-volume transactions, but for high-value capital injections or trade settlements, a tier-1 bank is recommended. Legal constraints: EMIs must be licensed by the ACPR as payment institutions or electronic money institutions; they cannot provide credit or hold client funds as deposits. Cross-border operations are permitted under EU passporting, but counterparty risk and service scope must be assessed.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For capital injections from non-resident shareholders, use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with the following purpose codes: (a) for equity capital: 'S301' (Investment in equity capital) or 'S302' (Investment in real estate) as per the IMF Balance of Payments Manual; (b) for shareholder loans: 'S303' (Other investment). The remitter must include the company's name and the purpose of payment in the wire instructions. The receiving bank will require a copy of the share subscription agreement or loan agreement for verification. For amounts exceeding €10,000, the bank will report to the French customs for balance-of-payments statistics. No prior authorization is needed, but the bank may request supporting documents to comply with AML/KYC.For trade settlements (e.g., paying foreign consultants or suppliers), use SWIFT MT103 for standard wire transfers. For larger transactions or when documentary evidence is required, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) via SWIFT MT700 is recommended. The clearing rails are the TARGET2 system for EUR-denominated payments and the SWIFT network for cross-currency. For EUR payments within the SEPA zone, use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) with the IBAN and BIC. For non-EUR payments, use correspondent banking via the bank's network.
