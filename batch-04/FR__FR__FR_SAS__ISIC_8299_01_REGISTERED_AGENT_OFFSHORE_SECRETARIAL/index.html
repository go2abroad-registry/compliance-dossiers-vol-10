Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital under French law for SAS; articles may set capital as low as €1, but any cash contribution must be fully subscribed and deposited into a French bank account or with a notary/Caisse des Dépôts before registration, blocked until certificate of incorporation is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate issued after a commission control visit (Permis de feu / attestation de sécurité) under the Code de la construction et de l'habitation if the premises host more than 30 persons or are open to the public; structural load inspection by a certified office for archive areas; workplace clearance and professional risk assessment (DUERP) issued by the employer in association with the état des servitudes and an occupational health check by the services de prévention et de santé au travail.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital under French law for SAS; articles may set capital as low as €1, but any cash contribution must be fully subscribed and deposited into a French bank account or with a notary/Caisse des Dépôts before registration, blocked until certificate of incorporation is issued.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), under the Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty. The principal online portal is impots.gouv.fr. Business tax matters are generally handled by the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE); non-established businesses requiring French VAT registration are generally handled by the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the competent Centre de formalités des entreprises/Guichet unique operated by INPI. Following registration in the Registre national des entreprises and, where applicable, the Registre du commerce et des sociétés, it receives a SIREN identifier and establishment-level SIRET identifiers; the SIREN is used as the basis of its French business tax identification. The company must separately activate its professional account on impots.gouv.fr and complete applicable corporate income tax, payroll, and other tax registrations. A French VAT number is obtained from the competent tax office and follows the format FR plus a two-character key and the company's nine-digit SIREN. A foreign company making French taxable supplies that require registration should apply to the SIEE before commencing those supplies, providing evidence of legal existence, business activity, and the identity and authority of its representative. A business established outside the EU generally must appoint a French VAT representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement or other statutory exception removes that requirement. Registration thresholds or small-business exemption rules do not generally excuse a non-established supplier from registration where its French transactions are taxable and no special rule applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS); France does not provide a general US-style check-the-box election. A qualifying newly formed, non-listed company may elect temporary partnership-style taxation under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code for up to five fiscal years, subject to statutory conditions including qualifying ownership, employee and turnover or balance-sheet criteria and restrictions on its principal activity. Certain qualifying family companies may elect partnership taxation under separate rules. These are limited statutory elections, not general hybrid-mismatch elections. French anti-hybrid rules implement applicable EU and OECD measures and do not create an elective entity-classification regime. The standard IS rate is 25%; a qualifying small company may benefit from the 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to statutory conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business, the domestic small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base) is subject to statutory turnover limits, generally EUR 37,500 for services and EUR 85,000 for goods for 2025, with higher tolerance limits of EUR 41,250 and EUR 93,500, respectively, subject to applicable rules and legislative changes. These are exemption limits, not a general registration threshold for foreign businesses. A non-established business making French transactions for which it is liable to account for French VAT generally must register from the first such transaction; no general turnover threshold applies. Cross-border EU small-enterprise relief may be available only if its specific conditions and notification requirements are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from the EU to a destination outside the EU may be exempt from French VAT with deduction rights, subject to the statutory conditions and reliable evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. Intra-EU supplies are generally exempt with deduction rights only when the customer and reporting conditions are met; they are not exports outside the EU. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer or supplier is offshore: place-of-supply rules determine whether French VAT applies, including the general B2B rule and its exceptions. For a French taxable person exporting goods or supplying qualifying services, input VAT recovery requires valid invoices, a business-purpose link, and compliance with reporting and evidentiary requirements.

  • Export customs declaration and customs exit results or equivalent evidence confirming exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, packing list, and transport documents appropriate to the shipment, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, or CMR
  • Proof of delivery or receipt by the customer where available
  • Proof of payment and accounting records linking the payment to the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory prerequisite
  • For services, contract, invoices, evidence of the customer's business status and location where relevant, and records supporting the applicable place-of-supply treatment

France does not generally require prior real-time clearance of domestic VAT invoices. Under the phased domestic e-invoicing reform, all businesses within scope must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance obligations are scheduled from that date for large and intermediate-sized businesses and from 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises. In-scope transactions are to be transmitted through an approved platform (plateforme agréée) and the central directory; transaction and payment data reporting (e-reporting) applies to specified transactions outside domestic e-invoicing, including relevant cross-border and consumer transactions. Foreign suppliers' obligations depend on establishment, transaction type, and the statutory scope of the reform. There is no general VAT digital stamp or universal real-time invoice-clearance requirement.

France imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. Export exemption claims may be denied or clawed back if exit from the EU or other statutory conditions are not substantiated, with output VAT, interest for late payment, and applicable tax penalties assessed. Depending on the circumstances, penalties may include the general 10% late-payment surcharge, increased surcharges for non-compliance after formal notice, and penalties of 40% for deliberate breaches or 80% for specified fraudulent or abusive conduct; criminal prosecution may apply to serious tax fraud. The exact assessment depends on the facts, applicable procedural rules, and the taxpayer's conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility of the euro on both current-account and capital-account basis under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Article L151-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code; France applies no exchange-control authorization system for ordinary business transactions, no parallel exchange market, and no repatriation or earmarking requirement on inbound or outbound payments; the SAS is free to hold multi-currency accounts, to remit dividends and management fees to non-resident shareholders without advance approval, and to settle cross-border service fees in any freely convertible currency; limited residual screening applies under the foreign direct investment (FDI) prior-authorization regime for sensitive sectors in Article L151-3 CMF, but ISIC 8299 registered-agent services are outside the strategic list; banking counterparties nevertheless enforce EU/OECD/OFAC sanctions and ACPR liquidity requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither France nor the euro area imposes a mandatory export-proceeds surrender obligation or a forced conversion ratio; a French SAS may lawfully retain 100% of export and service-proceeds in a foreign-currency account held at a French or foreign credit institution; however, resident companies must file the statistical balance-of-payments declarations ('déclaration de change') with Banque de France when cumulative gross flows cross the de minimis threshold of EUR 15 million per calendar year, using the BP-OVP limited portal; individual cross-border transfers exceeding EUR 10,000 in cash require customs declaration under Article L152-1 CMF; corporate retention of receivables abroad must be evidenced by the commercial contract, the export invoice, and the bank SWIFT reference (MT103), with records kept for 10 years for tax and customs verification.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), acting as macro-prudential supervisor, balance-of-payments registrar, and holder of the CPSS/EPB reporting gateway; enforcement of the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF) is shared with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking conduct and with the Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) for capital-market FX instruments; external financial regulatory guidance sits with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Relance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium viability with strict limitations: ACPR-licensed or PSD2-passporting payment institutions and electronic money institutions (notably Qonto, Payplug, and Shine) can technically onboard the French SAS remotely and provide fast, low-cost EUR collecting accounts and card-acceptance rails, but they cannot issue the 'attestation de dépôt de fonds' required for capital formation on a 'compte bloqué' unless they hold a full credit-institution license, nor do they provide reliable SWIFT correspondent clearing into Asia, the Gulf, or North America; moreover, for ISIC 8299 (TCSP-type registered-agent services), the AML/CFT classification mandates an account held at a licensed credit institution to validate the ACPR and Guichet-TCSP compliance relationship; therefore the blueprint requires the primary treasury account at a Tier-1 bank, with fintech/EMI accounts used only as secondary collection tunnels capped at 25% of monthly inflows.

on arrival of the capital the bank executes the 'attestation de dépôt de fonds' for the notaire or the CFE/INPI guichet, and in accordance with Article L227-2 CMF the funds remain blocked for five days where a single shareholder has contributed all assets; documentary commercial letters of credit are permitted only where physical registry documents or notarized packages are exported as goods; for a pure services business model the recommended method is a 30-60 day sight draft under a confirmed irrevocable L/C issued by the French bank, although most treasury flows should prefer open-account arrangements; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium viability with strict limitations: ACPR-licensed or PSD2-passporting payment institutions and electronic money institutions (notably Qonto, Payplug, and Shine) can technically onboard the French SAS remotely and provide fast, low-cost EUR collecting accounts and card-acceptance rails, but they cannot issue the 'attestation de dépôt de fonds' required for capital formation on a 'compte bloqué' unless they hold a full credit-institution license, nor do they provide reliable SWIFT correspondent clearing into Asia, the Gulf, or North America; moreover, for ISIC 8299 (TCSP-type registered-agent services), the AML/CFT classification mandates an account held at a licensed credit institution to validate the ACPR and Guichet-TCSP compliance relationship; therefore the blueprint requires the primary treasury account at a Tier-1 bank, with fintech/EMI accounts used only as secondary collection tunnels capped at 25% of monthly inflows.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to file corporate tax return

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties of €250 to €75,000 plus 0.5% of tax due per month of delay (maximum 5%), plus interest on unpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors face personal restitution obligations for unpaid taxes; potential disqualification from office and criminal prosecution for repeated or fraudulent non-filing
Tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties including fines up to 500% of evaded tax, imprisonment up to five years, and confiscation of illicit gains

Director personal liability: Piercing of the corporate veil leading to personal civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 500% of the tax adjustment plus interest and possible restitution of tax benefits

Director personal liability: Personal financial liability for restitution and potential criminal sanctions if intent to evade is proven
