Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) - Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (electronic single window, formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); substantive RCS registration executed by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce having territorial jurisdiction over the registered office.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title II: Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (common incorporation provisions) and Articles L.227-1 to L.227-31 (Société par Actions Simplifiée specific regime); implementing regulation R.224-9 (escrow deposit of cash contributions).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) constituting the company, filed electronically with the Guichet Unique, together with the electronic declaration replacing the former Cerfa M0 form and annexes (JAL publication attestation, capital deposit certificate, beneficial-owner declaration).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum share capital for an SAS (Article L.227-2 Code de commerce); capital may be set at EUR 1 and is freely fixed by the Statuts. For cash contributions, at least 50% of the subscribed amount must be paid up on incorporation into a blocked escrow account held with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the balance must be paid within 5 years. In-kind contributions must be fully delivered at incorporation and, where statutory thresholds are reached, appraised by a commissaire aux apports. Escrowed funds are released only upon presentation of the Kbis extract evidencing RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Before employees enter the premises, the employer must draft the DUERP (Articles L.4121-1 et seq. Code du travail), maintain a fire-safety register (registre de sécurité), and ensure electrical installations conform to NF C 15-100 with annual equipment checks. For office buildings not open to the public, no ERP classification applies; if any part is open to the public, an ERP 5th-category conformity declaration must be submitted to the SDIS. Structural load certification is recommended for any server room to validate floor capacity.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum share capital for an SAS (Article L.227-2 Code de commerce); capital may be set at EUR 1 and is freely fixed by the Statuts. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full convertibility for both current account transactions and capital movements pursuant to EU regulations and national legislation. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without quantitative restrictions, subject only to standard corporate tax compliance, beneficial ownership verification, and anti-money laundering reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must repatriate foreign currency proceeds from sales to non-EU destinations within six months of shipment date. Mandatory surrender to an authorised French bank at the official exchange rate is required unless a specific authorisation for delayed surrender or retention is granted by the Banque de France. Intra-EU trade proceeds are exempt from surrender requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. A 3.3% social contribution may apply to companies whose corporate income tax exceeds EUR 763,000, subject to the statutory turnover threshold and other conditions, producing an overall rate of approximately 25.83%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident legal entity, subject to domestic exemptions, European Union Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief, applicable tax treaties, anti-abuse rules, and beneficial ownership requirements. A 12.8% rate generally applies to dividends paid to non-resident individuals, subject to treaty or statutory relief.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on a date other than 31 December, the return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the return is generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the following year. The tax administration's annual filing calendar must be checked for the precise electronic deadline. Corporate income tax installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, with the final balance generally due by 15 May of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer pricing documentation is generally mandatory for French entities or groups meeting the statutory threshold of EUR 150 million in annual turnover or gross assets, subject to applicable group and ownership conditions. Entities below that threshold must still apply the arm's-length principle and provide adequate evidence during an audit. A simplified transfer pricing statement may be required for covered entities. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups meeting the EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold. Transactions of at least EUR 100,000 per category may require inclusion in the annual transfer pricing declaration, subject to the applicable filing rules.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a separate stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance remains material under the arm's-length principle, French anti-abuse doctrine, the principal-purpose test in treaties, beneficial ownership rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, anti-hybrid rules, and general anti-abuse provisions. The French SAS should maintain adequate French decision-making, personnel or genuinely controlled outsourced functions, premises or operational capacity, books and records, commercial rationale, risk control, and authority over the AI data-labeling and BPO activities. Conduit arrangements, artificial management fees, circular cash flows, and entities lacking functional capacity may be recharacterized or denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, 40% penalty for deliberate failure, potentially 80% penalty for fraudulent maneuvers or abusive conduct, late-payment interest, and criminal tax-fraud sanctions. Serious tax fraud may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated cases potentially carrying up to seven years and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability where they personally participated in fraud. They may also face civil or tax-debt liability where fraudulent maneuvers or serious and repeated management misconduct caused the company's tax insolvency. Travel restrictions or asset measures may arise in criminal or enforcement proceedings, but are not automatic consequences of an ordinary filing error.
Transfer pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment to arm's-length profit, possible withholding tax reassessment, denial of deductions, late-payment interest, and penalties for inadequate or missing transfer pricing documentation. A documentation-related penalty may be imposed subject to statutory minimum and maximum amounts and the value of the reassessed transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for knowingly approving non-arm's-length transactions, especially where records are falsified or assets are diverted. Criminal liability may arise if transfer pricing conduct forms part of tax fraud, fraudulent bankruptcy, money laundering, or abuse of corporate assets.
Shell company, artificial management, or treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or domestic exemptions, withholding tax at the statutory rate, reassessment under the abuse-of-law or anti-abuse rules, penalties, interest, and possible recharacterization of the arrangement or beneficial ownership.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement sham arrangements may incur criminal liability for fraud, money laundering, or abuse of corporate assets. Corporate protection does not shield individuals from personal participation in unlawful conduct.
Undeclared cross-border payments and withholding tax failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, interest, penalties, possible liability of the payer for the tax that should have been withheld, and sanctions for inaccurate or late reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they deliberately authorize withholding-tax evasion, conceal the recipient, or falsify payment records. Ordinary technical errors generally create corporate liability rather than automatic personal imprisonment.
VAT fraud and carousel transactions

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, denial of input VAT, penalties potentially reaching 40% or 80% depending on conduct, late-payment interest, joint liability in certain fraudulent chains, and criminal prosecution for aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where they knowingly organized or facilitated VAT fraud. Criminal sanctions may include imprisonment and fines, and courts may order confiscation or other asset measures.
Anti-money laundering, terrorist financing, and payment-rail misuse

Statutory sanction: Account restrictions, payment suspension, suspicious transaction reporting, asset freezing or confiscation, administrative sanctions, AML fines, and criminal sanctions for money laundering. Money laundering may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with aggravated cases carrying materially higher sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be prosecuted personally, face asset confiscation, management bans, and restrictions on conducting business. Travel bans may be imposed by judicial order where legally justified; they are not automatic for ordinary AML deficiencies.
Payroll, social-security, and undeclared labor violations

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid contributions, surcharges, penalties, employment-law damages, administrative sanctions, possible prohibition from public contracts, and criminal sanctions for concealed work.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for concealed work or deliberate social-security fraud and may incur civil liability for unpaid contributions and employee damages where statutory conditions are met.
Accounting falsification and corporate asset misuse

Statutory sanction: Rejection of accounts, tax reassessment, repayment of improperly distributed amounts, civil damages, director disqualification, and criminal sanctions for misuse of corporate assets or fraudulent accounting.

Director personal liability: The president and de facto managers may be personally liable for restitution, damages, director disqualification, and imprisonment where criminal elements are established. Limited liability does not protect intentional misconduct or misuse of corporate assets.
