Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Tax Administration under the Office of the Tax Commissioner (Ministry of Finance) – responsible for payroll tax and other local levies. Company registration is handled by the Registrar of Companies. Official portal: www.gov.bm

E-government filing portal

Bermuda does not operate a general corporate income tax; hence no standard national TIN or VAT ID is issued. A Bermuda Exempted Company obtains a Company Registration Number (CRN) and a Bermuda Business Number (BBN) from the Registrar of Companies upon incorporation. For payroll tax purposes, the entity must register with the Tax Commissioner as an employer within 15 days of the first payroll payment, obtaining a payroll tax account number. No other tax registration is required for exempted companies deriving income solely from outside Bermuda.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone – Bermuda Exempted Companies are non-resident entities, exempt from local income tax, capital gains tax, and stamp duty on shares. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – Bermuda Exempted Companies are non-resident entities, exempt from local income tax, capital gains tax, and stamp duty on shares. They are subject only to employment-related levies (payroll tax and social insurance contributions). A certificate of tax exemption under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 may guarantee additional exemption from any future Bermuda taxation until 2035 (renewable to 2050).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are available under Bermuda law. The only relevant statutory election is the application for a 'Tax Exemption Certificate' under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act, which locks in the entity's zero-rate direct tax position. Companies may also elect to register for payroll tax as a bundled or general employer, but this does not affect entity-level tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – Bermuda does not levy VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. The closest indirect charges are import customs duties under the Customs Act 2008 and payroll tax (classified as a direct employment levy).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST/sales tax registration threshold exists. For import duty, every importer must obtain a Customs Tariff Classification and file declarations per shipment. For payroll tax, registration is compulsory upon hiring the first employee, regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Bermuda's indirect tax system has no VAT/GST export zero-rating mechanism. Exported goods are outside the scope of customs duty (duty applies only at importation). Services rendered from Bermuda to foreign clients are not subject to any Bermuda indirect tax. For direct tax purposes, the exempted company's foreign-source income is exempt, and a 'No Tax Liability' letter can be obtained from the Ministry of Finance upon request.

  • Not applicable for VAT zero-rating – no such regime exists. For physical exports from Bermuda, the following customs documents are mandatory:
  • Customs Export Declaration (Bermuda Customs Form C5)
  • Commercial Invoice (English, detailing export value and description)
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (evidence of physical movement out of Bermuda)
  • Exporter's Registration / Customs Code (when applicable)

No e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists for indirect tax. Bermuda Customs uses the ASYCUDA World electronic single-window system for submission of customs declarations and duty assessment. Payroll tax returns are filed electronically through the Office of the Tax Commissioner's online portal. No digital stamping or API-based government clearance is required for invoices.

No export zero-rating fraud or clawback regime exists because there is no applicable indirect tax on exports. Customs misdeclarations in export documentation are subject to penalties under the Customs Act 2008, including fines up to 150% of duty evaded and potential imprisonment. For payroll tax, willful false declarations may attract fines and interest on unpaid amounts. The Tax Commissioner may conduct audits and make retroactive assessments for up to six years in cases of fraudulent or negligent returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at a fixed 1:1 rate to the US dollar (USD), and USD is legal tender and circulates freely throughout Bermuda. The capital account and current account are both fully open for a Bermuda Exempted Company: equity injections, intercompany loans, dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, liquidation proceeds and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders require no prior BMA exchange control approval and are subject to zero withholding tax under Bermuda law. Convertibility is therefore unrestricted in theory and in banking practice; the only substantive hurdle is the account-holding bank's internal credit and AML/CFT due diligence. A deemed-tax-exempt Bermudian company is not taxed on foreign-source income, and profit repatriation to the UK parent or other non-resident shareholders is not conditioned on a tax clearance certificate. Practical remittance friction arises exclusively from source-of-funds/source-of-wealth documentation required by the correspondent banking chain, especially where transactions originate from UK/EU fintech platforms such as Stripe or a UK-authorised Payment Institution. Because Bermuda is an offshore special territory of the United Kingdom and is not a member of the EEA, UK/EU merchant acquiring and Direct Debit rails generally require a UK/EU PSP as intermediary rather than direct BMA-licensed membership in payment schemes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory duty to surrender or repatriate export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for onshore crediting of export receipts. Under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and current BMA practice, a Bermuda Exempted Company may retain foreign currency proceeds (including SaaS subscription receipts, ERP/CRM software licence fees, cloud service revenues and trade receivables) indefinitely in offshore accounts denominated in USD, GBP, EUR or any freely convertible currency. There is no requirement to convert export receipts into BMD; the BMD/USD peg makes conversion economically neutral. Reporting obligations are limited to anti-money-laundering thresholds: the bank must file a suspicious activity report for transactions above BMD 25,000 only where the circumstances are unusual or the counterparty presents ML/TF risk, and the company must maintain underlying commercial documentation (invoices, contracts, merchant settlement statements) for a minimum of five years in accordance with the Proceeds of Crime Regulations 1998 and the BMA AML/ATF Code of Practice.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the central bank, currency board and foreign exchange control authority for the British Overseas Territory of Bermuda, pursuant to the Exchange Control Act 1972, the Exchange Control Regulations 1973 and the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969; HM Treasury of the United Kingdom retains concurrent jurisdiction over financial sanctions and the UK Sanctions List applicable to Bermuda as a UK Overseas Territory.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. A Bermuda Exempted Company is a legal customer of, but cannot itself be licensed as, a UK/EU Electronic Money Institution; Bermuda has no operational EMII licensing regime and the BMA does not issue PSD2 equivalent licences. For cross-border treasury purposes, the viable structure is to pair a licensed Bermuda commercial bank (Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda) as the master corporate account with an offshore digital EMI such as Wise, Airwallex or Revolut Business acting as a PSP/multi-currency settlement rail for Stripe Billing payouts and SEPA/Bacs Direct Debit collections. The EMI must be regulated under UK PSD2 or an EEA National Bank's EMI regime; it will classify the Bermuda entity as a non-EEA corporate customer and typically requires full KYC, proof of beneficial ownership, and a justification for the Bermuda nexus. Many EMIs decline a wholly Bermuda-based merchant because of perceived substance risk; therefore the recommended onboarding approach is to present the Bermuda Exempted Company as an intermediate holding/IP-licensing company within a UK group with consolidated financial statements. Direct Debit outbound from Bermuda is not permitted on the UK Bacs scheme; Direct Debit collection (company as originator/merchant) is feasible through a UK-authorised PSP that maintains the merchant account, while the PSP remits net settlement in USD/GBP to the Bermuda bank via SWIFT. The BMA expects that all customer-facing payment services and foreign exchange activities carried out with Bermuda clients are performed by licensed institutions; using an unlicensed offshore EMI as a pass-through account is legally sensitive and should be avoided.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing protocol for equity capital into the Bermuda Exempted Company is a SWIFT MT103 (ISO 20022) wire from the subscribing shareholder's own bank account to the company's segregated Capital Escrow Account at Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda. For USD, the wire is cleared through the Bermudian bank's U.S. correspondent (typically Bank of New York Mellon or JPMorgan Chase, New York), using a BOP/transaction narrative of 'share subscription — non-resident direct investment into Bermuda exempted company [registered number, entity ID BM_EXEMPTED_CO]'. For GBP, route via CHAPS or Faster Payments to the Bank of England settlement account of Butterfield's London correspondent and then onward SWIFT to Bermuda; for EUR, use TARGET2 via a eurozone correspondent. The beneficiary bank requires that the Payment Details field identify the company's Bermudian bank account and a purpose code consistent with cross-border equity investment. Inbound share subscriptions are not subject to Bermuda exchange control approval, but the bank will credit an escrow sub-ledger until it receives (i) the board resolution approving the share issue, (ii) the executed subscription agreement, (iii) a statement of source of funds for the electronic transfer, (iv) the updated register of members, and (v) a Director's Certificate confirming the aggregate capital raised. All files must be uploaded to the bank before the escrow is released for investment or operating expenditure. The transaction is reportable to the BMA only if it forms part of a suspicious pattern; otherwise, no regulatory filing is required. The company must record the capital injection in the Bermuda financial statements and file the annual return to the Registrar of Companies.The primary clearing rail for outbound trade disbursements is SWIFT MT103 (with a UETR reference) for supplier payments, software royalties, cloud infrastructure and intercompany charges. The Bermuda account bank will execute the payment through its correspondent network, typically same-day USD to U.S. beneficiaries, T+1 for EUR via TARGET2, and T+1 for GBP via CHAPS, with a narrative containing the relevant purpose code (e.g., 'payment for software licence fees' or 'payment for cloud hosting services'). For high-value or conditional transactions, the company may utilise a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Bermuda bank under ICC UCP 600, chiefly for hardware procurement or infrastructure equipment where the vendor requires documentary protection; the expiring bank will be in the vendor's country, and the advising bank must be a reputable institution. For recurring low-value disbursements, the company may aggregate supplier payments and route them through the integrated FX desk of Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda, or through a regulated UK/EU EMI with an API if the Bermuda bank provides correspondent plumbing. Stripe Billing payouts are settled by the merchant bank to the company in GBP/USD/EUR, and the funds are then swept to the Bermuda operational account via SWIFT; there is no direct debit outbound rail from Bermuda. For Bacs Direct Debit collection, the company must act as the creditor (originator) through a UK-authorised PSP, with the PSP collecting from UK customers and remitting the net aggregate to the Bermuda bank account. All outbound payments above GBP 10,000 must carry a clear purpose code and, where the beneficiary is a UK/EU tax resident, appropriate withholding tax certificates where required. The bank will maintain a sanctioned-party screening log and expects the company to maintain underlying invoices and contracts for each payment.Deliverable FX forwards (vanilla, up to 24 months) quoted in USD, GBP and EUR, executed under a bilateral FX Master Agreement between the Bermuda Exempted Company and the Bermuda account bank; these are the recommended first instrument given the BMD/USD 1:1 peg and the company's GBP/USD subscription/hosting mismatch.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing currencies of the company's emerging-market customer base; permissible for hedging subscription receivables denominated in non-convertible Asian or African currencies, with the payoff settled in USD by the Bermudian bank as a licensed counterparty, and requiring ISDA 2002 documentation and a netting agreement.Cross-currency swaps (USD/GBP and USD/EUR) converting the currency base of long-dated cloud infrastructure financing and matching expected subscription cash flows; each swap must be evidenced by a confirmation under an ISDA Master Agreement and is subject to the bank's credit limit.FX swaps (spot/forward combinations) for temporary treasury liquidity smoothing, permitted for up to 12 months, and commonly used to bridge timing differences between Stripe settlement dates and vendor payment dates.Vanilla FX options and flex forwards, permitted at the discretion of the licensed bank, with a board-approved treasury mandate and a signed options addendum; Bermuda imposes no exchange control restriction on the use of derivatives.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with material legal constraints. A Bermuda Exempted Company is a legal customer of, but cannot itself be licensed as, a UK/EU Electronic Money Institution; Bermuda has no operational EMII licensing regime and the BMA does not issue PSD2 equivalent licences. For cross-border treasury purposes, the viable structure is to pair a licensed Bermuda commercial bank (Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda) as the master corporate account with an offshore digital EMI such as Wise, Airwallex or Revolut Business acting as a PSP/multi-currency settlement rail for Stripe Billing payouts and SEPA/Bacs Direct Debit collections. The EMI must be regulated under UK PSD2 or an EEA National Bank's EMI regime; it will classify the Bermuda entity as a non-EEA corporate customer and typically requires full KYC, proof of beneficial ownership, and a justification for the Bermuda nexus. Many EMIs decline a wholly Bermuda-based merchant because of perceived substance risk; therefore the recommended onboarding approach is to present the Bermuda Exempted Company as an intermediate holding/IP-licensing company within a UK group with consolidated financial statements. Direct Debit outbound from Bermuda is not permitted on the UK Bacs scheme; Direct Debit collection (company as originator/merchant) is feasible through a UK-authorised PSP that maintains the merchant account, while the PSP remits net settlement in USD/GBP to the Bermuda bank via SWIFT. The BMA expects that all customer-facing payment services and foreign exchange activities carried out with Bermuda clients are performed by licensed institutions; using an unlicensed offshore EMI as a pass-through account is legally sensitive and should be avoided.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File documentation applies only to constituent entities of an in-scope multinational group with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more (approximately BMD 810 million at prevailing exchange rates) under the CIT Act 2023; entities below the threshold have no statutory TP documentation duty in Bermuda, although the ESR requires evidence of intra-group pricing for substance reporting.

Economic substance scope: The Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2018 and accompanying Income Tax Requirements apply to all exempted companies carrying on relevant activities, including holding entities, headquarters, distribution and service centres and IP businesses. A Vertical CRM cloud company that sells or licenses the software to the UK group while managing contracts from Bermuda should characterize its activity as either a distribution and service centre or pure IP business; each requires core income generation activity, senior management in Bermuda, adequate full-time employees, physical premises and core expenditure in Bermuda. A pure equity-holding subsidiary satisfies reduced substance requirements (employees and premises only). Below-threshold exempted companies cannot rely on the absence of CIT to avoid ESR compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax evasion and false CIT status declarations

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine up to BMD 10,000 on conviction for evasion; civil penalty up to 100% of the undercharged top-up tax plus daily default penalties of BMD 50 per day; the Commissioner may publish the names of defaulting entities.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly prepare or sign a false return are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of unpaid tax with interest; criminal courts may impose travel restrictions while proceedings are pending and order disqualification from acting as directors in Bermuda.
Shell company / absence of economic substance (ESR abuse)

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties starting at BMD 5,000 and reaching BMD 250,000 for substantive default, with continuing daily penalties; the Registrar may strike off and dissolve the company; on dissolution the assets vest in the Crown.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to meet the substance test are personally liable for the imposed fines; repeated abuse results in disqualification orders, travel bans under restraint orders in money-laundering-linked substance investigations, and possible criminal prosecution for fraudulent trading.
Anti-money laundering and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Regulations: custodial sentence up to 14 years and criminal fines on indictment; The Bermuda Monetary Authority can impose civil monetary penalties, asset freezing orders and revocation of any relevant registration.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners who knowingly permit laundering are criminally liable in their personal capacity; confiscation orders attach to personal assets; travel bans and extradition requests are routinely issued by the Supreme Court in predicate offshore abuse cases.
Failure to file annual returns and register maintenance

Statutory sanction: Late-filing financial penalties imposed by the Registrar, automatic strike-off proceedings and dissolution of the company; the protected cell and asset-shielding benefits of the exempted company are lost on dissolution.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held insolvency-liable for debts incurred after the strike-off and may be personally ordered to restore company assets; the Register of Corporate Insolvency disqualification applies for up to 5 years in Bermuda.
UK CFC and diverted profits exposure on the Bermuda vertical cloud structure

Statutory sanction: No additional Bermuda statutory sanction, but the UK parent attracts a CFC charge under TIOPA 2010 Part 9A at 25% on attributable profits and UK Diverted Profits Tax of 25% plus interest and penalties on the UK entity.

Director personal liability: UK HMRC may issue personal liability notices to directors who facilitated the avoidance, impose time-to-pay refusals and conduct criminal investigations under the UK Fraud Act; mutual legal assistance between the UK and Bermuda permits freezing orders, travel bans and extradition where evasion exceeds the statutory threshold.
