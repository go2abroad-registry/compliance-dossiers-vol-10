Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Registrar of Companies for corporate registration; Bermuda Tax Administration (Ministry of Finance) for direct tax and CIT registration; Bermuda Customs and Excise Department for import/export duty matters. Official portal: Government of Bermuda OneGov (www.gov.bm).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Bermuda exempted company is incorporated by filing a memorandum and articles of association with the Registrar of Companies. The Registrar issues a Certificate of Incorporation and a permanent Bermuda Company Number (BCN), which serves as the entity's primary tax identification identifier. No separate VAT or income tax number is issued unless the entity belongs to an in-scope multinational group under the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, in which case it must register with the Bermuda Tax Administration and obtain a Bermuda CIT tax reference number. The exempted company may separately apply for a Tax Exemption Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-owned Bermuda exempted company is incorporated by filing a memorandum and articles of association with the Registrar of Companies. The Registrar issues a Certificate of Incorporation and a permanent Bermuda Company Number (BCN), which serves as the entity's primary tax identification identifier. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda domestic law does not maintain a check-the-box election system. The relevant statutory device is an elective Tax Exemption Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966. For groups within the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, the Act contains mandatory and transitional election rules concerning the qualified domestic minimum top-up tax. A US taxpayer may file US Form 8832 to choose entity classification for US federal purposes, but that election has no Bermuda domestic tax law effect.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Customs_Import_Duty_Only__No_VAT_or_GST

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory VAT/GST registration threshold exists because Bermuda does not impose VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. An exempted company importing goods into Bermuda must register with Bermuda Customs and Excise as an importer regardless of turnover, and customs duty is assessed on the value of imported goods at applicable tariff rates.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Bermuda does not levy VAT or GST, so there is no export zero-rating or VAT refund mechanism. Export sales of goods or offshore services from Bermuda are outside the domestic indirect tax base. Physical exports may qualify for customs duty relief or re-export relief if the Bermuda Customs and Excise conditions are satisfied. For in-scope multinational groups, export profits remain potentially subject to the 15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax under the CIT Act 2023, notwithstanding the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Certificate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bermuda Customs and Excise Bill of Entry / export entry) where export processing is required
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Bermuda
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice proving receipt of foreign sales proceeds where exchange-control conditions apply

Bermuda does not mandate e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based continuous transaction controls for indirect tax purposes. Customs declarations for import and export are submitted electronically through the authorised Bermuda customs filing system where required; no VAT invoice clearance regime applies.

There is no VAT export zero-rating regime, so no VAT clawback is possible. However, false customs declarations or goods failing to satisfy re-export conditions can trigger reassessment, demand for unpaid duties, penalties of up to BMD 25,000 or the value of the goods, and forfeiture under the Customs Act 1962. For in-scope groups under the CIT Act 2023, erroneous reporting can attract penalty interest, late-filing penalties, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent conduct. The EUTPA tax protection certificate does not protect against penalties arising from fraud, misrepresentation, or breach of customs conditions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar under a fixed parity administered by the BMA. Both current-account transactions (dividends, trustee fees, employee payroll distributions) and capital-account transactions (equity injections, share redemptions, trust distributions) are freely convertible into BMD, USD, GBP, and EUR without prior BMA licensing, as Bermuda operates an open exchange-control regime with general permissions under the Exchange Control (General Exemptions) Order. Profit and capital remittance to the UK parent is unrestricted, subject only to standard AML/CFT reporting and bank-level due diligence. For the ESOP trust, no exchange-control restriction applies to inward trust funding from the UK parent or to outward distributions to employee beneficiaries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement; there is no mandatory conversion ratio and no prescribed repatriation timeline for export receipts or inward capital contributions. Funds remitted by the UK parent to the ESOP trust are treated as capital/open current transactions under the Exchange Control Act general permission, requiring no BMA consent and no forced conversion into BMD.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), operating under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969 and the Exchange Control Act 1972 as extended to Bermuda

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low feasibility for this ESOP trust. Bermuda does not host a licensed local electronic-money institution sector under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, and offshore digital EMIs (UK FCA e-money institutions or EU-licensed PSP/EMIs) are not recognized as licensed deposit-takers in Bermuda. Offshore EMIs may be used only as secondary payment gateways for GBP/EUR disbursements to UK beneficiaries, provided the primary clearing account is held with a Bermuda-licensed bank. The legal constraint is that EMI float does not constitute a regulated deposit in Bermuda and may introduce custodial and operational risk inconsistent with the Low_Risk_Standard classification. Recommended structure: a primary BMA-licensed bank account with the option of an FCA-registered EMI agent account for mass payroll continuity only.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Bermuda does not impose statutory transfer pricing documentation thresholds in local currency; Bermuda is not an OECD member and has not enacted a domestic transfer pricing regime. However, where the exempted company is part of a multinational group with UK or US operations, the parent entity's local transfer pricing rules (UK Part 4 Taxation (International and Other Provisions) Act 2010 with documentation thresholds of GBP 2 million for local file and GBP 50 million for master file) will indirectly apply, and Bermuda entities engaged in intra-group services must maintain contemporaneous documentation to satisfy substance reviews.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Amendment Act 2018 (as amended by the Economic Substance (Amendment) Act 2019) applies to Bermuda exempted companies carrying out relevant activities. Employee stock ownership trust administration is typically classified as a pure equity holding activity or as no relevant activity where the ESOP is held offshore. Pure equity holding entities are subject to a reduced economic substance requirement: compliance with Companies Act filing obligations and maintenance of a registered office in Bermuda. If the ESOP administration produces income from services sourced in Bermuda, the entity would be treated as carrying on a relevant activity and must comply with core income generation tests, including strategic direction and management in Bermuda, adequate physical presence, and adequate employees. Failure to satisfy economic substance tests triggers financial penalties and possible strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Facilitating Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: UK Criminal Finances Act 2017 corporate offence of failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion: unlimited fines and adverse public registry consequences; Bermuda Economic Substance penalties up to BMD 25,000 plus daily penalties and potential striking-off of the company.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for penalties up to BMD 25,000, be subject to disqualification orders, and face imprisonment for up to two years where they knowingly cause or permit the filing of a false economic substance declaration. UK civil proceedings may pierce the veil for sham arrangements, imposing personal liability for unpaid UK taxes plus interest and penalties.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance Amendment Act 2018: penalty of BMD 10,000 for failure to meet economic substance requirements, increased to BMD 25,000 and daily penalty of BMD 100 for continued non-compliance; the Registrar may strike the company off the register and cancel its exempted status, triggering immediate tax exposure and loss of limited liability protection.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are personally liable for penalties knowingly committed; non-resident directors who direct UK management may be subject to UK director disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986 where the company has abused its corporate form.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Bermuda Beneficial Ownership (Companies) Regulations 2018, fines up to BMD 25,000 and imprisonment up to two years for an officer in default; civil penalties apply per continuing breach and the company may be struck off.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate trustee may face extraditable money laundering charges if they knowingly conceal beneficial ownership to evade UK tax, giving rise to personal restraint orders, frozen bank accounts, and confiscation orders under UK Proceeds of Crime Act 2002.
Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalisation Abuse

Statutory sanction: UK HMRC can adjust taxable profits under the transfer pricing rules imposing additional UK corporation tax at 25% plus late payment interest; diverted profits tax may apply at 25% on diverted UK profits; penalties up to 100% of the understated tax are assessable for deliberate arrangements.

Director personal liability: Where a director knowingly signs false UK tax returns or creates fictitious invoices, personal liability for civil penalties and imprisonment for up to seven years under UK tax fraud provisions arises; the corporate veil may be pierced where the arrangement is found to be a sham.
