Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda; official portal: https://www.taxcommissioner.gov.bm. Bermuda Customs Department (Ministry of Finance) administers customs and tariff matters via the ASYCUDA World electronic clearance platform (https://www.gov.bm/customs).

E-government filing portal

Bermuda has no VAT, income tax, or corporate TIN regime. A Bermuda Exempted Company obtains a statutory Company Registration Number (File Number) from the Registrar of Companies at incorporation under the Companies Act 1981. For payroll tax purposes, the company must register as an employer with the Tax Commissioner and is assigned a Payroll Tax Account Reference. Foreign entities seeking customs facilitation must obtain an Importer Code from Bermuda Customs; no VAT ID exists. Tax protection status is evidenced by a certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Bermuda has no VAT, income tax, or corporate TIN regime. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Exempted companies may elect exempted undertaking status in their memorandum of association, entitling them to a statutory guarantee of no future Bermudian taxes on foreign-source income until 2035 under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966. No US-style check-the-box election is available under Bermudian law; US federal classification follows the Per Se corporation rules or an affiliated CFC/partnership analysis, and hybrid mismatch/hybrid entity elections are relevant only for UK-connected taxpayers under UK corporate tax rules. The Economic Substance (Substance Requirements) Act 2017 imposes a mandatory economic substance filing and compliance regime on entities carrying on relevant activities, including Distribution and Service Centre activities such as wholesale apparel liquidation trading.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/SUT system in Bermuda. The only indirect taxation applicable is Bermuda Customs Import Duty (ad valorem tariff) levied under the Customs Tariff Act 1978 and Customs Act 1911. There is no export duty and no sales or use tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. Any person importing commercial goods into Bermuda must register with Bermuda Customs for an Importer Code before the first import; there is no de minimis economic turnover threshold for commercial importers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Bermuda because there is no VAT/GST to zero-rate and no export duty on goods leaving Bermuda. A Bermuda Exempted Company engaged in off-price liquidation of branded overstock apparel enjoys statutory exemption from Bermudian tax on foreign-source profits; therefore no local zero-rating claim or export refund mechanism arises. For exports to the UK or EU, the zero-rating eligibility of a supply is governed by the destination jurisdiction's VAT rules (e.g., HMRC zero-rating of exported goods under the UK VAT Act 1994, section 30(6) and POBG regulations), requiring evidence of removal within prescribed time limits.

  • Bermuda electronic export declaration filed in ASYCUDA World
  • Commercial invoice describing the off-price branded apparel and declared value
  • Packing list with harmonized-system codes (e.g., Chapters 61-62 apparel headings)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export movement from Bermuda
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/ACH proof of payment for the exported goods
  • Signed purchase order or liquidation supply agreement with the overseas buyer

Bermuda Customs operates the ASYCUDA World system for mandatory electronic filing of import and export declarations, cargo manifests, and duty payments. All clearance is effected through authorised customs brokers/agents using EDI or the web-based ASYCUDA portal. There is no real-time fiscal e-invoicing, digital stamping, or invoicing clearance mandate, as no VAT system exists; customs declarations themselves serve as the fiscal control document.

Bermuda has no VAT zero-rating regime, so no retrospective VAT clawback exists. However, fraudulent customs declarations or misdeclarations of exported/imported goods are prosecuted under the Customs Act 1911 and Customs Tariff Act 1978: offending goods are liable to seizure and forfeiture, the declarant or exporter may be subject to monetary penalties equal to three times the value of the goods or the duty evaded, and directors may face criminal liability. Under the Economic Substance (Substance Requirements) Act 2017, failure to satisfy and prove economic substance for a Distribution and Service Centre activity (which includes wholesaling and storage of finished garments) attracts escalating fixed penalties imposed by the Minister, late-filing penalties, and potential striking-off of the exempted company; false substance declarations expose responsible officers to additional fines and sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) operates under a currency-board-style arrangement pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar (USD) with full convertibility in practice. Current-account convertibility is unrestricted: Bermuda is a UK Overseas Territory, and the UK's IMF Article VIII obligations ensure the absence of payment restrictions. Capital-account convertibility is effectively complete for non-resident entities: a Bermuda Exempted Company is statutorily designated 'non-resident' for exchange-control purposes, permitting free holding of USD, GBP, EUR and other foreign currencies, unrestricted foreign borrowing, and unapproved outward remittance. Profit remittance faces zero legal hurdles: no BMA consent, no withholding tax, no dividend tax, no stamp duty on dividends, and no capital-gains charge apply to the ISIC 4641 off-price garments liquidation trade. The only residual constraint is bank-level AML due diligence on the source and purpose of each material flow; there is no central-bank approval gate or 'circularization' requirement analogous to other emerging-market regimes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion regime applies to Bermuda Exempted Companies. Under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and BMA delegated authority, only persons resident in Bermuda are generally obliged to offer foreign-currency proceeds to an authorized dealer within a reasonable period (customarily one month). A Bermuda Exempted Company designated non-resident may retain 100% of USD/GBP/EUR export receipts indefinitely in onshore or offshore foreign-currency accounts. Mandatory conversion ratio: zero. The exporter must nonetheless maintain accurate commercial records (invoices, packing lists, bills of lading) to demonstrate the genuine trade nature of receipts for AML/ATF purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) - statutory central monetary authority, bank regulator, and administrator of the Exchange Control Act 1972 (Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969). The BMA exercises delegated exchange-control powers formerly held by the UK Treasury; no separate foreign-exchange administration bureau exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary operational flows; Low as a primary trade-clearing rail. Legal constraints: Bermuda does not license standalone e-money institutions under its own law; deposit-taking activity is reserved to holders of a banking license under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999. UK/EU FCA/PSD2-authorised EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Airwallex, Revolut Business) may onboard the Bermuda Exempted Company as a client outside Bermuda, but they cannot provide domestic Bermudian payment services, issue commercial letters of credit, or hold Bermudian escrow mandates. Many EMIs apply country-policy restrictions that exclude companies registered in offshore jurisdictions, making successful onboarding uncertain. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol: (1) Shareholder remits via SWIFT MT103 from a UK or international bank into the Bermuda bank's USD account; field 50K must state the full legal name of the remitting shareholder; field 70 must read 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - BM_EXEMPTED_CO'; field 72 must include the beneficiary account number and reference. (2) The remitting UK bank will classify the payment under BoE/ONS purpose code 'P0340 - Investment in Foreign Subsidiaries' or 'Capital Contribution'; no HMRC reporting or FEMA-style approval is required on the UK side. (3) The receiving Bermuda bank records the flow as inward foreign capital; no BMA exchange-control permit is required because the exempted company is designated non-resident. (4) Verification package: certificate of incorporation and certificate of compliance, register of members, board resolution authorizing the share issuance, share certificate, and SWIFT GPI tracking (UETR) for audit. (5) Recommended initial capital: USD 100,000-250,000 to satisfy bank appetite and demonstrate commercial substance; statutory minimum capital is zero under the Companies Act 1981.Primary clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for open-account supplier settlements (Bangladesh, China, Turkey, UK/EU) with full invoice and contract reference in field 70; MT700/MT707 for irrevocable, optionally confirmed commercial letters of credit issued through the Bermuda bank's trade desk (HSBC Bermuda and Butterfield typically require 1:1 cash collateral for the first year of LC issuance); MT420/MT421 for documentary collections where the brand seller and buyer maintain banking relationships. USD clearing is executed via the bank's New York correspondent over Fedwire/CHIPS; GBP and EUR clearings are executed via London and IBAN rails respectively. For the target rails in the mandate: 'Swift_Wire' is the standard inbound/outbound channel, while 'ACH Direct' is available only for domestic US collections (i.e., US buyers paying by ACH) through a USD sweep account at a US correspondent bank, not through the Bermudian entity itself; the Bermuda company cannot originate outbound ACH. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% standard Bermuda Exempted Company (no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, no capital duty); 15% for in-scope large multinational groups under the Bermuda CIT Act 2023 (Pillar Two QDMTT/IIR) for tax periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025 where group consolidated revenue is at least EUR 750 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (no Bermuda withholding tax on dividends paid by an exempted company; no advance corporation tax or dividend tax at source).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 60 days after the anniversary of incorporation, or within 60 days after the annual general meeting date where an AGM is held; exempted companies may dispense with the AGM by unanimous shareholder consent.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 6 months after the end of the company's financial year (Economic Substance Act 2018, s.8).

Deadline: · Frequency:

CIT return due within 15 months after the end of the CIT reporting period; associated CIT/QDMTT payment due within 6 months after the end of the reporting period (CIT Act 2023).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None for standard exempted companies outside CIT scope, because Bermuda does not impose corporate income tax and has no general transfer pricing documentation regime. For in-scope CIT groups, OECD-aligned Master File and Local File must be maintained where group consolidated revenue is at least EUR 750 million; BRA administrative guidance requires full contemporaneous documentation for controlled goods transactions exceeding BMD 1,000,000 per annum and controlled financing transactions exceeding BMD 2,000,000 per annum.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations 2018 (ESRA) apply. 'Distribution and Service Centre' is a relevant activity for the off-price apparel liquidation trade. The exempted company must demonstrate and maintain in Bermuda: (i) core income-generating activities (logistics, warehousing, purchasing, inventory management, pricing, sales, and distribution) performed locally; (ii) adequate full-time employees in Bermuda with day-to-day decision-making authority; (iii) physical premises in Bermuda; (iv) board meetings held in Bermuda with minutes kept at the registered office; and (v) annual operating expenditure in Bermuda proportionate to the income booked through the entity. Outsourcing all functions to an overseas parent or a third-party agent will cause failure of the economic substance test and trigger penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Offshore Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESRA penalty of BMD 250,000 for a first offence and BMD 500,000 for a subsequent offence; the Registrar may strike the company off, revoke the exempted certificate, and exchange information with UK HMRC and the EU under the applicable TIEA and the UK-Bermuda arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly caused the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of penalties; subject to restriction orders and travel bans; imprisonment up to 2 years under ESRA; disqualification as a director in the UK for up to 15 years under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986.
UK Tax Evasion / CFC Attribution

Statutory sanction: UK corporation tax at 19–25% on attributed profits, interest, and penalties of up to 100% of the tax for deliberate errors; unlimited fines for the company under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 offence of failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for CFC tax attributable to their deliberate conduct; imprisonment up to 2 years for tax evasion under TMA 1970 and the Fraud Act 2006; travel bans, asset freezing orders, and confiscation of personal assets.
AML / Sanctions Breach in the Liquidation Supply Chain

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Bermuda) Act 1997 and the AML/ATF Regulations 2008: fines up to BMD 100,000 per breach, asset freezing and forfeiture, revocation of the company's AML registration, and termination of banking relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failing to submit Suspicious Activity Reports; money-laundering convictions carry imprisonment up to 5 years and unlimited fines; civil restitution to affected victims; travel bans and seizure of personal assets pending trial.
Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Fine up to BMD 20,000 plus BMD 1,000 per day of continued default; suspension of the exempted status; restrictions on transfer of shares; potential Gazette strike-off.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly fail to provide BO information are subject to imprisonment up to 2 years and personal fines; courts may issue travel bans, restriction orders, and disqualification from serving as directors in Bermuda and the UK.
CIT/QDMTT Filing Fraud (In-Scope Groups)

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 150% of the avoided CIT plus statutory interest from the original due date; criminal prosecution for tax evasion with fines and public naming of the taxpayer; the BRA may also issue a summons for production of all transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: The senior accounting officer and every director signing the false return are personally liable for the penalty amount; imprisonment up to 5 years for fraudulent evasion; disgorgement of dividends received from the Bermuda entity; travel bans and freezing orders over personal assets.
Counterfeit Branded Merchandise Trade

Statutory sanction: Under the Bermuda Trade Marks Act 1974 and Copyright Act 2004: seizure and destruction of infringing goods, civil damages to brand owners, criminal fines up to BMD 50,000 per offence, and imprisonment up to 5 years; company registration may be cancelled as a matter of public interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for damages where the company is a façade for IP infringement; injunctions and freezing orders may be issued against directors; UK courts may enforce Bermuda IP judgments against UK-located assets of the directors, including personal residence and bank accounts.
