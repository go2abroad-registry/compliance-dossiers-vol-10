Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office in France under the French Commercial Code, including Articles L.123-11 and following. A domiciliation company may be used if it is authorized and the written domiciliation agreement satisfies statutory requirements, including a minimum-duration rule generally applicable to commercial domiciliation arrangements. A director's personal residence may be used within statutory limits. A virtual office is not sufficient by itself to establish operational substance, customs capability, transport-management capacity, transfer-pricing support, or tax residence facts. For transcontinental rail freight forwarding, the company should maintain documented personnel, contracts, operational procedures, customer and supplier files, compliance controls, accounting records and decision-making evidence proportionate to actual activity. No general French license is required merely to act as a freight forwarder or logistics intermediary, but rail carrier operations, customs representation, security-sensitive activities, transport of regulated goods and operations involving foreign sanctions may trigger separate authorizations or registrations.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI): formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; supplementary company and beneficial-owner data are maintained through the French National Business Register and accessible through INPI services.

Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation period equivalent to a 30-day reservation certificate. A proposed name may be checked through INPI data, the National Business Register, trademark databases, domain registries and relevant professional registers, but availability is not guaranteed until filing and does not eliminate third-party rights. If the INPI one-stop portal, registry reviewer or competent authority rejects or suspends the filing, obtain the electronic decision and stated defect, correct the name or supporting evidence, and resubmit within the portal. For a disputed administrative decision, submit a reasoned administrative challenge to the competent authority or registry within the applicable procedural period, generally seeking prior informal reconsideration or a recours gracieux, followed if necessary by a recours contentieux before the competent administrative or judicial court. A company-name infringement dispute is ordinarily handled before the competent commercial court, subject to the legal basis and claimant status. Obtain specialist French advice before using a regulated or geographically protected term.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France affords full convertibility of the EUR across both capital and current account categories pursuant to Articles 63–66 TFEU on free movement of capital. No exchange-control authorization is required for current-account payments, profit remittances, dividend distributions, or direct investment flows by an SAS to non-resident shareholders; the only statutory frictions are French withholding tax (flat 25%, or the reduced treaty rate, e.g., under the France–PRC and France–Kazakhstan DTTs) and the Banque de France statistical balance-of-payments reporting regime. The EUR is the statutory domestic payment currency under Article L.111-1 CMF, but French resident companies may lawfully open and maintain foreign currency accounts (comptes en devises) in CNY, USD, and RUB and may invoice cross-border freight services in any freely agreed currency. For an SAS engaged in transcontinental container block-train freight forwarding, capital injections (equity or shareholder advances) and outbound trade disbursements qualify under the EU capital-movement freedoms and require no prior French Treasury licence; the practical hurdles are purely AML/KYC documentary proof and EU/OFAC sanctions screening on Eurasian counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender duty and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A French resident SAS may retain freight-forwarding receivables in CNY, USD, or other foreign currencies in a comptes en devises held with an authorized French credit institution, or may convert freely into EUR. There is no statutory de-conversion timeline and no obligation to repatriate within a fixed number of days. The only mandatory requirements are (i) balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Banque de France, which becomes a direct reporting obligation for enterprises with annual cross-border flows exceeding the regulatory threshold (typically EUR 10 million of payments/receipts with non-residents), and (ii) full compliance with EU restrictive measures, most notably Regulation 833/2014 as amended, which can create de facto blocked-account treatment or mandatory refusal of conversion for proceeds originating from sanctioned Russian or Belarusian counterparties. In practice, the treasury policy should set voluntary daily EUR/CNY conversion bands and maintain CNY sub-accounts for settlement with Chinese railway platforms while keeping documentary proof of each underlying freight voyage.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the Code Monétaire et Financier, with prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment institutions performed by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for operational cash management and cross-border disbursement under PSD2 passporting: a European Economic Area-licensed Electronic Money Institution (e.g., licensed in Lithuania, the Netherlands, or Ireland) may provide payment accounts and payment services in France after lodging an ACPR passport notification and complying with French AML/CFT obligations toward Tracfin. Low-to-medium viability for the capital escrow function: French credit institutions generally require injected share capital to be held in a regulated deposit or escrow account (compte séquestre) maintained at an authorized bank, and EMIs cannot access TARGET2 settlement via Banque de France nor issue documentary letters of credit. Recommended architecture: a tier-1 French bank as principal house bank for escrow, L/C issuance, SWIFT settlement, and FX hedging, with one or two EEA EMIs as secondary disbursement rails for small-value, high-frequency payments to Chinese rail platforms and inland freight consolidators.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 credit to the SAS French IBAN (FR76 + RIB/BIC), with the French balance-of-payments motive code accurately reflecting the transaction nature: direct equity investment code for capital subscriptions, financial-operation code for shareholder advances (comptes courants d'associés), or loan/borrowing code for formal shareholder loans. The remitter must be the shareholder named in the underlying documentation, and the French bank will require the executed corporate resolution, loan or cash-pooling agreement, UBO declaration, and confirmation that funds originate from a bank account held in the remitter's name. Cross-border transfers exceeding the statutory threshold (EUR 10,000) are automatically captured by the bank for Banque de France statistical reporting.
