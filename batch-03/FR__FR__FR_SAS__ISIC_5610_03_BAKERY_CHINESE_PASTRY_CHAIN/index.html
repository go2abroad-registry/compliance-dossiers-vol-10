Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The articles determine the capital and subscription terms. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation; the balance may be paid within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed at incorporation. A deposit certificate from a bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations is required for cash capital before registration. There is no mandatory escrow account after registration.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain ERP fire-safety and accessibility approval from the competent municipality and Préfecture process before public opening where required. Maintain fire detection, extinguishers, emergency exits, electrical and gas safety, ventilation, and evacuation systems. Obtain landlord or municipal authorization for structural loads and heavy ovens, verify extraction and chimney compliance, complete required workplace risk assessment documentation, and implement occupational controls for heat, burns, flour dust, allergens, machinery, manual handling, and chemical exposure.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The articles determine the capital and subscription terms. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - French national tax authority; official portal: impots.gouv.fr; business registration portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique); e-invoicing/fiscal data portal: Chorus Pro (Portail Public de Facturation).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned French SAS: (1) The non-resident shareholder appoints a qualified representative, prepares the articles of association, and files incorporation via the Guichet Unique. (2) Incorporation automatically generates SIREN/SIRET registration through INPI. (3) The SAS must open a French registered office and obtain its numéro d'identification TVA intracommunautaire (FR + 11-digit key) from the competent DGFiP business tax office once taxable French supplies commence. (4) A foreign entity without a French permanent establishment that only makes B2B supplies into France generally does not need French VAT registration where the customer self-assesses under reverse charge; distance sales to French consumers may be reported under the EU OSS/non-union scheme. (5) A French SAS with non-resident shareholders must also file the annual tax return for legal entities and maintain an economic substance file sufficient to avoid constructive permanent establishment exposure under Article 209 I CGI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not apply a US-style check-the-box election. An SAS is automatically subject to entity-level corporate income tax (IS) on its worldwide profits to the extent the business is managed and controlled in France. Available statutory elections relevant to the SAS structure: (i) election to form a tax-integrated group / fiscal unity (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI where the parent holds at least 95% control; (ii) election for joint filing of VAT via the group VAT registration regime for closely-linked persons (assujetti unique) if conditions are met; (iii) no election is available to treat an SAS as a pass-through entity; and (iv) French hybrid mismatch rules operate automatically and non-electronically, with no proactive election available to alter the default mismatch treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general domestic economic turnover threshold triggers VAT registration in France. Any taxable person carrying out taxable supplies in France, including a foreign supplier, must register for French VAT unless the transaction benefits from reverse charge or the non-union OSS scheme for distance sales. The EUR 10,000 intra-EU distance-selling threshold concerns only cross-border intra-Community distance sales and does not remove French physical/natural place-of-supply nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of bakery, Chinese pastry, and food goods from metropolitan France to a destination outside the EU qualify for the 0% export VAT rate (exonération avec droit à déduction) under Article 262-I-1 CGI if: (i) the goods physically leave French territory for the non-EU destination; (ii) the transfer of ownership to the non-EU buyer occurs; (iii) the goods are exported under a valid customs export declaration; and (iv) export is completed within the statutory timeframe. Ancillary franchise royalty services, branding fees, and services directly connected with exported goods may also be zero-rated where the place of supply is effectively outside the EU or the services are directly linked to the export transaction under the VAT place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation EXA / DAU) with MRN number and customs exit validation
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or CMR transport document proving physical removal from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer proof evidencing settlement by the non-EU buyer
  • Commercial invoice describing the goods, quantity, unit price, incoterms, and destination
  • Packing list / weight certificate for customs and DGFiP verification
  • Phytosanitary certificate, veterinary/health certificate, or other agrifood export certificate where required
  • Buyer due diligence / KYC file confirming the non-EU status of the foreign customer
  • Certificate of origin or supporting non-preferential/preferential origin documents where applicable

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing under the French reform: from 1 September 2026, all taxable persons established in France must be able to receive e-invoices; large and mid-sized enterprises must issue structured e-invoices from 1 September 2026; small enterprises follow from 1 September 2027. E-invoices must be transmitted through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the Public Invoicing Portal (Chorus Pro/PPF) using structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII). Real-time e-reporting (données de transaction) is required for cross-border B2B/B2C flows and payment-related data. France does not currently use pre-approval fiscal stamping; instead, DGFiP performs centralised real-time data clearance and audit analytics through the PPF/PDP network.

DGFiP subjects export zero-rating files to stringent documentary audit. If the 0% rate is applied without timely and complete proof of physical export, or if goods are diverted into the French/EU domestic market, the VAT is retroactively clawed back together with disallowed input VAT. Late-payment interest (intérêt de retard) accrues at 0.20% per month, and graduated penalties apply: 40% for bad-faith declarations, 80% for intentional fraud or abusive arrangement, and criminal tax/fraud prosecution exposure for submission of false documents. Customs authorities may additionally apply seizure, confiscation, and customs fines where export declarations are irregular. Related-party export transactions, repeated credit-note adjustments, high-value pastry deliveries to franchisees outside the EU, and cash POS transactions receive heightened audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full convertibility for both current and capital accounts under EU and IMF standards. Profit remittances to shareholders are permitted without restriction provided supporting documentation is maintained for tax and regulatory purposes. Capital account transactions such as equity injections require prior declaration to the Banque de France via specific reporting channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

French exporters must surrender foreign exchange proceeds denominated in foreign currency to a French bank account within 90 days of receipt for most goods transactions. This rule applies to bakery-related exports of ingredients or finished products. Services and intangible exports generally do not trigger surrender obligations. All surrender transactions require matching commercial invoices, packing lists, and customs declarations.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore electronic money institutions face strict legal constraints in France under the Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the French Monetary and Financial Code. Cross-border digital payment solutions require local payment institution licensing or regulated partnerships, severely limiting full offshore EMI functionality for French entities.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €50 million consolidated group revenue for mandatory local file and master file

Economic substance scope: France has transposed EU Economic Substance Directive; applies to pure tax haven structures but not to genuine operating entities like bakery chain with local employees and operations

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Non-filing or late filing of tax returns

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines ranging from €750 to €75,000 plus daily surcharges of 0.5% of turnover; possible criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for tax debts; potential disqualification, fines, and imprisonment for willful non-compliance
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments increasing taxable profits by 500% of understated amount; penalties up to 5% of turnover

Director personal liability: Civil and criminal liability including restitution orders and possible jail sentences for fraudulent conduct
Underpayment of taxes or profit shifting evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion penalties including fines up to 500% of evaded tax and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability, asset seizure, and potential travel bans for directors involved in evasion
