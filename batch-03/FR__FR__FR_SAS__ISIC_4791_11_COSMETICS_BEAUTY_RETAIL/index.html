French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II — Articles L. 210-1 et seq. on general incorporation rules, L. 227-1 to L. 227-19 on the Société par Actions Simplifiée, and R. 123-31 et seq. on registration in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'entreprise (M0 formalities dossier) submitted electronically via the Guichet Unique; registration is evidenced by the Extrait Kbis.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS; the articles of association freely fix the capital. Cash contributions must be deposited and blocked in a regulated French bank until registration, and at least 50% of cash contribution value must be paid up at subscription, with the balance payable within five years.

Funding & unlock path: No civil defense or fire-safety certificate is required for an online-only head office. If the company opens a warehouse or customer-facing store, it must obtain fire safety inspection and approval from the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours, structural and electrical conformity certification, and DREETS occupational health and safety clearance before operation.

Not applicable to pure online retail of cosmetics; for any ancillary physical store or warehouse, the local urban planning and commercial licensing authorities (e.g., commission départementale d'aménagement commercial) and DREETS would exercise jurisdiction.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), operating via impots.gouv.fr; entity registration executed through the Guichet Unique and the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE); VAT and corporate tax administration by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); non-established foreign sellers register with the DGFiP's dedicated non-resident unit (SIE Non-Résidents).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SAS through the Guichet Unique to obtain the 9-digit SIREN (national identifier) and SIRET (establishment identifier) allocated by INSEE. Step 2: DGFiP automatically assigns a TVA intracommunautaire registration number (FR + 2-digit control key + SIREN) upon commencement of taxable activity; the number is activated for intra-Community declarations via the EORI-linked VAT dashboard. Step 3: For foreign non-established entities: file the specific VAT registration application with DGFiP (non-resident procedure), and if established outside the EEA, appoint a tax representative (représentant fiscal) jointly liable for VAT under Article 289 A CGI. Step 4: For online and cross-border distance sales, register with the French OSS (One Stop Shop) for intra-EU sales or IOSS for imports of low-value consignments not exceeding EUR 150. Step 5: Apply for an EORI number (FR + SIREN) for customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

SAS is by default subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, or IS) at entity level. Option for a passthrough-like income tax regime (Impôt sur le Revenu, or IR) is available under Article 239 bis A / 239 bis AA CGI: small, newly created SAS (less than 5 years old, fewer than 50 employees, annual turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, and at least 75% of capital held by natural persons) may elect IR taxation for the first 5 fiscal years; election must be transmitted to the SIE by the filing deadline of the first return. VAT election: French franchise en base de TVA (VAT registration threshold EUR 85,000 for goods) cannot be used for online marketplace-facilitated sales or by non-established sellers, but a resident SAS may apply it for small direct retail turnover. IT/export-related treaties and hybrid mismatch: France applies Article 212 bis CGI implementing ATAD; no elective hybrid-check-the-box mechanism exists in France and artificial hybrid structures are disregarded.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic established taxpayers: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for goods (franchise en base de TVA, Article 293 B CGI) before mandatory registration; for services the threshold is EUR 37,500. Non-established foreign entities: zero threshold; VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction in France. For e-commerce distance selling of cosmetics to French consumers: the EU cross-border threshold of EUR 10,000 (intra-EU distance sales) applies for OSS; sales above the threshold are taxable in France at 20%. Online marketplace rules under Article 283 CGI may deem the marketplace operator to be the supplier when it facilitates delivery through its platform (dropshipping); Shopify/PayPal as payment rails do not in themselves create the nexus, nexus is founded on the French establishment/warehouse and place-of-supply of B2C goods under Article 258 CGI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cosmetics (HS 3304/3305/3307) physically exported from France to a destination outside the European Union are zero-rated under Article 262-I CGI, provided title transfer occurred in France or the EU and the goods leave the EU customs territory within 90 days from the date of the relevant invoice (demonstrated by the export customs declaration). Intra-EU dispatches to VAT-registered businesses are exempt under Article 262 ter CGI subject to the buyer's valid TVA intracommunautaire number and the EC Sales List/DEB being filed. B2C distance sales to EU consumers are not zero-rated; they fall under OSS/IOSS and are taxable in the destination member state. Offshore services (e.g., formulation services, IT support, brand licensing for non-EU customers) may be zero-rated under Articles 259 and 259 A-D CGI where the recipient is established outside the EU and the benefit is located abroad; the invoice must state the reason for zero-rating (0% TVA - exportation / services hors UE).

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / DES H1 declaration) lodged in the DELTA-X customs system and the customs clearance receipt with the 10-digit export code
  • Commercial Invoice describing the cosmetics (US/HTS/HS codes 3304/3305/3307) and stating the Incoterms and export zero-rating clause
  • Bill of Lading (Connaissement maritime) or Air Waybill (LTA) evidencing the physical exit of goods from the EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Attestation de Change) or bank SWIFT/MT103 statement evidencing payment received in EUR from a non-French and non-EU account
  • CN 22/CN 23 customs declaration (for postal/parcel consignments) and, where applicable, the Proof of Export (AEP) or ECS documentation; for intra-EU dispatches, the buyer's TVA intracommunautaire number and the EC Sales List (Déclaration d'Échange de Biens - DEB)

France has adopted mandatory electronic invoicing (facturation électronique) phased under the Loi de finances 2024: from 1 September 2026, all VAT-registered businesses must be able to receive e-invoices, and large enterprises and mid-caps must issue e-invoices through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Partenaire de Dématérialisation (PDP); from 1 September 2027, small and mid-sized businesses (including typical SAS retail entities) must issue e-invoices equally. B2B invoices must use structured electronic formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII) with XML and mandatory electronic signature (Cachet); the PDP/PPF performs interoperability checks, and transaction data reaches DGFiP through e-reporting via API (no classical real-time tax clearance counter, but non-compliant invoices are rejected). Penalties escalate from EUR 15 per non-compliant invoice up to EUR 15,000 per entity per month for systematic non-transmission.

Export zero-rating claims are systematically verified by DGFiP through physical-goods-exit audits, reconciliation with customs data (DELTA-X), and review of the Attestation de Change. Failure to substantiate physical export within 90 days triggers retrospective recharacterization as domestic supply: VAT at 20% is clawed back from the original invoice date with statutory late-payment interest (intérêt de retard of 0.20% per month under Article 1727 CGI). Penalties for fraud include: 40% increase for bad-faith inaccuracies (Article 1729(a) CGI), 80% increase for fraudulent maneuvers (Article 1729(b) CGI), and criminal sanctions of EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment for tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI, plus publication of the sanction and referral to the Commission des Infractions Fiscales; refunds may be suspended and an audit of prior six-year limb can be reopened under Article L. 169 Livre des Procédures Fiscales.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current-account transactions (goods, services, income) and capital-account operations (investment, loans, dividends) in France, as guaranteed by Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the French Code Monétaire et Financier. For a French SAS, profit remittance (dividends, shareholder loan repayments, royalties) to EU/EEA investors is unrestricted; remittance to non-EU parties may require standard tax forms (e.g., Form 2766-SD for earnings distributed to non-residents) and must comply with EU sanctions and anti-money-laundering reporting, but no foreign-exchange approval or conversion quota is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists in French law. French exporters may freely invoice in any currency, retain foreign-currency balances, and convert them at their Bank of France-regulated financial institution without repatriation deadlines. However, the SAS must report all cross-border payment flows to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics when current thresholds are met (typically EUR 15,000 per transaction for the general monthly reporting), and the company must annually declare any foreign bank accounts on the French tax return (Article 1649 A CGI).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), together with the ACPR (Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority) for banking licensing/AML supervision, and the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for external financial policy. The European Central Bank (ECB) is the ultimate monetary authority for euro issuance and stability.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for a standard-risk e-commerce operator using EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Treezor, or passported EU EMIs like Revolut Business and Wise Business. Legal constraints: EMIs may provide payment services and a disputed-float account, but they are not credit institutions and cannot offer a secured capital-escrow account over a corporate investment; they also cannot provide overdraft or other treasury lending facilities. The SAS must verify the EMI holds a valid ACPR or passported license (Regafi registry) and understand that funds held at an EMI are not covered by the French deposit guarantee (FGDR). Offshore EMIs outside the EEA are practically unsuitable because they lack French IBANs and PSD2 passporting rights, which would create FX controls, sanctions, and tax-reporting friction.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Large proportionate gross turnover relative to declared e-commerce transaction values when using payment aggregators (Shopify Payments, PayPal), creating confusion between true sales and PSP settlement flows.

Root cause: Large proportionate gross turnover relative to declared e-commerce transaction values when using payment aggregators (Shopify Payments, PayPal), creating confusion between true sales and PSP settlement flows.

Defense evidence: Provide (i) the signed PCI DSS compliance attestation, (ii) commercial platform agreements with Shopify and PayPal, (iii) a 12-month transaction forecast with split of gross and net settlement amounts, (iv) audited or management accounts showing revenue reconciliation to PSP log files, and (v) a written operational explanation of rolling reserves and chargeback procedures.
Cross-border payment flows to/from non-EU markets, including beauty-supply imports from Asia, creating potential sanctions, customs, or tax-qualification questions.

Root cause: Cross-border payment flows to/from non-EU markets, including beauty-supply imports from Asia, creating potential sanctions, customs, or tax-qualification questions.

Defense evidence: Deliver an up-to-date matrix of active sales jurisdictions, customs import licences, EU/EC cosmetics compliance documents (Product Information File per Regulation EC No. 1223/2009), VAT and e-commerce-specific registrations (OSS/IOSS), and a confirmation letter from the bank's sanctions screening team that no shipment addresses are in restricted territories.
Non-EU beneficial ownership (UBO) held through a foreign holding structure, especially where the ultimate director is a remote individual without a French tax residence.

Root cause: Non-EU beneficial ownership (UBO) held through a foreign holding structure, especially where the ultimate director is a remote individual without a French tax residence.

Defense evidence: Submit a UBO declaration (under Article L.561-2-2 Monetary and Financial Code), certified passport copies, proof of residential address, an overseas tax file number, and a bank reference letter from the majority shareholder's own regulated financial institution. Additionally, provide legal documents demonstrating the source of capital contribution (e.g., approved shareholder resolution, notarized transfer agreement, or signed gift/sale contract).
Funds expected to be received from international payment providers (PSPs) that are themselves unlicensed in France, or where the PSP's settlement account is located in a third country not recognized under EU equivalence rules.

Root cause: Funds expected to be received from international payment providers (PSPs) that are themselves unlicensed in France, or where the PSP's settlement account is located in a third country not recognized under EU equivalence rules.

Defense evidence: Produce the PSP's FATF membership evidence, MIFID II/PSD2 registration, a copy of its French or host-country operating licence, confirmation that the PSP's settlement flows are routed through a French-licensed correspondent bank, and provide the bank with a technical integration diagram proving the PSP only acts as an intermediary and never takes legal title to the goods.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for an inbound shareholder loan or additional equity contribution: (i) send a SWIFT MT103 to the French company's bank, using the official IBAN and a detailed remittance field containing 'Capital contribution' or 'Shareholder loan per statutes, notarized resolution dated [date]'; (ii) include the Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment (commonly code 'C1' or 'D3' for capital transfers) in the message; (iii) transmit a copy of the SAS's Kbis certificate and an internal board/shareholder resolution authorizing the inflows and specifying the amount, currency, and final authorized capital; (iv) provide a source-of-funds letter for the sending bank; and (v) where the amount crosses the current statutory threshold for cross-border reporting, file the Banque de France direct-investment survey (RFE) within 30 calendar days. The sending party must ensure the deposit is made only after the bank has confirmed acceptance of the proposed account classification to avoid triggering anti-money-laundering review.For upstream supplier payments (cosmetic ingredients, packaging, finished goods), the standard clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for small and medium-value transfers (below EUR 250,000) with complete invoice references and commodity codes in field 70; (ii) Commercial Documentary Collections (URC 522) for medium-risk or new trading relationships; and (iii) an irrevocable Letter of Credit (ICC UCP 600) for high-value or first-time trade orders, especially with Asian manufacturers, ensuring the LC is opened at a tier-1 French bank with a corresponding-bank confirmation.
