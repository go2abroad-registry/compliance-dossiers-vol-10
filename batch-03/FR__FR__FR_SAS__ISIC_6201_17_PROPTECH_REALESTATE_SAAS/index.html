Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent Registry of the Commercial Court (Greffe du tribunal de commerce), following filing through the INPI-operated Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises; registration is in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-20 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); general company-formation provisions in Articles L. 210-1 et seq. and R. 210-1 et seq.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company-formation formalité submitted through the Guichet unique, including the signed articles of association (statuts) and required declarations and supporting documents; there is no separate statutory incorporation certificate to file.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; capital may be set at EUR 1. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of RCS registration. Cash contributions must be deposited before filing with an authorized bank, notary, or the Caisse des dépôts et consignations; deposited funds are released after proof of registration. Contributions in kind are generally fully contributed at formation and may require a contribution auditor, subject to statutory exemptions.

Funding & unlock path: No manufacturing-site clearance is ordinarily required for the stated activity. Office premises remain subject to applicable building and fire-safety requirements, including rules for establishments open to the public where applicable, and to French occupational health and safety obligations. Additional fire, structural, or environmental approvals depend on the premises, building use, and any regulated installation.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; capital may be set at EUR 1. Cash contributions must be deposited before filing with an authorized bank, notary, or the Caisse des dépôts et consignations; deposited funds are released after proof of registration. No industrial licensing board or manufacturing authority is ordinarily competent for the stated software-as-a-service activity. The INPI Guichet unique handles business formalities; the activity is not identified as a regulated profession or licensed industrial operation on the stated facts.

Separate authorization may apply if the company itself performs regulated real-estate agency, property-management, payment, or other regulated activities.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the official portal impots.gouv.fr; VAT registration and taxpayer identification are administered by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and the DGFiP central registry.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing or contracting with a French SAS must first secure a French SIREN/SIRET registration through the formalités entreprises.gouv.fr portal. For VAT, a Form CA20 must be filed with the competent SIE, after which the intra-Community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) is issued for EU-established operators. Non-EU-established suppliers may register through the non-EU OSS portal or by designating a statutory fiscal representative. The SIREN number is the national tax identification number (TIN) for fiscal correspondence and e-invoicing.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing or contracting with a French SAS must first secure a French SIREN/SIRET registration through the formalités entreprises.gouv.fr portal. For VAT, a Form CA20 must be filed with the competent SIE, after which the intra-Community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) is issued for EU-established operators. Non-EU-established suppliers may register through the non-EU OSS portal or by designating a statutory fiscal representative. The SIREN number is the national tax identification number (TIN) for fiscal correspondence and e-invoicing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in French law. An SAS is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (IS) under Article 206 of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI); it cannot elect pass-through status. Permitted elections include: (i) fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for a French parent holding at least 95% of eligible French subsidiaries; (ii) for certain leases, an option for VAT under Article 261 D CGI; (iii) elected territoriality choices in line with Article 209 I CGI concerning profits of foreign permanent establishments; and (iv) for hybrid mismatch purposes, no elective reclassification is permitted—ATAD anti-hybrid rules under Articles 205 and 212 bis CGI prescribe mandatory asymmetry adjustments rather than taxpayer elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 for principal goods suppliers and EUR 34,400 for service providers under the franchise en base rules; however, non-established foreign suppliers and OSS distance sales have no domestic local threshold and must register from the first euro. For intra-EU OSS distance sales to non-taxable persons, an EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies. PropTech SaaS digital services are taxed at the customer's place of establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération avec droit à déduction) is available for goods physically exported to a destination outside the EU under Article 262 CGI, subject to proof of transit via Customs Export Declaration. For PropTech SaaS services, zero-rating applies when the recipient is a business established outside the EU under Article 259 A CGI, or when the recipient is a non-EU final consumer receiving digitally supplied services under Articles 259 D and 283-2 CGI. Intra-EU B2B supplies qualify for the zero-rated intra-Community reverse charge under Article 262 ter (iv) CGI provided the customer supplies a valid VAT number and the service is not otherwise taxable at a fixed establishment in France.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / DAU) or Déclaration d'Échange de Biens (DEB) for physical exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or other transport evidence proving the goods left the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank/FX proof of payment repatriation for the exported goods or services

France mandates phased e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions and e-reporting for B2C transactions. From 1 September 2026, large and mid-sized businesses must issue and receive structured electronic invoices (Factur-X/UBL/CII) through certified Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires (PDP) or the Portail Public de Facturation (Chorus Pro); from 1 September 2027 the obligation extends to small and micro businesses. Invoice data must be transmitted to the DGFiP within one business day of issuance. Digital audit file (Fichier des Écritures Comptables) obligations continue to apply.

DGFiP audits export zero-rating claims with full documentation substantiation. If the export or offshore service is found fraudulent or unsubstantiated, the VAT improperly zero-rated is clawed back with penalty interest at 4% per month. Under Articles 1789 and 1790 CGI, a bad-faith assessable penalty of 40% applies; where the taxpayer deliberately concealed incomplete documentation, the penalty rises to 80%. Criminal fraud under Article 1741 CGI carries up to five years' imprisonment and a €75,000 fine (for a legal entity, up to €375,000 or five times the statutory individual fine). Customs code penalties for false declarations can also include confiscation of the underlying goods and fines up to ten times the value of the goods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and applies EU free-movement-of-capital rules. There are no general exchange controls or routine prior approvals for lawful current-account or capital-account payments, including ordinary cross-border investment, dividends, service fees, or loan payments. Banks must apply AML/CFT, sanctions, customer-due-diligence, and transaction-monitoring requirements. Dividend and other profit remittances remain subject to corporate-law distribution rules, available distributable profits, tax withholding where applicable, and documentation requested by the intermediary. Payments involving sanctioned parties, restricted jurisdictions, or regulated financial activities may be prohibited or require authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated and no mandatory foreign-currency conversion or surrender ratio. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign currency or in an eligible foreign account, subject to bank terms, AML/CFT and sanctions controls, applicable tax and accounting rules, and any statistical or reporting obligations applicable to the transaction. Cash or bearer negotiable instruments of EUR 10,000 or more crossing the EU external border must be declared under applicable EU and French rules.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France's central bank and participates in the Eurosystem. The French Treasury (Direction générale du Trésor) administers relevant financial and monetary rules; the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR) supervises banks and payment institutions within its remit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to high for routine collections and payments where the provider is authorized in France or passported under applicable EU rules. An electronic money institution is not a deposit-taking bank; safeguarded e-money is not equivalent to a bank deposit and may not carry deposit-guarantee protection. Confirm the provider's authorization, permitted services, client-fund safeguarding, French or SEPA IBAN availability, settlement limits, chargeback terms, and support for corporate capital-account requirements. Do not assume an offshore EMI account will be accepted for incorporation capital deposits or all banking counterparties.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The liasse fiscale must be filed within three months of the fiscal year-end; for a calendar-year SAS closing on 31 December, the deadline is 3 May of the following year, extended to 18 May when the return is submitted via the complete EDI e-filing procedure.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Each instalment is payable by the 15th day of the due month, or the following business day where the 15th falls on a non-business day; the balancing payment is settled together with the annual 2065-SD return.

Cadre 'Identification' (SIRET, NIC, APE/NAF) Corporate name, French SIREN/SIRET registration numbers, code APE (6201Z for computer programming), registered office address and legal-representative details of the SAS. Late or incomplete filing triggers a 10% statutory surcharge on the net CIT due, increased to 40% in cases of bad faith and 80% in cases of fraudulent maneuvering; late-payment interest accumulates at 0.20% per month from the day following the deadline; missing supporting schedules attract fixed fines of €150 each and the administration may proceed to automatic tax assessment where the return is absent.

Only revenue economically attributable to the French permanent establishment is reported; intercompany SaaS licence fees must be priced at arm's length and evidenced by contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. Late or incomplete filing triggers a 10% statutory surcharge on the net CIT due, increased to 40% in cases of bad faith and 80% in cases of fraudulent maneuvering; late-payment interest accumulates at 0.20% per month from the day following the deadline; missing supporting schedules attract fixed fines of €150 each and the administration may proceed to automatic tax assessment where the return is absent.
Box B — Bénéfice ou perte (résultat fiscal) Taxable profit or loss after reintegration of non-deductible expenses, application of depreciation and provisions, and reconciliation from accounting income via schedule 2058-A. The taxable result of a non-resident is computed under the same general rules but is limited to profits and expenses attributable to the French PE in accordance with Article 209 CGI and the applicable double tax treaty. Late or incomplete filing triggers a 10% statutory surcharge on the net CIT due, increased to 40% in cases of bad faith and 80% in cases of fraudulent maneuvering; late-payment interest accumulates at 0.20% per month from the day following the deadline; missing supporting schedules attract fixed fines of €150 each and the administration may proceed to automatic tax assessment where the return is absent.
Box Q — Déficits reportables (schedule 2058-F) Operational tax losses available as a deduction from future taxable profits, with the statutory annual cap equal to 50% of the taxable income exceeding €1,000,000. Only losses economically attributable to the French permanent establishment are eligible; head-office losses of the foreign parent cannot be set off against French PE profits. Late or incomplete filing triggers a 10% statutory surcharge on the net CIT due, increased to 40% in cases of bad faith and 80% in cases of fraudulent maneuvering; late-payment interest accumulates at 0.20% per month from the day following the deadline; missing supporting schedules attract fixed fines of €150 each and the administration may proceed to automatic tax assessment where the return is absent.
Box Z — Crédit d'impôt recherche (CIR) Research tax credit computed on qualifying expenditure: staff costs, depreciation of research assets, patent expenses and subcontracting costs related to R&D operations actually carried out in France. The CIR is claimable only where the qualifying R&D is effectively performed in France through the PE; the credit is recoverable as a refund where it exceeds the CIT due or where the company qualifies as a young SME. Late or incomplete filing triggers a 10% statutory surcharge on the net CIT due, increased to 40% in cases of bad faith and 80% in cases of fraudulent maneuvering; late-payment interest accumulates at 0.20% per month from the day following the deadline; missing supporting schedules attract fixed fines of €150 each and the administration may proceed to automatic tax assessment where the return is absent.
Line 'Transactions avec des entreprises liées' (statement n° 2257) Mandatory schedule disclosing transactions with domestic and foreign related enterprises: nature, volume, remuneration and transfer pricing method applied to SaaS licence fees, data licences, royalty flows and management charges. Non-resident groups must identify the related-party counterparty, the benefit test for the charges, and the arm's length method for each material intercompany transaction, failing which the administration may recharacterize the payments under Article 57 CGI. Late or incomplete filing triggers a 10% statutory surcharge on the net CIT due, increased to 40% in cases of bad faith and 80% in cases of fraudulent maneuvering; late-payment interest accumulates at 0.20% per month from the day following the deadline; missing supporting schedules attract fixed fines of €150 each and the administration may proceed to automatic tax assessment where the return is absent.
Part III — Acompte d'impôt sur les sociétés Quarterly prepayment equal to 25% of the estimated CIT for the current year, normally referenced to the preceding fiscal year's taxable base and disclosed CIT. A non-resident SAS with a French PE pays instalments under its French SIRET from a French euro account; for non-EU/EEA companies the instalment formalities must be performed through the appointed French tax representative. Late payment of an instalment draws a 5% late-payment surcharge on the unpaid amount plus 0.20% per month of late interest; a 10% correction may also be applied where the instalment base is under-declared by more than 10% of the final CIT liability.
