Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent Registry of Commerce and Companies (Registre du commerce et des sociétés) maintained by the territorial commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce), with filing submitted through the national business formalities portal operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 et seq. governing sociétés par actions simplifiées; Articles L.123-1 et seq. and R.123-1 et seq. governing commercial registration; Monetary and Financial Code provisions governing beneficial-owner declarations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of business formalities and application for registration in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS) through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, supported by the executed SAS articles of association and beneficial-owner declaration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 50 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; the articles must state the capital amount, and at least 50% of cash subscriptions must be paid upon incorporation. The balance must be paid within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with a bank, notary, or authorized depositary, which issues the certificate of deposit. Contributions in kind require valuation by a commissaire aux apports unless a statutory exemption is validly elected.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain vessel and terminal fire-safety approval from the relevant maritime authority, port authority, and, for land-based premises, the Service départemental d'incendie et de secours (SDIS) and competent prefectural or municipal authority. Secure structural, loading, lifting-equipment, electrical, and dangerous-goods inspections. Implement the French Labour Code occupational-risk assessment, workplace safety training, personal protective equipment, emergency procedures, and required liaison with the labour inspectorate and DREETS. Ro-Ro ramps, vehicle decks, cranes, fuel systems, and cargo-securing arrangements require documented inspection and maintenance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; the articles must state the capital amount, and at least 50% of cash subscriptions must be paid upon incorporation. The balance must be paid within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with a bank, notary, or authorized depositary, which issues the certificate of deposit. Contributions in kind require valuation by a commissaire aux apports unless a statutory exemption is validly elected.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), administered through the relevant Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and the official impots.gouv.fr portal. Customs and export formalities are administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) through the douane.gouv.fr and DELTA systems.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI). The filing generates the entity's SIREN and establishment SIRET identifiers and enables tax registration with the DGFiP. The company must submit its incorporation documents, registered office information, legal representative details, beneficial ownership information, articles of association, activity description, and bank details where required. The relevant SIE assigns or confirms the French tax account and corporate income tax obligations. VAT registration is requested from the SIE by submitting the applicable VAT registration information, expected taxable activities, intra-EU transactions, import and export activities, and banking details. A foreign entity without a French establishment generally applies to the designated non-established-business tax service or relevant SIE. A non-EU business may be required to appoint a French tax representative for VAT purposes unless an applicable legal exemption or mutual-assistance arrangement applies. EU-established businesses generally use the applicable French VAT registration procedure without a fiscal representative. The French VAT identification number normally uses the format FR plus a two-character key and the entity's SIREN number. EU VAT numbers should be validated through the VIES system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by default. A qualifying SAS may elect for income tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu) for a maximum period of five fiscal years, subject to statutory conditions including limits on employees, turnover or balance-sheet total, age of the business, ownership, and qualifying business activity. The election generally requires shareholder approval and notification to the tax administration within the statutory deadline. There is no general United States-style check-the-box election. French tax classification does not permit an unrestricted elective disregard of a SAS as a pass-through entity. Tax treatment of transparent or hybrid foreign entities is determined under French domestic law, applicable tax treaties, and anti-hybrid mismatch rules implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directives.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For French-established businesses eligible for the domestic franchise en base de TVA, the general turnover threshold is EUR 25,000, with a tolerance threshold of EUR 27,500 under the applicable rules. The thresholds are subject to statutory amendments and sector-specific rules. The franchise does not apply where the business voluntarily opts for VAT or where mandatory registration applies. A non-established business generally has no equivalent broad French turnover threshold: VAT registration may be required from the first taxable French transaction unless the transaction is subject to domestic reverse charge, a special scheme, or another statutory exception. Intra-EU acquisitions, imports, exports, and cross-border services may trigger separate registration, reporting, or customs obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced at 0% French VAT where the supplier can prove export and the goods have exited the EU customs territory. The supplier must retain the customs export declaration and reliable evidence of exit, normally the electronic export message or equivalent customs certification. Goods supplied to ships or aircraft may qualify only under specific statutory conditions, including the relevant vessel or aircraft eligibility and documentary requirements. Services supplied to a business established outside France are generally governed by the place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of French VAT rather than technically zero-rated. Certain transport, brokerage, logistics, and services directly connected with exports may qualify for exemption where the statutory conditions and documentary evidence are satisfied. For intra-EU supplies, the applicable regime is generally exemption with right to deduct, subject to valid customer VAT identification and proof of dispatch to another EU Member State. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, business-use evidence, and proof that the input VAT relates to activities giving a right of deduction.

  • Electronic export customs declaration lodged through the applicable DGDDI system, including the correct customs procedure, exporter, consignee, commodity classification, customs value, and transport information
  • Export Accompanying Document or equivalent customs reference and the electronic confirmation of exit from the European Union customs territory
  • Bill of lading, sea waybill, cargo manifest, or equivalent transport document identifying the goods, vessel, loading point, destination, and shipment date
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, sales contract, and purchase order or other transaction evidence linking the shipment to the zero-rated supply
  • Proof of delivery or destination evidence where required by the customs procedure or tax audit
  • Supplier VAT invoice showing the legal basis for the VAT exemption or zero-rate treatment and the customs declaration reference where applicable
  • For Ro-Ro vehicle transport, vehicle or cargo loading records, booking confirmations, gate-in or gate-out records, and carrier or terminal records capable of linking the vehicles to the export declaration
  • For services, contracts, customer VAT identification or establishment evidence, transport records, customer location evidence, and documents demonstrating the direct connection with an eligible export where an export-related exemption is claimed
  • Bank payment evidence or accounts-receivable records where relevant to substantiate the commercial transaction; France does not generally require an inward-remittance certificate as a universal statutory condition for export VAT exemption

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting under the Finance Act framework. All businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Large businesses and enterprises treated as ETIs must issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; small and medium-sized businesses must issue them from 1 September 2027, subject to legislative or regulatory changes. Invoices will be exchanged through an approved private platform (plateforme agréée), directly or through an authorized partner, rather than through a universal real-time clearance portal. Transaction data and, where applicable, payment data for transactions outside domestic B2B e-invoicing must be reported electronically to the French tax administration. Public-sector invoices are submitted through the Chorus Pro platform. France does not generally impose a universal digital-stamping requirement for export invoices. Electronic customs declarations and supporting records are handled through DGDDI systems, including DELTA, and must permit audit-level traceability.

France does not generally impose a universal statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption or zero-rating may be denied retrospectively where the supplier cannot prove export, EU exit, customer status, transport, or the commercial reality of the transaction. The unpaid VAT may be assessed together with late-payment interest, generally calculated at 0.20% per month under the applicable rules, and applicable tax penalties. Deliberate non-compliance may attract a 40% penalty; fraudulent or abusive manoeuvres may attract an 80% penalty, subject to statutory qualifications and procedural safeguards. Incorrect or fraudulent customs declarations may result in customs duties, import VAT reassessment, administrative fines, seizure, loss of simplified customs privileges, and potentially criminal proceedings. Serious tax fraud may also result in criminal sanctions under French law, including imprisonment and substantial fines. Input VAT deductions or refunds based on unsupported export transactions may be clawed back for the relevant audit period, with penalties and interest, and the taxpayer bears the burden of maintaining contemporaneous documentary evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a member of the Eurozone; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions under EU law and OECD liberalisation codes. There are no general exchange controls on capital movements, but certain reporting obligations apply for cross-border transfers exceeding €10,000 (or equivalent) to the French customs and tax authorities. Profit remittance (dividends) from a French SAS to non-resident shareholders is generally unrestricted, subject to compliance with anti-money laundering (AML) and tax documentation (e.g., Form 2561 for dividend withholding tax exemption under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive). For shipping activities, the tonnage tax regime may apply, but profit repatriation remains free. No prior authorisation is required for capital injections or trade settlements, but statistical reporting to the Banque de France is mandatory for balance-of-payments purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency earnings in foreign currency accounts (comptes en devises) or convert them to euros. However, all cross-border transactions must be reported to the Banque de France for statistical purposes via the 'Balance of Payments' reporting system (e.g., Form CERFA 14471 for direct investment, and monthly/quarterly declarations for trade transactions). There is no statutory timeline for conversion; the exporter may hold proceeds indefinitely, but must ensure compliance with EU sanctions and AML regulations. For Ro-Ro shipping services, the invoicing currency is typically USD or EUR; if USD, the company may maintain a USD account or convert as needed.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in coordination with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) and the Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR) for banking supervision. The Banque de France is the central bank and the primary authority for balance-of-payments reporting and foreign exchange regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. France recognises Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the EU PSD2 directive, but cross-border EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Qonto, or other EU-licensed EMIs) can open accounts for French entities. However, for a shipping company with high transaction volumes and LC requirements, traditional banks are more suitable. EMIs may lack full SWIFT correspondent banking capabilities for LC issuance and may have lower transaction limits. Legal constraints: EMIs must be authorised by the ACPR or another EU regulator; they cannot provide credit or hold client funds for extended periods. For Ro-Ro operations, an EMI may be used for operational expenses but not for capital escrow or large trade settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: The capital injection from a non-resident shareholder to the French SAS must be executed via SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code. For equity contributions, use the purpose code 'G300' (Investment in equity capital) or 'G301' (Investment in real estate) as per the Banque de France's balance-of-payments classification. The remitter must include the company's SIREN number and a reference to the capital increase resolution. The receiving bank will require a copy of the notarised minutes of the shareholders' meeting approving the capital increase and a certificate of deposit from the bank. For loans, use purpose code 'G200' (Loans and deposits). All inbound transfers must be reported to the Banque de France within 20 days of the transaction via the 'Rapport de Déclaration de Mouvement' (RDM) form. The bank will verify the source of funds and may request the underlying documentation.For trade settlements (e.g., charter hire, port fees, bunker fuel, and vessel maintenance), the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers and commercial letters of credit (LC) for large transactions. For LC issuance, the French bank will require a trade finance facility and a copy of the underlying contract (e.g., charter party). The LC can be issued in EUR or USD; the bank will handle the SWIFT MT700/MT707 messages. For regular disbursements, the company can use the bank's online platform to initiate MT103 transfers with proper purpose codes (e.g., 'S101' for trade in goods, 'S102' for services). All outbound transfers must be reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics. The bank may require supporting documents such as invoices, contracts, and customs declarations for each transaction.FX Forwards: Standard deliverable forwards (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) are permissible and commonly used to hedge freight revenue and operating expenses. The bank will require a credit line or collateral.FX Swaps: Currency swaps (EUR/USD) are permissible for longer-term hedging of capital expenditures or debt service. These are typically executed under an ISDA agreement.NDFs (Non-Deliverable Forwards): Not commonly used for EUR/USD because the euro is freely convertible; NDFs are more relevant for emerging market currencies. However, if the company has exposure to exotic currencies (e.g., in African or Asian ports), NDFs may be available but are subject to bank approval and may be restricted by French regulations. In practice, NDFs are not a standard instrument for EUR-based entities.Options: FX options (calls/puts) are permissible but may require more sophisticated risk management and are not listed in the schema; however, they are allowed under French law.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax. A 3.3% social contribution on corporate income tax may apply to companies with annual turnover of at least EUR 7.63 million and corporate income tax exceeding EUR 763,000, producing an effective rate of approximately 25.83% on the relevant tax liability.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident, subject to treaty relief, European Union Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief, domestic exemptions, and anti-abuse rules. A qualifying EU or European Economic Area corporate parent may obtain exemption where the statutory ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, and anti-abuse conditions are met. French resident individual shareholders are generally subject to a 12.8% income-tax withholding component, plus social levies under applicable rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on a date other than 31 December, the annual return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. For a 31 December year-end, the return is generally due by the second business day after 1 May of the following year, subject to the administrative filing calendar. Corporate income tax installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, with the final balance generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, or by 15 May for a 31 December year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A French entity is generally subject to full transfer-pricing documentation obligations where annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets are at least EUR 150 million, or where it belongs to a group meeting the applicable statutory threshold or has a qualifying relationship with an entity that meets the threshold. The documentation consists principally of a Local File and a Master File. A simplified annual transfer-pricing declaration may apply at lower thresholds, commonly where turnover or gross assets reach EUR 50 million or where the entity is within a qualifying group. Additional documentation may be required for controlled transactions and transactions with related parties in low-tax or non-cooperative jurisdictions.

Economic substance scope: France applies general anti-abuse rules, the abuse-of-law doctrine, anti-hybrid rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, beneficial-ownership principles, and specific anti-abuse provisions. France does not operate a single universally applicable economic-substance regulation equivalent to a standalone offshore ESR code. Substance is nevertheless material: the French SAS should maintain genuine decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or operational infrastructure, banking and accounting records, commercial risk, vessel-transport functions, and board governance proportionate to its activities. Treaty, EU directive, participation-exemption, interest, royalty, and principal-purpose analyses may fail where the entity is a conduit or lacks commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent accounting

Statutory sanction: Administrative tax reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties generally ranging from 40% to 80%, and criminal tax-fraud sanctions that may include imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000 for individuals. Aggravated cases may carry up to seven years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions. Corporate entities may face substantially higher criminal fines and ancillary sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur personal criminal liability, civil tax liability for fraudulent or repeated non-compliance, management-liability proceedings, asset seizure, and professional disqualification. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal proceedings. Imprisonment is possible for intentional tax fraud and related offenses.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Income adjustment, withholding-tax exposure, interest, and penalties. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation may trigger a penalty of at least EUR 10,000 and potentially the higher statutory percentage calculated by reference to the relevant transactions or reassessed income, subject to applicable French rules and caps.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for deliberate governance failures and criminal liability where documentation falsification, fraud, corruption, or money laundering is involved. Ordinary documentation failure does not automatically create personal tax liability.
Abusive profit repatriation and withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax, late-payment interest, penalties, possible 40% or 80% sanctions, and denial of treaty or EU directive relief. Payments involving non-cooperative jurisdictions may attract elevated withholding rates. The payer may be liable for tax that should have been withheld.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where they deliberately organized tax evasion, approved fictitious distributions, or breached statutory duties. Criminal exposure may arise for fraud, abuse of corporate assets, corruption, or money laundering.
Shell company, artificial structure, or treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or domestic exemptions, reassessment under abuse-of-law or principal-purpose principles, penalties up to 80%, interest, and possible criminal proceedings where fraud or concealment is established.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution, disqualification, asset seizure, and criminal sanctions where they knowingly implemented an artificial arrangement. Travel bans or reporting restrictions may be imposed by a court when legally justified; they are not automatic.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing violations

Statutory sanction: Regulatory sanctions, administrative fines, transaction restrictions, confiscation, and criminal money-laundering penalties. Money laundering may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 375,000; aggravated forms may carry up to ten years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 750,000, subject to statutory conditions. Corporate penalties may be higher.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners may be personally prosecuted, fined, disqualified, and imprisoned. Assets may be frozen or confiscated. Travel restrictions may be ordered during investigation or trial where authorized by law.
Customs, sanctions, and maritime trade violations

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, seizure, forfeiture, administrative fines, suspension of customs authorizations, sanctions-related blocking measures, and criminal penalties depending on the conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may be personally prosecuted for intentional customs fraud, sanctions evasion, document falsification, or complicity. Courts may impose fines, imprisonment, confiscation, and professional restrictions; travel bans depend on judicial orders.
False corporate records and insolvency misconduct

Statutory sanction: Restitution, asset recovery, bankruptcy-extension proceedings, director-management liability, personal insolvency restrictions, and criminal penalties for misuse of corporate assets, false accounts, or bankruptcy offenses.

Director personal liability: The SAS limited-liability shield does not protect directors from personal liability for fraud, gross mismanagement contributing to an insufficiency of assets, misuse of corporate assets, unlawful distributions, or criminal conduct. Courts may order civil restitution, disqualification, asset seizure, and imprisonment where applicable.
