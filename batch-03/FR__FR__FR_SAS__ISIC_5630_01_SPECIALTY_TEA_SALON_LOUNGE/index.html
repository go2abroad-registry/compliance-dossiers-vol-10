Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS; the capital is freely fixed by the articles of association. Where capital is subscribed in cash, at least 50% must be paid up at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. The paid-up portion must be deposited into a French bank account and blocked until the Kbis certificate is issued, after which it is released upon certification by the President.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: favorable inspection report from the communal Commission de Securite for a 5th-category type N ERP, covering extinguishers, emergency lighting, exits, alarm, and staff training. Structural load inspection: verification of mezzanine, terrace, or after-rigging elements to prove safe load capacity and operator accessibility. Occupational clearance: submission of the DUER to the DREETS, medical fitness of staff, first-aid equipment, electrical installation conformity, and safe food-handling workstations before opening.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not provide check-the-box elections or elective hybrid mismatch regimes. Taxation is strictly determined by legal form; SAS entities are subject to entity-level corporate income tax under the Code Général des Impôts with no elective options available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT rate applies to exports of goods to non-EU destinations or provision of services performed outside France for non-EU customers where the customer is not established in the EU and the service is not effectively used in the EU. Eligibility requires proof that the transaction constitutes an export and the customer is located outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of receipt of payment from non-EU entity

Mandatory electronic invoicing under the Facturation Électronique de Tiers regime for all B2B transactions since 1 October 2024, requiring compliant XML or PDF formats with embedded digital timestamps and stamps. Real-time reporting obligations apply via the PEPITES platform for qualifying enterprises; API integration with tax authorities is mandated for clearance, stamping, and compliance verification.

Fraudulent claims of zero-rating on exports may result in penalties of up to 40% of the unreported VAT amount, interest surcharges, and potential criminal prosecution under French tax law. Retrospective clawback of zero-rated VAT with loss of refund rights and administrative sanctions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to EU and French anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions, tax, customs, prudential, and financial-market rules. A French SAS may generally receive foreign investment, maintain euro and foreign-currency accounts, convert funds, pay foreign suppliers, distribute dividends, repay shareholder loans, and remit profits without a general prior foreign-exchange approval. Banks must verify the origin, purpose, beneficial ownership, economic rationale, and sanctions status of transactions. Dividend and other profit remittances require distributable profits, properly approved corporate accounts, withholding-tax analysis, treaty or EU relief documentation where applicable, and evidence that the payment is not an unlawful return of capital or prohibited financial assistance. Capital contributions and shareholder loans should be supported by subscription, loan, board, and accounting documents. Cash and payment-service restrictions, tax reporting, customs controls, and beneficial-owner disclosure obligations remain applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory obligation requiring a resident company to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, no general mandatory conversion ratio, and no universal statutory deadline for converting or repatriating commercial export receipts. Export proceeds may generally be held in euro or foreign-currency accounts and converted at the company's discretion, subject to contractual banking terms and applicable law. Export documentation, VAT and customs records, invoices, shipping evidence, payment records, accounting books, and transfer-pricing support must be retained under applicable French and EU requirements. Banks may request transaction-purpose information, commercial contracts, customs documents, and explanations of unusual balances. EU and French sanctions, anti-evasion, tax, customs, and anti-money-laundering rules may restrict particular counterparties or destinations. This assessment should be reconfirmed if France adopts emergency capital controls or if a transaction involves a sanctioned jurisdiction or regulated financial product.

Central bank supervision: European Central Bank for euro monetary policy and the Banque de France as France's national central bank and reporting, payment-system, and certain foreign-exchange supervisory authority. The French Treasury Directorate General of the Treasury, the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution, the Autorité des Marchés Financiers, the European Commission, and applicable EU sanctions authorities also regulate relevant banking, payment, capital-market, and foreign-exchange activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for euro collections, SEPA credit transfers, cards, expense management, and limited foreign-currency conversion, provided the provider is an EU-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution operating in France through passporting or an approved French framework. An EMI account is not a bank deposit and generally does not provide deposit-guarantee protection; safeguarded funds and client-money arrangements apply under the provider's licence. It may be unsuitable as the sole account for SAS capital formation, cash-intensive activity, merchant-acquiring settlement, large shareholder funding, trade finance, or transactions involving higher-risk jurisdictions. Offshore digital EMIs without EU authorisation, French passporting, or an appropriate local regulatory basis should not be used for regulated payment services to the SAS. The company must verify the provider's legal entity, licence, safeguarding model, service terms, settlement currencies, transaction limits, sanctions policy, and account-closure procedures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route paid-in share capital through a regulated French bank or other institution expressly accepted for SAS capital deposits into a designated capital deposit account. Use a clearly identified shareholder or parent account, not an unexplained third-party account. Provide the subscription agreement, articles, shareholder or board resolution, ownership chart, UBO evidence, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth evidence, payment instruction, and bank reference identifying the transfer as share capital or shareholder funding. For an EU euro transfer, use SEPA Credit Transfer where available; for non-SEPA or non-euro transfers, use SWIFT with complete ordering-customer, beneficiary, intermediary, purpose, and UBO information. France generally has no statutory foreign-exchange purpose-code regime equivalent to controlled-currency jurisdictions; use an accurate payment narrative such as 'paid-in share capital', 'capital increase', or 'shareholder loan', and comply with the bank's internal purpose-code and regulatory-reporting fields. Reconcile every incoming transfer to the subscription ledger and retain the full audit trail.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro suppliers within the SEPA area, subject to bank limits and sanctions screening. Use commercial documentary letters of credit, including irrevocable and, where appropriate, confirmed commercial LCs, for higher-risk suppliers or material shipments; define Incoterms, documentary conditions, inspection requirements, insurance, discrepancy handling, and sanctions representations. Ensure VAT, customs, sanctions, anti-bribery, and anti-money-laundering controls are aligned with the payment. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax rate. A qualifying small enterprise may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to the statutory ownership, turnover and paid-up-capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic French withholding rate on dividends and similar distributions paid to non-residents is generally 25%, subject to exemptions and treaty reductions. The EU Parent-Subsidiary regime may eliminate withholding where the statutory conditions are met, commonly including qualifying EU ownership, a minimum 10% holding, minimum holding-period requirements or an appropriate commitment, subject-to-tax status and anti-abuse compliance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual corporate income tax return and tax package are generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the standard filing deadline is the second business day after 1 May of the following year. The electronic payment of the final balance is generally due by the applicable filing deadline. Corporate income tax installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, subject to statutory exceptions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full French transfer-pricing documentation, including Local File and Master File concepts, is generally mandatory where the French entity's pre-tax turnover or gross assets is at least EUR 400 million, where it belongs to a group meeting the relevant EUR 400 million threshold, or where another statutory group or ownership condition applies. A French entity below the threshold remains subject to the arm's-length principle and may be required to provide simplified documentation and transaction evidence during an audit. The annual transfer-pricing declaration applies under separate statutory thresholds and conditions.

Economic substance scope: France has no separate general economic substance regulation equivalent to the UAE ESR regime. Substance is nevertheless relevant under the French anti-abuse doctrine, treaty principal-purpose and beneficial-owner analysis, EU anti-avoidance rules, controlled foreign company rules, transfer-pricing requirements, hybrid mismatch rules and abuse-of-law principles. The SAS should maintain real French decision-making, premises, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, commercial risk control, accounting records and business rationale proportionate to its specialty tea lounge activity. A conduit entity, artificial management-fee chain or debt structure lacking commercial substance may be denied treaty relief or deductions and may trigger reassessment and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, surcharges generally ranging from 40% to 80%, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that may include imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with enhanced penalties in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: The SAS normally shields shareholders and directors from ordinary corporate debts, but directors may incur personal civil liability for management faults, tax-fraud participation, asset diversion or insolvency misconduct. Criminal liability is personal; imprisonment, criminal fines, professional prohibitions and judicial restrictions may apply. Travel restrictions are not automatic and require a specific judicial or enforcement basis.
VAT fraud and cash-register suppression

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, interest, applicable tax surcharges, penalties for inaccurate or fraudulent returns, possible seizure or closure measures in serious cases, and criminal prosecution where intentional fraud is established.

Director personal liability: A director who personally organizes or knowingly approves VAT fraud may be criminally liable and may face director-liability proceedings, tax-payment liability under applicable French rules and disqualification from management. Corporate protection does not protect deliberate personal participation.
Employment, payroll and undeclared-work violations

Statutory sanction: Back payment of wages, social contributions, payroll taxes and interest; administrative and criminal penalties for travail dissimulé; loss of public subsidies and procurement eligibility; and possible business closure or exclusion measures.

Director personal liability: The responsible director or de facto manager may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, management bans and liability for unpaid contributions where statutory conditions are met. Directors may also be liable for employee claims if insolvency or deliberate misconduct is established.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and non-arm's-length related-party charges

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment, denial of deductions, withholding-tax exposure, interest, documentation penalties and applicable surcharges. France may also apply transfer-pricing penalties for failure to provide required documentation or declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil or criminal liability where they knowingly participate in fraudulent profit diversion, false invoicing or abuse of corporate assets. Ordinary tax adjustment alone does not automatically create personal liability.
Beneficial ownership, treaty abuse and shell-company conduct

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, collection of withholding tax, interest, 40% or 80% tax surcharges where applicable, abuse-of-law consequences and possible criminal fraud charges.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly submit false treaty documentation or implement a sham arrangement may face personal criminal and civil consequences, including fines, imprisonment in aggravated cases and management disqualification. Travel bans require a specific judicial order.
Anti-money-laundering and suspicious-payment failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative enforcement, transaction restrictions, confiscation, criminal money-laundering penalties and severe fines. Cash-payment limits applicable to professionals and consumers must be observed; the general professional cash-payment limit is EUR 1,000, subject to statutory exceptions.

Director personal liability: Knowingly facilitating laundering may result in personal criminal liability, imprisonment, fines, confiscation and management restrictions. Failure to implement reasonable controls may expose the director to regulatory and civil consequences even without proof of the underlying predicate offense.
Unlicensed alcohol, food-safety or public-premises operation

Statutory sanction: Administrative closure, licence suspension or withdrawal, fines, product seizure and criminal penalties for serious food-safety or licensing breaches.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and operational manager may be prosecuted personally for intentional or negligent breaches. Insurance coverage may be impaired where mandatory licences or safety procedures were absent.
Distribution while insolvent or abuse of corporate assets

Statutory sanction: Invalidation or recovery of distributions, repayment claims, tax reassessment, insolvency clawback, liability for insufficiency of assets and possible bankruptcy-related criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for an insufficiency of assets, management bans, personal contribution claims and criminal liability for misuse of corporate assets or fraudulent bankruptcy. The corporate veil may be disregarded only on legally established grounds, not merely because the company has tax debts.
