Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory 30-day company-name reservation mechanism for an SAS. A proposed name is normally used in the incorporation filing after clearance; an optional temporary name-protection or reservation arrangement with a private domiciliation, legal, or trademark service provider is contractual and not equivalent to statutory registry reservation. If the Guichet unique, INPI, registry authority, or another competent authority rejects or suspends the filing, obtain the written deficiency or refusal notice, correct the name or submit evidence of distinctiveness and authorization, and resubmit through the Guichet unique within the stated deadline. Where a formal registry refusal is maintained, request the legally available review or referral and pursue the applicable appeal before the competent commercial court or administrative court, depending on the issuing authority and nature of the decision. Separately assess trademark opposition, infringement, and unfair-competition exposure before using the name.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member and maintains full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. The euro is freely convertible, and EU Treaty free-movement-of-capital provisions apply. There is no general French foreign-exchange licensing regime for holding, converting, or repatriating foreign currency. Profit remittance from a French SAS to non-resident shareholders is not subject to exchange control approval. The practical hurdles are not convertibility but rather: (i) balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Banque de France for external transactions above the applicable threshold; (ii) bank anti-money-laundering scrutiny over the source of funds; (iii) French withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties paid to non-residents unless reduced by a tax treaty or the EU parent-subsidiary directive; and (iv) possible prior authorization under French foreign investment control rules if the non-EU shareholder invests in a sensitive French infrastructure activity. For an urban light rail EPC contractor, the transport-network character of the project should be assessed as potentially sensitive, especially if the project involves safety-critical catenary systems or the operation of transport infrastructure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender requirement on export proceeds. There is no mandatory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to liquidate foreign-currency receipts into euros. Export receipts may be retained in the currency of receipt, held in a French or foreign bank account, or converted at the commercial bank's discretion. The only material obligation is statistical reporting: resident entities must support the reporting of external transactions to Banque de France through their bank for operations above the balance-of-payments reporting threshold, generally EUR 50,000 per transaction. Supporting invoices, contracts, and customs declarations must be retained to evidence the nature and economic purpose of each export receipt.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France; Direction Générale du Trésor within the French Ministry of Economy and Finance for foreign investment control; ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) for banking prudential supervision; AMF (Autorité des Marchés Financiers) for financial market and derivative conduct regulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for incidental EUR operating flows and multi-currency visibility, but Low as the primary banking rail for an EPC infrastructure treasury. French and EU passportable electronic money institutions (EMIs) can issue payment accounts and process payments under PSD2, but they may not take deposits, may not grant credit, and generally cannot issue documentary letters of credit, on-demand guarantees, or hold sovereign-bond escrow structures in the manner of a French regulated credit institution. A public-works escrow and cross-border trade settlement structure should therefore be held at a full French credit institution or a large EU bank with a French branch. EMIs may be used only for small operational disbursements after approval by the main relationship bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

EUR capital injections should be routed by the non-resident shareholder to the SAS IBAN through the T2/Single Euro Payments Area clearing system, using a SEPA credit transfer or a high-value TARGET2 payment with a clear end-to-end reference, such as 'equity subscription' or 'shareholder loan under EPC finance plan'. For non-EUR currencies, instruct a SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer with the full beneficiary name, IBAN/BIC, UETR, and a purpose code or narrative identifying the transaction. The receiving French bank will request source-of-funds evidence, board resolutions, the subscribed capital work-flow, and proof of the underlying EPC contract. The bank will also include the transaction in its balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Banque de France if the amount exceeds the reporting threshold. Where the investor is outside the EU/EEA and the rail-transit scope may qualify as a sensitive activity, obtain prior foreign-investment authorisation from the Direction Générale du Trésor before effecting the inbound transfer. If a government bond is provided as employer security, do not treat it as operating cash; it should be held in a dedicated escrow account and released only against work certificates and joint instructions.EUR disbursements to subcontractors and vendors within France, the EU, or the EEA should be made by SEPA credit transfer using IBAN/BIC and a project-linked payment reference. For non-EUR or non-SEPA disbursements, the appropriate rail is SWIFT MT103 for commercial payments and MT202/MT202COV for correspondent bank cover. For international equipment purchases requiring payment security, the SAS should use documentary letters of credit issued under UCP600, communicated by SWIFT MT700, or standby letters of credit and first-demand guarantees under MT760. For public-works escrow drawdowns, payment should be made only against signed work-acceptance certificates, progress invoices, and any other conditions stated in the escrow agreement. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A qualifying small company may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to the statutory ownership, turnover and paid-in-capital conditions; the standard 25% rate applies above that amount.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic French withholding tax rate on dividends and similar distributions paid to a non-resident is generally 25%, subject to treaty reduction, statutory exemptions and applicable anti-abuse rules. Qualifying EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief may eliminate withholding where the parent, holding-period, subject-to-tax, legal-form and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. French resident corporate shareholders may qualify for the parent-company regime, generally excluding 95% of qualifying dividends with a 5% expense-and-charge add-back, subject to statutory conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the annual return and supporting tax package are generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the following year. For other year-ends, the return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The final corporate income tax balance is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month following the year-end, subject to the statutory calendar and electronic-payment rules. Instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, subject to applicable exceptions and amount thresholds.

Instalments are generally payable by 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following the financial year-end. A newly incorporated company or a company below statutory thresholds may qualify for limited instalment relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File documentation is generally mandatory for French entities or French permanent establishments meeting the EUR 150 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, or belonging to a group meeting the relevant statutory conditions. A simplified documentation obligation may apply below that threshold. The annual transfer-pricing summary declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies at a lower EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold or where the entity belongs to a group meeting the statutory criteria. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a single standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime equivalent to certain offshore ESR frameworks. Substance is nevertheless material under French transfer-pricing law, the general anti-abuse rule, beneficial-ownership principles, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, treaty anti-abuse provisions, principal-purpose-test standards, VAT rules and permanent-establishment analysis. A French SAS performing urban rail EPC activities should maintain real personnel, decision-making capacity, technical capability, project controls, premises, assets, risks and documented functions in France where those functions are claimed. Conduit entities, artificial management charges, circular financing, pass-through royalties and contractual allocations inconsistent with actual conduct are high-risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax fraud and falsified tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Additional tax, 40% or 80% administrative penalties depending on the conduct, late-payment interest and criminal tax-fraud sanctions. Ordinary tax fraud may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000; aggravated cases may carry up to seven years' imprisonment and fines of up to EUR 3 million, subject to the applicable facts and statutory provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, imprisonment, prohibition from managing a business, civil liability for deliberate or serious management misconduct and, in insolvency, contribution to insufficient assets. Asset freezing and travel restrictions may arise through criminal or enforcement proceedings; veil piercing is exceptional and fact-dependent.
Shell company, artificial arrangement and beneficial-ownership abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions, treaty benefits or tax exemptions; reassessment under the abuse-of-law doctrine; withholding tax at domestic rates; penalties potentially up to 80% for abusive or fraudulent conduct; late-payment interest; and possible criminal proceedings where false documents or tax fraud are involved.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil, tax and criminal liability for knowing participation, misrepresentation or management misconduct. Criminal confiscation, management bans, asset freezes and imprisonment may apply where the arrangement forms part of tax fraud, money laundering or organized fraud.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to arm's-length taxable income, denial of deductions, withholding-tax reassessment, late-payment interest, documentation penalties and potential failure-to-file penalties for the transfer-pricing declaration. A documentation failure may impair the taxpayer's defense and lead to a deemed or estimated assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for deliberate fraud, abusive management decisions, concealed distributions or insolvency-related misconduct. Ordinary pricing disagreement normally creates corporate tax liability rather than automatic personal liability.
Undeclared French permanent establishment or dependent-agent activity

Statutory sanction: French corporate income tax, VAT, payroll and social-security assessments, withholding tax where applicable, interest, penalties and possible business-tax obligations. Treaty protection may be denied where the factual permanent-establishment threshold is met or anti-fragmentation principles apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for intentional concealment, false filings, unpaid employee taxes or social contributions, and insolvency misconduct. Criminal sanctions are fact-dependent and are not automatic merely because a permanent establishment was incorrectly classified.
VAT and invoice fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, denial of input VAT, interest and penalties generally ranging from 40% for deliberate conduct to 80% for fraudulent maneuvers, with possible criminal prosecution for organized or substantial fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face criminal fines, imprisonment, management bans, personal liability for unpaid tax in exceptional circumstances and civil claims for losses caused by misconduct.
Money laundering, terrorist financing and AML failures

Statutory sanction: Regulatory, administrative and criminal sanctions may include fines, confiscation, business restrictions and imprisonment. Money laundering may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with aggravated cases carrying higher penalties, subject to the applicable French Criminal Code provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be prosecuted for knowing participation or facilitation. Personal fines, imprisonment, confiscation, asset freezes, management bans and travel restrictions may apply through judicial proceedings.
Corruption, bribery and public-procurement misconduct

Statutory sanction: Criminal corruption penalties may include imprisonment, substantial fines, confiscation, exclusion from public procurement and corporate compliance orders. Companies may also face contract termination, debarment and reputational sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors, employees, intermediaries and beneficial owners may face personal criminal prosecution, imprisonment, fines, confiscation, management bans and travel restrictions. Corporate liability does not prevent individual liability.
Unlawful distributions and disguised shareholder benefits

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, corporate-tax reassessment, withholding tax, penalties, director liability and potential criminal sanctions for misuse of corporate assets or false accounting.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore assets, compensate creditors and shareholders, and may face criminal prosecution for misuse of corporate property, breach of corporate duties or insolvency misconduct.
Sanctions, export controls and prohibited counterparties

Statutory sanction: Transaction blocking, asset freezing, administrative and criminal fines, confiscation, procurement exclusion and imprisonment may apply under EU and French sanctions law.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance personnel may face personal criminal liability for knowing or reckless breaches, including fines, imprisonment, asset freezes, management bans and travel restrictions.
