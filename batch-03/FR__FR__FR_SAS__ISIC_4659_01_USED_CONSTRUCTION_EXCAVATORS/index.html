Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official tax portal impots.gouv.fr. Customs registrations and export formalities are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI) through the French customs portal douane.gouv.fr. The European Union VAT identification number is issued and administered by the DGFiP.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is incorporated through the guichet unique operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI). The registration process results in allocation of a SIREN number for the legal entity, a SIRET number for each establishment, and an APE activity code. The company must submit its tax information and business commencement details to the competent SIE through the applicable registration and activity forms. Where the company carries out transactions giving rise to French VAT liability, it must request a French VAT identification number from the SIE. The VAT number generally uses the format FR plus a two-digit validation key and the nine-digit SIREN number. A non-established foreign entity must register with the designated French foreign-business tax service or competent SIE where it is liable for French VAT and cannot rely on a reverse-charge rule. A non-EU entity generally must appoint a French fiscal representative unless an applicable legal or treaty exemption applies. An EORI number must be obtained from French customs for customs clearance, import, export, or other customs activity. VIES validation should be completed for intra-EU transactions. Registration does not itself determine VAT liability; the place-of-supply, importation, domestic supply, export, and reverse-charge rules must be tested transaction by transaction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS) by default. A qualifying SAS may elect temporarily for income tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, or IR) if statutory conditions are met, including conditions relating to business activity, ownership, employee headcount, turnover or balance-sheet size, age of the company, and ownership by individuals or qualifying entities. The election is generally limited to five fiscal years and is subject to notification deadlines; it is not an unrestricted United States-style check-the-box election. A qualifying SAS may also be treated as a société de personnes in limited circumstances where statutory conditions are satisfied. France has no general domestic check-the-box regime. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive requirements and can deny deductions, impose inclusion, or restrict relief where hybrid instruments, hybrid entities, imported mismatches, or branch mismatches produce a non-taxation outcome. These rules do not create a general elective entity-classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the French VAT franchise regime applicable to commercial activities, the principal annual turnover threshold is EUR 85,000, with a tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500 under the applicable statutory conditions. The principal thresholds for services are EUR 37,500 and EUR 41,250. These thresholds concern eligibility for the franchise en base and are not a universal safe harbor for non-established businesses. A foreign business may be required to register from the first taxable transaction in France where no domestic reverse charge, distance-selling simplification, or other special regime applies. Intra-EU distance sales are generally subject to the EUR 10,000 Union-wide threshold, subject to the One Stop Shop regime. Import VAT is not subject to the domestic turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to a destination outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT with the right to deduct input VAT where the supplier, the supplier's customs representative, or an authorized intermediary can substantiate that the goods physically left the EU customs territory and that the statutory export conditions were met. The export declaration must identify the transaction and the customs exit must be confirmed electronically through the customs export control system. The seller must retain the commercial invoice, transport evidence, export declaration data, exit confirmation, and accounting records linking the goods to the invoice. Intra-EU supplies of goods may qualify for the zero-rate exemption where the customer is VAT-identified in another Member State, the goods are dispatched or transported to that Member State, the supplier reports the transaction correctly, and the supplier retains transport evidence. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore. Their treatment depends on the place-of-supply rules, customer status, use-and-enjoyment provisions, and any applicable exemption. The margin scheme for qualifying second-hand goods generally does not provide a conventional recoverable-input-VAT export profile; the resale status and acquisition history must be documented before applying any export or margin treatment.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the seller, purchaser, goods, values, VAT treatment, and transaction terms
  • Electronic export declaration submitted through the EU customs system, including the correct commodity code, customs procedure, customs value, origin information where required, and exporter or representative details
  • Master Reference Number or equivalent export declaration reference
  • Electronic confirmation that the goods exited the EU customs territory, including the exit results or exit accompanying record
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route, such as bill of lading, sea waybill, CMR consignment note, air waybill, rail consignment note, freight-forwarder certificate, or carrier delivery evidence
  • Packing list and serial-number, chassis-number, engine-number, or equipment-identification records for used excavators and rollers
  • Proof of delivery or import clearance in the destination country where available and commercially appropriate
  • Accounting records, inventory records, purchase documents, and customer contracts linking each exported machine to the export declaration and invoice
  • Import VAT and customs documentation for any prior importation where input VAT recovery or customs valuation is relevant
  • Bank payment evidence or other commercial settlement evidence where needed to corroborate the transaction; an inward-remittance certificate is not a general French statutory export-VAT requirement

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through registered private platforms (plateformes de dématérialisation partenaires, or PDPs), with the public portal PPF serving principally as a directory and reporting infrastructure under the revised implementation framework. From 1 September 2026, all French businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; large and mid-sized businesses must issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from that date. Small and micro-enterprises must generally issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from 1 September 2027. Electronic reporting of relevant B2C and cross-border transactions is phased in with the same implementation timetable. The system uses structured or hybrid invoice formats and mandatory data fields, including transaction, payment, VAT, and party information as applicable. France does not generally require real-time pre-clearance of every invoice before issuance. Invoices to French public-sector entities are submitted through Chorus Pro. Until the applicable mandate date, ordinary French invoice, record-retention, authenticity, integrity, and legibility requirements remain applicable. Used construction machinery transactions may require specific VAT treatment, including margin-scheme analysis for qualifying second-hand goods and reverse-charge analysis for qualifying cross-border or domestic transactions.

France does not impose a general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export proceeds should nevertheless be recorded and collected through normal accounting, banking, anti-money-laundering, customs, and transfer-pricing controls. If export exemption or zero-rate treatment is unsupported, the French tax administration may assess the VAT that should have been charged, together with late-payment interest and tax penalties. The principal penalty may be increased for deliberate failure, abuse of law, fraudulent conduct, or concealed activity; the highest fraudulent-evasion penalties can reach 80 percent of the reassessed tax, subject to the applicable statutory classification and procedural safeguards. Input VAT refunds may be denied or clawed back where export evidence is incomplete, inconsistent, fabricated, or not linked to the relevant goods. Customs irregularities may generate customs debt, import VAT reassessment, administrative fines, seizure or forfeiture measures, and potential criminal exposure for serious fraud, false declarations, smuggling, or document falsification. Voluntary correction, contemporaneous evidence preservation, accurate VAT returns, and reconciliation of invoice, inventory, customs, transport, and bank records are required to defend the exemption.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a euro-area member, maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Regulation (EC) No 1889/2005 and the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63-66. The euro is freely convertible for trade, capital movements, and profit remittances. No prior exchange-control approval is required for inbound equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, trade settlement, or dividend repatriation by a non-resident shareholder of a French SAS. Residual restrictions apply exclusively to (i) financial dealings with sanctioned jurisdictions/individuals under EU/UN sanctions, (ii) gold and cash movement declarations above EUR 10,000, and (iii) balance-of-payments statistical reporting to Banque de France for direct investment transactions exceeding EUR 15,000,000 annually or on an ad hoc basis where the counterparty is non-resident. Legal hurdles to profit remittance are negligible, but the bank must receive standard documentary evidence (audited financial statements, tax clearance, UBO declarations) and must file an 'indicateur de la balance des paiements' on the client's behalf.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds applies in France. Export receipts from used construction machinery may be retained in EUR or in any foreign currency account with a licensed credit institution. For foreign-currency accounts, the exporting French entity must comply with the Banque de France monthly reporting threshold (Règlement BCE/ECB 2533/98 and Banque de France instruction) when aggregate external transactions exceed EUR 12,500,000 in one financial year, or immediately for individual operations exceeding EUR 15,000,000. Invoice currency is freely negotiable; however, settlement in USD or non-EUR currencies triggers prudential FX exposure classification and standard 40% risk-weighting under CRR if unhedged. No conversion requirement, no quota, and no export license are imposed for used hydraulic earthmovers (HS 8429.51) provided the goods are not dual-use controlled and civil end-use is declared.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) together with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the macroprudential and banking supervision arm; Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty exercises residual foreign-exchange licensing powers under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium (conditional). French-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorized by ACPR, such as Qonto, Shine, or a foreign EMI passporting under the EEA, can provide multi-currency IBANs, EUR/USD payment accounts, and FX conversion on a software-as-a-service basis. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI does not offer lending, credit, or letter-of-credit issuance, so it cannot replace bank-issued Commercial LC or guarantee products required for trade settlement; (ii) EMI accounts are legally treated as 'fonds de monnaie électronique' and are not eligible as escrow or blocked capital accounts for inbound capital injection where the counterparty requires a true banking guarantee; (iii) EMI AML/KYC does not waive the French obligation to declare UBOs and may require a higher-risk category due to the used-machinery sector; (iv) French banking law forbids an EMI from settling transactions with unlicensed third parties; therefore, any cross-border disbursement must route via a supervised partner bank. Recommended use: operating sub-account and FX conversion, but not as sole repository for Stage 4 capital escrow or LC-backed trade flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity subscription by the non-resident parent must be executed through a licensed bank or an authorized payment institution. Standard rail: SWIFT MT103 for wire transfer in EUR to the SAS's French Operational Account, or a rector 'SPOT' transaction for settlement of foreign currency via TARGET2. The wire must include purpose code 'B016' (direct investment – contribution to capital) or 'B036' (acquisition of equity) under the Banque de France classification, referencing the SAS SIREN number and mandate 'Apport en societe / Capital'. For capital escrow account, the transfer must be made to a blocked 'compte séquestre' at the commercial bank, not an EMI, and the bank will require a notarized Minute of Increase of Capital and, where applicable, an attestation from the Domaines (service de fiscalité). The European Transfer of Funds Regulation (EU) 2015/847 requires full originator and beneficiary information; reject any transfer from an unregulated crypto exchange or anonymous intermediary. The bank will file a Balance of Payments declaration with Banque de France for any direct investment flow; if > EUR 15,000,000, the French entity must submit form 'Enquête fiche mouvement'. No central bank approval is required. To avoid 'reverse hybrid' tax treatment, the share capital should be paid on a bank account of the SAS in France and the bank certificate of deposit attached to the SAS registration dossier.Preferred rails for trade settlement with overseas suppliers of used construction machinery: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct wire settlement of advance payments or final payment, executed only after verification of supplier's bank details and invoice authenticity; (ii) Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP600, issued by the French bank via SWIFT MT700, confirmed by the beneficiary's bank in the supplier jurisdiction, with MT707 amendment for any change in price/proforma; (iii) Standby Letter of Credit (SBLC) or bank guarantee for performance bonds or after-sales service warranties, using MT760; and (iv) direct debit through SEPA for intra-EU payments in EUR with no FX conversion. For the used construction equipment trade, the recommended structure is a 20% deposit via MT103, 60% against presentation of Bill of Lading and pre-shipment inspection certificate, and 20% after inspection at destination. All outbound wires must include purpose code 'B011' (trade in goods) or 'B007' (services) and the statement 'Déclaration spéciale: rien à déclarer' when below thresholds. For payments to sanctioned countries or where goods could be diverted, an ICC Trade Register-compliant compliance certificate is mandatory. The clearing of LC proceeds may be in EUR or USD; if USD, bank must convert at prevailing interbank rate with a declared spread, and the commercial bank will include the transaction in the CRR large-exposure and FX risk framework.FX forward (Change à terme) with a licensed French credit institution for EUR/USD, EUR/CNY, or other major counterparties, limited to documentary underlying trade exposure and lasting no longer than 12 months.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) for currencies with restricted convertibility (e.g., CNY, INR) permitted only via a French bank acting as principal or through a swap execution facility, with contract referencing an ISDA master agreement or French ABA master agreement, provided the underlying trade receivable/payable is actual and the bank's KYC can prove transactional exposure.Cross-currency swap (XCCY) as an infrequent risk-management tool, eligible only for larger long-term financing structures and subject to EMIR clearing and margining requirements.FX options (plain vanillas) strict for hedging of committed commercial flows, not for speculation, and requiring a credit line with the bank.Commodity-linked hedging instruments are not relevant unless the contract price is pegged to scrap metal indices; if used, they must be bundled into the product margin and disclosed as part of the FX structure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium (conditional). French-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions authorized by ACPR, such as Qonto, Shine, or a foreign EMI passporting under the EEA, can provide multi-currency IBANs, EUR/USD payment accounts, and FX conversion on a software-as-a-service basis. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI does not offer lending, credit, or letter-of-credit issuance, so it cannot replace bank-issued Commercial LC or guarantee products required for trade settlement; (ii) EMI accounts are legally treated as 'fonds de monnaie électronique' and are not eligible as escrow or blocked capital accounts for inbound capital injection where the counterparty requires a true banking guarantee; (iii) EMI AML/KYC does not waive the French obligation to declare UBOs and may require a higher-risk category due to the used-machinery sector; (iv) French banking law forbids an EMI from settling transactions with unlicensed third parties; therefore, any cross-border disbursement must route via a supervised partner bank. Recommended use: operating sub-account and FX conversion, but not as sole repository for Stage 4 capital escrow or LC-backed trade flows.
