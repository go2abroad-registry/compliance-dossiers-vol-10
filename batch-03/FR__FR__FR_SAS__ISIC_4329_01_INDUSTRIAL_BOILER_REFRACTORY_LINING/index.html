Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid up on incorporation; the balance is payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with an authorized depositary, such as a bank or notary, and the deposit certificate included in the registration file. Contributions in kind are fully contributed at incorporation and may require a statutory auditor for contributions unless a legal exemption applies.

Funding & unlock path: No universal pre-operation civil-defense clearance applies to the business description alone. The premises and each worksite must meet applicable fire-safety, building, and workplace requirements. Industrial-site access and hot-work permits may be imposed by the site operator. Employers must assess occupational risks, prepare the required risk-assessment document, provide training and personal protective equipment, and comply with construction-site coordination rules where applicable. Confirm structural loading and fire precautions for each installation; construction-sector professional insurance, including decennial liability insurance, must be assessed against the actual contractual work and statutory scope.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. Cash contributions must be deposited with an authorized depositary, such as a bank or notary, and the deposit certificate included in the registration file. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP); online tax account and business tax services are accessed through impots.gouv.fr. Business formalities and registration data are also processed through the national Guichet unique operated by INPI.

E-government filing portal

Register the SAS through the Guichet unique to obtain its SIREN and establishment SIRET identifiers. The competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) assigns or confirms the French VAT identification number when the entity makes transactions requiring VAT registration; the number generally comprises the FR country prefix and a two-character key followed by the SIREN. Activate the professional account on impots.gouv.fr and submit applicable VAT returns electronically. A non-established foreign taxable person generally registers with the competent French non-resident tax service and appoints a French fiscal representative unless an applicable EU or mutual-assistance exception applies. A French-incorporated SAS is not treated as a non-established foreign entity merely because its shareholders are non-resident.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying newly created SAS may elect temporary partnership-style taxation under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code for up to five fiscal years, subject to ownership, activity, age, employee, and turnover or balance-sheet conditions. There is no general US-style check-the-box election. French and EU hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or require income inclusion in specified cases; they do not provide a general elective entity-classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Generally no turnover threshold for a SAS making taxable transactions for which it is liable to account for French VAT; registration is required before or when such activity begins. The small-business franchise en base may exempt eligible businesses below the applicable thresholds, including qualifying service businesses generally subject to a €37,500 base threshold and €41,250 tolerance threshold, and qualifying goods businesses generally subject to an €85,000 base threshold and €93,500 tolerance threshold. Eligibility and thresholds depend on activity, relevant-year rules, and transaction type; the franchise does not remove all registration or reporting obligations. For construction services, domestic B2B subcontracting may be subject to customer reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from the EU to a destination outside the EU may be exempt with deduction rights (effectively 0%) where statutory conditions are met and the supplier can prove export and exit from the EU. Supplies to qualifying customers in another EU Member State are generally treated under intra-Community supply rules, not as exports. Services are not automatically zero-rated because the customer or work is offshore; their VAT treatment depends on place-of-supply rules, customer status, service type, and any applicable French territorial or exemption rule. Refractory masonry and industrial installation services performed in France may be French-taxable, with reverse charge potentially applying to qualifying domestic subcontracting.

  • Customs export declaration and evidence of release for export and exit from the EU, including the export accompanying document and exit confirmation where available
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order or contract, packing list where relevant, and transport evidence such as bill of lading, CMR consignment note, or air waybill
  • Evidence identifying the goods and linking the customs and transport records to the invoiced supply
  • Proof of payment or other commercial records where relevant; a bank inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory condition for export VAT exemption

France is implementing mandatory domestic e-invoicing and e-reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; issuance obligations begin on that date for large and intermediate-sized businesses and on 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises, subject to any valid statutory amendment or deferral. In-scope invoices must be transmitted through an approved platform (plateforme agréée, formerly PDP) or the public invoicing portal, in compliant structured formats. E-reporting applies to specified transactions outside the domestic e-invoicing scope, including relevant cross-border and B2C transactions. This is not a general real-time fiscal-clearance or invoice-stamping system. Public-sector invoicing is separately subject to Chorus Pro requirements.

France imposes no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The supplier must retain reliable evidence supporting the VAT exemption and satisfy applicable invoicing and declaration requirements. Unsupported exemption may result in assessment of output VAT, late-payment interest, and tax penalties. In cases of deliberate fraud, penalties may be substantially higher and criminal sanctions may apply; deductions or refunds may also be reassessed under the applicable limitation periods.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully convertible currency regime for both current and capital accounts. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are permitted without restriction, subject only to standard corporate tax compliance, beneficial ownership declarations, and anti-money laundering reporting. No capital controls or surrender mechanisms apply to routine corporate transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds to a central bank account. Exporters must submit customs declarations and VAT returns declaring the value of exports. Transactions exceeding certain thresholds require enhanced reporting to the Trésor for statistical and tax purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France and Direction Générale du Trésor (DGT) under the Ministry of Economy

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for offshore digital EMIs due to strict French regulations classifying them as payment institutions requiring authorization. Local bank accounts preferred for compliance with AML and payment services directives.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal year ending 31 December, deadline is the second business day after 1 May of the following year. For other fiscal year-ends, within 3 months of the fiscal year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Résultat fiscal (taxable income) Report net taxable income after adjustments for non-resident entities. Late filing penalty of 5% of tax due, plus 0.5% per month of delay, capped at 10% of tax due. Additional penalties for failure to file electronically.
Box 2 Déficit reportable (loss carryforward) Indicate any loss carryforward from prior years, subject to annual cap. Late filing penalty of 5% of tax due, plus 0.5% per month of delay, capped at 10% of tax due. Additional penalties for failure to file electronically.

Box 4 Impôt sur les sociétés (corporate income tax) Calculate tax at applicable rate, considering any reduced rate for SMEs. Late filing penalty of 5% of tax due, plus 0.5% per month of delay, capped at 10% of tax due. Additional penalties for failure to file electronically.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million in annual turnover or total assets, or if the company is part of a group with consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 400 million. Additionally, companies with related-party transactions exceeding EUR 1 million must prepare a simplified TP file.

Economic substance scope: France applies general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) and requires economic substance for tax residency and treaty benefits. The concept of 'substance over form' is applied, and shell companies without real activity may be challenged.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 40% of the evaded tax (or 80% for abuse of circumstances), plus interest at 0.2% per month. Criminal penalties may include imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to €500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for criminal taxes if they committed fraud or gross negligence. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the company's tax debts if the company is deemed a sham.
AML

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for companies, and criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment for individuals. Additional penalties include license revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable and face imprisonment and fines.
