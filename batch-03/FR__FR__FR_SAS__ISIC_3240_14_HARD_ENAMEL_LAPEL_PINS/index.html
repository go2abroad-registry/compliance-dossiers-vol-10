UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Code monétaire et financier, Articles L561-1 through L561-50 and R561-1 through R561-60, governing French AML/CFT obligations, customer due diligence, enhanced measures, PEPs, sanctions, and beneficial-owner identification. Beneficial-owner registration is governed principally by Articles L561-46 through L561-50 and R561-55 through R561-60 of the Code monétaire et financier, together with the company-registration provisions of the Code de commerce and the applicable INPI filing rules. The beneficial owner is generally each individual holding, directly or indirectly, more than 25% of capital or voting rights, or otherwise exercising control through the mechanisms described in Article L233-3 of the Code de commerce; if none can be identified, the senior managing official is recorded under the statutory fallback rule.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a French registered office under Articles L123-11 and following of the Code de commerce. A commercial domiciliation company may be used if it is duly authorized and provides a compliant domiciliation agreement. A home address of the legal representative may be used subject to statutory conditions and confidentiality limitations. A virtual office is generally acceptable for a low-risk merchandise and e-commerce business during formation if it provides a genuine legal address, mail handling, identification of the occupant, and access to corporate records where required. It is insufficient as a substitute for operational substance where the company claims local manufacturing, warehousing, employment, regulated activity, or material French management functions.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI): https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr/

Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation procedure or universal 30-day reservation window for an SAS. Before filing, search the INPI company database, trademark database, European Union Intellectual Property Office records, domain names, and relevant commercial directories. If the Guichet unique or registry rejects the name, review the stated deficiency, submit a corrected name or supporting explanation through the INPI portal, and preserve the filing reference and rejection notice. A formal challenge may be made to the competent registry or administrative authority under the applicable procedural route; a judicial challenge may be brought before the competent commercial or administrative court depending on the nature of the decision. A name acceptance does not create trademark ownership and does not cure infringement risk.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the impots.gouv.fr portal; unified commercial and tax registration handled by the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) through guichet-entreprises.fr; the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) issues VAT registrations for non-resident entities.

E-government filing portal

A SAS is registered at formation via certified legal representatives; the RNE assigns SIREN and SIRET identifiers, and DGFiP automatically attributes the intra-Community VAT number under the format FR + two-digit control key + nine-digit SIREN. Non-EU foreign entities without a local establishment must file Form CERFA 15744 with the SIEE and may need to appoint a VAT fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) subject to DGFiP acceptance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions within the EU legal framework, governed principally by EU Directive 88/361/EEC and Articles L.151-1 et seq. and R.151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier). France maintains no general exchange-control authorisation requirement for euro-denominated cross-border flows. Capital account liberalisation extends to equity injection, intercompany loans, trade finance, and profit distribution. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted, but dividend distributions attract a 30% flat withholding tax (prélèvement forfaitaire unique) unless reduced under an applicable double tax treaty; a tax-residence certificate (attestation de résidence fiscale issued by the foreign competent authority) is required to substantiate treaty relief. Practical hurdles: the SAS must file annual financial statements (liasse fiscale), maintain an up-to-date register of beneficial owners (Registre des Bénéficiaires Effectifs), and evidence satisfied corporate income tax and VAT obligations before clearing distributions through the French banking system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds for resident legal entities transacting in euro or convertible foreign currencies within the OECD liberalisation framework. The statutory requirement is statistical: residents must report all cross-border wire transfers and non-resident counter-transactions to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments and monetary statistics purposes where the individual transaction exceeds €12,500 or the annual aggregate flows meet the monthly reporting threshold. The reporting obligation is time-critical and must, for the standard reporting channel, be satisfied on a monthly basis within 20 calendar days after the end of the reporting month; interbank settlement evidence, SWIFT MT103 copies, and Stripe/Etsy payout statements constitute the primary evidentiary records. Failure to report is an administrative infraction (contravention) under Article L.151-4 CMF; habitual non-compliance may be escalated to the ACPR as a financial-sector integrity event.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the European System of Central Banks; exchange-control administration and capital movement authorisation framework are jointly administered by the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty, with prudential supervision by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for EEA-authorised fintech EMIs; Low for non-EEA offshore digital EMIs. Qonto S.A., Shine (BNP Paribas subsidiary), and Revolut Ltd (UK entity passporting under PSD2) are viable onboarding counterparts for an e-commerce SAS holding no statutory licence and exhibiting a Standard Low-Risk AML profile, provided the EMI holds a valid e-money institution or credit institution licence granted by a European Economic Area national competent authority and has valid passport notifications registered with the ACPR. Legal constraint: an EMI is prohibited from granting commercial credit and may not facilitate undocumented cross-border settlements; the SAS must maintain a separate sponsor account or routing into a French credit institution when receiving periodic settlement payouts from Etsy Direct Pay and Stripe. Non-EEA EMIs (e.g. certain Asian or Caribbean aggregators) are not permitted to hold segregated client funds for a French corporate under French payment services regulation, and funds sourced from such structures will be blocked by French correspondent banks under TRACFIN reporting duties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from a non-resident shareholder must route via SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR (if the remitting account is in the SEPA zone) or SWIFT MT103 / MT202 COV (if remitting from outside the SEPA zone). The compliant purpose-code string used on the SWIFT tag 50/56/57/59 must mirror the DG Trésor statistical nomenclature: for share capital contribution use code 'N380' (equity capital), and for shareholder loans use 'A010' (financial intercompany loans). No prior exchange-control authorisation is required, but the Banque de France monthly statistical report must capture the economic nature of the flow and the counterparty country. Mandatory bank-side verification pack includes: the approval of the French bank's compliance unit to accept the credit, a funds-origin declaration on the bank's KYC form, notarised or apostilled share-capital subscription deed, and, where the injection exceeds €100,000, supporting source-of-wealth documentation from the remitting person or entity. TRACFIN reporting is triggered if the bank determines that the credit is even slightly suspicious; in the absence of the documented subscription deed the account may be frozen ex parte under Article L.561-15 CMF.For trade settlements against brass/enamel blank suppliers in Asia, the permitted clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct documentary/open-account telegraphic transfer with full invoice and customs declaration attached; (ii) commercial letters of credit issued by the French bank via SWIFT MT700 and governed by UCP 600 where the Chinese or Vietnamese supplier demands an LC; (iii) SEPA Priority Credit Transfer for intra-EEA suppliers; and (iv) documentary collections under URC 522 (cash against documents/DA/DP) as a secondary rail for low-value repeat purchasing of consumables. The French bank will require the corresponding import customs certificates (D30 declaration at the point of final export/import clearance) and TRACFIN orientation where the disbursement exceeds EUR 15,000 or is directed to a high-risk jurisdiction. For digital disbursements (Stripe/Etsy outbound returns and refunds) the rail is SWIFT MT103 or SEPA refund transfers from the platform's sponsor bank directly to the customer; the SAS must maintain reconciliation agreements with both platform sponsors to ensure refunds are not returned via a crypto-rail or personal consumer account.EUR/USD funded FX forward (plain vanilla) up to 24 months, OTC with the SAS's French bank under ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex, requiring delivery of the commercial sales contract as underlying exposure evidence.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) referencing non-EEA restricted-market currencies (e.g. CNY/CNH) permitted only for documented hedging of the import supply cost, with monthly fixing against the offshore RMB index and settlement net in EUR via the French bank's London or Paris book.FX option (European-style, cash-settled EUR/USD and EUR/GBP) purchased at a premium without the writing of options, authorised under the SAS's general treasury mandate.Cross-currency swap (EUR/USD) for balance-sheet hedging of a shareholder loan or long-term receivable, permissible only with the same credit institution holding the treasury account and subject to ISDA collateralisation.Benchmark-restricted interest-rate/futures overlay not required for the micro-ticket size of the pin business; any swap, NDF, or forward must be corroborated by an audit trail of underlying Etsy/Stripe future sales and supplier purchase orders to comply with the French Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF) eligibility rule for non-professional counterparties.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax on dividends and similar distributions to non-residents under the domestic rule, subject to exemptions and treaty reductions. Qualifying EU or EEA parent companies may obtain an exemption under the French parent-subsidiary regime, generally requiring a qualifying participation, holding-period and anti-abuse conditions. French residents generally receive dividends under a different withholding and advance-tax framework.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the return is generally due on the second business day following 1 May of the following year. For a financial year ending on another date, the return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The electronic deadline and payment date displayed by the tax administration must be observed where they provide a more specific administrative date. Corporate income tax instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, subject to statutory exceptions and final-payment rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million: mandatory full Local File and Master File documentation generally applies where the French taxpayer or relevant group meets the statutory turnover or gross-assets threshold, or where another statutory related-party condition applies. A simplified transfer-pricing declaration may apply from approximately EUR 50 million of turnover excluding tax or gross assets, including qualifying group situations. Arm's-length pricing and contemporaneous evidence remain required below these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone regime formally titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore ESR regimes. Substance is nevertheless relevant under French transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, the general anti-abuse rule, principal-purpose-test concepts, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, permanent-establishment analysis and anti-conduit principles. The French SAS should maintain genuine French management, decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or operational infrastructure as appropriate, commercial risk control, bank and accounting records, and evidence that functions and risks match the remuneration received.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent filing

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, 40% or 80% tax surcharges, late-payment interest, and criminal prosecution. Tax fraud may attract up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, with aggravated cases reaching seven years and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory aggravating factors and possible increases linked to proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, tax-liability proceedings for fraudulent or repeated failure to pay, civil restitution and asset-seizure exposure. Courts may impose management bans and professional disqualification. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal proceedings; imprisonment is possible for personally participated or knowingly facilitated fraud.
Undeclared permanent establishment or French business activity

Statutory sanction: Assessment of French corporate income tax, VAT, payroll and other applicable taxes, interest, 10% to 80% surcharges depending on conduct, and potential criminal referral for fraudulent concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they knowingly organized concealment, participated in fraudulent management or failed to comply with court orders. The foreign company and French SAS may also face joint or successor-liability issues depending on the facts.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions, reallocation of taxable profit to France, withholding-tax reassessment, documentation penalties, 40% or 80% surcharges in aggravated cases, and possible mutual-agreement or corresponding-adjustment disputes.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims for breach of management duties and criminal exposure if invoices or records are knowingly false. Personal liability is not automatic solely because an adjustment is made.
Illegal profit distribution and disguised dividends

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unlawfully distributed amounts, corporate-tax adjustments, dividend withholding tax, interest and penalties. Directors may also face corporate-law sanctions for unlawful distributions and misuse of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for restitution, damages to the company or creditors, management disqualification and, where intentional misuse is proven, criminal sanctions. A non-resident shareholder may be assessed for withholding tax as recipient or beneficial owner.
VAT and customs non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Customs debt, import VAT, recovery of incorrectly exempted VAT, interest, administrative fines, seizure or forfeiture of goods, and criminal prosecution for aggravated customs fraud or VAT fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may be prosecuted for intentional customs or VAT fraud and may face fines, imprisonment, confiscation and civil restitution. Personal liability depends on participation, authorization or serious management misconduct.
Anti-money laundering, beneficial ownership and sanctions

Statutory sanction: Regulatory reporting, account blocking, transaction refusal, administrative fines, criminal money-laundering sanctions, confiscation and sanctions for breaches of restrictive measures. Failure to file or update beneficial-owner information may attract statutory fines and court-ordered compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability, fines, imprisonment and confiscation where they knowingly facilitated laundering, sanctions evasion or concealment. Civil restitution and management bans may follow. Travel restrictions may be imposed by competent authorities or courts in relevant proceedings.
Shell company, fictitious management and abuse of corporate form

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment under abuse-of-law or anti-abuse principles, denial of treaty benefits, asset seizure, insolvency clawback, director-liability proceedings, and criminal penalties for fraud, forgery, misuse of corporate assets or money laundering where applicable.

Director personal liability: Courts may order contribution to insufficient company assets, extend insolvency proceedings, impose civil restitution, prohibit management and impose imprisonment for proven criminal conduct. Limited liability is not protection against personal fraud or deliberate misconduct.
Accounting, corporate-record and electronic-filing failures

Statutory sanction: Tax rejection of deductions, presumptive assessment, accounting penalties, late-filing surcharges, court-ordered filing, director-liability proceedings and possible criminal sanctions for false accounts or obstruction.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability to the company, shareholders and creditors and may face management bans or criminal sanctions for intentional falsification, concealment or obstruction.
Improper withholding-tax handling

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the full unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, interest, penalties and possible denial of treaty relief. Deliberate concealment may trigger enhanced penalties and criminal fraud exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable where they knowingly authorized non-remittance, falsified treaty documents or used a conduit arrangement. Personal liability is fact-dependent and is not created by an ordinary technical error alone.
