Name reservation / lock period

French law does not provide a stand-alone pre-incorporation name reservation. Protection of the corporate name is effective upon successful registration of the company with the RCS. If the Guichet unique or the Commercial Court registrar rejects the name, the applicant may (i) file a rectification request addressing the cited irregularity, (ii) immediately submit a revised corporate name, and/or (iii) appeal the refusal to the supervising judge of the Commercial Court (juge commis à la surveillance du registre du commerce et des sociétés) within 15 days of the refusal notice. The appeal is initiated by petition (requête) and is subject to the judge's procedural discretion.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce acting through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) single digital counter at formalities.inpi.fr; Infogreffe portal for RCS extracts; supervisory authority of the Ministère de la Justice.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce) — Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-27 for the Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS); Article L. 123-1 et seq. for registration at the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); Décret n° 84-406 of 30 May 1984 on RCS formalities; Code de l'environnement for conditional ICPE obligations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) accompanied by the M0 Declaration of Incorporation (Cerfa n° 10945*02) filed electronically via the INPI formalities portal and registered at the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés, resulting in issuance of the Kbis extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum share capital under Article L. 227-2 Code de commerce; capital is fixed freely in the statuts; at least 50% of the subscribed capital must be paid up at formation, with the balance due within five years; paid-up funds must be deposited in a blocked non-interest-bearing escrow account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the company en formation at a French credit institution or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and are released only upon RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: premises must comply with the Code de la construction et de l'habitation; the fire-safety opinion (avis du SDIS / Commission de Sécurité) is mandatory only for premises open to the public (ERP classification), which is inapplicable to internet-only retail unless a physical showroom is operated; for ICPE-listed warehouses, fire-prevention obligations are enforced by DREAL. Structural load: the building-code conformity attestation required by Décret n° 2019-522 must be delivered by a certified inspector. Occupational clearance: registration with DREETS, employee medical-fitness visits under the Code du travail, and the mandatory Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP) established and updated in consultation with the social and economic committee.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum share capital under Article L. 227-2 Code de commerce; capital is fixed freely in the statuts; at least 50% of the subscribed capital must be paid up at formation, with the balance due within five years; paid-up funds must be deposited in a blocked non-interest-bearing escrow account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the company en formation at a French credit institution or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and are released only upon RCS registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; online portal: impots.gouv.fr. VAT registration for non-established businesses is handled by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) in the competent territorial department, with certain centralised units for non-residents.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in France but making taxable supplies of goods or services in France must obtain a French VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) before carrying out taxable transactions. For non-EU established entities, France generally requires the appointment of an accredited fiscal representative, who applies for the VAT number on behalf of the foreign principal. For EU-established entities, direct application is possible by submitting Form 3571 (one-off registration) or via the EU One-Stop-Shop for distance sales; for permanent establishments an SIREN number is first obtained through the formalities centre (Centre de Formalités des Entreprises). Supporting documentation includes proof of identity of the legal representative, articles of association, certificate of tax residence from the home jurisdiction, evidence of economic activity, and the expected date of first taxable transaction. The SIE issues the VAT number within a statutory timeframe once the application is complete, subject to anti-fraud verification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Under French general tax law, an SAS is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level; no check-the-box or pass-through election exists for SAS. Available elections relevant to group structures include the formation of a French tax consolidation group (intégration fiscale) with a 95% ownership threshold; the territoriality election for global consolidated profits under specific rules; and the option for partnerships and other transparent entities to elect corporate income tax. Hybrid mismatch effects are constrained by the French implementation of the ATAD anti-hybrid rules, which operate through disallowance and inclusion adjustments rather than elective classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For entities established in France, the small-business exemption threshold for supplies of goods and retail-like activities is EUR 85,000 of annual turnover (EUR 37,500 for most services), as indexed upward for goods in certain cases; however, for non-established foreign sellers and distance sales to final consumers via online marketplaces there is no registration threshold and VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable transaction. Under the EU e-commerce package, the EU wide distance sales threshold is EUR 10,000, above which supplies are taxed in the country of destination unless the seller uses the Union One-Stop-Shop; France applies destination VAT to all such sales by non-established suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of movable goods outside the EU are zero-rated for French VAT provided the goods are physically dispatched or transported out of the EU under commercial documents satisfying the proof-of-export conditions. Offshore services and intangible supplies are not automatically zero-rated; they are treated under the B2B place-of-supply rules and are usually outside French VAT when performed for a taxable person established outside France, subject to the recipient's taxable status, the nature of the service, and the effective use and enjoyment rules. For retail e-commerce exports via TikTok Shop or similar platforms, the SAS must be the exporter of record or hold evidence that the goods left the EU through its own logistics arrangements; goods sold through online marketplaces that import and fulfil within the EU are not zero-rated at the French export stage and may instead fall under import VAT and deemed supply rules.

France is progressively implementing mandatory structured e-invoicing for B2B transactions from 1 September 2026 (large enterprises) and 1 September 2027 (small and medium enterprises), with B2C transactions subject to mandatory e-reporting. Invoices must be transmitted through the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF) or through certified dematerialisation platforms (Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires, PDPs) using formats such as Factur-X/Mixed, EDI, or agreed structured XML schemas. There is no general real-time fiscal clearance or stamping regime for VAT on e-commerce supplies; however, certified cash-register or sales-management software must comply with French tax fraud prevention requirements (NF 525, NF 203, or equivalent) and guarantee tamper-proof audit trails. For cross-border B2B services and goods subject to reverse charge, the obligation is to issue invoices stating the reverse-charge rule and to submit recapitulative EC sales lists and Intrastat declarations where applicable.

French customs and tax authorities apply stringent documentary consistency tests to export zero-rating claims. If the required export evidence is not produced on demand, or is insufficient, the VAT initially zero-rated is clawed back with late-payment interest (intérêts de retard, currently 0.20% per month) and additional penalties: 10% for absence of declaration, 40% for deliberate underdeclaration, 80% for fraudulent abuse of law, plus an additional tax abuse penalty and potential criminal fines for customs fraud where the export declaration is falsified. The statute of limitations for VAT adjustments is generally up to six years in cases of fraud or active concealment, and the burden of proof of satisfaction of the zero-rating condition rests on the exporting SAS. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may also trigger exchange-control penalties and, where the unrepatriated amount is treated as a distribution or residual profit, additional income tax exposure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully convertible currency regime under EU law and national FX regulations. Both current account transactions and capital movements are unrestricted for resident entities. Profit remittances to shareholders face no capital controls or surrender obligations. All FX transactions exceeding €10,000 must be declared to the Banque de France through the Système Economique et Financier (SEPA) reporting mechanism for statistical, monetary policy, and AML monitoring purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds required under French law. Companies may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore or domestic FX accounts. Export receipts must be statistically declared to customs authorities via the Single Administrative Document (SAD) process. FX transactions remain subject to full AML/KYC verification with no statutory timeline for conversion or repatriation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France
