Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS (Article L.227-2 C. com.); the share capital is freely determined in the Statuts. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) opened with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the depositary issues the attestation de dépôt de fonds, and the funds are released only upon completion of registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: if the registered office is open to the public as an ERP (type R), prior SDIS inspection and préfecture clearance are required; otherwise minimum fire-extinguisher maintenance and evacuation signage apply. Electrical conformity: Consuel certificate for new installations. Occupational: DUERP registered and displayed, first-aid equipment, and emergency instructions posted; CARSAT and DDETS verify compliance during inspections.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the professional portal impots.gouv.fr; VAT registration and electronic filing are managed through the DGFiP's online professional tax space (Espace Professionnel) and the Gérer mon entreprise / formalities portal at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities intending to establish a French SAS or otherwise carry on taxable activities in France must first complete company incorporation through the French business formalities portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). Upon incorporation, the French tax authorities automatically assign a SIREN number, a SIRET number for each establishment, and a French VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire). For a non-resident entity not forming a French company but making taxable supplies in France, the foreign entity must apply directly to the DGFiP non-resident service (Service des Impôts des Entreprises / Service de la DGFiP) for a French VAT number using Form No. 3551 (Cerfa 15583) or the specific non-resident VAT registration procedure; the application must include proof of foreign registration, the territorial scope of activities, and expected turnover in France. In addition to the VAT number, the taxpayer must obtain the compulsory French EORI number through the customs authority (Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects) for customs transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities intending to establish a French SAS or otherwise carry on taxable activities in France must first complete company incorporation through the French business formalities portal (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). Upon incorporation, the French tax authorities automatically assign a SIREN number, a SIRET number for each establishment, and a French VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire). For a non-resident entity not forming a French company but making taxable supplies in France, the foreign entity must apply directly to the DGFiP non-resident service (Service des Impôts des Entreprises / Service de la DGFiP) for a French VAT number using Form No. 3551 (Cerfa 15583) or the specific non-resident VAT registration procedure; the application must include proof of foreign registration, the territorial scope of activities, and expected turnover in France. In addition to the VAT number, the taxpayer must obtain the compulsory French EORI number through the customs authority (Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects) for customs transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the entity level and is not eligible for check-the-box pass-through treatment. The only relevant elective mechanisms are the option for tax integration (régime d'intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A of the French General Tax Code, available to a French parent company owning at least 95% of qualifying French subsidiaries, and the option for the régime des sociétés de personnes which is not available to an SAS. A qualifying small or medium enterprise may elect to be taxed at the reduced corporate tax rate on its first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit under Article 205 ter CGI, but this is not a classification election. For indirect tax purposes, there is no entity classification election; VAT grouping (assujettis unis) is available under Articles 256 C and 256 D CGI but is subject to strict conditions and does not alter the entity's status as a taxable person for supplies outside the group.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Foreign suppliers of digital services to French non-taxable persons are required to register and account for French VAT from the first euro of sales under the non-established taxable person regime (Article 259 D CGI), with no general threshold for establishment-based suppliers. For small businesses established in France, the franchise en base de TVA under Article 293 B CGI exempts from VAT and from the requirement to register where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 85,000 for sales of goods and certain services, and EUR 37,500 for other services including digital publishing, unless the mini one-stop-shop (OSS IOSS/One-Stop Shop) regime is used; for digital magazine portal services B2C, the relevant threshold is EUR 37,500, but once the business supplies digital B2C services to other EU member states, the special EU OSS scheme applies and the EU-wide threshold for OSS is EUR 10,000 of intra-EU distance and digital B2C services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

French VAT applies to goods and services supplies. Exports of goods outside the EU are zero-rated under Article 262 CGI provided that the goods are physically delivered or transported outside the EU by the seller or on behalf of the seller and are declared for export through the French customs system. For digital publishing services, exports of services to B2B customers outside the EU are outside the scope of French VAT under Article 259 B CGI if the customer is established outside the EU and the services are not attributable to a French fixed establishment; such supplies are characterized as zero-rated/outside the scope of VAT. For B2C digital services to non-EU customers, the place of supply is outside the EU and French VAT does not apply. Advertising services and sponsored media placements supplied to non-EU customers are also outside the scope where the recipient is established outside the EU. The exporter must retain evidence that the recipient is outside the EU and, if applicable, proof of the French exporter's status as a taxable person, the nature of the services, and the absence of a French fixed establishment supplying the services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation via DELTA / DAU) with MRN registration
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Intrastat declaration if intra-EU dispatch (if applicable for goods)
  • Export licence or permit if the goods are controlled

Since 2017, all taxpayer invoicing systems must be secured, accepted, or certified by the DGFiP under Article 289 CGI and the associated anti-fraud decree (Décret n° 2016-1091). Real-time clearance is not yet mandatory; France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and B2B transaction reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all taxable persons must be able to receive electronic invoices and must transmit transaction and payment data (données de transaction et de paiement) to the DGFiP through a registered dematerialization platform or the public portal. B2B e-invoicing on a national basis is mandatory from 1 September 2026 for all taxpayers. The new system requires structured electronic documents (Factur-X/UBL), but the fully real-time fiscal stamping model (e.g., Italian or Portuguese style) is not adopted. Existing anti-fraud software certification (NF 525 / 2031) remains applicable until mandatory e-invoicing takes effect. A digital stamping of invoices is not required; the DGFiP receives e-reporting data through certified platforms without a central real-time clearing house.

Export zero-rating and non-application of VAT are subject to DGFiP audit scrutiny. If the exporter cannot substantiate export delivery and payment, the DGFiP may assess VAT at the standard rate, demand the VAT amount, and impose the 10% late payment penalty under Article 1731 CGI plus monthly interest at 0.20% per month under Article 1727 CGI. In cases of fraudulent intention or abusive schemes, the 80% penalty for bad faith or fraud under Articles 1729 and 1729-0 A CGI applies, together with criminal fines under Articles 1741 and 1742 CGI for tax fraud, which may include imprisonment and a fine up to EUR 500,000. Retrospective assessment generally covers a six-year limitation period (Article L.169 of the Book of Tax Procedures), extended to ten years for fraudulent behaviour. The French customs administration may also apply customs penalties under the Code des douanes for false export declarations, and the tax authority can jointly demand recovery of taxes plus penal interest and publication sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro. The euro is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, prudential, statistical-reporting, and applicable foreign-direct-investment controls. A French SAS may generally receive foreign shareholder capital, shareholder loans, dividends, royalties, service fees, and other permitted cross-border payments without a general foreign-exchange approval. Profit remittances require distributable profits, properly approved financial statements, compliance with legal-reserve requirements, applicable withholding-tax rules, beneficial-owner and sanctions screening, and bank evidence of the underlying transaction. Dividend payments to non-residents generally do not attract French withholding tax where an applicable domestic exemption or tax treaty applies, but the exemption must be substantiated. Interest, royalties, management fees, and related-party payments require transfer-pricing support, corporate-benefit evidence, withholding-tax analysis, and documentation of applicable treaty relief. Payments to non-cooperative or sanctioned jurisdictions may be restricted or subject to enhanced controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the central bank and no mandatory conversion ratio or universal statutory deadline for converting foreign-currency export receipts. Export proceeds may normally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's discretion, subject to contractual terms, accounting requirements, tax reporting, sanctions controls, AML review, and accurate recording of trade transactions. Banks may request invoices, transport evidence, customs references, contracts, and proof of economic purpose. Cross-border payment and balance-of-payments reporting may apply through banking, statistical, customs, or regulatory reporting channels, but these requirements do not constitute a general export-proceeds conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, as the French central bank within the Eurosystem; monetary and euro-system matters are governed by the European Central Bank. Foreign-exchange and cross-border payment policy is also subject to applicable European Union rules, French financial legislation, the French Treasury Directorate General, the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, and the Autorité des marchés financiers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for operational payments and collection; low as the sole banking solution for incorporation capital, regulated escrow, tax payments, payroll, and complex treasury. A French SAS may use an EU-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution under passporting rules, subject to the provider's French servicing model, safeguarding framework, transaction limits, SEPA connectivity, beneficiary verification, and AML policy. An offshore digital EMI without EU authorisation, passporting, or a regulated correspondent relationship cannot lawfully provide ordinary payment-account services to the company merely through an online interface. EMI safeguarded funds are not equivalent to bank deposits and generally do not provide deposit-guarantee protection. Stripe and AdSense-type collection or payout accounts should be treated as commercial payment platforms, not substitutes for a primary French corporate bank account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Certified identity documents; proof of residential address; curriculum vitae and professional background; French registry extract or draft incorporation documents; articles of association; shareholder register; beneficial-owner filing evidence; board or shareholder resolution authorising account opening; business plan; first-year budget; expected transaction profile; and evidence of French commercial substance.
Opaque ownership, nominee arrangements, trusts, foundations, or a multi-tier foreign holding chain

Root cause: Opaque ownership, nominee arrangements, trusts, foundations, or a multi-tier foreign holding chain

Defense evidence: Complete ownership chart to each natural-person ultimate beneficial owner; certificates of incorporation and good standing for every intermediate entity; constitutional documents; registers of directors and shareholders; trust or foundation instruments where relevant; notarised or certified translations; beneficial-owner declarations; and a written explanation of control rights and economic ownership.
Unexplained inbound shareholder funding, intercompany loans, or capital injections from third-party accounts

Root cause: Unexplained inbound shareholder funding, intercompany loans, or capital injections from third-party accounts

Defense evidence: Executed subscription or shareholder-loan agreement; shareholder and board approvals; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds evidence; audited accounts or tax returns of the funding party; bank statements; payment trail from the beneficial owner's account; purpose-of-payment narrative; capital-deposit evidence; and tax or legal analysis of the funding instrument.
Digital publishing, advertising, platform, or payment flows inconsistent with the stated low-risk business model

Related-party royalties, management fees, data services, or advertising charges lacking commercial substance

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route paid-in capital to the French bank's dedicated capital-deposit account before SAS incorporation or before a documented capital increase, using the subscriber's verified account and a payment reference identifying the company, subscription, and capital-deposit purpose. The bank or notary should receive the draft articles, subscription schedule, shareholder resolution, identity and beneficial-owner documents, and source-of-funds evidence. After incorporation or capital increase, obtain the deposit certificate and transfer the funds to the operational account. For subsequent equity funding, use a formal subscription or contribution agreement and ensure the corporate resolutions, amended articles where required, registry filings, and accounting entries match the wire. For shareholder loans, route funds directly from the lender's verified account under an executed loan agreement and classify the transfer correctly. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments and SWIFT for non-euro payments; include accurate remitter, beneficiary, IBAN, BIC, legal name, registered address, and purpose information. There is no universal French statutory purpose-code list equivalent to certain controlled-currency jurisdictions; the bank's payment-purpose, regulatory-reporting, and AML classification must be completed truthfully. Avoid third-party funding, cash deposits, unexplained round-tripping, or split payments designed to evade review.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro settlements within the SEPA framework. Use SWIFT cross-border payment messaging, commonly an MT103 customer credit transfer or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for non-SEPA or non-euro payments; confirm correspondent-bank charges, value date, sanctions screening, beneficiary details, and payment repair procedures. Commercial letters of credit may be issued or advised through a relationship bank for qualifying goods transactions, although a digital magazine business will more commonly use open-account settlement, card acquiring, direct debit, or documented bank transfers for software, cloud, content, advertising, and professional services. Maintain purchase orders, contracts, invoices, delivery or service evidence, customs documents where goods are involved, tax treatment, supplier due diligence, and approval records. For intra-group payments, retain transfer-pricing and beneficial-owner documentation. French and EU payment rules do not generally require pre-clearance of ordinary current-account payments, but banks may suspend or reject transactions for AML, sanctions, fraud, insufficient information, or correspondent-bank restrictions.Spot EUR/foreign-currency conversion through an authorised bank or regulated investment firm.Deliverable forward exchange contracts for identified and documented commercial exposures, subject to bank suitability, credit-limit, collateral, confirmation, accounting, and, where applicable, MiFID II and EMIR requirements.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies subject to local convertibility or settlement restrictions, where offered by an appropriately authorised counterparty and legally permissible in the relevant currency jurisdiction; NDFs are generally unnecessary for ordinary EUR exposures.Currency swaps and cross-currency swaps for documented funding or recurring currency exposures, subject to counterparty eligibility, legal documentation, collateral, valuation, accounting, and EMIR obligations where applicable.Foreign-exchange options, collars, and other structured hedges only after suitability, product-governance, cost, leverage, early-termination, and downside-risk review; speculative trading unrelated to the company's underlying exposure should be prohibited under the treasury policy.SEPA cash concentration, multi-currency bank accounts, automated sweeps, and internal netting may be used for liquidity management, provided intercompany balances, interest, transfer pricing, legal capacity, and payment-services restrictions are documented.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 (annual consolidated group turnover or balance-sheet total of the ultimate parent), triggering mandatory preparation of the French Master File and Local File. Below the threshold, no annual pre-filed TP documentation is required, but the SAS must be able to produce a full arm's-length file upon tax audit; failure to produce the file when formally requested attracts a penalty of 0.5% of the value of the undocumented related-party cross-border transactions.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as a standalone regime, but the 'abus de droit' doctrine (Article L64 LPF), the GCC/BEPS anti-conduit case law and French beneficial-ownership rules require genuine French economic substance. The SAS must maintain its real seat of management, local board decisions, personnel, premises and business risks in France; otherwise it may be treated as a fictitious letterbox entity, stripped of treaty protection and reassessed with 80% penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment of unpaid CIT plus 40% bad-faith penalty and 0.4%/month late-payment interest; 80% abuse-of-law penalty where concealment is established. Criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI exposes individuals to up to 5 years' imprisonment and a €75,000 fine; legal entity fines are quintupled under Article 131-38 of the French Penal Code, with possible compulsory winding-up and confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: The director or de facto manager who signed the false return is prosecuted as perpetrator and may be sentenced to a management ban (interdiction de gérer), an order prohibiting exit from French territory, confiscation of personal property, joint and several liability for the evaded tax and all consequential interest and penalties.
Shell Company / Artificial Conduit (Société Fictive and Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may disregard the artificial structure under Article L64 LPF, tax the real economically substantive beneficiary in France and apply a penalty of 80% of the avoided tax (or 40% where no bad-faith intent is proven). Cross-border DAC6/DPR filing obligations carry a separate €10,000 penalty for each unreported tax-relevant arrangement.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil and impose joint civil liability on the ultimate owner and officers for unpaid French taxes, trigger an interdiction de gérer, freeze personal assets, prohibit exit from France and apply the Article 1741 criminal penalties in aggravated fraud cases.
