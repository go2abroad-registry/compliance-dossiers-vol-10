Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent filing channel is the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI). The company is registered in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés by the competent commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce or designated commercial court registry) for the registered office.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, including Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 to R.227-2 governing the Société par Actions Simplifiée; Articles L.123-1 et seq. governing the Trade and Companies Register; Articles L.123-11 et seq. governing registered offices; Articles L.561-46 and R.561-55 et seq. governing beneficial-owner declarations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company-formation filing submitted through the Guichet unique, supported by signed SAS articles of association, the declaration of incorporation particulars, the beneficial-owner declaration, and the required supporting documents. The resulting registration certificate is the Kbis extract.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation; the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed at incorporation. Capital must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts before filing, and the deposit certificate must be included. The amount stated is subject to applicable registry, beneficial-owner, and filing charges; the USD figure is an indicative conversion of the standard commercial registration charge and is not a fixed statutory USD tariff.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain airport operator approval, airside access permits, work permits, foreign-object-debris controls, vehicle and radio authorizations, and any DGAC or aerodrome safety clearances required by the contract. Obtain fire-safety review for buildings, fuel, bitumen, and plant installations from the competent local fire and rescue service where required. Verify structural load capacity for heavy paving equipment, temporary works, storage areas, and access routes through competent engineering inspection. Implement the French Labour Code occupational-risk assessment, single risk-assessment document, site coordination plan, worker training, personal protective equipment, hot-work controls, traffic separation, emergency procedures, chemical-risk controls, and required occupational-health arrangements.

There is no single general French manufacturing license for runway paving contractors. The relevant authorities are the prefecture of the department, the Direction régionale de l'environnement, de l'aménagement et du logement (DREAL), the airport operator, and, for civil-aviation operational matters, the Direction générale de l'aviation civile (DGAC). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — national tax administration; professional portal: impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel / EDI-TVA reporting); customs and indirect tax authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) — portal: douane.gouv.fr; business registration single window: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned French SAS: (1) file articles of association and register the entity on formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr to obtain SIREN/SIRET via INSEE; (2) submit Déclaration d'existence to the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) to obtain the TVA intracommunautaire (FR + 11-digit VAT number); (3) where no permanent establishment is created, register as a non-established taxable person with the DGFiP Non-Résidents unit and, if charged with customs/export formalities, obtain an EORI number from DGDDI; (4) appoint a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) if not established in France and VAT obligations are triggered. Standard turnaround: VAT ID issued within 2–4 weeks after registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) is inherently subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) on worldwide profits at a 25% standard rate (15% up to €42,500 for eligible small businesses); no statutory pass-through option exists for an SAS (profits are taxed in the entity's own hands; distributions attract withholding tax subject to treaty relief); territorial scope is the worldwide income principle, with foreign permanent-establishment profits eligible for treaty-based exemption or group consolidation. — For a foreign-owned French SAS: (1) file articles of association and register the entity on formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr to obtain SIREN/SIRET via INSEE; (2) submit Déclaration d'existence to the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) to obtain the TVA intracommunautaire (FR + 11-digit VAT number); (3) where no permanent establishment is created, register as a non-established taxable person with the DGFiP Non-Résidents unit and, if charged with customs/export formalities, obtain an EORI number from DGDDI; (4) appoint a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) if not established in France and VAT obligations are triggered. Standard turnaround: VAT ID issued within 2–4 weeks after registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) is inherently subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) on worldwide profits at a 25% standard rate (15% up to €42,500 for eligible small businesses); no statutory pass-through option exists for an SAS (profits are taxed in the entity's own hands; distributions attract withholding tax subject to treaty relief); territorial scope is the worldwide income principle, with foreign permanent-establishment profits eligible for treaty-based exemption or group consolidation.

Available election forms

France does not apply the US check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms for an SAS: (1) groupe d'intégration fiscale (French tax consolidation under CGI Art. 223A) election for 95%-owned group members; (2) election for tax transparency in limited sectors only (e.g., qualifying professional or research structures — not an ordinary SAS); (3) capital-gains rollover elections for reinvested proceeds and real-estate/REIT-type regimes for certain qualifying vehicles; (4) VAT group election (unité d'assujettissement under CGI Art. 256 C for economically integrated entities); (5) hybrid mismatch elections and notifiable filing under DAC6/ATAD rules where applicable; (6) option for the French territorial company tax regime in narrow statutory circumstances (e.g., real-estate investment vehicles).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For established French businesses: annual turnover ceiling of €91,900 (net of VAT) for supplies of goods and €36,800 for service/construction supplies triggers mandatory VAT registration; no franchise-en-base applies on new buildings or immovable works. Non-established foreign suppliers are registrable from the first euro of French taxable activity without any threshold. For this runway-paving contract (services connected with French immovable property), VAT registration is mandatory irrespective of turnover from the commencement of works in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération) applies to the export of movable goods physically dispatched/transported from France to a destination outside the European Union, provided the supplier retains proof of export within 3 months (or an extended 12-month period in approved cases) and records the departure in customs archives. Services connected with French immovable property — including airport runway surfacing and asphalt paving performed in France — are outside the export zero-rating exemption; their place of supply is France and 20% French VAT must be charged even where the contractual customer is non-resident. Intra-EU B2B supplies of goods may be zero-rated only where the customer's valid EU VAT ID is obtained and the French report (ESA) identifies the transaction. Foreign contractors performing works in France should claim refund of French input VAT via the 8th/13th Directive refund procedure rather than zero-rating of services.

  • French customs export declaration (Déclaration en Douane, regime EX) with customs clearance stamp/electronic validation
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill or CMR road consignment note evidencing movement out of the EU
  • Proof of physical export (delivery certificate from the carrier, destination customs entry, or Export Delivery Certificate)
  • Commercial invoice and full packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT bank credit instruction evidencing payment of the export price from the non-EU customer
  • EORI validation report and Intrastat declaration where intra-EU supplies are involved

France mandates structured electronic invoicing in a phased rollout: from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for all sectors. Domestic B2B invoices must be transmitted via the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF / Chorus Pro) or through accredited Private Dematerialisation Platforms (PDP), using the EN 16931 / Factur-X data formats with API transmission (3WS endpoints). Separate e-reporting (e-reporting) obligations apply to B2C sales and cross-border transactions. Real-time clearance and digital stamping as in other jurisdictions are not applied for construction services; the regime operates through deferred transmission of transaction data. Penalties scale from €15 per invoice (capped at €15,000 annually) up to increased sanction levels for recurrent non-compliance.

DGFiP and DGDDI may audit all export documentation; failure to furnish conclusive proof of export within the statutory period triggers reassessment of the excluded turnover at 20% French VAT, plus procedural penalties: 40% for insincere declarations (mauvaise foi), 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres (manoeuvre frauduleuse), regulatory interest at 0.2% per month, and possible loss of deduction rights. Customs declaration fraud is a criminal offence under Code des Douanes Art. 414/414-1 punishable by up to 3 years' imprisonment and fines up to €450,000. Clawback is retrospective to the beginning of the statutory audit window — normally 3 years, extended to 6 years for tax fraud, with no time limit where the claim is tainted by money laundering.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is part of the euro area and operates under full current-account and capital-account convertibility pursuant to EU Treaty provisions and the French Monetary and Financial Code. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, loan repayments and reinvestable capital outflows are not subject to exchange-control approval, provided that the underlying transaction is lawful, accurately reported and supported by appropriate corporate and banking documentation. Cross-border monetary flows above statutory thresholds must be declared to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments purposes. There are no material legal impediments to profit repatriation for a French SAS executing an international airport runway asphalt paving contract.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose any mandatory surrender of export proceeds. Exporters may freely retain domestic and foreign-currency export receipts without any statutory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. The only binding obligation is to declare cross-border transactions above the regulatory threshold pursuant to Articles L.161-7 and R.161-7 of the French Monetary and Financial Code. For an ISIC 4210 runway paving scope, all foreign receipts should therefore be remitted directly into the SAS operational account and identified with an appropriate balance-of-payments purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France and the French Foreign Exchange Administration (Direction Générale des Finances et du Contrôle des Changes, Banque de France)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium and only as a complementary treasury rail. EU-licensed electronic money institutions can provide fast SEPA and SWIFT payment accounts, but they are not credit institutions and generally cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit, provide trade-finance escrow structures or offer committed credit lines. Offshore digital EMIs without a French or PSD2 passport are likely to be treated as high-risk counterparties by French banks and should not be used as primary capital escrow or operational clearing accounts for an international airport infrastructure project.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million (annual turnover or total assets) triggers mandatory full transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File). For smaller entities, a simplified documentation requirement applies if turnover exceeds EUR 50 million.

Economic substance scope: France applies the 'substance over form' principle and has a General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR). While no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist, entities must demonstrate real economic activity (e.g., employees, premises, decision-making) to avoid challenges under the anti-abuse provisions and to benefit from treaty or EU directives.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: up to EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment (increased to 7 years for aggravated cases). Administrative penalties: 40% to 80% of the tax evaded, plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they have committed fraud or mismanagement. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment (up to 5 years) are possible.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 40% penalty for lack of substance. The company may be dissolved and directors fined up to EUR 375,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly used the entity as a sham. Travel bans and imprisonment (up to 3 years) may apply.
AML (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: up to EUR 750,000 fine and 10 years imprisonment. Asset forfeiture and confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment and fines. Civil liability for damages, travel bans, and prohibition from managing companies.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty for lack of documentation, and interest. If deemed a sham, 80% penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the tax adjustment if they knowingly participated in the manipulation. Civil penalties and potential criminal charges for tax fraud.
