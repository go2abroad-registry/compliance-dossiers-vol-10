Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) acting through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE), with the professional portal accessible via impots.gouv.fr and the French Customs and Indirect Taxation Directorate (DGDDI) for import/export controls.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing French taxable presence (e.g. through a fixed establishment or a French registered branch of an SAS) must obtain French tax identification through the Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises or the relevant SIE. The procedure comprises: (1) filing the initial registration file (SIREN/SIRET) with INSEE, (2) obtaining the TVA intracommunautaire (French VAT ID) from DGFiP where liable to French VAT, and (3) obtaining a French Customs EORI number from DGDDI for customs and dangerous goods freight forwarding. The registration file must identify the entity’s legal form (Société par Actions Simplifiée), beneficial owner, statutory representation, place of technically effective management (principal establishment in France), and business license for regulated hazardous-material transport.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate United States-style check-the-box election. An SAS is a company taxable at entity level on French corporation tax at the standard 25% rate, including distributed profits subject to the parent-subsidiary regime for qualifying shareholders. Available statutory elections include the election to form a French tax group (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI where a 95% French or EEA parent controls the chain; the election to be subject to the simplified real-tax regime for small and medium companies; and the ATAD anti-hybrid neutralisation mechanism under Articles 205 A et seq. CGI, which applies automatically rather than by elective check-the-box. No pass-through election is available for an SAS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0.00. France has no de minimis VAT registration exemption for suppliers established or having a fixed establishment in France; every taxable person effecting taxable supplies in the course of an economic activity must register for French VAT from the first euro of taxable turnover. For non-established suppliers supplying B2B freight-forwarding and ancillary dangerous-goods logistics services to French or EU customers, a French VAT ID is required from the first transaction without any turnover threshold. Distance-selling thresholds are replaced by the OECD/EU OSS regime and do not affect the dangerous-goods freight-forwarding business model, which is overwhelmingly B2B.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (TVA exonérée avec droit à déduction) for exports is available under Article 262-I CGI for goods definitively exported outside the European Union. A qualifying exporter must be able to demonstrate that the goods physically left the EU in their export state. Services ancillary to exported goods, including international freight forwarding, storage of goods in transit, customs brokerage and dangerous-goods transport, may be zero-rated pursuant to Article 259-A 1° CGI when the services are directly related to goods that have been exported and are directly attributable to transport to a destination outside the EU. For B2B services to non-EU recipients not falling within the place-of-supply reverse-charge rules, French VAT will generally not apply. Sufficient contemporaneous shipping, contractual, safety and banking evidence must be maintained for at least the French statutory records retention period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document SAD/DELTA) with export summary declaration (EXA/EXE) filed through French Customs DELTA system
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing actual physical departure of the dangerous goods from the EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/broker substantiation that export proceeds will be repatriated and accounted for within statutory timelines
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching HS codes, quantity, gross/net weight and shipping marks
  • Dangerous goods transport documentation (IMDG/ADR declaration, Material Safety Data Sheet, Transport Emergency Card / TREM card, stowage and segregation certificate) proving compliance with regulated classification rules
  • Logistics contract, forwarding order and letter of indemnity demonstrating the SAS acted as principal freight forwarder or contractual carrier for the export movement

France mandates progressive domestic e-invoicing from 1 September 2026 for large and intermediate enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small and very small enterprises, through certified dematerialisation platforms (PDPs) and the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) architecture. Structured electronic data (Factur-X/UBL/CII) and transaction-level e-reporting will be transmitted to DGFiP via the PPF. For export customs clearance, DGDDI requires digital submission of the Single Administrative Document (SAD/DELTA) on the DELTA platform, plus the export summary declaration (EXA/EXE) in ICS and real-time validation of dangerous-goods declarations against regulated classification codes. No French ‘fiscal stamping’ or invoice-level real-time clearance mechanism currently applies, but excise and safety-controlled movements of hazardous chemicals may be subject to EU Excise Movement and Control System controls and submission to French Customs for release of transit/export consignment.

French tax and customs authorities may undertake a retrospectivereclassification of zero-rated export transactions where export documentation is missing, contradictory, or fraudulent. The administration may assess the 20% French VAT as due from the exporter, add statutory late-payment interest, and apply penalties under Article 1729 CGI of up to 40% for negligence or up to 80% for serious misstatement or fraudulent conduct. Where the zero-rating claim is part of a systematic abuse using fictitious export documentation, the exporter and its officers may face criminal penalties under Articles 1741 and 1743 CGI, including imprisonment, financial penalties of up to EUR 1,000,000 and personal penalties, together with customs forfeiture sanctions for breach of DGDDI customs regulations. For dangerous-goods forwarders, failure to substantiate valid export may also trigger administrative suspension or withdrawal of the regulated transport licence and joint liability for unpaid excise duties or safety-control penalties under relevant environmental and transport laws.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a euro-area member state, has full convertibility of the euro for both current and capital account transactions in accordance with EU Capital Movements Directive 88/361/EEC and the TFEU free movement of capital provisions. Profit remittances from an SAS can be executed freely after statutory audit and tax compliance, subject to anti-money laundering / counter-terrorist financing (AML/CFT) screening, EU sanctions and restrictive measures, and the ordinary declaration duties set out in the Code monétaire et financier (CMF). No exchange control rationing, repatriation delays, or surrender requirements apply to EUR or freely convertible currencies. Non-EU direct investments, particularly into sectors affecting the transport of dangerous goods or dual-use sensitive supply chains, may trigger ex-ante screening under EU Regulation 2019/452 and the associated French foreign-investment control rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France maintains no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio for exporters; proceeds may be retained in EUR or any freely convertible currency. The only formal requirements are (i) accurate submission of export declarations in the customs Single Administrative Document (SAD/EX1) for the physical movement of dangerous goods, (ii) bank-driven reporting to the Banque de France for each cross-border flow exceeding the applicable reporting threshold (EUR 50,000 per operation for statistical balance-of-payments reporting), and (iii) compliance with EU sanctions, export-control licensing, and dual-use regulations for regulated hazardous chemicals or dangerous goods.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as banking and payment-institution supervisor, jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) at the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty for cross-border capital movement rules and FX-related authorizations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for primary treasury operations. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) without a PSD2 license properly passported into France cannot legally provide current accounts, payment services, or e-money issuance to a French-resident SAS. ACPR-licensed or EEA-passported fintech EMIs may offer secondary disbursement accounts, but they are not appropriate as the sole treasury rail for a High-Risk dangerous-goods forwarding entity that requires documentary credits, sanctioned-jurisdiction filtering, and specialist correspondent banking. Conventional tier-1 banks remain mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound non-resident capital contributions to the French SAS must be wired over SWIFT using MT103 for third-party transfers or MT202 for nostro/vostro interbank movements. The instructing bank must include a transparent human-readable purpose code in field 72: 'CAPITAL INCREASE' or 'APPORT EN COMPTE COURANT'; for balance-of-payments classification the EU ROC code used by French banks is typically 'B.7.1' or 'C.3.1' for direct-investment equity, and 'C.2.1' for shareholder loans, while custom references should state the notarial/social resolution date and capital-increase amount. The receiving French bank will require the shareholder resolution, proof of source of funds, and for any non-EU direct investment above EUR 1.5 million the mandatory ex-ante or short ex-post statistical declaration to the Banque de France using its Inbound Foreign Direct Investment reporting portal. If an escrow account is used for SAS formation, release to the operational account is permitted only after the deposit of the capital (blocage du capital) is lifted by the Caisse des Dépôts or legal depositary, and after registration of the SAS on the RNE.For international trade settlements France uses TARGET2 for intra-EU EUR corridors and the SWIFT network for global wires. Generic outbound cargo payments and freight disbursements are executed using SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary details and the relevant EU ROC purpose code 'C.2.4' (trade credit) or 'C.1.1' (services). Commercial documentary credits are issued through password-authenticated SWIFT MT700 conforming to ICC UCP600; for dangerous-goods transport, transport documents (airway bill, Bill of Lading, and Dangerous Goods Declaration) must be exact in description, quantity, UN number, and packing group. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for primary treasury operations. Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) without a PSD2 license properly passported into France cannot legally provide current accounts, payment services, or e-money issuance to a French-resident SAS. ACPR-licensed or EEA-passported fintech EMIs may offer secondary disbursement accounts, but they are not appropriate as the sole treasury rail for a High-Risk dangerous-goods forwarding entity that requires documentary credits, sanctioned-jurisdiction filtering, and specialist correspondent banking. Conventional tier-1 banks remain mandatory.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard French corporate income tax rate for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2022; qualifying SMEs pay 15% on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit and 25% on the remainder)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% (French domestic dividend withholding tax under Article 119 bis CGI) for dividends distributed to non-resident legal entities and individuals; treaty rates often reduce to 15%, 5%, or 0%. An EU/EEA parent company holding directly or indirectly at least 5% of the capital of the SAS for a continuous two-year period may qualify for a 0% withholding exemption under the French Parent-Subsidiary Directive implementation (Article 119 ter CGI).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 (full Local File and Master File obligations are triggered when the French-domiciled entity or the consolidated group to which it belongs has annual consolidated turnover or balance-sheet total exceeding EUR 400,000,000); simultaneously, any SAS with related-party flows exceeding EUR 1,000,000 is subject to simplified French form 2257 intercompany declaration

Economic substance scope: France has no separate Economic Substance Regulations comparable to offshore financial centres, but French abus-de-droit doctrine (Article L64 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales) plus DTA anti-abuse clauses impose a substance-over-form test. A DG forwarding SAS must maintain genuine operational substance: licensed dangerous-goods staff (IATA/IMDG-certified), physical offices or warehouses, insurance, fixed assets, bank accounts, invoices and contracts; otherwise treaty benefits may be denied and the entity recharacterised as a sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Civil: penalty of 10% up to 80% of the evaded tax plus monthly late interest of 0.20%. Criminal (CGI Article 1741): up to three years' imprisonment and EUR 45,000 fine; aggravated offence (offshore concealment or organised fraud) rises to seven years and up to EUR 250,000 fine, publicity judgment, and asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors risk criminal conviction and imprisonment, travel bans, prohibition from commercial activity for up to ten years, personal collection of unpaid taxes and penalties, and civil liability to the company for any resultant loss or insolvency.
Artificial Arrangement / Shell Company (Abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment of profits to the actual non-resident operator; 80% penalty under Article L64 LPFP plus late interest; denial of treaty benefits and, where applicable, reclassification of payments as constructive dividends subject to 25% withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers are jointly liable for reassessed taxes and penalties; the corporate veil may be pierced, leading to collection over personal assets, commercial disqualification, and criminal sanctions if intent is established.
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Terrorist Financing (AML/CTF)

Statutory sanction: Administrative: suspension or revocation of the DG forwarding licence, fines up to EUR 2,000,000 under ACPR/DO orders. Criminal: money-laundering offences under the Monetary and Financial Code are punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine; aggravated cases rise to ten years and EUR 20,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally subject to criminal imprisonment, fines, asset freezes, travel bans, and professional disqualification; they may also face tort liability for sanctions violations and damage to third parties.
Transfer-Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1735 bis CGI, a penalty equal to 5% of the amount of undisclosed or undocumentable intercompany transactions, with no statutory amnesty; additional ordinary reassessment plus late interest; penalty can reach 40%-80% in cases of deliberate or abusive manipulation.

Director personal liability: If directors knowingly caused or tolerated the defect, French courts may hold them personally liable for the penalty under management-fault (faute de gestion) doctrine, and criminal exposure arises where false documents are presented to the tax authority.
Unlawful Dividend Repatriation / Improper Treaty Claim

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as a dividend subject to the full 25% French withholding tax, plus interest and a 40% penalty for bad faith; if the covered payment is a royalty reclassified with intentional concealment, an 80% abuse-of-law penalty can apply; criminal fraud sanctions are available.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved the misclassification may be jointly required to remit the unpaid withholding taxes with penalties; criminal conviction can add imprisonment, travel bans, and judicial liquidation of personal assets.
