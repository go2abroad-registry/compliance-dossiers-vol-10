Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), with electronic administration available at portail impots.gouv.fr and the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in France must register for French VAT by filing the applicable registration form with the SIEE, supplying the company's foreign commercial registration certificate, a legalised copy of the constitutional documents, an extract of the trade register in the jurisdiction of establishment, proof of the actual place of business, and identity documents of the legal representative. Upon approval, DGFiP issues a SIREN number, a SIRET establishment number, and a French VAT identification number. A locally incorporated SAS is registered contemporaneously with incorporation at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce, with the SIREN issued by INSEE and the VAT number subsequently activated by DGFiP from the SIREN details. Registration for the special non-established taxable person scheme is carried out before the SIEE and includes designation of a local tax agent if no fixed establishment is created.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under French law. A SAS is a corporation subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by legal form, with no pass-through classification option. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; the French General Tax Code incorporates the ATAD anti-hybrid rules denying deductions in qualified mismatch situations. The only relevant corporate election is the option for tax consolidation by a qualifying French parent under the régime d'intégration fiscale, which is not a classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established foreign suppliers supplying electronically supplied services to French non-taxable persons have no general registration threshold and are taxable from the first supply. For intra-Community distance sales to French consumers, the harmonised EU threshold is EUR 10,000 in annual cross-border turnover. Domestic French micro-business thresholds for established taxable persons are EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services under CGI Article 302 septies, subject to periodic revision.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France or another EU Member State to a destination outside the European Union qualify for the French 0% VAT rate, provided the seller proves that the goods have been physically dispatched to a third country and the exportation occurs no later than 90 days after the supply. If goods are sold B2B to a customer outside the EU, French VAT is eliminated by place-of-supply rules without requiring zero-rating documentation. For B2C digital services, social casino and virtual slot games supplied to non-EU consumers are outside French VAT because the place of supply is the customer's residence outside the EU; for EU consumers, the OSS system or standard French registration applies depending on where the customer is established.

France is implementing mandatory structured e-invoicing under the e-invoicing reform: B2B invoices must be issued as structured electronic documents and circulated through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or certified dematerialisation platforms (plates-formes de dématérialisation partenaires, PDP). API-based transmission and e-reporting to the PPF is mandatory in phased dates for large enterprises, mid-sized enterprises, and SME/SAS. Digital stamping and real-time continuous transaction clearance are not yet active, but the PPF transmission creates a fiscal clearance obligation with files transmitted in real time upon invoice issuance.

France applies strict audit scrutiny to zero-rated export transactions. If the exporter fails to produce compliant export evidence within the statutory retention period or uses false or manipulated documentation, the DGFiP may retroactively re-apply 20% French VAT plus default interest. Negligence gives rise to a 40% penalty under CGI Article 1728/1729, while bad faith gives rise to an 80% penalty; criminal fines and imprisonment apply in cases of customs and VAT fraud. The administration may also disallow the export exemption, issue clawback assessments, and impose joint liability, and payment processors and gaming platforms may be exposed to ancillary payment-blocking measures in case of unresolved VAT liabilities.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France adheres to the Eurozone regime in which both capital and current accounts are fully convertible. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders encounter no statutory barriers for EU-established entities; however, all transactions exceeding €10,000 must be declared to the Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) and Banque de France for statistical and anti-money laundering monitoring purposes. No capital controls or surrender obligations apply to digital service exports.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Digital publishing receipts fall under current-account treatment with no mandatory surrender timeline or conversion ratio. Proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts upon prior Banque de France authorization if thresholds are exceeded, but repatriation remains unrestricted and subject only to standard tax reporting.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2022.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident shareholder, subject to exemptions and tax treaties. The French parent-subsidiary regime may reduce withholding to 0% where statutory conditions are met, commonly including qualifying EU or EEA corporate status, minimum 10% ownership and the required holding or commitment period. Treaty rates commonly range from 0% to 15%, depending on the recipient, ownership level and treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, filing is generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the following year. For a financial year ending on a date other than 31 December, the electronic filing deadline is generally within three months after the year-end. The applicable online calendar published by the French tax administration controls the exact date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Four instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, subject to the company's financial year and statutory exceptions. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month after year-end, or by 15 May for a 31 December year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally filed within the same deadline as the corporate income tax return, subject to the applicable financial year and tax-administration calendar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory full transfer-pricing documentation generally applies where the French entity or relevant group satisfies the statutory EUR 150 million threshold based on turnover or gross assets, or where specified group or ownership conditions bring the entity within the regime. A simplified annual transfer-pricing declaration generally applies to entities meeting the relevant EUR 50 million threshold or other statutory connection criteria. The precise threshold must be tested against the applicable financial year and current Article L13 AA and L13 AB rules.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone ESR regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdiction economic-substance statutes. Substance is nevertheless central to the French anti-abuse framework, beneficial-ownership analysis, EU Interest and Royalties Directive relief, parent-subsidiary relief, controlled foreign company rules, permanent-establishment analysis and transfer pricing. The SAS should maintain real decision-making, qualified personnel, premises or operational capability appropriate to its functions, risk control over the platform and game publishing business, ownership or licensed use of intellectual property, and contemporaneous commercial evidence. Artificial conduit arrangements, circular financing, unsupported royalties, cost allocations without benefit, and profit allocations inconsistent with functions and risks are high-risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, a penalty commonly up to 80% for fraudulent maneuvers, and criminal tax-fraud sanctions that may include imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000. Aggravated circumstances can increase exposure to up to seven years' imprisonment and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: A director may be personally ordered to pay tax debts under Article L267 of the French Tax Procedures Book where fraudulent maneuvers or serious and repeated breaches made collection impossible. Criminal liability may attach for personal participation, complicity or delegation failures. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from judicial control, criminal proceedings or enforcement orders.
Undeclared foreign accounts and assets

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, tax reassessment, interest and penalties may apply; the amount depends on the account jurisdiction, balance, reporting obligation and applicable statutory provision. Suspected concealed assets may also trigger enhanced audit and criminal tax-fraud exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling persons may face personal reporting liability where they own, control or operate the accounts. Personal exposure may include civil tax recovery, criminal liability for deliberate concealment and asset-freezing measures.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: French tax reassessment under the arm's-length principle, denial of deductions, withholding-tax exposure, late interest and documentation penalties. Missing or inadequate documentation may trigger the statutory greater-of penalty based on EUR 10,000 or a percentage of covered transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil tax liability under Article L267 where serious repeated noncompliance prevents collection and may face criminal exposure if they knowingly organize fraudulent profit shifting.
Shell company, conduit or beneficial-ownership abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or EU withholding exemptions, withholding-tax reassessment, abuse-of-law penalties commonly up to 80% in the most serious cases, late interest and possible criminal tax-fraud proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or knowingly implement the arrangement may incur personal civil and criminal liability. Corporate separateness does not automatically shield deliberate personal misconduct.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing failure

Statutory sanction: Depending on the regulated status and conduct, sanctions may include administrative reprimands, business restrictions, monetary penalties, confiscation and criminal prosecution. Payment institutions, regulated intermediaries and professional advisers may have direct TRACFIN reporting duties.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation, laundering, sanctions breaches or obstruction. Judicial asset freezes, bail conditions and travel restrictions may be imposed where legally justified.
Payment-platform and consumer-revenue concealment

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax, VAT and potentially withholding-tax reassessments, interest, late-filing penalties and fraud penalties. Digital platform records may be obtained or cross-checked during an audit.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for intentional concealment, false accounting, fraudulent tax filings or serious repeated compliance failures. Unjustified travel bans are not automatic but can arise in criminal proceedings.
VAT and digital-services noncompliance

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, interest, late penalties, refusal of input-tax deductions and potential fraud penalties. Marketplace and platform documentation may be required to establish gross sales, commissions, refunds and customer location.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise for deliberate VAT fraud, false records or serious repeated breaches causing uncollectible tax, including possible criminal proceedings.
Unlawful distributions and insolvency misconduct

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, civil damages, insolvency clawback, management bans, contribution to insufficient assets and, in serious cases, criminal penalties for misuse of corporate assets or bankruptcy-related offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to contribute to insolvency shortfalls, prohibited from managing companies and prosecuted for misuse of corporate assets or fraudulent bankruptcy. Travel restrictions depend on the criminal or insolvency proceeding and are not automatic.
