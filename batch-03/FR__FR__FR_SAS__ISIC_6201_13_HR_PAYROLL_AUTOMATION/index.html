Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry) for the registered-office jurisdiction, operating through the national Guichet Unique portal of the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 and L.227-1 to L.227-30 (SAS regime); implementing rules of the RCS electronic filing system.

Formation / constitutional filings

Single digital dossier for company creation containing the signed and dated Statuts (Articles of Association), the declaration of conformity (déclaration de constitution), and the certificate of publication of the legal announcement.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SAS has no legally fixed minimum share capital; the Articles freely fix the capital amount. Cash contributions must be blocked on a dedicated bank account in the name of the company-in-formation until the KBIS entry is issued when an obligation of deposit is applied; contributions in kind must be fully paid up at incorporation and independently appraised when applicable.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a fire prevention attestation from an approved inspection body or the local fire safety service confirming that the office space complies with the French Labour Code and construction regulations. Conduct a structural load assessment for server rooms or heavy archive units, complete an occupational health and safety audit by the occupational physician, provide first aid equipment and compliance with emergency exit rules, and secure the required workplace clearance for office occupancy.

Paid-in capital rule: SAS has no legally fixed minimum share capital; the Articles freely fix the capital amount. Cash contributions must be blocked on a dedicated bank account in the name of the company-in-formation until the KBIS entry is issued when an obligation of deposit is applied; contributions in kind must be fully paid up at incorporation and independently appraised when applicable.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The undersigned shareholders, acting in general meeting, resolve: (i) the Société par Actions Simplifiée is hereby created under the Articles of Association read and approved; (ii) the entire share capital is subscribed by the shareholders and, to the extent required, paid up in accordance with the contribution terms; (iii) the registered office is fixed at the address specified in the Articles, and the President is authorised to execute any lease; (iv) [Name] is appointed President, with full corporate authority; (v) the President shall complete all filings before the Commercial Court Registry and regulators and pay all incorporation expenses. This resolution takes effect immediately.

Not applicable; no industrial operating permit is required for an ISIC 6201 / NAICS 541214 payroll software business under French industrial installations regulations. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — official portal: impots.gouv.fr; business tax registration and VAT management via the professional portal 'Impôt particulier et professionnel' and the single registration system 'Gestion Unifiée de l'Immatriculation des Entreprises' / formalités-formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a French SAS must first complete corporate registration with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through a Centre de Formalités des Entreprises or the online Guichet Unique. The SIREN number, RCS registration, and the SIRET establishment identifier are automatically generated. The VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) is issued by the DGFiP upon collection of registration data from the tax office; in practice the French tax authorities assign the intra-Community VAT number after the company is registered and has filed its initial existence declaration. For a non-resident company without a permanent establishment, a direct application to the DGFiP's Service des impôts des entreprises is required, and a VAT representative (représentant fiscal) must be appointed if the company is not established in the EU. The SAS should file form 11386*12 and related Beneficial Ownership declarations, and must obtain a personal identification number (numéro fiscal) to access the online 'Espace Professionnel' for filing VAT returns and corporate income tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France applies entity-level taxation for SAS. The SAS is a corporate entity subject to corporate income tax on its worldwide income unless it qualifies for the territorial scope limitation for foreign permanent establishments. A French SAS may elect to be treated as a 'Société de personnes' under specific narrow rules only if it has opted for the regime of 'Transparence fiscale' pursuant to Article 239 of the CGI, which is available to certain holding entities; standard commercial SAS cannot check-the-box as a pass-through entity. Hybrid mismatch elections are governed by the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) implementation in the French General Tax Code (CGI), specifically Article 212 bis, with strict anti-hybrid rules; no general elective pass-through regime exists for a commercial SAS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,800 for sales of goods (deliveries) and EUR 36,800 for services for certain small businesses; however, the general EU distance selling threshold and the Union threshold for intra-Community distance sales is EUR 10,000 for domestic supplies to non-taxable persons when the supplier is established in France; for foreign non-established businesses, no registration threshold applies and VAT registration is required from the first euro of taxable supplies in France, subject to the Special Scheme for distance sales and the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the European Union and intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-registered acquirer in another Member State are eligible for zero-rating (0% VAT) under Articles 262-262 ter of the French General Tax Code. The zero-rating applies only if the seller retains proof of export within 90 days for EU supplies or within the legal deadlines for dispatch. Services related to exported goods, such as freight, customs brokerage, and certain international services, may also be zero-rated if they are directly linked to the physical export. The French administration requires valid evidence of the physical movement of goods and the purchase reference; if shipping terms and documents are incomplete, the export sale is subject to 20% VAT plus penalties. Offshore services provided to a non-French business customer outside France are generally zero-rated if the place of supply rules place the supply outside France under Article 259 A of the CGI.

France has mandated electronic invoicing for all B2B transactions, effective 1 September 2026 for large companies and 1 September 2027 for small and medium-sized enterprises, pursuant to the Finance Law 2023. E-invoicing must be delivered through a registered Dematerialization Operator (PDP) or the DGFiP Public Portal 'Chorus Pro' using the Factur-X (hybrid), UBL, or CII formats. The transmission of transaction data to the tax administration for tax clearance is mandatory via the 'e-TVA' and 'e-invoicing' ecosystem. The fiscal clearance mechanism requires digital stamping and real-time reporting of transaction data to the DGFiP through the PDP or the public portal; each invoice must obtain a unique digital identifier and be reported in a structured data feed. Chorus Pro is not only for public procurement invoices; it is being expanded to the general mandatory domestic e-invoicing system. No physical stamp duty applies; digital validation is via invoice data transmission and the new 'Facturation électronique' reform.

French customs and tax authorities conduct post-clearance audits of export files. If the taxpayer fails to produce the required export documentation within the statutory period or provides false documentation, the DGFiP may reassess the VAT at the standard 20% rate, apply 100% exposure to the VAT amount, and assess penalties under Article 1788 A of the CGI and Article 1737, paragraph 1 (50% penalty applicable to fraudulent VAT claims or 80% for abusive fraud). Additionally, the 'amende fiscale' under Article 1788 ter can be imposed (EUR 150 per missing proof) if the export evidence is incomplete. Criminal penalties for serious export fraud include imprisonment, fines, and potential customs fines equal to 1 to 3 times the value of the goods. The 180-day repatriation deadline is not explicitly codified as a French VAT rule; however, French tax practice requires that the payment from the foreign customer be received within a commercially reasonable period, and the failure to receive payment may trigger a written-off receivable that must be substantiated to avoid VAT clawback on the concept of taxable consideration under Article 266 of the CGI. If export documents are falsified or the transaction lacks economic substance, the VAT exemption is cancelled, the VAT becomes due, and the full legal penalties, including the 80% penalty for tax fraud, apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no general French exchange-control regime requiring prior approval for foreign-currency payments, intercompany funding, dividend remittances, loan repayments, or acquisition and disposal of foreign assets. Transactions remain subject to anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing rules, sanctions screening, beneficial-owner identification, tax reporting, transfer-pricing requirements, foreign-investment controls in sensitive sectors, and applicable European Union restrictions. A French SAS may generally remit post-tax profits, dividends, interest, royalties, and loan principal to non-residents, provided corporate approvals, distributable-profit rules, withholding-tax analysis, treaty documentation, transfer-pricing support, and bank compliance checks are satisfied. Dividend payments require legally available distributable amounts and, where applicable, approval by the competent corporate body. Banks may delay or reject payments lacking a clear economic purpose, source-of-funds evidence, beneficiary transparency, or supporting tax and corporate documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement for a resident company to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, convert a prescribed percentage into euros, or deposit export receipts into a designated domestic account. No general mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export proceeds may ordinarily remain in euros or foreign currency and may be received into French or foreign accounts, subject to banking, sanctions, customs, tax, accounting, and anti-money-laundering controls. Individual transactions may be subject to Banque de France statistical reporting, bank information requests, customs documentation, or sector-specific restrictions. Exporters should retain contracts, invoices, transport evidence, customs declarations, payment records, and reconciliation files for the applicable accounting, tax, customs, and anti-money-laundering retention periods.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. Foreign-exchange and cross-border payment policy is also governed by the European Union, the French Ministry for the Economy and Finance, the French Treasury Directorate, the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, and the Autorité des marchés financiers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for payment accounts, SEPA transfers, collections, foreign-currency payments, and treasury sub-ledgers; not equivalent to a French bank deposit account. An offshore electronic-money institution or payment institution may serve a French SAS only if it is duly authorised in its home European Economic Area state and validly passported into France, or otherwise authorised under the applicable French and European regime. A non-EEA provider generally cannot provide regulated payment or electronic-money services into France solely through an offshore website. Safeguarded electronic-money funds are not bank deposits and generally do not benefit from the French deposit-guarantee scheme. The company must verify the provider's register status, passport notification, safeguarding model, settlement-bank exposure, EUR and foreign-currency capabilities, insolvency protections, access to SEPA schemes, transaction limits, sanctions controls, data-processing arrangements, and business-continuity provisions. Use of an EMI does not remove French accounting, payroll, tax, sanctions, AML, or corporate-record obligations.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Incomplete or inconsistent beneficial-ownership information, including a nominee shareholder, multilayer foreign holding chain, unexplained control rights, or inability to identify the natural person ultimately controlling the SAS.

Root cause: Incomplete or inconsistent beneficial-ownership information, including a nominee shareholder, multilayer foreign holding chain, unexplained control rights, or inability to identify the natural person ultimately controlling the SAS.

Defense evidence: Current Kbis extract or equivalent registration evidence; articles of association; shareholder register; ownership chart to each natural-person ultimate beneficial owner; passports and residential-address evidence for directors, shareholders, and beneficial owners; board or shareholder resolutions; declarations of control; and, where relevant, certified corporate registry extracts, apostilles, translations, and trust or foundation documents.
Newly formed company with no operating history, no local revenue, limited capitalization, or a business plan involving substantial international payment volumes.

Root cause: Newly formed company with no operating history, no local revenue, limited capitalization, or a business plan involving substantial international payment volumes.

Defense evidence: Detailed business plan; product and service description; projected cash-flow and transaction-volume schedule; customer and supplier geography; signed contracts or letters of intent; pricing model; capitalization evidence; source-of-funds evidence; tax registration information; management biographies; expected counterparties; and a written explanation reconciling projected activity with the company's industry and staffing.
Payroll and HR software model potentially involving client payroll funds, employee funds, wage payments, tax remittances, or payment collection on behalf of third parties.

Root cause: Payroll and HR software model potentially involving client payroll funds, employee funds, wage payments, tax remittances, or payment collection on behalf of third parties.

Defense evidence: Legal analysis confirming whether the SAS only provides software or performs regulated payment, money-remittance, payment-initiation, account-information, or payroll-payment services; client-funds flowchart; terms of service; segregation and safeguarding analysis; contracts with licensed payment institutions or banks; prohibition of unauthorised custody of client funds; transaction-monitoring policy; refund and reconciliation procedures; and written confirmation of the regulatory perimeter.
High volume of incoming payments from unrelated foreign customers or payments routed through high-risk, sanctioned, or opaque jurisdictions.

Root cause: High volume of incoming payments from unrelated foreign customers or payments routed through high-risk, sanctioned, or opaque jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Customer master file; beneficial-owner and sanctions-screening results; invoices and contracts; service-delivery evidence; payment-purpose mapping; country-risk assessment; transaction-monitoring rules; escalation logs; correspondent-bank information; and documented prohibition or enhanced approval process for restricted jurisdictions and counterparties.
Transfers described as capital, loans, management fees, royalties, or intercompany settlements without consistent legal, accounting, and tax treatment.

Root cause: Transfers described as capital, loans, management fees, royalties, or intercompany settlements without consistent legal, accounting, and tax treatment.

Defense evidence: Executed shareholder or intercompany loan agreement; capital-increase documentation; bank certificate; board or shareholder resolutions; general-ledger entries; transfer-pricing policy; invoices; withholding-tax analysis; treaty forms; beneficial-owner evidence; repayment schedule; and reconciliation between the payment message, contract, accounting entry, and tax filing.
Unexplained source of wealth or source of funds for non-resident shareholders, directors, or investors.

Root cause: Unexplained source of wealth or source of funds for non-resident shareholders, directors, or investors.

Defense evidence: Audited financial statements; bank statements; sale agreements; dividend records; tax returns; investment-fund statements; loan agreements; inheritance or probate evidence; corporate transaction documents; and a signed funds-flow memorandum linking the original source to the proposed French account deposit.
Sanctions, politically exposed person, adverse-media, export-control, or dual-use-technology concern.

Root cause: Sanctions, politically exposed person, adverse-media, export-control, or dual-use-technology concern.

Defense evidence: Identity and nationality records; sanctions-screening reports; PEP declarations; adverse-media remediation memorandum; customer and supplier screening policy; end-use and end-user statements; export-control classification; jurisdictional risk assessment; compliance-officer approval; and evidence of blocked or rejected transactions where applicable.
Use of virtual assets, privacy-enhancing technologies, unregulated payment intermediaries, or cash-intensive funding.

Root cause: Use of virtual assets, privacy-enhancing technologies, unregulated payment intermediaries, or cash-intensive funding.

Defense evidence: Complete transaction history; regulated exchange records; wallet ownership evidence; blockchain analytics; source-of-funds tracing; tax treatment; AML controls of each intermediary; explanation of business necessity; and a documented policy prohibiting unidentified or unregulated funding sources.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) for fiscal years opened on or after 1 January 2022; with the 3.3% social surcharge (contribution sociale sur l'IS) applicable when the annual CIT exceeds €763,000, the aggregate top marginal rate is 25.83%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory withholding on dividends distributed by a French SAS to non-resident corporate shareholders (12.8% flat income-tax withholding plus 17.2% social levies), payable through the distribution statement Form 2561; subject to reduction or elimination by the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and double-tax treaties.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months of the fiscal year-end; for a fiscal year ending 31 December the deadline is the second business day after 1 May (normally 3 May; postponed to the next working day if 3 May falls on a weekend or public holiday). The balance of CIT (solde) is payable on the same date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year taxpayers: instalments due 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March of the following year; each instalment equals 25% of the CIT liability for the preceding fiscal year; due only if annual CIT exceeded €3,000 in the prior year.

Form 2065, Box 'Résultat fiscal' (net taxable income before set-offs) Aggregation of the company's profit before tax, after adding-back non-deductible expenses and deducting exempt items; the loss carry-forward and carry-back set-offs are disclosed in this field. The French SAS is tax-resident in France and discloses its worldwide income; income from foreign branch/subsidiary activities must be included or covered by the relevant treaty exemption. For a non-resident parent group, no consolidation is permitted in the French liasse; each legal entity files separately. Late filing of the liasse fiscale: 10% automatic increase of the tax (30 days delay), 40% for bad faith, 80% for abusive/fraudulent conduct (Article 1728 CGI), plus late-payment interest of 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI); late payment of the balance: 5% penalty plus 0.20% per month; Form 2257 defects: €150 per missing item of information; omissions can trigger spontaneous tax re-assessment and the 40% negligence penalty or 80% misstatement penalty.
Form 2058-SD, section 'Réintégrations diverses / Déductions diverses' Add-backs for non-deductible expenditure (e.g., excessive director remuneration, fines, 30% of certain luxury car depreciation, non-justified management fees) and deductions (e.g., prior-year losses, succession/carry-back). Cross-border HR payroll revenues are sourced by the French entity's residence and economic activity; transactions with non-resident group companies must be benchmarked and reported in the TP documentation attached to the return file. CIR is claimed by the French SAS on its own eligible French R&D expenditures; work conducted for a foreign parent must be carried out in France and be contractually and technically substantiated by records retained in France in case of audit. Required when French turnover or gross assets ≥ €50,000,000, or when the entity holds/ is held >50% by a foreign undertaking meeting that threshold; the statement must confirm the arm's length nature of intercompany SaaS IP royalties and payroll data-processing service fees. Late filing of the liasse fiscale: 10% automatic increase of the tax (30 days delay), 40% for bad faith, 80% for abusive/fraudulent conduct (Article 1728 CGI), plus late-payment interest of 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI); late payment of the balance: 5% penalty plus 0.20% per month; Form 2257 defects: €150 per missing item of information; omissions can trigger spontaneous tax re-assessment and the 40% negligence penalty or 80% misstatement penalty.
Form 2571, boxes for 'Résultat fiscal de référence' (reference taxable income) and 'Montant de l'impôt de référence' (reference tax base) Determining the instalment base by reference to the previous year's assessed income and tax. The instalment schedule follows the French entity's own tax result only; foreign parent's profit or loss is irrelevant to the French computation. Adjustments to the instalment amounts may be made upon assertion of loss carry-forwards, CIR or reduced-rate results.
