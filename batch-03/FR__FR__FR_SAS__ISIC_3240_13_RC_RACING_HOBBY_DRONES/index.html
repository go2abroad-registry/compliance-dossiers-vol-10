Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent local Registry of the Commercial Court (Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce) through the national Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 et seq. for the Société par Actions Simplifiée; Articles L.123-1 et seq. and R.123-1 et seq. for registration in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés; Articles L.561-46 et seq. for beneficial ownership filing.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of creation of a company filed through the Guichet unique, supported by executed SAS articles of association, capital deposit certificate, publication notice, and beneficial ownership declaration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts before filing, and the deposit certificate must be submitted. Contributions in kind require valuation by a commissaire aux apports unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied.

Funding & unlock path: No single national pre-opening occupational clearance applies to ordinary SAS manufacturing premises. The operator must complete workplace risk assessment in the DUERP, provide machine guarding, electrical safety, ventilation, personal protective equipment, chemical risk controls, training, and occupational-health arrangements. Fire-safety systems, emergency exits, extinguishers, alarm arrangements, and electrical inspections must comply with the premises classification. An ERP safety opinion or opening authorization is required if the site receives the public. Structural, fire, and electrical inspections must be obtained where required by the building owner, insurer, labor rules, or local authority.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts before filing, and the deposit certificate must be submitted. Contributions in kind require valuation by a commissaire aux apports unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) of the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique. Operative portals: impots.gouv.fr (tax filing, VAT returns, TVA intracommunautaire identification) and formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique des formalités d'entreprises). Large-business VAT compliance is channeled through the Direction des grandes entreprises (DGE, for turnover above EUR 400 million); all other SAS taxpayers file with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the SAS incorporation dossier with the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), replacing the former Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE); an SAS requires no minimum share capital. Step 2: INPI verifies the dossier and records the company at the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), generating the 9-digit SIREN code; INSEE then assigns a 14-digit SIRET number for each establishment. Step 3: DGFiP automatically allocates the TVA intracommunautaire number (format FR + 11 digits) through identification data transmitted by the Guichet Unique; no taxable transaction may occur before this number is effective. Step 4: A non-resident seller without a French establishment that is not covered by OSS or IOSS must apply directly to DGFiP using form Cerfa n°15755 (demande d'identification TVA en France), appoint a French fiscal representative where legally required, and obtain a French VAT number before any delivery of goods into France. The same registration activates the SIRENE database, the FEC (Fichier des Écritures Comptables) obligation, and the electronic filing channel (Espace Professionnel).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. An SAS is compulsorily subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) under Article 206(1) CGI at the entity level; there is no pass-through option analogous to a US check-the-box election. Territorial scope: resident SAS are taxed on worldwide profits, with foreign permanent establishment profits relieved under applicable tax treaties and allocated profits method; non-resident branches are taxed only on French-source profits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. An SAS is compulsorily subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) under Article 206(1) CGI at the entity level; there is no pass-through option analogous to a US check-the-box election. Territorial scope: resident SAS are taxed on worldwide profits, with foreign permanent establishment profits relieved under applicable tax treaties and allocated profits method; non-resident branches are taxed only on French-source profits. The standard IS headline rate is 25%, with a reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs whose turnover does not exceed EUR 7.63 million, a bracket normally available to a newly incorporated FPV drone retail SAS.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Available French statutory elections: (1) Fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI, available to a French parent holding at least 95% of its SAS subsidiaries, allowing group profit and loss consolidation via form Cerfa n°2095-FC; (2) Franchise en base de TVA under Article 293 B CGI, under which a goods seller with annual turnover up to EUR 85,000 may elect or waive the VAT simplification, although OSS and e-commerce obligations normally make VAT registration commercially mandatory; (3) option for the régime réel normal instead of the régime réel simplifié d'imposition for VAT instalments and filing cycles; (4) the 15% reduced IS rate on the first EUR 42,500 of profit is a statutory bracket, not an election; (5) anti-hybrid rules under Articles 212-III to 212-V CGI operate by statutory application and present no elective choice for a standard SAS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

0 EUR for a resident French SAS; VAT identification is compulsory from incorporation and no general small-trader exemption exists for a manufacturing and retailing entity. For non-established sellers, intra-EU distance sales and cross-border B2C supplies are subject to the single EU-wide threshold of EUR 10,000 per calendar year, above which French VAT applies at destination through the Union One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme; imports of consignments valued at EUR 150 or less may be cleared under the Import One Stop Shop (IOSS) without separate French VAT registration. The former national EUR 35,000 French distance-selling threshold was abolished on 1 July 2021 by the EU e-commerce VAT package.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exports under CGI Article 262-I-1° applies to FPV drone sales where: (i) title and risk pass to a non-EU buyer; (ii) the goods physically leave the EU customs territory, with dispatch occurring within 90 days from delivery and actual export generally within 12 months; (iii) the French SAS is the exporter named on the export customs declaration (DAU/EX-A); and (iv) authorized transport documentation substantiates arrival at destination. Ancillary services and digital add-ons supplied B2B to non-EU customers fall outside French VAT under the place-of-supply rule in Article 259-1 CGI (customer establishment rule). Intra-EU B2B deliveries are exempt only where the customer's valid French or other EU VAT ID is obtained and the EU sales list (DEB) is filed; retail B2C distance sales to EU final consumers are taxed at destination and declared through OSS. Input VAT attributable to exported goods remains fully creditable, creating a recurring VAT refund position for the drone manufacturer.

  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill or postal CN23 despatch note for courier exports)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate proving foreign-currency settlement through a French intermediary
  • Commercial invoice with Incoterms and export sale contract or purchase order
  • Exporter's transport and tracking evidence (carrier manifest, delivery confirmation at destination)
  • Export transaction register (journal des exportations) reconciling each zero-rated invoice to its customs entry

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing and e-reporting are being phased in under the French e-invoicing reform (successive Finance Acts, most recently Loi de finances pour 2024, Article 88). Published calendar: large enterprises and mid-sized companies must issue and receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; SMEs and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027. A proposed 2025 legislative amendment would postpone these dates by approximately one year, so the definitive timetable must be verified against the Finance Law and DGFiP guidance in force at onboarding. All domestic B2B invoices must be exchanged in structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII) via a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); PDPs connect to the PPF API layer and transmit e-reporting data on B2C transactions and payment flows. France does not apply per-invoice real-time fiscal stamping; enforcement relies on NF 525-certified cash register and point-of-sale software for retail sales (Article 88 of Law n°2016-1918), the annual FEC accounting file, and DGFiP data-mining of PDP and PPF streams.

DGFiP subjects zero-rated export chains to documentary inspection; any missing or inconsistent DAU, Bill of Lading, or inward remittance file causes recharacterization as a domestic taxable sale, with full 20% VAT reassessed plus intérêts de retard (0.2% per month) and penalties under Article 1729 CGI of 40% for bad faith or 80% for abusive manipulation or fraud. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day benchmark supports the administrative presumption that the export sale never materialized. Customs consequences under the Code des douanes include fines of one to three times the value of the goods (up to five times for false declaratory maneuvers) and confiscation of the goods and means of transport, as well as suspension or revocation of authorized exporter status. Criminal exposure arises under Article 1741 CGI and customs fraud provisions, with imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to EUR 500,000, increased for organized fraud. The statutory VAT reassessment period is generally three years (LPF Article L.169), extended to six years for undisclosed activity and up to ten years where fraudulent maneuvers are established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full euro convertibility on both current and capital accounts pursuant to Décret n°89-938 (29 December 1989), the OECD Codes of Liberalisation and TFEU Articles 63-66; no prior FX licensing, quotas or allocation systems apply to a French SAS. Current-account operations (trade settlement, service fees, royalties, licensing) are entirely free. Capital-account transactions for an inbound non-resident shareholder are free except for: (i) foreign direct investment screening under CMF Articles R151-3 et seq. that applies only to listed sensitive activities (defence, security, certain dual-use and critical technologies) - ISIC 3240 hobby racing drone manufacture is outside that list; (ii) dividend withholding tax on profit remittances to non-resident shareholders at the domestic rate (25% for corporate recipients, reduced by applicable double tax treaty; 12.8% plus social levies regime for individuals), which is a tax mechanism, not an FX restriction; (iii) financial sanctions and asset-freeze regimes (EU/UN consolidated lists) which may block payments to designated counterparties. No balance-of-payments approval is required for capital injection or dividend distribution; banks automatically report cross-border flows to Banque de France under its statistical collection duty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. France abolished compulsory surrender, forced conversion and repatriation timelines for export proceeds in 1989. An FPV drone exporter may retain export earnings in EUR or in a foreign-currency account (compte en devises) indefinitely, convert them at will, and use them for onward supplier payments without statutory deadline or mandatory conversion ratio. For non-EU-currency receivables, the collecting French bank reports the transaction to Banque de France under its balance-of-payments statistical obligation; the exporter has no separate filing duty for routine trade flows. Export receipts linked to sanctioned destinations, or inconsistent with the underlying customs value, may trigger TRACFIN reporting and bank-side suspension.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) with prudential licensing and AML/CTF supervision delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); foreign-exchange regulatory competence is framed by the Code monétaire et financier (CMF) and exercised under the Treasury's policy direction (Direction Générale du Trésor, Ministère de l'Économie); TRACFIN serves as the French financial intelligence unit for suspicious cross-border transaction reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High operational viability for ACPR-/PSD2-regulated electronic money institutions (Qonto, Shine, Manager.one, Wise Platform, Revolut Business) for daily treasury, Stripe/PayPal payout collection, multi-currency IBANs and remote onboarding of a French SAS. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot issue the attestation de dépôt des fonds required by French corporate law for the share capital subscription - the statutory escrow must be opened with a licensed bank or the Caisse des Dépôts; (ii) EMI client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts but are not covered by the FGDR EUR 100,000 deposit guarantee; (iii) EMIs often cap high-velocity supplier payments (typically EUR 100k/month) and offer no FX hedging products; (iv) multi-currency virtual IBANs are welcome for receivable collection but should not be used for capital injections to preserve audit and BOP traceability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) open the blocked capital-subscription account (compte bloqué) at BNP Paribas or Société Générale using the Kbis extract, statutes, UBO dossier and board resolution approving the capital increase; (2) the non-resident shareholder remits via SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR currencies or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR, with remittance information reading 'VERSEMENT CAPITAL SOCIAL - AUGMENTATION DE CAPITAL - SAS <SIREN>' and the bank's designated ISO 20022 purpose code for equity subscription (commonly 'EQUI' or the bank's own direct-investment menu code); (3) for remittances above EUR 15,000 from non-EEA counterparties, the collecting bank automatically files the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical declaration (operation type F5111 - direct investment equity); no client-side declaration is required; (4) the bank issues the attestation de dépôt des fonds (depositary certificate) under Articles L227-1 and L227-9 of the French Commercial Code, which the notary or registrar requires; (5) after share issuance and filing at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce, the escrow is released to the operational account; (6) verify that the inbound investment does not fall under the CMF R151-3 FDI screening list - toy-grade FPV drones are outside it, but a written classification memo should be retained; (7) if the remittance is in USD or CNY, execute a spot or forward conversion with the bank and retain the FX confirmation and the MT103 for audit; never commingle capital injection with trade receivables in the same wire.EU supplier payments: SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR) cleared same-day via SEPA rails at zero cross-border premium under EU Regulation 2021/1230. Non-EU supplier payments: SWIFT MT103 with Field 70 purpose code 'TRAD' referencing the purchase order and commercial invoice; for Chinese component suppliers, settle in EUR on the supplier's EUR account or execute a bank-side EUR/CNY conversion with SWIFT MT103 RMB instruction; the RMB CIPS corridor is available at BNP Paribas for dedicated RMB flows, but EUR-invoiced terms are preferred to minimise conversion friction. Large or first-time Chinese vendors: documentary letters of credit under UCP600 issued via SWIFT MT700, sight payment against air waybill and commercial invoice; confirmation recommended for amounts above EUR 100,000; a 50% advance / 50% against-document structure is standard for drone-component purchasing. Stripe and PayPal payouts are received on the operational EUR account and swept daily or weekly to the main treasury account via SEPA Instant; card platforms must not be used to disburse supplier funds - platform balances can be withdrawn only to the verified SAS bank account, preserving the audit trail.Deliverable FX forwards (contrats à terme de change) on EUR/CNY, EUR/USD and EUR/HKD with an ACPR-licensed credit institution under CMF Article L211-1, maturities up to 12 months, documented under the bank's standard ISDA/FX master or treasury framework agreement.FX swaps (swap de change) for same-day and next-day conversion of USD and CNY trade inflows into EUR, including tomorrow-next rolling structures.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY) permissible where the SAS holds a corresponding working-capital facility and the hedge matches underlying exposure.European-style FX options (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY) via the bank's derivatives desk under ISDA Master Agreement, with premium paid upfront and no retail leverage.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only for pairs involving a non-deliverable underlying currency (e.g., EUR/IDR, EUR/PHP); EUR/CNY is a fully deliverable pair, so an NDF is neither required nor standard for the France-China corridor; retail FX hedging via Stripe, PayPal or unlicensed platforms is prohibited.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High operational viability for ACPR-/PSD2-regulated electronic money institutions (Qonto, Shine, Manager.one, Wise Platform, Revolut Business) for daily treasury, Stripe/PayPal payout collection, multi-currency IBANs and remote onboarding of a French SAS. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a credit institution and cannot issue the attestation de dépôt des fonds required by French corporate law for the share capital subscription - the statutory escrow must be opened with a licensed bank or the Caisse des Dépôts; (ii) EMI client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts but are not covered by the FGDR EUR 100,000 deposit guarantee; (iii) EMIs often cap high-velocity supplier payments (typically EUR 100k/month) and offer no FX hedging products; (iv) multi-currency virtual IBANs are welcome for receivable collection but should not be used for capital injections to preserve audit and BOP traceability.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate; reduced 15% rate applies to the first EUR 38,120 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% statutory withholding tax under Article 119 bis CGI on dividends distributed by a French SAS to non-resident shareholders, unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or a lower treaty rate is available.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million in annual revenue or balance-sheet total triggers mandatory French Master File and Local File documentation; consolidated groups with EUR 750 million or more in group revenue trigger Country-by-Country Reporting. For a smaller FPV drone e-commerce SAS, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is nevertheless prudent when related-party purchases or royalties exceed a de minimis threshold.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply the Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but effectively requires the same substance through French tax-residence, permanent establishment, beneficial ownership, and general anti-abuse doctrines. A French SAS must have a genuine office, employees, management control and substantive operational decision-making in France to preserve its tax residence and to defend treaty benefits on repatriated profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent filing

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI imposes a criminal fine of EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years on conviction; this rises to EUR 1,000,000 and 7 years for organised tax fraud. Civil penalties include an 80% increase in reassessed tax for fraudulent manipulation, plus late-payment interest at 2.4% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally sign false returns, issue false accounting documents, or knowingly organise concealment are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax, penalties and interest; criminal conviction additionally exposes them to travel bans, civil rights restrictions, asset freezing and imprisonment.
Shell company and artificial tax avoidance

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may invoke abuse of law under Article L.64 LPF, impose an 80% penalty on the reassessed corporate tax, recharacterise the foreign hand-off as a French PE, and deny dividend, royalty or interest treaty protection.

Director personal liability: Directors of a fictitious SAS may be personally liable for corporate tax and penalties where the veil is pierced; foreign directors may face French travel or entry restrictions during judicial procedures and can be personally prosecuted for criminal tax fraud.
Transfer pricing manipulation and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: The tax administration can make a transfer pricing adjustment to the French SAS taxable profit, collect 2.4% annual interest, and apply a 40% penalty for lack of good faith. Failure to produce required transfer pricing documentation after formal request results in a EUR 50,000 fine, rising to EUR 100,000 after 30 days; CbCR non-compliance separately attracts sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany contracts knowing them to lack economic substance, or who make financial declarations without honest review, may be jointly liable for the recovery of unpaid tax where the company is left unable to pay; fraud-type transfer pricing conduct can also be prosecuted as criminal tax fraud.
AML and beneficial ownership compliance breach

Statutory sanction: Breaches of the French Monetary and Financial Code may trigger administrative fines, compliance injunctions, and criminal penalties of up to 5 years' imprisonment and EUR 375,000 for individuals; directors can be personally held as beneficial owners for failure to disclose, without prejudice to money-laundering penalties applied to the SAS.

Director personal liability: Directors who hold or control the SAS and deliberately conceal beneficial ownership face personal fines, imprisonment, asset freezing and, where the company is a mere vehicle for money laundering, unlimited recovery against personal assets under civil forfeiture rules.
