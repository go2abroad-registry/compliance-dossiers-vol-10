Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

INPI Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) as the single electronic filing window; Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered-office jurisdiction for RCS registration and Kbis issuance.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-33 (Société par Actions Simplifiée), Article L. 210-2 (formation, lawful object and capital), Articles R. 123-1 et seq. (RCS registration); implementing rules via Decree No. 2023-407 (Guichet Unique / INPI single window).

Formation / constitutional filings

Dossier unique de création d'entreprise (online M0/company creation form) filed via the INPI Guichet Unique, accompanied by signed Articles of Association (Statuts), bank capital deposit certificate, legal announcement certificate, and Declaration of Beneficial Ownership (DBE).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum or maximum capital for an SAS (Article L. 227-2); capital is freely fixed in the Articles (market practice: EUR 1,000-EUR 15,000). Cash subscriptions must be deposited before RCS registration in a blocked account (compte bloqué) opened with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the bank's attestation de dépôt des fonds is filed with the Guichet Unique and funds are released only upon presentation of the Kbis. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation and must be valued by a commissaire aux apports unless the statutory exemption applies. No escrow beyond the blocked deposit account is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: the office portion must comply with the general fire safety requirements of the Code du travail (accessible exit routes, illuminated emergency exits, portable extinguishers, and fire detection if premises size requires); public-access areas, if any, trigger ERP (Établissement Recevant du Public) classification and approval by the commission de sécurité. Warehouse clearance: for sites above 500 m³, a déclaration to the DREAL under ICPE rubrique 1510 plus a fire-safety assessment are required. Occupational clearance: registration with DREETS, display of health-and-safety notices, emergency first-aid kit, compliance with the Code du travail on air quality, lighting and worker protection, and, where applicable, the social and economic committee (CSE) formalities for staff above 11 employees.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum or maximum capital for an SAS (Article L. 227-2); capital is freely fixed in the Articles (market practice: EUR 1,000-EUR 15,000). Cash subscriptions must be deposited before RCS registration in a blocked account (compte bloqué) opened with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the bank's attestation de dépôt des fonds is filed with the Guichet Unique and funds are released only upon presentation of the Kbis. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation and must be valued by a commissaire aux apports unless the statutory exemption applies. No escrow beyond the blocked deposit account is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must obtain a French VAT number (numéro de TVA) and a corporate tax identifier (Numéro SIREN) by registering with the DGFiP through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). Non-established entities without an EU seat of business must file a specific registration form (Cerfa n° 15787) with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) territorialement compétent, and, if not established within the EU, must appoint a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) subject to DGFiP approval. The application requires standard corporate documentation, proof of non-resident legal existence, and identification of the actual place of management. The resulting numéro de TVA intracommunautaire (EU VAT ID) is required for intra-EU trade and export zero-rating documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An SAS is mandatorily taxed as a separate capital company under French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) and has no US-style check-the-box election. The principal statutory elections available are: (i) election for fiscal integration ('intégration fiscale') where the SAS is at least 95%-owned by a French parent, allowing group tax consolidation; (ii) election of the parent-subsidiary regime ('régime des sociétés mères et filiales') to benefit from the 95% exemption on qualifying dividend distributions; and (iii) election to file on a non-calendar year-end subject to tax authority approval. Hybrid mismatch elections are not permitted; French anti-hybrid rules under EU ATAD are mandatory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-established foreign suppliers: a taxable person not established in France must register for French VAT from the first taxable transaction physically performed in France unless the reverse charge rule or the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) regime applies. For domestic French small businesses, the franchise threshold is EUR 85,000 annual turnover for sales of goods and EUR 34,400 for services, but this is not available to foreign-established persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of specialty green coffee beans from France to an EU-external destination qualifies for zero-rated VAT (Art. 262-I-1° CGI) when the seller proves (i) dispatch of the goods after a binding sale, (ii) the goods physically leave French and EU territory, and (iii) export documentation is accepted by French customs. For intra-EU deliveries, zero-rating applies if the customer is a VAT-registered trader in another Member State and the goods are transported to that State (Art. 262-I-2°). The exporter must retain transport contracts, commercial invoices, customs export declarations (DAU/AES), and proof of exit or arrival. Green coffee beans imported from third countries into France are chargeable with import VAT at 20%, which must be paid upon customs clearance unless placed under a suspension arrangement such as customs warehousing.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation / DAU / Automated Export System record evidencing exit from the EU)
  • Bill of Lading or Shipping Company Transport Document evidencing physical delivery to a non-EU country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank SWIFT Credit Advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice issued in the name of the foreign buyer
  • Contract of Sale and Incoterms clause
  • Transport Contract and Insurance Certificate
  • Certificate of Origin and Phytosanitary Certificate (for green coffee beans)
  • VAT identification and export watch request (Art. 262 register)

France is rolling out a mandatory electronic invoicing and e-reporting reform ('Réforme Facturation Électronique'). B2B e-invoicing becomes compulsory from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for all VAT-liable businesses, including SAS entities. All B2B invoices must be issued in a structured format (Factur-X, UBL, or CII) and transmitted through a certified dematerialization platform (PDP) or the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF). E-reporting of all domestic B2C and export transaction data is simultaneously mandated to DGFiP. Currently, France does not operate a real-time pre-clearance or digital stamping system; clearance occurs through electronic data interchange with customs (DELTA) and tax validation via the invoicing platforms.

DGFiP systematically audits export zero-rating claims and may reconstruct the transaction if the export file is incomplete, inaccurate, or inconsistent. If the required proof is not produced within the audit window, the tax administration issues an assessment for the unpaid VAT at 20% plus interest (0.2% per month), increased by a 40% penalty for bad faith (mauvaise foi) and 80% for deliberate fraud under Arts. 1727 to 1729 CGI. False export documentation triggers criminal customs penalties under Arts. 414-415 French Customs Code, including forfeiture of goods, fines of up to three times the evaded amount, and imprisonment for smuggling. The standard assessment limitation period is three years, extended to six years in the presence of fraudulent activity or unexplained omissions, and the exporter must retain supporting records for at least ten years for definitive proof.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible; France maintains no exchange-control permit regime for current-account or capital-account movements. Current-account payments (green-coffee import settlements, commissions, freight, insurance) and capital flows between France and EU/EEA residents are unrestricted; transactions with non-EU parties enjoy the same freedom, subject only to (i) EU/UN financial sanctions (e.g., Reg. (EU) 833/2014 and 36/2012 as amended) and (ii) mandatory balance-of-payments statistical reporting by the receiving/remitting bank (per transaction >= EUR 2,000; monthly aggregate >= EUR 10,000; CMF Art. R.152-3). Profit remittance is free; no statutory dividend withholding applies to EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding >=5% under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive and CGI Art. 145; non-EU shareholders are subject to the applicable double-tax-treaty rate on dividends. No IEF/FDI prior approval (CMF Arts. L.151-3 et seq.) is triggered for a French non-regulated coffee wholesale importer classified under ISIC 4631.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Since EU capital liberalisation, France imposes no surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation deadline. Export receipts in USD or other convertible currencies may be retained indefinitely in a foreign-currency compte de devises at a French or EU bank without central-bank approval. Banks must file balance-of-payments statistics with Banque de France for each cross-border flow >= EUR 2,000 or when the monthly aggregate exceeds EUR 10,000 (CMF R.152-3). Physical transportation of cash or bearer negotiable instruments of EUR 10,000 or more into/out of France requires a customs declaration under EU Regulation 2018/1672.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France, Eurosystem member), with foreign-exchange policy and external-financial-regulation implementation shared with the Direction Generale du Tresor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy and Finance); prudential licensing and supervision of resident credit institutions and payment services is exercised by the ACPR (Autorite de Controle Prudentiel et de Resolution). Legal basis: Code Monetaire et Financier (CMF), Arts. L.151-1 et seq. and R.152-1 et seq.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as sole treasury rail; Medium as auxiliary settlement. ACPR-licensed French or EU-passported EMIs/payment institutions under PSD2 (e.g., Qonto, Shine; ManSA for Francophone-Africa corridors) may execute SEPA, outbound SWIFT MT103 and basic EUR/USD conversion, but cannot issue documentary credits (MT700), handle UCP 600 presentations, or provide high-value USD nostro clearing. Third-country (offshore) EMIs without an ACPR authorisation or EU passport cannot lawfully provide payment services to an FR SAS (CMF Arts. L.521-1 and L.572-1); their use as primary treasury rail is legally constrained and operationally inadvisable.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. A qualifying small enterprise may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to French SME eligibility conditions, including generally turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up capital, and at least 75% ownership by individuals or qualifying entities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For dividends paid by a French SAS to a non-resident corporate shareholder, the domestic withholding rate is generally 25%, subject to exemptions and treaty relief. The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption may eliminate withholding where the statutory ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. Dividends paid to a non-resident individual are generally subject to a different domestic regime, commonly 12.8%, before treaty analysis.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the annual return is generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the following year. For a financial year ending on another date, filing is generally due within three months after the closing date. Electronic filing rules and exceptional annual extensions must be verified for the relevant tax year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Instalments are generally payable on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December. The final balance is generally due on the 15th day of the fourth month following the year-end; a 31 December year-end generally produces a 15 May balance deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer-pricing documentation is generally mandatory where the French entity or group meets a statutory EUR 150 million threshold based on turnover or gross assets, or where specified ownership, control, tax consolidation, or comparable group conditions apply. A simplified transfer-pricing declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies at a EUR 50 million threshold or where specified related-party or group conditions are met. Documentation may be required below these thresholds to defend the arm's-length nature of material transactions.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone regime using the terminology of a separate Economic Substance Regulations filing comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. Nevertheless, French anti-abuse rules require genuine business purpose, real decision-making, appropriate personnel and assets, beneficial ownership, and consistency between contractual arrangements and actual conduct. The general anti-abuse rule, principal-purpose analysis, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, permanent-establishment principles, and beneficial-owner tests may deny treaty benefits or deductions for artificial arrangements. A French SAS importing and distributing coffee should maintain local commercial substance proportionate to its functions, including personnel or outsourced operational control, inventory management, customer and supplier contracts, customs capability, banking control, and board decision records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, and penalties of up to 80% of the evaded tax in aggravated cases. Criminal tax fraud may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, with higher penalties, including up to seven years' imprisonment and EUR 3 million, for aggravated or organized fraud cases, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur criminal liability, tax-fraud liability, management-fault liability, and civil damages. Courts may order professional disqualification, confiscation, or publication measures. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from judicial control, arrest warrants, or enforcement proceedings.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, denial of deductions, withholding-tax consequences, and penalties for documentation failure. The documentation penalty may generally be at least EUR 10,000 and may reach the higher of 0.5% of the relevant controlled transactions or 5% of the reassessed profits or taxable income, subject to the applicable statutory provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability where the conduct causes tax loss or insolvency and criminal liability where documentation is part of fraudulent concealment. A foreign parent does not automatically shield a director who knowingly approves abusive pricing.
Customs, import VAT, and origin fraud

Statutory sanction: Recovery of customs duties and import VAT, customs penalties, seizure or forfeiture of goods, withdrawal of customs simplifications, and potentially criminal prosecution for customs fraud. Food, traceability, sanitary, and deforestation-related supply-chain breaches may generate additional administrative or criminal consequences depending on the conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and customs representatives may be personally prosecuted for intentional customs fraud or false declarations. Civil recovery and asset seizure may follow. Travel restrictions or imprisonment depend on judicial proceedings and the seriousness of the offense.
Money laundering, terrorist financing, and beneficial-owner concealment

Statutory sanction: Suspicious-transaction reporting, account blocking, asset freezing, confiscation, administrative enforcement, and criminal money-laundering penalties. Basic money laundering may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with aggravated cases carrying materially higher penalties, including up to ten years' imprisonment and a EUR 750,000 fine, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate, facilitate, or negligently authorize the conduct may be criminally liable. Courts may order confiscation, professional bans, judicial control, and restrictions on movement. The SAS's limited liability does not protect intentional personal participation.
Shell-company and artificial treaty-benefit arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, application of domestic withholding, reassessment under the general anti-abuse rule, denial of deductions, interest, and penalties of up to 80% in cases of abuse or fraudulent conduct. A shell company is not unlawful solely because it has limited staff, but artificiality and abusive purpose create substantial exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for fraudulent abuse of rights, mismanagement, false corporate records, and creditor prejudice. The corporate veil may be disregarded in exceptional circumstances involving fraud, confusion of assets, or fictitious activity; imprisonment and travel restrictions require a separate judicial basis.
Illegal profit distribution and insolvency misconduct

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, director liability for management faults, possible misuse-of-corporate-assets offenses, insolvency proceedings, clawback of suspect-period transactions, and criminal penalties where statutory elements are met.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution, contribution to insolvency liabilities, management bans, and criminal prosecution for misuse of company assets or bankruptcy offenses. Travel bans and imprisonment are possible only where ordered by a competent court under the applicable offense.
Failure to withhold, report, or remit cross-border tax

Statutory sanction: Recovery of the unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, applicable interest, penalties, and possible denial of treaty relief. Deliberate concealment may escalate to tax-fraud proceedings.

Director personal liability: The legal representative may be exposed to personal liability in cases of intentional or repeated failure, particularly where the company is used to defeat tax collection. Civil recovery is more common than imprisonment absent evidence of deliberate fraud.
