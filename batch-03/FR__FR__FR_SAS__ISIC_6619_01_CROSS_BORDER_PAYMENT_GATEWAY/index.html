Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty, through impots.gouv.fr and the applicable Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Customs export formalities are administered separately by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI) through the DELTA customs systems.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). The filing generates the legal entity's SIREN and establishment SIRET identifiers through INSEE. The company then completes tax registration with the competent SIE and, where it conducts taxable transactions, requests a French VAT identification number. The VAT number normally has the format FR followed by a two-digit validation key and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères using the prescribed registration forms and supporting documents, including constitutional documents, proof of business activity, identification of legal representatives, banking information, and evidence of French taxable transactions. The DGFiP may require a French tax representative for certain non-EU VAT refund or registration situations. Registration does not replace mandatory authorization as a payment institution, electronic-money institution, payment-service agent, or other regulated provider under the Monetary and Financial Code; such authorization is generally administered by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). There is no general U.S.-style check-the-box election. An eligible SAS may elect temporary taxation under the individual income tax regime under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code, generally for a maximum of five financial years, subject to statutory conditions including size limits, qualifying ownership, individual participation requirements, management conditions, and an operating business activity. The election is not generally available to a company whose shares are listed or whose activity is predominantly financial or asset-management based. A qualifying group may also elect French tax consolidation under the applicable group-regime provisions, subject to ownership and other statutory conditions. French and EU anti-hybrid mismatch rules under Articles 205 B et seq. of the French Tax Code operate as mandatory anti-abuse rules; they do not constitute an elective hybrid classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the domestic franchise en base de TVA, the principal annual turnover threshold for services is EUR 37,500, with a higher tolerance threshold generally applicable under the statutory rules. The principal threshold for ordinary trading of goods is EUR 85,000. These thresholds are exemption thresholds, not a universal safe harbor from VAT registration. A non-established business may have to register from its first French taxable transaction, and the thresholds generally do not protect a business making transactions subject to reverse charge, importing goods, or otherwise requiring VAT reporting. B2B services supplied to a French business are often subject to customer reverse charge under the place-of-supply rules. Qualifying payment, transfer, and current-account transactions may be VAT-exempt under Article 261 C of the French Tax Code; technical, platform, data-processing, or administrative services are not automatically exempt merely because they support payment processing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched from France or another EU Member State to a destination outside the EU may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct, provided the supplier can substantiate export and satisfy the applicable invoicing and customs conditions. The evidence normally includes an electronically validated customs exit record or equivalent proof of exit, commercial documentation, transport evidence, and a consistent accounting trail. Services do not generally receive a domestic 0% export rate. B2B services supplied to a customer established outside France are normally governed by the place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of French VAT, subject to customer-status and evidence requirements. Certain qualifying financial and payment transactions are exempt under the specific French financial-services exemption, but technological or processing support services must be analyzed separately. VAT refunds require valid invoices, proof of taxable business use, and compliance with the applicable EU or non-EU refund procedure; non-established applicants may be subject to reciprocity, appointment of a tax representative, and filing deadlines.

  • Electronic export declaration lodged through the applicable French/EU customs system, generally DELTA, with the Movement Reference Number and validated exit result or equivalent customs proof of exit
  • Commercial invoice, packing list where relevant, transport contract, and Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other transport evidence appropriate to the shipment
  • Proof that the goods were dispatched to a destination outside the EU and that the supplier, purchaser, or authorized intermediary satisfies the statutory export conditions
  • Import or destination-country customs evidence where reasonably required to corroborate delivery outside the EU
  • Accounting records linking the invoice, export declaration, transport evidence, and VAT return
  • Inward remittance evidence only where relevant to the commercial transaction or another applicable requirement; France does not generally require an Indian-style Inward Remittance Certificate as a universal condition for export VAT exemption

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and transaction-data reporting through accredited private platforms (Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires) connected to the public directory and the public invoicing platform. The current statutory timetable provides receipt capability for all French VAT-taxable businesses from 1 September 2026 and issuance obligations for large and intermediate businesses from 1 September 2026, with issuance obligations for small and medium-sized businesses and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027, subject to legally enacted transition changes. France does not operate a universal real-time pre-clearance or digital-stamping model comparable to Italy's SDI for all invoices. Domestic B2B invoices must use structured electronic data containing the prescribed mandatory fields, and transaction and payment data may have to be transmitted through the designated platform. Cross-border B2B and B2C transactions are generally addressed through e-reporting rather than domestic B2B e-invoice transmission. Existing French invoice-content, record-retention, authenticity, integrity, and audit-trail requirements continue to apply.

The DGFiP may deny the exemption or input-tax recovery and assess the VAT retrospectively where export, customer status, transport, payment-service qualification, or place-of-supply evidence is inadequate. Reassessed VAT is generally accompanied by late-payment interest, currently calculated under the applicable French statutory rate, and may attract a 10% surcharge for late payment, a 40% surcharge for deliberate failure, or an 80% surcharge for fraudulent conduct or abusive concealment, depending on the facts and procedural findings. False, fabricated, or knowingly misleading customs or tax documentation may also trigger customs sanctions, administrative fines, recovery of improperly refunded or unpaid VAT, and criminal exposure for tax fraud or related offenses. Payment-service activities incorrectly treated as VAT-exempt may result in output-VAT assessments, denial or apportionment of input-tax deductions, interest, and penalties for the full statutory assessment period, subject to limitation rules and fraud-related extensions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and applies the EU liberalized capital-movement regime. Current-account and capital-account payments are generally freely convertible and transferable without routine exchange-control approval. A French SAS may receive and remit foreign currency, dividends, shareholder loans, interest, royalties, and trade payments through regulated financial institutions. Profit remittance requires distributable accounting profits, approval of annual accounts and dividend allocation, compliance with corporate-law reserve requirements, withholding-tax analysis, beneficial-owner and sanctions screening, and evidence that the payment is not a disguised capital distribution. Payment services and merchant-acquiring activity require authorization or registration under the French Monetary and Financial Code, generally through an ACPR-authorized payment institution, electronic-money institution, credit institution, or an appropriately structured agent arrangement. Client funds must be safeguarded and segregated from the company's operating funds; customer-money flows cannot be used to fund corporate liquidity. Cross-border transfers remain subject to AML, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions, tax-reporting, transaction-monitoring, and beneficial-owner verification requirements. Foreign accounts held by French tax residents may trigger French tax reporting obligations, including annual account declarations where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may remain in euro or foreign-currency accounts and may be converted at the holder's discretion, subject to contractual bank terms, accounting, tax, AML, sanctions, and payment-services requirements. Export documentation, commercial invoices, customs evidence, merchant settlement reports, and reconciliation records should be retained to substantiate the commercial purpose and beneficial ownership of incoming funds. Banks may request transaction-purpose information and may apply internal reporting or enhanced due-diligence procedures. Payments involving sanctioned jurisdictions, high-risk customers, unusual routing, virtual assets, or unexplained third-party settlement may be delayed, rejected, or reported to Tracfin.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem, with prudential and payment-services supervision conducted by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR), under the European Central Bank framework where applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operating treasury and payment connectivity; Low to Medium for the core regulated PSP account structure. An offshore digital EMI cannot automatically provide regulated payment services in France. An EEA-authorized payment institution or electronic-money institution may passport services into France under PSD2 through the required notification process, while a non-EEA provider generally requires an appropriately authorized French or EEA entity, branch, agent, or banking partner. The SAS must verify the provider's authorization, safeguarding model, fund segregation, insolvency protection, settlement timetable, scheme access, safeguarding account location, data-processing arrangements, complaint handling, and termination rights. Offshore accounts do not remove French AML, sanctions, tax, accounting, PSD2, safeguarding, consumer-protection, or payment-institution obligations. A fintech account should not be used for client-money safeguarding unless the provider and account structure are legally capable of performing that function.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity through a regulated bank to the SAS's designated capital-deposit account before incorporation or to the corporate operating account after registration. Use the exact legal name of the SAS, registered address, French IBAN, BIC, and a payment narrative identifying the transaction as share capital subscription, share-premium contribution, or shareholder loan. France does not use a universal foreign-exchange purpose-code system for ordinary corporate capital transfers; the bank may instead request a transaction-purpose classification, underlying agreement, capital-deposit certificate, board or shareholder resolution, and supporting corporate records. The remitting bank and receiving bank should verify the originator, ultimate beneficiary, source of funds, beneficial owners, currency, intermediary banks, and sanctions status. For shareholder loans, document principal, maturity, interest, arm's-length terms, repayment waterfall, transfer-pricing support, and applicable withholding-tax treatment. Record the foreign-currency amount and euro value in the accounting ledger, reconcile bank statements to the capital register, and retain SWIFT messages and supporting documents. Where an international investment, regulated activity, or sector-specific filing is applicable, complete the relevant French or EU notification before or after funding as legally required.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT MT103 or the current ISO 20022 equivalent for non-SEPA cross-border payments. The payment file should contain the exact beneficiary name, IBAN or account number, BIC, invoice number, contract reference, purpose, currency, and ultimate beneficiary information. Use commercial documentary letters of credit, including confirmed or unconfirmed Commercial LCs, for higher-risk trade corridors, new counterparties, or document-controlled shipments. Banks may require commercial invoices, purchase orders, transport documents, customs declarations, insurance documents, sanctions screening, and proof of goods or services. For a PSP, merchant settlement must follow the contractual settlement cycle and safeguarding rules; operating-account payments must not be funded from safeguarded customer money. Fedwire is a US domestic clearing rail and is not a direct French rail; USD payments may access Fedwire through a US correspondent bank, with applicable intermediary-bank, sanctions, cut-off, and beneficiary-screening controls. Maintain payment approval segregation, dual authorization, beneficiary-change verification, daily bank reconciliation, failed-payment monitoring, and complete audit trails.Deliverable FX forwards with an authorized bank or investment firm for forecast or committed commercial exposures, supported by a documented hedging policy, underlying exposure, maturity, counterparty limits, valuation controls, and accounting treatment.Currency swaps and cross-currency swaps for recurring funding, liquidity, or balance-sheet exposures, subject to contractual netting, collateral, counterparty-credit, valuation, and EMIR-related requirements where applicable.FX options and option collars for asymmetric protection of forecast receipts or payments, subject to suitability, premium, valuation, accounting, and governance controls.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies or jurisdictions where physical delivery is restricted or impractical, provided the product is legally available to the entity, the bank is authorized, sanctions and local-market rules are satisfied, and the settlement is documented against a genuine exposure.Spot FX conversions for operational settlement, merchant payouts, payroll, tax, and treasury rebalancing; use competitive execution, rate verification, and independent reconciliation.Hedging instruments must not be used for unauthorized proprietary speculation. The SAS should maintain board-approved dealing authorities, ISDA or equivalent master documentation, collateral and margin procedures, counterparty limits, confirmation controls, independent valuation, derivative reporting analysis, and a documented link between each hedge and the underlying currency exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for operating treasury and payment connectivity; Low to Medium for the core regulated PSP account structure. An offshore digital EMI cannot automatically provide regulated payment services in France. An EEA-authorized payment institution or electronic-money institution may passport services into France under PSD2 through the required notification process, while a non-EEA provider generally requires an appropriately authorized French or EEA entity, branch, agent, or banking partner. The SAS must verify the provider's authorization, safeguarding model, fund segregation, insolvency protection, settlement timetable, scheme access, safeguarding account location, data-processing arrangements, complaint handling, and termination rights. Offshore accounts do not remove French AML, sanctions, tax, accounting, PSD2, safeguarding, consumer-protection, or payment-institution obligations. A fintech account should not be used for client-money safeguarding unless the provider and account structure are legally capable of performing that function.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (art. 219 I CGI) plus 3.3% social surcharge on CIT exceeding EUR 763,000 for companies with turnover above EUR 7,630,000; reduced 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for SMEs meeting turnover and capital criteria

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on dividends distributed to non-resident corporate shareholders (art. 119 bis 2 CGI); 12.8% for non-resident individuals within the 30% flat tax (PFU, art. 200 A CGI); additional 3% levy on distributions to non-residents (art. 235 ter ZCA CGI) unless treaty-relieved

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Under-reported Gateway Revenue

Statutory sanction: 40% penalty for manquement délibéré and 80% for organised fraud (art. 1729 CGI); late-payment interest at 0.20% per month; criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (art. 1741 CGI), increased to EUR 3,000,000 and 7 years when committed through a tax-haven shelter (art. 1741-1 CGI)

Director personal liability: The president and directors may be jointly and severally liable for the SAS tax debt where fraud is proven (art. L. 267 LPF); parallel criminal measures include asset freezes, confiscation, travel bans (interdiction de quitter le territoire), posting of judicial sureties, and disqualification from management
Unauthorised Payment Institution Activity

Statutory sanction: Art. L. 572-1 C. mon. fin.: 7 years imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine for unauthorised provision of payment services; confiscation of assets derived from the offence; public sanction; corporate fine for the SAS up to EUR 3,750,000 as the 5x multiplier under art. 131-38 Penal Code

Director personal liability: Directors who implement or tolerate the unauthorised activity face the same criminal penalties; travel bans and prohibition from professional activity up to 5 years under art. L. 572-2 C. mon. fin.; personal ACPR sanctions of up to EUR 5,000,000 and loss of fitness-and-propriety for banking licences
