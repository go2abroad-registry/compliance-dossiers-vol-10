Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office in France under Articles L.123-11 and R.123-167 et seq. of the Commercial Code. A domiciliation company or compliant virtual office may be used if the provider is authorized and the domiciliation contract satisfies French requirements. A director's private residence may be used subject to statutory conditions and applicable lease, condominium and planning restrictions. For Class III medical-device manufacturing, virtual-office use is insufficient as operational substance: the company requires an appropriate manufacturing, quality-system, storage, traceability, vigilance and regulatory location. A separate production site must comply with Regulation (EU) 2017/745 on medical devices, applicable French Public Health Code requirements, CE-marking and conformity-assessment obligations, and any ANSM or notified-body requirements. A registered virtual office should therefore be limited to corporate correspondence and must not be represented as the manufacturing or regulatory site.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

French Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises at https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr/, operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI); resulting registration data is recorded in the Registre national des entreprises (RNE) and, for commercial entities, the competent commercial registry

Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation procedure with a universal 30-day reservation period for a SAS. Conduct availability checks before signing the articles, reserve matching domains and, where commercially important, file a trademark application with INPI or EUIPO. If the Guichet unique or registry rejects or suspends the filing, obtain the written reason, correct the articles or supporting documents, and resubmit through the Guichet unique within the stated cure period. If the decision is attributable to the competent registry, use the available administrative or judicial remedy indicated in the decision, including referral to the president of the competent commercial court where applicable for a registry refusal or omission. Trademark refusals or objections must be challenged before INPI within the prescribed opposition or appeal deadline, or before the competent court where judicial review is available. A name rejection does not itself create a statutory extension right.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate formalities and legal/tax identifier registration submitted through the Guichet Unique operated under the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) must file an incorporation dossier through the Guichet Unique. Upon successful registration the company automatically obtains a SIREN/SIRET number, a French tax identification number, and, when necessary, an intra-EU VAT identification number. The VAT/TIN allocation is electronic, typically completed within two to four weeks after submission of the static constitutive documents and identification of directors and beneficial owners.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities establishing a Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) must file an incorporation dossier through the Guichet Unique. Upon successful registration the company automatically obtains a SIREN/SIRET number, a French tax identification number, and, when necessary, an intra-EU VAT identification number. The VAT/TIN allocation is electronic, typically completed within two to four weeks after submission of the static constitutive documents and identification of directors and beneficial owners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The SAS is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Available statutory election/adjustment mechanisms: (i) election to be taxed as a persona with limited passthrough for qualifying small SAS structures and certain dedicated holding/professional property companies under Article 239 quinquies of the CGI; (ii) election to integrate into a French IS tax consolidation group (intégration fiscale) where a French-resident entity holds at least 95% of the SAS shares; (iii) election for the reduced corporate rate applicable to 'young innovative enterprises' (JEI status) if the SAS carries on eligible R&D-intensive medical-technology activities; (iv) hybrid-mismatch election/disclosure obligations under Article 61 CGI implementing EU BEPS Action 2 and requiring submissions on structured arrangements leading to outcomes inconsistent with French or foreign tax characterisation; no US-style check-the-box election is available in France.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€85,000 annual taxable turnover for sale of goods (including processed goods and medical-device manufacturing output); €37,500 annual taxable turnover for other services; threshold applies under Article 293 B of the French CGI and is assessed on a calendar-year/ongoing-activity basis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union are zero-rated if the physical movement of the goods to a non-EU territory is demonstrated and evidenced. For class III coronary drug-eluting stents, zero-rating requires actual shipment from the EU, sale to a non-EU customer, and proof that the goods left the customs territory of the European Union within the regulatory time window. Intra-EU supplies of the stents may also benefit from a 0% rate if the acquirer is identified for VAT in another EU member state, the goods are dispatched to another EU member state, and an intra-community acquisition report is documented; failure to meet the documentary burden converts the supply into a domestic taxable transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/customs declaration in the Single Administrative Document system – EX-A or EX-AU)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (connaissement / lettre de transport aérien)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank attestation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency)

France has enacted a phased e-invoicing and e-reporting reform: issuance of structured electronic invoices (Factur-X or PEPPOL BIS 3.0) via the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified private platform; mandatory from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-cap entities, and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises; B2B invoicing flows and B2C e-reporting will be transmitted in near-real time to the DGFiP. For export transactions to non-EU territories, clearance is effected through the customs declaration (DAU) in the EU Customs Decision System and supporting transport evidence; no continuous electronic VAT stamping of each export transaction is imposed for zero-rating purposes.

If an export zero-rating claim is unsupported, false, or corrected after inspection, the DGFiP recharacterises the transaction as a domestic supply and assesses TVA at the applicable rate (20% for medical devices unless separately classified). Civil penalties include 40% of the tax due for deliberate neglect or fraud (manquement délibéré), 80% for abusive manoeuvres (abus de droit) and an additional 0.2% monthly late-payment interest (intérêts de retard). Criminal consequences may include fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment for tax evasion directors, together with joint several liability of corporate managers. The statute of limitation is extended to six years for undisclosed BFEE-linked offshore arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains a regime of full capital account convertibility as a founding member of the Eurozone. Capital transactions including equity injections, dividend remittances, and interest payments are freely convertible without quantitative restrictions. Residents must file declarations with the Banque de France for transactions exceeding €100,000 to support statistical reporting. Profit remittance is subject only to standard corporate tax clearance and beneficial ownership verification; no additional FX surrender obligations apply to intra-EU flows. Current account transactions benefit from automatic convertibility with mandatory bank reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

French exporters must repatriate all foreign exchange proceeds within twelve months of shipment date. Banks require original commercial invoices, packing lists, transport documents, and proof of export delivery. No mandatory surrender ratio exists; proceeds must be credited in EUR to the exporter's designated account. Non-compliance may result in regulatory referral to the Banque de France and administrative penalties under the Monetary and Financial Code. All companies must retain supporting documentation for at least ten years.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions such as N26 or Revolut offer limited cross-border corporate treasury services for French SAS entities. Legal constraints include absence of full banking license, strict AML obligations, and regulatory prohibition on handling Class III medical device payments due to elevated risk classification. Offshore digital EMIs cannot support full export clearing or hedging functions and require additional French banking partner integration.
