Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) or, for qualifying non-established foreign businesses, the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE); online tax administration portal: impots.gouv.fr. Customs registrations and export formalities are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI) through douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is incorporated and reported through the guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). Following registration, the competent registry and INSEE issue the SIREN and establishment-specific SIRET identifiers. The company requests or confirms its French VAT identification number with the competent SIE, generally in the format FR plus the two-digit key and nine-digit SIREN. A non-established foreign entity carrying out taxable transactions in France applies to the SIEE or other designated DGFiP service using the prescribed registration and VAT documentation, including constitutional documents, foreign tax certificate, business activity description, French customer or transaction information, identification of the legal representative, and banking details where requested. A business conducting customs operations must separately obtain an EORI number from DGDDI. Registration does not arise merely from passive ownership of investments where the activity is outside the scope of VAT, but VAT registration may be required for taxable services, intra-Community acquisitions, imports, or other taxable transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Subject to statutory eligibility conditions, including limits concerning ownership, employee headcount, turnover or balance-sheet size, and the nature of the activity, an unlisted SAS may elect temporarily for income tax treatment under the regime applicable to certain partnerships and qualifying companies. The election is generally limited to five financial years and must be made within the statutory deadline. There is no general United States-style check-the-box election for a French SAS. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion where arrangements produce deduction or non-inclusion or duplicate deduction outcomes; they do not constitute a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the French franchise en base regime, the principal turnover thresholds are generally EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods, sales for consumption on the premises, and accommodation, and EUR 37,500 for services, with higher tolerance thresholds generally of EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250 respectively, subject to applicable year, sector, transaction, and legislative rules. A pure investment holding company that only holds securities or receives dividends generally has no taxable economic activity for VAT purposes. Taxable management, advisory, administrative, or other services supplied by the family office are assessed under the applicable VAT rules; registration may also be required for intra-Community acquisitions, imports, reverse-charge transactions, or transactions exceeding the applicable franchise thresholds. The French VAT threshold rules have been subject to recent legislative changes and transitional measures and must be verified for the relevant filing year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT with the right to deduct input VAT when the supplier can establish that the goods left the European Union and satisfy the documentary and transactional requirements. Evidence generally includes the customs export declaration, electronic exit confirmation, commercial invoice, transport documentation, and a clear link between the supplier, goods, consignee, and export movement. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for exemption where the customer has a valid VAT number in another EU Member State, the goods are dispatched to that Member State, and the supplier retains required transport and reporting evidence. B2B services supplied to a business established outside France are generally governed by place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of French VAT or subject to reverse charge, rather than technically being zero-rated exports. Passive investment income, including dividends, is generally outside the scope of VAT; taxable family-office services require separate analysis.

  • Electronic customs export declaration and its Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Electronic confirmation that the goods exited the European Union, or equivalent customs exit evidence
  • Commercial invoice identifying the French supplier, customer, goods, value, and export destination
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or other appropriate transport document
  • Packing list and purchase or sales documentation sufficient to link the goods to the export declaration
  • Import or delivery evidence in the destination country where required or commercially available
  • Proof of payment or contractual evidence where necessary to substantiate the transaction, although France does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a statutory export-VAT document
  • For intra-Community supplies, the customer’s valid EU VAT number, transport evidence, and required EC Sales List reporting

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through approved private platforms connected to the public directory and the public invoicing portal. The framework is not a universal real-time pre-clearance model. All French businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Issuance obligations generally begin on 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and medium-sized enterprises and on 1 September 2027 for small and micro-enterprises, subject to statutory transitional rules. B2B domestic invoices are transmitted in prescribed structured formats, including permitted hybrid formats such as Factur-X, through an approved platform. B2C and certain cross-border transactions may require electronic reporting rather than invoice clearance. Required invoice data, payment information, transaction classification, and VAT details must be transmitted in the prescribed digital format; VAT invoices remain subject to French retention and audit-access requirements.

France does not impose a general 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export VAT exemption or refund claims may be reassessed where exit, transport, customer, invoice, or transaction evidence is incomplete, inconsistent, fabricated, or unavailable. The tax authority may recover improperly exempted or refunded VAT, assess statutory late-payment interest, and impose penalties under the French Tax Code, including penalties commonly equal to 40% for deliberate non-compliance and up to 80% for fraudulent maneuvers or abusive conduct, subject to the facts and applicable procedural rules. Customs sanctions, repayment of improperly obtained customs benefits, and seizure measures may also apply. Serious intentional tax fraud may trigger criminal proceedings, including fines and imprisonment under applicable French criminal tax-fraud provisions. Records should generally be retained for the statutory tax limitation period, commonly three years plus the current year for VAT assessments, with longer periods applying in specified circumstances.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains unrestricted capital account convertibility with no regulatory restrictions on capital movements or profit remittances for resident entities. Current account transactions are freely convertible at market rates. Profit remittances to shareholders are permitted without convertibility hurdles, subject only to standard corporate governance and tax withholding requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds. All exports require customs declaration under the Single Window system. Voluntary statistical reporting to the Banque de France is available for transparency but not obligatory. FX transactions occur at prevailing market rates with no statutory conversion ratios.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: fines up to 500% of evaded tax, imprisonment up to 5 years; civil restitution orders

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for unpaid taxes; possible disqualification, fines, and imprisonment for willful misconduct
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the company; imposition of back taxes, penalties up to 500% of tax benefit sought

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing; directors face personal liability, travel bans, and criminal sanctions
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines exceeding €1 million; possible criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Personal fines, disqualification from directorships, and imprisonment for serious breaches
