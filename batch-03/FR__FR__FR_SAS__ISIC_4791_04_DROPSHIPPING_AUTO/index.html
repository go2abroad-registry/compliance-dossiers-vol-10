Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); portal: impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities without a permanent establishment must appoint an accredited fiscal representative and file a French registration application (Form 1476-SD) with the DGFiP, or use the online 'Création d'entreprise' procedure to obtain a SIREN/SIRET number and an intra-community VAT ID under Article 286 CGI for all taxable operations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities without a permanent establishment must appoint an accredited fiscal representative and file a French registration application (Form 1476-SD) with the DGFiP, or use the online 'Création d'entreprise' procedure to obtain a SIREN/SIRET number and an intra-community VAT ID under Article 286 CGI for all taxable operations.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime; French tax legal unity election (intégration fiscale) is available under Article 223 A CGI and may extend to eligible foreign-owned groups; hybrid mismatch adjustments apply under Articles 212 bis and 212 bis A CGI implementing ATAD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,800 annual turnover for supplies of goods (Article 293 B CGI); EUR 37,500 for services; no threshold for non-established foreign suppliers — immediate VAT registration required; EUR 10,000 union-wide threshold applies to intra-EU distance sales of goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Article 262-1 CGI; dropshipping supplies shipped from French territory to non-EU customers are eligible provided the exporter holds transport evidence and demonstrates receipt outside the EU. Offshore services supplied to non-EU customers may be zero-rated only when tied to exported goods; purely digital services to non-EU consumers fall under the reverse-charge or OSS schemes.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Mandatory e-invoicing for domestic B2B transactions phased from September 2026, with e-reporting to DGFiP through the Portail Public de Facturation via certified dematerialization platforms (PDPs) and API integration; no real-time fiscal stamping applies to export invoices.

Claiming fraudulent export zero-rating triggers retrospective VAT recovery, a bad-faith penalty of 80% of underpaid tax under Article 1737 CGI, applicable interest, and criminal liability under Article 1741 CGI; supporting transport, customs, and payment evidence must be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full convertibility for both capital and current account operations under EU Treaty provisions and the French Monetary and Financial Code. There are no exchange control authorizations for trade settlements, dividend distributions, or capital movements involving EUR within the EEA. Non-resident owners of a French SAS may freely remit dividends and liquidation proceeds after satisfaction of corporate taxes, dividend withholding tax (30% standard or reduced by treaties), and documentary declaration requirements. Residual frictions arise from AML/KYC obligations, EU comprehensive sanctions, and mandatory statistical declarations to the Banque de France for cross-border payments exceeding reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline for export proceeds. A French-resident exporter is permitted to maintain foreign currency accounts with authorized credit institutions and to retain all export earnings offshore, subject to balance-of-payments reporting. French resident entities must, however, provide the Banque de France, via their intermediary bank, with transaction-level data for cross-border funds transfers and must file annual direct investment reports when applicable. Exports to sanctioned jurisdictions are strictly prohibited and subject to DG Trésor controls.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (central bank of France) acting within the Eurosystem; the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance retains competence for exchange rate policy, financial sanctions, and balance-of-payments statistical oversight. Prudential regulation and AML supervision of credit institutions are exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH as a secondary treasury gateway, but NOT as the sole primary banking rail. ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto and Treezor, and passport-holders under PSD2 such as Revolut Business, provide rapid remote onboarding, segregated sub-accounts, and multi-currency EUR/USD/GBP account capabilities. They can legally support settlement of operational flows, supplier disbursements, and payment gateway payouts. Critical limitations: EMIs cannot extend credit, issue documentary letters of credit, or hold paid-in share capital in a legally compliant compte bloqué arrangement; for those functions a licensed credit institution is mandatory. Foreign EMIs without an EU passport cannot operate in France and will not satisfy French treasury compliance expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial incorporation of a French SAS, the subscribed share capital must be deposited into a temporarily blocked account (compte bloqué) at a French credit institution in the name of the company in formation. The bank issues a certificate of deposit (attestation de blocage de fonds) delivered to the depositary or notary; the funds are released only after the company is registered at the RCS. For subsequent capital increases by a foreign parent, the amount is wire-transferred by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA to the company's dedicated account at the relationship bank, with the instruction explicitly identifying the transaction as an 'Apport en compte-courant d'associé' or 'Augmentation de capital'. The bank requires supporting board resolutions, updated UBO declarations, and proof that the remitting entity is the true legal owner of the funds. All inbound transfers above €10,000 are captured by AML controls and balance-of-payments statistical collection by the Banque de France.Supplier payments are executed through SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) using the company's bank or an EMI partner. For recurring mid-value disbursements, a standing bank-file transformation with Straight-Through Processing (STP) can be established. For high-value or two-step drop-shipping arrangements with Chinese or global suppliers, an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit (UCP600) with a French advising bank is strongly recommended to mitigate counterparty risk. Late payment interest accrues at 0.2% per month for the first 10 days and 0.5% per month thereafter, plus a potential additional 5% penalty for failure to comply with e-filing obligations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File obligation arises where the French entity or its group has consolidated turnover (excluding VAT) above EUR 400 million, or cross-border controlled transaction volume exceeds EUR 100 million; simplified transfer pricing documentation is required for entities with intra-group transactions above EUR 50 million or total assets exceeding EUR 50 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but enforces substance-over-form through Article L64 of the Livre des procédures fiscales (abuse of law) and the beneficial ownership doctrine under tax treaties and EU Directives; a French SAS must demonstrate genuine management, operational control, staff, and premises to support its dropout-shipping model and to access treaty relief or intra-group arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (artificial or sham arrangements)

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of transactions, disallowance of deductions, 80% fraud penalties on reassessed taxable income, publication of sanctions, criminal fine for tax evasion up to EUR 500,000 for legal entities and EUR 250,000 for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers face criminal imprisonment of up to five years (increased for organised evasion), civil joint liability for all unpaid corporate taxes, interest and penalties, as well as travel bans, asset freezes and a prohibition on managing companies.
Late or non-filing of the statutory corporate tax return

Statutory sanction: 10% automatic surcharge; 40% penalty after a mise en demeure remains unfilled; 0.2% to 0.5% monthly late-payment interest; collective assessment may produce an 80% penalty; additional EUR 250 per document penalty for each year of missing accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt under Article L267 LPF where the defect demonstrates gross negligence or manoeuver; personal summons to tax authorities, restriction of passport, and a ban from holding public contracts are available enforcement tools.
Money Laundering and suspicious cross-border flows

Statutory sanction: Administrative freezing of the entity's bank accounts, filing with Tracfin (French financial intelligence unit), seizure of company assets, and criminal fines of EUR 1 million for legal entities engaged in money laundering, increased to EUR 5 million in cases of organised money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors face up to ten years imprisonment for organised money laundering, confiscation of personal and corporate property, a travel ban, restriction of civil and professional rights, and an order of restitution for the laundered sums with aggravated fines.
