France does not operate a statutory corporate-name reservation system; protection arises upon effective RNE registration. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS; the capital is freely fixed in the Statuts (market practice EUR 1 to EUR 5,000). Cash contributions must be paid up on subscription to at least 10% of nominal value under Article L.225-3 applied through Article L.227-2 of the French Commercial Code, with the balance payable within five years. The paid-up funds must be deposited with a French credit institution or consigned with a notary (attestation de dépôt/consignation) before RCS filing; no State escrow account regime applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministry of Public Accounts; digital portal: impots.gouv.fr; corporate registration entry point: Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises operated by INPI at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; non-established foreign entities apply to the DGFiP non-resident unit (Service des Impôts des Entreprises compétent / Direction des Non-Résidents, Noisy-le-Grand).

E-government filing portal

For the French SAS: (1) file articles and statutory declarations via the Guichet Unique to obtain the SIREN/SIRET number (INSEE); (2) INSEE data is transmitted ex officio to DGFiP, which issues the intra-Community VAT number (format 'FR' + two-key checksum + 9-digit SIREN) within roughly 15 days; (3) open the 'espace professionnel' on impots.gouv.fr to operate the online tax account. For a foreign trading entity not established in France: complete simplified direct VAT registration (form CA14, 'demande d'immatriculation d'un assujetti non établi'), or join the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS non-union scheme) for B2C distance sales of IoT hardware; the registration grants a French VAT number. A tax (fiscal) representative may be imposed for third-country operators carrying out taxable transactions under Article 289 A of the French General Tax Code.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the SAS is a non-transparent company subject to corporate income tax - impôt sur les sociétés - at the 25% standard rate, with the 15% reduced rate applicable on the first EUR 42,500 of profit for qualifying small companies; territorial scope: French-resident SAS are taxed on worldwide profits, with exemption or foreign tax credit for permanent-establishment profits under Article 209 CGI; no general tax-exempt zone relief applies to the smart home IoT retail scope, though small-business franchises and regional aid schemes may create partial exemptions).

Available election forms

Check-the-box classification does not exist under French law. Available elections: (a) option for tax-transparent treatment of an eligible SAS holding company under Article 239 bis AA CGI; (b) option for the real VAT regime (régime réel) by micro-enterprises; (c) option for tax-consolidated 'intégration fiscale' (Articles 223 A et seq. CGI) for groups owning at least 95% of subsidiaries; (d) reduced-rate small-company election for CIT; (e) anti-hybrid and hybrid-mismatch rules under Article 212 bis CGI (ATAD 1/2 transposition) override mismatching entity classifications and restrict deduction/non-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established foreign operators (EU and third-country): EUR 0 - registration is compulsory before the first taxable transaction in France. French-established micro-businesses may claim the 'franchise en base de TVA' for annual turnover up to EUR 85,000 (sales of goods) or EUR 37,500 (services), with tolerance thresholds of EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250; the franchise is not available to non-established sellers. For intra-EU distance sales of goods to consumers, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies (country-of-origin taxation below the threshold); sales above the threshold must be declared via OSS and taxed at the French 20% rate from the first euro of French distance sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the EU are VAT-exempt with right to deduction under Article 262-I CGI; intra-EU B2B supplies are exempt under Article 262-ter CGI provided the customer's EU VAT ID is valid and the goods are physically transported to another Member State. Software-enabled smart home IoT hardware is zero-rated under these provisions when supplied with tangible hardware; pure electronic services (e.g., cloud subscriptions) are outside the export zero-rating rules and are taxed under the B2B place-of-supply rule (Article 259 A CGI - customer establishment). To claim the 0% rate the seller must possess, before the VAT return is filed, objective evidence that the goods left France/EU territory (administrative doctrine generally expects physical exit within three months of dispatch), including transport contracts and customs documentation; refunds of residual input VAT on export operations are recoverable under the ordinary credit-refund procedures (monthly/quarterly claims) with the benefit of the right of immediate deduction. Note: French tax legislation contains no fixed FX-repatriation date; the 180-day parameter of this dossier corresponds to the internal benchmark for turning export invoices into collection evidence, with the operative civil payment cap set at 60 days from invoice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU - Document Administratif Unique, or H1 electronic export declaration lodged via the DELTA/ECS IT systems of the DGDDI)
  • Transport proof: Bill of Lading (connaissement), CMR waybill or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure of the goods from French/EU territory
  • Commercial invoice, sale contract/purchase order linking the goods to a specific non-EU or EU (intra-community) customer
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT payment evidence matching invoice settlement (useful to substantiate commercial reality under DGFiP audit practice)
  • For intra-EU supplies: customer VAT number validation output and EC Sales List (recapitulative statement / 'déclaration d'échanges de biens' as applicable)

France operates a 'Y-model' e-invoicing/e-reporting clearance system centred on the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) and accredited Partner Dematerialisation Platforms (PDPs). There is no per-invoice real-time fiscal stamp or continuous transaction control like the Italian SDI; instead, structured invoice data and transactional e-reporting flow periodically from businesses' software to DGFiP via certified PDPs/API. Mandatory timetable (2026 Finance Act): from 1 September 2026 every taxable person must be able to receive electronic invoices and all suppliers must transmit e-invoices for domestic B2B transactions; obligation to issue structured e-invoices from 1 September 2027 for large enterprises ('grandes entreprises') and mid-size enterprises ('ETI'); from 1 September 2028 for small and micro enterprises; cross-border B2B and B2C e-reporting obligations are phased in on the same dates (cross-border e-reporting from 1 September 2027 and B2C data from 1 September 2028). Accepted formats: Factur-X (hybrid), UBL or CII.

DGFiP may reassess export zero-rating within the standard 3-year statutory limitation period (Article L.169 of the Book of Tax Procedures); if export evidence is missing or fraudulent, 20% VAT is clawed back on the net selling price with late-payment interest (0.20% per month, Article 1727 CGI) and graduated penalties: 40% for inaccurate/incomplete declarations (Article 1728 CGI), 80% for deliberate (bad faith) conduct (Article 1729 CGI), and 150% for fraudulent manoeuvres ('manœuvres frauduleuses') or abuse of law. Export zero-rating fraud also triggers the criminal offence of tax fraud (Article 1741 CGI) - up to EUR 750,000 fine and 5 years' imprisonment for ordinary fraud (enhanced up to EUR 3,000,000 and 10 years for organised schemes 'bande organisée'), plus separate customs penalties and possible 'avis de mise en recouvrement' disgorgement; invoices and export files must be retained for the full 6-year commercial retention period to counter retrospective clawback.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France has no general exchange controls restricting current-account payments or capital transfers. The euro is freely convertible, and a French SAS may generally receive foreign investment, hold foreign-currency accounts, pay overseas suppliers, and remit dividends or other lawful distributions abroad. Transfers remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, tax, company-law, and bank due-diligence requirements. Dividends require distributable profits or reserves, valid corporate approvals, and applicable withholding-tax analysis; treaty or EU relief may require supporting residence and beneficial-ownership documentation. Cross-border payment and account data may be reportable for statistical purposes under applicable Banque de France and EU rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory currency-conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in euros or foreign currency, subject to ordinary tax, accounting, sanctions, and statistical-reporting rules. No general foreign-exchange rule requires conversion or repatriation within a specified period; transaction-specific reporting or documentation obligations may apply.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France’s central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. France’s Ministry for the Economy and Finance administers applicable financial and monetary legislation; the Direction générale du Trésor handles relevant regulatory functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to high for routine euro collections and payments where the provider is authorized in France or another EEA state and may lawfully provide services in France. An EEA-authorized electronic money institution may generally rely on passporting, subject to its permissions and service model. A non-EEA offshore EMI has no automatic EU passport and may need an appropriate French or EEA authorization or partner. E-money is not a bank deposit and is not covered by the deposit-guarantee scheme; confirm safeguarding, payment limits, supported currencies, and acceptance for share-capital deposits before relying on the provider.

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150,000,000 of annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets, or membership in or control by an enterprise meeting the statutory threshold, generally triggers full French transfer pricing documentation under Article L13 AA of the French Tax Procedures Code. A simplified transfer pricing documentation obligation may apply to other entities under Article L13 AB. Material controlled transactions remain subject to the arm's-length principle regardless of the documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a single standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is assessed through the arm's-length principle, the abuse-of-law doctrine, general anti-abuse rules, beneficial ownership concepts, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, anti-hybrid rules, withholding tax anti-abuse provisions, and EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive anti-abuse requirements. The French SAS should maintain genuine French commercial activity, decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or operational infrastructure, appropriate capitalization, inventory and logistics records, bank accounts, customer contracts, and contemporaneous board and accounting records. A conduit company with no commercial rationale may lose treaty or domestic relief and may face reassessment.
