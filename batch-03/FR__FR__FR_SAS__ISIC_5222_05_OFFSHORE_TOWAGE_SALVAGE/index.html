Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) operated by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry) having jurisdiction over the registered office; filings are administered through the electronic single window (Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book I and Book II: Articles L. 210-1 to L. 210-10, L. 221-1 to L. 227-27 (SAS-specific provisions), R. 227-1 to R. 227-15; Council Regulation (EU) 2021/1139 for maritime sector where applicable.

Formation / constitutional filings

Formulaire M0 – Déclaration de constitution de société (immatriculation au RCS); accompanied by the signed Articles of Association (statuts constitutifs), legal publication certificate (certificat de parution), and capital deposit certificate.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for an SAS; the stated capital may be as low as EUR 1. If cash contributions are used, the full amount must be subscribed and deposited in escrow with a French credit institution, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary. The escrow is released to the company upon issuance of the RCS registration certificate (extrait Kbis).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for any shore installations under the local fire service (Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours – SDIS); structural load inspection of quayside or warehouse facilities where applicable; workplace health and safety clearance via the Labour Inspectorate (Inspection du Travail) and compliance with the French Labour Code and ILO Maritime Labour Convention 2006; survival, lifesaving, and salvage firefighting equipment onboard must carry valid classification society certificates (e.g., Bureau Veritas) and be approved in the vessel safety certificate.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for an SAS; the stated capital may be as low as EUR 1. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), including the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) for eligible non-established businesses. Principal online portals are impots.gouv.fr for tax administration and formal tax filings, and the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated through the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI) for entity registration formalities.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS obtains its SIREN and SIRET identifiers through the INPI Guichet unique upon incorporation. The DGFiP then assigns or confirms the French tax account and corporate income tax registration. A French VAT identification number is requested from the competent SIE, generally after incorporation and before commencing taxable transactions requiring VAT registration. The VAT number normally comprises the country prefix FR, a two-digit control key, and the entity's nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity carrying on taxable activities in France must register with the SIEE or the relevant competent tax office, submit identification and constitutional documents, evidence of business activity, bank and representative details where applicable, and obtain a French VAT number. A non-EU business generally must appoint a French tax representative unless an applicable statutory exemption or mutual-assistance arrangement applies. EU-established businesses may register directly without a French fiscal representative, subject to the applicable VAT rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Certain SASs may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime for up to five fiscal years if the statutory eligibility conditions are satisfied, including qualifying ownership, activity, age, and size requirements. This is not a general United States-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the EU ATAD II framework and can deny deductions, impose inclusion, or otherwise neutralize outcomes involving hybrid entities, instruments, or permanent establishments. The election must be made within the statutory period and notified to the competent tax authority.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France does not provide a general VAT-registration threshold for non-established businesses making taxable supplies in France; registration and VAT accounting may be required from the first taxable transaction. For French-established businesses using the domestic franchise en base de TVA, the principal turnover thresholds are generally EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and specified equivalent activities and EUR 37,500 for services, subject to statutory tolerance thresholds, activity-specific rules, and legislative changes. Businesses exceeding the applicable franchise thresholds must transition to normal VAT obligations. Cross-border B2B services and transactions subject to reverse charge can create filing obligations even where no French VAT is collected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from the European Union to destinations outside the EU may be exempt from French VAT with the right to deduct input VAT, provided the supplier establishes that the goods left the EU customs territory and satisfies the documentary and invoicing requirements. For towage and salvage, services are not automatically zero-rated merely because they are performed offshore or involve a foreign customer. B2B services are generally located under the customer-location rule and may be subject to reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction. Specific French VAT exemptions may apply to services directly required for qualifying sea-going vessels engaged in international navigation, including qualifying towing, salvage, and related services, provided the statutory vessel, use, contractual, and documentary conditions are met. Services connected with vessels or operations that do not satisfy those conditions require transaction-by-transaction analysis. VAT refunds require valid invoices, eligible business expenditure, and evidence that the input tax relates to taxable or exempt-with-credit activities.

  • Electronic export declaration accepted by French customs or the competent EU customs system, including the Movement Reference Number where applicable
  • Export accompanying document and customs exit confirmation or equivalent proof that the goods exited the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, contract, packing list, and transport documentation appropriate to the transaction
  • Bill of lading, sea waybill, charter-party evidence, or other maritime transport record where relevant
  • Proof of delivery or destination outside the EU where required to substantiate export status
  • For qualifying vessel-related exemptions, vessel registration and classification evidence, proof of international-navigation status, service orders, work reports, and evidence that the services were directly required for the vessel or its cargo
  • Customer VAT identification and reverse-charge evidence for applicable B2B services
  • Banking and payment records supporting the underlying transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a generally mandatory French export-VAT document

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through approved platforms or the public invoicing portal framework. The regime is not a universal real-time pre-clearance system. Mandatory receipt of electronic invoices is scheduled for all businesses from 1 September 2026. Mandatory issuance is scheduled from 1 September 2026 for large and medium-sized businesses and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro-enterprises, subject to the applicable legal timetable. Electronic invoices must use an approved structured or hybrid format and contain required invoice data. E-reporting of relevant B2C and cross-border transactions will apply according to the statutory timetable. Businesses must maintain reliable digital records, VAT ledgers, and audit trails; no general digital tax stamp is required for every invoice.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export-VAT qualification. The taxpayer must retain reliable evidence supporting the VAT treatment and produce it during a DGFiP or customs audit. Unsupported exemption or zero-rating may be reassessed as French VAT, generally at the applicable rate, with recovery of wrongly deducted input VAT, late-payment interest, and tax penalties. Penalties may include a percentage surcharge for insufficient or inaccurate filings, increased sanctions for deliberate or fraudulent conduct, and customs penalties for false declarations or failure to substantiate export. Criminal liability may arise in cases involving intentional tax fraud, forged documents, concealment, or organized fraudulent schemes. The precise assessment depends on the nature of the error, taxpayer conduct, cooperation, limitation periods, and applicable customs or tax provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-zone jurisdiction with fully convertible capital and current accounts. No exchange-control approval or treasury licence is required for ordinary commercial payments, profit remittances, or capital operations under the Code monétaire et financier. The only material compliance obligations are statistical declaration to the Banque de France through resident banking intermediaries, EU/OECD capital freedom requirements, potential scrutiny under the French outward-direct-investment screening regime in strategic sectors, and post-event reporting for direct investment flows exceeding administrative thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion into euros in France. Export revenues may be retained in any currency in resident bank accounts. However, all cross-border transactions are subject to balance-of-payments reporting by the resident bank to the Banque de France, and the exporter must maintain underlying contracts, invoices, import-export documents, and bank records for the statutory six-year limitation period.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Central Bank of France) with the Direction des Statistiques Monétaires et de la Balance des Paiements responsible for foreign-exchange statistics, and the Direction Générale du Trésor of the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Sovereign Industrial and Digital Sovereignty acting as the principal financial regulator for capital movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

French Interbank payment institutions and electronic money institutions may offer operational e-current accounts and cross-border usance payment rails, but comprehensive viability is Medium with restrictive use for capital escrow and high-value salvage settlements. An EMI can support routine operational payments, but tier-1 regulated commercial banks remain mandatory for capital injection escrow accounts, documentary letters of credit, and treasury hedging activities. EMIs generally lack the correspondent banking depth, documentary credit issuance authority, and Lloyd's-form escrow acceptance infrastructure required in an offshore towage and salvage contract flow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to the French SAS must be executed via SWIFT MT103 in EUR to the dedicated capital escrow account opened in the name of the company, with field 70 'Remittance Information' stating clearly 'CAPITAL INCREASE / APP' and the share-subscription reference. The remitter must provide the subscribing deed, minutes of the shareholders' meeting or board resolution, the notarised or certified capital increase statement, and proof of legitimacy of funds for a complete restriction-free credit. French resident banks must capture the economic purpose code for balance-of-payments statistics, and any individual transaction of at least EUR 10,000 triggers automatic anti-money-laundering verification and TRACFIN notification obligations. Required supporting documents include the updated Kbis extract, SIRET number, UBO declaration, and evidence that the capital deposit is not originating from an unlicensed or opaque arrangement incompatible with EU anti-money-laundering rules.Outbound trade disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 for immediate wire settlements when the disbursement exceeds the bank's online banking limits, and documentary USANCE or sight letter of credit (Lettres de Crédit Documentaires) executed under UCP600 for non-immediate or escrowed marine salvage awards. For emergency towage and salvage disbursements, payment can be transmitted through the bank's high-velocity euro rail (European SEPA Credit Transfer or SWIFT EUR single-euro area) with real-time liquidity management. The settlement platform must support Lloyd's Standard Form of Salvage Agreement provisions; specifically, security to the salvors via bank guarantee or escrow is often required before the release of ship or cargo, and the bank should allow the making of an escrow account deed with multiple-security interests under French law.Outright foreign-exchange forwards and non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) with French credit institutions for hedging commercial foreign-currency exposures directly arising from tug invoices and salvage awards in currencies such as USD and GBP.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps and FX swaps for managing matched funding and large marine-asset capital leases in foreign currencies.Premium-bearing foreign-exchange options and zero-cost collars covering the residual FX exposure of receivables receivable in volatile emerging-market currencies.Commodity-linked hedging is permitted only when directly incident to the fuel-oil and bunker-cost exposure of the towing fleet.
