Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), with registration in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés maintained by the competent commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, principally Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 to R.227-2 governing sociétés par actions simplifiées; Articles L.123-1 et seq. and R.123-1 et seq. governing the Trade and Companies Register and registration formalities; Monetary and Financial Code provisions apply separately if the business constitutes regulated credit, lending, payment, investment, or fund activity.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of creation and application for registration in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés through the INPI Guichet unique, supported by the signed SAS articles of association and beneficial ownership declaration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 40 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to an ordinary SAS. The articles determine the share capital, and at least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription; the balance may be paid within five years. Contributions in kind generally require an independent contribution auditor unless the statutory exemptions are satisfied. Capital is normally deposited in a temporary account with a French bank, notary, or authorised payment institution, which issues the certificate of deposit before registration. The stated USD amount is an approximate conversion of the ordinary RCS registration fee of EUR 37.45 and excludes the separate beneficial ownership filing fee and any publication or professional charges.

Funding & unlock path: No industrial clearance is required for ordinary leased office premises beyond landlord and building compliance. The premises must satisfy French fire-safety and accessibility rules applicable to its classification, including emergency exits, evacuation arrangements, fire equipment, and electrical safety. Workplace risk assessment and the document unique d'évaluation des risques professionnels are required for employees. Structural load inspections are required only where the premises, equipment, storage, or works create a relevant structural risk.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies to an ordinary SAS. The articles determine the share capital, and at least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription; the balance may be paid within five years. Contributions in kind generally require an independent contribution auditor unless the statutory exemptions are satisfied. Capital is normally deposited in a temporary account with a French bank, notary, or authorised payment institution, which issues the certificate of deposit before registration. The stated USD amount is an approximate conversion of the ordinary RCS registration fee of EUR 37.45 and excludes the separate beneficial ownership filing fee and any publication or professional charges.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable to a non-industrial, office-based revenue-based financing company. If the company later operates physical premises or a regulated financial activity, the relevant authorities may include the local prefecture, Direction régionale de l'environnement, de l'aménagement et du logement (DREAL), ACPR, or AMF.

Category: No general manufacturing or industrial operating permit is required for the stated office-based financing activity. Any ACPR or AMF authorisation, registration, or notification must be assessed separately against the actual financing, fund-management, credit, payment, and solicitation model.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible within the Eurosystem. France applies EU-wide capital liberalization with no exchange controls on current or capital account operations inside the EEA; non-EU flows are also freely convertible but attract statutory declaration duties and, in narrow cases, prior authorization under CMF L152-1 to L152-7 and R152-1 et seq. Profit remittances by an SAS are unrestricted: dividends may flow abroad under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0% withholding where conditions are met) or a 25% treaty-reduced rate otherwise; practical hurdles are limited to UBO registration, demonstrable economic substance, and Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender ratio or mandatory conversion applies to export receipts. French residents receiving cross-border funds must, however, file an electronic transfer declaration (TSC — Déclaration de transfert de sommes) with the Banque de France for each transaction or aggregated series of transactions equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500, via the OneGate platform under CMF R152-3. Proceeds remain fully disposable in EUR or any foreign currency and may be retained offshore only insofar as they are declared for French corporate tax and BOP statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, CMF), with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the banking supervisory arm and the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) responsible for external financial policy; EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (TFEU Arts. 63-66) are directly applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Qonto, Recyclib, or pan-EU EMIs passporting into France under EMD2) can operate as effective SEPA collection and euro-disbursement rails, and some offer proprietary FX conversion. Legal constraint: an EMI cannot hold client funds for lending, grant trade credit, or otherwise act as a credit institution without a separate ACPR credit license, and safeguarded client balances may not be reinvested in the treasury portfolio. Feasibility rating: Medium — acceptable for daily receipts and payouts, but a full-service tier-1 bank is mandatory for the capital escrow, drawdown facility, and FX-hedging lines.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

SAS presents as a royalty-financing fund without a regulated banking license, raising the CMF L511-1/L511-2 banking-monopoly red flag (suspected illegal credit activity).

Root cause: SAS presents as a royalty-financing fund without a regulated banking license, raising the CMF L511-1/L511-2 banking-monopoly red flag (suspected illegal credit activity).

Defense evidence: French counsel legal opinion characterizing the arrangement as non-banking proprietary revenue-based financing; executed funding contracts with the digital-brand portfolio; audited annual accounts proving the absence of retail deposits; ISIC 6499 explanatory letter; corporate articles (statuts) demonstrating that funds are proprietary and not third-party savings.
Ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs) are non-EEA or offshore-resident, triggering enhanced due diligence under the French AML/CFT regime (Monetary and Financial Code L561-1 et seq.).

Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs) are non-EEA or offshore-resident, triggering enhanced due diligence under the French AML/CFT regime (Monetary and Financial Code L561-1 et seq.).

Defense evidence: Notarized and apostilled UBO registry declaration (RCS extract from the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce); source-of-wealth memorandum with bank statements of each UBO; tax residence certificates for all shareholders; passport copies certified by a French consulate; organizational chart of the whole ownership chain.
High-cadence outbound SWIFT MT103 wires to foreign digital-brand counterparties may be flagged as unusual or potentially suspicious payment activity by the bank's transaction monitoring system.

Root cause: High-cadence outbound SWIFT MT103 wires to foreign digital-brand counterparties may be flagged as unusual or potentially suspicious payment activity by the bank's transaction monitoring system.

Defense evidence: Contractual waterfall of the revenue-based financing model; underlying cash-flow schedule sent to the bank in advance; FX hedge confirmations evidencing real commercial purpose; internal AML/CFT manual covering sanctions screening, PEP checks, and transaction limits; statutory auditor (Commissaire aux comptes) certificate validating treasury movements.
Apparent mismatch between the modest share capital (no minimum for an SAS) and expected treasury volume, raising concern about a shell vehicle or money-laundering conduit.

Root cause: Apparent mismatch between the modest share capital (no minimum for an SAS) and expected treasury volume, raising concern about a shell vehicle or money-laundering conduit.

Defense evidence: French registered-office lease recorded in the RCS; local corporate officer mandate; parent/beneficial owner comfort letter; annual budget and projected cash-flow forecast; an opening balance sheet demonstrating the economic substance of the financing activity; references from a previous onboarding bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route euro capital injections via TARGET2 through SWIFT MT103; for multi-currency or USD flows use correspondent banking with an MT202/MT103 COVER. The bank will require the SAS board resolution (extrait du procès-verbal), the share issuance deed or hybrid-financing agreement, and confirmation that the remitter is the declared UBO or the group treasury entity.Use SEPA Credit Transfer (standard or instant SCT/Inst) for euro disbursements within the EEA; SWIFT MT103 for USD/GBP and non-EEA euro trade payouts with full structured beneficiary data (50k/59a) and pre-screening; standby or commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP 600 for larger trade settlements requiring documentary security; and SEPA Core/B2B Direct Debit mandates for recurring royalty-advance recoveries from funded counterparties.
