Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum capital for a SAS; articles may set capital freely, typically €1 to €100. Cash contributions must be at least 50% paid-up on incorporation and the balance within 5 years; in-kind contributions must be fully paid-up and are subject to a statutory auditor's valuation report unless the shareholders unanimously waive it. No escrow account is legally mandatory for cash contributions, but funds must remain on deposit with a French bank or an approved depositary until the RCS registration certificate is issued if substantive capital is declared.

Funding & unlock path: General workplace safety clearance for the registered office is required: the office must not exceed REACH/RMS occupational safety thresholds under the Code du Travail. A fire safety attestation is required only when the premises host more than 19 employees or are open to the public. For a standard e-commerce office, an employer self-assessment under the single risk document (DUERP) and installation of basic fire extinguishers are sufficient; no pre-activity civil defense approval is needed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full EEA capital-account convertibility. France applies Articles 63 to 66 TFEU and the EU Capital Movements Directive; restrictions exist only under EU safeguard clauses. Current-account transactions (Amazon FBA proceeds, supplier payments, logistics and marketing costs) are freely convertible and require no prior Treasury authorization. Capital-account items (SAS share-capital injection, intra-group loans, profit and dividend remittance) are equally unrestricted. Frictions are limited to (i) the domestic dividend withholding regime (generally 25% under the 2024 finance-law framework and 30% standard flat tax for distributions, subject to treaty relief), (ii) Banque de France statistical reporting obligations for cross-border financial flows, and (iii) standard banking source-of-funds verification. No obligation exists to route FBA proceeds through a French bank; any ACPR-supervised PSP with an EEA passport may lawfully settle the SAS receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no statutory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline for export receipts. The SAS may retain USD, GBP or CNY proceeds in foreign-currency accounts at any licensed bank or ACPR-compliant payment institution. Administrative duties are limited to (i) Banque de France balance-of-payments data filing for qualifying cross-border transfers, (ii) customs declaration of physical cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more, and (iii) retention of underlying transaction documents (Amazon settlement statements, customs declarations, import invoices) for anti-money-laundering and treasury audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Central Bank of France), acting in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty), which retains statutory authority over foreign-exchange controls, capital-movement regulation and investment screening under Articles L.151-1 to L.165-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier). Banking licensing and AML supervision are exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution). The Banque de France also serves as the sole competent authority for balance-of-payments statistics and cross-border payment reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High for ACPR/EU/EEA-regulated fintech and e-money institutions; conditional for offshore providers. The French SAS may lawfully use Qonto (ACPR-licensed credit institution) and Stripe (ACPR-licensed payment institution) as operating and settlement rails; Airwallex is viable through its EU/EEA EMI authorization or its French branch-agent arrangement for multi-currency collection and FX conversion. A critical legal constraint is that an unlicensed offshore EMI without an EU/EEA passport cannot lawfully hold funds or issue French IBANs for the SAS, and engaging such a provider exposes the entity to ACPR enforcement risk as an unregulated service dependency. The recommended blueprint therefore confines fintech EMIs to collection, FX conversion and payment-acceleration sub-rails, while the Tier-1 French commercial bank remains the executive liquidity anchor and final settlement destination.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share-capital contributions are wired via SWIFT MT103 from a non-EEA remitter, or via SEPA Credit Transfer when the shareholder maintains an EEA IBAN, to the SAS capital escrow account (compte de dépôt de capital) at BNP Paribas or Société Générale. The remittance instruction must carry the Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose classification for direct investment, typically 'Apport de capital - Investissement direct', together with the shareholders' meeting minutes, the French incorporation draft and the source-of-funds trail. Once the SAS is operational, supplementary shareholder funding (compte courant d'associé) is settled through MT103 or SEPA CT under the associated-loan classification; all cross-border messages must include the ISO 20022 Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR), full beneficiary structure and a matching payment reference to prevent rejection or AML quarantine. Release of escrowed funds is authorized only after registration of the SAS with the Greffe and verification by the bank of the release conditions.Trade payables and operating deductions use three parallel clearing rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA B2B Direct Debit for EU-based costs, including recurring Amazon FBA fee collections initiated under the SAS IBAN mandate; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-EU transfers (China and Hong Kong suppliers, freight, customs and warehousing) with invoice number, Incoterms and UETR in the remittance fields; and (iii) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit (UCP 600, at-sight or usance) arranged with the Société Générale or Crédit Agricole trade desks for large USD/CNY production batches requiring bank-intermediated settlement. Stripe and Airwallex payout sweeps are converted to EUR at transparent market-based rates and swept into the Tier-1 operational account on the next available treasury cycle. All non-EUR disbursements exceeding the applicable threshold are reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics, and every payable is matched to an underlying customs-cleared import or marketplace service invoice.Deliverable EUR/USD and EUR/GBP foreign exchange forwards (maturities from one week to twelve months) with BNP Paribas or Société Générale under an ISDA 2002 master agreement; permitted without prior French Treasury authorization when used to hedge the Amazon FBA settlement cycle.EUR/CNH Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) referencing the CNH offshore fixing, permissible for hedging imported inventory exposure; onshore CNY remains non-deliverable for French non-resident counterparties and NDFs must be executed with the bank's global-markets desk under EMIR margin rules.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps (CCIRS) converting long-dated USD or CNY commercial borrowings into EUR, available where an underlying documented commercial debt exists and the SAS retains non-financial-counterparty status with respect to EMIR clearing thresholds.Purchased FX options on EUR/USD and EUR/CNH, including zero-premium collars and premium-limited option spreads, used to cap conversion costs on projected FBA revenues; internal treasury policy restricts hedge notional to a maximum of 70% of the twelve-month projected marketplace turnover.PSP-treasury automated FX conversion executed by Airwallex or Stripe at transparent mid-market rates for payment-volume conversion, recognized as a separate hedging and settlement sub-rail alongside bank deliverable forwards, with monthly consolidated reporting into the corporate treasury ledger.

Calendar-year companies: second working day after 1 May of the following year; off-calendar-year companies: within three months of the fiscal year-end; automatic extension may apply to online filers assisted by a licensed accountant.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File required where the French entity's annual turnover or gross assets exceed EUR 400 million, or where the group consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 400 million (Article L.13 AA CGI); simplified documentation may be required where group turnover exceeds EUR 50 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the Gulf model; however, genuine substance (staff, premises, management and strategic decision-making in France) is required to rely on DTA benefits, EU directives, and to defeat the French general anti-abuse rule (abus de droit, Article L.64 LPF).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful Failure to File

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty: 40% (bad faith) or 80% (fraud) of unpaid tax plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month; criminal conviction: fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under Article 1741 CGI.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable as authors or accomplices; managing director of the SAS faces imprisonment, fines, civil restitution of avoided taxes, travel restrictions, and possible asset seizure.
Shell Company / Artificial Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Abuse of law recharacterisation under Article L.64 LPF; 80% penalty in case of abuse; income reallocated to the actual beneficial owner, plus late-payment interest and potential loss of treaty protections.

Director personal liability: If the director controls the artificial shell, the corporate veil can be pierced for tax debts and penalties; directors may face joint and several liability, travel bans, and criminal prosecution for fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under Article 57 CGI; add-back of artificial profits; penalties of 40% or 80% for unjustified documentation; late-payment interest; possible criminal tax evasion charges.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation can face personal administrative fines and civil liability; in serious cases criminal sanctions apply and personal assets of directors may be attached under Article L.267 LPF.
Money Laundering / Concealed Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: AML violations under Articles L.561-1 et seq. of the Monetary and Financial Code: administrative fines up to €1 million or up to 10% of annual turnover; criminal money laundering sanctions up to 10 years' imprisonment and confiscation of funds.

Director personal liability: Directors directly involved may face criminal liability, personal fines, travel restrictions, provisional attachment of personal assets, and civil damages to third-party creditors.
