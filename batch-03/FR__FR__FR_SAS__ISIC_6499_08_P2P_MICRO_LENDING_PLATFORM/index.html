Competent corporate registry

Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises (INPI single digital portal) and the territorial Registry of the Commercial Court (Greffe du Tribunal de commerce) of the department where the registered office is situated.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.227-1 to L.227-30 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); Decree No. 67-236 of 23 March 1967 on commercial registry formalities; Code monétaire et financier Articles L.547-1 to L.547-12 and Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 for the regulated crowdfunding loan intermediation activity.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts constitutifs (Articles of Association) executed in private deed or before a notary, submitted electronically via the 'Guichet unique' with the standardised incorporation application (formulaire M0), the bank or notarial certificate of cash capital deposit, the declaration of beneficial owner (déclaration des bénéficiaires effectifs) and the attestation of publication of the legal announcement.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS; the share capital is freely fixed by the shareholders in the statuts. Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and paid up prior to registration and must be escrowed with a French bank or a notary (dépôt des fonds) until the certificate of registration (Kbis) is issued. For the regulated P2P micro-lending permit, the ACPR additionally applies the own-funds requirement under Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 (EUR 25,000 or one quarter of fixed overheads, whichever is higher).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member. The euro (EUR) is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. Exchange controls were abolished; inbound and outbound capital, equity injections, intercompany loans, dividends, royalties, loan repayments, and profit remittances are not subject to prior authorisation or quantitative limits. Profit remittance faces no FX-specific hurdle after corporate tax and withholding tax compliance. Residual frictions arise from AML/CFT, sanctions screening, balance-of-payments statistical reporting, and sector licensing. Banks must verify the purpose and origin of funds, and high-risk fintech activity may trigger enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Resident entities may retain export proceeds in EUR or foreign currency and are not required to convert them within a prescribed timeline. Reporting obligations to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics remain for certain international transactions, generally through the declarative portal and depending on applicable thresholds; these are statistical filings, not FX surrender requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) is the central bank; foreign-exchange policy is administered by the Direction générale du Trésor (DG Trésor), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique. Prudential licensing and AML/CFT supervision for banks, payment institutions, electronic money institutions, and crowdfunding service providers are exercised by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR) and the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate to high for EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment service providers (PSPs) passporting under PSD2/EMD2; low for non-EU offshore EMIs. EU-licensed EMIs may provide multi-currency accounts, cross-border collections, and wallet payouts, provided client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts at an EU credit institution or invested in secure low-risk assets. Non-EU EMIs cannot passport into France and must rely on reverse solicitation, local partnership, or a French/EU licence. ACPR authorisation is mandatory for payment services, e-money issuance, and certain lending/crowdfunding models; AMF authorisation is required for crowdfunding service providers under Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 and for certain intermediation activities. AML/CFT, sanctions, and consumer-credit rules constrain cross-border viability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection is typically executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COV from a shareholder or investor account to the French SAS corporate account. Standard purpose codes include INVESTMENT CAPITAL, EQUITY CAPITAL, SHARE CAPITAL, INTERCOMPANY LOAN, and LOAN. Banks verify the capital-increase documentation, notarised or certified articles, K-bis, shareholder resolutions, UBO data, and source of funds. No prior FX authorisation is required. EUR settlements use TARGET2/T2 or SEPA Credit Transfer; non-EUR injections involve correspondent banking and FX conversion. Balance-of-payments statistical declarations to Banque de France may apply above thresholds.Outbound trade and platform disbursements use SWIFT MT103 for clean payments, MT202 for bank-to-bank transfers, SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EUR payments, SEPA Core/B2B Direct Debit for collections, and commercial letters of credit or documentary collections under UCP 600 and URR 725. Cross-border trade settlement may use MT700/MT760 for LC issuance and amendment. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million group consolidated turnover or total assets; additional mandatory declaration for intercompany payments to non-residents exceeding EUR 100,000 per annum via Form 2257-SD. Local File and Master File must be maintained at the French fiscal seat within 30 days of a request by the tax administration.

Economic substance scope: France applies no Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of the offshore type, but imposes an effective place-of-management doctrine, Article 165 bis CGI and anti-abuse rules (Article L.64 LPF, EU ATAD). The SAS must demonstrate actual decisions, staff, premises, and real lending operations in France, otherwise tax benefits, treaty access, and Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief are denied as artificial arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Fraudulent Omission in CIT Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative: 40% fraud penalty, 80% penalty for abusive arrangement, plus late-payment interest (0.20% per month). Criminal: under Article 1741 CGI, up to EUR 500,000 fine and five years' imprisonment; the fine may be doubled to the amount of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers are jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalties, and interest under Article L.267 LPF; may face imprisonment, placement on a French travel ban (interdiction de quitter le territoire), prohibition on managing a business, and civil restitution of unpaid corporate taxes.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Article L.64 LPF abuse of law doctrine: restatement of the entire arrangement, additional income tax, 80% aggravation penalty, and denial of DTA benefits; if part of a sham, the tax administration can disregard the legal entity and tax the recipient/parent directly.

Director personal liability: The president of the SAS may be personally assessed for the taxes re-determined; possible civil liability for damages, criminal prosecution under Article 1743 CGI for fraudulent registration or concealment, and international travel bans to guarantee execution of the assessment.
AML / Unlicensed Financial Activity

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions under Articles L.571-4 and L.541-8 of the French Monetary and Financial Code: up to EUR 750,000 fine and five years' imprisonment for illegal exercise of credit operations; ACPR administrative penalties up to EUR 100 million or 10% of annual turnover; licence withdrawal and injunction to cease business; interest on unlawful loans is void.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers face criminal conviction, automatic prohibition on corporate management, travel bans, and personal orders for restitution of interest or commissions; assets can be seized as security in criminal proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Violation / Deficient Documentation

Statutory sanction: Fixed fine of EUR 15,000 for non-disclosure of Form 2257-SD (Article 1732 ter CGI); transfer-pricing adjustment is supplemented by a 40% penalty for clearly fictitious acts and 0.20% monthly interest; in case of abuse, 80% penalty (Article 1729 CGI). Criminal exposure can arise if deliberate concealment exceeds 10%, plus a potential fine of EUR 500,000.

Director personal liability: The legal representative who knowingly executes or approves non-arm's-length pricing is jointly liable for the resulting tax and penalties; personal civil damages may be claimed by minority shareholders and the fiscal administration; the court may order an anti-departure order and confiscation of corporate assets.
