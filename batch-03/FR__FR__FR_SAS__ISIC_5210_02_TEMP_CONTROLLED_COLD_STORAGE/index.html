Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum paid-up capital for SAS; capital is freely set in the articles. For cold storage operations, a minimum of €10,000 is recommended to cover initial licensing and insurance. Capital must be fully paid up at incorporation if in cash (deposited in a blocked bank account) or in kind (valued by a statutory auditor if value exceeds €30,000). No escrow account is required by law, but the bank issues a certificate of deposit.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (Commission de Sécurité) – required for warehouses over 2,500 m²; structural load inspection by a certified engineer; occupational health clearance (Médecine du Travail) for employees; compliance with the Labor Code (Code du travail) for temperature-controlled environments (minimum 12°C for work areas, but cold storage areas must have emergency procedures).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via impots.gouv.fr; export customs and indirect-duty jurisdiction: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via douane.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the French SAS obtains SIREN and SIRET identification numbers from the Registre National des Entreprises (INSEE). The SAS then applies through the DGFiP online portal 'gérer mon numéro de TVA' in its impots.gouv.fr account to obtain the French intra-Community VAT number (FR-SIREN). Where customs operations occur, the SAS separately obtains an EORI number from DGDDI. Foreign shareholders require no local tax identifier, and because the SAS is a resident company, no non-resident fiscal representative is needed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law applies no check-the-box or partnership look-through election; a Société par Actions Simplifiée is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (Article 206 CGI). Primary statutory elections include: (i) French tax consolidation ('intégration fiscale') for a French parent owning at least 95% of qualifying subsidiaries; (ii) election between the simplified real regime and the normal real regime for corporate income tax reporting based on turnover thresholds; (iii) optional Group VAT registration from 1 January 2023 allowing a qualifying French head company to treat itself and its 95%-owned French subsidiaries as a single taxable person; and (iv) no hybrid or territorial mix is available for a resident SAS, the worldwide income of which is within the French corporate income tax charge, subject to applicable tax treaties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration exemption applies to a French-established trader; the micro-enterprise 'franchise en base de TVA' relief is available only to resident businesses with annual turnover not exceeding EUR 36,800 for services or EUR 91,900 for goods. A non-established foreign supplier must register for French VAT before its first taxable supply in France, with no minimum threshold; the EU intra-Community B2C distance-sales threshold of EUR 10,000 is relevant only to cross-border remote sales and does not waive registration for B2B supplies or warehousing services physically performed in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods dispatched by the seller or on the seller's behalf to a destination outside the EU customs territory qualify for 0% VAT under Article 262-I-1° CGI. Cold-storage warehousing and related handling services physically performed in France qualify for zero-rating where the services are directly connected with goods that are being exported outside the EU (Article 262-II-2° CGI) or where the goods are placed in an authorized customs-duty suspension or export warehouse arrangement. The exemption also covers services supplied for goods in transit under the external transit procedure, provided the service provider retains documentary evidence of the export destination and the goods leave the EU within the statutory period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/EUA - Document Administratif Unique) with customs release confirmation from the DELTA export system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from EU customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt and repatriation of export sale proceeds within the statutory 180-day period

Mandatory electronic invoicing is phased in under the 2023 Finance Act: large enterprises from 1 September 2026 and small and medium-sized enterprises from 1 September 2027 must issue structured electronic invoices through accredited platforms, and all established taxable persons must already be able to receive such invoices. Invoices to public-sector entities must be submitted through Chorus Pro. The reform also introduces mandatory e-reporting of transaction data to DGFiP. France does not presently impose real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance digital stamping, or continuous API validation of VAT invoices prior to issuance; compliance is enforced through periodic VAT returns (CA3) and the e-reporting/declaration channel.

Claiming export zero-rating without valid supporting documents triggers retrospective VAT assessment under Articles 262 and 283 CGI, plus late-payment interest (intérêts de retard). Bad-faith under-declaration draws a 40% penalty, and fraudulent maneuvers or production of forged export evidence draw an 80% penalty. Criminal tax fraud (Article 1741 CGI) may additionally be prosecuted with penalties up to EUR 750,000 and ten years' imprisonment for senior officers. Customs authorities may impose separate confiscation fines and penalties under the Code des douanes, and the responsible export manager may be jointly liable in private law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is part of the euro area; the euro is fully convertible. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are liberalised under Article 63 TFEU and the Code monétaire et financier (Articles L151-1 et seq.). Profit remittance through dividend distributions and shareholder loan repayments is unrestricted, subject to applicable withholding tax and treaty relief. No prior exchange-control approval is required for capital inflows or outflows. Banks must report individual cross-border transactions of EUR 50,000 or more to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics; this is a filing duty, not a restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists in French law, and no statutory repatriation timeline applies to euro- or foreign-currency export receipts. Export receivables may be retained in authorised foreign-currency bank accounts opened with French prudentially licensed banks. All export proceeds must transit through French banking channels and remain subject to anti-money laundering, anti-terrorist financing, and tax-reporting obligations. No specific conversion ratio or timing requirement is imposed for cold-chain logistics service receivables.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking supervision and the Direction Générale du Trésor for exchange control policy

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for auxiliary treasury accounts only. A French-authorised electronic money institution (EMI), such as Qonto or Shine (regulated by ACPR under the PSD2 regime), may open operational accounts in EUR and provide virtual IBANs within the SEPA zone. However, an EMI cannot hold funds above Article L525-4 CMF limits in the same manner as a credit institution, cannot grant credit to clients, and cannot offer investment or FX derivative services. A non-EEA EMI, including post-Brexit UK EMI, no longer benefits from EU passporting and cannot provide principal banking services in France without establishing an EU-authorised branch or using a regulated partner. EMIs are not viable for capital-escrow, intra-group cross-border liquidity, or hedging transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from a non-resident shareholder must be made through an authorised French bank using a SWIFT MT103 (for outside the SEPA area) or an SEPA Credit Transfer (for EU/EEA investors) in EUR to the SAS's French IBAN (FR76 + RIB). The payment message must contain the purpose code 'CAP_1' or the equivalent proprietary code of the receiving bank, with remittance language clearly stating 'Dépôt de capital – souscription d'actions SAS [legal name] – exercice [year]'. For each transaction of EUR 50,000 or more, the French bank will file the balance-of-payments declaration to Banque de France. Before crediting the funds to the operational account, the bank may require a certified copy of the shareholder subscription resolution, the capital-increase deed, and an attestation from the management accepting the fund transfer.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax on dividends and similar distributions paid to non-residents, subject to exemptions, EU directives, tax treaties, anti-abuse rules, and applicable procedural requirements. Dividends paid to a qualifying EU parent may be exempt under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, commonly requiring at least 5% ownership, a qualifying holding period, and anti-abuse compliance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the fiscal year. For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the return is generally due on the second business day following 1 May of the following year, subject to the applicable DGFiP calendar and any officially granted electronic filing tolerance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150,000,000 of French turnover excluding tax or gross assets, or applicable group-relationship and control conditions, generally triggers the full transfer-pricing documentation obligation, including Local File and Master File requirements under French law. A separate annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies at the EUR 50,000,000 threshold or where other statutory controlled-transaction conditions are met. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a single standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless assessed under French transfer-pricing rules, the abuse-of-law doctrine, the principal-purpose test in applicable treaties, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled-foreign-company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, EU anti-avoidance rules, and general business-purpose principles. A French SAS should maintain real French decision-making, personnel or operational capacity appropriate to its cold-storage activity, premises, equipment, risk control, customer contracts, accounting records, and commercial rationale. Conduit arrangements, circular financing, artificial management fees, and entities lacking operational substance present material reassessment and penalty risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate tax evasion and fraudulent accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties generally ranging from 10% to 80%, and criminal tax-fraud sanctions. Aggravated tax fraud may carry imprisonment of up to seven years and a fine of up to EUR 3,000,000, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur criminal liability, civil liability to the company or creditors, and personal tax-payment liability where fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated non-compliance makes collection impossible under Article L.267 of the French Tax Procedures Book. A court may impose management bans, asset seizures, judicial control, or travel restrictions where legally justified; such measures are not automatic.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment of taxable income, withholding-tax exposure, interest, and transfer-pricing documentation penalties. Failure or material inadequacy of documentation may trigger a penalty generally based on the higher of a statutory minimum and a percentage of controlled transactions or reassessed profits, subject to the applicable law and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors can face civil liability for knowingly approving artificial arrangements and may be personally exposed in cases of fraud, asset diversion, insolvency misconduct, or deliberate obstruction of a tax audit.
Undeclared dividend, interest, or royalty distribution

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, interest, penalties, potential 40% or 80% surcharges in aggravated cases, and possible denial of treaty or EU-directive relief.

Director personal liability: The legal representative may be pursued for tax-payment liability where deliberate or grossly negligent conduct caused non-collection. Criminal exposure may arise if the omission forms part of tax fraud or fraudulent accounting.
VAT fraud and carousel transactions

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, repayment of improperly claimed credits, late-payment interest, penalties up to 80% for fraudulent conduct, and criminal prosecution. False invoicing and organized VAT fraud may produce aggravated criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be criminally prosecuted and may face asset seizure, management bans, personal civil liability, and collection proceedings where they knowingly participated in or facilitated the fraud.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of deductions or treaty benefits, transfer-pricing and permanent-establishment adjustments, withholding-tax recovery, abuse-of-law penalties, interest, and possible application of anti-avoidance provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for knowingly implementing artificial structures, especially where the arrangement causes tax loss, creditor prejudice, insolvency, or asset diversion. Veil piercing is exceptional and fact-dependent, not automatic.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-owner violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative, regulatory, and criminal sanctions under French AML rules, including fines, professional restrictions, confiscation, and imprisonment depending on the underlying offense. Transactions may be frozen or rejected.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, compliance personnel, and beneficial owners may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation, concealment, or facilitation. Asset freezing and judicial travel restrictions may apply where ordered by competent authorities.
Cold-storage licensing, food, pharmaceutical, and safety non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative closure, suspension or withdrawal of licenses, product seizure or destruction, corrective orders, administrative fines, contractual claims, and criminal sanctions for endangerment, fraud, or unlawful operation.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur criminal and civil liability for safety breaches, environmental damage, personal injury, product loss, or knowingly falsified compliance records. Insurance coverage may be denied for deliberate violations.
Customs and import-export misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Recovery of customs duties and import VAT, customs penalties, seizure or forfeiture, and criminal sanctions for aggravated customs fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and customs representatives may be personally exposed for intentional misdeclarations, document falsification, or participation in organized customs fraud.
Labor, payroll, and social-security concealment

Statutory sanction: Back assessments of social contributions, surcharges, labor penalties, repayment of public aid, administrative closure, and criminal sanctions for concealed work or employment-law offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for concealed work, unlawful employment, workplace endangerment, or deliberate non-payment of employee and social-security obligations.
Insolvency misconduct and asset diversion

Statutory sanction: Clawback of transactions, extension of insolvency proceedings, contribution to company debts, bankruptcy-related bans, criminal bankruptcy sanctions, and invalidation of abusive distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal contribution to liabilities, management bans, disqualification, asset seizure, and imprisonment for fraudulent bankruptcy or serious management misconduct.
