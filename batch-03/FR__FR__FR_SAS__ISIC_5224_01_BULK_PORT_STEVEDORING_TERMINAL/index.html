Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), with registration in the Registre national des entreprises (RNE) and the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS) maintained by the competent commercial court registry or tribunal des activités économiques registry.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 et seq. governing sociétés par actions simplifiées; Articles L.123-1 et seq. and R.123-1 et seq. governing registration; Articles L.561-46 et seq. governing beneficial-owner disclosure; applicable provisions of the Civil Code for contracts, contributions, and registered-office arrangements.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic application for immatriculation of a société par actions simplifiée to the RNE and RCS through the Guichet unique, accompanied by the signed statuts, beneficial-owner declaration, registered-office evidence, and incorporation notices.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 214 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital applies to a SAS. The amount is determined by the statuts. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must generally be fully released at incorporation. Cash funds are deposited in a blocked account with a French bank, notary, or authorized deposit institution and released after registration against the Kbis extract. The amount excludes the legal-notice publication fee, beneficial-owner filing fee, professional fees, and any applicable chamber or sector charges.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain fire-safety approval or operational clearance through the competent fire and rescue service (SDIS) and port authority where required; maintain fire-water capacity, emergency access, hazardous-area controls, alarms, extinguishing systems, evacuation plans, and a site emergency plan. Secure building permits and completion or conformity documentation for structures, cranes, silos, conveyors, and storage areas, including structural-load and stability assessments by qualified professionals. Complete workplace risk assessment (document unique), occupational-health arrangements, machinery conformity and periodic inspections, ATEX assessment where explosive atmospheres may arise, lifting-equipment inspections, PPE controls, worker training, and any required consultation or notification to labour authorities and CARSAT.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to a SAS. The amount is determined by the statuts. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must generally be fully released at incorporation. Cash funds are deposited in a blocked account with a French bank, notary, or authorized deposit institution and released after registration against the Kbis extract. The amount excludes the legal-notice publication fee, beneficial-owner filing fee, professional fees, and any applicable chamber or sector charges.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) for corporate income tax and VAT; customs and export-related matters are administered by Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI). Business registration and statutory tax ID procurement are effected through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (entreprendre.service-public.fr) and the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés) court greffe.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS, the legal representative files the articles of association and constitutive documents via the Guichet Unique with the local RCS court; the Greffe issues the K-bis extract, automatically assigning the SIREN/SIRET identifiers and the definitive French VAT number (FR + INSEE key) after verification by DGFiP. For foreign entities not established in France, a direct VAT registration application is made through the Guichet Unique (or Form 1170 in transitional scenarios); non-EU applicants must appoint an accredited tax representative (représentant fiscal) jointly liable for French VAT. The entity must also obtain an EORI number from DGDDI for customs procedures. The process generally takes between two and six weeks depending on completeness and tax representative accreditation.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (SAS is a capital company subject to corporate income tax on its worldwide income; permanent establishment exposure is evaluated under French territoriality rules for foreign operations). — For a French SAS, the legal representative files the articles of association and constitutive documents via the Guichet Unique with the local RCS court; the Greffe issues the K-bis extract, automatically assigning the SIREN/SIRET identifiers and the definitive French VAT number (FR + INSEE key) after verification by DGFiP. For foreign entities not established in France, a direct VAT registration application is made through the Guichet Unique (or Form 1170 in transitional scenarios); non-EU applicants must appoint an accredited tax representative (représentant fiscal) jointly liable for French VAT. The entity must also obtain an EORI number from DGDDI for customs procedures. The process generally takes between two and six weeks depending on completeness and tax representative accreditation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SAS is a capital company subject to corporate income tax on its worldwide income; permanent establishment exposure is evaluated under French territoriality rules for foreign operations).

Available election forms

France does not operate US-style check-the-box elections. Available statutory elections relevant to a bulk-port stevedoring SAS include: (i) VAT group election ('assujetti unique') under Article 256 C of the CGI; (ii) French tax consolidation ('intégration fiscale') for groups holding at least 95% of the subsidiary's shares; (iii) option for simplified real VAT regime if turnover thresholds allow; and (iv) election for treatment as a separate taxable entity for VAT on certain joint terminal arrangements. The SAS cannot elect partnership-style pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold applies to foreign suppliers not established in France (obligation from the first euro of taxable turnover). Domestic micro-enterprises benefit from the franchise en base: EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services (2024 thresholds). The port stevedoring service generally falls under the EUR 37,500 threshold, but the actual SAS terminal operator, being a commercial entity of scale, is normally registered regardless of the domestic threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for export of goods from the EU is mandatory under Article 262-1 of the CGI where the supplier is a taxable person, the goods are physically dispatched outside the EU, and the departure is evidenced by customs export declaration within the statutory deadlines. Services directly related to the exported goods—including stevedoring, dock handling, and bunkering at marine bulk terminals—qualify for zero-rating under Article 275 of the CGI if the service is rendered to the consignor or consignee of the exported goods and is directly linked to the export dispatch. Intra-EU dispatches are subject to a separate exemption regime and require the buyer's valid EU VAT ID, not the export zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en douane d'exportation / DAE) with the validated export exit confirmation from DGDDI
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or equivalent ocean transport document showing cargo lifted at a French port and consigned to a destination outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice issued to the foreign purchaser, stating the export or customs reference
  • Original contract or service agreement for stevedoring/terminal services demonstrating direct linkage to an export cargo
  • Proof of physical exit from the EU (e.g., destination port arrival certificate, customs-scale note, or P&I survey confirmation)
  • Bank statement or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of the export consideration and, where applicable, the inward remittance certificate

France is implementing mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting under the Facturation Électronique reform. All taxable persons must accept e-invoices through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) by 1 September 2026; large and mid-size enterprises must issue e-invoices via the PPF or an accredited partner platform (PDP) by that date, and all taxable persons by 1 September 2027. Invoices must be transmitted in structured electronic formats (Factur-X, UBL, or CII) through certified PDPs using API connections. No fiscal stamp is applied by DGFiP; rather, clearance is achieved through near-real-time transmission of invoice data to the tax authority, supported by a checksum generated by the platform. VAT on imported goods continues to be settled under the customs electronic declaration systems (DELTA).

Export zero-rating is heavily scrutinized by DGFiP and DGDDI during VAT audits. If a taxpayer fails to produce documentary evidence within the audit period, the administration will reassess VAT at the standard rate and apply interest de retard (0.20% per month) plus penalties of 10% to 80% under Articles 1728 and 1729 of the CGI, increasing to 100% for organized fraud or abusive maneuvers (manœuvres frauduleuses). Criminal sanctions for tax evasion can reach 5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 75,000 fine. Where zero-rating is invalid, the input VAT deducted on goods and services attributable to the export is retroactively clawed back, and the tax representative, if any, may be held jointly and severally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies the EU Treaty free movement of capital provisions. The euro is fully convertible and no prior exchange-control authorization is required for current or capital account transactions. Profit remittance and dividend distribution to non-resident shareholders are freely transferable through registered credit institutions, subject to standard corporate tax and, if applicable, 25% dividend withholding tax unless treaty relief or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption is available. Beneficial ownership declarations and banque de France statistical reporting obligations must be observed for all cross-border financial flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender or convert export proceeds into euros in France. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency in eurozone bank accounts. However, all cross-border transfers exceeding EUR 50,000 per calendar month must be reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes on form FDEB (or through the bank's electronic reporting channel). Commercial shipments must be supported by inbound and outbound customs declarations and freight/shipping evidence for treasury reconciliation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French central bank) together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the French Monetary and Financial Code; the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) supervises credit institutions and payment services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for EU-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions operating under PSD II, such as Qonto, MangoPay, and various ACPR-licensed platforms, for day-to-day operational liquidity. Legal constraints require that the EMI hold segregated client funds in a French or EEA credit institution, and that the EMI registers with the ACPR or another EEA competent authority. A licensed stevedoring terminal should not rely solely on an EMI for statutory port-authority clearing, escrow requirements, or complex trade settlement; use EMIs as supplemental payment circuits only.

Defense evidence: PEP assessment report, public tender concession agreement, state concession contract, irrevocable payment guarantees, and correspondence evidencing arms-length commercial terms for the stevedoring concession.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 / MT202 through a French licensed credit institution. The payment narrative must state 'Injection de capitaux' and identify the corporate purpose (e.g., 'Constitution / augmentation de capital'). Attach the signed equity subscription deed, statutory resolution of shareholders, and Article 1 EU Regulation 2023/1113 travel-rule information. For a capital increase, the receiving bank requires RCS filing proof. The correct balance-of-payments classification for the transfer is equity capital, reported as 'F1 – Participations et actions' to the Banque de France. Escrowed injections should be first settled on a segregated capital escrow account, verified against the notarial deed, and only then swept to the operational account after registration.Domestic and euro-area operational payments use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT).
