Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), acting under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique. Professional filing portal: impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel). Business and VAT identification is procured through the single-window Guichet Unique des formalités d'entreprises operated by INPI (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr). Customs and export-declaration counterpart: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via its ATLAS/DELTA electronic clearance platforms. Electronic invoicing infrastructure: Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) with accredited Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires (PDPs).

E-government filing portal

Route A for a French-established SAS: incorporation is filed electronically via the INPI Guichet Unique; DGFiP automatically issues the SIREN/SIRET and the French intra-Community VAT identification number in the mandatory format FR + two check digits + 9-digit SIREN. No separate VAT-registration application is required at formation; the SAS must confirm activation of the VAT account with its local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and file corporate income tax (IS) and VAT returns through impots.gouv.fr. Route B for a non-French remote seller supplying into France: EU-established sellers register directly at the competent SIE using Form 3559-SD without a fiscal representative; non-EU sellers without an EU establishment must file Form 3571-SD and appoint a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) under CGI Article 289 A, who becomes jointly and severally liable for French VAT unless the seller is established in a treaty country with mutual administrative assistance. For pure B2C distance sales of imported consignments not exceeding EUR 150, the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) election on the French OSS portal avoids a full VAT registration; intra-EU B2C distance sales are covered by the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) above the EUR 10,000 annual distance-sales threshold. In the nutraceuticals segment, the statutory DGCCRF notification as a 'complément alimentaire' must precede first marketing and is routinely cross-checked by DGFiP auditors against the VAT registration and export records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a société par actions simplifiée (SAS) is an opaque legal person subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) under CGI Article 206, computed on a territorial-scope basis: profits attributable to French business operations are taxable in France, whereas profits from foreign fixed establishments and foreign real estate are excluded absent an approved worldwide-profits election under CGI Article 209 A. No pass-through or fiscal-transparency election is available for an SAS under French domestic law; the SAS itself is the sole VAT taxable person for all its supplies.

Available election forms

France has no US-style 'check-the-box' election and offers no elective hybrid-mismatch regime; hybrid and reverse-hybrid outcomes are neutralised automatically by statute under the ATAD/NIEC transposition (loi n° 2019-759). Available elective mechanisms for an SAS: (1) intra-group fiscal integration (CGI Articles 223 A et seq.) at a 95%+ shareholding, permitting group profit pooling, loss transfer and neutrality on intra-group transfers; (2) the small-business reduced corporate rate of 15% on the first EUR 42,500 of annual profit where group turnover is EUR 10 million or less (CGI Article 219 I-b); (3) worldwide/consolidated profits election (CGI Article 209 A) subject to prior ministerial approval; (4) the small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base de TVA, CGI Article 293 B) below the applicable turnover ceilings for goods and services, which forfeits input-VAT deduction and is incompatible with OSS/IOSS participation and export-resale models; (5) OSS/IOSS participation for cross-border B2C distance sales and low-value import consignments; and (6) the single-taxable-person group VAT election (assujetti unique) now transposed into French law, available to qualifying VAT groups.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible, and France maintains no current-account or capital-account exchange restrictions. Capital movements, payments and transfers between France and non-residents are fully liberalised under EU Directive 88/361/EEC, implemented by CMF Articles L221-1 to L221-3. Profit remittance by a French SAS is unrestricted once the annual financial statements have been approved by the shareholders. The practical hurdles for an e-commerce nutraceutical entity are limited to (i) the Banque de France monthly balance-of-payments reporting obligation for cross-border transactions aggregating more than EUR 750,000 in a calendar year; (ii) French withholding tax on dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders, generally 25% for a non-resident company under Article 119-bis of the French General Tax Code (CGI) and 30% global levy for non-resident individuals, reduced or eliminated under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (0%) or an applicable double-tax treaty; and (iii) inbound foreign direct investment screening only where the non-EU investor operates in a defined sensitive sector. Online dietary-supplement retail under ISIC 4791 is not a sensitive sector, so no prior FDI authorisation is required, although a CERFA 16473 declaration may be triggered above threshold levels in certain group structures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A French exporting SAS may retain, denominate, transfer or convert its export proceeds freely in EUR or foreign currency; there is no repatriation deadline. Two compliance obligations apply: (i) any bank account opened outside France must be declared annually to the French tax administration on Form 3916-bis; and (ii) if the aggregate value of the company's transactions with non-residents exceeds EUR 750,000 per calendar year, the exporter must file a monthly balance-of-payments declaration with the Banque de France via the TéléBOP system, using the non-resident transaction report, which captures export receipts and financial flows. No conversion into euros is required.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as the central bank and monetary authority, together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance, which exercises foreign-exchange control and foreign-investment screening powers under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, CMF), notably Articles L511-1, L151-1 to L152-5 and R152-1 to R153-5.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability: Medium-High as a complementary rail, Low as a sole treasury rail. ACPR-licensed French payment institutions and electronic-money institutions, including Qonto, Pōs, Treezor (Groupe BPCE) and Vialink, offer remote onboarding, SEPA-instant credit transfers and SWIFT connectivity, and are a legal option for a French SAS. However, standard acceptable-use policies of most EMIs exclude high-risk nutraceutical merchant activation, and EMIs are not a substitute for a high-risk merchant acquirer for card clearing. A non-EEA offshore digital EMI is not compliant as a primary treasury rail because it cannot hold funds in France, cannot provide a French IBAN under the CMF rules applicable to payment services, and creates AML/UBO transparency and substance-evasion risks. Any EMI used must be an EEA-licensed institution passported into France under the CMF pass-porting regime, with a registered branch or appointed agent, and its balances must be consolidated and disclosed in the SAS statutory accounts.

(d) consumer refunds and chargeback settlements cleared through the high-risk merchant acquiring account via the Visa and Mastercard dispute rails; and (e) intra-group cash-pooling through a zero-balancing SEPA structure supported by a written intercompany financing agreement to avoid recharacterisation as undeclared 'compte courant d'associé'.Outright EUR/USD deliverable forward (achat à terme de devises) for USD-denominated ingredient purchases and international marketing costs, tenors typically 1 to 24 months, requiring an underlying commercial exposure and execution under the FBF or ISDA Master Agreement.Outright EUR/CNH deliverable forward and, where the supplier requires onshore CNY settlement, a non-deliverable forward (NDF) on EUR/CNY executed through a Paris or London dealing desk, so that no onshore CNY account or convertibility approval is needed.Vanilla European-style EUR/USD and EUR/CNH foreign-exchange options (put/call) with net premium upfront, used to hedge budget rates while preserving upside on uncertain cross-border e-commerce flows.FX swaps (swap de change) for managing short-term mismatches between incoming USD or CNH receipts and euro operating disbursements, with maturity not exceeding 12 months.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (swap de devises) available only where a long-term supplier financing facility or intercompany loan exists, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and subject to EMIR trade reporting and clearing-threshold self-assessment, with the SAS qualifying as a non-financial counterparty below the clearing threshold.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability: Medium-High as a complementary rail, Low as a sole treasury rail. ACPR-licensed French payment institutions and electronic-money institutions, including Qonto, Pōs, Treezor (Groupe BPCE) and Vialink, offer remote onboarding, SEPA-instant credit transfers and SWIFT connectivity, and are a legal option for a French SAS. However, standard acceptable-use policies of most EMIs exclude high-risk nutraceutical merchant activation, and EMIs are not a substitute for a high-risk merchant acquirer for card clearing. A non-EEA offshore digital EMI is not compliant as a primary treasury rail because it cannot hold funds in France, cannot provide a French IBAN under the CMF rules applicable to payment services, and creates AML/UBO transparency and substance-evasion risks. Any EMI used must be an EEA-licensed institution passported into France under the CMF pass-porting regime, with a registered branch or appointed agent, and its balances must be consolidated and disclosed in the SAS statutory accounts.
