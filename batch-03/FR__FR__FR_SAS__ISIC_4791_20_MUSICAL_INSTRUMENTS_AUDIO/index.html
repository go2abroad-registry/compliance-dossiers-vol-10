Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum share capital for an SAS (may be set at €1); at least 50% of the subscribed capital must be paid up in cash and deposited and blocked (compte bloqué) with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until registration; the balance must be released within five years. A blocked-account deposit certificate must be produced with the filing.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety compliance under Articles R.4227-1 et seq. of the Code du travail: annual verification of extinguishers, unobstructed emergency exits, fire drills, and evacuation planning for premises with employees. Structural load inspection of storage racking in accordance with NF E25-038 / Eurocode standards and Articles R.4321-1 et seq. If a public-access showroom is operated, an opening authorization from the commission de sécurité (ERP type M) is mandatory; a non-public warehouse requires only a work-premises declaration (Déclaration d'ouverture d'un établissement) to the Direction régionale de l'économie, de l'emploi, du travail et des solidarités (DREETS).

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum share capital for an SAS (may be set at €1); at least 50% of the subscribed capital must be paid up in cash and deposited and blocked (compte bloqué) with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until registration; the balance must be released within five years. A blocked-account deposit certificate must be produced with the filing.

Category: No sectoral pre-approval operating license is required for ISIC 4791 online retail operations. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — portal: impots.gouv.fr; company registration and INSEE: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered upon incorporation via the Guichet Unique, obtaining a SIREN/SIRET from INSEE; the same identifier is used for VAT with a 'FR' prefix key issued by the DGFiP (Service des Impôts des Entreprises). Foreign-owned SAS entities must file the statutory incorporation documents, identify the chairman and legal representative, and select the appropriate tax regime. Non-French entities with no permanent establishment making taxable supplies in France must register as non-established for VAT with the DGFiP, or may elect to use the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for distance sales of goods and cross-border services. All VAT registration requests are processed electronically through the DGFiP/impots.gouv.fr portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. An SAS is mandatorily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the standard 25% rate (15% on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for eligible small enterprises). Available elections include: (i) group tax consolidation (régime d'ensemble / fiscal integration) under CGI Article 223 A with a minimum 95% ownership chain; (ii) VAT grouping (assujetti unique) for related French entities; (iii) the micro-BIC/light reporting regime for very small SAS with turnover below statutory thresholds; and (iv) the JEI (jeune entreprise innovante) corporate income tax exemption election for qualifying innovative enterprises. Hybrid mismatch elections are not permitted; French anti-hybrid rules under CGI Article 212 bis apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic VAT registration threshold for resident taxable persons; any taxable supply in France triggers immediate registration. An optional franchise en base exemption is available for annual turnover not exceeding EUR 85,000 for goods or EUR 37,500 for services. For distance sales from other EU member states, French VAT registration is required once EU-wide distance sales exceed EUR 10,000, unless using OSS. Non-established non-EU sellers are registrable for French VAT from the first supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exemption with right to deduction) applies to definitive exports of goods to destinations outside the European Union where the goods physically leave the EU customs territory, and to intra-Community supplies to EU VAT-registered customers when the customer's valid VAT number is held and a recapitulative statement (DEB) is filed. For online sales of musical instruments and audio gear to non-EU consumers, the seller must ensure the goods are dispatched from France to an address outside the EU with full transport documentation. Pure offshore services are generally outside French VAT under the place-of-supply rules; a service supplied to a business customer outside France is taxable in that customer's country and not zero-rated in France.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane EX1 / départ) with attached commercial invoice
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or international air waybill showing physical movement outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment/foreign exchange documentation evidencing settlement of export proceeds

Mandatory e-invoicing for B2B supplies is phased in from 1 September 2026 (large and mid-size taxpayers) and 1 September 2027 (all taxable persons). Invoices must be transmitted through the public portal (PPF) or a certified private platform (PDP) using structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII); B2C e-reporting of transaction and payment data to the DGFiP is likewise required. Clearance is not real-time/continuous, but all e-invoices must be auditable via the PPF/PDP before deduction by the buyer; non-compliant invoices cannot support input VAT recovery.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to high audit scrutiny by the DGFiP. If documentation is missing or false, the tax authority may reassess output VAT on the invoice amount for the statutory limitation period (generally up to 3 years, extended to 6 years for concealed operations), plus late-payment interest (0.2% per month), a 40% penalty for substantial under-declaration or bad-faith increases of 80% under CGI Articles 1728-1729, and potential criminal tax-fraud consequences (up to EUR 30,000 fine and one year imprisonment). Refunds of input VAT previously granted may be clawed back retroactively.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full-convertibility jurisdiction within the euro-area capital and payments union: the euro is freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions, and France maintains no exchange-control restrictions on trade settlement, dividends, interest, royalties, or repatriated share capital. Applicable restraints are limited to (i) EU and national reporting duties, including balance-of-payments statistical returns to the Banque de France, customs declarations under the Union Customs Code and Intrastat/EXTRASTAT reporting, and CRS/FATCA disclosures for non-resident-related account holders; (ii) the foreign-direct-investment screening regime under Articles L151-3 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code, which does not apply to a non-sensitive online retailer of musical instruments and audio gear; and (iii) EU and French restrictive measures and financial sanctions. Profit remittance assessment: dividends paid by an SAS to a qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholder benefit from the Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption (0% French withholding tax), while withholding tax at 25% or a lower applicable tax-treaty rate applies to non-qualifying or non-treaty beneficiaries. No quantitative conversion limits, no government approval files, and no repatriation licences are imposed on a retail-export activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory-conversion requirement exists in France or the wider euro area: export receipts in euro or foreign currency may be retained freely in local or foreign-currency accounts, converted at commercial bank rates, and used offshore without any mandatory conversion ratio or prescribed repatriation timeline. The remaining compliance obligations are documentary and statistical: export invoicing must be documented through the French customs single window, intra-EU dispatches must be reported under the Intrastat/Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens obligations, non-EU exports must be declared in the customs export system, and the entity's cross-border payments are captured by the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting framework. The SAS should retain invoices, shipping documents, and PSP settlement records for at least ten years to preempt any bank or tax de-risking review.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (the national central bank of France), operating within the Eurosystem and under the monetary policy framework of the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange and capital-movement monitoring is administered by the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministry of the Economy and Finance, while prudential licensing and AML/CFT supervision of banks and payment institutions is performed by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), and Tracfin acts as the financial intelligence unit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High viability for EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions and digital payment institutions (such as Wise, Revolut Business, Qonto, Shine, and Airwallex) that hold an authorisation from the ACPR or from another EEA supervisor and passport their payment services into France under the Revised Payment Services Directive (PSD2). Low-to-Medium viability for a fully offshore EMI domiciled outside the EEA (e.g., a post-Brexit United Kingdom hub or a non-EEA jurisdiction) because such institution may serve the French SAS only through an ACPR-licensed French branch or a French-licensed agent, cannot rely on EU passporting, and will be subject to additional segregated-funds, sanctions, and AML/CFT supervision constraints. Legal constraints: an EMI account is not a bank deposit protected by the French deposit guarantee fund (FGDR); the SAS retains full AML/reporting responsibility through the payment institution; and certain tier-1 banks de-risk clients whose dominant cash-flow streams originate from multiple cross-border PSPs.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax rate. An eligible small or medium-sized company may qualify for a 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to the statutory conditions, including paid-up capital held at least 75% by individuals or qualifying companies and annual turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid by a French SAS to a non-resident are generally subject to 25% French withholding tax for a non-resident corporate beneficial owner, before any exemption or treaty reduction. Rates and procedures differ for individual recipients. A qualifying EU parent may obtain exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive and French implementing rules, subject to ownership, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, and other conditions. Treaty rates depend on the recipient's residence and the applicable treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

File within three months after the end of the fiscal year. For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the statutory deadline is the second working day after 1 May; an administrative electronic-filing extension is commonly granted but should not be treated as a change to the statutory deadline. The corporate tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a 31 December year-end, it is generally due by 15 May. Installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million: French local- and master-file documentation obligations generally apply to a French entity or French tax group meeting the statutory turnover or gross-asset threshold, or belonging to a group meeting the corresponding threshold. The threshold rules include specified ownership and control conditions. A separate annual summary declaration (form 2257-SD) applies to entities within its statutory scope. Certain cross-border related-party transactions remain subject to arm's-length rules and disclosure requirements even where the full-file threshold is not met.

Economic substance scope: France does not generally apply a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime. French tax authorities may nevertheless challenge artificial arrangements under domestic anti-abuse rules, the abuse-of-law doctrine, general anti-abuse rules, transfer-pricing provisions, controlled foreign company rules, permanent-establishment principles, and treaty principal-purpose or limitation provisions where applicable. The SAS should maintain evidence of real functions, personnel, decision-making, assets, risks, commercial purpose, and actual performance of intra-group services.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion, deliberate concealment, or fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, and surcharges that may reach 80% in specified cases. Serious tax fraud may be prosecuted under the French Tax Code and Penal Code; penalties can include substantial criminal fines and imprisonment, with enhanced consequences in aggravating circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors or officers who personally participate in or knowingly facilitate fraud may incur criminal liability. A court may impose imprisonment, fines, professional disqualification, and, where legally justified, personal or joint payment liability. Civil veil-piercing is not automatic.
Transfer-pricing misstatement or inadequate documentation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, interest, and applicable surcharges. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation can attract a penalty generally calculated at 0.5% of the relevant transactions, subject to a statutory minimum generally of EUR 10,000 per audited year; other penalties may apply to incomplete or inaccurate documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil, tax, or criminal exposure where they knowingly authorize fraud or a breach of statutory duties. Liability is fact-specific and does not arise merely from an adjustment.
Withholding-tax evasion or treaty-abuse arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax, late-payment interest, applicable surcharges, and possible penalties for inaccurate or missing declarations. Criminal consequences may arise where the conduct constitutes fraud or document falsification.

Director personal liability: The SAS and responsible officers may be exposed under applicable tax and criminal rules if they knowingly facilitate evasion. Director liability is not automatic and depends on statutory grounds and evidence.
Corporate-law distributions and unlawful return of capital

Statutory sanction: The distribution may be recoverable, and directors may incur civil liability. In insolvency, management sanctions and personal liability may apply where statutory conditions, including misconduct and causal contribution to insufficiency of assets, are established.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to repay sums or compensate the company or creditors where the applicable legal tests are met. Imprisonment or travel restrictions are not automatic consequences of an unlawful distribution.
Money laundering, terrorist financing, or sanctions breach

Statutory sanction: Potential asset freezes, transaction prohibitions, administrative or criminal fines, confiscation, and imprisonment under the applicable French, EU, and international rules. The precise sanction depends on the offense, actor, and facts.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation or legally culpable failure to comply. Civil veil-piercing is not automatic; travel restrictions require a legal basis and competent-authority action.
Shell-company, artificial-presence, or permanent-establishment misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Possible French tax reassessment, withholding-tax exposure, interest, surcharges, and penalties under domestic law and applicable treaties. The tax result depends on actual functions, assets, risks, and treaty facts.

Director personal liability: Personal exposure may arise for intentional fraud, breach of directors' duties, or statutory liability in insolvency. A foreign shareholder's ownership alone does not establish director liability or pierce the corporate veil.
Late filing, payment, or failure to maintain accounting records

Statutory sanction: Late filing surcharges, late-payment interest, applicable payment penalties, estimated assessment, and record-keeping or audit-related sanctions under the relevant provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable where statutory conditions for personal tax liability, misconduct, or criminal responsibility are met. Administrative penalties are ordinarily assessed against the company.
