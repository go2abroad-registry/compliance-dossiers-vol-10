Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registry of the Commercial Court) acting as registrar of the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); filings are transmitted electronically through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises (guichet-entreprises.fr) operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

Code de commerce (French Commercial Code), Book II, Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (general company provisions), L.224-1 (share capital payment rules), L.225-2 (cash contribution escrow) as adapted, and L.227-1 to L.227-27 (Société par Actions Simplifiée regime covering incorporation, governance, and President's powers); implementing rules at R.123-30 et seq. (RCS filing); Decree No. 2021-300 of 18 March 2021 (electronic one-stop shop for business formalities).

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'une personne morale (M0 form) submitted through the electronic single window, accompanied by the statuts constitutifs (articles of association) and the exhibits enumerated in the filing dossier.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 10 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; the articles of association freely determine the capital amount. Cash subscriptions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) held at a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the bank delivers a certificat du dépositaire des fonds which must accompany the RCS filing, and the funds are released only upon presentation of the issued Kbis. The articles may provide for staggered payment of cash subscriptions within five years, provided the full capital is subscribed at incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: If offices or warehouses are operated, the premises must obtain fire safety conformity: for ERP (établissement recevant du public) category 5 premises, clearance is delivered by the local safety commission or SDIS; for ICPE warehouses, the DREAL declaration and the associated fire-resistance and means-of-escape requirements govern. On the occupational side, the employer must prepare the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP), affiliate to the statutory occupational health service (Service de Prévention et de Santé au Travail) by the first hire, and maintain first-aid, evacuation, and safety registers in accordance with the French Labour Code (Articles L.4121-1 et seq.).

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; the articles of association freely determine the capital amount. Cash subscriptions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) held at a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the bank delivers a certificat du dépositaire des fonds which must accompany the RCS filing, and the funds are released only upon presentation of the issued Kbis. The articles may provide for staggered payment of cash subscriptions within five years, provided the full capital is subscribed at incorporation.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

DREAL (Direction Régionale de l'Environnement, de l'Aménagement et du Logement) — residual authority for installations classées pour la protection de l'environnement (ICPE); no specific industrial licensing board is required for a pure wholesale trading mandate in non-ferrous metals.

Category: No separate industrial or operational permit is prescribed for the wholesale distribution of Grade A refined copper cathodes. The ordinary trading authorization is the RCS registration as evidenced by the Kbis extract (Code de commerce, Article L.123-36). The 'revendeur de métaux' declaration under Article L.541-1 of the Code de commerce does not apply because the activity concerns primary refined metal, not scrap or second-hand metal.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances; tax portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; company registry: Guichet Unique (INPI) and Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); customs authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity forming a French SAS: (1) file incorporation formalities through the Guichet Unique (INPI) with articles, beneficial owner declarations, and registered office proof; (2) INSEE issues SIREN and SIRET; (3) DGFiP automatically assigns a French VAT ID if taxable operations are declared, otherwise the entity files form M0 with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). For non-established foreign entities making taxable supplies in France: register directly with the SIE using form M0 and supporting documents (certificate of incorporation, articles, proof of EU establishment, bank details, and VAT registration in home state); non-EU entities must appoint a French fiscal representative unless an exemption applies. EU distance sellers and certain B2C suppliers may use the One-Stop Shop (OSS/IOSS). Customs EORI number is obtained from DGDDI for import/export operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the standard 25% rate (2025). No default pass-through. Territorial scope: French companies are taxable on worldwide profits, with treaty and foreign-branch relief. Not a tax-exempt zone. Eligible for parent-subsidiary regime and tax consolidation.

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box system. Available elections include: (i) tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Art. 223 A CGI for groups with 95% ownership; (ii) VAT group (assujettissement unique) under Art. 256 C CGI; (iii) limited option for partnership tax transparency for SAS under Art. 239 bis AB CGI subject to statutory conditions; (iv) simplified VAT regime where turnover thresholds are met. Anti-hybrid rules under Art. 205 I bis CGI apply; no domestic hybrid mismatch election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold applies to non-established taxable persons making taxable supplies in France. Domestic franchise en base thresholds: €85,000 for goods and €37,500 for services (2024/2025). Intra-EU acquisitions threshold: €10,000. Wholesale trade in refined copper cathodes generally exceeds all thresholds and is fully VATable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the EU are VAT-exempt at 0% under Art. 262-I CGI, provided the goods actually leave the EU and the exporter holds customs proof. Intra-EU supplies to VAT-registered customers are 0% under Art. 262-ter CGI with a valid customer VAT ID and VIES verification. Offshore services may be outside the scope of French VAT or zero-rated under place-of-supply rules. For Grade A refined copper cathodes, zero-rating requires a commercial invoice, transport document, and customs exit certification.

France is phasing in mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) and accredited Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs). Receiving obligation for all VAT-registered businesses from September 2026; issuing obligation for large and mid-sized companies from September 2026, and for SMEs/micro-enterprises from September 2027. Public-sector e-invoicing currently uses Chorus Pro. No general real-time fiscal clearance yet; structured formats, qualified electronic signatures/certificates, and lifecycle reporting are required. Non-compliance penalties include €15 per invoice (annual cap €15,000) and €250 per transmission failure.

Failure to substantiate export zero-rating results in VAT reassessment at 20%, late interest of 0.20% per month (2.4% per year), and penalties of 40% for lack of evidence or 80% for fraudulent intent under Art. 1729 CGI. Criminal penalties for tax evasion under Art. 1741 CGI: up to 5 years imprisonment and a €500,000 fine; aggravated organized fraud: up to 7 years and €2,000,000. Customs penalties apply under the Code des douanes. Retrospective audit period is generally 3 years, extended to 10 years for concealed activity or offshore arrangements. France has no general statutory FX repatriation deadline; 180 days is a common contractual or documentary credit term.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a member of the Eurozone; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls on capital movements, profit remittances, or dividend distributions, subject to compliance with EU anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-terrorist financing (CTF) regulations. However, all cross-border transfers exceeding €10,000 must be reported to the French customs and tax authorities for statistical and monitoring purposes. Profit remittance is unrestricted, but must be supported by audited financial statements and tax clearance certificates to avoid withholding tax issues.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds in France. Exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts (comptes en devises) and are free to use them for trade settlements or convert to euros at market rates. However, all export receipts must be declared to the Banque de France via the balance-of-payments reporting system (BOP) within 30 days of the transaction. The reporting is electronic and mandatory for all transactions exceeding EUR 12,500. No minimum conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in coordination with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) and the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) for banking supervision. The Banque de France is the central bank and the primary authority for monetary policy and financial stability, while the Treasury oversees foreign exchange regulations and balance-of-payments reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Fintech EMIs (Electronic Money Institutions) licensed under the EU PSD2 directive, such as Qonto, Shine, or Revolut Business, can provide multi-currency accounts and cross-border payment services. However, for high-value physical commodity trading (copper cathodes) involving documentary letters of credit (LC) and LME warrant transfers, traditional banks are preferred due to their established trade finance infrastructure, correspondent banking networks, and ability to handle complex documentary compliance. EMIs may face limitations in issuing or confirming LCs and may have lower transaction limits. Legal constraints include the need for the EMI to hold a valid ACPR license and to comply with French AML/KYC regulations, which are stringent for high-risk sectors like metals trading.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

High transaction volume and rapid turnover in a newly established entity without prior trading history

Root cause: High transaction volume and rapid turnover in a newly established entity without prior trading history

Defense evidence: Provide a comprehensive business plan, audited financial projections, contracts with suppliers and buyers, and evidence of physical commodity storage or logistics arrangements. Include a detailed explanation of the supply chain, source of funds, and expected transaction patterns. Submit corporate registry extracts (Kbis), shareholder structure, and beneficial ownership declarations.
Cross-border payments to/from high-risk jurisdictions (e.g., tax havens or sanctioned countries) for copper procurement

Root cause: Cross-border payments to/from high-risk jurisdictions (e.g., tax havens or sanctioned countries) for copper procurement

Defense evidence: Maintain a complete trade documentation trail: purchase orders, invoices, bills of lading, warehouse receipts (LME warrants), and inspection certificates. Provide a compliance certificate confirming the origin of goods and the absence of sanctions exposure. Implement a robust sanctions screening process and provide evidence of due diligence on all counterparties.
Use of complex corporate structures or multiple intermediary entities in the transaction chain

Root cause: Use of complex corporate structures or multiple intermediary entities in the transaction chain

Defense evidence: Disclose the full corporate structure, including all intermediate holding companies, and provide a rationale for the structure. Submit organizational charts, shareholder agreements, and proof of economic substance for each entity. Ensure that all entities have valid tax registrations and are not shell companies.
Unusual payment patterns, such as early settlement or prepayment for goods without corresponding delivery

Root cause: Unusual payment patterns, such as early settlement or prepayment for goods without corresponding delivery

Defense evidence: Provide a clear explanation of the trade terms (e.g., Incoterms) and payment milestones. Submit the underlying contract and any amendments. Include evidence of the physical movement of goods, such as transport documents and insurance certificates. Maintain a transaction log and reconcile payments with shipments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections (e.g., shareholder loans or equity contributions) must be routed via SWIFT wire transfers using the appropriate purpose codes. For equity contributions, use the BOP code '110' (Direct investment) or '120' (Capital in cash). For shareholder loans, use '130' (Loans and credits). The transfer must be accompanied by a declaration to the Banque de France via the BOP reporting system within 30 days. The receiving bank will require a copy of the corporate resolution authorizing the injection, the shareholder agreement, and proof of the source of funds. For amounts exceeding EUR 10,000, the bank will conduct enhanced due diligence and may request a certificate of tax residence for the sending entity.Outbound trade disbursements for copper cathode purchases are typically executed via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for direct payments, or via documentary letters of credit (LC) for larger, more secure transactions. For LCs, the bank will issue an irrevocable LC (MT700) in favor of the seller, with the buyer's bank confirming the LC. The clearing rails involve the correspondent banking network, with settlement in EUR or USD (commonly USD for LME trades). For LME warrant transfers, the payment is often made via MT103 with the warrant transfer instruction separately. All outbound payments must be reported to the Banque de France under the BOP system. The bank will require the underlying trade documents (invoice, bill of lading, inspection certificate) to process the payment and ensure compliance with AML/CTF regulations.Forward contracts (FX forwards) for EUR/USD and other major currencies, with tenors up to 12 months, available from French banks and international banks with a French presence.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with limited convertibility, though not typically needed for EUR/USD; may be used for exotic currencies if the copper supplier is in a restricted market.Currency swaps (FX swaps) for managing short-term liquidity and rolling over positions, subject to ISDA agreements and collateral requirements.Options (FX options) for hedging price volatility, though these are less common for physical commodity traders and may require additional credit lines.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax on taxable profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 25% on dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to domestic exemptions, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, applicable tax treaties, and anti-abuse requirements. A qualifying EU or EEA parent may obtain exemption where statutory ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A French entity is generally subject to full transfer-pricing documentation requirements where its turnover or gross assets are at least EUR 400 million, or where it belongs to a group meeting the statutory conditions. A transfer-pricing declaration is generally required for entities meeting the EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold or other statutory connected-party criteria. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a standalone ESR regime equivalent to the United Arab Emirates or certain offshore jurisdictions. Nevertheless, French anti-abuse rules, the arm's-length principle, beneficial-ownership standards, controlled foreign company rules, interest-limitation rules, anti-hybrid rules, principal-purpose testing, general anti-abuse provisions, and EU ATAD measures require genuine commercial rationale and adequate substance. A French copper-trading SAS should maintain French decision-making, transaction-control functions, qualified personnel or outsourced operational capacity, inventory and logistics evidence, treasury controls, customer and supplier due diligence, and records demonstrating that its margin reflects functions, assets, and risks actually assumed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, repayment of tax, late-payment interest, and penalties generally up to 80% in aggravated cases. Criminal tax fraud may result in imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with higher sanctions for aggravated or organized conduct, including imprisonment of up to seven years and fines of up to EUR 3 million in qualifying cases.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal prosecution, civil tax-liability proceedings for fraudulent or repeated non-payment, management bans, asset seizure, and restitution. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be imposed through judicial measures in appropriate proceedings. The corporate veil is not routinely pierced; personal exposure generally requires personal participation, serious management fault, fraud, or statutory director-liability grounds.
VAT carousel fraud and missing-trader arrangements

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, denial of input-tax recovery, interest, penalties up to 80% for fraudulent conduct, joint and several recovery in qualifying circumstances, and criminal sanctions for organized fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may incur criminal liability, personal tax-liability orders for serious management misconduct, asset freezes, professional disqualification, and imprisonment where they knowingly participated in the scheme.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profits, denial of deductions, withholding-tax exposure, documentation penalties, late-payment interest, and penalties generally ranging from 10% to 80% depending on the taxpayer's conduct and the degree of concealment.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise for deliberate participation, fraudulent bookkeeping, or serious management misconduct. Directors should approve and retain contemporaneous benchmarking, intercompany agreements, functional analyses, and transaction-level evidence.
Shell company and beneficial-ownership concealment

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment under anti-abuse principles, beneficial-owner and corporate-register sanctions, account freezing, contract termination, AML reporting, and potential criminal penalties for fraud, money laundering, or false corporate declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors, de facto managers, and beneficial owners may face criminal prosecution, asset confiscation, management bans, civil restitution, and imprisonment. Travel bans may be ordered by a court where legally justified.
Anti-money laundering, terrorist financing, and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Suspicious transaction reporting, account restrictions, regulatory enforcement, administrative fines, confiscation, and criminal sanctions. Sanctions and export-control breaches may also cause contractual termination, banking de-risking, and loss of market access.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may incur criminal and regulatory liability for knowing participation or serious control failures, including fines, imprisonment where applicable, professional bans, asset seizure, and judicial travel restrictions.
False accounting and commercial-document fraud

Statutory sanction: Accounting corrections, tax reassessment, civil damages, commercial penalties, and criminal prosecution for forgery, use of forged documents, fraud, or fraudulent accounting.

Director personal liability: The president, managing officers, beneficial owners, accountants, and other participants may face individual criminal liability, fines, imprisonment, asset confiscation, management bans, and civil restitution.
Customs and import-control violations

Statutory sanction: Recovery of duties and import VAT, customs fines, seizure or forfeiture of goods, customs investigation, and criminal prosecution in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Company officers and persons directing the transaction may face personal customs liability, criminal fines, imprisonment in serious cases, asset seizure, and restrictions on managing companies.
Illegal dividend distribution and unlawful profit extraction

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful dividends, director liability, corporate-law penalties, tax reassessment, withholding-tax recovery, interest, and penalties. Insolvency-related claims may include clawback and mismanagement actions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore misappropriated amounts, compensate creditors, bear insolvency liabilities for serious management faults, and face criminal sanctions for abuse of corporate assets or fraudulent bankruptcy.
Corporate insolvency and creditor prejudice

Statutory sanction: Clawback of transactions, insolvency proceedings, personal contribution to liabilities, director-management bans, and criminal bankruptcy sanctions.

Director personal liability: The president and de facto managers may face civil contribution claims, asset seizure, management prohibitions, criminal fines, and imprisonment for fraudulent bankruptcy or asset dissipation.
