A virtual or domiciliation office is generally permissible for a French SAS if supplied by an authorized domiciliation provider or otherwise supported by a legally valid premises arrangement. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full convertibility of the euro for both current account and capital account transactions, pursuant to the EU Treaty free movement of capital (Articles 63-66 TFEU), the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier), and OECD liberalization codes. All exchange controls have been eliminated since 1990. Profits, dividends, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted freely to non-resident shareholders without prior central bank authorization, provided the company files the mandatory Balance of Payments declarations for each flow exceeding EUR 12,500 and that the remittance is supported by audited/approved financial statements evidencing distributable reserves. No concession or surrender requirement applies. The only potential restriction arises under the French foreign investment review (régime des investissements étrangers en France, Articles R151-1 et seq. CMF), which does not apply to electronic catalog B2B distribution of industrial maintenance supplies; the activity is classified as non-restricted and fully liberalized. Banks apply standard diligence to the beneficial owner, and tax residency documentation of the shareholder (e.g., tax certificate) is required for treaty benefits on dividend withholding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in France. A French SAS is free to hold export receivables in foreign currency, invoice in any currency, contractually set settlement terms, and decide the moment of conversion into euros; however, the proceeds must transit through an account held with an authorized credit institution established in the EEA. For statistical purposes, any export receipt exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported monthly to the Banque de France via the BOP/DP2 declaration mechanism (Form BOP/1 or digital filing), and the company must document the underlying commercial invoice and delivery evidence to support the transaction's economic reality.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting within the Eurosystem under the supervision of the European Central Bank (ECB), with prudential control delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) and foreign exchange reporting administered by the Banque de France's Balance of Payments Directorate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for an EEA/EU-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) passporting into France under the ACPR; Low for an offshore EMI (UK post-Brexit, US, or Singapore entity). Legal constraints: (i) an offshore EMI cannot provide a French or SEPA IBAN, issue French IBANs, or hold client funds for a French SAS without an ACPR establishment license or an EMU passport, exposing the company to unregulated deposit risk and tax/reporting friction; (ii) only an EMI licensed in the EEA (e.g., French agrément holders or EU passporting EMIs such as Qonto, Shine, or similar entities authorised by ACPR or an equivalent EU supervisor) can lawfully offer payment accounts in euros; (iii) EU Regulation (EU) No 260/2012 prohibits IBAN discrimination for SEPA payments, so the SAS may accept payments via a non-French EU IBAN, but the Banque de France reporting duty and tax office correspondence require an accessible French-address communication channel; (iv) the registered capital deposit cannot be held by an EMI — only a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts is legally acceptable for the capital escrow. Recommendation: use an EU EMI for operational receivables and payouts, while maintaining the principal treasury in a Tier-1 brick-and-mortar bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) The non-French shareholder executes a SWIFT MT103 wire to the blocked capital escrow account titled in the name of the 'SAS en formation' at a French bank, with Field 70 Remittance Information stating 'Apport de capital social' and a reference to the future RCS number; (2) the bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts issues the capital deposit certificate (attestation de dépôt des fonds) for the full amount of the share capital; (3) after registration at the RCS, the funds are transferred from the escrow to the company's operational current account; (4) the inflow must be disclosed in the accounting books under equity (capital social) and the corresponding cross-border movement reported to the Banque de France via the BOP reporting module if the amount exceeds EUR 12,500; (5) if the injection is structured as shareholder loan (apport en compte courant d'associé), the MT103 Field 70 must state 'Prêt d'associé' and a formal loan agreement must be retained for the tax authority and the bank; (6) neither prior foreign exchange authorization nor foreign investment clearance is required for the MRO distribution sector, but the legal representative must verify that the direct investment notification exemption under Article R151-1 CMF applies and, if any doubt, file a pre-clearance request with the Ministry of Economy (Direction générale du Trésor) before the inflow; (7) all cross-border instructions must include the company's SIREN number and bank reference, and the bank will conduct a source-of-funds verification requiring the contribution statement signed by the Chairman.Primary clearing rails for a French B2B MRO distributor: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) in EUR for eurozone suppliers and intra-EU settlement, with mandatory IBAN, BIC, and structured creditor reference, cleared through T2 (TARGET2) same-day (SCT Inst for instant, up to EUR 100,000); (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-euro payments to global suppliers (USD, GBP, CNY), executed via the bank's foreign exchange desk with purpose code (Field 71A) and remittance details; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT707) for large or recurring procurement with documentary requirements — typically irrevocable usance LC issued by the French bank for the benefit of wholesale suppliers, covering commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or AWB, and insurance certificate; (4) Documentary Collections (D/P, D/A) as a cost-efficient alternative for medium-value purchases with established counterparties; (5) corporate purchasing cards (two-party or three-party B2B card programs) for low-value online MRO purchases, mapped to the e-commerce payment rails; (6) receivables clearing via acquiring banks and PSPs (Visa, Mastercard) through an ACPR-licensed acquiring institution, settled via SEPA CT daily sweeps into the operational treasury account; each disbursement must be supported by matching purchase order, goods receipt, and supplier invoice to satisfy French tax documentation and bank EDD, and cross-border flows above EUR 12,500 must be captured in the monthly BOP declaration.Deliverable FX Forwards (Achats/Ventes à Terme de Devises) with ACPR/ECB-supervised credit institutions covering EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CNY commercial exposures, documented under an ISDA/FBF Master Agreement with a maximum tenor of 12 months and strictly aligned with underlying trade invoices.FX Swaps (Swaps de Change) for liquidity smoothing on simultaneous EUR and foreign-currency cash flow timelines, subject to the same trade documentation rule.Plain Vanilla Currency Options (Options de Change) — both calls and puts — to protect against adverse EUR/USD and EUR/CNY moves on inventory purchases, permitted for commercial hedging only, not for speculative open positions.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) restricted to exotic/non-fully-convertible emerging-market currencies (if any future supplier route requires such exposure), booked through a Paris-based bank counterparty under an emerging-market NDF Annex, with settlement in EUR and full underlying documentary support.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) for long-term balance sheet hedging of intercompany loans from foreign shareholders, matched to the maturity and coupon structure of the debt, permissible without prior authorization and reported to the Banque de France for BOP purposes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €50 million consolidated group revenue

Economic substance scope: France implements EU DAC6 mandatory disclosure for cross-border arrangements; economic substance requirements under anti-avoidance rules for holding companies and intra-group financing

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties: fines up to 500% of evaded tax, imprisonment up to 5 years, confiscation of assets

Director personal liability: Directors face joint and several personal liability; possible travel bans and disqualification from management
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Dissolution of company, back-payment of taxes with interest, fines up to €1 million

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil; personal restitution orders and potential criminal liability
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 500% of tax adjustment plus interest; possible criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Personal fines and imprisonment for fraudulent or grossly negligent TP practices
AML / Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1 million or 5% of annual turnover; criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability, imprisonment and disqualification from corporate roles
