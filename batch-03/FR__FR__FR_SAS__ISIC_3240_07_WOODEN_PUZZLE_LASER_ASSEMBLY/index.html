A purely virtual office is not permissible for an SAS engaged in physical assembly and production of laser-cut wooden puzzles. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS. Cash subscriptions must be released in at least 50% at subscription, with the balance paid within five years; the released funds must be deposited in a blocked escrow account with a bank, Caisse des Dépôts, or notary until RCS registration.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain an SDIS fire-safety inspection and certificate of fire-safety compliance, including smoke detection, extinguishers, evacuation signage and a laser-equipment interlock; obtain a structural load inspection and electrical conformity certificate confirming the floor and power supply are suitable for the laser cutter; prepare and maintain a Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP), appoint a laser-safety officer, use Class 1 enclosures or equivalent guarding, and complete the DREETS workplace registration.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty. Official taxpayer portal: www.impots.gouv.fr. Company formation, SIREN/SIRET allocation and TIN delivery are coordinated via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

The French SAS is incorporated electronically through the Guichet Unique, which transmits the dossier to the commercial court registry (Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce), INSEE for SIREN (9 digits) and SIRET (14 digits), and DGFiP for tax registration. Once INSEE validates the registration, DGFiP issues the French VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire: FR + 11 digits) in accordance with CGI Article 286-ter, without any separate application for established French entities. A non-EU foreign entity carrying out taxable supplies in France (e.g., selling goods held in a French FBA warehouse) must file the non-resident VAT registration form (Cerfa n° 14945*06) with the DGFiP non-resident business tax office; when such entity has no establishment in the EU, a DGFiP-accredited fiscal representative (responsable solidaire) must be appointed and is jointly liable for the VAT under CGI Article 289-A, unless the OSS/IOSS scheme is electable for distance sales. Foreign entities acquiring French real estate or participating in French supply chains must also secure a separate French TIN where required for withholding or property taxes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. The SAS is unconditionally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS, CGI Art. 206(1)); there is no pass-through eligibility for shareholders. Ordinary corporate profits are taxed at the standard 25% corporate income tax rate from fiscal year 2024 onward. The French territorial scope taxes all profits arising from establishments and activities carried on in France, while profits derived from foreign permanent establishments are exempt provided they are subject to adequate taxation in the foreign State. — The French SAS is incorporated electronically through the Guichet Unique, which transmits the dossier to the commercial court registry (Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce), INSEE for SIREN (9 digits) and SIRET (14 digits), and DGFiP for tax registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The SAS is unconditionally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS, CGI Art. 206(1)); there is no pass-through eligibility for shareholders. Ordinary corporate profits are taxed at the standard 25% corporate income tax rate from fiscal year 2024 onward. The French territorial scope taxes all profits arising from establishments and activities carried on in France, while profits derived from foreign permanent establishments are exempt provided they are subject to adequate taxation in the foreign State.

Available election forms

France has no US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections for the SAS include: (i) election to form a fiscal integration group where a French-resident head company holds at least 95% of the SAS (CGI Art. 223-A), allowing a single group-wide IS computation; (ii) election to form a VAT single taxable person ('assujetti unique') group under CGI Art. 256-C; (iii) election to use the simplified real tax regime (régime réel simplifié) for small and medium businesses; (iv) option to make the SAS taxable as a group member under the parent-subsidiary participation exemption where applicable. Hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD transposition, CGI Art. 212-bis) apply mandatorily and cannot be waived by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Goods supplies within France: basic VAT exemption threshold (franchise en base) of €85,000 and upper threshold of €93,500 for 2025 (CGI Art. 293-B). Cross-border intra-Community distance sales to consumers of goods: €10,000 EU-wide annual threshold unless the OSS election is made. Sellers holding goods in France (e.g., Amazon FBA stock) are required to register for French VAT from the first Euro of French taxable turnover, as no turnover threshold applies to domestic stock-based sales. Exporters commonly register voluntarily to secure zero-rating on export transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU are eligible for the 0% VAT rate (CGI Art. 262-I-1°) where the goods are physically dispatched outside the EU pursuant to a customs export declaration, transport documents, and the export contract. Intra-Community supplies to another EU taxable person are zero-rated under CGI Art. 262-ter provided the customer's EU VAT number is validated and the relevant EC Sales List is filed. Services directly linked to exported goods and services supplied to non-EU recipients without establishment in France may be zero-rated under CGI Arts. 259-A and 262-I-2°. For e-commerce FBA exports, the seller must retain the DAU/EEX customs acceptance and carrier evidence. France does not impose a formal statutory FX repatriation deadline; the system default of 180 days is used administratively as the documentary time window for proving receipt of export proceeds in audits and refund reviews.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/EEX) accepted by French Customs (DGDDI)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and SWIFT/bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • EU Sales List (EC Sales List) and Intrastat filings where applicable for intra-Community dispatches

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing applies in France from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size taxpayers and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro businesses. Invoices must be issued in the structured EN 16931 / Factur-X (PEPPOL) format through an accredited PDP (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire) and transmitted to the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) DGFiP API. B2C transaction data are reportable electronically to the PPF. France does not impose real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; verification is post-event and decentralised, with risk-based audits of e-invoice flows.

Where 0% export VAT is claimed without sufficient documentary proof of physical export, DGFiP recharacterises the transaction as a taxable domestic supply and assesses 20% French VAT together with late-payment interest at 0.20% per month. Penalties escalate to 40% of the assessed tax for deliberate misconduct (manquement délibéré) under CGI Art. 1729 and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres; false or fictitious export invoices expose the taxpayer to the 50% specific penalty under Art. 1737. The ordinary assessment limitation is three years, extended to six years for concealed activity or tax fraud. Criminal prosecutions under CGI Art. 1741 may result in imprisonment up to five years and fines up to €500,000, plus mandatory clawback of previously refunded export VAT and suspension of advance-refund procedures until the audit is closed.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under full capital and current account convertibility as an EU member state and IMF Article VIII currency. Euro-denominated transactions face no restrictions. Capital account items including profit repatriation, dividend distributions, and equity contributions are unrestricted subject only to standard corporate and tax compliance. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders encounter no convertibility hurdles beyond routine 12.8% withholding tax and automatic exchange of information reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds applies under French law. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency bank accounts without conversion. Companies must however file statistical declarations with the Banque de France for trade balance purposes and customs declarations for statistical reporting. No fixed timeline exists for surrender; voluntary FX conversion is available through banking channels at prevailing market rates.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

25% for distributions made to non-resident corporate shareholders; 12.8% final withholding at source for non-resident individual beneficiaries, possibly supplemented by social levies where French social-security rules apply.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Master File and Local File documentation is mandatory under Article L13 AA of the French Book of Tax Procedures for entities whose annual turnover or total balance-sheet assets equal or exceed EUR 400 million. Entities below that threshold must nevertheless provide simplified transfer-pricing documentation under Article L13 AB when related-party transactions exceed EUR 200,000 per fiscal year. Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a distinct offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. However, a French SAS is subject to the general anti-abuse doctrine (abus de droit under Article L64 LPF), the OECD BEPS Principal Purpose Test under tax treaties, and permanent-establishment/effective-management jurisprudence. A company with no physical premises, no employees, no local decision-making, and no active business in France risks being recharacterised as a sham or tax-resident elsewhere, thereby losing treaty benefits and facing reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI, tax evasion is punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine; aggravated or organised offences carry up to seven years' imprisonment and a EUR 3,000,000 fine. Administrative penalties of 80% of the omitted tax plus 0.20% monthly interest are also imposed.

Director personal liability: A director who personally participates in the evasion may be criminally convicted, subjected to a ban on managing a business (interdiction de gérer), ordered to pay restitution, and held civilly liable for the corporate tax debts through an action en comblement de passif under Article L651-2 of the French Commercial Code if the company is liquidated.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance

Statutory sanction: The company may be disregarded under the French abus de droit doctrine; the tax authorities can impose an 80% penalty on the reassessed tax, deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test, and shift the tax residence or profit allocation to the actual place of management.

Director personal liability: Directors may be pursued personally for corporate tax liabilities resulting from misuse of the legal entity, face criminal sanctions for complicity in tax evasion, and be subject to travel bans (interdiction de quitter le territoire) and prohibitions on holding managerial office.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may reassess profits using the arm's-length standard and impose the 5% documentation penalty under Article 1732 bis CGI on the amount of the adjustment when required documentation is not provided within 30 days of a request; interest at 0.20% per month accrues. Repeat non-cooperation can lead to an additional fine of up to EUR 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false or artificial intercompany agreements may be jointly liable for the resulting corporate tax assessment and can be criminally prosecuted for concealment where pricing manipulation is intentional.
Withholding Tax and Payment Compliance

Statutory sanction: The company remains liable for the unpaid withholding tax, plus late-payment interest and a penalty of 5% of the unpaid withheld tax under Article 1731 CGI; in case of intentional omission, the 80% bad-faith penalty applies.

Director personal liability: The president of the SAS can be personally liable for failure to remit source taxes if found to have acted intentionally; criminal fines and imprisonment for fraudulent concealment of withheld taxes may be imposed, and travel bans and management bans are available.
