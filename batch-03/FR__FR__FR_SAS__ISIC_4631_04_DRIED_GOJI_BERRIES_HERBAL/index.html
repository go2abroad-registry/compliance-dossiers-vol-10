Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS. The articles must state the share capital. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Contributions in kind must be fully paid when subscribed. Funds may be deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released after registration. The stated USD fee is an indicative conversion of the applicable French registration charge, which is assessed in euros and may change.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain landlord and municipal compliance evidence for the premises, maintain fire detection, extinguishers, emergency lighting, evacuation routes, and fire-risk controls, and obtain approval from the competent fire-safety commission where the premises are an établissement recevant du public or otherwise regulated. Verify structural floor loading and racking safety for stored goods. Complete employer occupational-risk assessment, workplace safety documentation, training, and inspection obligations under the French Labour Code; consult the occupational health service before or upon hiring personnel.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), French Public Finance Directorate, operating under the Ministry of Economy, Finance, and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; official portals: impots.gouv.fr (professional taxpayer space), formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique company-registration portal operated by INPI since 1 January 2023), and douane.gouv.fr (customs single window, DELTA/GUN, EORI management).

E-government filing portal

A French Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) is incorporated and tax-registered in one synchronised filing through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), which routes the dossier to INSEE, DGFiP and URSSAF. DGFiP automatically allocates within 5–10 business days: (i) SIREN (9-digit national identifier), (ii) SIRET (14-digit establishment identifier), and (iii) the intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 11 characters (e.g., FR12345678901). For export operations, the same filing or a direct DGFiP EORI application issues the EORI number consisting of 'FR' + SIREN (e.g., FR12345678900), which is mandatory for submitting the DELTA export customs declaration and for demonstrating zero-rated export entitlement. A non-resident shareholder does not alter the procedure: the SAS must appoint a French président and maintain a registered office in France. Foreign entities not incorporated in France but carrying out French taxable supplies must obtain a French VAT number through the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); non-EU principals are required to appoint a jointly and severally liable fiscal representative under Article 289-A CGI.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: France (metropolitan territory and Corsica) with worldwide taxation of tax-resident SAS profits under Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS); no Pass_Through_Eligibility for SAS or SASU structures; no Tax_Exempt_Zone applies to a metropolitan French trading company. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: France (metropolitan territory and Corsica) with worldwide taxation of tax-resident SAS profits under Impôt sur les Sociétés (IS); no Pass_Through_Eligibility for SAS or SASU structures; no Tax_Exempt_Zone applies to a metropolitan French trading company. Standard IS headline rate is 25%; reduced rate of 15% applies to the first €42,500 of annual taxable profit for qualifying SMEs (turnover ≤ €10M, with at least 75% of share capital held by individuals or qualifying SMEs).

Available election forms

France has no US-style check-the-box regime; entity-level tax status is fixed by law. Available elections: (1) automatic reduced 15% IS rate on the €42,500 profit tranche under Article 219 CGI; (2) fiscal integration ('intégration fiscale') under Article 223-A CGI for groups owning at least 95% of French subsidiaries, enabling group-wide result offset; (3) 'régime mère-fille' participation exemption under Articles 145 and 216 CGI, electing 95% exemption on qualifying dividends; (4) ministerial-authorization worldwide group consolidation ('bénéfice mondial' / 'bénéfice consolidé') under Articles 209-quinquies and 209-quinquies-A CGI; (5) mandatory ATAD anti-hybrid rules under Article 212-bis CGI, which impose documentation and disallowance obligations without any discretionary opt-out; (6) voluntary VAT registration despite turnover below the 'franchise en base de TVA' threshold, which activates the right to charge VAT and deduct input VAT.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is compulsory from the first euro of taxable turnover, subject to the small-business exemption ('franchise en base de TVA', Article 293-B CGI): €91,900 annual turnover for wholesale of goods (dried goji berries and herbal botanicals fall under goods), €36,800 for services; tolerance band extends exemption up to €101,500 for goods and €40,600 for services where prior-year turnover did not exceed the base threshold. Exceeding the threshold triggers compulsory registration effective from the first day of the month of exceedance. For non-established EU sellers using the OSS distance-selling scheme, the French distance-sale threshold is €10,000 of aggregated EU-wide cross-border distance sales; import VAT is payable separately on entry into the French customs territory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating (exonération avec droit à déduction) is granted under Article 262-I-1° CGI for goods physically exported outside the EU customs territory. Eligibility requires: (i) a sales invoice explicitly stating 'Exportation hors UE — TVA exonérée Article 262-I CGI'; (ii) a validated export customs declaration filed through DELTA/GUN generating a Movement Reference Number (MRN); (iii) physical departure of the goods from the EU within 90 days of dispatch, evidenced by transport documentation (ocean bill of lading, CMR, air waybill, or postal receipt); (iv) the export contract, orders and banking evidence of payment. Direct services incidental to the export (international freight, handling, inspection, brokerage) are also zero-rated under Article 262-I-2° CGI. Intra-Community B2B supplies to EU VAT-registered customers are exempt under Article 262-ter CGI subject to VIES validation of the customer's VAT number and filing of the DEB recapitulative statement and Intrastat declarations. Input VAT is reclaimed on the periodic CA3 return; exporters in a permanent credit position may use the monthly 12-12-12 refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration validated through DELTA/GUN generating an MRN (Movement Reference Number), together with the pre-departure export declaration (DPD) and the accompanying export document (EAD) where applicable
  • Bill of Lading (maison or container B/L), sea waybill, CMR road consignment note, air waybill (AWB), or postal/courier export receipt evidencing physical EU exit within 90 days
  • Commercial invoice (facture de vente) with the clause 'Exportation hors UE — TVA exonérée Article 262-I CGI' and full buyer/consignee details
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / banking proof of settlement (SWIFT MT103 or wire-transfer receipt) evidencing collection of export proceeds within the 180-day repatriation window
  • Intra-Community recapitulative statement (DEB) and Intrastat declaration when the supply qualifies as an EU intra-Community delivery
  • Phytosanitary certificate, certificate of analysis, and botanical/herbal quality certificate for dried goji berries (Lycium barbarum) and herbal botanicals, satisfying French and destination-country border controls
  • EUR.1 movement certificate or Importer's Statement on Origin for preferential tariff claims, where the import contract requires preferential origin
  • International sales contract or purchase order evidencing the wholesale B2B export transaction and the agreed Incoterms
  • Bank payment order / foreign-exchange receipt confirmation reconciling the invoice value with the repatriated amount

France is implementing a phased mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting reform under the 2024 Finance Act. From 1 September 2026, all French taxable persons must have the technical capacity to receive structured e-invoices (EN 16931 / Factur-X) through the Public Invoicing Portal (PPF — Portail Public de Facturation) or a State-accredited dematerialisation platform (PDP); large and mid-sized enterprises must issue structured e-invoices from that date, while SMEs and micro-enterprises must issue from 1 September 2027. From 1 September 2027, mandatory e-reporting of B2C transaction data and cross-border payment flows is transmitted to DGFiP through PPF/PDP APIs. Transmission is near real-time via REST/JSON APIs; each invoice must contain the SIREN, French/EU VAT identification number, VAT rate and legal article reference for zero-rating. Digital stamping is optional for authenticity and integrity of the invoice; non-compliant or missing e-invoices expose the issuer to a €15 per-document penalty and loss of input VAT deduction rights.

DGFiP may verify the export substantiation file within the general 5-year statutory limitation period (Article L. 169 LPF), and customs fraud control extends to a 10-year window under the Customs Code. Defective or missing export evidence converts the zero-rated revenue into taxable domestic turnover at 20%, with full clawback of deducted input VAT plus late-payment interest of 0.40% per month (Article 1727 CGI). Penalties escalate to 40% for bad faith (mauvaise foi) and 80% for deliberate fraud (manœuvres frauduleuses) under Article 1729 CGI; ancillary per-document fines apply under Articles 1737 and 1789–1791 CGI for missing invoices, false documentation or non-compliant e-invoicing. Criminal exposure is governed by Article 414-1 of the French Customs Code (export fraud by false declaration): fines of between one and two times the evaded amount, imprisonment of three to five years, confiscation of the exported goods, transport vehicles and bank assets, publication of the judgment, and joint and several liability of the managing director and any appointed fiscal representative.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, customs, and reporting controls. A French Société par Actions Simplifiée may receive foreign capital, hold foreign-currency accounts where offered by its bank, convert receipts through authorized payment service providers or credit institutions, pay non-resident suppliers, and remit dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, and loan principal subject to corporate approvals, arm's-length pricing, withholding-tax rules, treaty or EU directive conditions, beneficial-owner verification, and sufficient distributable reserves. There is no general French exchange-control approval for ordinary inbound investment or profit repatriation. Dividends paid to non-residents may be subject to French withholding tax unless reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to anti-abuse, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, and substance conditions. Interest, royalties, service fees, and related-party financing remain subject to transfer-pricing, deductibility, withholding, VAT, and documentation requirements. Banks may delay or reject transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions, opaque ownership, unusual payment purposes, high-risk commodities, or inadequate commercial evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement for exporters to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general requirement to convert foreign-currency export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may remain in permitted foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's commercial discretion, subject to the bank's account terms, accounting in euros, customs and VAT evidence, sanctions screening, and applicable statistical or regulatory reporting. Export invoices, transport records, customs declarations, contracts, and payment evidence should be retained under French accounting, tax, customs, and anti-money-laundering record-retention rules. Where a transaction is subject to special sanctions, dual-use controls, agricultural or food controls, or reporting obligations, those controls apply independently of currency conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange and capital-movement rules are principally derived from European Union law, French monetary and financial legislation, and applicable sanctions regulations administered by the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational payments and treasury sub-accounts; Low to Medium for initial capital escrow, regulated trade-finance, high-value commodity flows, and complex foreign-currency structures. An offshore digital electronic-money institution cannot substitute for a French bank account where a French notary, incorporation agent, registry process, lender, or counterparty requires a qualifying French or European credit institution account. An EU-authorized EMI or payment institution may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, SEPA transfers, and certain foreign-exchange services under passporting or local authorization, but it is not a bank, does not generally provide deposit-taking, and may not offer documentary letters of credit, lending, cash pooling, or all requested currencies. The company must verify the provider's authorization in the EBA or relevant national register, safeguarding model, settlement location, sanctions policy, payment limits, client-money protections, data-processing arrangements, and account-closure rights. Payments remain subject to French and EU AML, sanctions, fraud, payment-services, tax, customs, and beneficial-owner controls.

Route paid-in capital through the designated French capital escrow account of a qualifying credit institution when required for incorporation or a capital increase, using the subscriber's account and a payment reference identifying the SAS, capital subscription, and subscription agreement. For subsequent equity injections, use a bank transfer supported by the shareholder or board resolution, subscription documentation, updated ownership records, and the applicable registry filings. Shareholder loans should be transferred under a written loan agreement stating principal, currency, maturity, interest, repayment terms, subordination if applicable, and related-party pricing. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT MT103 or equivalent ISO 20022 cross-border messaging for non-SEPA or non-euro payments. Include accurate originator, beneficiary, ultimate payer, purpose, invoice or agreement reference, and intermediary-bank data. Do not use personal accounts, undisclosed third-party funding, cash, crypto settlement, or split transfers designed to avoid controls. For larger or higher-risk purchases, use a commercial documentary letter of credit issued or confirmed by an authorized bank, incorporating the sales contract, Incoterms, required transport documents, certificate of origin, certificate of analysis, inspection certificate, insurance document, packing list, and customs evidence.
