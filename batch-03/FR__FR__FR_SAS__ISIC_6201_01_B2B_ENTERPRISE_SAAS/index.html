Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de commerce (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés — RCS), filing exclusively via the Guichet Unique electronic portal operated by INPI (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr)

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce) — general company provisions L.210-1 et seq., SAS-specific provisions L.227-1 to L.227-30, and RCS registration provisions R.123-1 et seq.; implemented through the Guichet Unique under Décret n°2021-1628 and Loi n°2021-1729 du 22 décembre 2021.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration unique de création d'entreprise (single incorporation declaration, form 11677*02 digitized) filed as 'Création d'une société — SAS', which simultaneously triggers RCS registration, INSEE SIREN/SIRET allocation, URSSAF affiliation and DGFiP tax identification.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SAS share capital is freely fixed in the articles; French law imposes no statutory minimum and no fixed escrow percentage. The full subscribed cash capital must nevertheless be credited to a temporary blocked account (compte bloqué) at a credit institution and is released to the company only upon issuance of the Kbis registration certificate; in-kind contributions require a commissaire aux apports valuation unless a waiver applies, and contributions must be effectively made before registration.

Funding & unlock path: Office facilities must satisfy the Code du travail's workplace safety standards: written single risk assessment (DUERP), periodic electrical installation verification, workstation ergonomics, and registration of staff with the occupational health service (médecine du travail). Premises open to the public must meet the ERP fire-safety code: for standard office use under fifth-category thresholds, a simple compliance declaration suffices; the prior hiring declaration to URSSAF (DPAE) and, for any new building, the certificate of structural conformity complete occupational clearance. No major-risk (ICPE) evacuation plan is required.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for ISIC 6201 (software programming) — a B2B SaaS venture is a fully liberal commercial and tertiary activity exempt from industrial authorization; the Direction Générale des Entreprises (DGE) has only an optional innovation-support role; no préfecture or licensing board permit is required.

Category: None — the sole operating title is the RCS registration evidenced by the Kbis extract; no manufacturing, operating or premises-based industrial permit exists for enterprise software activities; supplementary filings with CNIL or AMF authorization apply only if sub-activities enter regulated sectors such as payment services or financial data processing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by default. A qualifying SAS may make a temporary election for taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu), generally for a maximum of five financial years, if statutory conditions concerning size, age of the business, ownership, management, voting rights, and turnover or balance-sheet limits are satisfied. The election must be made within the statutory deadline and is subject to revocation and eligibility rules. France has no United States-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implementing ATAD 2 apply to relevant cross-border arrangements; they do not provide a general elective hybrid-entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold permits a French SAS conducting taxable business activities to remain outside VAT registration where VAT registration is otherwise required. The French franchise en base de TVA exemption for services generally applies below EUR 37,500 annual turnover, with a tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250, subject to the applicable year-specific rules and activity classification. The corresponding principal threshold for goods is generally EUR 85,000, with a tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500. A business using the franchise cannot recover input VAT and must monitor the applicable thresholds. VAT registration may nevertheless be required from the outset for intra-EU transactions, imports, exports, reverse-charge transactions, or an election to charge VAT. B2B SaaS supplies are generally governed by the customer-location rule and may be outside French VAT where supplied to a business customer established outside France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT only where the exporter can establish that the goods left the EU customs territory and all export conditions are satisfied. The exporter must retain the customs export declaration, the electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs proof, commercial invoices, transport evidence, and a consistent accounting trail. For B2B SaaS and other electronically supplied services, the principal rule is generally not a French zero rate but application of the place-of-supply rule: a service supplied to a business customer established outside France is normally taxable at the customer location, with the customer accounting for VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism where applicable. The supplier must verify and document the customer's business status, location, VAT number or equivalent tax evidence, contractual recipient, user and billing information, and the actual nature of the service. Supplies to EU business customers generally require reverse-charge invoicing and may trigger European Sales List reporting. French input VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices, business-purpose evidence, proper VAT treatment, and timely VAT-return reporting. Export VAT refunds are subject to documentary verification and may be denied or reclaimed if export conditions are not proven.

  • Electronic export customs declaration, including the applicable customs procedure, commodity classification, customs value, and EORI identifiers
  • Movement Reference Number or equivalent customs reference
  • Customs exit confirmation, such as the electronic exit result or equivalent proof that the goods left the European Union
  • Bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, courier manifest, or other independent transport document
  • Packing list and proof of delivery where relevant
  • Sales contract, purchase order, customer correspondence, and accounting records linking the shipment to the invoice
  • Import or destination-country customs evidence where reasonably available

  • Bank receipt or payment records where relevant; France does not generally require an inward-remittance certificate as a universal statutory condition for export VAT treatment

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through accredited private platforms and the public invoicing infrastructure. From 1 September 2026, all French-established businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices, and large and medium-sized businesses are scheduled to issue electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions. Small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises are scheduled to issue such invoices from 1 September 2027. Electronic reporting applies to transactions outside the domestic B2B e-invoicing perimeter, including certain B2C and cross-border transactions, according to the statutory timetable. France does not generally operate a universal real-time VAT clearance requirement for every invoice. Required data must be transmitted in prescribed formats through an authorized platform, with invoice identity, supplier and customer identifiers, tax amounts, transaction classification, payment terms, and applicable cross-border or reverse-charge data. Digital archiving, invoice integrity, authenticity, readability, and audit-trail requirements apply.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Exporters must nevertheless retain adequate commercial, customs, accounting, transport, customer-location, and payment records and comply with applicable anti-money-laundering, customs, and financial-reporting requirements. If zero-rating or non-taxation is unsupported, the DGFiP may reassess French VAT, deny or claw back input-VAT credits or refunds, and charge late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month, subject to statutory rules. Deliberate non-compliance may attract a 40% penalty; fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse may attract an 80% penalty, in addition to recovered VAT and interest. False customs declarations may result in customs duties, customs penalties, seizure, and potentially criminal liability. Fraudulent VAT refund claims, falsified export evidence, or knowingly incorrect customer-location or reverse-charge treatment may also result in criminal tax-fraud exposure and exclusion from simplified compliance or refund procedures.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and operates within the European Union single market and the euro-area framework. Current-account payments, including customer receipts, supplier payments, payroll, service fees, royalties, dividends, interest, and ordinary profit remittances, are generally freely transferable without prior foreign-exchange approval. Capital-account movements are also generally liberalized, subject to anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions, tax, foreign-direct-investment screening, company-law, and beneficial-ownership requirements. An SAS may remit dividends or other distributions to non-resident shareholders only after legally distributable profits are established, annual accounts are approved, mandatory legal reserves and other restrictions are satisfied, withholding-tax treatment is determined, and the payment is supported by corporate resolutions and accounting records. Related-party loans, management charges, royalties, interest, and other cross-border payments must be commercially justified, arm's-length where applicable, properly documented, and reported or withheld where required. SaaS software development and ordinary enterprise software licensing generally do not fall within France's sensitive-sector foreign-direct-investment authorization regime, but the analysis must be repeated if the business handles sensitive technology, defense-related data, critical infrastructure, regulated health data, or other protected activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement for exporters to surrender or mandatorily convert export proceeds into euros, and there is no general mandatory conversion ratio or statutory repatriation deadline for ordinary software and SaaS export receipts. Proceeds may generally remain in euros or foreign currency, subject to bank documentation, sanctions screening, accounting, tax, and applicable balance-of-payments reporting obligations. The SAS should retain customer contracts, invoices, delivery or acceptance evidence, bank statements, and foreign-currency accounting records. Any reporting requested by the Banque de France or a regulated financial institution must be completed within the applicable reporting period. Export controls, sanctions, VAT evidence, and transfer-pricing documentation remain separate from foreign-exchange convertibility.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, with foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments oversight shared with the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty, including the Directorate-General of the Treasury

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High for payment collection, SEPA transfers, foreign-currency accounts, and treasury support where the provider is an EEA-authorized payment institution or electronic-money institution passported into France. Medium for use as the sole banking relationship because an EMI is not a credit institution, generally does not provide ordinary deposit protection, and must safeguard customer funds rather than accept deposits as a bank. Offshore providers outside the EEA cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to French customers merely by operating from abroad; they require an appropriate French or EEA authorization, passporting basis, or regulated correspondent arrangement. Wise Europe S.A. and similar EEA-regulated providers may be operationally suitable subject to onboarding, supported currencies, local account availability, transaction limits, safeguarding terms, and source-of-funds review. Stripe Billing is a payment-acceptance and merchant-services rail, not a substitute for a French corporate bank account. Maintain a domestic bank account for share-capital deposits, taxes, payroll, statutory payments, reserve liquidity, and contingency operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route share subscriptions and shareholder funding through an account in the SAS's legal name at a French credit institution or an appropriately regulated EEA provider, with the transfer originating from the subscriber's or lender's own verified account. Use a clear payment reference identifying the SAS, the subscription or loan nature, and the relevant corporate resolution. For a new SAS, use the bank's blocked capital-deposit account until the share capital is validly formed and the deposit certificate is issued; subsequently transfer funds to the operational account after registration and completion of the bank's release process. Provide the bank with the subscription agreement, articles, capital table, corporate resolutions, beneficial-owner evidence, source-of-funds evidence, and originating bank details. For non-euro funds, confirm conversion rates, intermediary-bank charges, value date, and whether the recipient account accepts the currency. There is no single universal French purpose-code system for ordinary corporate capital transfers; the bank may require an internal transaction purpose, ISO payment message information, loan or equity classification, and supporting documents. Avoid unexplained third-party funding, informal netting, cash delivery, or transfers through personal accounts. Reconcile the incoming transfer to the general ledger and retain the complete audit trail.Use SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area, subject to bank limits and fraud controls. Use SWIFT cross-border credit transfers, typically ISO 20022 pacs.008 in modern bank channels and legacy MT103 where still supported, for non-SEPA or non-euro supplier payments. Include accurate ordering-customer, beneficiary, ultimate-debtor, invoice, purpose, and sanctions-screening data; select OUR, SHA, or BEN charge allocation according to the contract and bank capability. For material or higher-risk purchases, use a commercial documentary letter of credit, standby letter of credit, documentary collection, or bank guarantee. A commercial LC should specify issuing and confirming banks, applicable UCP 600 rules, required documents, Incoterms, shipment or service milestones, presentation period, currency, expiry, discrepancy handling, and sanctions and export-control conditions. For SaaS services, ordinary wire or SEPA settlement is generally more practical than an LC, but an LC may be relevant for hardware, implementation, cloud infrastructure, or cross-border procurement with documentary performance conditions. Maintain purchase orders, supplier invoices, contracts, acceptance evidence, VAT treatment, customs records for any hardware, and payment approvals. Apply dual authorization, beneficiary-change callback controls, transaction limits, and reconciliation to accounts payable.Deliverable forward exchange contracts with an authorized bank or regulated investment firm for forecast or committed foreign-currency receipts and payments, subject to appropriate purpose, tenor, limits, collateral, and documentationNon-deliverable forwards for currencies where physical delivery is restricted or commercially impractical, subject to counterparty availability, local-market rules, EMIR and MiFID-related obligations, and documented hedging purposeCross-currency swaps and currency swaps for longer-dated financing or recurring currency exposures, subject to ISDA or equivalent master documentation, collateral terms, valuation, accounting designation, and counterparty-credit approvalForeign-exchange swaps for short-term liquidity and currency-date managementCurrency options, collars, and other structured hedges where proportionate to the exposure and approved under the treasury policy; avoid leveraged or speculative structures unless separately authorizedEuro-denominated SEPA settlement and natural hedging by matching foreign-currency receipts with foreign-currency supplier costs, where commercially and operationally feasibleAll derivatives should be entered into with regulated counterparties, documented under an appropriate master agreement, linked to identifiable exposures, monitored for counterparty and market risk, and recorded under applicable French GAAP or IFRS hedge-accounting policies; speculative dealing should be prohibited by the treasury policy

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High for payment collection, SEPA transfers, foreign-currency accounts, and treasury support where the provider is an EEA-authorized payment institution or electronic-money institution passported into France. Medium for use as the sole banking relationship because an EMI is not a credit institution, generally does not provide ordinary deposit protection, and must safeguard customer funds rather than accept deposits as a bank. Offshore providers outside the EEA cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to French customers merely by operating from abroad; they require an appropriate French or EEA authorization, passporting basis, or regulated correspondent arrangement. Wise Europe S.A. and similar EEA-regulated providers may be operationally suitable subject to onboarding, supported currencies, local account availability, transaction limits, safeguarding terms, and source-of-funds review. Stripe Billing is a payment-acceptance and merchant-services rail, not a substitute for a French corporate bank account. Maintain a domestic bank account for share-capital deposits, taxes, payroll, statutory payments, reserve liquidity, and contingency operations.
