Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty. Professional portal: impots.gouv.fr. Company registration gateway: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (INPI sole door for company formalities in France). Non-resident VAT desk: Service des Impôts des Non-Résidents (SINR), 10 rue du Centre, 93460 Noisy-le-Grand. EU VAT number validation: VIES (https://ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies/).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: For a French SAS, incorporation is filed through the INPI one-stop shop (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), triggering issuance of the SIREN (9-digit national registry number) and SIRET within 3-5 business days. Step 2: DGFiP automatically allocates the French intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN; the VAT certificate is retrievable via the company's professional account on impots.gouv.fr. Step 3: For a non-established foreign seller, a formal VAT registration application (Cerfa n° 12855, 'Immatriculation d'un assujetti non établi') is filed with the SINR, attaching the foreign commercial registry extract, proof of foreign VAT status, and, for non-EU applicants, a mandatory fiscal representation mandate (Article 289 A CGI, approved representative). Registration is acknowledged within 15 business days and completed in approximately 5 weeks; the VAT number is effective on issuance. Step 4: For operations subject to domestic reverse charge (waste/scrap), the French client is itself liable for VAT remittance; the non-resident supplier still requires a French VAT ID if it is carrying on a permanent revenue-generating activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope_France / No_Tax_Exempt_Zone. An SAS is by default fully subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) under Articles 206 and 209 CGI, on worldwide profits when its registered office or place of effective management is in France. Standard CIT rate: 25% for fiscal years opened from 1 January 2022. No pass-through treatment is available to an SAS. Metropolitan France offers no general tax-exempt zone for industrial/commercial activity; tax advantages are limited to specified overseas territories (Guyane, Mayotte) and incentive regimes not applicable to the base profile.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' election exists. Statutory election levers: (i) French fiscal group integration (Article 223 A CGI) for subsidiaries held at 95% or more, enabling consolidated CIT settlement; (ii) VAT group registration ('assujetti unique', Article 256 C CGI), allowing group VAT consolidation among closely aligned French entities; (iii) hybrid-mismatch alignment under Article 202 C CGI and the French ATAD transposition: taxpayers may obtain a binding legal ruling (rescrit fiscal) to align the French tax classification of a foreign hybrid entity and neutralize deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; (iv) the regime of tax-transparent partnerships (régime des sociétés de personnes, Article 239 A CGI) is not available to an SAS; (v) for certain small companies, an option can be made to remain within the individual income tax regime (Article 239 B CGI) solely in the formation period, but this is strictly limited and incompatible with an SAS used for cross-border trading. Elections are filed with the local SIE before the end of the relevant fiscal year and are irrevocable for the chosen duration.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The general small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base, Article 293 B CGI) applies up to EUR 91,900 of turnover for supplies of goods (2025) and EUR 36,800 for services. However, supplies of non-ferrous scrap, waste and recoverable materials (including shredded copper wire) are excluded from the franchise and are mandatorily subject to VAT, with the domestic reverse-charge mechanism (Article 283-2 CGI) making the French customer liable for VAT. Consequently, the effective registration threshold for this business is EUR 0: every French SAS trading in scrap copper wire must be VAT-registered from the first transaction. A EUR 10,000 annual intra-EU distance-sales threshold applies for sales to private consumers in other Member States; B2B supplies are governed by the place-of-supply rules of Articles 258 and 258 A CGI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export deliveries of goods are exempt with right to deduction/refund pursuant to Article 262-I-1° CGI and BOI-TVA-EXP-30-10. Claim conditions: (i) the supply transfers the right to dispose of the goods; (ii) the goods are physically shipped or transported outside the European Union by the seller, on the seller's behalf, or — in the framework of a chain supply — the EX-PAYS/EX-1 export customs declaration proves exit from EU territory; (iii) the goods leave the EU within 90 days of the supply (extension possible with valid justification); (iv) the seller retains the customs export declaration (DAU) and transport documentation consigning the copper scrap wire to a non-EU destination; (v) the buyer is identified and the commercial invoice states the applicable exemption. Non-ferrous scrap copper wire (HS 7404) qualifies as tangible goods for VAT purposes; the goods may transit through a customs warehouse before exit without breaking the zero-rating chain. Zero-rating does not require prior authorization but is subject to post-export audit substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration Unique d'Exportation, DAU) — digital EX-1 document lodged via the DELTA-X customs system, validated with the MRN (Movement Reference Number) and evidencing the HS 7404 product, weight and exit office.
  • Bill of Lading — original negotiable ocean B/L, CMR waybill or multimodal transport document showing the consignee outside the EU and the port/place of actual exit, dated within 90 days of supply.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank credit evidence (SWIFT MT103 / account statement) demonstrating receipt of the export proceeds in the French exporter's account, aligned with Article 913 of the French Monetary and Financial Code.
  • Commercial Invoice — issued without VAT, referencing Article 262-I-1° CGI and stating 'Exportation hors Union Européenne – TVA non applicable'. Must match weight, grade and value of the copper scrap.
  • Supplier Purchase Documents and Weighboridge Ticket (pèse-billet) — evidencing the chain of custody of the shredded copper wire from acquisition to shipment and supporting the scrap classification.
  • Transport and Insurance Contracts — delivery order, transit declaration (e.g., T1/community transit consignment note) and cargo insurance policy naming the French exporter as insured.
  • Weight and Quality Certificate — sworn assay/laboratory certificate of copper purity at the Port d'embarquement, essential for customs valuation and ISIC 4669 consistency.
  • Intrastat / STATLINES Declaration — where exit involves intra-EU movement prior to final non-EU export, the Intrastat statement and customs event history must be retained in the audit file.

France is rolling out a mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting framework without a real-time fiscal clearance stamp. Timeline: from 1 September 2026, all taxable persons established in France must be able to receive e-invoices, and large/mid-size businesses must issue e-invoices; from 1 September 2027, small and micro enterprises must also issue e-invoices. Mandatory formats: Factur-X (EN 16931 hybrid), UBL 2.1, or CII. Invoices are transmitted through the free public platform PPF (Portail Public de Facturation) or a certified PDP; transaction and payment data are e-reported to DGFiP via the PPF within 10 days of invoice issuance. B2G invoicing is already mandatory on the Chorus Pro platform. For international B2B transactions, e-reporting of sales and payment data applies; the duty to transmit applies to the supplier. There is no digital stamping, continuous fiscal sealing (socle), or pre-clearance authorization system at the national level; DGFiP applies automated data analytics and cross-checks against customs declarations (DELTA) and bank payment data.

DGFiP scrutinizes export files through a dedicated risk-scoring engine comparing customs MRN data, commercial invoices, transport waybills and bank MT103 messages. A claim of export zero-rating that is not substantiated results in forced re-invoicing of 20% VAT under Article 269 CGI plus late-payment interest (0.2% per month, Article 1727 CGI). Where the insufficiency is deemed a maneuver frauduleuse or abuse of right, a penalty of 80% of the tax due applies under Articles 1729 and 1729-0-1 CGI. Criminal sanctions under the French Customs Code (Articles 414 and 415) reach fines of up to five times the value of the goods, confiscation of the cargo or means of transport, and imprisonment of up to 10 years for organized smuggling. Company directors and de facto officers, as well as the SAS itself, are jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalties and fines (Articles 267 quinquies CGI and 1742 CGI). Fraudulent zero-rating may additionally trigger loss or suspension of fiscal clean status, refusal of the OEA (AEO) customs authorization, delisting from the recycler authorized-tax regime, and reporting to the French interbank AML unit (Tracfin) in cases covered by the Medium-Risk_Enhanced AML classification.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is an EU/OECD member and part of the eurozone; the euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account operations under the free movement of capital principle of the TFEU. No exchange controls apply to ordinary trade, profit remittance, or capital movements. Profit and dividend outbound transfers are permitted for legally established entities, subject to beneficial-ownership verification, anti-money-laundering/CFT screening, and tax reporting obligations. There is no mandatory conversion of export receipts and no prior administrative authorisation for standard commercial payments, except statistical reporting obligations to Banque de France for large cross-border transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

French law imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may retain foreign currency balances in authorised foreign-currency accounts through intermediary banks. However, export receipts must comply with EU wire-transfer transparency rules (Regulation (EU) 2015/847), cash declarations above EUR 10,000 under the Customs and Monetary Code, and Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical declaration where applicable. Documentary records of the underlying purchase order, invoice, bill of lading, and customs declaration must be kept for at least 10 years.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting as central bank and balance-of-payments reporting agent; foreign exchange regulatory oversight is exercised with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) and the ACPR (Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) can open payment accounts and process SWIFT MT103 inbound/outbound wires, but they generally cannot provide documentary letters of credit, capital-escrow accounts, or comprehensive trade-finance products needed for recycled scrap copper import settlement. EMIs are subject to the EU E-Money Directive and French Monetary and Financial Code restrictions: they may not take deposits or perform credit/lending operations, and client funds must be safeguarded in regulated accounts. For a recycling trading vehicle, an EMI is viable only as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail, not as the primary clearing infrastructure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are routed by SWIFT MT103 wire from the shareholder's bank to the SAS's euro-denominated operational account. The wire must carry a clear balance-of-payments purpose code (e.g., capital contribution or shareholder subordinated loan) and remitter description matching the shareholder name on file. The receiving French bank will verify the beneficiary IBAN, BIC/SWIFT, legal entity name, and corporate KYC pack; for larger capital movements, the resident agent bank is responsible for monthly balance-of-payments reporting to Banque de France, including the transaction value, country of the counterparty, and purpose classification. No French exchange-control authorisation is required, but documentary evidence such as a board resolution, share transfer agreement, and shareholding structure must be retained.Outbound trade settlements are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers and documentary letters of credit. For commercial letters of credit, the French bank issues a revocable or, preferably, irrevocable LC under UCP 600, payable against presentation of the commercial invoice, packed list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, quality certificate, and weight certificate. Documentary collection under URC 522 is also available for stable counterparties. For direct SWIFT MT103 payments, the remitter must provide the customs export declaration, purchase order, invoice, and transport documents, and the payment field must contain the correct trade purpose code. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) can open payment accounts and process SWIFT MT103 inbound/outbound wires, but they generally cannot provide documentary letters of credit, capital-escrow accounts, or comprehensive trade-finance products needed for recycled scrap copper import settlement. EMIs are subject to the EU E-Money Directive and French Monetary and Financial Code restrictions: they may not take deposits or perform credit/lending operations, and client funds must be safeguarded in regulated accounts. For a recycling trading vehicle, an EMI is viable only as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail, not as the primary clearing infrastructure.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, CGI Article 219) applicable to French-resident SAS entities for fiscal years opened on or after 1 January 2022

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax (retenue à la source) on dividends distributed by a French SAS to non-resident companies under CGI Article 119 bis; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% for an uninterrupted 24-month period; reduced DTA rates (commonly 10%-15%) apply to residents of treaty partners such as China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50,000,000: a French SAS must prepare and maintain the Transfer Pricing Master File and Local File under CGI Article L. 13 AA when its annual French turnover (CA HT) or the consolidated turnover of the group exceeds EUR 50 million; the simplified transfer pricing declaration (Form 2065-SD) is likewise required above that threshold, and all documentation must be provided to the DGFiP within 30 days of a formal request.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute as in the UAE, but substance and commercial rationality are enforced through the abuse-of-law rule (Article L. 64 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales), the general anti-abuse rule, the Principal Purpose Test under the BEPS Multilateral Instrument (Article 29), and the requirement that treaty and EU directive benefits are granted only to genuine economic operators with local personnel, premises, and decision-making capacity; artificial shell arrangements are disregarded and recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanction under CGI Article 1741: fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years, extended to EUR 2,000,000 and 7 years in aggravated or organised circumstances; the fiscal administration additionally reassesses the tax base with a 40% bad-faith penalty or 80% fraudulent-conduct penalty, plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors and the président of the SAS who participate in the fraud are personally exposed to criminal fines and imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced through solidarité fiscale, allowing seizure of personal assets; travel bans may be imposed pending criminal investigation, and the court may impose the interdiction de gérer (ban on managing commercial enterprises).
Failure to File Tax Returns and Liasse Fiscale

Statutory sanction: Taxation d'office of the corporate income tax base by the administration; automatic 10% penalty, increased to 40% if the return is filed within 30 days after formal notice, and 80% in cases of fraud; late-payment interest at 0.20% per month; minimum EUR 100 fine per missing schedule.

Director personal liability: Where the company is insolvent and the omission is deliberate, the président may be subject to a personal monetary claim for the unpaid corporate tax; criminal liability for intentional non-compliance may result in fines, imprisonment, and a ban on conducting business activities.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Rectification under CGI Article 57: the DGFiP re-determines French taxable profit at arm's length; the upward adjustment is subject to a 40% penalty for serious mispricing, rising to 80% for abuse of law, plus late interest; the recharacterization may convert the excess into a constructive dividend subject to 25% withholding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors approving the intercompany pricing policy face joint and several liability for the reassessed tax, penalties, and interest if the arrangement is held to be artificial; criminal prosecution for tax evasion can include personal imprisonment and travel restrictions.
Abuse of Law / Shell Company and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Application of the abuse-of-law procedure: the transaction is disregarded, benefits are denied retroactively, income is recharacterized at the French statutory 25% rate with a 25% hidden-distribution withholding tax, and an 80% abuse penalty applies on the tax avoided, together with late interest.

Director personal liability: The président and beneficial owners who knowingly maintain the artificial structure are personally liable for the reassessed tax and penalties through the solidarité mechanism; they may be subjected to faillite personnelle (personal bankruptcy), interdiction de gérer, and criminal sanctions including imprisonment and travel bans.
Money Laundering and AML Violations in Physical Scrap Trading

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Articles L. 561-1 et seq.): individual offenders face up to 5 years imprisonment and fines up to EUR 1,000,000; corporate entities face fines up to EUR 5,000,000, asset confiscation, and disqualification from certain business activities; failure to report to Tracfin additionally triggers administrative sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and the effective beneficial owner of the SAS are personally exposed to criminal conviction, fines, confiscation of personal assets, freezing orders on bank accounts, and travel bans preventing departure from French territory while under judicial examination.
