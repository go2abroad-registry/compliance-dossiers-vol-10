Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce maintaining the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); submissions are made through the INPI-operated Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.210-1 et seq. and L.227-1 to L.227-30 (SAS); Decree No. 2007-431 as amended.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'entreprise (M0 form) filed through the Guichet Unique, together with the signed articles of association (statuts) and all applicable annexes.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No legal minimum capital for an SAS; capital is freely fixed in the articles. Cash contributions must be paid up to at least 50% at incorporation, with the balance within five years. Cash funds are deposited temporarily in a bank or with the Caisse des Dépôts and released only after RCS registration; no escrow is statutorily mandated for ordinary cash. In-kind contributions must be fully released and valued by a commissaire aux apports when required.

Funding & unlock path: Standard office occupancy compliance is required under the Code de la construction and the Code du travail. This includes a fire-safety certificate for any building receiving employees or the public, electrical installation verification, sanitary and accessibility checks, and visible emergency instructions. No separate fire-brigade licence is required for low-capacity office premises; where an établissement recevant du public exceeds 300 persons, prior fire-safety commission approval may be required.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum capital for an SAS; capital is freely fixed in the articles. Cash contributions must be paid up to at least 50% at incorporation, with the balance within five years. Cash funds are deposited temporarily in a bank or with the Caisse des Dépôts and released only after RCS registration; no escrow is statutorily mandated for ordinary cash. In-kind contributions must be fully released and valued by a commissaire aux apports when required.

The capital is hereby fully subscribed and accepted, in cash and in kind as applicable. RESOLVED FURTHER: The registered office at [address], France, is adopted for the company and registered with the RCS. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable; DREAL (Direction Régionale de l'Environnement, de l'Aménagement et du Logement) would be the competent authority only if future operations trigger ICPE thresholds.

Category: No sectoral operating licence required for ISIC 6391 financial newswire aggregation in France; RCS registration with APE code 6391Z and standard employer registrations are sufficient.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial Sovereignty; portal: impots.gouv.fr. For non-established foreign entities, the relevant VAT registration service is the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE).

E-government filing portal

SAS established in France: incorporate via the online Guichet Unique (guichet-entreprises.fr) with articles of association, registered office details, and president/shareholder information; obtain RCS registration with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce; INSEE/DGFiP then allocates SIREN, SIRET, and the intra-Community French VAT number (FR + two-digit key + SIREN). Foreign non-established entity supplying taxable services in France: register directly for VAT with DGFiP/SIEE through the dedicated online teleprocedure on impots.gouv.fr; provide constitutional documents, foreign tax registration proof, expected turnover, business activity description, and, if outside the EU, appoint a French tax representative where required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — SAS established in France: incorporate via the online Guichet Unique (guichet-entreprises.fr) with articles of association, registered office details, and president/shareholder information; obtain RCS registration with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce; INSEE/DGFiP then allocates SIREN, SIRET, and the intra-Community French VAT number (FR + two-digit key + SIREN). Foreign non-established entity supplying taxable services in France: register directly for VAT with DGFiP/SIEE through the dedicated online teleprocedure on impots.gouv.fr; provide constitutional documents, foreign tax registration proof, expected turnover, business activity description, and, if outside the EU, appoint a French tax representative where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election and no fiscal pass-through election exists for a French SAS; an SAS is compulsorily subject to entity-level corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Hybrid mismatch rules under Article 212 quinquies and related provisions of the CGI are mandatory, not elective. The only relevant elective corporate tax mechanism is French tax consolidation ('intégration fiscale') available to a 95% French parent, plus the separate authorization regime for worldwide/consolidated profits under Articles 209 quinquies and 209 sexies of the CGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France generally applies no turnover threshold for VAT registration of a non-established foreign supplier; any taxable supply made in France triggers registration unless covered by the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) mechanism (distance sales threshold EUR 10,000). For small established French businesses, the French 'franchise en base' applies: EUR 85,000 turnover for goods and EUR 37,500 for services, with safeguards for digital services and occasional transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports outside the EU are taxable at 0% under CGI Article 262-I where the goods physically leave the EU and the supplier retains satisfactory proof of export. Services directly related to exported movable property may be exempt under Article 262-II. For the target financial-newswire API business, French VAT is normally excluded by place-of-supply rules: B2B supplies to a taxable customer established outside France are taxable in the customer's jurisdiction (reverse charge for EU customers; outside French VAT for non-EU customers); B2C digital services to non-EU consumers are outside French VAT, and EU B2C supplies may be reported through the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) scheme. Eligibility for 0% or no-French-VAT treatment requires documented customer identification, VAT/tax registration evidence, contracts, and payment records.

  • Customs export declaration (DAU) validated through DELTA-X
  • Commercial invoice
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR, or postal/export carrier receipt
  • Proof of physical exit from the EU (carrier certificate, destination customs entry, or seal evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing foreign-currency repatriation
  • Customer's foreign tax/VAT identification number and underlying contract or order documentation

France will require structured electronic invoicing under the réforme de la facturation électronique: domestic B2B invoices must be transmitted through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) using Factur-X, UBL, or CII formats, phased from 1 September 2026, with small and micro enterprises from 1 September 2027. There is no real-time tax-authority clearance or digital stamping; e-reporting (API-based reporting to DGFiP) is required for B2C and cross-border transactions not covered by e-invoicing.

DGFiP may rigorously audit export proof, delivery terms, and foreign-exchange repatriation records. If a 0% export exemption is disallowed for missing evidence, false declaration, or fraud, the company will be assessed for unreported French VAT at 20%, plus late-payment interest under CGI Article 1727 and penalties up to 40% for bad faith (Article 1729-1) or 80% for deliberate tax fraud/concealment (Article 1729-2). Criminal prosecution under CGI Article 1741 can result in fines up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to five years. Input VAT credits previously claimed in respect of the zero-rated export can be clawed back with interest and penalties.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member with no general foreign-exchange controls restricting current-account payments, capital transfers, dividend payments, or repatriation of investment proceeds. Cross-border euro payments within the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA) generally use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer where supported; other currencies and non-SEPA destinations generally use correspondent banking and SWIFT. Payments remain subject to sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing controls, tax rules, bank documentation requirements, and applicable reporting obligations. Dividend distributions require distributable reserves and valid corporate approvals under French company law, with withholding-tax and treaty analysis as applicable. A French SAS should maintain records supporting the commercial purpose, beneficial ownership, source of funds, and tax treatment of significant cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to repatriate or convert export proceeds into euros and no mandatory export-proceeds conversion ratio. Receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank terms, accounting and tax requirements, sanctions controls, and applicable financial reporting. There is no general statutory conversion deadline for ordinary export receipts. Banks may request invoices, contracts, shipping or service evidence, and payment-purpose information.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France's central bank and participates in the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. The Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR), within the Banque de France, supervises French credit institutions and certain payment and electronic-money institutions. The Direction générale du Trésor administers relevant financial and external-financial reporting rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for routine euro collections and payments if the provider is an EEA-authorized payment institution or electronic-money institution permitted to provide services in France, including through valid passporting where applicable. An EMI balance is electronic money or safeguarded client funds, not a bank deposit, and generally is not covered by the French deposit-guarantee scheme. Verify the provider's authorization, French service permissions, safeguarding arrangements, account-holder name, supported currencies, transfer limits, and access to SEPA and SWIFT. Do not rely on an offshore EMI as the sole operating or capital account where counterparties, tax authorities, or the company's bank require a French or EEA credit-institution account. Stripe may support payment processing but should not be treated as a substitute for a corporate bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Open the SAS account or, where required, a capital-deposit account with a French credit institution or other eligible depositary. Route subscribed share capital from the shareholder's own verified account to the depositary account, identifying the SAS, subscriber, and purpose as share capital or capital increase. Provide the subscription documents, incorporation or capital-increase resolutions, ownership and source-of-funds evidence, and remitter account proof. Obtain the deposit certificate and complete the release process after registration or the applicable corporate formalities. For shareholder loans, use a separate documented loan agreement, approvals, repayment terms, and accounting treatment; do not describe debt funding as equity. SEPA Credit Transfer is generally appropriate for euro transfers within SEPA; use SWIFT with accurate beneficiary, bank, and payment-purpose details for non-SEPA or non-euro transfers. The bank may request supporting documents and may delay or reject transfers pending AML, sanctions, or source-of-funds review. France does not have a universal corporate FX purpose-code regime comparable to exchange-control jurisdictions.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within SEPA, subject to bank availability and limits.
