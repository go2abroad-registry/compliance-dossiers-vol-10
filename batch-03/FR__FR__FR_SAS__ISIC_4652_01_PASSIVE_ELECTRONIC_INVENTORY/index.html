Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), with registration maintained by the competent greffe of the Commercial Court.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 to R.227-2, supplemented by Articles L.123-1 et seq. concerning the Trade and Companies Register and Articles L.225-3 and L.225-129 et seq. concerning share capital contributions applicable to SAS where relevant.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company formation filing submitted through the Guichet unique, including the declaration of creation of a legal entity, articles of association, beneficial ownership declaration, and supporting exhibits.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

An SAS may be incorporated with capital of EUR 1. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation and the balance within five years of registration. Cash contributions are deposited into a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts account pending issuance of the certificate of deposit. In-kind contributions require valuation unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied.

Funding & unlock path: Before opening a warehouse, verify building-use authorization, fire compartments, emergency exits, extinguishing systems, alarm and evacuation arrangements, electrical conformity, and load-bearing capacity with the owner, insurer, and competent local authorities. An establishment receiving the public may require an ERP safety opinion or authorization from the mayor and departmental safety commission. Occupational safety duties include risk assessment, a documented DUERP, training, manual-handling controls, suitable storage racks, and periodic inspection of relevant equipment.

Paid-in capital rule: An SAS may be incorporated with capital of EUR 1. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation and the balance within five years of registration. Cash contributions are deposited into a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts account pending issuance of the certificate of deposit. In-kind contributions require valuation unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances; primary portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; business registration/formal procedures: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; customs and export declarations: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), portal www.douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS obtains its statutory identifiers through incorporation formalities filed on the Guichet Unique operated by the INPI; INSEE immediately issues SIREN (company identifier) and SIRET (establishment identifier), with the APE/NAF code corresponding to ISIC 4652 (for wholesale of electronic components: APE 4652Z). The DGFiP, acting on the incorporation data, automatically assigns the intra-Community VAT identification number (format FRxx xxxxxxxxx) and the DGDDI issues the EORI number (FR + SIREN) for customs operations. A foreign non-established entity must instead apply to the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) in Paris, submitting the anti-fraud pre-registration documentation and the relevant CERFA forms, after which the SIEE allocates a French VAT number and, where applicable, an EORI number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' regime; a SAS is by default subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) at entity level. Statutory elections available include: (i) option for income-tax transparenition under Article 239 bis C CGI for small SAS conducting industrial, commercial, artisanal or agricultural activities with fewer than 50 employees and annual turnover not exceeding EUR 10,000,000, exercisable for renewable five-year periods; (ii) election for fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI, allowing a jointly-consolidated tax group with 95%-held French subsidiaries; (iii) the anti-hybrid regime under Articles 209 IX and 212-1 bis CGI, which allows no opt-out but requires taxpayer self-declaration and neutralization of hybrid mismatch outcomes; (iv) limited VAT options, such as option for taxation of exempt property lettings. All elections are filed with the local tax office (SIE) or the DGFiP as applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For an established French SAS, VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction with no monetary threshold, except that the 'franchise en base' small-business exemption may apply where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 91,900 for supplies of goods or EUR 36,800 for services. A non-established EU seller of distance-sold goods to France becomes registrable when its annual intra-EU distance sales exceed EUR 10,000 (with OSS option retained below that threshold). A non-established third-country seller has no monetary exemption and must register upon the first taxable sale in France, subject only to the special IOSS border-import scheme for low-value parcels not exceeding EUR 150.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods destined outside the European Union qualify for the 0% export rate under Article 262-I CGI. Intra-EU dispatches of the passive electronic inventory to a purchaser holding a valid EU VAT number qualify for exemption with credit (effectively 0%) under Article 262 ter CGI, provided an EC Sales List is filed and the goods are physically transferred to another Member State. For non-EU exports, title and possession must pass to the overseas buyer and the goods must physically leave the EU within the applicable statutory period; closely related transport, handling and insurance services forming part of the export contract can also be zero-rated. Supplies to French customers, sales to private individuals, and invoicing without actual cross-border movement cannot be zero-rated and are subject to normal TVA at 20%.

  • Customs export declaration: DAU/DELTA-X electronic entry filed through the Guichet Unique National (GUN) of the DGDDI, correlated with the EORI number
  • Transport evidence of removal from EU territory: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR consignment note naming the French SAS as shipper/exporter
  • Commercial invoice bearing the export reference and, for intra-EU movements, the customer's valid intra-Community VAT number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation bancaire d'encaissement / justificatif de rapatriement des recettes d'exportation) issued by the collecting bank, proving the export proceeds were received from the foreign buyer
  • EC Sales List (état récapitulatif) filed for intra-EU dispatches and, where applicable, Intrastat declarations

France mandates structured electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions under a phased reform: all taxpayers must accept e-invoices from 1 September 2026; large enterprises must issue e-invoices from 1 September 2026, mid-sized companies (ETI) from 1 September 2027, and SMEs/sole traders from 1 September 2028. Invoices flow through an accredited private dematerialization platform (PDP) or the central Portail Public de Facturation (PPF), which automatically transmits tax-relevant data to the DGFiP; cross-border B2B supplies and B2C transactions are covered by a complementary e-reporting obligation. Accepted structured formats are Factur-X, UBL 2.x and UN/CEFACT CII, and transmission uses public API connections to the PPF. France does not apply a per-invoice real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping, but integrity, authenticity and readability must be guaranteed through the PDP/PPF chain or a qualified electronic signature.

DGFiP audits apply strict substantive scrutiny to export zero-rating claims; if the statutory documentation bundle is absent or inconsistent, the 0% rate is clawed back and the VAT becomes due on the full selling price, plus monthly late-payment interest of 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI) and penalties of 40% for lack of good faith (mauvaise foi) or 80% for fraudulent maneuvers, abuse of law or active concealment (Articles 1729 and 1729-0 A CGI). Falsified export documents, sham flows and fictitious foreign customers additionally constitute tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI, punishable by a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and five years' imprisonment, increased to EUR 2,000,000 and seven years for organized fraud, without prejudice to personal tax liability, publication penalties, attachment orders and customs sanctions by the DGDDI for false customs declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under full EU liberalisation of current and capital account movements (TFEU Articles 63-66). The euro is freely convertible. Dividend, interest, royalty, and liquidation proceeds from a French SAS are freely remittable to non-resident shareholders without exchange-control approval. Practical hurdles are not convertibility but documentary compliance: UBO declarations, proof of corporate existence, tax residence certificates for treaty relief, and the 28% domestic dividend withholding tax reduced under applicable tax treaties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose statutory surrender requirements on export proceeds. Exporters may retain and manage foreign-currency receivables freely. Under the balance-of-payments framework, cross-border financial transactions equal to or above EUR 12,500 must be reported by the resident collecting bank to the Banque de France; no separate exporter filing is required. Merchandise import/export settlements must be supported by customs declarations and underlying trade documentation to secure VAT and corporate deduction rights.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments statistical reporting is administered by the Banque de France under the policy oversight of the French Ministry of Economy and Finance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW-MEDIUM as primary treasury bank. ACPR-licensed French payment institutions can process cross-border payments and FX transactions but cannot hold customer deposits or provide credit facilities, bank guarantees, or documentary letters of credit. EEA-authorised EMIs may passport into France under the Payment Services Directive and register with the ACPR; UK and other offshore EMIs cannot passport into France post-Brexit and would require separate French authorisation or an agency arrangement. Use of an EMI is therefore recommended only as an ancillary collections/payments rail, not for capital escrow or trade-finance settlement.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Full UBO ownership chart traceable to identifiable natural persons; certified copy of the shareholder's passport or identification; valid articles of association (statuts) and Kbis extract; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds statement; reference letter from a recognised banking institution; tax residence certificate issued by the shareholder's competent authority.
Passive electronic-inventory distribution showing high-value, low-bulk shipments with inconsistent invoice-to-payment matches

Root cause: Passive electronic-inventory distribution showing high-value, low-bulk shipments with inconsistent invoice-to-payment matches

Defense evidence: Master supply and distribution agreements; supplier pro-forma invoices and final commercial invoices; INCO terms clearly stated; customs import declarations (DAC / déclaration en détail); warehouse stock reports; transport documentation (AWB, bill of lading, CMR); and audited financial statements demonstrating a plausible commercial margin and inventory flow.

Root cause: Capital injection routed as a shareholder loan or mixing subscription proceeds with third-party payments

Defense evidence: Notarised shareholder loan agreement or share subscription resolution; capital contribution report by a statutory auditor (commissaire aux apports) where applicable; UBO declaration filed with the French company registry; notarised legalisation/apostille for foreign documents; bank statements from the remitting bank sourced to the beneficial owner; explanatory board minutes describing the commercial purpose of the treasury operation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's bank to the French entity's EUR-denominated operational account at a tier-1 French commercial bank. The payment must carry an ISO 20022 purpose code of DINV (Direct Investment) or CAPC (Capital Payment), with remittance information stating 'Souscription d’actions — Apport de capital SAS' and the corporate registration number. The French receiving bank automatically logs the transaction in the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical framework. For non-EU direct investments outside sensitive regulated sectors there is no prior ministerial approval, but the bank may require a completed Form No. 1350 (Declaration of Direct Investment) for statistical and financial-relations purposes. If the investor operates in a sector listed under Article R153-2 of the French Monetary and Financial Code or holds more than 25% voting rights, screening under the French FDI regime must be analysed before execution.For import settlements of passive electronic components, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 (USD/EUR supplier payments), SEPA Credit Transfer for EU suppliers, and documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) issued under UCP 600 for high-value or credit-protected purchase orders. Disbursements must reference the commercial invoice number, transport document, and INCOterm to preserve import VAT deduction and corporate deduction rights. For fiscal year ending December 31, the return must be filed by the second business day after May 1 of the following year. For other fiscal year-ends, the deadline is within 3 months of the fiscal year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 (Résultat fiscal) Taxable income or loss after adjustments (reintegration of non-deductible expenses, deduction of tax credits). Non-resident companies with a French permanent establishment must report French-source income in this box; losses may be carried forward subject to the annual cap. Late filing penalty: 10% of the tax due if filed within 30 days of the deadline; 40% if filed after 30 days. Interest for late payment: 0.20% per month on unpaid tax. Additional penalties for failure to file electronically: €150 per return.
Box 2 (Plus-values / moins-values) Net capital gains or losses from disposal of fixed assets. Report gains from French real estate or business assets; treaty relief may apply if a permanent establishment exists. Late filing penalty: 10% of the tax due if filed within 30 days of the deadline; 40% if filed after 30 days. Interest for late payment: 0.20% per month on unpaid tax. Additional penalties for failure to file electronically: €150 per return.
Box 3 (Crédits d'impôt) Tax credits (e.g., CIR, CII, foreign tax credits) applied against the corporate income tax liability. Non-residents may claim foreign tax credits only if a tax treaty provides for such relief; supporting documentation required. Additional penalties for failure to file electronically: €150 per return.
Box 4 (Réductions d'impôt) Tax reductions (e.g., for investments in overseas territories). Generally not applicable to non-residents unless the activity is carried out in France. Late filing penalty: 10% of the tax due if filed within 30 days of the deadline; 40% if filed after 30 days. Interest for late payment: 0.20% per month on unpaid tax. Additional penalties for failure to file electronically: €150 per return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: 400,000,000 EUR (turnover or total assets exceeding this threshold triggers mandatory Master File and Local File preparation). For smaller companies, simplified documentation is required if related-party transactions exceed €50,000 in aggregate.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but applies OECD BEPS principles and requires economic substance for tax residency and treaty benefits. Anti-abuse provisions (e.g., Article 155 A of the French Tax Code) may recharacterize transactions lacking substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (Article 1741 of the French Tax Code). Civil penalties: 80% of the evaded tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for tax debts if they have committed fraud or used the company as a shield. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, denial of treaty benefits, and imposition of 40% penalty for abuse of law (abus de droit).

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for the company's tax liabilities if they are found to have created a fictitious structure.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1 million (or 10% of turnover) and criminal penalties for money laundering (up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine).

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable for money laundering if they knowingly participate or fail to report suspicious transactions.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty for lack of documentation, and interest for late payment. In severe cases, criminal penalties for tax evasion may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant transfer pricing policies.
