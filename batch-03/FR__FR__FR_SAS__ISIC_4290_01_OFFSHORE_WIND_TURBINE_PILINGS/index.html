Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office in France under Articles L.123-11 et seq. of the French Commercial Code. A domiciliation company or virtual-office provider may be used if it is legally authorized, has a written domiciliation agreement, verifies the identity and authority of the client, and maintains the required records. A virtual office is generally unsuitable as the sole operational arrangement for offshore wind foundation installation because the activity requires project management, engineering, procurement, safety, maritime, environmental and contractor substance. Maintain accessible corporate records, a functioning French management interface, appropriate personnel or subcontracting arrangements, project documentation, insurance and operational evidence.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS; capital may be set in the articles, including at EUR 1. For cash contributions, at least 50% of each subscribed amount must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Cash is deposited with a bank, notary, or other legally authorized depositary pending registration; the deposit is released after registration. Contributions in kind generally require a contribution auditor unless a statutory exemption applies.

Funding & unlock path: Before mobilization, obtain required site-owner and project safety approvals, prepare the applicable prevention plan or coordination documentation, and comply with the Labour Code, maritime safety rules, and occupational risk-assessment duties. Fire-safety and structural approvals apply to relevant buildings, installations, and works under their specific regimes; there is no universal offshore-piling-company clearance. Verify vessel, lifting-equipment, occupational health, emergency-response, and site-specific inspections before work.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

No single national industrial licensing board grants a general offshore-piling contractor licence. Project and site authorizations are handled by the competent prefect (including the maritime prefect where relevant), with technical review by the relevant DREAL and other competent maritime, environmental, and construction authorities. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministry of Economy and Finance; official portal: impots.gouv.fr; business incorporation and tax ID registration performed via the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

The French SAS is incorporated via the Guichet Unique, which triggers automatic RCS registration and sequential issuance of (a) a 9-digit SIREN as the French tax identification number, (b) a 14-digit SIRET establishment identifier, and (c) a French intra-Community VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 2-digit key + SIREN), all delivered electronically by the DGFiP through the INFOGREFFE / INSEE chain. A non-resident foreign entity with no permanent establishment in France must apply directly to the DGFiP for a non-established taxable person VAT number (assujetti non établi) by completing the DGFiP's specific registration procedure on impots.gouv.fr, providing certified identity documents, proof of the foreign tax registration, and appointing an accredited tax representative (représentant fiscal) if it seeks a VAT refund under the 13th Directive or performs taxable transactions subject to French collection duty. The SIREN is mandatory before issuing any domestic B2B invoice; alternatively, for imports/works received by a French VAT-registered customer, the parties may apply the reverse charge and the foreign entity need not register unless it undertakes local supply chains or owns a fixed establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime. An SAS is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level under CGI Art. 206. The principal statutory election mechanisms are: (a) option under CGI Art. 239 bis AA allowing a small-qualifying SAS to elect quasi-transparent taxation in the hands of shareholders for a non-renewable period of five fiscal years; (b) election for fiscal integration (régime d'intégration fiscale) under CGI Art. 223 A whereby a parent company owning directly or indirectly at least 95% of the SAS may form a consolidated French tax group; (c) notification/designation mechanics for hybrid mismatch arrangements under the ATAD, requiring filing of Form 2257-SD with DGFiP where the French entity participates in a structured cross-border arrangement involving hybrid entities or financial instruments; and (d) the VAT classification election for mixed supplies — the SAS must apply the predominant-purpose test under CGI Art. 258 to 259-D, with no independent ECI election, and may opt for the OSS regime only for intra-EU B2C digital and distance-sale transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No quantitative turnover threshold triggers registration for an SAS established in France: VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction. The only relevant ceilings are the optional VAT exemption thresholds (franchise en base de TVA): €85,800 per annum for predominant goods activities and €34,400 per annum for predominant services activities (thresholds revalorised every three years by decree), but these exemptions are unavailable in practice where the SAS supplies construction/installation services to VAT-liable customers subject to reverse charge. For a non-established foreign supplier of offshore installation services, registration is not required when the French customer self-accounting for reverse charge applies; for EU distance sales of goods to French consumers, the EU-wide €10,000 threshold for the One-Stop-Shop applies.

France is implementing structured e-invoicing and e-reporting reform. From 1 September 2026, large enterprises must transmit domestic B2B invoices through a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation); SMEs must comply from 1 September 2027. Invoices must respect the EN 16931 semantic standard (Factur-X format minority). Transactions with foreign counterparties, including exports, require e-reporting of transaction and entity data rather than full e-invoicing. Customs exports are declared electronically in the DELTA-G system; France does not apply real-time fiscal stamping or continuous controls to VAT invoices, but DGFiP cross-references e-invoice data, customs declarations, and bank settlement records for audit matching.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies the euro and, as an EU Member State, generally permits free movement of capital and payments under Article 63 TFEU, subject to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering rules, prudential requirements, tax rules, and specified security-related controls. There is no general requirement for prior approval to convert euros or remit dividends, interest, royalties, or sale proceeds abroad. Distributions remain subject to corporate-law requirements, available distributable profits, withholding-tax rules, treaty or EU relief procedures, beneficial-owner documentation, and bank checks. Cross-border payments may be reportable for balance-of-payments or statistical purposes; reporting is not ordinarily an exchange-control approval. The company should confirm transaction-specific reporting and documentation requirements with its bank and French advisers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for ordinary commercial export receipts. Export proceeds may generally be retained in euros or foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank account terms, sanctions screening, tax and accounting obligations, and any applicable statistical reporting. Invoices, customs evidence, contracts, and payment-purpose information should be retained to substantiate receipts and payments.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France’s central bank and participates in the Eurosystem. The Direction générale du Trésor (French Treasury) administers applicable foreign-exchange and financial-relations rules under the Monetary and Financial Code. The European Central Bank and applicable EU law govern relevant monetary and payment-system matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. An EU-authorised electronic money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts and cross-border transfers under its passporting rights, subject to its permitted services, safeguarding arrangements, onboarding policy, and sanctions and AML controls. An EMI account is not necessarily a bank deposit and may not provide a French SAS formation capital-deposit certificate or the full credit, trade-finance, escrow, and letter-of-credit capabilities required for offshore-wind projects. Verify the provider’s authorisation in the relevant official register, account-holder and safeguarding terms, currency coverage, transfer limits, and SWIFT access. Non-EU providers must establish an applicable lawful basis to serve the French company; their services do not displace French and EU compliance obligations.

Non-EU providers must establish an applicable lawful basis to serve the French company; their services do not displace French and EU compliance obligations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400 million (turnover or total assets) triggers mandatory Master File and Local File; also required if related-party transactions exceed €400 million.

Economic substance scope: France applies general anti-abuse rules and substance requirements for tax residency and treaty benefits; no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but requires real economic activity, personnel, and premises to avoid recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% on understated amounts, plus interest; criminal prosecution may result in fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if they participated in or authorized fraudulent acts; civil restitution and potential travel bans.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, penalties up to 100% of tax avoided, and potential nullification of corporate status.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for abusive tax arrangements, including fines and imprisonment for complicity in tax fraud.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for companies, and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; additional regulatory sanctions and license revocation.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned for AML violations; may also face civil liability for damages and travel bans.
