Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the National Institute of Industrial Property (INPI), with registration in the National Business Register (RNE) and, for commercial companies, the competent Commercial Court Registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), principally Articles L.210-1 to L.210-9, L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1; corporate registration provisions in Articles L.123-1 et seq. and R.123-1 et seq.; beneficial-owner filing under Article L.561-46 of the Monetary and Financial Code.

Formation / constitutional filings

Online declaration of incorporation and registration (formality unique), accompanied by the signed SAS articles of association, beneficial-owner declaration, and supporting evidence; the historical M0 form is incorporated into the electronic filing process.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 85 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; EUR 1 is legally sufficient, subject to adequate capitalization. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions are deposited into a bank or qualifying deposit account before registration and released after production of the Kbis extract. Contributions in kind generally require an independent contribution auditor unless a statutory exemption applies.

Funding & unlock path: Before commissioning, obtain municipal building and premises approvals where required, demonstrate fire-compartmentation, alarm, emergency access, extinguishing systems, chemical-storage controls, and evacuation arrangements to the Service départemental d'incendie et de secours (SDIS) and competent municipality. Complete structural load and machinery-installation assessments, electrical and pressure-equipment inspections, occupational-risk assessment, chemical-risk controls, machine guarding, worker training, and workplace documentation under the French Labour Code. Fire-safety clearance is especially required where the premises are an establishment open to the public or otherwise subject to a specific public-safety regime.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; EUR 1 is legally sufficient, subject to adequate capitalization. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions are deposited into a bank or qualifying deposit account before registration and released after production of the Kbis extract. Contributions in kind generally require an independent contribution auditor unless a statutory exemption applies.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – taxation and VAT; Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) – customs and import controls. Official portals: https://www.impots.gouv.fr, https://www.formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, and https://www.douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must (1) determine whether its French activities constitute a fixed place of business or a permanent establishment (PE); (2) if a PE exists, file the French branch registration through the centralized company formalities service (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) providing certified copies of constitutional documents, director identification, power of attorney, and proof of a legal representative in France; DGFiP then assigns a SIREN/SIRET and a French VAT number; (3) if there is no French PE but taxable French supplies are made, the seller must apply for direct VAT registration with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) using the form applicable to non-established taxable persons (No. 1515-SDK), providing the foreign VAT number, bank account details, and the effective date of first taxable supply; (4) customs operations require an EORI number issued by DGDDI for import and export declarations; (5) all statutory registrations must be completed before the first qualifying transaction.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must (1) determine whether its French activities constitute a fixed place of business or a permanent establishment (PE); (2) if a PE exists, file the French branch registration through the centralized company formalities service (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) providing certified copies of constitutional documents, director identification, power of attorney, and proof of a legal representative in France; DGFiP then assigns a SIREN/SIRET and a French VAT number; (3) if there is no French PE but taxable French supplies are made, the seller must apply for direct VAT registration with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) using the form applicable to non-established taxable persons (No. 1515-SDK), providing the foreign VAT number, bank account details, and the effective date of first taxable supply; (4) customs operations require an EORI number issued by DGDDI for import and export declarations; (5) all statutory registrations must be completed before the first qualifying transaction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists in France. An SAS is automatically subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level. Available statutory elections include: (a) tax consolidation ('intégration fiscale') under Article 223 A of the French General Tax Code (CGI) for at least 95%-owned French subsidiaries; (b) VAT grouping ('assujetti unique') under Article 256 C bis CGI; (c) tax-neutral rollover elections for certain corporate reorganisations under Articles 210 A and 210 B CGI; and (d) advance pricing arrangements for cross-border transfer pricing. Hybrid mismatch treatment is mandatory under Article 212 bis CGI and cannot be voluntarily opted out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 for French-established taxable persons; registration is due from the first taxable transaction. For intra-EU distance sales of goods, the pan-EU threshold is EUR 10,000 annual turnover (unless the OSS scheme is selected). The domestic 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption provides a small-business VAT relief below EUR 85,000 (goods) and EUR 37,500 (services), but it is an exemption mechanism rather than a registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies under Article 262 CGI where: (i) goods are physically exported outside the European Union by the supplier or on its behalf, and the export is supported by a customs export declaration accepted by DGDDI; (ii) goods are dispatched to another EU Member State and delivered to a customer holding a valid EU VAT number, with the corresponding recapitulative statement and transport evidence; (iii) services directly connected to exported goods, or international transport services, are supplied to a customer established outside French VAT territory. For sterile examination nitrile gloves, the manufacturer or exporter must hold a valid French or EU VAT number and must retain full commercial and customs evidence demonstrating the movement of goods out of France. For intra-EU supplies to a hospital, distributor or public body, the purchaser's valid EU VAT number and proof of transport are determinative.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAE) accepted by DGDDI
  • Bill of Lading (original or certified copy) or Airway Bill where applicable
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and EUR.1 Movement Certificate or Exporter's Statement if preferential origin is claimed
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 wire evidence showing export proceeds credited to France
  • For intra-EU supplies: customer's valid EU VAT number, delivery note, CMR/CIM contract or equivalent transport proof, and the recapitulative statement (VI/SAF-T) filed with DGFiP

E-invoicing becomes mandatory for domestic B2B transactions in France, phased from 1 September 2026 (large taxpayers) with full implementation for SMEs and ETIs by 1 September 2027. Invoices must be transmitted through certified private platforms (PDPs) or the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation – PPF) using structured commercial formats (UBL, CII, Factur-X). The regime integrates near real-time fiscal clearance of invoice and transaction data to DGFiP. No physical stamping is required, but electronic data must be complete, authentic, and interoperable with French public accounting systems.

French tax authorities may reopen VAT assessments within the statutory limitation period (3 years for timely filed VAT returns; up to 10 years where returns were omitted) and may retrospectively claw back zero-rating benefits where export documentation is absent, incomplete, fraudulent, or inconsistent with actual movement of goods. Reassessment includes unpaid VAT, monthly late interest (0.20%), a 40% error penalty, or a 80% penalty for bad faith and qualified tax fraud; criminal prosecution is possible in serious cases. Customs authorities may impose confiscation and fines under the French Customs Code. Company directors may also be held civilly and personally liable where the fraudulent export scheme is attributable to manifested inaction or deliberate misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains a fully convertible regime for both current and capital accounts under EU harmonization. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are permitted following submission of corporate tax returns, beneficial ownership declarations, and any applicable withholding tax forms. No capital controls apply to legitimate business transactions; however, all FX transactions are subject to French AML/CFT monitoring and reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds is required. Export receipts must be declared to customs authorities via the Douane system for statistical, VAT, and corporate tax purposes. Companies may retain foreign currency earnings in dedicated accounts or convert to euros at prevailing market rates without compelled surrender, subject only to standard transaction monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited viability for offshore EMIs due to strict French regulatory framework administered by the ACPR under PSD2 and the forthcoming MiCA regulation. Cross-border electronic money services targeting French residents require full licensing and may face operational restrictions.
