Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS; the founders freely fix the capital in the statuts (commonly €1 to €1,000). Cash contributions must be fully subscribed and paid up (libérées) to at least 50% at constitution, the balance within five years or as the statuts provide; in-kind contributions are fully released at subscription, with a mandatory appraiser's report (commissaire aux apports) when the in-kind contribution exceeds €30,000 or represents more than half of the share capital, unless unanimously waived. Before registration, the cash capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the depositary's attestation is filed with the RCS, and funds are released only upon presentation of the Kbis.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), under the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Online portal: impots.gouv.fr (professional area 'Espace Professionnel'); corporate e-filing and e-invoicing via 'Chorus Pro' / 'Portail Public de Facturation' (PPF). Customs authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) for export declarations via DELTA/Bacchus.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS: formation through the 'Guichet Unique' (INPI) automatically generates the SIREN/SIRET corporate identifier and assigns a TIN; a VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire, FRXXXXXXXXX) is issued by DGFiP upon confirmation of taxable activity, normally before the first taxable transaction. For a foreign entity without a French establishment: statutory identification is obtained via form 1548 SD ('Cerfa 14464') submitted to the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE), accompanied by certified constitutional documents, a foreign VAT certificate, and a power of attorney; a French fiscal representative is mandatory for non-EU principals; alternatively, EU businesses may register under the One Stop Shop (OSS) for distance sales or rely on the B2B reverse charge. The SAS, being a French incorporated entity, is a French tax resident and registers directly with the SIE in its registered-office department.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law has no domestic check-the-box election; classification is statutory. The SAS is by default subject to corporatation tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) under CGI Art. 206-1. Elections available: (i) option for income tax (IR) under CGI Art. 239 bis AB for new small/medium enterprises (up to 5 fiscal years; max share capital thresholds and at least 25% held by individuals); (ii) equity-holding company option under CGI Art. 239 bis AA; (iii) VAT group (assujetti unique) election under CGI Art. 267 ter for closely linked French entities; (iv) fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under CGI Art. 223 A by a parent holding at least 95% of French-resident subsidiaries; (v) anti-hybrid and ATAD elections regarding controlled foreign company rules, hybrid financial instruments and hybrid entities under CGI Arts. 209, 212, 239 as transposed from EU Directive 2016/1164. No statutory 'partnership-style' pass-through classification is available to an SAS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR - No de minimis turnover threshold exists for businesses established in France: any taxable economic activity triggers immediate mandatory VAT registration. For non-established foreign suppliers, registration is required from the first French taxable supply; however, the EU-wide intra-Community distance-sale threshold of EUR 10,000 (per calendar year, total of all EU B2C distance sales) applies to EU-established businesses under the OSS scheme, while B2B supplies of services follow the customer-location place-of-supply rule with reverse charge (CGI Arts. 259 A-D), and intra-Community acquisitions of goods by a French VAT-registered SAS are taxable in France without any threshold (abattement for occasional acquirers exists only for limited activities).

Phased mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting under the 2023 Finance Act (CGI Art. 289 septies): all French-established businesses (including the SAS) must be able to receive compliant e-invoices from 1 September 2026, and must issue e-invoices for all B2B transactions from 1 September 2027, transmitted through the State-run PPF or accredited dematerialization platforms (PDPs). Accepted formats: Factur-X (hybrid PDF-A3/UBL), UBL 2.1 or CII. Transaction and payment data (e-reporting / DTP) must be submitted to DGFiP simultaneously. No continuous real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is in force (unlike Italy/Hungary); periodic VAT returns (CA3/CA12) and Intrastat filings remain the controlling mechanism, but the e-invoicing mandate creates near-real-time transaction traceability and DGFiP audit access.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility under EU law (Article 63 TFEU). EUR is freely convertible. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted, subject to standard transfer pricing documentation, withholding tax on dividends (default 25%, reduced under applicable tax treaties), and anti-abuse reporting. No prior central bank approval is required for routine commercial or capital transactions. However, transactions exceeding EUR 50,000 with non-resident counterparties must be reported to the BdF via the 'Déclaration des Opérations avec l'Étranger' system for statistical purposes. Large capital movements may trigger AML/CFT reporting under TRACFIN guidelines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds. Under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 and French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier), export receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted at market rates. Exporters must report receipts for balance-of-payments statistics within 20 business days of receipt via the BdF's online portal. No minimum conversion ratio; no statutory timeline for repatriation beyond statistical reporting. For sulfur mineral ore trade, receipts from letter of credit or SWIFT MT103 settlements are freely usable for offshore payments or retained in EUR/USD accounts.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) and Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); foreign exchange policy administered by the Directorate General of the Treasury (DG Trésor) within the Ministry of Economy and Finance. France is a eurozone member; monetary policy is delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Legal for authorized electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (PIs) licensed by the ACPR. Cross-border viability is medium for EUR/SEPA rails; low for USD/commodity trade settlements. EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Qonto can provide multi-currency IBANs but impose transaction size limits (often EUR 1-5 million per transaction) and lack correspondent banking relationships for documentary letters of credit. Offshore digital EMIs not licensed in the EU cannot passport into France. For sulfur ore trade with LC requirements, traditional Tier 1 banks are mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to a French SAS must be wired via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the corporate EUR account. Purpose codes: 'INV' for equity capital, 'LOAN' for shareholder loans. Verification requires the SAS's K-bis (company registration extract), UBO declaration, and board resolution. For non-resident shareholders, the wire must include the shareholder's full name, address, and tax identification number. Amounts exceeding EUR 50,000 must be reported to the BdF within 20 business days. No prior authorization required, but the bank will perform AML screening. Capital can be retained in EUR or converted to USD for trade settlement.Clearing rails for sulfur ore trade settlements: SWIFT MT103 for clean payments, SWIFT MT700/MT701 for commercial letter of credit issuance, and SWIFT MT760 for guarantees. Documentary collections via MT400/MT410. For LC transactions, French banks require full set of shipping documents: bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and inspection certificate. Payments may also be routed via SEPA (EUR) for EU counterparties. USD payments are cleared through correspondent banks (e.g., BNP Paribas New York, Société Générale New York). Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax on taxable profits. A 3.3% social contribution may apply to companies whose corporate income tax exceeds EUR 763,000 and whose annual turnover is at least EUR 7.63 million, producing a maximum combined effective rate of approximately 25.83%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. The rate may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. A qualifying EU parent may obtain exemption where ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, legal-form, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending on a date other than 31 December, the return is generally due within three months after the fiscal year-end. For a 31 December year-end, the return is generally due on the second business day after 1 May of the following year. Electronic payment of instalments and the balance follows the tax administration's published calendar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full French transfer-pricing documentation, including a local file and master file under Articles L13 AA and L13 AB of the French Tax Procedures Code, generally applies where the French entity or qualifying French tax group has annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, or belongs to or is controlled by an entity meeting the statutory threshold. Certain controlled transactions and entities may be subject to documentation, declaration, or information obligations below that threshold. The annual transfer-pricing summary generally uses Form 2257-SD where applicable, and CbC reporting applies to in-scope multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France has no separate Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations regime of general application. Substance is tested through French transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, the abuse-of-law doctrine, the principal-purpose test in treaties, anti-hybrid rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, the general anti-abuse rule, and EU anti-avoidance measures. A French SAS should maintain genuine French management and control, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or operational infrastructure, decision-making records, commercial risk assumption, bank-account control, and evidence that its mineral-trading functions and margins correspond to its actual activities. A conduit, circular-financing, or nominal-residence structure may be denied treaty or participation benefits and may generate reassessment, withholding tax, interest, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties generally ranging from 40% to 80%, and criminal tax-fraud sanctions that can include imprisonment of up to seven years and fines of up to EUR 3 million in aggravated cases, subject to the facts and applicable statutory provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, personal tax penalties, civil contribution claims, management-ban orders, and asset-seizure exposure. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be imposed by judicial authorities in appropriate criminal proceedings.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Profit Diversion

Statutory sanction: Corporate income tax reassessment, withholding-tax exposure, interest, 40% or 80% penalties depending on intent, and specific transfer-pricing documentation penalties. Customs valuation and VAT consequences may also arise where import or export prices are affected.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for deliberate participation, serious management errors, fraudulent accounting, or insolvency-related misconduct. Personal liability is not automatic and requires the applicable statutory or judicial basis.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse, or Beneficial-Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or domestic exemptions, withholding-tax assessment, principal-purpose-test or abuse-of-law adjustment, late-payment interest, and penalties generally up to 80% in cases of fraudulent conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for intentional misrepresentation, fraudulent assistance, or misuse of corporate assets. Civil restitution, management restrictions, and criminal measures may apply; travel bans and imprisonment require judicial proceedings and statutory grounds.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist-Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative or criminal sanctions may include fines, professional restrictions, confiscation, and imprisonment depending on the underlying conduct. Suspicious transaction reporting obligations apply primarily to regulated professionals; the company must nevertheless comply with sanctions, customs, banking, and applicable AML controls.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may incur criminal liability for knowingly facilitating money laundering, sanctions evasion, or terrorist financing. Civil claims, confiscation, management bans, and imprisonment may apply; travel restrictions depend on judicial action.
Economic Sanctions, Export-Control, and Dual-Use Goods

Statutory sanction: Transactions may be blocked or confiscated, licenses may be withdrawn, and criminal fines and imprisonment may apply under French and EU sanctions legislation. Contract invalidity, banking termination, customs seizure, and loss of insurance may also result.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for intentional or negligent sanctions violations, with possible imprisonment, fines, confiscation, management bans, and civil damages. Personal exposure is heightened where red flags were ignored or controls were deliberately bypassed.
False Accounting and Corporate Records

Statutory sanction: Accounting correction, tax reassessment, corporate and criminal penalties, potential auditor notification, and insolvency-related sanctions. Fraudulent distribution of assets or false accounts may constitute a criminal offense.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability, civil damages, director disqualification, management bans, and contribution to insolvency shortfalls where false accounts or mismanagement caused loss.
Withholding-Tax and Dividend Distribution Failure

Statutory sanction: The French company may remain liable for the unpaid withholding tax, plus interest and penalties. Improper distributions may require repayment and may trigger corporate, tax, and criminal consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for deliberate withholding failures, unlawful distributions, or fraudulent insolvency conduct. Civil restitution and management restrictions may apply; imprisonment requires a qualifying criminal offense and judicial determination.
Customs, Import-Valuation, and VAT Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Customs duty and import VAT reassessment, customs penalties, seizure or forfeiture, late-payment interest, and possible criminal prosecution for smuggling or false declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors and customs representatives may face personal liability for intentional customs fraud, fines, confiscation, and imprisonment where criminal thresholds are met.
Failure to Maintain Tax and Transfer-Pricing Records

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may reject deductions, impose an adverse assessment, apply documentation penalties, and use estimated assessment procedures. Missing evidence materially increases audit and penalty risk.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil or criminal exposure where record destruction, concealment, or deliberate obstruction is involved. Ordinary documentation failure generally creates company-level liability unless aggravated conduct is proven.
Insolvency, Asset Stripping, and Director Mismanagement

Statutory sanction: Court-supervised insolvency proceedings, clawback of transactions, personal contribution to all or part of the insolvency shortfall, management bans, and criminal bankruptcy sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution, liability for the insufficiency of assets, asset confiscation, management prohibition, and imprisonment for bankruptcy offenses. Travel bans may be imposed by a court where legally justified.
