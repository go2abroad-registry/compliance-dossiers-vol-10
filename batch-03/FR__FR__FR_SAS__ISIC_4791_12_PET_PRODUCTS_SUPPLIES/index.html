Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum share capital for SAS (art. L.227-1); capital is freely fixed in the Statuts. Cash contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation and deposited in a blocked account with a French credit institution or with the Caisse des Dépôts, evidenced by a bank certificate of funds release included in the filing dossier. Non-cash contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report (commissaire aux apports) if the value exceeds EUR 30,000 or represents more than half of the capital.

Funding & unlock path: Under the French Labour Code, the company must maintain a Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP) covering both head-office and warehouse employees before operations begin; general fire-safety measures (extinguishers, escape routes, periodic checks) and electrical-installation conformity certificates are required in any leased workplace. If the warehouse or showroom is open to the public, the relevant ERP fire-safety registration applies. There are no structural-load permits specific to e-commerce storage below the ICPE warehouse threshold; building permit requirements only apply if the tenant carries out covered internal refurbishments of more than 150 m², in which case a prior declaration is filed with the municipal urbanism authority.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum share capital for SAS (art. L.227-1); capital is freely fixed in the Statuts. Cash contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation and deposited in a blocked account with a French credit institution or with the Caisse des Dépôts, evidenced by a bank certificate of funds release included in the filing dossier. Non-cash contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report (commissaire aux apports) if the value exceeds EUR 30,000 or represents more than half of the capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French Public Finance Directorate; portal: impots.gouv.fr / Pro à Pro; VAT registrations via formalities entreprises (INSEE / guichet unique)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must register for French VAT via the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) before commencing taxable activity, unless they are within the EU OSS regime (in which case they register in the member state of establishment). The process requires submission of certificate of incorporation, proof of foreign tax residence (PE or non-established status), and EORI number if importing. The DGFiP issues a French VAT number (Numéro d'Identification TVA, FR + 11 digits). Failure to register triggers penalties and retrospective VAT assessment from the first taxable transaction.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — French SAS is fully taxable in France at the standard corporate income tax (IS) rate (25% from 2022 onward, reduced rates for small businesses on the first EUR 42,500 of profit). As a tax resident entity, it is subject to worldwide income, with territorial exemptions under tax treaties. There is no pass-through election for SAS. The SAS is not eligible for US-style check-the-box; French law imposes fixed taxation under the Code Général des Impôts (CGI) Art. 206. — Foreign entities must register for French VAT via the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) before commencing taxable activity, unless they are within the EU OSS regime (in which case they register in the member state of establishment). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — French SAS is fully taxable in France at the standard corporate income tax (IS) rate (25% from 2022 onward, reduced rates for small businesses on the first EUR 42,500 of profit). As a tax resident entity, it is subject to worldwide income, with territorial exemptions under tax treaties. There is no pass-through election for SAS. The SAS is not eligible for US-style check-the-box; French law imposes fixed taxation under the Code Général des Impôts (CGI) Art. 206.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under French law. The only relevant elections are (i) VAT option for the franchise en base de TVA (small business exemption) for taxpayers whose turnover is below EUR 85,000 for goods (art. 293 B CGI), which is a derogation and generally not recommended for exports for refund purposes; (ii) Tax consolidation from the 5th year under Article 223 A CGI (integrated fiscal regime); (iii) Election for territoriality of profits under Article 209 B for foreign branches (rare). Hybrid mismatch rules apply under ATAD implementation (CGI Art. 212 bis) to deny deductions in cross-border mismatch situations; no elective mitigation is available except alignment of entity treatment in the counterparty jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for French VAT; any taxable person making taxable supplies in France must register, regardless of turnover, unless the EU OSS scheme applies (distance sales threshold EUR 10,000 across all EU member states for an EU-established business; non-EU businesses have no threshold and must register from the first sale unless OSS immaterial). For a French SAS (a French entity), VAT registration is mandatory from the first transaction, subject to franchise en base exemption threshold (EUR 85,000 for goods, EUR 37,500 for services) which is optional and generally not exercised for export-orientated e-commerce activities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT at 0% applies to exports of goods supplied from France to outside the EU (Article 262-I-1 CGI) and to intra-Community supplies of goods (Article 262-0-bis CGI) provided that the goods are dispatched or transported to another EU member state and the supplier retains proof of transport. For e-commerce retail of physical pet products, the transport must be completed by the seller (or a carrier on his behalf) to a destination outside France but within the EU; for exports outside the EU, the goods must leave the EU customs territory within the statutory period. Pet supplies (pet food and non-veterinary care devices) are standard-rated goods; however, zero-rating is applied strictly on the movement of the goods, not on the nature of the product, except for qualifying animal feedingstuffs which may be eligible for reduced 5.5% rate (art. 278 bis) if classified as feeds – but for export zero-rating, movement criterion governs. Offshore services (e.g., digital services) to non-EU customers are outside the scope of French VAT under Article 259 B CGI (B2B section 196 directive) or subject to the place of supply rule (the customer's location).

  • Customs Export Declaration (document administratif unique / DAU) or EXA for e-commerce shipments, with validated exit confirmation by customs (MRN status)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or air waybill / transport document evidencing physical dispatch from France to a non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank transfer / foreign exchange receipt) proving payment from the non-EU customer (for proof of consideration and customs value consistency)
  • Contract or purchase order from the foreign customer
  • Proof of transport (carrier invoices, logistics tracking with destination proof, CMR notes for road transport)
  • For intra-community supplies: a valid French intra-community declaration (EC lists) and a valid customer VAT registration number validated via VIES

France is implementing a phased mandatory e-invoicing rollout effective 1 September 2026 (deferred from July 2024). All VAT- taxable persons established in France must receive e-invoices through a certified PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) or the public portal (Chorus Pro). Clearance is not real-time fiscal stamping; the French model uses continuous transmission and data reporting (e-reporting) within one business day. API requirements are defined by DGFiP specifications; e-invoices must be formatted in Factur-X, UBL, or CII. For outbound sales to non-French businesses, e-reporting of cross-border invoices is mandatory. Failure to comply results in EUR 15 per invoice penalty (capped EUR 15,000 per year) and additional 5% late filing penalties.

The DGFiP audits export zero-rating claims with high scrutiny, especially in e-commerce where suspected artificial export schemes ('carrousel') are common. If the exporter cannot substantiate the actual physical removal from the EU or the intra-community nature of the transaction, the VAT becomes due at the standard rate, subject to the 20% surcharge for serious inaccuracies (40% for deliberate fraud, 80% for abusive fraud or obstruction) plus interest at 0.20% per month. Clawback is applied retrospectively for up to 3 years (6 years in case of undisclosed activity, 10 years in certain fraud situations, and no time limit for VAT fraud involving criminal offence). In addition, personal liability may be imposed on the SAS legal representative under article L. 267 LPF for tax debts resulting from grossly negligent or fraudulent management. On conviction for VAT fraud (tax evasion), penalties can reach EUR 75,000 and 2 years imprisonment per individual; corporate fines are at EUR 375,000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current-account and capital-account transactions. Under EU primary and secondary law, no exchange-control approval is required for cross-border payments, profit remittances, or capital movements. Profit repatriation is unrestricted, subject only to standard corporate income tax withholding rules, anti-abuse treaty checks, and France's domestic tax-residency substance requirements. The euro is fully legal-tender and freely transferable across the Single Euro Payments Area and global SWIFT network.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists for export receipts in France. There is no prescribed timeline for repatriation and no obligation to convert foreign currency into euros. Exporters may retain foreign currency accounts domestically or abroad, subject to ordinary tax declaration obligations and transfer-pricing documentation if related parties are involved.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem framework under the European Central Bank's monetary authority and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) for investment-screening and balance-of-payments statistical reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions and payment institutions are viable as supplementary collection/settlement rails under the PSD2 passporting regime. Non-EEA EMIs, particularly UK-licensed firms post-Brexit, are not automatically passported and maintain only limited viability as ancillary channels, not substitutes for a French/EU-licensed banking relationship. Use of such EMIs must not obscure the underlying beneficial ownership of funds and must be fully disclosed in the corporate account application to French banks to prevent AML-kyc rejection. Overall feasibility rating: Medium for EU-passported EMIs; Low for offshore non-EU EMIs acting as principal treasury settlement accounts.
