Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office in France under Commercial Code Articles L.123-11 and L.227-1. A domiciliation company may be used if it is authorized under applicable Commercial Code provisions and a compliant domiciliation contract is maintained. A virtual office is not adequate as the sole operational site for manufacture of absorbable polyglycolic-acid braided surgical sutures and needles. The business should maintain suitable manufacturing, quality-control, storage, traceability, and regulatory premises. Medical-device manufacture is subject to Regulation (EU) 2017/745, including manufacturer quality-management, technical-documentation, conformity-assessment, vigilance, post-market-surveillance, and UDI obligations, as applicable. There is no general corporate-law manufacturing licence merely because the entity is an SAS, but product and establishment requirements may involve the competent French and EU authorities, including ANSM responsibilities where applicable.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; VAT and corporate tax registration through the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE).

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS, the principal tax identifier is the SIREN (company registration number) and intra-community VAT number obtained through the INPI 'formalités' portal. A non-resident entity without a permanent establishment must obtain a French TIN for VAT purposes by filing form 2044 (or equivalent European standard form) with the SIE for non-residents, attaching proof of foreign registration, name of local fiscal representative if required, and the expected date and nature of first taxable supply. Registration is mandatory before the first taxable transaction in France.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SAS, the principal tax identifier is the SIREN (company registration number) and intra-community VAT number obtained through the INPI 'formalités' portal. A non-resident entity without a permanent establishment must obtain a French TIN for VAT purposes by filing form 2044 (or equivalent European standard form) with the SIE for non-residents, attaching proof of foreign registration, name of local fiscal representative if required, and the expected date and nature of first taxable supply. Registration is mandatory before the first taxable transaction in France.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a 'check-the-box' entity classification regime. A SAS is ipso facto subject to corporation tax at the entity level. Available elections include: (i) the opt-in for fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A of the French General Tax Code (CGI) for groups with at least 95% ownership, allowing income deferral; (ii) the option for territorial corporations to reduce tax base under territoriality rules; and (iii) in limited cases, an option for VAT posting on a cash basis. No direct hybrid mismatch election exists; France implements ATAD hybrid-mismatch neutralizing rules that override contractual characterizations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No minimum turnover threshold for foreign manufacturers: VAT registration is triggered by any taxable supply of goods situated or distributed in France. For domestic micro-enterprises only, the franchise en base thresholds are €85,000 for goods and €37,500 for services (2025 ceiling). Cross-border distance sales of goods to private individuals within the EU are subject to the €10,000 annual European threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of surgical suture needles to non-EU destinations are exempt from VAT with right to credit (true zero-rating) under Article 262, 1° of the CGI, provided the goods physically leave the European Union and evidence of export is retained. Intra-Community supplies to VAT-registered EU customers are exempt under Article 262, 1° of the CGI with the customer's French intra-community VAT number. Offshore services ancillary to exported goods may be zero-rated where the recipient is established outside the EU and the services directly relate to the exported goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU) with customs office stamp or Electronic Export Accompanying Document (EAD) showing actual exit of the goods
  • Bill of Lading or other international transport document confirming departure from France/EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank statement evidencing international payment in foreign currency)

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing reform (facturation électronique) takes effect 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized enterprises and 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises. All invoices must be transmitted through an approved Platforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public national portal 'Chorus Pro' in a structured XML format (UBL, CII, or Factur-X). This system does not require real-time government fiscal clearance; it operates as a structured-data e-invoicing and transaction-reporting mandate with mandatory electronic transmission, but no digital stamping. Fiscal data must be retained for six years and be made available to DGFiP upon request.

DGFiP may disallow and claw back the zero rate if the exporter fails to prove physical export within 90 days of dispatch, or if goods are re-delivered into the EU. Missing substantiation triggers VAT reassessment at 20%, plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month. Abuse of export exemption is subject to a 40% bad-faith penalty or 80% penalty for fraudulent scheming (manœuvres frauduleuses) under Article 1729 CGI, along with potential criminal sanctions (Article 1741 CGI) of up to €500,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment. Documentary records must be retained for 6 years under audit inquiry.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full liberalization of current and capital account transactions under EU law (Article 63 TFEU) and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. The euro (EUR) is fully convertible; no exchange control licensing is required for routine trade settlements, capital injections, intercompany financing, or profit repatriation. Remittance frictions are documentary, not administrative: banks require valid underlying contracts, invoice/receipt packages, sanctions screening, and Banque de France statistical reporting where monthly cross-border flows per counterpart country exceed EUR 100,000. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are freely remittable after the SAS shareholder meeting resolution, subject to applicable French withholding tax (generally 12.8% under the prélèvement non libératoire plus surcharge, reduced or exempted under tax treaties the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU), and banks will request the distribution resolution, audited financial statements, and proof of full corporate income tax filing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement exists. Since the dismantling of exchange controls in the late 1980s and the full application of EU free movement of capital, France does not mandate conversion, repatriation timelines, or surrender ratios for export receipts. Export proceeds in EUR or any convertible foreign currency may be retained in bank accounts in France or abroad, provided the reporting bank captures the transaction for Banque de France balance-of-payments statistics and the exporter maintains complete customs export declarations and shipping documentation. The only binding obligations are (i) accurate BOP coding of each trade payment, (ii) compliance with EU sanctions/embargoes, and (iii) use of a licensed bank/corridor for settlements in restricted currency jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier), with prudential and AML/CFT supervision coordinated by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution); cross-border balance-of-payments statistics and reporting are administered by Banque de France under Regulation (EU) No 184/2005 and French implementing rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium (restricted). PSD2-passported or ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto and other French digital finance platforms allow remote onboarding, fast SEPA/instant-payment connectivity, and efficient EUR operational collections, making them suitable for day-to-day treasury and sub-threshold disbursements. However, their cross-border trade viability is constrained: EMIs generally lack a full banking license, cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, may not maintain direct SWIFT MT103 correspondent relationships for high-value third-country wires, and are legally required to safeguard client funds in segregated low-risk accounts under Articles L.526-1 et seq. of the Code Monétaire et Financier. Consequently, an EMI can act only as a front-end liquidity tool; the statutory trade clearing for surgical-suture exports must be executed through a Tier-1 bank with full trade-finance and documentary-credit capabilities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection for the SAS is routed via SWIFT MT103 wire from the non-resident subscriber's bank to the blocked capital-dedicated account or the capital escrow account opened by the notary/bank in the exact legal name of the French entity. The bank requires the notarial subscription and payment certificate (attestation de souscription et de versement du capital) or blocked-funds attestation issued by the depositary institution; the payment purpose field must expressly identify 'share capital contribution / direct investment – equity (apport en capital social)' using the applicable French balance-of-payments reporting code under the Banque de France nomenclature, and the transfer must cite the RCS identification of the SAS. The receiving bank verifies the share-increase or incorporation deed, applies sanctions and UBO screening, confirms that all remitters/payees match documented shareholders, and files the monthly statistical declaration where the country-specific monthly aggregate exceeds EUR 100,000. Upon registration of the SAS and issuance of the RCS/Kbis extract, the blocked funds are released to the operational account and the treasury can activate trade-finance related credit lines.Outbound trade settlements for raw-material procurement (e.g., polyglycolic acid-based polymer, surgical needle blanks, packaging components) and delivered finished surgical sutures are cleared over four complementary rails: (i) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for direct telegraphic-transfer disbursements against verified invoices; (ii) SWIFT MT700-series documentary letters of credit (sight or usance, with optional confirmation from a third-country correspondent) for high-value export batches where payment-security against bills of lading and insurance certificates is contractually required; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT/instant SCT) for intra-EEA EUR payments below the documentary-credit threshold; and (iv) bank-payment obligations or house clearing for strategic distributor corridors with high transaction frequency. Every outbound payment must be mapped to the correct Banque de France BOP goods/services code, underpinned by the commercial invoice, export declaration, customs entry, and transport documents, vetted against EU/RU sanctions lists and embargoed jurisdictions, and logged for audit trail and bank suspicious-transaction reporting thresholds.Outright deliverable FX forwards ('change à terme') in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CNY with ACPR-licensed credit institutions under ISDA or French COSSI master agreements, tenors aligned with trade-payable maturities.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for non-convertible or non-deliverable trade currencies (e.g., INR, BRL, KRW) executed as OTC derivatives with a bank licensed by the ACPR and within documented risk-mitigation limits.Plain-vanilla FX options (purchased puts/calls) covering USD-denominated procurement exposure for surgical raw materials.Cross-currency swaps (currency and interest-rate swaps) hedging matched-funding of medium-term cross-border trade-finance facilities or intercompany loans.Embedded FX forwards attached to documentary letters of credit, allowing automatic conversion of LC proceeds into EUR at maturity with the issuing or nominated bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50 million (annual turnover or balance sheet total) for the French entity to trigger mandatory full transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File); a simplified transfer pricing documentation requirement applies to entities with turnover below EUR 50 million but with intra-group cross-border flows exceeding EUR 500,000; documentation must be submitted in French or English and be available within 30 days of a tax authority request.

Economic substance scope: Applicability of Economic Substance Regulations (ESR): France does not apply formal Economic Substance Regulations per se; however, the French abuse of law doctrine (abus de droit) and the general anti-abuse rule under Article 155 A CGI require the French PE or SAS to demonstrate actual operational substance, including the presence of staff in France, decision-making authority, risk-bearing capacity, and substantive inventory levels for suture manufacturing; shell companies without operational substance are exposed to recharacterization, denial of deductions, and 80% penalty for artificial arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude Fiscale

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI: imprisonment of up to 5 years and fine of EUR 500,000 (increased to EUR 3,000,000 where funds were laundered through a foreign account); the fine may be doubled and the taxpayer may be required to post penal sureties; administrative penalty of 80% of the tax avoided under abusive practices, plus late payment interest and formal penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and decision-makers face personal criminal liability for fraudulent filing; corporate veil piercing is facilitated by French law where fraudulent intent is proven; directors may be banned from managing a company for up to 15 years, subjected to travel bans, deprived of civil and family rights for up to 10 years, and be ordered to pay restitution of unduly shifted profits; additional civil liability under Article 1843-5 of the French Civil Code may attach to the SAS president and general manager, and company assets may be frozen.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Under the abuse of law procedure, the tax administration disregards the arrangement, taxes the underlying profits at the shareholder or parent level, imposes an 80% penalty under Article 1729 CGI, and may seek cancellation of any obtained DTA benefits; additionally, all management fees and royalty payments become non-deductible.

Director personal liability: Officers and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax under the abuse of right doctrine; travel bans may be imposed pending judicial investigation; directors face a civil tort claim for damages by the French State and potential corporate dissolution proceedings for lack of real seat.
Withholding Tax Non-Collection

Statutory sanction: The payer is personally liable for the unremitted withholding tax and is assessed the full 25% amount plus 10% late-payment fine and monthly 0.7% interest; criminal liability under Article 1753 CGI for intentional failure to deduct withholding tax may arise, carrying imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 45,000.

Director personal liability: The SAS president who signs the withholding declaration is personally liable for all sums due; failure to pay leads to enforcement against personal assets of the responsible director after a notice of lien; judicial travel restrictions may be imposed upon opening a tax prosecution.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the French Monetary and Financial Code: laundering offence (Article 324-1) carries 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 375,000; aggravated laundering where the offence is committed in a professional capacity carries 10 years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine; the competent authority (Tracfin) can freeze bank accounts and assets; the company may be dissolved judicially.

Director personal liability: Directors knowing of the laundering are criminally liable and may be subject to bank confiscation orders, asset freezes, travel bans, and prohibitions from managing commercial companies for up to 15 years; civil restitution of laundered property is ordered.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of EUR 5,000 per undocumented transaction; in cases of deliberate failure to disclose related-party transactions, penalty of 5% of the net amount of the disclosed transactions; French tax authorities may conduct a deemed tax assessment based on the administration's own transfer pricing assumptions and impose a 10% gross-up.

Director personal liability: Directors are not ordinarily personally liable for a corporate TP misfiling unless they were aware of the incompleteness and signed the declaration personally; if the failure is part of a deliberate fraud pattern, personal criminal responsibility under Article 1741 may attach, bringing travel bans and disqualification.
Failure to Appoint French Tax Representative

Statutory sanction: Withholding tax at source is increased to the full statutory rate; the non-resident payee's DTA claim is rejected, and late-payment penalties of 10% are added; the tax administration may issue an immediate collections order to the French payer.

Director personal liability: When the non-resident parent is represented by a French SAS whose president is the payer, the president may be held liable for the unpaid withholding tax plus penalties; travel bans could be imposed during criminal enforcement for non-payment of tax debts exceeding EUR 3,000.
