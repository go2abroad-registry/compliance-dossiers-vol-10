Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent commercial registry is the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), administered by the locally competent commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce). Applications are submitted through the national Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), including Articles L.210-1 to L.210-9, L.227-1 to L.227-20, R.123-1 et seq., and applicable provisions concerning beneficial-owner declarations. The SAS is also subject to the Civil Code provisions on companies and the French Monetary and Financial Code where applicable.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of incorporation and registration formalité submitted through the Guichet unique, supported by the executed SAS articles of association, capital deposit certificate, beneficial-owner declaration, and required identification and premises documents.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital is required for an SAS; EUR 1 is legally sufficient, subject to commercial credibility and banking requirements. For cash contributions, at least 50% of subscribed share capital must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Funds are deposited into a blocked company-formation account, and the bank issues a certificate of deposit. Contributions in kind must be fully paid when subscribed and may require a commissaire aux apports report, subject to statutory exemptions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); web portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; local operational desk: Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); national non-resident VAT registration unit for foreign entities; corporate income tax supervision for large groups: Direction des Grandes Entreprises (DGE); INSEE allocates SIREN/SIRET; e-invoicing transmission hub: Portail Public de Facturation (PPF).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity not established in France must apply for a French VAT identification number before its first taxable transaction by filing the statutory non-resident VAT registration application (form available on impots.gouv.fr, submitted to the competent SIE or the DGFiP national non-resident registration service); INSEE issues SIREN/SIRET for any French permanent establishment, and the intra-Community VAT ID is delivered in the format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN. For a French SAS, incorporation through the guichet unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) automatically generates the SIREN/SIRET and the NAF code 58.21Z (édition de jeux électroniques), following which the SIE activates the French VAT number. OSS (Union and non-Union) registrations for EU B2C digital services are filed electronically on the impots.gouv.fr one-stop-shop portal. Processing typically takes one to three weeks; a non-established foreign business may limit itself to OSS non-Union registration for EU B2C digital sales, but any French establishment, inventory, or B2B wholesale activity requires a full French VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law contains no check-the-box election. The SAS is by default subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on worldwide profits under Article 206-1 CGI. Available elections: (i) the five-year option for personal income tax under Article 239 bis AB CGI for small SAS satisfying the SME criteria (≤50 employees, ≤EUR 10M turnover, capital held by individuals); (ii) French fiscal integration under Article 223 A CGI where a French parent holds at least 95% of the shares; (iii) the VAT One-Stop-Shop (Union and non-Union) schemes under Article 289 A CGI for B2C digital services; (iv) mandatory EU B2B reverse-charge allocation under Article 283-2 CGI (no election); and (v) no hybrid-mismatch election is available: France applies the ATAD anti-hybrid rules in Articles 212 bis and 239 CGI, and foreign check-the-box classifications of the SAS are disregarded for French tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Zero registration threshold for digital services: the SME VAT exemption (franchise en base de TVA, Article 293 B CGI) is excluded for electronically supplied services such as MMORPG content and in-app purchases, so the SAS must register for French VAT from the first taxable euro. For comparison, the 2025 franchise thresholds are EUR 91,900 for supply of goods and EUR 36,800 for services (upper limits EUR 101,000 and EUR 39,100 respectively). For intra-EU B2C digital supplies, the EUR 10,000 Union-wide threshold of Article 59 quinquies CGI applies: below it, French VAT is chargeable on all EU consumer sales; above it, OSS filing applies at the consumer's country rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical exports of goods outside the EU are zero-rated VAT-exempt under Article 262-I-1° CGI provided the exporter proves physical departure from the EU within three months of supply. For the MMORPG activity, which is digital services rather than goods, the applicable framework is the territoriality rules of Articles 259, 259 A to 259 D CGI: (1) B2B digital services supplied to customers established outside France are outside French VAT (reverse charge in the customer's jurisdiction); (2) B2C digital services supplied to consumers outside the EU are outside French VAT; (3) B2C digital services supplied to EU consumers are taxable in the consumer's member state and may be declared under OSS; (4) app-store sales distributed through Apple App Store and Google Play are treated as deemed supplies by the marketplace operators, which collect and remit the VAT, the SAS receiving net-of-VAT proceeds while retaining its own French VAT registration and reporting duties for direct channels.

  • Commercial invoice in original, expressly stating the 0% VAT/exemption basis (Article 289 CGI)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR road consignment note, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank settlement records evidencing FX repatriation of export proceeds
  • Digital-services evidence pack: framework contract, delivery and server logs, customer IP address and BIC/SWIFT country data, and OSS quarterly returns for destination-state-taxed supplies

France does not operate real-time fiscal stamping or transaction-clearance (CTC) approval. Under Articles 289 bis et seq. CGI, mandatory structured e-invoicing (Factur-X, UBL, or CII XML) and e-reporting are phased in: reception of e-invoices becomes mandatory for all enterprises on 1 September 2026; issuance and e-reporting become mandatory for large and mid-sized enterprises on 1 September 2026 and for all enterprises, including the SAS, on 1 September 2027. Invoices and transaction/payment data must be transmitted through an accredited dematerialization partner (PDP) or the State PPF via authenticated API flows; no digital stamp is affixed, but DGFiP receives structured taxable data in near-real time, enabling automated cross-checking of B2B, B2C, and export transactions.

Unjustified export zero-rating or failure to produce the documentary proof bundle triggers immediate VAT repayment demand with retrospective clawback, late interest at 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI), and statutory penalties: 10% for late filing, 40% for deliberate inaccuracy (manvaise foi), and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres (Articles 1728 and 1729 CGI). Criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI may result in fines up to EUR 750,000 (EUR 3 million with aggravating circumstances) and imprisonment of up to five years (seven years with aggravating circumstances). The DGFiP may reassess within three years (Articles L. 169 and L. 180 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales), extended to six years for undisclosed activities and ten years for unreported foreign assets or receivables (Articles L. 176 and L. 180 B LPF). VAT refund claims (crédit de TVA / remboursement) may be suspended pending complete substantiation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France observes full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement and Articles 63-66 TFEU. EUR-denominated operations within France are unrestricted, and non-EUR accounts (USD, JPY, KRW) can be opened freely at French banks. Capital-account formalities exist only for inbound foreign investment in sensitive sectors (defence, cybersecurity, dual-use, critical infrastructure and certain AI/R&D services) under Decrees 2019-1590 and 2023-1294; electronic game publishing (ISIC 5820) is not a regulated sector, so no prior IEF (Investissements Étrangers en France) approval is required for capital injection into the SAS. Profit remittance is not subject to foreign-exchange approval: the SAS may distribute dividends freely in EUR or foreign currency. The principal friction is tax: outbound dividends to corporate non-residents are normally subject to French withholding tax at 25% (Article 119 bis 2 CGI), reduced to 0% for a qualifying EU/EEA parent under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (minimum 10% holding and 2-year minimum holding period), or reduced under an applicable tax treaty; the bank will require the shareholder's certificate of tax residence and the SAS distribution resolution to apply the correct treaty rate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or repatriation requirement exists. France abolished mandatory export-receipt surrender and conversion obligations in 1990, and as a euro-area member there is no central-bank rule requiring the SAS to convert USD or other foreign-currency proceeds into EUR, nor any statutory deadline for deposit of export receipts inside France. Proceeds may be held in multi-currency accounts in France or with an EU/EEA bank, or reinvested abroad, subject only to the corporate treasury policies of the SAS and the relevant tax rules. A statistical monitoring obligation remains under the Banque de France balance-of-payments framework: each cross-border payment of EUR 12,500 or more (and each remittance between a French resident and a non-resident) is reported by the client's bank using the client's stated purpose code; the SAS is legally required only to identify the nature of the transaction accurately on the payment order.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), the French central bank and member of the Eurosystem/European Central Bank. Banking and e-money institutions are supervised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) holding policy authority for foreign-exchange regulation and the screening of inward foreign direct investment.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for French-licensed challenger banks (Qonto, Shine) as an operational account for an early-stage SAS; Medium for EEA-passported EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) as secondary treasury rails; Low for offshore or non-EEA EMIs as the principal treasury bank. Legal constraints: an EMI incorporated outside the EEA cannot provide payment services or hold client funds in France without a full ACPR licence; an EEA EMI may only passport its licence into France under PSD2. The French Treasury (DGFiP) routes tax payments through French-regulated credit institutions, and the SAS's main corporate tax and VAT filings (via EBICS with ISO 20022) require an established French bank. An offshore EMI cannot provide a locally clearing French IBAN on Target-2/SEPA, cannot issue the required French BIC for French treasury operations, and is not accepted for cash-management arrangements. Recommended blueprint: principal operating account at a Tier-1 French bank, with EMIs used only for card programmes, vendor FX sweeps and ancillary collection of platform payouts.

Recommended blueprint: principal operating account at a Tier-1 French bank, with EMIs used only for card programmes, vendor FX sweeps and ancillary collection of platform payouts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File required if annual turnover or gross assets exceed €400 million; simplified documentation (Form 2257) required if turnover > €50 million and related-party transactions > €100,000.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but applies general anti-abuse rules (e.g., Article 155-A CGI) and substance requirements for tax residency and treaty benefits. A French entity must have real economic activity (employees, premises, decision-making) to avoid challenges under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in tax treaties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 80% of the evaded tax (if intentional) plus interest at 0.5% per month; criminal fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (Article 1741 CGI).

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for corporate tax debts if they participated in fraudulent acts; travel bans and asset freezes possible; imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of taxable income, penalty of 40% (or 80% if abusive) on the adjustment, plus interest; potential criminal charges for tax fraud if intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation may face personal fines and imprisonment; civil liability for damages to the company.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, 40% penalty for lack of substance, and potential criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if the corporate veil is pierced; travel bans and extradition risks in serious cases.
AML / Financial Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for entities, criminal penalties for directors (up to 5 years imprisonment), and confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment and fines; civil liability for damages to third parties.
