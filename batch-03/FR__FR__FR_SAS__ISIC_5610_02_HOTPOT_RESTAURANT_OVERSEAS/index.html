Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent registry is the Registre National des Entreprises maintained by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle, with commercial registration processed through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises and reviewed by the competent Greffe du tribunal de commerce.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, principally Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 et seq. governing the Société par Actions Simplifiée; Articles L.123-1 et seq. governing commercial registration; Articles L.561-46 et seq. governing beneficial ownership disclosure; and Civil Code Articles 1832 et seq. governing companies.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic application for immatriculation of a société commerciale submitted through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, together with the declaration of beneficial owners and supporting formation documents.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 65 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The articles must state the share capital. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must generally be fully paid at subscription. Funds may be deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations and released after registration. The amount excludes the beneficial-owner filing fee, legal-notice publication cost, professional fees, and possible additional registry charges.

Funding & unlock path: The premises must satisfy ERP fire-safety and accessibility requirements, including emergency exits, alarm and extinguishing equipment, evacuation signage, emergency lighting, and safe public occupancy. Gas, electrical, extraction, ventilation, and structural installations must be inspected as applicable. The operator must maintain the DUERP, provide occupational-health arrangements, conduct required worker training, implement hygiene and personal-protective procedures, and retain inspection and maintenance records.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The articles must state the share capital. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must generally be fully paid at subscription. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), including the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and, for non-established foreign businesses, the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE); principal digital portals are impots.gouv.fr and the French business formalities portal formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is incorporated through the single business formalities portal, which transmits registration information to the competent registries and tax authorities. The resulting SIREN identifies the legal entity and the SIRET identifies each establishment. The SAS must file its business creation and tax information with the competent SIE, including its activity, registered office, establishments, bank details, accounting period, and VAT position. The French VAT number is issued by the DGFiP and normally consists of the prefix FR, a two-character control key, and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity without a French establishment that makes taxable transactions in France applies to the SIEE for a French VAT number using the applicable registration documentation, transaction evidence, identity and corporate documents, banking information, and proof of business activity. An EU-established business generally does not need a French fiscal representative; a non-EU-established business generally must appoint an accredited French fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance exemption applies. VAT registration must be completed before taxable activity requiring French VAT collection, and intra-EU operators must also obtain the applicable EU VAT identification status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by default. A qualifying SAS may elect temporary income-tax treatment under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code if statutory conditions are satisfied, including qualifying ownership, activity, turnover, employee, age, and non-listed-company requirements. The election is generally available for a maximum period of five fiscal years and must be notified within the statutory deadline. There is no general United States-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework and can deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences; they do not provide a general elective hybrid classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business using the franchise en base de TVA, the principal annual turnover threshold for services, including restaurant services, is EUR 37,500, subject to the applicable tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250 under current rules. A business exceeding the relevant threshold loses the exemption under the statutory timing rules. A SAS conducting taxable restaurant operations may nevertheless elect or be required to operate under the normal VAT regime, and VAT registration can be required from the first taxable transaction where the franchise regime is unavailable or not applicable. Reduced VAT rates may apply to qualifying food and non-alcoholic beverage sales, while restaurant services and alcoholic beverages generally require transaction-level classification rather than automatic application of the 20% rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct, provided the supplier establishes that the goods physically left the EU customs territory and that the export was made by or on behalf of the supplier or the customer in accordance with French VAT rules. Intra-EU supplies may qualify for exemption where the customer has a valid VAT number in another Member State and the goods are transported to that Member State, supported by the required transport and reporting evidence. Services supplied to a foreign business may be outside the scope of French VAT under place-of-supply rules, but this is not generally a zero-rated export. Restaurant and catering services are normally taxable where physically performed; an overseas brand or customer location does not by itself create export treatment. Export refunds and input VAT recovery require valid invoices, customs evidence, transport evidence, accounting records, and consistency between VAT returns, customs declarations, and commercial documentation.

  • French or EU customs export declaration containing the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Electronic confirmation of exit from the EU customs territory or equivalent customs exit certification
  • Commercial invoice identifying the exporter, customer, goods, values, delivery terms, and destination
  • Packing list and product identification records
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or courier proof of delivery
  • Evidence linking the goods, invoice, customs declaration, and transport record
  • Import or destination-country customs evidence where necessary to corroborate arrival
  • VAT return and accounting records supporting the exemption and input VAT deduction
  • Intra-EU VAT identification and transport evidence where the transaction is an exempt intra-Community supply

France does not currently operate a universal real-time invoice-clearance system for all domestic transactions. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is being phased in: all French-established businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance obligations apply to large and intermediate-sized enterprises from 1 September 2026 and to small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027, subject to legislative and regulatory implementation details. E-invoices must be transmitted through an approved private platform, with structured data reported to the French tax administration. B2C and certain cross-border transaction data are subject to e-reporting requirements. Public-sector invoices are submitted through the Chorus Pro system. Businesses must maintain compliant point-of-sale records, VAT invoices, credit notes, audit trails, and accounting data; there is no general requirement for digital fiscal stamping or prior DGFiP clearance of ordinary restaurant invoices.

France has no general foreign-exchange control rule requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. Export receipts may therefore be held or received through ordinary commercial banking arrangements, subject to anti-money-laundering, sanctions, accounting, and payment-service requirements. If export exemption is unsupported, the DGFiP may reassess output VAT, deny related input VAT recovery, charge late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month, and impose penalties generally ranging from 10% for specified filing or payment failures to 40% for deliberate conduct and up to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights. Serious tax fraud may also trigger criminal sanctions, including imprisonment and substantial fines, with higher limits in aggravated cases. Customs declarations may be corrected and customs debt, import VAT, customs penalties, and other administrative sanctions may be imposed under the Union Customs Code and French law. Refunds obtained through false export evidence may be clawed back, with penalties assessed retrospectively.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full capital account convertibility under EU law and IMF Article VIII obligations. Current account transactions, including profit remittances to non-resident shareholders, face no convertibility restrictions. Large-value transactions exceeding €100,000 require statistical reporting to the Banque de France. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal provided beneficial ownership documentation and source-of-funds verification are maintained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory surrender in France. Companies must file export declarations via the Foreign Trade Single Window (BDF) using the Single Administrative Document (SAD) within statutory timelines. No statutory conversion ratios or surrender obligations apply; proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts. Statistical reporting to the Banque de France is mandatory within 30 days of export.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France
