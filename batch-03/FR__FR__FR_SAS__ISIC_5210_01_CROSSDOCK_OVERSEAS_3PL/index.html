Competent corporate registry

The competent local Trade and Companies Registry (Registre du commerce et des sociétés, RCS), maintained by the clerk's office (greffe) of the territorially competent commercial court; filings are submitted through the national business formalities portal operated by INPI.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), including Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-20 for the société par actions simplifiée (SAS), Articles L. 123-1 et seq. for the Trade and Companies Register (RCS), and Articles L. 561-46 et seq. of the Monetary and Financial Code for beneficial-owner reporting.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company-formation filing through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises, including the signed SAS articles of association (statuts), required declarations, and beneficial-owner filing.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. For cash contributions, at least 50% of subscribed shares must be paid at incorporation; the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Cash must be deposited with an authorized depositary, and a deposit certificate is included in the filing. Deposited funds are generally released to the company after registration. In-kind contributions are fully contributed at formation and may require a contribution auditor unless a statutory exemption applies.

Funding & unlock path: Before opening, confirm building-use and planning compliance, fire-safety measures, emergency access, and any required building or fire-authority inspections. A warehouse open to employees must comply with the Labour Code, including risk assessment and a written occupational risk assessment document (DUERP). Structural load capacity must be verified for the intended racking and stored loads; applicable building, fire, and workplace approvals depend on the facility's use and classification.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — impots.gouv.fr; dedicated 'Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères' (SIEE) for non-established foreign corporations, together with the customs authority DGDDI for import/export VAT suspension regimes.

E-government filing portal

A foreign corporation must file the SIEE registration application (Cerfa number under the 'Demande d'immatriculation au service des impôts des entreprises étrangères' form), accompanied by the foreign VAT/TIN certificate, articles of incorporation, proof of the appointed French tax representative (régime de représentant fiscal) if not established in the EU, and a description of French taxable activities. DGFiP processes the file and issues a French SIREN/SIRET number and a French VAT number (FR + check digits); registration is mandatory before first taxable transaction unless a reverse charge or OSS/IOSS applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (SAS is always subject to corporate income tax — no pass-through eligibility or partnership election). — A foreign corporation must file the SIEE registration application (Cerfa number under the 'Demande d'immatriculation au service des impôts des entreprises étrangères' form), accompanied by the foreign VAT/TIN certificate, articles of incorporation, proof of the appointed French tax representative (régime de représentant fiscal) if not established in the EU, and a description of French taxable activities. DGFiP processes the file and issues a French SIREN/SIRET number and a French VAT number (FR + check digits); registration is mandatory before first taxable transaction unless a reverse charge or OSS/IOSS applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SAS is always subject to corporate income tax — no pass-through eligibility or partnership election).

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime; possible election for tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under CGI Art. 223 A where an SAS holds 95%+ of French subsidiaries; hybrid mismatch and anti-reverse-hybrid rules (ATAD) apply under CGI Art. 209/212, with no elective pass-through mechanism for an SAS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

French-established businesses: mandatory registration when taxable turnover exceeds EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for supplies of services under the 'franchise en base' exemption; non-established foreign businesses have a EUR 0 threshold and must register for French VAT before any taxable supply in France, unless reverse-charge or OSS/IOSS interim rules apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, zero-rating applies under CGI Art. 262-II (and EU VAT Directive Art. 146) when goods are physically dispatched/transported to a third country or intra-EU recipient, with proof of departure; for services, zero-rating applies where the B2B customer belongs outside the EU, resulting in a place of supply outside France; logistics and cross-dock services physically performed in France for foreign B2C customers may remain subject to French VAT if the place of supply is in France.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU, including ENS route and customs exit confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading / airway bill / CMR duly stamped by French or EU customs as having left the EU
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT/MT103 or bank credit advice evidencing payment from the foreign buyer or acquirer) to substantiate FX repatriation.

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting will be phased in from 1 September 2026 (reception for all taxable persons; issuance for large and mid-size businesses) and 1 September 2027 (issuance by SMEs/micro-enterprises). Invoices must be transmitted through certified dematerialisation platforms (PDP/OD) via API in structured Factur-X/UBL formats, and transaction data must be reported to DGFiP's 'Annuaire' and AIF concentration hub. There is no prior fiscal stamping/real-time clearance requirement; compliance is enforced via audit trails.

The exporter must substantiate the physical exit and receipt of consideration; if proof is deficient, DGFiP will claw back the zero-rating, reassess French VAT on the taxed amount, and charge late-payment interest; deliberate misstatements attract a 40% penalty and organised fraud/misrepresentation an 80% penalty; customs fraud may separately trigger fines up to the value of the goods and criminal sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; France does not operate exchange controls, dual exchange rates, transaction licences, or prior central-bank approvals on current-account or capital-account movements between France and other EU/EEA states. Capital flows between France and non-EU jurisdictions are equally liberalized, subject to EU financial sanctions and French foreign-investment screening rules for specifically defined sensitive sectors. Profit remittances by a French SAS (dividends, branch profits, interest and royalties) are not blocked by FX law; the only real hurdles are source-country withholding taxes (e.g., 25% or treaty-reduced on non-EU dividends) and documentary substantiation for the paying bank. A French SAS may hold foreign-currency accounts domestically and, subject to annual tax disclosure, maintain bank accounts abroad. Every qualifying cross-border wire transfer above the applicable surveillance threshold is systematically recorded by the intermediary and reported to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics; practical approval is therefore a bank-compliance matter rather than a central-bank matter.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds. Since full liberalization of the French exchange-control regime, exporters, freight forwarders, and 3PL logistics operators are free to keep their foreign-currency receivables abroad or in French foreign-currency accounts without any euro conversion obligation. The residual duty is statistical: French credit institutions must report to Banque de France all cross-border monetary transactions above the applicable threshold, and French companies may in addition be required to answer Banque de France balance-of-payments surveys. For an overseas warehouse / cross-dock fulfilment company, the underlying export documentation (invoices, customs declarations, warehouse contracts, order-fulfilment reports) must be retained to support each cross-border receipt if the bank is asked to justify a transaction.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, under the coordination of the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; prudential and AML/CFT supervision of banks and payment/e-money institutions is exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions that passport into France under PSD2, notably Airwallex, Qonto, Wise Business, and Revolut Business. These fintechs provide fast API-driven cross-border payouts, foreign-currency merchant settlement, and multi-currency IBAN collection solutions at materially lower cost than Tier-1 incumbent banks. Legal constraints appear for 'offshore' EMIs located outside the EU/EEA: a Singapore, Hong Kong, or UK-only EMI cannot independently hold a French clearing account or maintain a genuine French payment account unless it obtains an EU/EEA licence, passports into France, or operates through an ACPR-authorized agent. Recommended structure: use Airwallex or Qonto for operational payment rails and payout automation, and maintain one Tier-1 French bank account for capital-escrow duties, documentary LC/collections, and contingency liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For pre-incorporation capital, open a temporary French bank account in the name of the 'SAS en formation' and instruct the parent to transfer funds via normal international banking channels. For inbound EUR from EEA, a SEPA Credit Transfer is sufficient; for non-EEA inbound, use SWIFT MT103 with the receiving French bank's BIC and the SAS's IBAN. Use ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI (Equity Investment) for share capital or INTC (Intercompany Payment) for shareholder loans, and place a clear narrative in Field 70: 'Capital subscription - SAS [Name] - transformation of French entity'. The bank will normally request a signed subscription deed, the draft legal status (statuts), UBO list, and a statement of no proceeds from suspicious sources. After the Kbis is issued, funds are released to the operational account. All inbound transfers above the regulatory reporting threshold are automatically captured in Banque de France balance-of-payments statistics; no prior central-bank approval is required for this non-sensitive 3PL activity, but the bank will verify the transaction against the foreign-investment-screen (I 151-3 CMF) only if the investor falls into a sensitive sector or is a foreign state entity.Use SWIFT MT103 for high-frequency supplier payments, freight charges, customs payments, and disbursements to overseas warehouse operators; use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments to EEA beneficiaries. For larger or contractually risky procurement of cross-border inventory, an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) or documentary collection (D/P or D/A) settled through the French bank is permitted and recommended. SWIFT GPI tracking should be enabled for end-to-end visibility. Each outward payment should name the underlying invoice, contract number, and customs reference; payment instructions must contain the beneficiary's full legal name, IBAN/account, and BIC. These fintechs provide fast API-driven cross-border payouts, foreign-currency merchant settlement, and multi-currency IBAN collection solutions at materially lower cost than Tier-1 incumbent banks. Legal constraints appear for 'offshore' EMIs located outside the EU/EEA: a Singapore, Hong Kong, or UK-only EMI cannot independently hold a French clearing account or maintain a genuine French payment account unless it obtains an EU/EEA licence, passports into France, or operates through an ACPR-authorized agent. Recommended structure: use Airwallex or Qonto for operational payment rails and payout automation, and maintain one Tier-1 French bank account for capital-escrow duties, documentary LC/collections, and contingency liquidity.
