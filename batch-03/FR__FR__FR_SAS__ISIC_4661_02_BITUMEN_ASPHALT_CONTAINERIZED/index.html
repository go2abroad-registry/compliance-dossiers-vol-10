Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the French Ministry of Economy and Finance; online service portals include impots.gouv.fr for corporate tax and VAT administration and formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr for company formation and SIREN/SIRET registration via the INPI Guichet Unique.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS owned by foreign entities, incorporation is effected by filing the proposed articles of association with the INPI Guichet Unique through an appointed legal representative and registered office; the registry issues a Kbis extract containing the SIREN/SIRET identification number. The DGFiP then automatically allocates the French intra-community VAT number (Numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) to the established taxable person. A non-established branch or a direct foreign taxable person must apply separately using form P0i/SiREU to the competent non-resident business tax office (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) to obtain a direct VAT ID, along with appointment of a tax representative where required, followed by registration for business rates (CFE) and, if applicable, corporate income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SAS owned by foreign entities, incorporation is effected by filing the proposed articles of association with the INPI Guichet Unique through an appointed legal representative and registered office; the registry issues a Kbis extract containing the SIREN/SIRET identification number. The DGFiP then automatically allocates the French intra-community VAT number (Numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) to the established taxable person. A non-established branch or a direct foreign taxable person must apply separately using form P0i/SiREU to the competent non-resident business tax office (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) to obtain a direct VAT ID, along with appointment of a tax representative where required, followed by registration for business rates (CFE) and, if applicable, corporate income tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French SAS status is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax, and no US-style check-the-box election exists. Available elections include fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under CGI Art. 223 A for holding companies owning at least 95% of French subsidiaries, the option to form a single VAT-group (assujetti unique) with affiliated taxable persons, and elections under applicable double-tax-treaty rules for allocating profits or claiming foreign tax credits. Hybrid mismatch elections are constrained by the EU ATAD regime and French Art. 212 bis CGI denial rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 0 (no minimum threshold for an established French taxable person; registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction). For non-established EU suppliers undertaking distance sales to private consumers, the EU OSS cross-border threshold is EUR 10,000; non-EU suppliers have no threshold and must register for any taxable supply in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under CGI Art. 262-I-1, a supply of goods qualifies for the 0% French export VAT rate only if the goods are physically dispatched or transported from French/EU territory to a non-EU destination, the exporter holds a valid customs export declaration accepted by French Customs (DGDDI), and the exporter retains conclusive evidence that the goods left the EU within the prescribed period. For containerised bitumen and asphalt in drums or bags, the SAS must demonstrate legal title sale to the foreign buyer, container-level transport from the French port of loading to the third-country destination, and coherence between the invoiced quantities, the customs declaration, and the outbound logistics documents. Directly connected offshore services (including freight and handling services for the exported goods) can also be zero-rated under CGII Art. 262-II where the recipient of those services is a non-EU customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/ENS) accepted and released by French Customs, containing the container numbers and the final non-EU destination
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent multimodal CMR/through transport document) evidencing carriage of the containerised bitumen out of the EU to the foreign buyer's port of discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 bank notification proving collection of the export sale proceeds from the overseas buyer

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting for French-established taxable persons under the national reform governed by CGI Art. 289 and implementing circulars. B2B invoices must be issued through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the national public portal (FNFE), using structured data formats such as Factur-X, EDI/UBL, or equivalent, with transaction-level data transmitted to DGFiP for pre-validation and VAT monitoring. No physical fiscal stamping or real-time revenue clearance machine is currently imposed for standard containerised wholesale exports; however, the digital transmission to DGFiP and the PDP of invoice data constitutes mandatory functional fiscal clearance, with interoperability based on the FNFE API specifications.

French Customs (DGDDI) and DGFiP cross-reference export declarations, Bill of Lading data, and VAT refund/zero-ration claims through electronic matching systems; audit triggers include missing transport evidence, shipment to high-risk destinations, margin anomalies, and incomplete FX receipts. An unjustified export zero-rating is retrospectively reversed by issuing a VAT assessment: French VAT at 20% becomes immediately due together with late interest at 0.2% per month, a general insufficiency penalty of 40%, and an 80% increase for bad-faith misconduct or organized fraud. The standard three-year tax recovery period is extended to six years where fraudulent intent is shown, and criminal fiscal prosecution under CGI Art. 1741 can be brought concurrently.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has no general exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions within the framework of EU law. Current-account payments, including imports, exports, freight, insurance, services, dividends, interest, and royalties, are generally freely transferable. Capital movements involving EU and third-country jurisdictions are generally permitted, subject to anti-money-laundering, sanctions, tax, customs, beneficial-ownership, financial-accounting, and statistical-reporting requirements. A French SAS may generally receive foreign investment, maintain foreign-currency accounts, pay non-resident suppliers, repay shareholder loans, and remit dividends after approval of annual accounts, satisfaction of creditor-protection requirements, withholding-tax analysis, and confirmation that distributable profits exist. Banks may require contracts, invoices, customs evidence, board resolutions, shareholder registers, tax residency certificates, transfer-pricing support, and source-of-funds evidence before executing or releasing transfers. Payments involving sanctioned parties, high-risk jurisdictions, opaque ownership, unusual trade routes, over-invoicing, under-invoicing, or unexplained third-party settlement may be rejected or reported. Dividend, interest, royalty, and service-fee remittances remain subject to French tax, treaty, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, and transfer-pricing rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement for exporters to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, no general mandatory conversion ratio, and no general deadline requiring conversion of foreign-currency export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the company's commercial discretion. The company must maintain complete commercial, customs, accounting, VAT, sanctions-screening, and bank-payment records and comply with applicable statistical, tax, customs, and anti-money-laundering reporting obligations. Specific obligations may arise for cash or bearer instruments, regulated goods, sanctions-related transactions, external statistical reporting, customs declarations, or transactions involving restricted jurisdictions. Cross-border cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more entering or leaving the EU require the applicable customs declaration, and French customs or financial-intelligence reporting requirements may apply to suspicious or inadequately documented transactions. Bank-specific cut-off, documentary, and transaction-monitoring controls do not constitute a statutory currency-surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, as the French central bank within the Eurosystem, under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange and international financial regulatory functions are also allocated to the French Treasury Directorate-General and the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution where applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational payments and foreign-currency collection; low as the sole treasury bank for a newly incorporated, foreign-owned French SAS engaged in physical commodity trading. An EU-licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, SEPA transfers, card services, foreign-exchange conversion, and certain cross-border payment services, but it is not necessarily a deposit-taking bank and client-funds safeguarding is not equivalent to deposit insurance. An offshore digital EMI without EU passporting, an EU establishment, or a licensed payment partner cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to the French company on a cross-border basis merely through an application interface. EMIs may restrict commodity trading, high-value international wires, cash-intensive activity, sanctioned corridors, third-party payments, and transactions lacking customs or invoice evidence. Maintain a regulated French or EU bank as the primary account for capital, payroll, taxes, letters of credit, and emergency liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For share capital, use the French SAS capital-deposit account opened in the company's formation name or through the appointed formation professional. Transfer funds from the shareholder's own account by SEPA or SWIFT, identify the payment as share capital subscription, and provide the subscription agreement, shareholder resolution, draft statutes, ownership chart, source-of-funds evidence, and bank transfer confirmation. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. A qualifying small company may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to applicable ownership, turnover and capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. The rate may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. A qualifying EU corporate parent may generally obtain exemption where the statutory ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the return is generally due on the second business day after 1 May. For other year-ends, the return is generally due within three months after the year-end. The electronic filing calendar published by the French tax administration controls the precise date. The final corporate income tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a 31 December year-end, the balance is generally due by 15 May.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full French transfer-pricing documentation generally applies where the French entity's annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets is at least EUR 150 million, or where it belongs to, controls or is controlled by an enterprise meeting the statutory EUR 150 million threshold, subject to the detailed conditions of the French Tax Code. The annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies at the EUR 50 million threshold or where the entity is connected with an enterprise meeting the relevant statutory threshold. Controlled transactions may remain subject to the arm's-length principle and documentation requirements below these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: France has no single standalone economic-substance regulation equivalent to a separate ESR statute. Substance is enforced through the arm's-length principle, beneficial-ownership requirements, the general anti-abuse rule, abuse-of-law doctrine, controlled-foreign-company rules, interest limitation rules, anti-hybrid rules and treaty principal-purpose tests. The SAS should maintain genuine French decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or documented operational infrastructure, commercial risk management, bank-account control, books and records, and evidence that its bitumen procurement, logistics, inventory and customer functions are actually performed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties generally ranging from 40% for deliberate misconduct to 80% for fraudulent conduct or certain abusive arrangements, and potential criminal tax-fraud prosecution. Serious tax fraud may result in imprisonment of up to seven years and a criminal fine of up to EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions and judicial determination.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, civil restitution, personal tax-payment liability for fraudulent or repeated non-compliance under French tax law, management-ban orders and, in appropriate cases, travel restrictions imposed by judicial authorities. Limited liability does not protect intentional fraud or personal participation.
Value Added Tax Fraud and Missing-Trader Fraud

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid VAT, denial of input-tax credit, interest, 40% or 80% penalties depending on conduct, possible joint-liability measures and criminal prosecution for organized or fraudulent VAT schemes.

Director personal liability: Managers who knowingly participate may face criminal fines, imprisonment, civil recovery and management disqualification. Directors may be pursued personally where their conduct caused or facilitated the tax loss.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to arm's-length taxable profit, withholding-tax exposure, interest, documentation-related penalties and, where applicable, a penalty of up to 5% of the profits transferred or the statutory minimum amount, subject to the governing provisions and procedural findings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they deliberately approve sham arrangements, falsify records or organize tax fraud. A mere unsuccessful pricing position, without fraud or gross misconduct, does not ordinarily create automatic personal liability.
Shell Company, Beneficial Ownership and Abuse of Law

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, recharacterization of income, withholding-tax assessment, anti-abuse penalties, interest, potential abuse-of-law surcharge and criminal sanctions where concealment or fraud is established.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims, criminal prosecution, management bans, asset-seizure measures and judicial travel restrictions where they knowingly operate a sham structure or conceal beneficial ownership.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist-Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Regulatory and administrative sanctions, transaction blocking, confiscation, criminal prosecution for money laundering or terrorist financing, fines and imprisonment. Applicable penalties depend on the underlying offense and the person's participation.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be individually prosecuted for knowing facilitation, concealment or negligent failure where a specific statutory duty applies. Corporate status does not shield intentional AML offenses.
Customs, Sanctions and Import-Control Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Customs reassessment, seizure, forfeiture, administrative fines, loss of customs simplifications and criminal sanctions. Sanctions violations may carry substantial fines and imprisonment depending on the measure breached and the conduct involved.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and officers may be personally prosecuted, fined, disqualified or subject to asset restraint where they authorize or knowingly tolerate customs or sanctions violations.
Corporate and Accounting Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Civil damages, accounting penalties, nullity or recovery of unlawful distributions, insolvency clawback, management-liability proceedings, bans on managing companies and criminal sanctions for specified offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered to contribute to all or part of an insolvency shortfall for management faults, prohibited from managing companies, ordered to repay diverted assets and, for serious misconduct, imprisoned or fined.
