UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Primary AML/CFT rules are contained in the French Monetary and Financial Code, Articles L.561-1 to L.561-50 and R.561-1 to R.561-63, implementing EU AML requirements. Beneficial-owner identification and filing for legal entities are governed principally by Articles L.561-46 to L.561-50 and R.561-55 to R.561-63 of that Code, together with Articles 40 and 41 of Decree No. 2021-1097 of 19 August 2021 and relevant provisions of the French Commercial Code. Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office under Articles L.123-11 and following of the French Commercial Code. Commercial domiciliation is permitted through an authorized domiciliation company subject to Articles L.123-11-2 to L.123-11-5 and related regulatory requirements. A virtual office is generally acceptable for registration and corporate correspondence but is weak evidence of commercial substance for a cross-border express courier business. The company should maintain accessible operational records, management availability, customer and carrier contracts, customs and transport documentation, accounting records, and evidence of actual French activity. If the company operates vehicles or performs regulated road transport, a virtual office alone will not satisfy operational, licensing, vehicle, storage, or transport-manager requirements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility is guaranteed for both current and capital account transactions under Article 63 TFEU (free movement of capital) and French law. Inbound capital injection into an SAS and outward profit remittance require no prior exchange-control authorization or visa. Funds must transit through an ACPR-supervised institution or a passported EEA payment institution. Dividend remittances to non-resident shareholders may be subject to the 30% flat tax (prélèvement forfaitaire unique) unless reduced under an applicable double-taxation treaty; this is a tax-stage parameter, not an FX convertibility barrier. Banks apply standard AML source-of-funds and UBO verification before executing any transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio apply in France or the eurozone. The SAS may freely retain export receipts in EUR or any convertible foreign currency. However, every transaction with a non-resident exceeding EUR 15,000 per calendar month must be reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes; this reporting applies to both inbound capital injections and outbound commercial settlements and is mandatory for resident legal entities.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem; prudential supervision is delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution), while exchange-policy interpretation and regulatory implementation rest with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for operating treasury flows. French-licensed EMIs (e.g., Qonto) and EEA EMIs passported into France under PSD2 (e.g., Revolut, Wise) offer multi-currency IBANs and FX accounts within 2-5 business days, with strong API connectivity for courier billing and disbursement automation. Legal constraint: passported EMIs cannot act as escrow agents or issue capital-blocked attestations. Offshore EMIs located outside the EEA (e.g., post-Brexit UK-only entities) cannot passport into France and require a full ACPR e-money licence or French payment-agent registration; their viability for core treasury purposes is Low and they should be used only as secondary convenience rails.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax rate (impôt sur les sociétés).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax on dividends and similar distributions paid to non-residents, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable tax treaties. Qualifying EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief may eliminate withholding where the parent, ownership, minimum holding-period, subject-to-tax, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally within three months after the end of the fiscal year. For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the return is generally due by the second business day after 1 May of the following year. The balance of corporate income tax is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, and by 15 May for a 31 December year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The initial CFE declaration is generally due by 31 December of the year in which the establishment is created. Modification information is generally due by the first business day following 1 May of the relevant year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File documentation is generally mandatory where the French entity or group satisfies the EUR 400 million threshold based on turnover or gross assets, or where the entity is controlled by or controls an entity meeting the statutory threshold. A Country-by-Country Report generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million. The annual transfer pricing summary declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies at a EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, or in specified cases involving significant controlled transactions.

Economic substance scope: France has no separate regime titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless required under French transfer pricing, beneficial-ownership, treaty anti-abuse, general anti-abuse, controlled foreign company, interest limitation, withholding-tax, and permanent-establishment rules. The French SAS should maintain real French decision-making, operational personnel, premises, assets, risk control, books, bank activity, and commercial functions consistent with its courier business. A conduit company, artificial invoicing entity, or entity lacking control over risks may lose treaty benefits, deductions, or tax residency recognition.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax fraud and fraudulent accounting

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties may reach 80% of tax avoided, in addition to assessed tax and late-payment interest. Criminal tax fraud may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of EUR 500,000, with aggravated penalties of up to seven years and EUR 3 million in serious cases, including use of shell entities, foreign accounts, or organized fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, imprisonment, management bans, and civil claims. Courts may impose director liability for tax debts where fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated management failures made collection impossible. Travel restrictions are not automatic and require a competent judicial or administrative measure.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, denial of deductions, recharacterization of distributions, withholding-tax assessment, late-payment interest, and penalties generally ranging from 40% for deliberate conduct to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres. Missing or inadequate documentation may support a transfer pricing adjustment and a specific documentation-related penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil and, where intentional evasion is established, criminal liability. They should approve documented policies, maintain contemporaneous comparables, and ensure that contractual terms reflect actual functions, assets, and risks.
Permanent-establishment and profit-allocation failure

Statutory sanction: French corporate income tax, VAT, payroll and local tax assessments may be imposed on attributable profits, with interest and penalties. Treaty relief may be denied where the factual permanent-establishment conditions are satisfied.

Director personal liability: Managers may face civil liability for intentional misrepresentation and criminal exposure where the structure was designed to conceal a taxable presence. Personal exposure is fact-dependent and is not created solely by a tax assessment.
VAT and customs fraud

Statutory sanction: Repayment of VAT and customs duties, interest, administrative fines, seizure of goods, suspension or loss of customs simplifications, and potentially criminal prosecution for smuggling or fraudulent declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors and customs representatives may be personally prosecuted for intentional false declarations or organized fraud. Civil recovery and professional authorization consequences may apply; imprisonment depends on the specific customs or tax offense.
Shell company and artificial substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, application of withholding tax, reassessment of corporate income tax, disallowance of expenses, penalties for abuse of law or fraudulent manoeuvres, and possible application of anti-conduit and controlled foreign company principles.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for fraudulent management, tax fraud, abuse of corporate assets, or insolvency-related misconduct. Veil piercing is exceptional and requires serious personal misconduct; it is not automatic merely because the company is wholly foreign-owned.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing failure

Statutory sanction: Regulatory sanctions may include warnings, reprimands, fines, business restrictions, withdrawal of authorization, and criminal sanctions for money laundering or terrorist financing. Criminal money laundering may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a EUR 375,000 fine, with aggravated penalties of up to 10 years and EUR 750,000 in specified cases.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and responsible employees may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation, facilitation, or deliberate failure to implement required controls. Asset freezing, confiscation, and judicial travel restrictions may apply where ordered by competent authorities.
Sanctions and export-control breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative freezing, seizure, license restrictions, customs measures, substantial criminal fines, and imprisonment may apply under French, European Union, and applicable international sanctions regimes.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be prosecuted for intentional or negligent sanctions violations, depending on the applicable measure. Corporate authorization and banking relationships may be suspended or terminated.
Withholding-tax and profit-repatriation failure

Statutory sanction: The French company may owe the unwithheld tax, late-payment interest, and applicable penalties, commonly 10% for late payment and up to 40% or 80% for deliberate or fraudulent conduct. Treaty-relief claims may be rejected.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for deliberate non-remittance, fraudulent concealment, or serious management misconduct. Personal liability is not automatic for an ordinary administrative error corrected promptly.
Insolvency and wrongful trading

Statutory sanction: French insolvency courts may impose asset shortfall liability, prohibition from managing companies, bankruptcy-related sanctions, annulment of suspect-period transactions, and criminal penalties for bankruptcy offenses.

Director personal liability: Managers may be ordered to contribute to all or part of the company's insufficiency of assets and may face management bans or imprisonment for fraudulent bankruptcy. A general travel ban is not automatic and requires a judicial order.
Corporate records and beneficial-owner non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Formal notices, fines, tax reassessment, refusal of deductions, judicial orders to file, and criminal sanctions for false accounts or obstruction may apply.

Director personal liability: The president and directors may incur civil liability for losses caused by deficient governance and criminal liability for intentional false statements, obstruction, or misuse of corporate assets.
