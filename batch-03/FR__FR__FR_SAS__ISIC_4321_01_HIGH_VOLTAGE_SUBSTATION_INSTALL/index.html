Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

INPI (Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle) — Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises, which transmits the dossier to the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) and to the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) kept by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title II, Chapter VII: Articles L.227-1 through L.227-24 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); general corporate provisions Articles L.210-1 through L.210-10; Civil Code Articles 1832-1844-17; implementing provisions in Décret n° 67-236 of 23 March 1967 (codified at R.227-1 et seq. and R.210-1 et seq. of the Code de commerce).

Formation / constitutional filings

Dossier unique d'immatriculation consisting of the electronic formalité M0 (Cerfa n° 15679*) filed through the INPI Guichet Unique portal, leading to RNE registration and issuance of the K-bis extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS; the share capital is freely fixed in the articles but must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be released in accordance with the articles; funds paid before registration must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a French credit institution, Caisse des Dépôts or notarial escrow, with release to the President only upon RCS registration. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's report (rapport du commissaire aux apports) unless waived under law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the SDIS (Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours) including the permis de feu programme for hot works and the installation of fire barriers; structural load inspection by a registered bureau de contrôle covering the 500kV GIS hall floors, crane pads, and containment bunds; and occupational clearance delivered by a COFRAC-accredited electrical verifier confirming NF C18-510 habilitations, the plan de prévention per Décret n° 92-158, and emergency rescue coordination for live-voltage work.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for an SAS; the share capital is freely fixed in the articles but must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be released in accordance with the articles; funds paid before registration must be deposited in a blocked escrow account (compte bloqué) with a French credit institution, Caisse des Dépôts or notarial escrow, with release to the President only upon RCS registration. Contributions in kind require a statutory auditor's report (rapport du commissaire aux apports) unless waived under law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial Sovereignty; taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; company registration formalities portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; registry: RCS via Infogreffe/INPI; customs excise and import/export portal: douane.gouv.fr with the DELTA declaration system.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS: (1) execute articles of association (statuts), publish the legal notice and file the incorporation dossier with the RCS through the Guichet des Formalités Entreprises; (2) after registration at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce, INSEE allocates the SIREN, which constitutes the corporate statutory tax ID, and the SIRET for each establishment; (3) DGFIP opens the income-tax/corporate-tax account and assigns the French VAT intra-Community identification number (format FR + two-digit key + SIREN); (4) if the SAS engages in cross-border trade, apply separately to customs for an EORI number (FR + SIREN). For a foreign entity without a French establishment performing taxable operations in France, no small-business VAT exemption is available: the non-EU entity must appoint a French tax representative (intermédiaire fiscal) and file a specific VAT registration application with DGFIP (Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères); EU-resident non-established suppliers may register directly and, where applicable, use OSS/IOSS for distance sales. The SIREN is the official TIN for filings; DGFIP does not issue a separate corporate tax ID.

The SIREN is the official TIN for filings; DGFIP does not issue a separate corporate tax ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SAS is an independent legal entity, not pass-through; it is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) under Article 205 CGI; the French territoriality principle under Article 209 CGI determines the taxable base for profits attributable to French operations; the SAS is a full IS taxpayer and has no transparent classification).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or partnership pass-through election exists for an SAS. The SAS is by legal form subject to corporate-level taxation. Available elections include: (a) French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A et seq. CGI, elected by a French parent holding at least 95% of directly or indirectly held French subsidiaries; (b) parent-subsidiary participation exemption (régime mère-fille) under Articles 145 and 216 CGI, generally 95% exemption for qualifying dividends if the shareholding threshold and holding period are met; (c) OSS/IOSS elections for qualifying distance-selling supplies to final consumers; (d) for eligible infrastructure groups, the option for tax unity with a French head company. EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD) anti-hybrid rules are implemented in France and override contractual or entity classification mismatches; an SAS cannot elect transparent tax treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For established small enterprises, no VAT registration is required until annual taxable turnover exceeds the franchise thresholds under Article 293 B CGI: EUR 85,800 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,300 for supplies of services (amounts periodically adjusted). Non-resident and non-established taxable persons have a zero threshold: they must register for French VAT before making their first taxable supply in France, including installation services for high-voltage substations on French immovable property.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under Article 262-I-1° CGI if the supplier proves by customs and commercial transport records that the goods were actually dispatched or carried out of French/EU customs territory pursuant to a sale, and if the invoice, contract and payment conditions corroborate the export. Intra-EU dispatches to a VAT-registered customer also qualify at 0% under Article 262-I-2° when the customer's valid EU VAT ID is obtained and the recapitulative statement is filed. Services supplied to non-EU customers are outside French VAT where the place of supply is located outside France under the general cross-border B2B rule; however, services relating to French immovable property, including on-site design-build and installation of a high-voltage substation attached to land, are deemed supplied in France and are subject to 20% French VAT regardless of the customer's jurisdiction. The exporter must hold the complete documentary evidence package when filing the VAT return and retain it for the statutory retention period (generally six years).

  • Customs export declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document/DELTA exit confirmation)
  • Bill of Lading or transport waybill (B/L, AWB, CMR or rail consignment note) evidencing physical movement outside France/EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT statement evidencing receivable settlement
  • Commercial invoice and signed export sales contract
  • Packing list and export loading certificate
  • Certificate of non-EU destination or delivery receipt where required by French customs or the destination tax authority

France requires structured B2B e-invoicing on a phased statutory timetable: from 1 September 2026 all large enterprises must be able to receive and issue e-invoices, and from 1 September 2027 small and medium enterprises must issue e-invoices; all domestic B2B invoices must be transmitted through DGFIP-accredited dematerialisation partners (Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires, PDPs) or the public platform (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF). The French system is a data-clearance and tax-analytics model: invoice data is reported to DGFIP in real time through the PDP/PPF network, but France does not apply a per-invoice digital stamp. Accepted structured formats are Factur-X, OASIS UBL and UN/CEFACT CII. Third-party software integrations must use certified API connections, and cross-border B2B transactions are reported through the separate e-reporting obligation. Non-resident suppliers with a French VAT account are also required to transmit their e-invoices/e-reporting data in accordance with this mandate.

The DGFIP and the National Customs Intelligence Directorate (DNRED) cross-check DELTA export exit data against e-invoicing/e-reporting data, recapitulative statements and banking records. An unsupported or fraudulent export zero-rating claim is recharacterised: the VAT is clawed back at 20%, the exported input VAT is disallowed, refunded amounts are recovered and interest at 0.20% per month runs from the due date. Civil penalties are charged under Articles 1728 and 1729 CGI: 40% for non-intentional understatement, 80% for bad faith, abusive tax arrangement (abus de droit) or fraudulent documentation, and up to 100% for organised major fraud. Criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI may result in imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, and the French Code des Douanes may also impose forfeiture or confiscation of the goods and means of transport in cases of customs export fraud.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for ordinary current-account transactions. France has no general exchange-control regime, no general approval requirement for cross-border payments, and no general restriction on repatriation of dividends, interest, royalties, loan principal, or liquidation proceeds. Capital-account transactions are generally permitted, subject to EU sanctions, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing controls, foreign-investment screening, tax rules, corporate-law requirements, beneficial-ownership transparency, and bank-specific documentation. A French SAS may receive foreign equity, shareholder loans, and trade-related funds through regulated payment institutions or banks. Dividend and interest remittances require appropriate corporate approvals, distributable profits or valid debt documentation, withholding-tax analysis, transfer-pricing support where applicable, and evidence of the commercial purpose and beneficial recipient. Banks may delay or reject transfers involving sanctioned jurisdictions, opaque ownership, high-risk intermediaries, unexplained third-party payments, or incomplete source-of-funds evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory requirement for a French company to repatriate export proceeds to France, surrender export receipts to a French bank, convert a prescribed percentage into euros, or complete conversion within a prescribed statutory period. Export receipts may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be received through foreign or European payment accounts, subject to contractual banking terms, accounting requirements, tax reporting, sanctions screening, customs and VAT documentation, and anti-money-laundering controls. Commercial banks may request invoices, transport documents, customs declarations, contracts, delivery evidence, and explanations for payment deviations. Foreign accounts and account income may trigger French tax reporting obligations, including annual reporting where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, within the Eurosystem and subject to the monetary policy framework of the European Central Bank. French foreign-exchange and payment compliance also involves the French Treasury, the Directorate-General of Customs and Indirect Taxes, the French Financial Markets Authority, the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority, and Tracfin for anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine operating payments and collections, but not a complete substitute for a French relationship bank. An electronic-money institution or payment institution licensed in France or another EEA state may provide payment accounts, SEPA transfers, cards, and certain foreign-currency services under passporting or local authorization. The provider must safeguard customer funds and may impose transaction, jurisdiction, or settlement limits. An offshore provider outside the EEA cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services in France without an applicable authorization, exemption, or licensed intermediary arrangement. A fintech account should not be assumed to satisfy French SAS incorporation-capital deposit requirements, bank guarantees, bid-bond requirements, lender covenants, public-procurement requirements, or commercial-letter-of-credit issuance requirements. Account continuity, safeguarding, insolvency protection, data location, transaction limits, sanctions controls, and access to SWIFT or correspondent banking must be contractually verified.

Request for immediate international payments before the company has established normal operating history or before the bank has completed beneficial-ownership verification

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity contributions through a regulated French or EEA bank to an account held in the SAS's name or, before incorporation, to a qualifying capital-deposit account opened with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts mechanism where applicable. Use the investor's own bank account and identify the transfer as a capital contribution, share subscription, or shareholder loan. For an SAS formation or capital increase, retain the subscription form, shareholder or board resolution, articles or amendment, capital table, deposit certificate, Kbis or filing evidence, and proof of beneficial ownership. For a shareholder loan, retain a signed loan agreement, maturity, interest, repayment terms, transfer-pricing support where relevant, and evidence of the lender's authority. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT cross-border transfer for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions. France does not use a universal statutory foreign-exchange purpose-code system for ordinary corporate wires; provide a clear payment narrative in the transfer reference or SWIFT field 70 and comply with the receiving bank's internal purpose-code requirements. The receiving bank may request MT103 or ISO 20022 payment confirmation, originator and beneficiary information, source-of-funds evidence, and corporate authorizations. Reconcile the receipt to the capital or loan ledger and retain banking evidence for accounting, tax, audit, and AML purposes.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible EUR supplier payments, subject to bank limits and beneficiary verification. Use SWIFT cross-border payments for non-SEPA currencies or counterparties; MT103 remains a commonly recognized customer-credit-transfer format, while banks are progressively migrating to ISO 20022 formats such as pacs.008. For significant equipment purchases, public-sector projects, unfamiliar counterparties, or jurisdictions presenting performance or country risk, use an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit, preferably subject to UCP 600, issued or confirmed by a reputable bank. Require defined presentation documents, including commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, certificate of origin, inspection certificate, insurance document, and installation or acceptance certificate where commercially appropriate. Use bank guarantees, advance-payment guarantees, performance guarantees, and retention guarantees where project contracts require them. Apply four-eyes payment approval, segregation of duties, beneficiary call-back verification, invoice and purchase-order matching, sanctions screening, duplicate-invoice controls, and payment-limit governance. Confirm VAT, withholding-tax, customs, import-control, and beneficial-payee requirements before settlement.Deliverable EUR/foreign-currency forward contracts with an authorized bank, used to hedge documented forecast or committed commercial exposures; apply written treasury mandates, maturity limits, counterparty limits, and underlying-exposure documentation.Non-deliverable forwards for currencies or jurisdictions where local convertibility or delivery is restricted, subject to bank availability, applicable market rules, sanctions screening, documentation of the underlying exposure, and EMIR, MiFID II, and regulatory-reporting requirements where applicable.Currency swaps or cross-currency swaps for recurring funding, shareholder-loan, or long-dated project exposures, subject to ISDA or equivalent master documentation, collateral terms, valuation controls, counterparty credit limits, and applicable EMIR obligations.Plain-vanilla currency options and collars for non-linear protection, subject to suitability, fair-value, premium, accounting, and governance controls; avoid speculative structures unrelated to identifiable business exposures.Foreign-currency current accounts and natural hedging through matching receipts and payments in the same currency, subject to liquidity, concentration, and account-risk limits.For an ordinary French SAS, derivatives should be used for risk reduction rather than proprietary speculation. The company should document the hedged item, hedge ratio, forecast certainty, tenor, counterparty, valuation method, accounting treatment, collateral exposure, and termination authority.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for routine operating payments and collections, but not a complete substitute for a French relationship bank. An electronic-money institution or payment institution licensed in France or another EEA state may provide payment accounts, SEPA transfers, cards, and certain foreign-currency services under passporting or local authorization. The provider must safeguard customer funds and may impose transaction, jurisdiction, or settlement limits. An offshore provider outside the EEA cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services in France without an applicable authorization, exemption, or licensed intermediary arrangement. A fintech account should not be assumed to satisfy French SAS incorporation-capital deposit requirements, bank guarantees, bid-bond requirements, lender covenants, public-procurement requirements, or commercial-letter-of-credit issuance requirements. Account continuity, safeguarding, insolvency protection, data location, transaction limits, sanctions controls, and access to SWIFT or correspondent banking must be contractually verified.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No automatic local-file filing threshold solely based on annual turnover in France. Upon administrative request, a Master File and Local File are mandatory when the controlled group’s consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 400,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting is separately required where group consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000. An SAS below these thresholds should still keep transfer pricing documentation proportionate to the size and risk of its related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the same manner as some offshore centres; nevertheless, concepts of effective place of management, beneficial ownership, the anti-abuse doctrine under Livre des procédures fiscales Article L64, and the BEPS MLI principal-purpose test impose de facto substance requirements. A SAS must maintain premises, employees, real operational decision-making, and valid commercial records to preserve French tax residence and treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and intentional mis-declaration (CGI Art. 1741; LPF Art. L16)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full tax understatement, interest for late payment, and penalty of 40% for bad faith or 80% for fraudulent concealment; criminal conviction carries imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine of EUR 500,000, increased to 7 years and EUR 3,000,000 in organised groups or where an artificial or overseas infrastructure is used to hide income.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs or submits false accounts may be criminally prosecuted, personally ordered to pay restitution, prohibited from managing a company, subjected to travel bans where flight risk exists, and held civilly liable to creditors and the tax administration.
Transfer pricing documentation default and artificial pricing (LPF Arts. L13 AA, L13 B; CGI Art. 238A)

Statutory sanction: The tax administration may re-determine taxable income, reject the claimed deduction, apply market-based profit allocation, demand unpaid tax with monthly interest, and impose surcharges of up to 40% for insufficient declarations or 80% for organised abuse; separate fines can apply for failure to produce transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who suppress documentation, approve sham contracts, or knowingly create unsubstantiated intercompany flows can be pursued for negligent management, required to satisfy unpaid corporate tax liabilities, and face criminal liability as accomplices to tax fraud.
Shell-company structure and treaty abuse (abus de droit; BEPS MLI principal-purpose test)

Statutory sanction: Ignoring the intermediary, taxing the French beneficiary or the foreign corporate shareholder directly on the underlying income, denying treaty benefits, recharacterising the arrangement, and applying the 80% penalty for artificial abuse; the statutory time limit for reassessment can be extended to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise or benefit from the artificial structure may be personally liable for restitution of avoided taxes, held in contempt for non-cooperation, banned from corporate office, prohibited from leaving the jurisdiction, and prosecuted for laundering the fiscal benefits.
AML and beneficial ownership concealment (LCB-FT; Penal Code Arts. 324-1 et seq.)

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is punishable by up to 5 years’ imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine for an individual, increased to 10 years and EUR 750,000 for organised laundering; French AML authorities may also freeze funds, suspend bank transactions, and impose administrative sanctions against the corporate entity and its management.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly directs, structures, or hides suspicious payment flows is personally exposed to criminal penalties, confiscation of assets, travel bans, management bans, and civil claims for restitution; the corporate veil will be pierced where the director is the true actor behind the laundering arrangement.
