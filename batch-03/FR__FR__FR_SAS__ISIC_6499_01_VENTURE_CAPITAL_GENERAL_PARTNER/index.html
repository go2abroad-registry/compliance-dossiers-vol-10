0% VAT rate applies to exports of goods outside the EU and services performed outside France for non-EU clients. Eligibility requires documented proof that the goods are exported or services are performed outside the EU with no domestic consumption benefit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and is subject to the EU single-market framework. Current-account transactions, including payment for services, dividends, interest, royalties, and ordinary trade, are generally freely convertible and transferable, subject to AML, sanctions, tax, accounting, and payment-services controls. Capital-account movements are also generally unrestricted within the EU and with third countries, but banks may require enhanced evidence for foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, fund commitments, distributions, acquisitions, or transactions involving high-risk jurisdictions. There is no general French approval requirement for an SAS to receive foreign capital or remit lawful profits. Profit distributions require distributable profits, valid corporate approvals, satisfaction of French corporate-law and tax requirements, and applicable withholding-tax analysis. Regulated venture-capital activity may require AMF authorization or registration, an authorized alternative investment fund manager, fund-level controls, and segregation of management-company and fund money.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement for an exporter or service provider to surrender foreign-currency export proceeds to a French bank, convert them within a prescribed period, or convert a mandatory percentage into euros. Export proceeds may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank documentation, accounting, tax, sanctions, and AML requirements. Banks may request invoices, contracts, customs evidence, service-delivery evidence, beneficial-owner information, and an economic-purpose explanation. Cash declarations apply to physical cash and certain bearer instruments crossing the EU external border, generally at or above EUR 10,000, under applicable EU and French rules; this does not apply to ordinary bank transfers. Statistical or regulatory reporting may arise through the Banque de France, customs, tax authorities, or the bank, depending on the transaction and reporting framework.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem, together with the European Central Bank for monetary policy and the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty for applicable French foreign-exchange and capital-movement rules. Prudential, market-conduct, payment-services, and investment-management supervision may also involve the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution and the Autorité des Marchés Financiers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ordinary operating payments and low-to-medium for regulated venture-capital treasury. An EU-licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro accounts, payment accounts, SEPA transfers, and certain foreign-currency services through passporting or a French establishment, but it is not equivalent to a French deposit-taking bank and may not provide statutory capital-deposit services, full commercial lending, custody, escrow, or regulated fund-bank functionality. Offshore digital EMIs face correspondent-bank, safeguarding, sanctions, source-of-funds, transaction-limit, and account-closure risks. A French SAS should retain a regulated French or EEA bank as its primary account provider, use an EMI only as a documented secondary payment rail, and confirm that the provider's license covers the relevant payment and foreign-exchange services.

Defense evidence: Detailed regulatory memorandum; AMF authorization, registration, or exemption analysis; AIFM appointment or authorization evidence where applicable; fund-management and investment-advisory agreements; organizational chart; compliance manual; AML/CFT risk assessment; investor onboarding policy; and confirmation that company funds are segregated from fund or investor assets.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. A qualifying small enterprise may access a 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to applicable ownership, turnover, capital and paid-in-capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. The domestic rate may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, or another statutory exemption. The French parent-subsidiary exemption generally requires qualifying ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax and anti-abuse conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on a date other than 31 December, the return is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. For a 31 December year-end, the return is generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the following year. Corporate income tax instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, subject to statutory exceptions. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a 31 December year-end, it is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month or the applicable May deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets generally triggers the full French transfer-pricing documentation obligation for the relevant French entity, subject to statutory scope rules. A master file and local file may be required for qualifying multinational groups and controlled transactions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. French documentation and annual disclosure obligations can also apply below the EUR 150 million threshold in specified circumstances, including a tax authority request or qualifying related-party arrangements.

Economic substance scope: France has no single general economic substance regulation equivalent to certain offshore ESR regimes. Substance is nevertheless assessed through French anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership principles, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, hybrid mismatch rules, principal-purpose and treaty anti-abuse standards, and the general abuse-of-law doctrine. A venture-capital GP SAS should maintain French commercial rationale, qualified personnel, premises or operational capability, decision-making records, risk control, service agreements, fund-management evidence, bank records and contemporaneous valuation support. A conduit entity with no genuine functions, risks or decision-making may lose treaty or deduction benefits and may expose the group to reassessment.
