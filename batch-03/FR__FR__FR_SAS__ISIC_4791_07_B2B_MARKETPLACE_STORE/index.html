Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce) for the registered office, with formalities submitted through the national Guichet unique operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI). The legal entity is registered in the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) and, where commercially applicable, the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.123-1 et seq.; Civil Code provisions on contracts and company formation also apply. The SAS is governed primarily by its articles of association, subject to mandatory statutory rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of creation of a legal entity (formalités de création) submitted through the Guichet unique, supported by the signed SAS articles of association and prescribed corporate, identity, beneficial-owner, premises, and publication evidence.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 32 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS. Capital may be fixed at EUR 1 or another amount stated in the articles. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash capital is deposited into a blocked account with a bank, notary, or authorized deposit institution, which issues a certificate of deposit. Contributions in kind generally require an auditor's report unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied. The stated fee is an approximate USD conversion of the commercial registration charge and excludes legal-publication, beneficial-owner, professional, banking, and advisory costs.

Funding & unlock path: No special industrial clearance is required for a purely office-based marketplace. The operator must maintain workplace health and safety documentation, emergency procedures, fire-risk controls, evacuation arrangements, and required fire equipment. A leased or operated warehouse requires landlord and local fire-safety compliance, appropriate inspections, structural and storage-load verification, electrical and fire-system checks, and any applicable occupational-health assessment. Public-access premises may also require établissement recevant du public (ERP) compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS. Capital may be fixed at EUR 1 or another amount stated in the articles. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash capital is deposited into a blocked account with a bank, notary, or authorized deposit institution, which issues a certificate of deposit. Contributions in kind generally require an auditor's report unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied. The stated fee is an approximate USD conversion of the commercial registration charge and excludes legal-publication, beneficial-owner, professional, banking, and advisory costs.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The undersigned, being the duly appointed President and/or members of the governing body of [Company Name] SAS, resolve that: (1) the Company accepts and approves the subscriptions for the shares specified in the incorporation documents and acknowledges receipt of the required paid-up cash capital; (2) the registered office is established at [full French address]; (3) [name] is appointed President for the term and under the conditions set out in the articles; (4) [names] are appointed, if applicable, as Directeur Général(s); and (5) the President is authorized to complete all filing, publication, banking, tax, and registry formalities.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for ordinary electronic B2B marketplace operation without manufacturing, regulated goods, or an industrial installation. If the company operates a warehouse or other potentially classified installation, the relevant authority is the Prefecture of the department, supported by the Direction Régionale de l'Environnement, de l'Aménagement et du Logement (DREAL).

Category: No general manufacturing or industrial operating permit is required for a standard B2B electronic marketplace. The company must comply with French e-commerce, consumer, commercial, tax, data-protection, product-safety, and distance-selling requirements. A warehouse may require an Installation Classée pour la Protection de l'Environnement (ICPE) declaration, registration, or authorization depending on its activities and thresholds.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – official taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; business registration portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

For foreign entities, mandatory French VAT registration is handled by the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) via form CERFA n° 10643*04 / foreign-company VAT application, submitted through the DGFiP foreign taxpayer unit (Pôle Etranger). Non-EU entities must appoint a tax representative in France unless an EU VAT number is already held; required documents include constitutional documents, power of attorney, identity evidence, proof of foreign registration, and anticipated French business description. Upon complete filing the entity receives an intra-community VAT identification number (FR-NNN…) and, where applicable, a SIREN/SIRET for fixed establishments; for designated cross-border e-commerce sellers, simplified registration via the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) non-established registration interface on impots.gouv.fr is available.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – a French Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) is irrebuttably treated as a separate taxable entity subject to impôt sur les sociétés (IS) at the standard corporate rate; no check-the-box pass-through election exists. Territorial_Scope: French corporate income tax applies only to profits attributable to a French permanent establishment or, absent PE, to French-source revenues; no metropolitan France Tax_Exempt_Zone status applies. — For foreign entities, mandatory French VAT registration is handled by the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) via form CERFA n° 10643*04 / foreign-company VAT application, submitted through the DGFiP foreign taxpayer unit (Pôle Etranger). Non-EU entities must appoint a tax representative in France unless an EU VAT number is already held; required documents include constitutional documents, power of attorney, identity evidence, proof of foreign registration, and anticipated French business description. Upon complete filing the entity receives an intra-community VAT identification number (FR-NNN…) and, where applicable, a SIREN/SIRET for fixed establishments; for designated cross-border e-commerce sellers, simplified registration via the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) non-established registration interface on impots.gouv.fr is available.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – a French Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) is irrebuttably treated as a separate taxable entity subject to impôt sur les sociétés (IS) at the standard corporate rate; no check-the-box pass-through election exists. Territorial_Scope: French corporate income tax applies only to profits attributable to a French permanent establishment or, absent PE, to French-source revenues; no metropolitan France Tax_Exempt_Zone status applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in France. Available elections include: (i) option for French tax consolidation under the régime de l'intégration fiscale (Article 223 A ff. CGI) by a parent holding 95% of a French subsidiary; (ii) application of parent-subsidiary participation exemption (régime mère-fille) with 95% dividend exemption upon making the necessary election; (iii) election to apply worldwide taxation (régime mondial) for foreign permanent establishments under Article 209-II CGI; (iv) for partnerships and similar transparent vehicles, opt-in to corporate income tax under Article 206/239; and (v) anti-hybrid mismatch notifications / neutrality elections under Articles 212, 212-bis CGI as implementing ATAD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic small-business franchise thresholds: EUR 91,900 for supplies of goods and EUR 36,800 for supplies of services per calendar year (slightly higher reduced-reality thresholds apply); foreign established persons have no franchise exemption and must register from the first taxable French transaction; distance-selling / OSS cross-border threshold for EU arrivals of goods is EUR 10,000; certain B2B marketplace facilitation rules require VAT registration irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération) applies to the export of movable tangible goods physically dispatched or transported out of the European Union by or on behalf of an established French VAT-taxable person; eligibility requires actual exit from the EU, a bona fide commercial sale or stock transfer, and the exporter to hold proof of dispatch within 90 days of delivery/availability; export of services is generally subject to the B2B place-of-supply rule and is outside French VAT when the customer is established outside the EU, provided the services are not physically linked to French immovable property; zero-rating is denied for goods merely placed in bonded or customs warehouse arrangements without subsequent export.

  • Customs export declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation) validated through DELTA-XI / ESL
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR note
  • Commercial invoice reflecting the exported goods and incoterms
  • Export packing list
  • Customs authorisation / exit confirmation (acquit of exit)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT bank credit proof evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Domestic B2B e-invoicing is mandatory phased from 1 September 2026 (large enterprises) and 1 September 2027 (SMEs), with structured data (UBL/CII/FACTUR-X) transmitted to the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or an accredited private plateform; B2C transactions require e-reporting through PPF; no real-time continuous fiscal transaction clearance for retail VAT exists as of the current date, but imports and exports must be electronically declared through the DGFiP/Douane DELTA framework; goods sold via marketplaces may trigger customs and e-commerce import VAT clearance obligations on the marketplace as deemed VAT collector.

The French tax administration under Article 272, 283, 257 and 268 CGI audits export substantiation during statutory limitation period (3 years, extended to 6 years for concealment); unjustified or fraudulent zero-rating triggers or re-invoices VAT at 20% plus late payment interest of 0.20% per month, penalties of 40% for deliberate misconduct ('mauvaise foi') or 80% for abuse/defraudation, and possible criminal fines and imprisonment; marketplace operators acting as deemed VAT collectors face joint and several liability for uncollected import VAT where export evidence is falsified or missing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible in both current and capital account transactions. There are no French foreign-exchange controls, no prior authorisations for investment or repatriation, and no restrictions on payments or transfers between residents and non-residents under the EU Treaty free movement of capital. Profit remittance is not subject to FX hurdles, but dividend distributions to non-French shareholders may trigger French withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate, potentially reduced under tax treaties or exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. Banks will require proof of distributable reserves, a director's resolution, beneficial-owner declaration, and applicable tax forms for the remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory provision requiring the surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Export receivables may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, converted at market rates, or used directly for external commercial payments. There is no fixed repatriation timeline and no minimum conversion ratio. French banks must, however, declare cross-border transaction flows to Banque de France for balance-of-payments and monetary statistics, and all transactions remain subject to EU sanctions screening.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting within the Eurosystem, with the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR) and the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) overseeing foreign-exchange policy and balance-of-payments statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High for operational payment accounts, but Low for regulated capital escrow or statutory deposit functions. EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may passport their services into France and can support corporate treasury accounts, local IBANs, and payment flow automation for a B2B marketplace. Offshore EMIs without an EEA passport and without ACPR registration are not compliant and should not be used for SAS treasury. EMIs cannot grant credit, cannot create money, and are not substitute for a full credit institution when letters of credit, import financing, or large uncertain FX settlements are needed.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Multi-layer offshore shareholding with opaque intermediate holding companies

Root cause: Multi-layer offshore shareholding with opaque intermediate holding companies

Defense evidence: Complete legal ownership chart from ultimate natural persons to FR_SAS; certificate of incorporation and registry extract for each intermediate entity; annual financial statements or tax returns proving economic substance; French beneficial-owner declaration (Déclaration de bénéficiaire effectif); notarised subscription or transfer deeds; explanation of any nominee or fiduciary arrangement.
Non-resident directors or beneficial owners resident in a high-risk or tax-cooperative-review jurisdiction

Root cause: Non-resident directors or beneficial owners resident in a high-risk or tax-cooperative-review jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Valid passport and proof of residential address of each individual; official tax-residence certificate; source-of-wealth document; regulated-bank reference letter covering the previous three years; professional qualification and business-history summary; details of the underlying commercial purpose in France.
Inconsistent cash-flow pattern versus declared B2B marketplace activity

Root cause: Inconsistent cash-flow pattern versus declared B2B marketplace activity

Defense evidence: Audited or management financial statements of FR_SAS; ERP journal and order-management records; platform seller/supplier contracts; monthly marketplace settlement reports; shipping and customs documents (SAD/ECS); VAT returns; bank statement reconciliation of all incoming and outgoing commercial payments.
Politically Exposed Person (PEP) or adverse-media profile on director, shareholder, or beneficial owner

Root cause: Politically Exposed Person (PEP) or adverse-media profile on director, shareholder, or beneficial owner

Defense evidence: Enhanced due diligence questionnaire; PEP status declaration and explanation; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds report covering the twelve months before account opening; independent professional letters of reference; results of media and sanctions screening; undertaking to maintain enhanced monitoring and provide annual updates.
Export-control and sanctions exposure in the B2B product portfolio

Root cause: Export-control and sanctions exposure in the B2B product portfolio

Defense evidence: Denied-party screening reports for all counterparties; harmonised-system tariff classification of traded goods; French customs export licences or EU general export authorisation memos; end-user/end-use certificates; corporate sanctions compliance policy; transaction-level escalation log and risk-assessment matrix covering embargoed destinations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for an inbound euro equity contribution from a non-French shareholder is a SWIFT MT103 credit to the FR_SAS EUR IBAN, or a SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001.001.09) if the shareholder is located in the EEA. The payment narrative field must clearly state 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION / INCREASE OF CAPITAL' and include the SAS corporate name and SIREN number. The sending bank should use an ISO 20022 purpose code of 'EQUI' (equity purchase) or 'INVS' (investment) if its system permits; the French receiving bank will map the transaction to Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical category for foreign direct investment in equity. Verification package: notarised capital-increase resolution, updated K-bis, shareholder board resolution, written payment instruction from the subscriber, proof of the subscriber's identity and source of funds, and pro-forma compliance with Banque de France BOP reporting requirements.For trade settlement within the SEPA zone, use SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001.001.09) with unique end-to-end reference and an ISO purpose code such as 'SUPP' or 'TRAD'. For non-EEA or foreign-currency disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 with UETR and clear beneficiary bank details; for documentary trade, issue an irrevocable commercial letter of credit by SWIFT MT700 under UCP600, receive confirmations by MT710, and settle drawings via MT752/MT103/MT910.
