Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SAS. The shareholders freely set the capital in the articles; upon formation, all subscribed shares must be fully paid if the capital is a stated amount. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked account at a bank or notary ('certificat de dépôt de fonds') and may not be released until RCS registration is complete. In-kind contributions require a commissaire aux apports valuation report if their value exceeds EUR 7,500 or the aggregate in-kind contributions exceed one-half of the share capital, unless all shareholders unanimously waive the report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance from the Commission de sécurité (departmental fire safety committee), structural survey by a certified contrôleur technique, workplace health and safety clearance by DREETS for any employees, and for public buildings, access restrictions and maintenance licenses must be filed with the Mairie.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SAS. The shareholders freely set the capital in the articles; upon formation, all subscribed shares must be fully paid if the capital is a stated amount. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked account at a bank or notary ('certificat de dépôt de fonds') and may not be released until RCS registration is complete. In-kind contributions require a commissaire aux apports valuation report if their value exceeds EUR 7,500 or the aggregate in-kind contributions exceed one-half of the share capital, unless all shareholders unanimously waive the report.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy and Finance; national tax portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; business formalities portal: guichet-unique.inpi.fr.

E-government filing portal

Foreign investors form a Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) before a notary or under private deed, then file a single digital application on the Guichet Unique des Entreprises (INPI). INSEE issues the SIREN (9 digits) and SIRET (14 digits); DGFiP automatically assigns the French VAT number (FR + SIREN + 2-digit key) and corporate tax file. Non-resident entities without a French establishment may register directly with the DGFiP SIE/SIP for VAT; a fiscal representative is required only in limited cases for non-EU established taxable persons not covered by mutual assistance. SIIC regime requires listing on a French regulated market and AMF approval.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the standard 25% rate on worldwide profits, subject to foreign tax credit and participation-exemption rules. Pass-through treatment is not default; a five-year-old-or-less SAS may elect partnership tax regime under Art. 239 bis AB CGI. Territorial scope: France taxes worldwide profits of French-established companies; foreign branches may benefit from treaty exemptions. Tax_Exempt_Zone: not applicable domestically. SIIC election possible if listed and conditions met: exemption from corporate income tax on qualifying rental income and capital gains, subject to 95% distribution obligation and exit tax on certain gains.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box. Available French elections include: (i) SIIC regime under Arts. 208 C et seq. CGI for listed real estate investment companies; (ii) tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Arts. 223 A et seq. CGI for 95%-owned French subsidiaries; (iii) VAT option for commercial leases under Art. 260-2° CGI; (iv) partnership tax regime for qualifying SAS under Art. 239 bis AB CGI; (v) hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD transposed in Arts. 205 B, 209 B CGI and EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold for non-established taxable persons making taxable supplies in France, for intra-Community acquisitions, or for B2B services subject to reverse charge. Domestic small-business franchise en base thresholds: EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services (2025, subject to annual adjustment). Commercial property leasing may be VAT-exempt unless the lessor opts for VAT; residential leasing is exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT under Art. 262-I CGI where goods are exported outside the EU and the exporter holds proof of export within the statutory period. Services: 0% VAT or out-of-scope treatment for services directly related to exports/imports, international transport, and certain intra-Community operations under Art. 262-II CGI; B2B services to non-EU customers are generally outside the scope of French VAT under place-of-supply rules (Art. 259 CGI). Export zero-rating requires the supplier to be a taxable person acting as such, valid commercial invoice, and evidence that the goods left the EU or services were supplied to a non-EU customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/EX-A) with MRN and customs exit certification
  • Commercial invoice showing VAT exemption and exporter/customer details
  • Transport document: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR, or rail/road consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence for export proceeds
  • Proof of exit from EU customs territory and, for services, contract and customer non-EU establishment evidence

B2G e-invoicing via Chorus Pro is mandatory. B2B e-invoicing reform: from September 2026, all taxable persons must be able to receive electronic invoices and large/mid-sized companies must issue them; from September 2027, all companies must issue electronic invoices. Transmission occurs through certified Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires (PDPs) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF), with invoice metadata and e-reporting to the tax authority. No central real-time clearance stamp equivalent to Italy; compliance requires API connectivity, structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII), and lifecycle status reporting.

France has no general exchange-control repatriation deadline for export proceeds; foreign exchange is free, but contractual and transfer-pricing documentation applies. If export zero-rating is denied, DGFiP reassesses French VAT plus 10% late interest; intentional breach triggers a 40% penalty, and fraudulent export VAT claims can trigger an 80% penalty under Art. 1729 CGI. Criminal sanctions under Art. 1741 CGI include up to 5 years imprisonment and fines up to EUR 500,000 (or five times the fraud amount). Retrospective audits typically cover six years for undeclared VAT, with clawback of refunded VAT and possible joint liability for directors.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France provides full capital and current account convertibility for EUR and all major currencies by virtue of Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the EU Capital Movements Directive. There is no general exchange control approval regime for euro-denominated transactions. Profit remittance in the form of dividends is freely permitted for a French SAS, subject to the legality of the distribution under corporate law, the absence of over-leveraging or insolvency, the identification of ultimate beneficial owners, and the filing of the French dividend withholding tax reporting forms (e.g., n°2561-FU) for any payments to non-residents. The effective hurdle is documentary: the paying bank will require the SAS articles, board or shareholder decision authorising distribution, and, if treaty relief is claimed, a valid French tax residence certificate from the recipient and adequate substance documentation. Since the SAS is legally presumptively established by foreign shareholders, additional transparency declarations may be requested under the French 'Monetary and Financial Code' (Articles L.561-1 et seq.) for anti-money laundering purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender of export receipts into EUR. For export settlements received in EUR within the euro area, there are no surrender or repatriation accounts. For non-EUR export proceeds, the exporter must ensure that receivables are settled in a commercial bank account in France or an authorised foreign account, and must comply with the French Balance of Payments (FECI) monthly reporting obligation to Banque de France when cumulative flows exceed the de minimis threshold. Payment duration rules under the French Commercial Code (Code de commerce) ordinarily cap payment terms for commercial goods at 30 days after end of month or 45 days after invoice date, and 150 days for exports of capital goods or certain complex purchases, but these rules concern payment periods rather than FX conversion. The SAS must therefore maintain a proper receivable ageing policy and instruct debtors to remit funds with accurate payment references to avoid classification as undeclared capital inflow.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting within the Eurosystem and under the monetary policy framework of the European Central Bank (ECB); foreign exchange policy is additionally coordinated with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not permitted to provide the core corporate treasury settlement for a French SAS as a substitute for a bank account held with a licensed credit institution. The use of a non-French EMI is categorised as a low-to-medium viability ancillary payment channel only. Under French banking monopoly rules (Articles L.311-1 and L.311-9 of the Monetary and Financial Code), the routine receipt of commercial funds and the keeping of corporate accounts for an SAS require a licence as a credit institution in France or a valid EU passporting arrangement via an authorised agent. A purely offshore EMI without an ACPR or ECB-recognised passport will fail the KYC and documentary sealing requirements necessary for the REIT commercial model. If an EMI is used, it should be exclusively for small-value outbound disbursement, must not become the principal account for dividend distribution wire, and should be ring-fenced from any account holding source funds whose ultimate origin is non-EU.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The SAS should designate its French operational account as the sole receiving account for inbound capital injections. Non-EUR equity or shareholder loan flows should be initiated via SWIFT MT103 or SEPA-based euro transfer with the payment purpose expressed as 'Equity investment / share subscription' or 'Shareholder loan disbursement', accompanied by the corporate resolution and a bank KYC package. For euro transactions within the euro area, use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) with mandatory IBAN and payment reference 'SCRA' or contractual file number. The French banking counterparty will internally report the poste 'INVESTISSEMENT / CAPITAL SOCIAL' under the FECI balance-of-payments declaration when the transaction is completed. For any investment from non-EU jurisdictions, the bank must be able to verify the legal origin of funds via an apostilled certificate of incorporation, UBO declaration, and source-of-wealth documents before acceptance. A dedicated Capital Escrow Account is recommended for holding subscriptions pending the registration of the capital increase, to avoid commingling with operational rents.Trade disbursements are cleared either by SEPA Credit Transfers within the eurozone or by SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for non-euro payments. For property acquisition and construction payments to third-party sellers, the SAS should use a documented payment instruction with the transaction reference 'Real estate purchase settlement' and, when applicable, an underlying notarised deed. For cross-border trade or vendor financing where documentary control is necessary, a Letter of Credit conforming to UCP600 can be structured as a commercial LC issued by the SAS's French bank in favour of the seller, with payment advised through the seller's bank. For smaller disbursements, ACH-style collections are not commonly used on the French domestic side; French direct debit is 'Prélèvement' under the SEPA Core Direct Debit scheme. Dividend distribution wires must be executed through SWIFT MT103 or SEPA SCT with explicit beneficiary bank details, tax withholding reference, and, if required, a copy of the Form 2561-FU submitted to the tax authority. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are not permitted to provide the core corporate treasury settlement for a French SAS as a substitute for a bank account held with a licensed credit institution. The use of a non-French EMI is categorised as a low-to-medium viability ancillary payment channel only. Under French banking monopoly rules (Articles L.311-1 and L.311-9 of the Monetary and Financial Code), the routine receipt of commercial funds and the keeping of corporate accounts for an SAS require a licence as a credit institution in France or a valid EU passporting arrangement via an authorised agent. A purely offshore EMI without an ACPR or ECB-recognised passport will fail the KYC and documentary sealing requirements necessary for the REIT commercial model. If an EMI is used, it should be exclusively for small-value outbound disbursement, must not become the principal account for dividend distribution wire, and should be ring-fenced from any account holding source funds whose ultimate origin is non-EU.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate recipient, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable tax treaties. A qualifying EU or EEA parent may obtain exemption under the French implementation of the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring a qualifying ownership interest, minimum holding period or commitment, appropriate legal form, subject-to-tax conditions, and anti-abuse compliance. The domestic rate for certain non-corporate recipients may differ.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on a date other than 31 December, the annual return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. For a 31 December year-end, the return is generally due on the second business day after 1 May of the following year. The final tax balance is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, or by 15 May for a 31 December year-end, subject to the applicable electronic payment calendar. Installments are generally due by 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, subject to statutory exceptions and thresholds.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Withholding generally must be declared and paid by the 15th day of the month following the payment month, subject to the specific income category and current electronic-filing rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million: full transfer-pricing documentation obligations generally apply where French turnover excluding tax or gross assets reach at least EUR 150 million, or where statutory related-entity or ownership conditions otherwise bring the taxpayer within the regime. The documentation comprises a master file and local file under French law and OECD principles. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups meeting the EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: France has no single standalone economic substance regulation equivalent to the regimes of certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless required through the general anti-abuse doctrine, principal-purpose and treaty anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership standards, controlled foreign company rules, hybrid-mismatch rules, interest limitation rules, and French permanent-establishment principles. A real-estate investment structure should maintain French decision-making capacity, qualified personnel or appropriately documented outsourced functions, premises where commercially required, bank and accounting controls, genuine investment activity, risk management, and evidence that directors exercise actual authority. A French SAS is not a tax-exempt REIT merely because its activity consists of holding income-generating real estate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, surcharges up to 80%, and criminal penalties generally including up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine; aggravated cases may carry up to seven years' imprisonment and a EUR 3 million fine, with higher exposure in specified organized or offshore circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur criminal liability, civil liability for faute détachable or intentional misconduct, tax-payment liability for fraudulent conduct or serious repeated non-compliance, confiscation exposure, professional disqualification, and court-imposed management bans. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal proceedings.
False REIT or SIIC Qualification

Statutory sanction: Denial or withdrawal of SIIC treatment, ordinary corporate income tax on previously exempt profits or gains, interest, penalties, and possible 40% or 80% surcharge where the position was deliberate or abusive.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability to the company and shareholders, criminal exposure for fraudulent disclosures, and personal tax-payment liability where fraud or serious management failures caused non-payment.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment, secondary adjustment or constructive distribution treatment, withholding tax, interest, documentation penalties, and tax surcharges. Failure to produce required documentation may attract a penalty generally up to 0.5% of the reassessed transaction amount or 5% of the reassessed profits, subject to statutory minimums and caps.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for deliberate tax evasion, abuse of corporate assets, breach of fiduciary duties, and civil restitution where the arrangements lack business purpose or documentation.
Beneficial Ownership and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, withholding at the domestic or punitive rate, recovery of unpaid withholding, interest, 40% or 80% penalties, and application of the French abuse-of-law or principal-purpose rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil and criminal consequences where they knowingly approved sham arrangements, false certificates, or distributions designed to evade withholding tax.
Permanent Establishment and Place-of-Effective-Management Failure

Statutory sanction: French corporate income tax, VAT, payroll and local-tax assessments, interest, filing penalties, withholding exposure, and penalties for failure to register or maintain required records.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur liability for knowingly operating an undeclared French business, obstruction of tax audits, employment violations, or fraudulent interposition of a foreign company.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist-Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Administrative or professional sanctions, fines, transaction restrictions, confiscation, criminal money-laundering penalties, and potential imprisonment. Real-estate professionals and regulated intermediaries may be subject to ACPR, TRACFIN, or professional disciplinary measures.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for laundering, complicity, concealment, or failure to implement legally required controls; assets may be frozen or confiscated and management bans may be imposed.
Illegal Dividend or Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, corporate-law sanctions, potential abuse-of-corporate-assets liability, tax recharacterization as hidden distributions, withholding tax, and penalties.

Director personal liability: SAS officers and directors may be personally liable for restitution and damages and may face criminal sanctions where they knowingly approved fictitious dividends or distributions prejudicing creditors.
Accounting, Record-Keeping, and Audit Obstruction

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment based on estimated income, 10% to 80% tax penalties, rejection of deductions, accounting-law sanctions, and criminal penalties for obstruction, fraud, or document falsification.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for obstruction, destruction or falsification of evidence, management misconduct, and creditor or shareholder losses.
Withholding-Tax and Information-Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of tax from the payer, late-payment interest, 5% or other applicable payment surcharge, inaccurate-return penalties, and aggravated penalties where withholding was intentionally evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where they deliberately authorized non-withholding, concealed payments, or failed to remit amounts collected on behalf of the State.
Real-Estate VAT, Registration Duty, and Local-Tax Misclassification

Statutory sanction: VAT, transfer-duty, local-tax and corporate-tax reassessment, interest, penalties, denial of input VAT recovery, and potential 40% or 80% surcharges for deliberate or abusive treatment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability and criminal exposure for deliberate false declarations, concealment of taxable property transactions, or misuse of VAT collected from customers.
