Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the official portal impots.gouv.fr; entity incorporation filings are submitted through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; export customs controls and export declaration acceptance are handled by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

A foreign shareholder forms the SAS through the Guichet Unique, which transmits the dossier to the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce. Upon incorporation, the entity is assigned the 9-digit SIREN, a SIRET identifier for each establishment, and is automatically registered for impôt sur les sociétés with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). VAT registration and the intra-Community VAT number are granted by the DGFiP's VIES system upon the taxable activity filing; the French TIN is the SIREN itself and no separate application is required. A non-resident company establishing an SAS must appoint a registered fiscal representative for VAT administration and corporate tax notices where no permanent establishment in France exists; all registration acts are paperless and completed within approximately two to three weeks of the electronic filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; entity classification follows the civil-company legal form under the CGI, and an SAS is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax at entity level. Available statutory elections include: option for VAT consolidation among financially linked enterprises (article 267 C CGI); election of the simplified real VAT regime (régime simplifié) for taxpayers below the statutory turnover ceilings, with annual declaration and quarterly payments; option to report VAT on a cash-receipts basis; exercise of voting rights in the fiscal integration regime (intégration fiscale) under article 223 A CGI for an eligible French parent; and, under the ATAD framework, application of the anti-hybrid neutrality election for controlled transactions under article 209 IX CGI as well as alignment elections for hybrid financial instruments and hybrid permanent establishments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover-based registration threshold for a French-established SAS; any taxable economic activity triggers compulsory VAT registration upon incorporation. For non-established EU sellers, the intra-Community distance-sale and OSS threshold is EUR 10,000 (ex VAT) EU-wide, which is irrelevant to B2B bulk petrochemical wholesale supplies where the customer's valid VAT number supports an intra-Community exemption. The small-business franchise-en-base exemption (EUR 50,000 for goods traders) is not applicable to a wholesale SAS operating in bulk polymer distribution, whose turnover necessarily exceeds the bracket.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under CGI Articles 262 and 262 ter, a wholesale trader of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules may apply the 0% VAT rate on sales of goods physically dispatched to a third-country destination provided five conditions are cumulatively met: (i) the operation is a transfer of ownership to the purchaser; (ii) the goods are dispatched or transported outside the European Union by the seller or on the seller's behalf within three months of the sale, extendable to six months in limited inward-processing cases; (iii) transport documentation evidences departure from French territory; (iv) the export customs declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation) has been accepted by the DGDDI; and (v) the seller maintains commercial and accounting records demonstrating the physical exit, with the proof retained for six years. Intra-Community supplies to an EU industrial customer are instead exempted under Article 262 ter CGI on condition of the customer's valid French VAT registration status in VIES and the submission of the French recapitulative statement.

  • DAU/EAD – Déclaration d'Exportation and its customs release confirmation (visa de la DGDDI) generated through the DELTA-D export system
  • Transport contract evidencing third-country destination: maritime Bill of Lading (connaissement), CMR road waybill, air waybill, or multimodal transport document
  • Commercial invoice referencing the export declaration number, the foreign customer's identity, and the Incoterms governing the transfer of dispatch risk
  • Physical departure proof: customs release statement, container tracking receipt, forwarding instructions, and the carrier's arrival certificate from the destination port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement receipt evidencing receipt of proceeds in EUR from the foreign buyer within the statutory monetary deadline

France mandates structured electronic invoicing for all domestic B2B transactions under the obligations introduced by the loi de finances pour 2022 and rescheduled by the loi de finances pour 2023. The system operates through the Chorus Pro government platform or approved private dematerialization platforms (Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires – PDP); no per-transaction real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is applied in France. Invoice data must be transmitted in structured format upon issuance and e-reporting flows are transmitted to the DGFiP periodically; the compulsory e-invoicing sequence begins on 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises, with small and micro enterprises following on 1 January 2027. Invoices issued to foreign customers remain outside the mandatory national clearance channel, and export documentation must nevertheless be retained for the standard six-year tax evidence period.

The DGFiP cross-matches the declarative file against the DGDDI export database (DELTA), French foreign-trade statistics, and the bulk cargo exchange records to verify that zero-rated petrochemical exports physically left the EU within the statutory three-month window. Where the export proof is missing, incomplete, or tardy, the tax authority re-characterizes the zero rating as fully taxable and recovers the unduly exempted VAT with default interest of 0.2% per month (article 1727 CGI), plus a 40% negligence penalty for material omission (article 1728 CGI) or an 80% penalty for deliberate concealment or abuse of law (article 1729 CGI). Fraudulent export zero-rating claims expose the entity and its managers to criminal fines of up to EUR 500,000 and five years' imprisonment under article 1741 CGI, exclusion from public procurement, and confiscation of the goods; parallel customs penalties under article 414 of the Code des douanes provide separate imprisonment and fines on the export organizer and the liable customs declarant.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a Eurozone member, maintains full capital and current account convertibility under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). No FDI screening approval is required for an SAS engaged in wholesale petrochemical trading; non-resident ownership is permitted without prior authorization. Profit remittance to non-EU shareholders is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate distribution documentation (dividend vouchers, board minutes, tax residence certificates) and, where treaty relief is claimed, the appropriate certificate of residence. The euro is legal tender; foreign currency accounts are available for settlement and hedging, but all statistical cross-border flows above Banque de France thresholds (generally EUR 150,000 per financial operation or EUR 75,000 for certain instruments) must be declared electronically via the OFIMA reporting system. There are no exchange controls on the timing of profit repatriation or capital liquidation, and no differentiated FX regime for commodity or petrochemical trading companies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirement for export proceeds in France. Eurozone law proscribes any obligation to remit hard-currency receipts to the central bank or to convert export proceeds into euros at a prescribed ratio. However, under the French Monetary and Financial Code and Banque de France regulations, all export receipts (whether denominated in EUR or foreign currency) must be collected through a resident credit institution or approved payment institution and must be declared for balance-of-payments statistical purposes if the transaction value exceeds EUR 12,500 per individual operation or where annual aggregated flows exceed EUR 150,000. Invoices for exports of petrochemical polymers must conform to commercial usance conventions and be settled through documentary instruments (typically irrevocable conformed LCs) or open account within negotiated terms, but no timing limit on retention of foreign currency balances exists and there is no requirement to keep receipts in France beyond standard commercial practice.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (acting under the European Central Bank / ESCB framework for monetary policy and prudential supervision; Directorate General for Statistics for balance-of-payments reporting)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for ancillary treasury operations, but Low for primary settlement of bulk petrochemical trade. Legal constraints: Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) licensed under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) and the French E-Money Act may hold funds and route SEPA/ECHF payments, but they are not permitted to take deposits or provide credit and are not official counterparties for irrevocable documentary letters of credit (LCs) or bank guarantees. EMIs are viable for small value disbursements, employee expenses, and FX conversion at low ticket sizes, but for ISIC 4669 wholesale bulk polymer flows requiring multi-million euro wire transfers and shipping-document-backed LCs, a fully licensed French commercial bank or a EU credit institution branch is mandatory. A cross-border overseas EMI with no French passport under PSD2 must be rejected; only EU/EEA EMIs with a branch or passported agent in France are viable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an SAS, capital contributions are paid by wire into a recipient bank account in France. The transfer from a non-EU shareholder must be executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 (if no beneficiary details required) with the exact purpose listing 'Additional paid-in capital / Capital injection for wholesale petrochemical trading activity under ISIC 4669'. French banks will treat the inflow as a financial operation and require the receiving institution to obtain a copy of the corporate resolution of the shareholders, the bank account statement showing the credit, and confirmation that the contribution does not originate from shell-company veil breaches. If the capital is injected as shareholder advance (compte courant d'associé), the routing must be labeled 'Loan agreement between SAS and shareholder dated DD/MM/YYYY' to avoid recharacterization as taxable income. For capital transfers exceeding EUR 150,000 in a calendar month, the bank will submit an OFIMA declaration to the Banque de France. The receiving French bank must be provided with the beneficiary ISIN/LEI and the sworn translation of the French articles of association to validate the account ownership.Outbound trade settlements for bulk polymer purchases from suppliers in non-EU jurisdictions are cleared through (i) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for open-account payments, (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit (LC) issued under ICC UCP 600 (and related eUCP rules) in European banks, typically with confirmation added by a global bank in the supplier's country, and (iii) documentary collections (DP/DA) under ICC URC 522 for more sensitive counterparties. For high-value transborder flows, the French paying bank is required to report the transaction to the Banque de France under the balance-of-payments regime and may require an advance declaration if the operation equals or exceeds EUR 150,000 or where the counterparty is located in a third country. A recommended structure for this ISIC 4669 scope is a framework Standby LC issued in favour of top-tier polymer suppliers (e.g., in the Netherlands, Saudi Arabia, South Korea, USA, or Qatar), coupled with MT103 wires against delivery. For Chinese or Southeast Asian suppliers, a usance LC with a sight confirmation fee is preferred to guarantee the timing of title transfer. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% standard corporate income tax rate; top effective rate approximately 25.8% including the 3.3% social contribution on tax payable.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% on gross French-source dividends paid by an SAS to non-residents; reduced to 0% for qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (generally requiring a direct holding of at least 10%).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 in annual gross revenue or total gross balance-sheet assets for the local entity; the Master File/CbCR reporting threshold is set at EUR 750,000,000 for the consolidated group turnover.

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations certificate system, but applies the OECD substance-over-form standard: an SAS used as a shell or letterbox without real local employees, physical assets, management decisions or commercial activity will be denied treaty benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test and may be recharacterised under the French GAAR (abus de droit). A wholly artificial structure cannot enjoy reduced WHT, participation exemptions or foreign tax credit benefits, and risks treatment as a taxable sham with retrospective reassessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude Fiscale

Statutory sanction: Economic penalty of 80% of evaded tax plus monthly late-payment interest at 0.2%; criminal court penalty under Article 1741 CGI of five years' imprisonment, a fine of up to EUR 1,050,000 (increased to seven years for aggravated offences) plus publication and confiscation orders.

Director personal liability: The managing director, chairman and de facto directors may be prosecuted personally even where the company is the formal taxable entity; directors can be ordered to pay all evaded taxes, interest and penalties jointly with the company under Article L.267 LPF, and may suffer a travel ban, asset freezing order and prohibition from managing a commercial undertaking.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment substituting an arm's-length profit, with taxation of the difference, late-payment interest and a 40% penalty for inadequate documentation; a fixed fine of EUR 10,000 may also be imposed for failure to surrender TP documentation; double tax exposure is mitigated through mutual agreement procedures and MAP arbitration.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly set or approved artificial intercompany margins can be held jointly and severally liable for the additional corporate tax and penalties; serious manipulation evidence may support criminal fraud charges and lead to travel bans, professional disqualification and confiscation of personal assets.
Shell Company / Abusive Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA and EU directive relief, retroactive reassessment at the 30% withholding tax rate, GAAR penalty of 80% where artifice is proved, and extension of the regular three-year statute of limitations to ten years; foreign intermediary structures may be struck down under the abuse of law procedure (article L.64 LPF).

Director personal liability: The nominal directors and actual beneficiaries risk joint civil liability for all withholding taxes, penalties and interest; travel restrictions, temporary interdiction to manage companies and, in egregious fraud cases, imprisonment for tax evasion or money laundering are applicable.
Money Laundering and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: French criminal penalties for money laundering (Code monétaire et financier, art. L.465-1) for corporate entities can reach EUR 5,000,000 or five times the value of laundered funds, while individuals face five years' imprisonment and fines up to EUR 375,000; administrative sanctions and licence withdrawal may also be applied by ACPR/TRACFIN.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge of or negligence contributing to money laundering can be prosecuted personally, with civil restitution orders, freezing of bank accounts, passport seizure, travel bans, prohibition from holding board positions, and, for serious breaches, imprisonment before the case is remitted to the public prosecutor; the corporate veil is pierced in respect of deliberately obscured cash flows and undisclosed beneficial owners.
