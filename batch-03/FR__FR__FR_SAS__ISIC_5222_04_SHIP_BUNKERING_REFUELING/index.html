Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation; the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

SAS is by default subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS). Under limited conditions, a new SAS may elect for income tax application (régime fiscal des sociétés de personnes) for 5 years, but this election is unavailable for most commercial activities and must be disregarded for active marine bunkering. No check-the-box election exists in France; hybrid mismatch elections follow EU ATAD implementation, mainly internal safe-harbour provisions requiring filing with the DGFiP in annual returns.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general registration threshold for supplies of goods or services performed in France by foreign entities; any taxable person making taxable supplies in France must register from the first euro. For E-commerce/non-established distance sellers, the EU-wide distance sales threshold applies (EUR 10,000 across all EU member states). Domestic micro-businesses may benefit from the EUR 91,900 (goods) / EUR 36,800 (services) exemption thresholds under Article 293 B CGI, but foreign suppliers cannot rely on these exemptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the European Union may be zero-rated for French VAT if the exporter proves dispatch/transport outside the EU under Article 262-1 CGI. For marine bunkering and lubricant replenishment, supplies of fuel and lubricants to vessels engaged in international navigation are zero-rated or exempt under Articles 262-II-1° and 278-0 bis A CGI, provided the vessel falls under the definition of international transport (or maritime shipping for more than 20 days or commercial voyages). The service must be physically supplied at the vessel's location; supply to EU flagged vessels on intra-EU routes is generally taxable. Supporting evidence must demonstrate that the vessel fulfills the qualification of 'international traffic' and the fuel was loaded onboard.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane, DELTA / EXA electronic documentation with the France Customs Single Window (GUN) certificate)
  • Bill of Lading (or, where applicable, sea waybill / mate's receipt)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Payment Confirmation showing the export proceeds received on a French or foreign bank account
  • Bunker Delivery Receipt / Delivery Note signed by the ship's master or authorized vessel agent
  • Contract or sale agreement for marine fuel/bunkers identifying vessel name, IMO number, port of supply, and quantity
  • Proof of vessel's international navigation status (e.g., ship registry certificate, voyage itinerary)

Mandatory electronic invoicing for B2B domestic transactions is being phased in from 1 September 2026 (large enterprises) and 1 September 2027 (SMEs), using structured electronic formats (E-invoicing via PEPPOL/SEML/FACTUR-X) and mandatory e-reporting E-reporting is transmitted in real time through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF), requiring API or certified partner connection. Invoices must fulfil authentication requirements, and no perpetual digital fiscal stamping is applied, but real-time clearance controls increasingly apply via the PPF.

Zero-rating claims are subject to strict documentary proof. In case of fraud or unjustified zero-rating, the French tax authorities may issue an assessment for unpaid VAT plus late payment interest (0.2% per month), late filing penalties (5% to 40% of the tax), a 40% tax delinquency penalty for intentional misstatements, and up to a 80% penalty in case of abusive fraud or opposition to tax inspection. Criminal prosecution for customs or VAT fraud may result in imprisonment up to 5 years and a fine up to EUR 375,000 as well as potential revocation of the exporter's VAT registration and extended audit scrutiny.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a full euro-area convertibility regime and accepts IMF Article VIII obligations; capital movements and payments are liberalised within the EU under TFEU Articles 63-65 and vis-à-vis third countries, subject only to (i) EU/UN financial sanctions and asset-freezing measures, (ii) prior authorisation under the French foreign-direct-investment screening mechanism for strategic sectors covering energy-supply security and dual-use activities, which may require a clearance opinion if a non-resident parent acquires control of a port fuel logistics SAS, and (iii) prudential statistical reporting thresholds. Current-account transactions, including bunker sales abroad, freight settlements, agency commissions and marine lubricant purchases, are freely executable without FX licence or advance declaration. Profit remittance is unrestricted via ordinary dividend distributions, subject to French withholding tax at the standard non-treaty rate of 25%, reducible to 0% for a qualifying EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive corporate shareholder or reduced under double-tax treaties for non-EU shareholders, together with the obligation to file Form 2561 and to substantiate beneficial ownership under anti-money-laundering rules. No exchange-control visa is required to open a capital escrow account in favour of the SAS.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No French or EU statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation deadline applies to export or bunkering proceeds; export earnings may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR or any freely convertible currency. Bunkering of vessels engaged in international navigation is classified as an export of goods under the EU VAT Directive (Articles 146-148) and qualifies for zero-rated VAT and excise exemption under Articles 262 and 162 of the Code général des impôts, with commercial documentation required to evidence the international voyage. The sole regulatory constraint is the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical reporting régime (RONO): payments to or from non-residents equal to or exceeding EUR 15,000 per individual transaction, or cumulative monthly cross-border flows above EUR 150,000, must be captured and reported by the intermediary bank; the SAS must code each wire with the correct purpose description, such as exportation de biens - approvisionnement de navires, apport en capital, dividende or compensation, so that non-existent surrender obligations are not de facto triggered by miscoded remittances.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with prudential supervision of credit institutions delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) and foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments administration co-ordinated with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the Code monétaire et financier, notably Articles L151-2 to L151-4, L561-1 et seq., and the règlement général de l'ACPR.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium viability. French-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Blank and Shine, chartered by the ACPR under the PSD2 framework, can deliver rapid IBAN collection, multi-currency wallets and digital invoicing for a newly formed SAS, and are suitable for transit liquidity and regular operating payments up to prudent commercial caps. However, an EMI is not a substitute for a full credit institution: it cannot issue standby letters of credit or commercial documentary credits, cannot grant overdrafts or revolving credit facilities, and must hold client funds under the e-money safeguarding régime, which prevents the construction of a true capital escrow plus LC treasury hub. Cross-border EEA EMIs holding a valid passport may onboard the SAS remotely, whereas UK-regulated EMIs have lost EU passporting rights after Brexit and cannot lawfully serve the French market without establishing a French branch. For capital escrow, SWIFT MT103 liquidity and Standby-LC issuance, a Tier-1 commercial bank account remains mandatory as the treasury backbone.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended inbound routing for the French SAS: (1) open a dedicated capital-injection escrow sub-account with BNP Paribas or Société Générale, ring-fenced from trading operations; (2) execute the subscription via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's clearing bank with remittance information stating CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - SAS FR_SAS - REGISTRATION NO and the Banque de France purpose code EQUITY - APPORT EN CAPITAL; (3) attach the board minutes approving the reserved capital increase, notary or signature validation, shareholder identification and intra-group VAT status; (4) ensure that any individual wire of EUR 15,000 or more, or cumulative monthly flows above EUR 150,000, is automatically captured by the receiving bank under the RONO balance-of-payments reporting régime; (5) where the foreign parent remits USD, pre-book a same-day EUR/USD spot conversion through the French bank to eliminate FX drag; (6) file the capital-increase amendment with the INPI registry and update the RBE within the statutory one-month window; (7) retain bank confirmation of source of funds and economic purpose to satisfy FATF-aligned French AML obligations, including the prohibition of anonymous accounts. A subordinated shareholder loan (compte courant d'associé) can complement equity capital using the same SWIFT routing with purpose code SHAREHOLDER LOAN - COMPTE COURANT ASSOCIE, supported by a written loan agreement bearing an arm's-length interest rate to prevent tax recharacterisation.Clearing rails for bunkering disbursements are structured across four layers: Layer 1, SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated EU/EEA suppliers, covering port service calls, lubricant replenishment from Rotterdam/Antwerp terminals and barge logistics; Layer 2, SWIFT MT103 corporate wires for USD-restated FOB cargo-priced fuel purchases, executed through the direct correspondent USD nostro concentration of BNP Paribas or Société Générale, with mandatory OFAC and EU sanctions filtering on every wire; Layer 3, commercial documentary letters of credit under ICC UCP 600 and eUCP 2.0 for term-based refinery and large-flexi purchases, issued by the Tier-1 French bank on a sight or 15/30/60-day tenor basis with presentation of bunker delivery notes, quantity certificates and commercial invoices; Layer 4, Standby Letters of Credit under ISP98 issued by the Tier-1 bank as security for delivery performance or post-fuelling payment obligations to ship operators and suppliers. Supplementary mechanisms include assignment of receivables (cession Dailly) under French law for bunker-receivables financing, daily MT940/camt.053 cash-position reports, a payment factory with automated sanctions screening, and an integrated bank fuel-card for high-frequency small port dispatches. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium viability. French-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Blank and Shine, chartered by the ACPR under the PSD2 framework, can deliver rapid IBAN collection, multi-currency wallets and digital invoicing for a newly formed SAS, and are suitable for transit liquidity and regular operating payments up to prudent commercial caps. However, an EMI is not a substitute for a full credit institution: it cannot issue standby letters of credit or commercial documentary credits, cannot grant overdrafts or revolving credit facilities, and must hold client funds under the e-money safeguarding régime, which prevents the construction of a true capital escrow plus LC treasury hub. Cross-border EEA EMIs holding a valid passport may onboard the SAS remotely, whereas UK-regulated EMIs have lost EU passporting rights after Brexit and cannot lawfully serve the French market without establishing a French branch. For capital escrow, SWIFT MT103 liquidity and Standby-LC issuance, a Tier-1 commercial bank account remains mandatory as the treasury backbone.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying small or medium-sized company may apply the 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to the statutory conditions, including turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million and fully paid-up share capital held at least 75% by individuals or qualifying companies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic withholding rate on dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate shareholder is generally 25%, subject to exemptions and treaty reduction. A 12.8% domestic rate generally applies to individual recipients, subject to applicable rules. Qualifying EU/EEA parent companies may obtain an exemption under the French parent-subsidiary regime, subject to ownership, holding-period, beneficial-owner, and anti-abuse conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: French full transfer-pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) generally applies where the French entity's annual turnover, gross assets, or specified group relationship thresholds reach EUR 400 million, including applicable thresholds for a controlling or controlled group entity. A separate annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies at a EUR 50 million threshold, subject to statutory group and ownership tests. Thresholds and scope should be tested against the current French rules and the relevant fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: France does not impose a general Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore regimes. French rules nevertheless require genuine commercial rationale and may deny benefits or adjust results under transfer-pricing rules, the general anti-abuse rule, the abuse-of-law doctrine, beneficial-ownership principles, controlled foreign company rules, and other anti-avoidance provisions. The SAS should maintain evidence of decision-making, personnel, assets, risk control, contracts, and operational capacity consistent with its stated bunkering functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest, and surcharges may apply. Intentional fraud may be referred for criminal prosecution; serious tax fraud can result in imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with higher penalties possible in aggravated cases under the French Tax Code and Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability where they participated in, directed, or knowingly facilitated the offence. A court may impose fines and imprisonment; tax debts may be imposed personally in defined cases of fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated non-compliance. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty.
Transfer-pricing misstatement and undocumented related-party dealings

Statutory sanction: Taxable profit may be adjusted, with interest and applicable surcharges. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation can trigger a penalty generally equal to the greater of EUR 10,000 or 0.5% of the transactions concerned, subject to statutory conditions and caps; separate penalties may apply to incomplete or inaccurate disclosures.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for intentional participation in fraud or for specified serious breaches, but ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments do not automatically pierce the corporate veil. Maintain contemporaneous benchmarking, intercompany agreements, and evidence supporting functions, assets, and risks.
Unlawful dividend or withholding-tax treatment

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, late-payment interest, and surcharges. Improper distributions may be recoverable under French company law and may expose responsible persons to civil or criminal consequences depending on the conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability where statutory conditions for fraudulent conduct, misappropriation, or serious tax non-compliance are met. Treaty relief should be supported by current residence and beneficial-ownership evidence and reviewed before payment.
Shell-company or artificial arrangement

Statutory sanction: Authorities may deny treaty or tax benefits, recharacterize transactions, assess tax and interest, and apply abuse-of-law penalties. Criminal sanctions may apply if the arrangement involves fraud, forged records, or concealment.

Director personal liability: Personal liability depends on the director's conduct and the applicable civil or criminal provisions; limited liability is not a defense to a director's own fraud or criminal acts. A travel ban is not an automatic consequence.
AML, sanctions, and illicit bunkering proceeds

Statutory sanction: Depending on the offence, sanctions may include criminal fines, imprisonment, asset seizure or confiscation, administrative measures, and restrictions on business activity. Penalties depend on the specific AML, sanctions, customs, and criminal-law provisions breached.

Director personal liability: Directors and other responsible persons may be prosecuted for their own participation, instruction, or knowing facilitation. Corporate status does not shield personal criminal liability; imprisonment and asset confiscation may apply where authorized by law.
Unlicensed or non-compliant bunkering operations

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include administrative fines, suspension or withdrawal of authorizations, tax and excise reassessments, seizure of goods, and criminal sanctions where the relevant breach is an offence.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for personal participation or for specific statutory management offences. Confirm permits, port and customs procedures, excise treatment, and environmental requirements for each operating location and product.
