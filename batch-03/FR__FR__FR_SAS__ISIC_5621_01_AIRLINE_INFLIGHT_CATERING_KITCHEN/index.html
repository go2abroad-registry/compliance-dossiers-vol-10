Applicable AML statute: Articles L. 561-1 through L. 561-50-1 and R. 561-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, 'CMF'); Law No. 2016-1691 of 9 December 2016 (Sapin II Law on transparency, anti-corruption and modernisation of economic life); Articles L. 561-46 through L. 561-49 CMF establishing the beneficial owner (UBO) register and declaration filing; Decree No. 2017-1094 of 28 June 2017 on the UBO declaration; Order of 26 July 2017 on the standard UBO declaration form; Regulation (EU) 2015/847 on information accompanying transfers of funds; Council Regulations (EU) No. 269/2014, No. 2580/2001 and No. 2015/1750 on restrictive measures; Regulation (EU) 2018/1673 on countering money laundering by criminal law; ACPR, TRACFIN and DG Trésor AML/CFT and financial sanctions guidelines.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Partially permissible. Under Article L. 227-4 of the French Commercial Code, an SAS must state a registered office (siège social) in its bylaws; the seat may legally be (a) the domicile of the President, (b) a leased commercial office, or (c) a domiciliation company authorized under Articles L. 123-11-1 and R. 123-167 et seq. of the Commercial Code, provided the office is genuine and effective for third parties. A virtual office alone is therefore sufficient only for the corporate seat. For the present ISIC 5621 airline inflight catering kitchen, a virtual office is NOT permissible as the sole establishment because the activity requires a physical, licensed production facility (agrément sanitaire / déclaration sanitaire under hygiene regulations), customs bonded status (régime de l'entrepôt sous douane for import/movement food handling), and operational airport ramp access; the licensed kitchen and storage premises must be real, occupied and verifiable.

✓ Partner licensed company-secretary channels provide local registered address and government mail forwarding.

Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

No standalone statutory name reservation system exists in France. Availability screening is performed via: (i) the national companies registry database at data.inpi.fr (INPI, Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle); (ii) infogreffe.fr (RCS records maintained by the Greffes des tribunaux de commerce); (iii) the single official incorporation filing portal 'Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises' operated by INPI at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; and (iv) publication of the mandatory legal notice (annonce légale) in an authorized Journal d'Annonces Légales (JAL). The same name may be rejected if identical or confusingly similar to an existing RCS entry or a registered trademark of national or EU effect.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum capital required; capital may be set at €1. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation, with the remainder within 5 years. No escrow account is required unless capital exceeds €225,000 or contributions in kind necessitate a commissaire aux apports.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (avis de la commission de sécurité) from the local fire department; structural load inspection by a bureau de contrôle (e.g., Socotec, Bureau Veritas); workplace clearance (médecine du travail) and compliance with occupational health and safety regulations (Code du travail).

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum capital required; capital may be set at €1. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation, with the remainder within 5 years. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully convertible eurozone regime with complete capital account convertibility and current account convertibility. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted and expeditious, subject only to standard corporate documentation, beneficial ownership verification, and FATCA/CRS reporting. No capital controls or FX surrender obligations apply to legitimate corporate transactions; remittance hurdles are negligible for EU-parented entities and moderate for non-resident ownership structures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds is required under French law. Exporters may retain foreign currency receipts in offshore or domestic FX accounts and convert freely through authorized banks at market rates. Statutory timeline is zero; voluntary surrender is permitted but not mandated. All export documentation must reference commercial invoices and comply with transfer-pricing rules where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are licensed and supervised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR). Cross-border digital EMI operations are feasible only through French or EU licensing under the Payment Services Directive and e-money framework. Offshore-only digital EMIs face prohibitive legal constraints including minimum capital of EUR 350,000, strict ongoing reporting, and mandatory local agent or partnership requirements. Not recommended for high-volume aviation-related payments without a French entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions or shareholder loans to French SAS accounts must be executed via SWIFT MT103 using the entity's IBAN and BIC. Purpose codes include 'CAP' for capital contribution or 'LOAN' for intercompany funding. All wires require supporting documentation (board resolution, shareholder agreement, tax identification confirmation) and are subject to automated AML screening and beneficial ownership checks. Verification is mandatory within 48 hours of receipt.Outbound payments for airline inflight catering operations utilize SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers or Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) through correspondent banking networks. Settlements reference specific supplier contracts, purchase orders, and delivery confirmations.
