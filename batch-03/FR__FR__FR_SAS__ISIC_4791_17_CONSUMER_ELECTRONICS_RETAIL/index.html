Competent corporate registry

Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), acting as operator of the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises and keeper of the Registre National des Entreprises; the competent local commercial court registry validates commercial registration data.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 et seq. governing the Société par Actions Simplifiée; applicable provisions on incorporation, management, accounts, and filings also include Articles L.123-1 et seq. and R.123-1 et seq.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of company formation submitted through the Guichet Unique, accompanied by the signed constitutive articles of association, beneficial-owner declaration, and supporting documents. The resulting registration is recorded in the Registre National des Entreprises and the relevant Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies; capital may be set at EUR 1 or a higher amount. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Funds may be deposited with a bank, notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the deposit certificate must support the filing. In-kind contributions are generally subject to an auditor valuation, subject to statutory exemptions.

Funding & unlock path: No general pre-opening industrial clearance is required for an ordinary online retailer. Commercial premises open to the public must satisfy applicable ERP fire-safety and accessibility rules, with inspection by the competent local authority or fire and rescue service where required. Warehouses must comply with workplace health and safety, emergency access, electrical safety, structural load, fire-prevention, and occupational-risk assessment requirements. Building permits, prior declarations, or local planning approvals may be required for fit-out, change of use, signage, or construction.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; professional e-administration portal: impots.gouv.fr (espace professionnel for VAT and corporate income tax filings); company registration guichet unique: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; French customs authority for export proof: douane.gouv.fr; EU VAT number validation: VIES system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the SAS through the guichet unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, filing the statuts, registered office details and beneficial-owner declaration (RBE); the registration bodies (RCS/INSEE) assign the 9-digit SIREN and SIRET numbers which serve as the universal corporate identifier, and the Kbis extract is the official certificate of registration. Step 2: The DGFiP automatically attributes the TVA intracommunautaire number (format FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN) upon completion of incorporation and affiliation to the tax authority. Step 3: A non-established EU seller carrying on taxable transactions in France without a French establishment must register directly with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) using Cerfa No. 15576; a non-EU seller must additionally appoint a tax representative (représentant fiscal) domiciled in France under CGI Art. 289 A. Step 4: Register the entity on impots.gouv.fr 'espace professionnel' and activate the electronic filing channels for CA3 VAT returns, annual results, and the EC sales list (état récapitulatif clients assujettis).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under the eurozone single currency framework; the euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account transactions in accordance with EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier). No exchange control restrictions apply to payments between France and other EU member states, and transfers to non-EU jurisdictions are generally unrestricted. Profit remittance hurdles arise solely from corporate tax documentation, dividend distribution formalities, and anti-money-laundering verification of beneficial ownership; no separate currency conversion license or surrender requirement exists. Capital account liberalization is absolute except for temporary safeguard measures or sanctions regimes; payments and transfers may require declaration under the balance-of-payments reporting regime for statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender export proceeds to the central bank, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts denominated in foreign currency. French and EU law fully liberalize all current payments, allowing exporters to retain, convert, or hedge foreign currency receivables freely. Exporters must, however, report cross-border flows exceeding EUR 12,500 to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes via the OFBIA portal or through banking intermediaries, and must maintain underlying commercial documentation for audit and customs verification.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), the French banking and insurance supervisory authority operating under the Banque de France. The Direction Générale du Trésor within the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Recovery administers exchange control and capital movement authorizations, while the Banque de France executes monetary operations and balance-of-payments statistics.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium to High, subject to licensing and regulatory constraints. A fintech entity operating as an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) must be authorized under the EU PSD2 regime, either through a French ACPR license or through a valid EU/EEA passport under the single-market framework. Online retail merchants may use EMIs for collection and payout services, but an EMI is not authorized to conduct commercial banking, lending, or investment activities; custody of corporate operational funds is permitted only under e-money safeguarding rules. Cross-border digital EMIs without a European branch can generally provide payment services into France under the EU 'passport' system, but full treasury and clearing capabilities require an established correspondent banking relationship and may face higher AML/KYC scrutiny for online electronics retail due to cross-border delivery complexity. Financial Crime Risk rating: Standard to Medium.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident corporate ownership structure with opaque UBO chain

Root cause: Non-resident corporate ownership structure with opaque UBO chain

Defense evidence: Full organizational chart, certificate of incorporation and Articles of Association of each tier, notarized identity documents for all natural persons with 25%+ ownership or control, register of beneficial owners (Registre des bénéficiaires effectifs) filed with the French Registrar of Companies, and sworn translations into French.
Electronic commerce with high cross-border transaction volume and geopolitical sanctions exposure

Root cause: Electronic commerce with high cross-border transaction volume and geopolitical sanctions exposure

Defense evidence: Detailed business plan, pro forma financial projections, customer jurisdiction matrix, sanctions screening policy, IP geolocation controls, shipping and logistics documentation chain, commercial contracts with Chinese and EU suppliers, export control and dual-use attestations, and evidence of end-to-end traceability of goods.
Use of alternative payment rails (Stripe, PayPal) generating fragmented settlement flows

Root cause: Use of alternative payment rails (Stripe, PayPal) generating fragmented settlement flows

Defense evidence: Payment service provider agreements, merchant account statements, reconciliation policies, chargeback and refund procedures, clearing account architecture, and a treasury compliance manual demonstrating that all payment flows have a definable commercial purpose and are attributed to the SAS's operational account.
Insufficient equity capital for the projected activity scale

Root cause: Insufficient equity capital for the projected activity scale

Defense evidence: Shareholder commitment letters, forecasted capitalization plan with escrow wires from a qualified EU bank, bank reference letters from the parent or financing institution, audited balance sheet projections, and evidence that the minimum capital requirements of the SAS (no statutory minimum) are exceeded in line with a credible financial plan.
Adverse media or politically exposed person (PEP) status associated with a shareholder or director

Root cause: Adverse media or politically exposed person (PEP) status associated with a shareholder or director

Defense evidence: Independent source-of-wealth report, notarized declaration of absence of criminal proceedings, professional biography, regulatory clearance certificates from the home jurisdiction, and a senior-management endorsement letter from a French licensed regulated institution or legal counsel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols require the use of SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) for international inbound payments, with mandatory structured reference to the SAS bank account, IBAN, and legal entity name. Purpose codes under the Balance of Payments classification should be used: for equity capital injection use 'S.1.2 Equity capital - participations' (code 500/501 in French statistical nomenclature) or the appropriate ECB BOP code 'E510' for capital contributions; for shareholder loans use 'S.1.2 Debt - loans' (code 401) unless the loan is deemed recharacterized as equity. All inbound payments must be accompanied by a written board resolution, an updated shareholding register, and the French 'Déclaration de transfert de fonds' if the amount exceeds the statutory threshold for statistical reporting (EUR 12,500 for monetary movements). To prevent anti-money-laundering delays, the escrow account must be established before transfer, and the originator must provide a complete chain of ownership and title documentation including the intermediary and beneficiary bank details. ACH/SEPA rails are not permissible for non-euro or non-EEA-origin transfers; only SWIFT or correspondent banking channels are acceptable for inbound injections from non-EU parent companies.For outbound trade settlements to EU counterparties, the default clearing rail is SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) using IBAN and SEPS (not applicable) — for non-EU suppliers, use SWIFT MT103 with standard settlement instructions and reference to the underlying commercial invoice. Letter of Credit (Commercial LC) is recommended for high-value or documentary compliance-based purchases, principally from Asia, using an irrevocable, confirmed or unconfirmed LC opened by the French bank through SWIFT MT700/MT720. For Chinese supplier payments, use cross-border CNY or USD settlement rails via the French bank's correspondent network, ensuring the payment purpose code is aligned with the customs declaration and the 10-digit HS code for consumer electronics. Trade settlement documentation must include proforma invoice, airway bill or bill of lading, packing list, and inspection certificate; the French bank will screen against OFAC, EU, and UN sanctions lists.
