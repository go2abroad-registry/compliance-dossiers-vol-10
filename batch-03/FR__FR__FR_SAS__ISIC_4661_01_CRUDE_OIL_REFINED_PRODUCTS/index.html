Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for SAS under Article L.227-2; capital is set freely in the Articles. Cash contributions must be fully deposited into a blocked escrow account at a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the block is released only upon completion of RCS registration. In-kind contributions must be fully paid-up on subscription; preference shares and share premiums are permitted.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification - Commission de Sécurité (SDIS) clearance for any location open to the public; structural inspection (verification de stabilité) of storage building, racking and tank foundations by an approved inspection body; workplace visitation clearance by the Direction Régionale de l'Économie, de l'Emploi, du Travail et des Solidarités (DREETS) covering the use of elevated work equipment, hazardous atmospheres, and emergency response plans (Plan d'Opération Interne / POI, where storage is installed); compliance with the code of work (Code du travail) on fire extinguishers, first aid and evacuation drills.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), France; electronic tax filing portal: impots.gouv.fr (professional space 'Espace professionnel'); company incorporation and TIN (SIREN/SIRET) registration through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises operated by INPI at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; VAT registration is processed by the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – File a single electronic M0 application on formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (articles of association, beneficial-owner declaration, director consent, and non-resident parent corporate documents legalised or apostilled as applicable). Step 2 – The Guichet Unique transmits the dossier to the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce, which issues the RCS registration, the 9-digit SIREN, the 14-digit SIRET, and the Kbis extract evidencing legal existence. Step 3 – DGFiP automatically assigns the intra-Community VAT number (format 'FR' + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN) and opens the corporate income tax and VAT file with the SIE. Step 4 – If invoicing must commence before the VAT number is allocated, the SAS should use the accelerated SIE pre-registration procedure or, for non-resident entities without a French fixed establishment carrying out taxable supplies in France, appoint a fiscal representative (Article 289 A CGI) and obtain a direct non-resident French VAT number via form 1071-SD.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An SAS is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (IS) under Article 206-1 CGI; no pass-through option and no US-style check-the-box election exist in French law. Available elections: (i) fiscal integration (groupe fiscal intégré) under Article 223 A CGI where a French parent holds at least 95% of the capital and voting rights of its French subsidiaries; (ii) option for the worldwide or consolidated profit regime (bénéfice mondial ou consolidé, Articles 209 quinquies and 209 octies CGI) subject to express DGFiP authorisation; (iii) hybrid-mismatch rules under ATAD 1 and ATAD 2 (Directives (EU) 2016/1164 and 2017/952, transposed by the 2019 and 2020 French Finance Acts) apply automatically to cross-border arrangements with no elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base under Article 293 B CGI: EUR 85,000 of annual turnover for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for supplies of services, beyond which VAT registration is mandatory. For this marine-bunker wholesale activity the franchise is practically unavailable and registration is required from the first taxable transaction, as the counterparties are VAT-registered traders and the volume of energy trading under ISIC 4661 exceeds the threshold ab initio.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating / exemption with credit applies: (i) exports of goods outside the EU at 0% under Article 262-I CGI providing the goods physically leave the EU territory; (ii) intra-Community supplies to a buyer holding a valid EU VAT number at 0% under Article 262 ter CGI; (iii) for this specific marine-fuel scope, supplies of bunker fuel and distillates for the provisioning (avitaillement) of seagoing vessels engaged in international navigation are exempt with deduction under Article 262-II-2° CGI, mirroring Article 14(1)(c) of Directive 2003/96/EC, provided the operator holds the required accise licence and the product is delivered under the excise suspension system (entrepôt fiscal de stockage); (iv) a zero-rating applied without substantiation is recharacterised as a domestic taxable supply at the 20% standard rate. Resulting input-VAT credits from export activity are refundable through the quarterly refund claim form 3517-SD (Article 242-0 C of Annexe II CGI) on presentation of the underlying export documentation bundle.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/DEX) validated in the DELTA customs system and exit-confirmed by the French customs office of departure
  • Bill of Lading (connaissement) or, for intra-EU movement, CMR or air waybill certifying physical removal from France within 90 days
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 credit proof evidencing payment and foreign-exchange repatriation from the foreign buyer
  • For intra-Community supplies only: the buyer's validated EU VAT number and the corresponding recapitulative statement (DEB/EU Sales List) entry

Mandatory e-invoicing reform (Ordonnance 2021-1190, Law 2022-1726, Decree 2024-1267): from 1 September 2026 all French taxable persons must be able to receive structured e-invoices (Factur-X, UBL or CII) and large companies (grandes entreprises and ETIs) must issue e-invoices through an accredited Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); from 1 September 2027 all PMEs and micro-enterprises must issue e-invoices. The PDP/PPF performs automated format validation and transfers e-reporting transaction and payment data to DGFiP. France does not apply real-time fiscal stamping or digital seals for VAT; clearance is deferred, but PPF data enables near-real-time risk reconciliation. In parallel, the SAS must file the recapitulative statement of intra-Community supplies and the EU sales list under Article 289 C CGI for movements of fuel products.

DGFiP verifies the 90-day export-evidence rule, the DEB/SAF-T reconciliation, and actual receipt of proceeds within 180 days; where the bundle is incomplete or fraudulent, the zero-rating is clawed back with standard-rate VAT (20%) reinstated retroactively, plus monthly late-payment interest of 0.20% (Article 1727 CGI) and an accuracy penalty of 40% for bad faith (mauvaise foi) or 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law (manœuvres frauduleuses / abus de droit, Article 1729 CGI, LPF L64). The French customs authority may additionally enforce excise and customs penalties for undeclared fuel movements under the Code des douanes, including penalties up to the value of the goods for misrepresentation. Non-repatriation of export proceeds is treated as a customs-exchange offence and may trigger a 25% penalty on the unremitted amount, with DGFiP concurrently denying input-VAT deduction on the corresponding supply and rejecting any related VAT refund claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a eurozone member, maintains full convertibility of the euro for both current and capital account operations. No prior foreign-exchange authorization is required for ordinary commercial settlement relating to wholesale distribution of crude oil and refined products. However, French-resident entities must file Banque de France cross-border traffic declarations (OFB) for each payment with a non-resident counterparty or account when the transaction exceeds EUR 100,000. Dividend and profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are legally permitted upon demonstration of distributable reserves and actual corporate substance, subject to domestic withholding tax conventions, the interest barrier limitation, transfer-pricing documentation, and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive benefits where applicable. France applies no discriminatory measures against inward foreign direct investment except for EU screening mechanisms applicable to critical sectors; the wholesale petroleum brokerage sector is not subject to foreign-ownership restrictions but does require statutory licensing and compliance with sectoral regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation in France requiring exporters of goods, including petroleum products, to surrender export proceeds to the central bank or to convert foreign currency receipts into euros within any mandatory timeline. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency in French or EU bank accounts. Nonetheless, the French customs authority (DGDDI) requires export declarations for goods above the Intrastat threshold, and Banque de France OFB reporting applies when any individual cross-border cash transaction with a non-resident exceeds EUR 100,000. For treasury prudence, the entity should establish an internal conversion policy limiting open foreign-currency balances to 72 hours and executing conversions through an approved EU credit institution at central mid-market rates.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France; cross-border monetary, financial, and foreign exchange reporting is jointly overseen with the Direction Générale du Trésor under French Monetary and Financial Code Articles L.151-1 to L.165-1 and EU Regulation 1847/2019.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for primary treasury operations and cross-border commodity settlement. Digital electronic-money institutions and Italian/Spanish EMIs operating into France (e.g. Qonto, Shine, Bunq, Wise Business) generally reject wholesale fuel-distribution businesses because of the elevated AML/CFT risk profile. They cannot issue Standby Letters of Credit, handle documentary trade collections, or provide real-time commercial settlement under SWIFT MT103 at the scale required by ISIC 4661. Their viable use is limited to ancillary payroll, local expenses, and card disbursements where the total monthly turnover remains below the EMI's own low-risk threshold, and only when a full-service Tier-1 commercial bank remains the treasury back-bone.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection into the French SAS will be executed by SWIFT wire from the non-resident parent or shareholder, using EUR-denominated transfer to the designated Tier-1 capital escrow account. The French statutory requirement is notification of Banque de France through the OFB reporting system whenever the individual cross-border flow exceeds EUR 100,000, which will always apply for capital injections of the intended size. The bank will require a certified copy of the board resolution, the shareholders' agreement, a certificate of deposit of funds from the legal representative (attestation du dépositaire), an updated Kbis, and UBO declarations. The SWIFT payment must include a clear economic-purpose descriptor such as 'Subscription of share capital' or 'Shareholder current-account advance' and the French purpose code corresponding to a capital transaction; the receiving bank will record the code for OFB transmission. Incoming funds cannot be commingled with trade settlements until the legal existence and share capital increase have been registered with the commercial registry (RCS).Disbursements for physical crude oil and refined product purchases will run primarily through the international clearing rail of SWIFT MT103 direct-bank wires from the French operational account to the seller's account, with each instruction stating the precise underlying invoice reference and trade document identifier. Documentary Standby Letters of Credit (SBLC) and Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP600 and URR725 will be used for secured settlement, with the French bank acting as issuing or confirming bank. The entity may open independent import/export accounts in EUR and USD to allow netting of commodity inflows and outflows. For energy-derivative hedging transactions executed on-exchange or bilaterally, settlements will clear through central counterparties (CCPs) under EMIR or, for OTC physical contracts, through SEPA and SWIFT in accordance with the banque de France payment systems rules. They cannot issue Standby Letters of Credit, handle documentary trade collections, or provide real-time commercial settlement under SWIFT MT103 at the scale required by ISIC 4661. Their viable use is limited to ancillary payroll, local expenses, and card disbursements where the total monthly turnover remains below the EMI's own low-risk threshold, and only when a full-service Tier-1 commercial bank remains the treasury back-bone.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident entity, the domestic withholding rate is generally 25%, subject to treaty relief and statutory exemptions. A qualifying EU parent may obtain an exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, subject to ownership, holding-period, beneficial-owner, anti-abuse, and other conditions. Rates and procedures differ for individual recipients.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer-pricing documentation is generally required where the French entity, or a qualifying related entity in its ownership chain, has annual turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, or meets specified ownership or control links to an entity meeting that threshold. A simplified annual transfer-pricing statement is generally required for entities meeting the lower statutory threshold, currently EUR 50 million in turnover or gross assets, subject to the applicable statutory conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a general standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. French tax authorities may nevertheless challenge arrangements lacking commercial substance or genuine economic activity under domestic anti-abuse, transfer-pricing, beneficial-ownership, permanent-establishment, and treaty anti-abuse rules. The SAS should maintain evidence of decision-making, personnel, premises, operational capacity, risk control, and functions consistent with its energy-trading activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax fraud and false tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest, and penalties that may reach 80% of the evaded tax. Criminal tax fraud may be punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine; aggravated cases may carry higher penalties, including up to seven years' imprisonment and a EUR 3 million fine, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability where their own conduct satisfies the offense elements. A court may order publication, confiscation, or other ancillary penalties. Personal recovery of unpaid tax may be ordered in specified cases of fraudulent or repeated non-compliance; imprisonment or travel restrictions are not automatic consequences of an ordinary corporate tax debt.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, late-payment interest, and applicable accuracy penalties may apply. Failure to provide required full documentation may attract a specific fine based on the greater of EUR 10,000 or the statutory proportion of the reassessed profits or relevant transactions, subject to the applicable legal rules and limits.

Director personal liability: The company is primarily liable for tax adjustments and administrative penalties. A director may incur personal liability for intentional participation in fraud or other offenses; no automatic veil piercing follows solely from a transfer-pricing adjustment.
Unlawful profit distribution or withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The company may be assessed for unpaid withholding tax, interest, and penalties. Directors may incur civil or criminal exposure if they knowingly authorize unlawful distributions or participate in fraudulent filings.

Director personal liability: Personal liability depends on the director's conduct and the applicable statutory or civil-law basis; it is not automatic for a payment or withholding error.
Money laundering, sanctions evasion, and illicit commodity trade

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is generally punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with increased penalties for aggravated circumstances. Sanctions breaches may carry substantial criminal and administrative penalties under applicable French and EU law, depending on the conduct and instrument breached.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees may be prosecuted for their own knowing participation. Confiscation and other ancillary penalties may apply. Travel restrictions are not an automatic sanction and require a separate lawful basis.
Unlicensed or non-compliant energy trading

Statutory sanction: Sanctions depend on the specific activity and breached regime and may include administrative fines, suspension or withdrawal of authorization, tax and customs assessments, seizure, and criminal penalties where an offense is established.

Director personal liability: A director may be personally liable for an offense in which the director participated or for a specific statutory duty breached. Liability is not established solely by the company's failure; the applicable sectoral rule and facts control.
