Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), French Ministry of Economy and Finance — National portal: impots.gouv.fr; non-resident and foreign enterprise desk: Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE), 10 rue du Centre, 93108 Montreuil Cedex; centralized corporate registration interface: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique) and infogreffe.fr (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 (domestic SAS): statutory incorporation documents (statuts, acte constitutif, M0) are filed through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, generating the SIREN, SIRET, and NAF/APE Code 4100B registration identity and creating the DGFiP tax file for Impôt sur les Sociétés. Step 2: DGFiP automatically attributes the French VAT registration number (FR + two-digit key + nine-digit SIREN) to taxable persons; where import/export or intra-EU transactions are declared, the operator confirms activation through the impots.gouv.fr professional space (Espace Pro) and elects payment modalities. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a Société par Actions Simplifiée is a capital company irrevocably subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, CGI Arts. 206 and 209-I) at 25% standard rate, with no domestic-law pass-through mechanism. Territorial scope: under the French territoriality principle only profits from operations conducted in France enter the IS base; profits of foreign permanent establishments are generally exempt in France unless the parent opts for worldwide taxation. Non-resident shareholders may lawfully hold 100% of the SAS. A 3% surtax (contribution, CGI Art. 235 ter ZCA) is levied on income distributed.

Available election forms

US-style check-the-box classification is not available under French law; the SAS tax classification is fixed. Available elections include: (i) French fiscal integration (Intégration Fiscale, CGI Art. 223 A) permitting a French parent holding at least 95% of a French subsidiary to consolidate taxable results; (ii) option for the worldwide/global territorial regime (Régime Monde, CGI Art. 209 quinquies) with foreign tax credit instead of territorial exemption; (iii) option to form a French VAT group (Assujettis Uniques, CGI Art. 256 C III) permitting the hospital EPC contractor, suppliers, and installation services companies to file a single VAT return; (iv) options under the OSS/IOSS frameworks for B2C supplies; and (v) option for the Group Finance Agreement/Treasury Centralization regime (régime de centralisation de trésorerie). Anti-hybrid rules under EU ATAD apply automatically and offer no elective relief.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France imposes no general economic-turnover registration threshold: any person carrying on a taxable economic activity, including a single occasional supply, must obtain French VAT registration (CGI Art. 286). Small established enterprises may use the optional small-business exemption (Franchise en Base de TVA) up to EUR 85,000 annual turnover for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for services (scale applicable in 2025); the SAS medical gas and pipeline installation contractor, executing large hospital contracts, will exceed the franchise thresholds at first contract invoicing and must therefore register immediately with no de minimis allowance. EU-established suppliers may defer French registration for B2C distance sales up to the EUR 10,000 Union-wide threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies under CGI Art. 262-I-1 where goods are sold and physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union within a period normally ending on the last day of the third month following the month of delivery; the exporter must hold the customs export declaration, transport documents evidencing physical exit of the goods, and commercial documentation identifying the non-EU purchaser. Services ancillary to the exported goods (e.g., on-site commissioning, installation, and terminal manifold connection supplied as part of the exported medical gas system) qualify for the Art. 262-II exemption where the conditions of direct linkage and delivery outside the EU are met. Independent installation and pipeline works performed on immovable property located in France are taxable in France under the Art. 259-A-2° place-of-supply rule and are chargeable at 20% VAT, without export zero-rating; shipments to EU Member States are exempt only as intra-EU supplies under Art. 262 ter with the customer's EU VAT number and Article 289 C recapitulative statement filing.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR International Consignment Note evidencing physical removal of the goods from the European Union
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 foreign-exchange bank credit advice evidencing settlement by the non-resident purchaser
  • Export Commercial Invoice and underlying purchase order or contract stating Incoterms and ex-France price basis
  • Commissioning and installation acceptance certificate signed at the foreign hospital site evidencing delivery outside the EU

France mandates structured electronic invoicing and transaction data transmission under the 2026-2027 reform (Loi de Finances 2022): from 1 September 2026, large enterprises, and from 1 September 2027, all other taxable persons, must issue B2B invoices in structured format (Factur-X, UBL, CII) and transmit them through either a DGFiP-accredited Plateforme de Démonstration (PDP) or the national Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); B2C transactions require simultaneous automated e-reporting of transaction and payment data to DGFiP. This framework performs validation and digital clearance within five business days of issuance; France does not apply an Italian-style real-time fiscal stamping API, but hospital EPC invoices must carry all mandatory legal mentions (SIREN, VAT number, and contract references) and site-issued cash registers must remain NF 525 anti-fraud certified.

DGFiP verifies export files under the standard three-year limitation period (CGI Art. L.169 LPF), extended to six years for undisclosed activity or ten years for suspected fraud. Where export proof is absent, incomplete, or the goods have not in fact left the EU, the zero-rating is reversed and French VAT is clawed back at 20%; recovery carries 0.20% monthly late interest (CGI Art. 1727), a 40% penalty for deliberate non-declaration (manquement délibéré), 80% for fraudulent maneuvers (manœuvres frauduleuses), and possible additional sanctions under Art. 1732 for fictitious invoicing. Criminal prosecution for VAT fraud (CGI Art. 1741) exposes the managing directors and the appointed French fiscal representative to fines up to EUR 500,000 and up to five years' imprisonment, joint and several liability, and debarment from public hospital tenders; automated data analytics cross-checking customs CIE export movements with VAT returns (Fichier des Écritures Comptables) flags bill-and-switch and circular export schemes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts; intra-EU capital flows are unrestricted under Articles 63 and 64 TFEU, while non-EU flows are liberalised subject to CMF Articles L151-1 to L151-4 and R151-1 to R151-19. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to corporate income tax (IS) compliance, withholding tax on dividends payable to non-resident shareholders under Article 119 ter CGI as reduced by the relevant double tax treaty, and annual financial statement registration; there is no exchange-control blockage. The material caution for this SAS is that the installation of hospital medical gas pipeline networks (oxygen, nitrous oxide, vacuum terminal manifolds) may qualify as an activity essential to the protection of public health within the meaning of Article R151-3 CMF, so a non-EU investor acquiring control must obtain prior authorisation from DG Trésor under the investment-screening regime in force since the decree of 29 December 2023; absent that authorisation, the Banque de France may suspend the inbound capital flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender requirement and no statutory conversion ratio on export receipts. An exporter may lawfully retain export proceeds indefinitely in any convertible foreign currency, repatriate them at its commercial discretion, or convert them on the interbank market through an established credit institution (intermédiaire agréé). The only compulsory obligation is declarative: the depository bank reports each cross-border flow into the Banque de France balance-of-payments system, and the exporting entity must identify the economic motive (trade in goods under NAF 41.00B / 43.21A) and the applicable SWIFT purpose code. Underlying export documentation (customs export declaration, bill of lading, commercial invoice) must be preserved for 5 years to satisfy AML record-keeping and statistical verification requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as the Central Bank and statistical agent for balance-of-payments reporting, together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministère de l'Économie as the Foreign Exchange Administration and foreign investment screening authority under the Code monétaire et financier (CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, with hard legal caveats. French-licensed or EU-passported electronic money institutions (Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business, Wise Business) can provide multi-currency accounts, payment initiation, FX conversion, and SEPA/SWIFT rails under PSD2 and CMF Articles L525-1 to L525-8, which are adequate for operating cash and supplier disbursements. However, an EMI is not a credit institution: it cannot issue UCP 600 documentary credits, pre-finance trade, issue bid bonds, performance guarantees, cautions or avals required in hospital EPC contracts, act as depositary for an SAS capital increase, or carry full intermédiaire agréé status for Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting. Therefore the SAS must retain a licensed commercial bank as its primary treasury and LC counterpart and use an EMI only as a secondary payments rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Stepwise compliant route: (1) if the subscribing shareholder is non-EU and the SAS performs sensitive healthcare-listed activities, obtain DG Trésor prior authorisation before transferring any funds; (2) the subscribing shareholder executes a SWIFT MT103 credit transfer quoting purpose code CAPC (capital contribution), the SAS SIREN/SIRET, and the dedicated blocked-account reference; (3) the French receiving bank credits the funds to the capital blockage account and issues the attestation de dépôt des fonds, enabling the président to file the capital increase with the commercial court registry; (4) after registry registration and tax formalities, the funds are released to the operational EUR account; (5) the bank and the SAS record the flow as direct investment, cash capital contribution, in the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting and retain the UETR, SWIFT confirmations, blocked-funds certificate, and share subscription deed indefinitely. Outbound dividend or shareholder-loan repayment flows reverse the same labelling using the corresponding direct investment or intercompany loan purpose codes.Primary rail for hospital EPC imports (copper tubing, pressure regulators, oxygen manifolds, vacuum pumps): irrevocable documentary letters of credit under UCP 600 issued by the French bank via SWIFT MT700 and discharged by MT400 or MT202 COVER settlement upon compliant presentation of commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, and medical gas equipment conformity certificates. Open-account settlements on the SWIFT gpi network via MT103 with purpose code TRAD and full invoice and bill-of-lading references are acceptable for low-value consumables. Domestic subcontractor payments run over SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant; receipts from public hospitals or healthcare authorities arrive as wires into the operational account quoted on the K-bis; the treasury should automatically convert surplus FX balances into EUR at booking to avoid unhedged residual exposure.Outright deliverable FX forwards (change à terme ferme) for EUR/USD and EUR/CNH with tenors matched to EPC project milestonesFX swaps (swap de change) for temporary cross-currency liquidity bridging between EUR and USDNon-deliverable forwards (NDF) on non-convertible or restricted currencies, where offered by an authorised French credit institution for offshore CNH/USD hedgingVanilla FX options (call and put) and risk reversals documented under an FBF/ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Credit Support AnnexCross-currency swaps (EUR/USD and EUR/CNH) aligned to long-term shareholder loan or project financing maturitiesMonthly automatic FX conversion sweeps under a pre-approved treasury credit line with audited exposure limits

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium, with hard legal caveats. French-licensed or EU-passported electronic money institutions (Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business, Wise Business) can provide multi-currency accounts, payment initiation, FX conversion, and SEPA/SWIFT rails under PSD2 and CMF Articles L525-1 to L525-8, which are adequate for operating cash and supplier disbursements. However, an EMI is not a credit institution: it cannot issue UCP 600 documentary credits, pre-finance trade, issue bid bonds, performance guarantees, cautions or avals required in hospital EPC contracts, act as depositary for an SAS capital increase, or carry full intermédiaire agréé status for Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting. Therefore the SAS must retain a licensed commercial bank as its primary treasury and LC counterpart and use an EMI only as a secondary payments rail.
