Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum capital for an SAS; share capital must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions are deposited into a blocked bank account opened in the name of the company in formation or consigned with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations. The funds remain blocked until RCS registration and are released only upon production of the certificate of incorporation (K-bis). In-kind contributions require a statutory asset report if not waived by unanimous shareholders and if not material under law.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain building and fire safety clearance from the SDIS fire brigade or an approved inspector, confirming fire detection, egress, extinguishers, and specific risk zones for lithium-battery or electronic assembly operations. Arrange a structural load inspection certificate by a certified engineer where equipment or storage racks exceed floor load limits. Obtain workplace occupational health clearance through the company medical service, complete the 'Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels', and as applicable register installation use under the Labour Code through DREAL or the occupational prevention bodies.

Paid-in capital rule: No legal minimum capital for an SAS; share capital must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions are deposited into a blocked bank account opened in the name of the company in formation or consigned with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations. The funds remain blocked until RCS registration and are released only upon production of the certificate of incorporation (K-bis). In-kind contributions require a statutory asset report if not waived by unanimous shareholders and if not material under law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is a fully convertible currency. France has no exchange-control authorization regime: capital movements and payments (current and capital account) are completely liberalized under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Articles L.151-1 et seq. of the Code Monétaire et Financier. Profit repatriation, dividend distributions, repayment of shareholder loans, and trade payments to non-residents are unrestricted, subject only to (i) domestic withholding taxes and treaty relief, and (ii) statistical declaration obligations. No prior FX license, surrender, or conversion mandate applies to inward or outward settlements. Financial institutions are obliged solely to channel transactions through licensed credit institutions and to report suspicious flows to Tracfin.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export proceeds in France; exporters may freely retain and manage euro or foreign-currency earnings. The only compliance duties are informational: (a) monthly balance-of-payments declaration to the Banque de France for transactions with non-residents cumulatively equal to or exceeding EUR 1,000,000 per calendar month or per transaction threshold; (b) Intrastat/extended Intrastat and customs ENS declarations for physical goods flows; (c) retention of export documentation (invoices, Kbis, customs SAD, ATR/CO certificates) for five years for tax and customs inspection.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the European System of Central Banks under the European Central Bank (ECB); foreign-exchange administrative and balance-of-payments reporting competences are exercised by the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministry of Economy and the Banque de France Balance-of-Payments Service (Service de la Balance des Paiements).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium transactionally but Low as sole banking relationship. French ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, PayFit) provide SEPA, SWIFT-adjacent, and multicurrency accounts and are viable for day-to-day collections/disbursements. However, only a licensed credit institution (not an EMI) is eligible to act as depositary of SAS capital under Article L.227-8 of the Code de Commerce and to issue the 'certificat de dépôt des fonds' required by the notary; French banks also generally refuse foreign-resident current accounts held at UK or other offshore EMIs. Use a regulated French credit institution for statutory capital and core treasury, and reserve EMIs strictly as payment-acceptance add-ons.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity funding is executed via SWIFT MT103 (SWIFT MT103 or SEPA CT for EU-origin transfers) denominated in EUR from the foreign parent to the SAS's French operational or blocked capital account at a licensed credit institution. The wire must carry the purpose narrative 'Apport en capital social – SAS' and the applicable Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose code (direct-investment capital contribution); the receiving French bank will issue a 'certificat de dépôt des fonds' evidencing the payment, which the notary requires before registering the SAS with the RCS (RCS registration). Funds must remain blocked in a dedicated depositary account until legal constitution; thereafter they are free. If the aggregated monthly cross-border flow reaches EUR 1,000,000, the SAS is obliged to file the Banque de France monthly declaration via the FIDES electronic desk; there is no application for prior FX approval and no authorization threshold beyond standard reporting.Outbound settlements for supplier payments, component imports, and service fees are executed through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers (for standard T/T), SWIFT MT700 documentary letters of credit (for import/export LC transactions), and SEPA SCT/SCT Inst for euro payments inside the EEA. USD disbursements are settled over the CHIPS/CHATS correspondent network through French correspondents. Every payment must carry the correct Banque de France purpose code and the underlying trade dossier (commercial invoice, purchase order, packing list, transport document, and pre-shipment inspection certificate where applicable); multi-currency accounts are available to offset conversion costs. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard corporate income tax rate applicable since 1 January 2022; 26.5% for fiscal years ending 31 December 2021)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% general domestic withholding tax rate under article 119-2 of the French General Tax Code on dividends paid by a French company to non-resident corporate shareholders; 12.8% fixed income tax plus 17.2% social levies (total 30%) for non-resident individuals; 75% rate where the recipient is established in a non-cooperative jurisdiction.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return must be filed within 60 days of the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year taxpayers the deadline is 3 May of the following year (extended to the next business day when 3 May falls on a weekend or French public holiday).

Box AB (Form 2065-SD) Déficit reporté imputé: loss carryforward from prior years applied against the current-year taxable profit, subject to the EUR 1,000,000 plus 50% annual cap. Only losses economically attributable to the French PE may be carried forward; foreign-parent or non-French group losses are never deductible against French PE income. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File documentation (maître-fichier and fichier pays par pays) arises under article L.13 AA LPF where the French SAS, individually or together with tax-linked enterprises, exceeds EUR 400,000,000 in annual turnover or gross balance-sheet assets; a simplified documentation duty applies to entities with annual turnover exceeding EUR 50,000,000 that have controlled intercompany transactions; Country-by-Country Report obligations apply to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000 (article 223 quinquies C CGI).

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain a formal economic substance regime akin to ESR in zero-tax offshore centers; equivalent substance and anti-avoidance doctrines apply with equal force: the abus de droit rule (article L.64 LPF), the acte anormal de gestion doctrine rejecting transactions lacking corporate interest, OECD DEMPE substance analysis for the transfer of IP and risk for the color Doppler ultrasound console R&D, the arm's-length principle under article 57 CGI, and the Principal Purpose Test under the MLI that France applies in covered treaties including the France-PRC DTA; a letter-box entity or marketing conduit without real French substance, employees, premises, or decision-making functions cannot benefit from treaty rate reduction or surplus repatriation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: CIT reassessment plus 40% surcharge for deliberate misstatement, 80% surcharge for fraudulent maneuvers; late-payment interest at 0.20% monthly; criminal conviction under article 1741 CGI carrying up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 250,000 fine (doubled for the corporate entity), asset freezing, and exclusion from public contracts.

Director personal liability: A director of the SAS who knowingly participates in or orders the fraudulent filing can be criminally prosecuted as a co-principal; the court may impose a travel ban, prohibition from exercising corporate management (interdiction de gérer), and the director may be held personally liable for the uncovered tax and penalties through the action en responsabilité pour insuffisance d'actif under article L.651-2 of the French Commercial Code.
Abusive Treaty Shopping and Shell Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the MLI Principal Purpose Test; recharacterization of dividends or royalties as taxable distributions at the 30% domestic rate; abus de droit penalty of 80% of the amount understated with a EUR 12,500 minimum fine under article L.64 LPF and article 1729 CGI; criminal referral for tax fraud in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers who set up or maintained the artificial structure face the 80% abuse penalty being extended personally where the civil court pierces the veil upon a finding of fictitious incorporation or fraudulent use of the corporate entity; management bans and travel restrictions may be imposed as ancillary criminal penalties.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Automatic fine of up to EUR 10,000 per missing or incomplete document under article L.13 AA LPF; full TP reassessment of French taxable base; 40% surcharge on the resulting assessment; exemption of the taxpayer from the adjustment if the documentation was properly prepared and timely produced remains available as a defense.

Director personal liability: The SAS's legal representative is not automatically liable for TP penalties but becomes personally exposed through complicity in tax fraud, breach of fiduciary duty in approving non-arm's-length contracts, or where the court orders recovery from the director under the theory of faute de gestion in collective insolvency proceedings.
AML and Money Laundering through Trade Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of mispriced transactions, 80% fraud penalty, and possible confiscation of the medical imaging assets; money laundering conviction under articles 324-1 and 324-4 of the French Criminal Code carries up to five years' imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine for individuals; the corporate entity is subject to a fivefold multiplier of the fine, plus dissolution or prohibition from practicing commercial activities for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally organized or financed the laundering scheme face individual criminal liability, preventive and peremptory travel bans, seizure of personal and corporate bank accounts, prohibition from managing a French undertaking (interdiction de gérer), and the civil judge may order the director to personally repay the tax debt plus penalties under the veil-piercing rules of article L.651-2 of the Commercial Code.
