E-government filing portal

Established SAS: (1) file incorporation at the INPI Guichet Unique, obtaining RCS registration and INSEE SIREN/SIRET; (2) DGFiP allocates the intra-community TVA number (FR+11 characters) after INSEE notification to the SIE; (3) open the tax account at the competent SIE and select the VAT regime (normal or simplified real); (4) obtain an EORI customs number via DGFiP/customs (needed for all export declarations) and register for the DEB/EC Sales List scheme. Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election exists in France: an SAS is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (Article 206(1) CGI) on its worldwide profit (subject to PE/asset attribution and treaty relief, e.g., US-France DTA arts. 5/7). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account operations. France applies EU Treaty provisions (TFEU Articles 63–66) guaranteeing free movement of capital and unrestricted profit remittance. No prior approval is required for dividend distributions, loan repayments, or capital repatriation to shareholders in EU or non-EU jurisdictions, provided the underlying transactions are bona fide and supported by standard accounting documentation. Capital contributions (equity or shareholder loans) must be reported via the Banque de France's mandatory 'Lettre de déclaration de transaction financière' (Annexe 1 / Form CRM) for statistical purposes only when the transaction exceeds the reporting threshold (currently €15,000 per transaction for monetary movements with non-residents). Withholding tax at 25% (at source) applies to dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders absent a tax treaty (rates may be reduced under the France–[Country] DTA and under Paired Parent-Subsidiary Directive). No currency inconvertibility risk or local FX rationing exists; the EUR is a free-floating reserve currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no statutory timeline for repatriation. Exporters may retain full receipts in EUR or hold foreign-currency accounts with any authorized EU/EEA credit institution. Invoice settlement can be made under open account, advance payment, or documentary collection terms. The only residual obligation is indirect: proceeds received from non-EU customers that are paid via SWIFT to a French bank account must be properly classified with a Balance of Payments (BOP) purpose code for national statistics, and the enterprise must maintain records for ACPR/TRACFIN anti-money laundering scrutiny. No conversion ratio applies; the enterprise may freely choose to convert or hold foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (prudential supervision delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution – ACPR; exchange control administration under the Direction Générale des Finances Publiques and Banque de France for statistical Balance of Payments reporting)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for operational/non-credit payment flows; Medium for core treasury banking. French-regulated EMIs (Qonto, Shine, Lydia) and EU-passporting EMIs (Revolut Business, Wise Business, N26 Business, BNP's specialized neobanks) may open accounts under the PSD2 licensing regime with fully remote KYC video verification. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs cannot grant credit or offer commercial loan facilities without a separate credit institution license; (ii) salary payments must be made through an account that supports SEPA Direct Debit and Instant SEPA Credit; (iii) certain merchant acquiring mandates (e.g., direct acquiring for Shopify Payments) may require settlement into a bank account held at a licensed credit institution rather than an EMI; (iv) TRACFIN reporting duties and UBO verification are identical to traditional banks; (v) French corporate tax administration (DGFiP) aligns anti-fraud identifiers to the EMI's KYC record; (vi) EMIs offering only virtual IBANs may trigger enhanced scrutiny under ACPR guidance on 'virtual IBAN' transactional opacity. Recommended structure: maintain a traditional Tier-1 bank account as the principal treasury anchor and use an EMI as a secondary collection/payout rail for merchant settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: (i) Shareholder funds transfer via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR) to the SAS's EUR IBAN at the French Tier-1 bank; (ii) the MT103 must carry a BOP purpose code consistent with OECD/Eurostat Balance of Payments classification – for equity injection, use code 'SD' or the Banque de France statistical code 'P400' (equity capital) or, for shareholder loans, 'P300' (loans) with the 6-digit BOP code '71000' (direct investment equity); (iii) a formal capital increase must be executed notarially and filed with the Greffe within the statutory timeframe; (iv) the bank will request a copy of the 'Déclaration de Bénéficiaires Effectifs' and a statement confirming no sanction-listed counterparties; (v) if the injected amount exceeds €15,000, the Banque de France's mandatory statistical declaration (Form CRM – 'Déclaration de change') must be filed electronically within 30 days of the transaction. Hedge-linked inflows (e.g., prepayment of export receivables) should be routed under code '80000/88000'. Verification: the bank's compliance unit will require a copy of the subscription agreement, board minutes, and evidence of tax residence of the investing entity (tax certificate) for CRS/FATCA reporting.Primary rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (instant or standard) for EUR-denominated supplier payments within the EU/EEA (free up to €50,000, 1 business day settlement); (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR international transfers to suppliers in China/Hong Kong/US (advices must include the correct purpose code '300' – goods trade, commercial invoice reference, and Incoterm; typical cost €10–€50 per transfer with settlement T+1 to T+2); (iii) Commercial Documentary Letter of Credit (UCP600) for large procurement contracts of synthetic fabrics, wigs, and accessory components from non-OECD suppliers (typically issued by the French bank as L/C at sight or 90 days; requires a dedicated trade finance sub-limit and underlying proforma invoice); (iv) Standby LC or bank guarantee for licensing-secured purchase obligations (rarely needed for the low-risk standard tier); (v) Merchant settlement outflows: Stripe and Shopify Payments (via Stripe Connect) remit net card settlement proceeds to the French IBAN in EUR using SEPA Credit; if the platforms are configured in USD, the merchant must hold a USD concentration account to avoid conversion at the platform level. Recommended structure: maintain a traditional Tier-1 bank account as the principal treasury anchor and use an EMI as a secondary collection/payout rail for merchant settlements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% flat standard French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, 'IS') under Article 219 CGI. A reduced rate of 15% applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit per 12-month period for companies with net turnover strictly below EUR 10 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders under Article 119-2 CGI; distributions to French-individual residents are subject to the 30% flat tax (PFU). The EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, transposed through the French mère-fille regime, provides a 0% withholding exemption where the EU parent holds at least 5% of the capital for at least 2 years; non-EU treaty shareholders may claim reduced rates (commonly 15% general or 5% for substantial holdings, e.g., France-US treaty).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A French SAS must prepare full Master File and Local File where its annual net turnover or gross balance-sheet assets exceed EUR 400,000,000, or where it is owned at least 50% (directly or indirectly) by a non-resident entity meeting the same threshold (Art. L. 13 AA LPF). Simplified transfer pricing documentation is triggered from EUR 50,000,000 annual turnover (Art. L. 13 AB LPF). Country-by-country reporting applies to groups of French resident entities with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a standalone ESR regime in the Gulf/Cayman sense. Instead, substance is legally enforced through: (i) the abuse-of-law doctrine (abus de droit, Art. L.64 LPF) for artificial structures; (ii) the OECD MLI Principal Purpose Test embedded in applicable tax treaties, denying treaty benefits where one of the principal purposes is treaty shopping; (iii) the EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules (Art. 212 bis CGI); and (iv) the requirement of a genuine French permanent establishment. A hollow French SAS interposed in the cosplay retail supply chain solely to access French tax advantages or treaty rates can be disregarded, repriced, and penalised at 80%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years; aggravated fraud raises the penalties to EUR 3,000,000 and 7 years. Tax reassessment is increased by 80% and the judgment may be published in the press at the offender's cost.

Director personal liability: The SAS president and de facto directors who participate in the fraud are personally prosecuted; sanctions include criminal fines, travel bans, freezing of personal assets, prohibition from managing a business, and loss of tax compliance integrity for future licensing or public procurement.
Failure to File or Wrongful Filing

Statutory sanction: Surcharge of 10% (within 30 days), 40% (after 30 days), or 80% for fraudulent manoeuvre; monthly late interest of 0.20%; ex officio assessment (taxation d'office) without opportunity to dispute the tax base; automatic tax levy without prior declaratory right.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is personally and severally liable for penalties and withholding tax that was not paid over where the default results from his or her deliberate misconduct (Art. L.267 LPF), and may be subject to commercial court disqualification.
Abusive Shell Arrangement / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Abuse of law procedure (Art. L.64 LPF) entailing restitution of the avoided tax, 80% penalty, interest at 0.20% per month, and possible rejection of treaty claims under the Principal Purpose Test; the arrangement may be recharacterised as a conduit disregarded for French tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed or implemented the artificial scheme are jointly and severally liable for the reassessed tax, penalties and interest; in severe cases they face fraud charges, corporate disqualification, and a ban from holding office in French regulated entities.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of EUR 150,000 for non-communication of transfer pricing documentation (Art. 1735 ter B CGI); incomplete documents attract fines of EUR 15,000 per item; the tax authority may reprice the transactions and assert withholding tax deficiencies on recharacterised distributions.

Director personal liability: Mangers who knowingly sign TP documentation containing false valuations are exposed to personal prosecution for fraud; the company's tax debt can be recovered from directors under Art. L.267 LPF where non-filing or concealment is established.
Money Laundering of Tax Fraud / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to EUR 750,000 and imprisonment of up to 10 years for aggravated money laundering (Art. 324-2 Criminal Code); additional confiscation of the laundered assets, closure of the business, and publication of the conviction.

Director personal liability: Director and beneficial owner face personal travel bans, seizure of bank accounts and real property, freezing of shares, and a commercial ban; the corporate veil is pierced under the theory of independent fault (faute détachable) committed by the manager for the aggregate amount of laundered funds.
