Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry), acting as registrar for the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); mandatory electronic filing channel is Le Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II Title II Chapter VII, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-30 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); general incorporation rules under Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10; RCS registration provisions under Articles L.123-1 to L.123-12 and R.123-1 to R.123-151; Décret n°84-406 of 30 May 1984; mandatory electronic filing through the Guichet Unique pursuant to Article L.123-36.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts constitutifs (Articles of Association) executed by all shareholders, submitted with the online company-creation dossier and, where applicable, the official M0 information set equivalent to Cerfa n°15884*03 (déclaration de constitution de personne morale).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For an SAS there is no statutory minimum share capital; the articles freely set capital. Cash contributions are deposited into a blocked account opened in the company's name with a French bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the RCS/Kbis extract is obtained. Unless the articles require full payment at subscription, at least one-half of each cash share's nominal value must be paid up on subscription and the balance may be paid within five years.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupancy, obtain the building owner's current fire-safety certificate for the leased office premises; maintain fire extinguishers, evacuation signage and an emergency plan. Employee health and safety monitoring must be arranged with a Service de Prévention et de Santé au Travail. No structural-load inspection is required for ordinary administrative office occupancy unless the landlord or local building rules require a technical attestation for the specific building category.

Paid-in capital rule: For an SAS there is no statutory minimum share capital; the articles freely set capital. Cash contributions are deposited into a blocked account opened in the company's name with a French bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the RCS/Kbis extract is obtained. Unless the articles require full payment at subscription, at least one-half of each cash share's nominal value must be paid up on subscription and the balance may be paid within five years.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — the activity is administrative office administration of an employee share incentive plan/ESOP trust; no industrial installation requires authorization under the French Environmental Code.

Category: No statutory operating license is required for ordinary administration of employee share incentive plans; if the scope expands to discretionary management of financial instruments or third-party assets, an AMF registration or authorization under Articles L.531-1 and L.532-1 of the Monetary and Financial Code may be required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), including the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE); official portal: https://www.impots.gouv.fr. Corporate legal and establishment registration is coordinated through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI): https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS must complete incorporation and establishment formalities through the INPI Guichet unique. The filing transmits information to the Registre national des entreprises and the competent tax authorities. The entity receives a SIREN number for the legal entity and, where applicable, a SIRET number for each establishment. The French corporate tax account is linked to the SIREN and the entity's tax account with the competent SIE. A VAT-liable entity must request or activate a French VAT identification number from the DGFiP. The French VAT number generally consists of the prefix FR, a two-character control key, and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity without a French establishment that is required to register for French VAT applies to the SIEE, submits constitutional documents, proof of business activity, identification of directors and beneficial owners, evidence of taxable transactions, and—where applicable—an appointment of a French fiscal representative. Entities established in another EU Member State generally do not require a French fiscal representative, subject to the applicable EU VAT rules. VAT registration is not itself proof of French permanent establishment or corporate-tax residence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS). An eligible SAS may elect temporary partnership-style taxation under the French income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu, or IR) for a maximum period of five financial years, subject to statutory conditions including qualifying ownership, active commercial or professional activity, fewer than 50 employees, turnover or balance-sheet limits, and qualifying shareholder participation. The election must generally be notified within the statutory deadline and cannot ordinarily be renewed after the five-year period. There is no general United States-style check-the-box election. France applies specific anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive; these rules can deny deductions, impose inclusion, or restrict relief where a cross-border entity or instrument produces a deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcome. An employee share ownership or incentive-plan administration arrangement does not, by itself, create a tax-transparent classification or a special tax-exempt status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For services, the principal French franchise en base de TVA threshold is EUR 37,500 of annual turnover, with a higher tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250 under the applicable rules. For supplies of goods, the principal threshold is EUR 85,000, with a higher tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500. These thresholds generally determine eligibility for the small-business VAT exemption rather than the existence of a taxable transaction. A business may be required to register or file VAT returns before reaching the threshold for intra-Community transactions, imports, exports, reverse-charge transactions, or transactions not eligible for the franchise. A French SAS providing employee share-plan administration services should normally assess the services thresholds and the place-of-supply rules separately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT where the exporter is able to prove exportation and the goods have exited the EU customs territory. The principal evidence is an accepted export customs declaration linked to an electronic exit result or equivalent customs proof. Supplies to another EU Member State are generally exempt intra-Community supplies rather than exports and require valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, and reporting compliance. B2B services are generally taxed at the customer's establishment under the EU place-of-supply rule and are invoiced without French VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism; this is not technically an export zero-rating. Services supplied to customers outside the EU may fall outside French VAT where the place-of-supply rules allocate taxation outside France, subject to the nature of the service and special rules. Employee share-plan administration, financial services, securities transactions, and services connected with financial instruments require transaction-by-transaction analysis because exemptions, intermediary rules, and use-and-enjoyment provisions may apply.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, description, value, currency, and delivery terms
  • Electronic export customs declaration and Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs exit result or other legally accepted proof that the goods left the European Union customs territory
  • Transport evidence, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, carrier certificate, or equivalent logistics record
  • Packing list and, where relevant, import documentation or delivery confirmation in the destination country
  • Contract, purchase order, and correspondence establishing the export transaction
  • Evidence linking the invoice, goods, customs declaration, and transport records
  • Customer identification and, for intra-Community supplies, valid customer VAT identification number and VIES validation evidence
  • Bank payment evidence or other commercial settlement evidence where relevant to the audit file
  • For services treated as outside the scope of French VAT, customer establishment or tax-status evidence, service agreement, deliverables, and records supporting the place of supply
  • An inward remittance certificate is not generally a mandatory French VAT export document; French VAT substantiation principally relies on customs exit evidence, transport records, and place-of-supply documentation

France is introducing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through approved private platforms (plateformes de dématérialisation partenaires, or PDPs), with the public invoicing portal operated by the Agence pour l'informatique financière de l'État serving as the central data-routing infrastructure. From 1 September 2026, all French businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices; issuance obligations apply from that date to large enterprises and tax groups and from 1 September 2027 to medium-sized, small, and micro-enterprises, subject to the legally applicable implementation timetable. Domestic B2B invoices must use a structured or hybrid format and transmit required invoice data, payment data, and, where applicable, transaction data. France does not generally operate a universal pre-clearance requirement under which every invoice must receive real-time tax-authority approval before issuance. Cross-border B2B invoices, B2C transactions, and transactions outside the domestic mandatory scope may remain subject to electronic reporting or other digital-data requirements. Businesses must maintain audit-ready electronic records and comply with French invoice-content, retention, authenticity, integrity, and accessibility requirements.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline requiring export proceeds to be brought into France within 180 days. Export proceeds must nevertheless be recorded accurately and supported by ordinary accounting, customs, banking, and anti-money-laundering documentation. If zero-rating or an exemption is unsupported, the DGFiP may reassess the unpaid French VAT, deny or recover an input-VAT refund, charge late-payment interest generally calculated at 0.20% per month under the applicable rules, and impose administrative penalties. A 40% penalty may apply for deliberate failure or deliberate misstatement, and an 80% penalty may apply for fraudulent conduct or abuse of rights, subject to statutory conditions. Failure to issue or retain compliant invoices may trigger additional penalties. Customs violations may result in recovery of duties and taxes, seizure, fines, and other customs sanctions. Serious intentional tax fraud can also create criminal exposure, including imprisonment and substantial fines. A company claiming VAT relief must retain the evidence for the applicable statutory retention period and promptly correct unsupported claims; the tax authority may claw back relief for prior periods within the applicable assessment limitation period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and operates within the European Union single market and the euro area. The euro is freely convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions, tax-reporting, beneficial-ownership, and financial-services restrictions. A French SAS may generally receive foreign capital, pay dividends, make permitted intercompany payments, repay shareholder loans, and remit profits to non-resident shareholders without prior foreign-exchange approval. Dividend distributions require distributable profits, approved financial statements, proper corporate approvals, and compliance with French withholding-tax rules, treaty relief, European Union parent-subsidiary requirements where applicable, and beneficial-owner documentation. Interest, royalties, management fees, and service charges require contractual, transfer-pricing, withholding-tax, and substance support. Banks may delay or refuse transactions where source of funds, economic purpose, ownership, sanctions status, tax residence, or supporting documentation is insufficient. Cash and payment-reporting obligations may apply to cross-border transactions and are separate from currency-convertibility rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general requirement to repatriate or convert export receipts into euros within a prescribed period. Export proceeds may ordinarily remain in foreign-currency accounts or be received through SEPA, correspondent-bank, or other permitted payment rails. The company must retain commercial invoices, contracts, transport and customs evidence, payment records, and records supporting the underlying transaction. Export transactions remain subject to EU and French customs, sanctions, export-control, VAT, statistical, and anti-money-laundering requirements. Banks may impose contractual receipt deadlines, documentary requirements, or internal conversion policies even where no statutory surrender rule applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, with prudential and anti-money-laundering supervision by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR), foreign-exchange policy coordination by the French Treasury Directorate (Direction générale du Trésor), and customs and trade-control administration by the Directorate General of Customs and Indirect Taxes (Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for payment-account, SEPA collection, payroll, card, and foreign-currency payment functionality, provided the provider is an authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution in the European Economic Area and is permitted to passport services into France. An offshore institution without EU authorisation, passporting, a French branch, or a compliant correspondent arrangement should not be used as the sole treasury bank. An electronic-money account is not a bank deposit and may not provide deposit-guarantee protection equivalent to a credit institution. Fintech onboarding remains subject to French and EU AML, sanctions, beneficial-ownership, tax-residence, source-of-funds, transaction-monitoring, safeguarding, and account-closure controls. A regulated fintech account should supplement, rather than replace, a French or EU credit-institution operating account for capital escrow, statutory payments, payroll continuity, and high-value treasury activity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route subscribed share capital through a French or EEA credit institution into the SAS capital deposit account or, after incorporation, into the SAS operational account designated for equity funding. Use a payment instruction identifying the SAS as beneficiary, the investor as remitter, the legal basis as share subscription, capital increase, shareholder contribution, or shareholder loan, and the precise corporate documentation reference. France generally does not impose a universal foreign-exchange purpose-code regime for ordinary corporate wires; the remitting and receiving banks may nevertheless require a payment purpose, ISO 20022 category purpose, economic-activity code, invoice or agreement reference, and source-of-funds information. Reconcile the remitter, investor, beneficial owner, corporate resolution, subscription agreement, amount, currency, and bank statement. For a capital increase, obtain the deposit certificate and complete the applicable French corporate filings before treating the funds as legally contributed capital. For shareholder loans or intercompany funding, maintain a written agreement, arm's-length terms, transfer-pricing support, withholding-tax analysis, thin-capitalisation and interest-deduction review, and evidence of beneficial ownership. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments where available and SWIFT correspondent transfers for non-euro currencies. Screen all parties, banks, jurisdictions, and payment references against applicable EU and French sanctions requirements.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro supplier and payroll payments, and SWIFT cross-border customer credit transfers for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to High for payment-account, SEPA collection, payroll, card, and foreign-currency payment functionality, provided the provider is an authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution in the European Economic Area and is permitted to passport services into France. An offshore institution without EU authorisation, passporting, a French branch, or a compliant correspondent arrangement should not be used as the sole treasury bank. An electronic-money account is not a bank deposit and may not provide deposit-guarantee protection equivalent to a credit institution. Fintech onboarding remains subject to French and EU AML, sanctions, beneficial-ownership, tax-residence, source-of-funds, transaction-monitoring, safeguarding, and account-closure controls. A regulated fintech account should supplement, rather than replace, a French or EU credit-institution operating account for capital escrow, statutory payments, payroll continuity, and high-value treasury activity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million annual consolidated turnover or total balance-sheet assets triggers the full Local File and Master File obligation (Articles L.13 AA, L.13 AB CGI). Entities with annual cross-border controlled transactions of at least EUR 200,000 must keep a simplified version of transfer-pricing documentation subject to on-demand production.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a separate jurisdictional 'Economic Substance Regulations' regime for standard trading companies, but applies the full OECD/BEPS substance doctrine through the General Anti-Abuse Rule (abus de droit, L.64 LPF) and treaty-access conditions. The ESOP trust administrator must show real economic presence — physical office, payroll, day-to-day management, independent board, client-file custody, and actual decision-making in France — to avoid treaty denial, thin-capitalisation (Article 212 CGI), and BEPS-destination penalties. French tax charters entitle the tax administration to proportionate re-characterisation when a corporation lacks the capacity to generate its reported income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Late payment interest 0.2% per month; deliberate bad-faith penalty of 80% of the asserted tax (Article 1729 CGI). Criminal sanction under Article 1741 CGI: up to 5 years' imprisonment and EUR 250,000 fine (EUR 2,000,000 for a legal person), loss of licences, and public publicity of the decision.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the evaded tax, face interlacing of debt recovery on personal assets, temporary ban on managing legal persons, travel bans and expulsion orders; when active concealment is shown, the director can be imprisoned under ordinary criminal rules.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: A GAAR recharacterisation neutralises the scheme and the whole tax advantage is repaid (Article L.64 LPF); 100% anti-abuse penalty on the tax avoided if it is proved that the arrangement has no other commercial reality; inadmissibility of the treaty claim and automatic single corporate taxation.

Director personal liability: Piercing the veil allows the tax administration to recover the tax from directors as guarantors if they have transferred, concealed or dissipated assets. A travel ban (interdiction de sortie du territoire) is issued where the unpaid tax exceeds EUR 5,000; foreign nationals may be prohibited from entry to the Schengen territory and may face criminal confiscation of private assets in lieu of corporate debt.
Anti-Money-Laundering / CFT Failure by ESOP Trustee

Statutory sanction: Under the Monetary and Financial Code (Articles L.561, L.562? ): individuals are exposed to up to 5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine; legal persons face a EUR 2,500,000 fine, asset confiscation, prohibition on conducting financial trust business for up to 3 years, and removal from the French administrator register. The ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) may issue sanctions up to EUR 10m plus 5% of turnover in more serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable for any deliberate laundering participation; civil courts may order damages, freezing of personal bank accounts, and professional occupation bans. The prosecutor may impose travel restriction if money flow involves a foreign high-risk state, and directors may be committed for immediate bail where recidivism is shown.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Late documentation fines: EUR 10,000 per missing document; a fine equal to 10% of the reassessed amount if the documentation is absent at audit; full adjustment of transfer price, additional 40% penalty for bad faith, and interest accrual. For privileged failure, a penalty of EUR 50,000 may be issued for non-response after notice.

Director personal liability: Documentation failures directly engaged in by the statutory director can trigger personal recovery of the additional deficit, and in egregious circumstances the director's property can be sold and a director ban pronounced. Recklessness in intercompany pricing can be construed as a tortious civil fault, leading to personal monetary compensation to the French tax authority.
