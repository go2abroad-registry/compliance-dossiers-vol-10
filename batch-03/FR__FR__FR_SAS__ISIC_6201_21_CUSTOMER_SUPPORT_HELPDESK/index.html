Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), with substantive legal registration by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered-office jurisdiction (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés - RCS).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (general company law) and L.227-1 to L.227-10 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); Décret n° 2021-1803 du 23 décembre 2021 on the Guichet Unique formalities; relevant articles of the Code de l'environnement for non-industrial service activities.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de Constitution de Société (electronic M0 declaration) filed via the official portal formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; the registry subsequently issues the official Extrait Kbis as proof of incorporation.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS under Article L.224-2 of the French Commercial Code. However, the full subscribed capital must be paid up at incorporation; cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked capital account opened at a bank, notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the funds are released only upon issuance of the Extrait Kbis.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: for premises receiving the public (ERP), a fire safety declaration to the Commission de Sécurité (Préfecture de Police) and periodic visits apply; for pure office premises a simple structural and fire-safety conformity statement under Code du travail Articles R.4227-1 et seq. is required. Structural load clearance is recommended for server and telecom rooms. Occupational health: registration with the occupational health service (Service de Prévention et de Santé au Travail / CARSAT) and compliance with Code du travail Articles L.4121-1 and R.4222-1 on ventilation and workplace safety.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS under Article L.224-2 of the French Commercial Code. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). The principal online tax portal is impots.gouv.fr; business-formation formalities are filed through the INPI-operated Guichet unique.

E-government filing portal

Register the SAS through the Guichet unique before commencing business. The formalities are transmitted to the competent registries and tax authorities; the company receives a SIREN and establishment-level SIRET. The SIE assigns or confirms the French VAT identification number, generally comprising FR, a two-digit control key, and the nine-digit SIREN. The company should activate its professional account on impots.gouv.fr and request VAT registration or confirmation from the SIE if the VAT number is not issued automatically or taxable transactions require it. Non-established foreign businesses making taxable supplies in France generally apply to the designated non-resident tax service and may need a French fiscal representative if established outside the EU/EEA and outside a qualifying mutual-assistance arrangement. EU-established businesses generally do not require a French fiscal representative.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Register the SAS through the Guichet unique before commencing business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). An eligible qualifying SAS may elect temporary income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, IR), generally for no more than five fiscal years and subject to statutory ownership, activity, size, age, and consent conditions. This is not a US-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implement applicable EU and OECD measures; no general election permits a SAS to choose a transparent classification for cross-border purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a France-established business, the domestic VAT exemption for small businesses (franchise en base) generally applies to services only while prior-year turnover does not exceed EUR 37,500, subject to a EUR 41,250 current-year tolerance threshold; exceeding the applicable threshold ends the exemption under the statutory rules. These thresholds do not operate as a general registration safe harbor for foreign businesses, and other special rules may apply. A French SAS making taxable domestic supplies must register once liable to account for VAT. For SaaS and helpdesk services, place-of-supply rules are material: qualifying B2B supplies are generally taxed where the business customer is established, often with customer-accounted reverse charge; B2C electronically supplied services are generally taxed where the consumer is located, with EU cross-border B2C VAT potentially reportable through the Union OSS. The EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold is a limited place-of-supply rule for qualifying EU-established suppliers, not a general French VAT-registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the EU may be exempt with a right to deduct input VAT when the statutory export conditions are met and the supplier can substantiate that the goods left the EU. Intra-EU supplies of goods may qualify for exemption where the customer is VAT-identified in another Member State and the supplier holds prescribed evidence and reporting support. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: B2B services are generally located where the customer is established under the general rule, while B2C electronically supplied services are generally taxed where the consumer is located; specific statutory exceptions apply. A French supplier should retain evidence of customer status, contractual scope, place of supply, payment, and any required VAT-number and reporting checks.

  • Export customs declaration and evidence of exit from the EU customs territory, such as the electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs-certified proof
  • Commercial invoice, customer order or contract, and transport evidence such as a bill of lading, air waybill, or carrier delivery record, as relevant
  • Evidence supporting the customer's location and status and the applicable place-of-supply treatment for services, including VAT identification verification where relevant
  • An inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory prerequisite for export VAT exemption; retain payment and foreign-exchange records where relevant to substantiate the transaction

France does not generally require real-time tax-authority clearance or fiscal stamping of invoices. Domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction/payment e-reporting are being phased in through approved or registered platforms under the French reform: all in-scope businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance and reporting obligations begin 1 September 2026 for large and intermediate-sized enterprises and 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises, subject to statutory scope and any implementing changes. The platform transmits required invoice data to the tax administration. Cross-border and B2C transactions are generally subject to e-reporting rather than the domestic B2B e-invoicing requirement where applicable.

France imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated to France. Incorrectly exempted or zero-rated supplies may result in assessment of unpaid VAT, late-payment interest, and tax penalties. The standard tax penalty for an intentional breach is generally 40%; fraudulent conduct may attract a 80% penalty, subject to the facts and statutory conditions. False documentation, concealed transactions, or other serious conduct may also trigger separate civil or criminal consequences. Input VAT deductions or refunds may be denied or recovered retrospectively where export evidence, customer status, place of supply, or required declarations cannot be substantiated.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible. France applies complete current-account and capital-account convertibility pursuant to Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Articles L152-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code. No prior exchange-control licence, foreign-exchange approval or central-bank clearance is required for capital contributions, profit remittance, dividend distributions, or payments for exported electronic services. Profit-remittance friction is exclusively fiscal: distributions by an SAS to non-resident corporate shareholders normally bear French withholding tax at 25%, reducible under an applicable double-tax treaty (e.g., 0-15% under the France-US treaty) or fully exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the EU-resident corporate shareholder satisfies the holding period and participation thresholds; source-country exchange-control approval does not exist for euro-denominated flows. Soft compliance frictions derive from the ACPR/FATF high-risk jurisdiction lists and EU Regulation 2020/1155 sanctions screening, which may delay execution but do not impair legal convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. No statutory surrender ratio, repatriation deadline or mandatory conversion rule applies to French export receipts. Exchange controls were fully liberalised effective 1 January 1990 (Law No. 88-1149 of 23 December 1988) and the freedom principle is codified in Article L152-1 of the Monetary and Financial Code; SaaS subscription receipts may be collected in EUR or foreign currency and retained indefinitely. Residual obligations: (i) statistical Balance-of-Payments reporting to the Banque de France through the servicing credit institution for each cross-border wire transfer meeting the individual declaration threshold currently applicable; (ii) declaration of cross-border physical cash and bearer instruments above EUR 10,000 under EU Regulation 2018/1672; (iii) maintenance of complete electronic-service invoicing to evidence the export transaction for VAT and transfer-pricing purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) for monetary, statistical and balance-of-payments reporting functions, acting within the Eurosystem; the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) of the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty for external financial relations and foreign investment screening; the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for prudential supervision of banks and payment institutions engaged in onboarding, KYC and AML controls.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (i) the non-resident parent board passes a written resolution approving the SAS capital increase; (ii) funds are transmitted by SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA SCT in EUR) to the SAS's EUR IBAN citing ISO 20022 ExternalPurposeCode 'INVE' with remittance narrative 'French SAS share capital contribution / apport en numéraire'; (v) no ex-ante authorisation from the Direction Générale du Trésor is required because an ISIC 6201 helpdesk/SaaS business is outside the sensitive sectors listed in Article R151-3 of the Monetary and Financial Code, unless the product includes cybersecurity or security-operations functionality; (ii) SWIFT MT103 with gpi tracking for non-EU and foreign-currency counterparties (US cloud infrastructure, sub-agents, overseas IBAN payees) using ISO 20022 purpose codes such as 'SUPP' (supplier settlement), 'ROYA' (royalty/licence fees) and 'DIVI' (dividend distribution), with the French bank performing the balance-of-payments classification; (iii) commercial letters of credit are unnecessary for electronic-service exports and should be refused as non-cost-effective; the operative documentary toolkit is the VAT-compliant invoice, proof of service delivery and e-contract; (iv) subscription proceeds from Stripe Billing and PayPal settle in EUR to the primary operational account under the processors' standard settlement calendars, after which treasury sweeps redistribute liquidity to the disbursement account.FX spot (change au comptant) for the conversion of USD/GBP balances accumulated from multi-currency Stripe and PayPal settlements, executed through the treasury bank or the fintech EMI's FX desk without derivative documentation.Outright FX forwards (change à terme) with maturities up to 12 months, documented under an ISDA/FBF master agreement with the French treasury bank, executed only to hedge a committed and verifiable invoice pipeline and classified as fair-value hedges under Article L211-1 of the Monetary and Financial Code.European plain-vanilla FX options and zero-premium option combinations for hedging uncertain cross-border subscription revenue, subject to prior board resolution, credit-relationship approval and mandatory EMIR reporting.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) where the EUR is the settlement leg, permitted only for currencies lacking deliverable onshore forward markets (e.g., certain African and emerging-market currencies) and executed through an ACPR-licensed counterparty to preserve French balance-of-payments transparency.Cross-currency interest rate and principal swaps (XCCY/CIRS) reserved for long-term non-EUR debt or locked-in foreign-currency revenue exceeding EUR 5 million annually, requiring a legal entity identifier (LEI), EMIR margining and trade-repository reporting.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High viability as complementary collection and disbursement layer; Medium viability as sole treasury hub. ACPR-licensed French payment or electronic-money institutions (Qonto, Shine, Mangopay, Stripe France) may lawfully deliver SEPA IBANs, payment accounts and FX conversion services without a full banking licence; legal constraint is that payment-account funds are guaranteed by the partner credit institution within the EUR 100,000 FGDR cap and such EMIs cannot provide Banque de France refinancing, commercial letters of credit, or classical credit facilities. Cross-border EMIs (Wise via Belgian licence, Revolut via Lithuanian prudential passporting) may operate in France under PSD2 passporting but should be restricted to FX-optimised payouts for contractors and ad-hoc disbursements. Stripe Billing and PayPal remain the contractual inbound subscription rails for this Low_Risk_Standard profile, settling in EUR to the primary French operational account.
