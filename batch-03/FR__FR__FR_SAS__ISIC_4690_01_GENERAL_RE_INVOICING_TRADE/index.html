>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Primary provisions are Code monétaire et financier Articles L.561-2, L.561-2-2, L.561-4-1, L.561-5, L.561-5-1, L.561-6, L.561-10, L.561-15, L.561-32 and L.561-46 to L.561-50; implementing provisions include Articles R.561-1 to R.561-22. A beneficial owner is generally an individual owning, directly or indirectly, more than 25% of capital or voting rights, or exercising control by other means; if no individual is identifiable after reasonable investigation, the relevant legal representative or equivalent senior managing official is recorded in accordance with applicable law.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office in France under the Code de commerce. Domiciliation at an approved domiciliation company is generally permissible under Code de commerce Articles L.123-11-1 et seq., subject to an eligible contract, disclosure of the registered office, access to company records and compliance with the domiciliation provider's regulatory obligations. A director's personal residence may be used subject to statutory conditions and duration limits. For an offshore trade intermediary and commercial re-invoicing hub, a virtual office is legally possible but presents elevated banking, VAT, customs, transfer-pricing, permanent-establishment, economic-substance and AML risk. The company should maintain accessible books and records, genuine management activity, documented contracting and invoicing functions, local accounting and tax support, operational evidence, transaction files, counterparties' due diligence and a commercially credible reason for the French location.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum share capital (may be as low as EUR 1); however, for cash contributions at least 50% of the par value must be paid up at incorporation (Art. L.227-2) and the balance within 5 years. Funds are placed in a blocked escrow-type account in the name of the 'société en formation' with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and are released only upon presentation of the Kbis registration certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate for the leased offices (attestation ERP Type X for premises open to the public, or simple declaration of mixed-occupancy building per Article R.123-22 of the Construction and Housing Code); structural load inspection evidenced by the landlord's ERP/ERPS conformity certificate; occupational clearance: registration of premises with CARSAT (Risques Professionnels), preparation of the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP) before first hiring, electrical installation verification per Articles R.4214-1 to R.4214-18 of the Labour Code, and compliance with workplace health and safety standards of Code du travail Article L.4121-1.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLUTION ONE — Share subscription. All shareholders unanimously subscribe one hundred ordinary shares and release at least fifty per cent of their par value per Article L.227-2 of the Commercial Code. RESOLUTION TWO — Registered office. The shareholders adopt the registered office set out in Article 4 of the Statuts and authorise the President to complete all registry and tax filings for that address. RESOLUTION THREE — Officer appointment. The shareholders appoint [Given Name] [Surname] as President of the SAS, with full corporate powers. RESOLUTION FOUR — Powers. The President receives delegation to open bank accounts, sign commercial contracts, and execute incorporation formalities before the Greffe and the INPI Guichet Unique.

Category: Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés — Extrait Kbis (automatic trading-premises clearance); no specific industrial or operational permit is required for general wholesale re-invoicing trade

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has no general exchange controls. Current-account transactions, including imports, exports, services, dividends, interest, royalties and ordinary profit remittances, are generally freely convertible and transferable through regulated financial institutions. Capital-account transactions are also generally unrestricted, subject to EU and French foreign-investment screening, sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, beneficial-ownership and bank-compliance requirements. A French SAS may remit dividends or other distributions only after legally distributable profits are established, statutory accounts are approved, required losses and reserves are addressed, and applicable withholding-tax, treaty-relief and beneficial-owner documentation is satisfied. Interest, royalties, management fees and related-party payments require arm's-length support, transfer-pricing documentation, corporate-benefit evidence, withholding-tax analysis and invoice or agreement support. Payments involving sanctioned jurisdictions, sanctioned persons, restricted goods or unexplained circular flows may be blocked or reported. Cash transactions and certain cross-border payment or statistical reporting obligations may apply depending on amount, instrument, counterparty and transaction type.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio or general deadline requiring export receipts to be converted into euros. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's discretion. The company must retain commercial, customs, banking and accounting evidence supporting the transaction, accurately record foreign-currency balances and recognize exchange differences under French accounting rules. Cross-border payments may be subject to bank, customs, statistical, sanctions and anti-money-laundering controls. Export declarations and customs evidence must be completed where legally required, and VAT treatment must be supported by appropriate transport and customs documentation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework; foreign-exchange, customs and statistical reporting also involve the French customs authority, Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects, and the European Central Bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational payments and foreign-currency collections, but insufficient as the sole treasury platform for a re-invoicing hub. A French SAS may use an EU-authorized electronic-money institution or payment institution under passporting rules, subject to the provider's French servicing status, safeguarding model, permitted services, transaction limits and risk appetite. An offshore digital EMI without EU authorization, passporting, or an EU-regulated partner cannot lawfully provide regulated payment-account or payment-execution services to the company merely through an online platform. EMIs generally do not provide bank deposits, capital escrow services, full trade-finance capability or equivalent deposit-guarantee protection. The company should maintain a regulated French or EU bank as its primary account, segregate customer or third-party funds where applicable, and document payment purposes, counterparties, beneficial owners, source of funds and expected transaction corridors.

Root cause: Re-invoicing-hub model with large cross-border flows but limited employees, premises, inventory or operating substance.

Defense evidence: Board-approved business plan; transaction-flow diagram; customer and supplier contracts; invoices and purchase orders; logistics and customs process; evidence of personnel, office, service providers and decision-making in France; expected monthly volumes; gross-margin analysis; and written explanation of the entity's commercial role.

Weak tax and accounting substance, unexplained related-party margins, or suspected VAT carousel or fraud exposure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route paid-in capital through a regulated French or EU bank to the SAS capital escrow account using an international wire transfer or SEPA transfer where applicable. The payment reference should identify the French SAS, subscriber, share subscription, amount, currency and corporate purpose. Provide the bank with the subscription agreement or capital contribution resolution, identity and beneficial-owner documents of the subscriber, source-of-funds evidence, bank-account ownership evidence and incorporation file. After registration, provide the Kbis extract or equivalent registration evidence so the bank can release the blocked funds to the operational account. Shareholder loans, cash pooling and intercompany funding should be routed under signed agreements with documented terms, transfer-pricing support, tax analysis and a clear purpose code or payment narrative. Maintain the commercial invoice, purchase order, contract, transport document, customs declaration, import or export evidence, VAT analysis and sanctions screening record.
