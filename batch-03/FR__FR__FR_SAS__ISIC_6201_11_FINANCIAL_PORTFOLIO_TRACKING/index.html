UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Primary AML/CFT obligations arise under the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier), principally Articles L.561-1 to L.561-50 and implementing Articles R.561-1 to R.561-22. Beneficial-owner identification is governed principally by Articles L.561-2-2 and R.561-1 to R.561-3. Corporate beneficial-owner reporting is governed by the Commercial Code, principally Articles L.561-46 to L.561-50, and the Register of Beneficial Owners provisions applicable through the national company register. Company formation and filing are performed through the INPI Guichet unique under the PACTE framework and related Commercial Code provisions. French entities must identify natural persons owning, directly or indirectly, more than 25% of capital or voting rights, or otherwise exercising control; if no such person can be identified after reasonable investigation, the senior managing official is reported as the default beneficial owner.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - impots.gouv.fr; corporate entity formalities and TIN procurement through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr and the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE)

E-government filing portal

The foreign-owned SAS is incorporated via the Guichet Unique; INSEE then issues SIREN and SIRET, which constitute the French corporate taxpayer identification numbers. VAT registration is effected through the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); for intra-Community and cross-border activity the DGFiP assigns an intra-EU VAT number of the form FR + twodigit key + SIREN. The responsible officer must provide identity evidence, the SAS articles of incorporation, proof of registered office, and, where applicable the foreign parent's certificate of tax residence. Registration is mandatory on commencement of any taxable activity; no formality is needed to be treated as a French resident company for corporate income tax purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — The foreign-owned SAS is incorporated via the Guichet Unique; INSEE then issues SIREN and SIRET, which constitute the French corporate taxpayer identification numbers. VAT registration is effected through the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); for intra-Community and cross-border activity the DGFiP assigns an intra-EU VAT number of the form FR + twodigit key + SIREN. The responsible officer must provide identity evidence, the SAS articles of incorporation, proof of registered office, and, where applicable the foreign parent's certificate of tax residence. Registration is mandatory on commencement of any taxable activity; no formality is needed to be treated as a French resident company for corporate income tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box pass-through election. A SAS is default-taxed at entity level under French corporate income tax. Relevant elective mechanisms include: (i) fiscal unity election under CGI Article 223 A, allowing a French parent owning at least 95% to consolidate French subsidiaries; (ii) VAT group election under Article 256 C ter CGI, enabling closely connected French entities to be treated as a single taxable person; and (iii) automatic, non-elective hybrid-mismatch neutralization regimes implementing ATAD, with no optional opt-out. A newly created SAS may, in limited circumstances and subject to anti-abuse rules, opt for certain isolated timing regimes under the CGI but cannot elect partnership-style pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 10,000 - distance sales and B2C digital services by non-established EU suppliers; for the resident French SAS there is no turnover threshold for substantive VAT registration - registration begins with the first taxable transaction. A micro-business franchise exemption applies below EUR 37,500 per annum of service turnover (including SaaS/fintech subscriptions) or EUR 85,000 for goods; once exceeded, mandatory VAT registration applies. Non-established non-EU digital suppliers must register or use the Non-Union OSS immediately without a local euro threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods, French VAT exemption with credit applies (0% effective rate) where goods are exported from the EU to a third country and proof of export is retained. For services - including the application and financial portfolio tracking SaaS - the supply is outside French VAT where the customer is established outside France (B2B, Article 259-1 CGI) or where the B2C customer is resident outside the EU; intra-EU B2B supplies are zero-rated if the customer provides a valid EU VAT identification number and the supplier files recapitulative statements; B2C digital services to EU consumers in other Member States must be declared via OSS at the destination-state VAT rate. Documentary substantiation must demonstrate the place of establishment or residence of the customer and, for goods, the physical movement through export customs clearance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/exit summary declaration) endorsed by French customs for physical export consignments
  • Transport document (B/L, CMR, or air waybill) showing dispatch from France/the EU to a destination outside the EU, including date and place of exit
  • Bank settlement proof such as an Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT notification evidencing collection of export consideration from the foreign buyer (used primarily for services substantiation)

France has enacted mandatory B2B electronic invoicing under Articles 289-0 to 289-1 CGI. Phased entry into force is scheduled from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for SMEs, subject to final implementation decrees. All domestic B2B invoices must be structured in a format meeting EN 16931 (Factur-X/UBL or equivalent) and transmitted to the DGFiP through accredited Private Dematerialization Platforms (PDP) or the public portal; no per-document fiscal stamp or digital cachet is required. B2C sales of digital services are not subject to transactional e-invoicing but the SAS must report cross-border B2C digital supplies under the EU OSS (Union or Non-Union) quarterly to the French tax authority. The DGFiP also accepts cleared real-time reporting through certified APIs between PDPs and tax systems; retailers and platform sellers must retain raw digital sales logs for audit.

The burden of proving the export or foreign place of supply rests on the SAS. Loss of documentation or non-monetary reconciliation triggers immediate VAT reinstatement at 20% with interest at 0.20% per month. Penalties for bad-faith inaccuracies are 40%, and for deliberate fraud or abusive evasion 80%, under Article 1729 CGI; criminal tax evasion under Article 1741 CGI carries a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and up to five years' imprisonment. Customs false-export penalties may reach double the duty evaded. Fraudulent claims for input-VAT refunds in the export context are additionally subject to clawback with the same 80% penalty regime, and senior management may be jointly liable under French abuse-of-right and money-laundering rules.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro. There are no general French exchange controls restricting ordinary current-account payments or capital transfers, and capital is generally freely transferable under EU law. Payments remain subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing rules, tax and customs requirements, bank screening, and any applicable sector-specific restrictions. Profit distributions and other cross-border remittances are not subject to a general foreign-exchange approval or conversion requirement, but must be properly authorized under corporate law, supported by accounts and tax records, and subject to applicable withholding tax, treaty relief, transfer-pricing, and beneficial-ownership requirements. A payment to a sanctioned or otherwise restricted destination or person may be prohibited or require authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds by a fixed deadline or surrender a specified percentage of foreign-currency receipts for conversion into euros. Export receipts may generally remain in foreign currency or be credited to an eligible foreign or domestic account, subject to bank terms, sanctions screening, accounting and tax obligations, and any applicable statistical reporting. Customs export formalities and VAT evidence requirements remain applicable. There is no universal export-payment purpose-code regime; banks may request transaction purpose, invoice, contract, shipping, and customs documentation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France's national central bank and part of the Eurosystem, under the monetary-policy authority of the European Central Bank. The French Treasury (Direction générale du Trésor) is responsible for relevant government financial and external-financial matters. Prudential supervision of significant banks is conducted through the European Central Bank's Single Supervisory Mechanism; the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR) supervises other relevant institutions and financial activities in France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for ordinary euro collections and payments where the provider is authorized in the European Economic Area and its passporting status, permitted services, and French operating arrangements are verified. An electronic money institution account is not a bank deposit and generally does not provide deposit-guarantee protection; safeguarding is not equivalent to deposit insurance. Provider limits, supported currencies, correspondent-bank access, transaction monitoring, and restrictions on higher-risk counterparties may constrain use. Use a French or EEA bank account as the primary treasury account and treat an EMI account as a supplementary operating or collections account. Offshore providers without applicable EEA authorization or passporting should not be relied on to provide regulated payment services in France.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route capital contributions from the shareholder's account directly to an account in the SAS's legal name. Obtain the bank's instructions for any pre-incorporation capital-deposit or capital-escrow process; after registration, arrange release to the operational account and retain the deposit certificate, subscription documents, corporate approvals, and registration evidence. For subsequent equity or shareholder-loan funding, use a bank wire or SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments and SWIFT for non-euro or non-SEPA payments, with accurate payer and beneficiary legal names, IBAN/BIC, payment reference, and a clear description such as share subscription or shareholder loan. France has no universal FX purpose code; use the bank's requested payment-purpose field and provide the relevant agreement, invoice if applicable, source-of-funds evidence, and corporate approval. Avoid third-party remitters and personal accounts. Screen counterparties and destinations for sanctions and retain records for accounting, tax, and bank review.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer, where available and appropriate, for euro payments within the SEPA area. Use SWIFT customer credit transfers, commonly documented as MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for international payments outside the applicable SEPA route or for supported foreign currencies; confirm charges, correspondent banks, value date, and beneficiary details. Settle ordinary software, cloud, marketing, and supplier invoices against contracts and invoices, with approval controls and reconciliation. Documentary commercial letters of credit are generally unnecessary for routine digital-services procurement but may be used for qualifying goods transactions if agreed by the parties and supported by the bank; documentary collections and guarantees may also be available subject to bank underwriting. Do not describe a commercial letter of credit as a payment rail equivalent to an MT103; it is a trade-finance instrument with documentary conditions. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25.0% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on net taxable profit for all fiscal years opened on or after 1 January 2022; reduced 15.0% rate applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit per fiscal year for qualifying SMEs with turnover below EUR 10 million and a minimum capital fully paid-up (Article 219 CGI).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25.0% withholding tax on dividends distributed by a French SAS to non-resident corporate shareholders (Article 119 quater and 119 bis CGI); non-resident individuals are subject to a 12.8% flat withholding plus social levies (7.0% to 10.2% depending on residence and EU/EEA status); distributions to EU/EEA corporate shareholders holding at least 10% of capital for 24 months are exempt from the withholding tax under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as transposed in Article 119 quater 6° CGI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million of annual consolidated turnover or total assets at worldwide group level triggers the mandatory preparation and filing, upon request, of a full transfer-pricing documentation package (Master File and Local File) under Article L.13 AA of the French Book of Tax Procedures; the French filing includes a specific documentation form (Form 2257-SD) only when the group meets the EUR 400 million threshold; below the threshold, the SAS must still retain basic transfer-pricing records (functional analysis, comparability data, intercompany agreements) and respond to an on-site audit demand within 30 days; country-by-country reporting (CbCR) applies to groups with consolidated global revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply zero-tax-rate Economic Substance Regulations of the type enacted in the UAE, Cayman Islands or other preferential regimes; the functional equivalent in French law consists of (i) the anti-abuse rule (abus de droit) under Article L.64 of the Book of Tax Procedures, (ii) the artificial-arrangement doctrine codified in Article 205 A CGI applying the OECD anti-avoidance test to treaty and EU-directive benefits, (iii) the beneficial-ownership requirement attached to dividend, interest and royalty relief, and (iv) the permanent-establishment substance test for foreign entities operating in France. For a French SAS, genuine economic substance is evidenced by local management, employees with real decision-taking authority, a physical office, a French bank account, books and records prepared in France, and a business purpose consistent with the declared ISIC 6201 activity; a shell SAS invoicing remote software services without any local substance faces re-characterisation of its transactions, denial of treaty protection, a 40% to 80% surcharge and veil-piercing exposure for directors.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent underreporting (fraude fiscale, Article 1741 CGI)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 (EUR 3,000,000 and 7 years' imprisonment in aggravated cases involving organised gang or use of offshore vehicles); civil surcharge of 40% of the understated tax for bad faith, 80% for clandestine activity; 0.20% monthly late-payment interest; denial of deduction of the fraudulent expenses; publication of the sanction decision.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers are criminally liable in person: imprisonment up to 7 years, fines up to EUR 3,000,000, travel bans, and confiscation of personal assets; the tax administration may pierce the veil where the SAS was a mere cloak, holding the director civilly liable for restitution of the unpaid tax and penalties; directors who intentionally signed false returns face disqualification from managing commercial companies for up to 15 years.
Shell company and artificial arrangement (abus de droit; activite occulte; Article 205 A CGI)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the whole arrangement with the true beneficiary taxed in France; additional IS plus a 80% penalty where the other party to the abusive scheme is located in a non-cooperative jurisdiction (ETNC); 40% penalty in other abus de droit cases; denial of treaty benefits; daily default fines for failure to respond to a rectification notice; possible strike-off of the SAS from the commercial register.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the artificial contracts or moved the funds are jointly and severally liable for the corporation's tax debt under Article L.267 of the French Book of Tax Procedures when their intentional fault is proven; in criminal courts, directors who orchestrated the sham face imprisonment, ineligibility for public and corporate office, travel bans and personal confiscation of the funds transferred.
Anti-money-laundering and KYC failure (Monetary and Financial Code, Articles L.561-1 et seq., L.574-1)

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanctions by ACPR of up to EUR 100 million or 10% of annual turnover; criminal sanctions for money-laundering: 5 years' imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine for a director, and up to EUR 1,000,000 or 10 times the laundered amount for the corporate entity; failure to report suspicious transactions: fine up to EUR 15,000 for individuals and EUR 75,000 for companies; prohibition on exercising certain functions for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or fail to prevent laundering are personally guilty of a criminal offence: 5 years' imprisonment, EUR 375,000 fine, confiscation of assets attributed to the director, travel bans, and disqualification from managing a regulated or unregulated company; civil liability to third-party victims may be asserted; the ACPR may require the removal of the director as a fitness-and-properness matter.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit shifting to non-cooperative jurisdictions (Article 57 CGI; Article 238 A CG; ATAD anti-hybrid rules)

Statutory sanction: Reintegration of the profit into the French tax base with adjustment of the taxable result; 75% penalty on sums paid to entities in non-cooperative territories (Article 238 A CGI); denial of deductibility of the entire invoice where the recipient has no substance; 0.20% per month late-payment interest; transfer-pricing documentation failures: EUR 15,000 per missing document and EUR 5 per page, capped at EUR 1,050,000; criminal fine for false declarations affecting the tax base.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the abusive intercompany agreements are jointly and severally liable for the additional IS and penalties under Article L.267 LPF if their bad faith is demonstrated; the sums paid across borders without real consideration may be characterised as misuse of corporate assets (abus de biens sociaux), carrying 5 years' imprisonment, EUR 375,000 fine, ineligibility for corporate management, and confiscation; the corporate veil is pierced for the restitution of the diverted amounts.
Late filing and non-payment of corporate tax (Articles 1730, 1731 and 1737 CGI)

Statutory sanction: 10% late-filing majoration, increased to 40% for deliberate default and 80% for concealed activity; late-payment interest of 0.20% per month; formal notice adds a further 10% surcharge; EUR 300 e-filing default charge; automatic enforcement against the SAS's bank accounts, trade receivables and VAT credit balances (saisie administrative à tiers détenteur); potential loss of the right to carry forward losses.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the company to continue trading while failing to discharge its tax obligations expose themselves to liability under Article L.651-2 of the Commercial Code for wrongful continuation of an insolvent business (comblement du passif), personal disqualification from management, and travel restrictions; in cases of deliberate tax evasion, the director is criminally liable and may be imprisoned, with the court ordering the personal payment of the company's tax debt.
