Official search database

Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation period. Before filing, search the RNE/INPI databases and relevant trademark databases; a database search is not a legal clearance. If a formal registration refusal is issued, obtain the reasoned decision and follow the appeal notice and deadline stated in it; challenge a registrar's refusal through the competent court under the applicable company-register procedure. Correcting the proposed name and submitting a new filing is often the practical route. A trademark refusal or opposition follows the separate INPI trademark procedure and deadlines.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), accessed primarily through impots.gouv.fr and the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Corporate formation and registration data are filed through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). Customs registration is administered separately by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI), including EORI registration.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is incorporated and registered through the INPI Guichet unique. The registration process generates the enterprise SIREN and establishment SIRET numbers and transmits corporate information to the DGFiP. The French VAT number is generally formatted as FR followed by a two-character control key and the nine-digit SIREN. The SAS must identify itself with the competent SIE, provide its corporate registration extract, constitutional documents, registered-office details, business activity, bank information, expected taxable transactions, and evidence of intra-EU or import activity where relevant. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the DGFiP service competent for non-established businesses or the designated SIE, submits its foreign registration documents, tax-residence evidence, representative details where required, transaction profile, and supporting commercial documentation, and obtains a French VAT identification number before transactions for which French registration is required. An EORI number must be obtained separately from the DGDDI for customs imports and exports. VAT registration may be required regardless of turnover for imports, intra-EU acquisitions above the applicable statutory threshold, intra-EU supplies, distance sales subject to French VAT, or transactions outside the French VAT franchise.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level. A qualifying SAS may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu) for a maximum period of five fiscal years, generally requiring conditions concerning business age, employee count, turnover or balance-sheet size, non-listing, ownership, and management participation. France has no general US-style check-the-box election for a SAS. The entity may also make VAT options, including an option to become subject to VAT where it qualifies for the franchise en base, and may participate in French tax consolidation where statutory group conditions are met. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules apply by operation of law and do not constitute a discretionary entity-classification election.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. No general French exchange-control approval is required for ordinary trade payments, dividends, intercompany settlements, shareholder loans, or capital contributions involving non-residents. A French SAS may generally remit profits after approval of annual accounts, satisfaction of statutory reserve requirements, payment or provisioning of applicable French withholding tax, and completion of corporate-law and bank compliance checks. Banks may require evidence of the underlying transaction, beneficial ownership, tax residency, sanctions screening, and source of funds. Transfers involving sanctioned persons, high-risk jurisdictions, unusual payment chains, cash, or regulated financial instruments may be restricted or reported. Cross-border payments remain subject to anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions, tax, customs, and statistical-reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement for exporters to repatriate or surrender foreign-currency export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio or fixed conversion deadline. The company may retain export receipts in euro or foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank terms, accounting rules, tax reporting, sanctions controls, and documentary evidence. Export documentation, customs declarations, VAT treatment, and payment records must be retained under applicable French and European requirements. Banks and payment institutions may collect transaction information for regulatory, statistical, sanctions, and anti-money-laundering reporting. Cash or bearer-instrument movements of EUR 10,000 or more into or out of the European Union are subject to customs declaration requirements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank, with foreign-exchange and payment-services rules principally derived from European Union law. The French Treasury, the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, the Autorité des marchés financiers, and French Customs also have relevant supervisory or reporting responsibilities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for an EU or European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution authorised under applicable EU rules and lawfully passported into France; low for an offshore institution without EU or EEA authorisation. A fintech account may support SEPA collections, supplier payments, and certain foreign-currency services, but it does not replace a French bank where a blocked incorporation-capital account, bank financing, documentary trade finance, cash services, or local relationship management is required. The company must verify the institution's authorisation, passporting status, safeguarding model, legal account holder, IBAN jurisdiction, permitted services, transaction limits, and insolvency protections. Offshore digital wallets may be rejected for corporate capital, regulated payment activity, or high-value trade flows and may trigger enhanced due diligence.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on gross dividends paid by a French SAS to non-French-resident corporate shareholders (CGI Art. 119-2). For non-resident individual shareholders, the final withholding rate is 12.8%, subject to applicable treaty provisions and the high-income surcharge where relevant.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a standard 31 December fiscal year-end, the annual return must be filed by the second business day following 1 May of the next year (usually 2 or 3 May). For a non-calendar fiscal year-end, the return is due within three months after the close of the fiscal year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million in annual turnover or gross assets in the French balance sheet triggers the mandatory preparation and production of the full French Local File and Master File (CGI Art. L.13 AA and L.13 AB). An annual transfer pricing declaration (Form 2257-SD) is mandatory where turnover or gross assets exceed EUR 50 million. Below these thresholds, the administration may still require a simplified transfer pricing questionnaire for any related-party flow, and the French SAS must be able to justify every cross-border intercompany pricing decision on request.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as found in GCC or BVI-type frameworks. France instead applies a substance-based anti-abuse architecture: the abuse of law doctrine (L.64 LPF), the abnormal act of management doctrine, the French GAAR (CGI Art. 205 A), the Principal Purpose Test in France's DTAs and the MLI, and BEPS Action 6. A French SAS conducting matcha import and distribution must maintain genuine economic substance (effective French management, local decision-making, employees, allocated risks and assets, and commercial activity performed in France) to secure treaty benefits, resist recharacterization as a conduit entity, and support the arm's-length allocation of profit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties under CGI Art. 1741: up to 5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 75,000 fine per individual; aggravated cross-border or organized fraud carries up to 7 years' imprisonment and a EUR 100,000 fine, plus confiscation orders. Civil tax penalties add 80% of the evaded tax for concealment (or 40% for bad faith) and 0.20% monthly late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: The president, directors and de facto managers are prosecutable as authors or accomplices. Courts may impose travel bans, prohibition on managing companies, deprivation of civil rights, and civil restitution of the tax loss. Where the SAS is shown to be a mere shell, the corporate veil is pierced and the directors and shareholders become jointly liable for the corporate tax debt.
Shell Company / Abusive Arrangement (Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the arrangement under L.64 LPF, reassessment of the profit in France, an 80% penalty on the reassessed tax (reducible to 40% with spontaneous disclosure), and monthly late-payment interest. Treaty benefits may also be denied under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Directors who organized the abusive scheme bear personal civil liability for the reassessed tax and penalties. Criminal exposure for tax fraud arises where intent is proven, with travel bans and prohibition from holding management positions potentially imposed.
