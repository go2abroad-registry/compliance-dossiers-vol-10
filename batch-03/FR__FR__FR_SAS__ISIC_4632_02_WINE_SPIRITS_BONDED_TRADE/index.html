Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual offices are permitted for a SAS under the requirements of Law n° 2005-882 of 2 August 2005, provided the domiciliation service provider offers a physical reception facility, meeting room, and guaranteed mail handling. However, for bonded warehouse wholesale trade in wine and spirits, a purely nominal virtual office will not satisfy substantive commercial or customs conditions. The French Customs and Indirect Taxation Authority (Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects) requires a licenced physical bonded warehouse or contractual access to one, along with adequate storage and traceability infrastructure. Therefore, a physical operational address is mandatory for licensing approval even if a virtual office is accepted for postal residency.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for SAS; however, capital must be fully subscribed and paid up to at least 50% of the nominal value upon incorporation, with the remainder paid within 5 years. No escrow account required unless capital is contributed in kind, which requires a statutory auditor's report (commissaire aux apports).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the local fire brigade (SDIS) for the warehouse, structural load inspection by a certified engineer (bureau de contrôle), and occupational health clearance from the CARSAT (regional health insurance fund) for workplace safety compliance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Relance; portal: impots.gouv.fr. Customs and indirect duties (including alcohol excise) administered by Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI); portal: douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

1) Non-resident entity without permanent establishment (PE) in France: apply with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIE) using Form 1605-SD legally translated into French; DGFiP issues a French VAT number (numéro TVA intracommunautaire) and, if a PE is established, a SIREN/SIRET registration via the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE). 2) For alcoholic beverages/wine, register with DGDDI for trade and excise licences, obtaining an excise authorization (agrément d'accise). 3) For bonded operations, obtain the specific customs authorization for 'entrepôt douanier' (customs warehouse) through DGDDI. 4) Required documents include certified copy of registration certificate in country of origin, director identity, shareholding structure, proof of European or ICC transit obligations, and the scope of wine/spirits operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — A Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) is in all cases subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the entity level on its worldwide income, subject to the territoriality limitation for permanent establishments situated abroad; only exceptional statutory transparency options (under Article 239 bis AA of the French General Tax Code) may apply within the first five fiscal years.

Available election forms

SAS may elect Article 239 bis AA CGI to be taxed as a société de personnes (pass-through) for eligibility-limited cases; an option for VAT grouping under Article 256 C CGI; for hybrid financing or hybrid transfer-pricing structures, anti-hybrid election and disclosure under Article 212 bis CGI and annual Form 2257-SD; treaty-based allocation of profits to foreign permanent establishment under the tax treaty network; no 'check-the-box' as in the US, but entities must file itemized elections through the income tax return.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 annual turnover threshold for principal activity of supplying goods in mainland France; EUR 37,500 for service or other activities; no registration threshold for non-resident suppliers making any taxable supply in France (VAT obtains immediately where no French fixed establishment exists).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the European Union qualify for the 0% VAT rate (exemption with the right to deduction) provided the goods physically leave the EU customs territory through a customs export declaration and documentary evidence of departure is held; intra-EU dispatches to a taxable customer registered for VAT also qualify for 0% subject to presentation of a valid European VAT number and recapitulative statements; wines and spirits transferred from customs warehouse (bonded warehouse) to a third country are zero-rated upon final release into export; offshore services ancillary to exported goods (freight, customs clearance, physical inspection) may also be zero-rated if directly linked to the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation) — Single Administrative Document (SAD/DAU) accepted by DGDDI
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), CMR, or air waybill, together with warehouse removal certificate or transit document proving physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (for non-EU settlement) or other FX/bank confirmation of payment received in foreign currency

Mandatory electronic invoicing reform: all B2B invoices must be issued through certified dematerialization platforms (PDP) or the French Public Invoicing Portal (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF) with a phased roll-out from September 2026 for large enterprises; structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII) and API submission of transactional data to DGFiP for e-reporting; real-time fiscal clearance is not applicable; for cross-border supplies, e-reporting to DGFiP is used for control; alcohol and spirits inside bond must also satisfy DGDDI electronic excise movement reports (Electronic Administrative Documents, e-AD) and customs export declarations.

Retrospective VAT reassessment is triggered when zero-rating is unsupported by complete export documentation; penalties under Article 1728 CGI: 40% for bad-faith failure and 80% for conscious fraud, plus late payment interest at 0.20% per month (Article 1727 CGI); criminal penalty exposure under Articles 412-1 and 414-1 of the French Customs Code for fraudulent export claims of up to double the evaded duties and confiscation; the tax authority’s right of reassessment is extended from the standard three-year period to six years where the export activity is hidden or fraudulent (LPF Article L186).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under a fully convertible currency regime for both current and capital accounts. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted for EU-compliant entities, subject only to CRS/FATCA reporting, beneficial ownership disclosure under the French Monetary and Financial Code, and AML/KYC verification. No capital controls or profit remittance restrictions apply to properly structured French SAS entities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds required under French or EU law. Exporters must submit customs declarations and report foreign exchange transactions to the Banque de France via the Système Economique de Déclaration (SED) within 30 days. Conversion to euros occurs at prevailing market rates with no forced ratios or surrender timelines.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France and European Central Bank (ECB)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility; French regulations impose strict licensing requirements under MiCA and the French Banking Law, prohibiting or restricting offshore electronic money institutions for cross-border activities without local partnerships and full AML compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400 million (annual turnover or gross total assets of the French entity or the group it controls/belongs to) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File obligations; OECD Country-by-Country Report is required for groups whose consolidated annual revenue is €750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Although France does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, genuine economic substance is a core requirement for treaty benefits, for exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, and for avoiding the principal-purpose test under the MLI. A French SAS engaged in bonded wholesale wine and spirit trading must maintain real substance: local decision-making, qualified staff, stock custody records, warehouse licences, customs bonds and independent books. Shielding activity behind a shell French entity without local management will expose it to anti-abuse challenges under Art. L.64 LPF and to taxation of its passive income under CFC rules (Art. 209 B).
