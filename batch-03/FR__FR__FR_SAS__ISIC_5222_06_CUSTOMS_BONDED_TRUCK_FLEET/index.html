Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS; capital may be nominally EUR 1 or more. For cash contributions, at least 50% of each subscribed share must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions are deposited into a blocked company-formation account, and the depositary issues a certificate of deposit. Contributions in kind generally require an auditor's valuation unless all statutory waiver conditions are satisfied.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain municipal planning and building approvals for any depot or warehouse, consult the relevant Service départemental d'incendie et de secours (SDIS), and implement fire detection, extinguishing, emergency access, hazardous-material storage, evacuation, and prevention measures. Structural load, vehicle circulation, racking, electrical, and fuel-installation inspections must be completed where applicable. Maintain a French DUERP workplace-risk assessment, driver and warehouse training, occupational-health arrangements, accident procedures, working-time compliance, and required vehicle and equipment inspections.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS; capital may be nominally EUR 1 or more. For cash contributions, at least 50% of each subscribed share must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions are deposited into a blocked company-formation account, and the depositary issues a certificate of deposit. Contributions in kind generally require an auditor's valuation unless all statutory waiver conditions are satisfied.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible; France applies the EU free movement of capital (Art. 63 TFEU) and the Code monétaire et financier, with no substantive distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility. Cross-border remittances of dividends, branch profits, loan interest, and principal are not subject to exchange-control approval. The only administrative hurdles are: (i) ex-post FDI screening in defence, critical-infrastructure, and sensitive sectors — not applicable to ISO 5222 bonded road feeder trucking; (ii) statistical balance-of-payments and direct-investment filings to the Banque de France, which are declaratory rather than authorising; and (iii) French profit-extraction taxation, namely IS at 25% and dividend withholding at 25%, or 30% PFU for individuals, reduced under tax treaties and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France abolished the mandatory surrender ('régime de rapatriement') of export receivables during the 1989–1990 exchange-control liberalisation. There is no statutory reinstatement timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; the SAS may lawfully hold USD, GBP, or other export proceeds in designated foreign-currency accounts. Residual obligations are limited to: (i) physical cross-border cash movements above EUR 10,000 must be declared; (ii) export invoices and receipts must be properly documented for VAT, customs (DGDDI), and corporate tax audits; (iii) instructing banks file anonymised balance-of-payments declarations to the Banque de France where prescribed thresholds are met; and (iv) underlying supporting documents must be retained for at least 10 years.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), acting with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances as the foreign-exchange administration; bank and payment-institution prudential supervision is exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditional / Medium viability. An EU/EEA electronic-money institution holding an ACPR-authorised single passport may legally provide operational payment accounts, multicurrency wallets, and pay-out rails to a French SAS, subject to PSD2 conduct rules and ACPR registration. However, EMIs cannot issue the statutory blocked-funds certificate ('certificat de dépôt des fonds') required for cash capital formation under the Code de commerce, and they are not regarded as full 'établissements de crédit' for French customs guarantee business. Offshore or non-EEA EMIs without an ACPR passport are legally prohibited from providing payment services to French residents and are therefore non-viable for treasury settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound funding is routed by SEPA credit transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001) using purpose codes INTC (intercompany transfer), LOAN (shareholder loan), or EQUI (equity injection), credited to the French IBAN (FR76...); non-EUR injections are routed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 with the instructing bank quoting the French CFONB/Banque de France statistical motif so that the receiving bank can complete its balance-of-payments filing. Where the proceeds constitute initial share capital, the bank issues the statutory certificate of blocked funds; where they are shareholder loans, the transaction must be evidenced by a written loan deed for tax thin-capitalisation and transfer-pricing defence. No prior FX-control authorisation is required, but the SAS must confirm that the non-resident investor's sector is outside the DG Trésor strategic FDI list; bonded road feeder trucking is not a regulated sector.Clearing rails for trade settlements combine: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border foreign-currency disbursements to subcontractors and foreign airports; (ii) commercial letters of credit conforming to UCP 600 where large airport cargo contracts require documentary settlement; (iii) SEPA Instant and SEPA Credit Transfer for domestic and EU-EUR operational payouts covering fuel, tolls (Télépéage), maintenance, and subcontractor invoices; and (iv) DGDDI direct-debit or dedicated customs current-account credits for bonded-warehouse duties and guarantees. French banks execute foreign-currency payments under standing-order FX facilities, and the treasury should instruct payments before the FX cut-off to minimise nostro funding costs.Outright OTC FX forwards (change à terme) maturing up to 24 monthsFX swaps (swaps de change) and long-dated swap combinationsNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) for non-fully-convertible exotic currencies under an ISDA master agreementFX options (options de change) and call-spread/zero-cost collarsCross-currency interest rate swaps for matched asset-liability hedging of USD/GBP-denominated fleet financing
