Competent corporate registry

INPI Guichet Unique des Formalités (single electronic filing window); downstream judicial control and registration by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce in the territorial jurisdiction of the registered office, which maintains the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce): Articles L. 210-1 to L. 210-10 (general company provisions), Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-29 (Société par Actions Simplifiée), Articles L. 123-1 to L. 123-33 (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés), and Articles R. 224-1 et seq. (registration procedure and filing formalities).

Formation / constitutional filings

Company incorporation dossier filed as Form M0 (Demande d'immatriculation au RCS) together with the signed Articles of Association, filed electronically via the INPI Guichet Unique portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum share capital is required for an SAS (capital may lawfully be €1 or any higher amount). If capital is fixed, cash contributions must be paid up to at least 50% at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. The paid-up portion must be deposited to a blocked escrow account maintained with a French bank, the Caisse des Dépôts, or a notary, and may be released only after the delivery of the Kbis registration extract. Contributions in kind must be fully transferred at incorporation and reported in the Articles of Association. No statutory auditor (Commissaire aux Comptes) is required unless statutory turnover/balance-sheet/workforce thresholds are exceeded or the bylaws so provide.

Funding & unlock path: For any physical office and registered premises, obtain a fire safety classification and, where occupancy thresholds apply, a fire commission certificate for the premises (ERP class or simple office declaration). There is no mandatory civil defense clearance for software activity. The employer must carry out a single risk assessment document (Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels, DUERP) covering working-from-home, screen use, and electrical equipment, and register the staff health file; if more than 50 employees, establish the social and economic committee (CSE) and follow the occupational health and safety procedures.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum share capital is required for an SAS (capital may lawfully be €1 or any higher amount). If capital is fixed, cash contributions must be paid up to at least 50% at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. The paid-up portion must be deposited to a blocked escrow account maintained with a French bank, the Caisse des Dépôts, or a notary, and may be released only after the delivery of the Kbis registration extract. Contributions in kind must be fully transferred at incorporation and reported in the Articles of Association. No statutory auditor (Commissaire aux Comptes) is required unless statutory turnover/balance-sheet/workforce thresholds are exceeded or the bylaws so provide.

None for ISIC 6201 software publishing and remote access services; coordinate with the Direction Générale des Entreprises (DGE) for digital policy matters and with the CNIL for personal data in screen-sharing and IoT telemetry processing.

The legal operability proof is the Kbis extract; for regulated sectors involving critical infrastructure or health data, a sectoral authorization may be assessed as a fact-based case, but none is mandated for the ordinary SaaS commercial model.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) of the French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; online portal: impots.gouv.fr (professional e-service 'Gérer mon entreprise'). Customs and indirect taxes are jointly administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI); customs portal: douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate the SAS through the Guichet Unique des formalités des entreprises, transmitting the company statutes and declaration of the beneficial owner. Step 2 — Obtain from INSEE the SIREN and SIRET numbers and complete the enterprise register. Step 3 — Declare the start of the taxable activity; DGFiP automatically attributes the French intra-Community VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire) linked to the SIRET. Step 4 — Register for the DGFiP electronic filing service (impots.gouv.fr) for VAT returns (Form CA3) and corporate income tax returns. For a non-resident entity carrying out taxable supplies in France without a French establishment, file Form 3916-sd with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises to obtain a French VAT number; a tax representative may be required under Article 289 A of the French General Tax Code where the taxable person is not established in the EU and no VAT identification has been obtained in France.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a US-style check-the-box system. An SAS is by default a French corporate income taxpayer (impôt sur les sociétés) and cannot elect pass-through treatment. The only transparency option under Article 239 bis AB of the French General Tax Code is available to a small SARL/EURL meeting restrictive membership and turnover conditions, not to an SAS. Hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD are mandatory and may recharacterise cross-border payments or entities; no elective classifications are available outside the statutory framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

An established French SAS has no VAT registration threshold; French VAT compliance begins with the first taxable transaction. The small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA) for service activities, including remote desktop access and IoT telemetry software, applies only while annual turnover is ≤ EUR 36,600 (periodically adjusted by decree; a separate reduced threshold applies to certain regulated supplies). For intra-EU cross-border supplies of electronic services to consumers, a single Union-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies before French VAT or VAT One-Stop-Shop (OSS) registration becomes mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods, exports from French/EU customs territory qualify for the French VAT exemption with right to input VAT deduction under Article 262-1 of the French General Tax Code, provided the goods are physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU and documentary proof of export is retained. For remote desktop access, screen sharing and IoT telemetry software supplied to a B2B customer established outside France, the supply is treated as outside the French VAT scope because the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction under Article 259-1 of the French General Tax Code; proof of the customer's non-French business identity and VAT registration number is essential. B2C electronic services supplied to non-EU consumers are likewise outside the French VAT scope. No separate zero-rating certificate is required for these services; the important requirement is proper documentation of the customer's jurisdiction and, where relevant, the recipient's foreign VAT registration.

  • Customs export declaration (DAU / ECS) validated by DGDDI evidencing the physical departure of the goods from the EU
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, air waybill or equivalent transport document showing outbound carriage to a non-EU destination
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement proving receipt of export proceeds and FX repatriation
  • Commercial invoice, sales agreement and any technical documentation supporting the transaction
  • Customer's foreign VAT identification number and, where applicable, EC Sales List evidence for intra-community supplies

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing for French domestic B2B transactions is phased in from 1 September 2026 (all businesses must be able to receive e-invoices; large and mid-sized suppliers must issue e-invoices) and from 1 September 2027 (small and medium-sized suppliers must issue e-invoices). Invoices must be issued in a DGFiP-approved structured format (Factur-X, UBL, CII or equivalent) and exchanged through an accredited dematerialisation platform (PDP) or the Public Invoice Portal (PPF). The transmission platform automatically forwards mandatory invoice data and VAT e-reporting data to DGFiP through secured APIs. No real-time fiscal clearance or Italian-style digital stamping is currently mandated; the model is an audit-oriented e-reporting pipeline with full interoperability requirements.

Export zero-rating and out-of-scope service supplies are subject to intensive audit review by DGFiP and DGDDI. If the alleged export did not physically occur, the documentation is false or incomplete, or the customer's foreign VAT status is invalid, the VAT exemption or out-of-scope treatment is retrospectively withdrawn; all input VAT previously recovered or refunded is clawed back and the output VAT is reassessed for the full six-year limitation period. Penalties include an 80% bad-faith penalty for deliberate concealment, a 40% penalty for substantial omissions, and 30% or 10% for failures to file or declare, plus monthly late-payment interest at 0.20%. Criminal tax fraud under Article 1741 of the French General Tax Code can result in a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of up to five years. Under the Customs Code, fraudulent export declarations may lead to forfeiture of goods, a fine equal to one to three times the evaded duties and additional sanctions against the exporting company and its legal representatives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains no exchange-control regime; the euro is fully convertible and France observes IMF Article VIII obligations. Both current-account operations (software subscriptions, royalties, SaaS fees, trade in goods) and capital operations (equity injection, shareholder loans, dividend/profit remittance) are liberalized under TFEU Articles 63-66 without pre-authorization or quantitative limits for ISIC 6201 activity. Practical remittance hurdles are tax-driven only: dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders are subject to a 25% withholding tax under Article 119 bis 2 of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI), reduced to 0% where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions (minimum 5% holding, two-year retention) are met, and treaty relief may further cap source taxation. Royalties and software service fees remitted to non-residents may attract withholding under Article 182 B CGI at 25% unless reduced by treaty or eliminated by the royalty directive for payments to EU beneficiaries. Transfer-pricing documentation under Article L. 13 AA CGI must support any cross-border intercompany payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. France abolished export-proceeds surrender and conversion obligations in 1990; a French SAS may freely retain foreign currency in authorized foreign-currency accounts and may denominate invoices in any currency. The sole residual obligation is statistical reporting: every payment or credit between a French resident and a non-resident equal to or exceeding EUR 50,000 (single transaction or logically linked series) must be reported to the Banque de France under Regulation No. 2012-04 (as amended by Regulation No. 2023-01) for balance-of-payments statistics; transactions between EUR 10,000 and EUR 50,000 are aggregated and transmitted by the reporting bank quarterly. The enterprise must produce supporting invoices, contracts, and purpose codes upon demand. Cross-border cash or bearer transfers of EUR 10,000 or more require a customs declaration under Article L. 152-1 of the Code Monétaire et Financier. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (member of the Eurosystem/ESCB) is the central monetary authority; exchange-control and foreign-investment statistical oversight is shared with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty), with operational AML/CFT supervision delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

High feasibility for day-to-day treasury and PSP settlement through ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto (ACPR EMI licence, French IBAN) and Shine (Société Générale subsidiary), or EU-licensed EMIs passported into France (e.g., Revolut via its ACPR-branch status). Legal constraint: EMIs may issue electronic money and execute payment transactions but may not grant credit, open documentary credits, or act as statutory depositary for capital increase blocking certificates ('certificat de dépôt de fonds'), which under Articles L. 225-227 and L. 227-12 CGI/C. com. can only be issued by a licensed credit institution. Offshore digital EMIs without an ACPR single-passport or without registration as a payment agent under Article L. 521-3 Code Monétaire et Financier cannot lawfully provide payment services into France and must not receive French customer traffic; they may only act as white-label technical providers. For a French SAS with no minimum capital requirement, no legal capital-escrow account is mandated, but investors and auditors commonly require a Tier-1 bank account for substantial equity injections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity funding is executed by SEPA Credit Transfer (EUR, using the SAS's FR76 IBAN) or by SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR remitters (field 50K ordering customer, field 59 beneficiary account, field 70 purpose 'Apport en capital – augmentation de capital'), with the euro leg settled through TARGET2. No prior ministerial authorization applies to foreign investment in a French software company (ISIC 6201 is outside the strategic-sector decree of 31 December 2019 as amended). The bank requires the subscription deed, the shareholders' minutes approving the capital increase, the certificate of funds deposit, and source-of-funds evidence (investor bank statements, liquidation proof, or salary warrants). Any payment or series equal to or above EUR 50,000 is reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics; where the investor acquires at least 10% of voting rights, an inward FDI statistical declaration is filed with the Treasury/Banque de France OFI within 20 days of completion. Because the SAS has no minimum legal capital, no statutory blocking escrow is required, but if the parties contractually agree to hold funds pending inscription au RCS, the receiving bank may issue a blocked-deposit certificate.Euro disbursements within the EEA and SEPA zone are cleared via SEPA Credit Transfer on the T2 circuit, with SEPA Instant (limit EUR 100,000) available for real-time settlements; non-EEA or non-euro payments are routed via SWIFT MT103 (fields 32A, 57A, 71A, 72) through correspondent ACH systems (Fedwire/CHIPS, CHAPS, CIPS, ECS) with same-day or next-day value. Trade settlements involving documentary conditions use an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 and governed by UCP 600 with eUCP 2.0 addendum; MT707 amendments, MT752 payment authorizations, and Standby Letters of Credit under ISP 98 are available for IoT hardware and OEM component purchases. Every outbound transfer requires underlying pro-forma invoices or definitive contracts, and amounts exceeding EUR 50,000 trigger the bank's payment-reporting obligation plus an export declaration where physical goods cross borders. Stripe and Paddle, as the target payment rails, operate as licensed EU payment institutions and settle collected funds by SEPA Credit Transfer into the SAS's euro operational account, which remains fully compatible with the French clearing architecture and with the Banque de France reporting regime.Outright FX forward (change à terme) with a French or EU supervised credit institution covering EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CHF exposures arising from SaaS subscriptions, MSP contracts, or supplier invoices; the hedge must mirror an underlying commercial exposure, as purely speculative treasury trading is outside the permitted scope for a non-financial corporate and may raise prudential and directors'-duty issues.FX swap (swap de change) for rotating short-term USD or GBP positions where disbursements to non-EEA vendors are made in batches and where a single forward leg would create settlement-date mismatch.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) cash-settled in EUR on illiquid currencies (e.g., INR for Indian development subcontractors, TRY, NGN) where currency deliverability or convertibility restrictions in the counterparty jurisdiction prevent physical settlement; the NDF notion that must tie to a verifiable underlying invoice amount.Cross-currency interest rate swap (basis swap) executed under an ISDA Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex where the SAS holds a EUR floating-rate loan and a USD-denominated contract portfolio; EMIR trade-reporting obligations apply and the clearing threshold for non-financial counterparties must be monitored.FX option collar structures involving purchased options only (zero-cost collar or capped forward) through an EU-regulated market or a bank under an ISDA/CSA framework; writing naked options is prohibited for ordinary treasury risk-management purposes.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for day-to-day treasury and PSP settlement through ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto (ACPR EMI licence, French IBAN) and Shine (Société Générale subsidiary), or EU-licensed EMIs passported into France (e.g., Revolut via its ACPR-branch status). Legal constraint: EMIs may issue electronic money and execute payment transactions but may not grant credit, open documentary credits, or act as statutory depositary for capital increase blocking certificates ('certificat de dépôt de fonds'), which under Articles L. 225-227 and L. 227-12 CGI/C. com. can only be issued by a licensed credit institution. Offshore digital EMIs without an ACPR single-passport or without registration as a payment agent under Article L. 521-3 Code Monétaire et Financier cannot lawfully provide payment services into France and must not receive French customer traffic; they may only act as white-label technical providers. For a French SAS with no minimum capital requirement, no legal capital-escrow account is mandated, but investors and auditors commonly require a Tier-1 bank account for substantial equity injections.
