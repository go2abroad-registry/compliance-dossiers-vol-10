Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS; EUR 1 is legally permissible, although higher capital is commercially advisable. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Cash contributions are deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des dépôts et consignations, which issues a deposit certificate. The deposit account is released after registration. In-kind contributions are generally transferred at incorporation and may require a commissaire aux apports unless a statutory exemption applies. The stated USD fee is an approximate exchange-rate equivalent of the standard commercial registration charge and excludes legal-notice publication, beneficial-owner filing, translations, professional fees, and sectoral permits.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Generale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the public portal impots.gouv.fr; French customs and indirect-tax control at borders is exercised by Direction Generale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) for TIR customs and export/import formalities.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a French SAS or branch registers online through the Guichet Unique des Formalites des Entreprises (formalities.entreprises.gouv.fr), obtaining the INSEE-issued SIREN/SIRET identifier; DGFiP then allocates the intra-Community VAT number (FR followed by 11 digits) to the registered establishment. Foreign suppliers without a French establishment but triggering French VAT liability must apply directly to the DGFiP (Service des Impots des Entreprises) under the non-established taxable person registration procedure.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing a French SAS or branch registers online through the Guichet Unique des Formalites des Entreprises (formalities.entreprises.gouv.fr), obtaining the INSEE-issued SIREN/SIRET identifier; DGFiP then allocates the intra-Community VAT number (FR followed by 11 digits) to the registered establishment. Foreign suppliers without a French establishment but triggering French VAT liability must apply directly to the DGFiP (Service des Impots des Entreprises) under the non-established taxable person registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The SAS is by default subject to French entity-level corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Societes); it may elect pass-through income tax treatment under CGI Article 239 bis AA where the SME conditions are satisfied; a group may elect 100% consolidation (integration fiscale) under CGI Article 223 A; VAT grouping as a single taxable person may be elected under CGI Article 1693 ter; hybrid mismatch outcomes are constrained by the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive implementation in French domestic law, and France does not operate unilateral check-the-box elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA: lower exemption thresholds of EUR 91,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 36,800 for supplies of services, with upper tolerance thresholds of EUR 101,000 and EUR 42,500 respectively (2023 rules); non-established foreign suppliers generally cannot benefit from the franchise and must register from the first taxable transaction in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (i) goods physically exported outside the European Union where the customs export declaration and transport evidence demonstrate exit from EU customs territory (CGI Article 262, 1°); (ii) supply of international freight transport services to a business customer established outside France, where the B2B place-of-supply rules locate the supply outside French VAT scope (CGI Article 259 B); and (iii) intra-Community dispatches to a VAT-registered customer holding a valid intra-Community VAT number, supported by proof of movement. For TIR road transport, zero-rating is substantiated by the TIR Carnet, CMR waybill, and customs transit documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/ENS and, where applicable, TIR Carnet)
  • CMR Waybill (Contrat de transport international de marchandises par route)
  • Bill of Lading or Road Consignment Note
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Proof of exit from France and EU customs territory (customs stamps or electronic clearance data)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment evidence

France mandates structured electronic invoicing and e-reporting under the 2021/2022 e-invoicing reform: from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size businesses and 1 September 2027 for small and micro businesses, invoices must be issued in Factur-X, UBL or CII format through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the Public Invoicing Portal (PPF). No per-transaction digital stamping applies; transaction-line data must be transmitted to DGFiP through the PPF API environment and directory systems, and platforms must maintain interoperable APIs and complete audit trails.

DGFiP may audit the export substantiation file within the statutory limitation period (generally the ordinary three-year period under Article L.169 of the LPF, extended where omissions or fraudulent conduct exist). Unsupported export zero-rating triggers VAT reassessment with late-payment interest of 0.20% per month, penalties of 40% for deliberate inaccuracies and 80% for abusive or artificial arrangements under Article 1729 CGI, and potential criminal tax-fraud liability under Article 1741 CGI (up to five years' imprisonment, extended to ten years in aggravated cases). Incorrectly claimed refunds of input VAT are clawed back in full, with the burden of proof resting on the taxpayer to demonstrate the reality of the underlying export transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a regime of full convertibility for the euro on both current and capital accounts. Residents and French entities may freely transfer funds abroad, subject to tax declaration thresholds (e.g., €100,000 annually for capital movements) and AML compliance. Profit remittances to EU shareholders are unrestricted, with no capital controls or mandatory surrender mechanisms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender of export proceeds required in France. Exporters must file customs declarations within 30 days of shipment under TIR Carnets procedures. Conversion occurs at market rates with no fixed ratios. Tax authorities require supporting documentation for VAT and corporate tax credits; bonded transport compliance is mandatory but FX surrender is not.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasible under EU PSD2 and French regulations for digital payments, but constrained for physical trucking operations; offshore EMIs require local banking partnerships and face strict authorization and AML requirements.
