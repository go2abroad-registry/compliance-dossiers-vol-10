Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises operated by INPI (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); name and prior-rights verification databases: INPI Data (data.inpi.fr) and INFOGREFFE search portal for the Registre National du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS)

Name reservation / lock period

France does not operate a statutory pre-registration name-reservation system; a proposed SAS name is examined only at the moment the incorporation file is lodged with the Guichet Unique. Upon rejection on name grounds, the applicant receives a formal notice of deficiency and may submit a rectified filing (dossier rectificatif) within 15 days through the same Guichet Unique. If the name is refused because it conflicts with a prior registered business name or protected trademark, the applicant may: (i) obtain a written authorization from the prior right holder, (ii) amend the corporate name, or (iii) contest the rejection. Administrative remedies include a two-month 'recours gracieux hiérarchique' before the INPI Director General, followed by a contentious administrative appeal (recours contentieux) before the administrative court of the INPI's seat; commercial disputes over name infringement fall within the exclusive jurisdiction of the Tribunal de Commerce, and coexistence agreements or trademark settlements are commonly executed in parallel.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Cash contributions may be deposited with a French bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts; the deposit certificate is required for registration. Contributions in kind generally require an auditor's valuation unless statutory exemptions apply.

Funding & unlock path: No special industrial clearance is ordinarily required for an online retailer. Premises open to the public require applicable ERP fire-safety and accessibility compliance, including inspection or authorization where required. Warehouses must maintain fire precautions, emergency routes, electrical safety, storage controls, and workplace risk assessments under the French Labour Code. Structural-load review is required where shelving, heavy printers, cylinders, or other concentrated loads are installed.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Cash contributions may be deposited with a French bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts; the deposit certificate is required for registration. Contributions in kind generally require an auditor's valuation unless statutory exemptions apply.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) – France’s national tax administration, operating under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty. Primary digital portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; business e-administration via www.chorus-pro.gouv.fr for e-invoicing and www.gérer-son-entreprise.gouv.fr for company-related filings.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS formed domestically: (1) register the company with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the Guichet Unique, which generates the SIREN and SIRET identifiers and transmits incorporation data to DGFIP; (2) DGFIP automatically assigns the company’s corporate tax number and, upon confirmation of French VAT-exercising status, issues a French intra-Community VAT identification number in the format FR + 11 key digits; (3) if the foreign parent is a non-EU entity, the SAS is itself the French taxable entity and no permanent-establishment registration is required, but the parent must not maintain a separate branch in France unless separately registered. For a foreign entity operating directly in France without a French legal entity: a French tax representation (représentant fiscal agréé) must be appointed for VAT and certain taxes, and the entity must file a formality of registration at the Centre des Impôts et des Finances Publiques (SIE) of its principal French place of business; a simplified VAT registration can be obtained online. All registrations require a valid legal form proof, identity documents, and details of intended French turnover.

All registrations require a valid legal form proof, identity documents, and details of intended French turnover.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Société par Actions Simplifiée is by default subject to French corporate income tax, impôt sur les sociétés, under CGI Art. 206(1); profits are taxed at entity level on globally taxable income, but France applies a territoriality principle limiting corporate tax to profits attributable to French operations unless a group election is available).

Available election forms

France does not operate a U.S.-style ‘check-the-box’ classification election; the tax status of an SAS is fixed by law as a capital company subject to corporate income tax. Available elections include: (a) optional tax integration under CGI Art. 223A for a French parent owning 95% of subsidiaries, allowing a single tax group to offset sub-group profits and losses; (b) the option for parent-subsidiary participation exemption under the régime mère-fille, reducing dividend taxation, and (c) for certain qualifying small businesses, the simplified VAT cash accounting or payment franchise regime may be elected, but no entity-partnership pass-through election is available for an SAS. France applies EU and domestic anti-hybrid mismatch rules without elective mechanisms; reporting obligations apply under DAC6/MDR for reportable cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established taxable person, no compulsory VAT registration is required where annual taxable turnover does not exceed the franchise en base de TVA threshold under CGI Art. 293 B. As of the 2024 regime, the threshold is EUR 91,900 for supplies of goods (margin inclusive), and EUR 36,800 for supplies of services; a lower reduced threshold applies for certain professions. For a non-established e-commerce seller selling goods to French consumers, there is no absolute registration threshold for local VAT – distance sales of goods installed/arriving in France are taxable from the first euro, but the OSS (One-Stop Shop) procedure may be used for intra-EU distance sales and IOSS for imports of low-value consignments, avoiding separate French registration in many cases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: VAT is zero-rated on the export of tangible goods outside the European Union only if the goods physically leave the EU customs territory within the legal timeframe and the transfer of ownership or supply for consideration is made to a customer established outside the EU. The tax exemption under CGI Art. 262-I-1° requires the exporter to hold contemporaneous evidence of export such as the customs export declaration (DAU) and proof of physical dispatch (transport document) issued to or from France; proof of exit from the EU must be obtained within 12 months from the time of supply for most direct exports. For services: B2B services supplied to a customer established outside the EU are generally subject to the reverse-charge outside France and are outside the scope of French VAT or zero-rated where directly linked to exported goods (e.g., transport and ancillary services under CGI Arts. 259 and 262). An SAS must retain commercial and banking evidence linking the transaction to actual overseas delivery; mere issuance of a zero-rated invoice without substantiation of export is legally ineffective and leads to VAT collection liability.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Échange de Biens / DAU or export summary declaration) accepted by French Customs SDGF/Inward and Outward Processing system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill/CMR transport document evidencing physical exportation from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit proof showing the foreign buyer’s payment received on a French bank account and matching the consideration
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and written export contract/order
  • Proof of customer’s non-EU establishment (e.g., VAT registration certificate or United Kingdom/third-country commercial register extract)

France is transitioning to mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting for domestic B2B transactions under the 2024 Finance Law. A real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is not applied historically, e.g., unlike Italy or Mexico, but from the phased implementation dates (currently from 1 September 2026 for large/mid-market enterprises and 1 September 2027 for all other taxable persons, subject to legislative confirmations) all established suppliers must issue structured e-invoices through a certified Partner Dematerialisation Platform (PDP) or the Chorus Pro public portal, using formats compliant with EN 16931 (Factur-X, UBL 2.1 or CII). E-reporting data (including intra-EU B2B supplies) must be transmitted in real-time or periodically through the same secured API interfaces to DGFIP; invoices must be digitally readable and unalterable, but France does not yet require real-time tax authority clearance of each individual invoice before issuance. Marketplaces and payment service providers have separate reporting duties under the EU DAC7 framework, and all VAT-relevant cash flows should be traceable through the electronic reporting platform.

French tax authorities conduct targeted checks on export zero-rating claims, requiring all documentary evidence to be produced upon request. Failure to substantiate the zero rate, untimely export (after the statutory period), or fictive/bogus export arrangements triggers a reassessment of output VAT at the 20% standard rate, with interest for late payment and penalties under CGI Arts. 1727 and 1729: 40% for serious misstatements, 80% for abusive fraud, further escalated where artificial or circular structures are found. Fraudulent export schemes may additionally attract criminal tax penalties under Art. 1741 CGI and customs penalties (e.g., fine up to EUR 37,500 and/or seizure), as well as the right of VAT authorities to deny deduction of input VAT associated with the export transaction. The documentation bundle must be retained for at least six years and demonstrably transmitted to the relevant authorities; the 180-day FX repatriation default is treated as a strong audit indicator, and non-repatriation without valid commercial reason creates a rebuttable presumption of non-export or fiction of supply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a eurozone Member State, guarantees full euro convertibility for current-account and capital-account operations under TFEU Article 63 and the Code monétaire et financier. Non-resident shareholders face no exchange-control approval for equity contributions, reinvested earnings, liquidation proceeds, or disposal of shares. Dividend remittances from the SAS to a foreign corporate parent are freely payable but tax-encumbered: the 25% French domestic withholding tax on dividends is reduced to 5% under the France-China Double Taxation Treaty (2013) when the beneficial owner directly holds at least 25% of the issued capital, or to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when the distribution routes through an EU intermediate holding company with economic substance and a minimum 5% holding. No FX rationing, currency bands, conversion quota, or central-bank approval veto is applicable; the only procedural screen is the IEF direct-investment notification, which is not triggered for non-sensitive online retail distribution of desktop 3D printers and consumables.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation or compulsory conversion ratio exists in France. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in EUR or in any foreign currency in resident or non-resident accounts. The sole mandatory requirement is statistical balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque de France for monthly settlements exceeding EUR 50,000 per non-resident counterparty, submitted within five business days through the TELEFAC electronic collection system; no repatriation timeline, forced conversion, or export recall quota is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (central bank and balance-of-payments statistical supervisor within the Eurosystem/ECB); Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as banking licensing and prudential authority; Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) as administrator of the EU FDI screening (IEF) regime and exchange-control-related payment procedures.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

High for EU-licensed EMIs and payment institutions passporting into France under PSD2, notably Qonto (French ACPR-licensed EMI), Shine (French payment institution consolidated under Societe Generale), and Revolut Business (Revolut Bank UAB of Lithuania); these are fully lawful treasury vehicles for marketplace settlement capture, virtual cards, and API reporting. Low for offshore non-EU EMIs (UK-only authorized firms, UAE or Singapore money transmitters): such entities cannot lawfully receive funds from the French SAS without a separate ACPR licence or passport, as this would breach the reserved activity of receiving funds from the public under Article L511-1 of the Code monétaire et financier. If two licensed banks refuse account opening, the SAS may invoke the legal right to an account (droit au compte) before the Banque de France under Article L312-1.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder subscribes SAS equity by issuing an SWIFT MT103 in EUR through a correspondent bank to the blocked capital escrow account (compte de dépôt) designated at the onboarding French lender. The instruction must cite the Banque de France balance-of-payments direct-investment-equity category and reference the subscription resolution (procès-verbal de souscription), the statuts, and the RBE/registration project. The depositary holds the funds until the Kbis formalities are completed at the commercial court registry, then releases the escrow to the unblocked operational account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A reduced 15% rate applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying small companies, generally subject to turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up share capital, and at least 75% individual ownership or equivalent qualifying conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The general French withholding tax rate on dividends and similar distributions paid to a non-resident is generally 25%, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable tax treaties. Dividends paid by a French subsidiary to a qualifying EU parent may be exempt under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring a qualifying ownership percentage, minimum holding period, subject-to-tax status, and anti-abuse compliance. A French company receiving dividends from a qualifying subsidiary may also access the participation exemption regime, generally with a 5% add-back, subject to conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the return is generally due on the second business day following 1 May of the following year, subject to the current year's official calendar. For a financial year ending on another date, the return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The tax administration may provide an electronic filing tolerance or additional electronic period, but the applicable official deadline must be verified for the relevant year. The balance of corporate income tax is generally due by the fifteenth day of the fourth month following year-end, or by the fifteenth day of the fifth month for a 31 December year-end, subject to current administrative rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer pricing documentation under French Tax Procedure Code Article L13 AA is generally required where the French entity or relevant group satisfies the EUR 400 million threshold based on annual turnover or gross assets, or where specified ownership, control, or French tax-consolidation conditions apply. A separate annual simplified transfer pricing declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies to entities meeting the EUR 50 million threshold or other statutory conditions. The EUR 400 million threshold is not a safe harbor from the arm's-length principle for smaller entities.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone economic substance regime equivalent to certain offshore economic substance regulations. Substance is nevertheless enforced through the arm's-length principle, abuse-of-law doctrine, principal-purpose anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation, permanent-establishment principles, and general anti-avoidance provisions. A French SAS should maintain French commercial decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, inventory and logistics evidence, customer and supplier contracts, local banking and accounting records, board governance, and demonstrable functions and risks. A French entity that is only a contractual invoicing shell, lacks decision-making capacity, or systematically shifts profit to a foreign affiliate faces recharacterization and penalty risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Administrative tax reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties up to 80% in aggravated cases, and criminal prosecution. Tax fraud may result in imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000; aggravated cases may reach seven years and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory aggravating conditions. Corporate fines may be multiplied under French criminal law.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face criminal prosecution, personal tax liability in cases of fraudulent or repeated conduct causing tax loss, civil claims, asset recovery, and professional disqualification. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal proceedings where legally justified.
VAT carousel, import VAT, and OSS non-compliance

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties generally from 10% to 80% depending on conduct, customs duties and import VAT, seizure or customs sanctions, and possible criminal fraud proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they knowingly organize fraudulent VAT arrangements, participate in customs fraud, or continue unlawful conduct after notice. Ordinary corporate VAT debt does not automatically become personal debt.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment under the arm's-length principle, non-deductibility of expenses, withholding tax exposure, interest, transfer pricing penalties, documentation penalties, and possible escalation under anti-abuse rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for breach of management duties and criminal exposure where records or invoices are knowingly falsified. Personal liability is not automatic solely because the company loses a transfer pricing dispute.
Shell company and lack of French substance

Statutory sanction: Profit attribution to the correct jurisdiction, permanent-establishment or agency recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, withholding tax, reassessment, interest, and penalties. Sham or abusive arrangements may be challenged under the abuse-of-law doctrine.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil, tax, or criminal liability if they knowingly operate a fictitious structure, divert assets, or organize fraud. Veil piercing is exceptional and requires legally recognized misconduct; it is not automatic for inadequate substance.
Illegal dividend or unlawful profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, director liability, corporate sanctions, potential criminal penalties for unlawful dividends, and withholding tax reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize unlawful distributions may be personally liable for restitution and damages and may face criminal sanctions. Travel bans or imprisonment require a separate judicial basis and are not automatic.
Accounting and corporate-record falsification

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, accounting penalties, rejection of deductions, criminal prosecution for false accounting or related offenses, and insolvency-related sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face imprisonment, fines, management bans, civil restitution, and liability for an insufficiency of assets in insolvency where management misconduct contributed to the shortfall.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing controls

Statutory sanction: Transaction freezing, regulatory measures, confiscation, administrative or criminal fines, and prosecution for money laundering. Obligations depend on the business activity and regulated status of the entity or service provider.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly facilitate laundering may face imprisonment, fines, confiscation, and professional prohibitions. Directors may be liable for governance failures where a specific statutory AML duty applies.
Customs, product-safety, and environmental compliance for 3D printers and consumables

Statutory sanction: Customs duty and VAT recovery, seizure, administrative fines, product withdrawal or recall, sales prohibition, and criminal sanctions for serious or intentional breaches.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal sanctions where they knowingly market unsafe or non-compliant products, obstruct recalls, or falsify conformity documentation. Corporate limited liability does not protect intentional personal misconduct.
Failure to withhold or report cross-border payments

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, late-payment interest, filing penalties, denial of treaty relief, and possible anti-abuse reassessment.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur liability for deliberate withholding failures or fraudulent arrangements. Ordinary inadvertent filing errors generally remain corporate liabilities subject to statutory penalty procedures.
Insolvency and wrongful continuation of business

Statutory sanction: Asset recovery, clawback of transactions, director-management bans, contribution to insolvency liabilities, bankruptcy-related criminal sanctions, and potential confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face civil restitution, contribution to the insufficiency of assets, management bans, fines, and imprisonment in serious cases. Courts may impose travel restrictions only where specifically authorized by applicable procedural law.
