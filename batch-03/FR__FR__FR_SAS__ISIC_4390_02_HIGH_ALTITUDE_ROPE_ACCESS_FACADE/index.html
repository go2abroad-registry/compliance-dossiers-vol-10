Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for corporate income tax and VAT collection; Direction des Impôts des Non-Résidents (DINR), 10 rue du Centre, 93199 Noisy-le-Grand, France, for foreign non-established taxable persons; national tax portal: www.impots.gouv.fr. Company registration and tax identifier issuance are centralized through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) and its 'Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises' at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Customs export and import clearance is administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) through the DELTA/DEMS electronic filing platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the standard M0 creation dossier for the SAS with the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) issues SIREN (9-digit national identifier), SIRET (14-digit establishment identifier) and the NAF/APE code aligned to ISIC 4390. Step 2: DGFiP automatically registers the entity for corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) and territorial economic contribution (CET) using the SIRET as the national tax ID; no separate TIN application is required for a resident SAS. Step 3: Obtain the French VAT intra-Community number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN) by filing the VAT registration form (Cerfa No. 16216 / Form 3559) with the competent SIE, normally within 15 days of the first taxable transaction. Step 4: For a foreign entity performing rope-access facade works in France without a permanent establishment, apply as a 'non-established taxable person' to the DINR using Form 3559-NR before the first taxable operation; operators established outside the EU must additionally appoint an accredited fiscal representative in France under art. 289-A of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI), while EU-established operators need no representative. Step 5: Default to the normal monthly VAT return (CA3) regime; DGFiP activates the operator's records in the EU VIES database and issues the final intra-Community VAT ID upon completion of anti-fraud screening.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French tax law does not offer a US-style check-the-box or elective transparency regime. The SAS is statutorily classified as an entity-level taxpayer subject to corporate income tax under art. 206(1) CGI at the standard 25% headline rate and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Available statutory elections include: (i) reduced IS rate of 15% on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs with turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million (art. 219-II CGI); (ii) election for fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under art. 223-A CGI where a French parent holds at least 95% of the SAS, enabling single-group taxable result; (iii) option for the parent-subsidiary 'régime mère-fille' participation exemption on qualifying 5%-or-more holdings; (iv) ministerial-agreement options for global or consolidated result (bénéfice mondial/consolidé) in exceptional cases; (v) option for VAT grouping (assujettissement unique en TVA) under art. 1693-ter CGI for closely linked entities with financial, economic and organizational interconnections; (vi) option for a 3-year full or partial IS exemption as a Jeune Entreprise Innovante (JEI) where qualifying R&D expenditure thresholds are met; and (vii) the reduced taxation of dividends within the mother-daughter regime. No elective hybrid mismatch election exists: France implements ATAD (EU Directive 2016/1164) mandatorily, so hybrid payments, reverse hybrids and double-deduction mismatches are neutralized compulsorily and cannot be activated or deactivated by taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Established suppliers benefit from the small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA) under art. 293-B CGI: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for sales of goods and EUR 37,500 for provision of services. However, for non-established foreign suppliers the threshold is nil: any taxable supply in France triggers compulsory VAT registration from the first euro. Under art. 259-3 CGI, services relating to immovable property—including rope-access facade repair, structural glass replacement and installation works on supertall skyscrapers—are taxable in France wherever the property is located, irrespective of the supplier's establishment. Intra-Community distance sales of goods into France are taxable only above the EUR 10,000 EU-wide One-Stop-Shop (OSS) threshold, whereas construction services on French immovable property remain taxable from the first euro with no threshold relief.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération with right to deduct) applies under art. 262-I CGI to definitive exports of goods outside the European Union evidenced by customs clearance, and under art. 262-ter CGI to qualified intra-Community dispatches evidenced by the customer's valid EU VAT identification number and transport documentation. For the rope-access facade activity, works physically performed on immovable property situated in France fall within the French place-of-supply rule under art. 259-3 CGI and are chargeable to 20% VAT; export zero-rating is not available for domestic facade operations. Zero-rating is claimable only for (i) prefabricated glass/facade modules sold and physically exported without installation, (ii) materials separately invoiced and exported for incorporation into works located outside France, and (iii) facade services executed entirely outside the EU, which are outside the scope of French VAT rather than formally zero-rated. For B2B services to foreign clients under the general art. 259-A CGI place-of-supply rule, the supplier must prove the recipient's establishment outside France and demonstrate that the supply is not connected with French immovable property; a construction contract on French skyscrapers cannot be recharacterized as an export service.

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting reform (2023 Finance Act and Décret n°2022-1299; CGI art. 289 bis): from 1 September 2026 all French-established taxable persons must be able to receive structured electronic invoices; large and mid-size enterprises must issue them from 1 September 2026, and small/micro enterprises from 1 September 2027. Invoices must flow through the national Public Invoicing Portal (Portail Public de Facturation, PPF) or a certified private dematerialization platform (PDP) using the mandatory API (API Facturation / API Partenaires) and one of the mandatory formats Factur-X 1.0, UBL 2.1 or CII 16B. Fiscal clearance is quasi real-time: transaction and payment data (e-reporting) must be transmitted to DGFiP no later than the day following invoice issuance and within 10 days of payment; the PPF serves as the central tax data hub. B2G invoices addressed to French public entities must additionally be routed via Chorus Pro with a mandatory service code. Zero-rated export invoices, although exempt with credit, remain subject to the same e-invoice and e-reporting transmission requirements.

DGFiP may claw back the full VAT incorrectly zero-rated, add statutory late-payment interest (intérêt de retard, approximately 0.2% per month), and apply the 40% bad-faith penalty (mauvaise foi), the 80% fraudulent-maneuver penalty (manœuvre frauduleuse) under art. 1729 CGI, or up to 100% in cases of organized tax fraud. Fictitious export schemes trigger the extended five-year assessment period under art. L. 186 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales (LPF) — extended to ten years where the taxpayer is established outside France or conceals its activity. Criminal sanctions under art. 1741 CGI can reach five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, aggravated to seven years and EUR 3,000,000 for organized gang fraud; art. 1737 CGI adds civil fines of up to EUR 37,500 per dishonest invoice; and the administration may suspend the taxpayer's right to invoice, block refunds, and revoke access to the e-invoicing portal. An audit file must substantiate the physical exit of goods within the statutory proof deadlines (normally three months for exports, one year for intra-Community movements) and the corresponding foreign-exchange settlement within 180 days of shipment; failure to repatriate proceeds may result in the reinvoicing and collection of French TVA on the unreported export value under the exchange-control provisions of the Code Monétaire et Financier.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France has full legal and practical convertibility for both current and capital account transactions, subject only to EU free movement of capital under TFEU Article 63 and French prudential or balance-of-payments reporting duties. There is no central bank approval requirement for inward equity subscription or outward profit remittance; no FX conversion, allocation, or surrender obligation applies to euro-denominated receipts. Dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted from an FX standpoint, provided the SAS follows French corporate distribution formalities, verifies applicable withholding tax relief under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or bilateral tax treaty, and satisfies DAC6/MDR or anti-abuse documentation requirements. Restrictions affecting capital movements exist only for strategic and defense sectors, dual-use goods, national security screening, or targeted financial sanctions imposed by the EU, UN, OFAC, and the French consolidated sanctions list.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose statutory surrender of export proceeds and does not require mandatory conversion into EUR. Export receipts from cross-border rope-access facade services may be retained in foreign currency in a dedicated currency account at an authorized credit institution without a maximum holding period. There is no minimum conversion ratio and no repatriation deadline. However, all cross-border monetary flows, including export proceeds, are subject to the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting regime and to EU/UN sanctions screening; the reporting obligation commonly rests on the intermediary bank unless the SAS is itself designated as a declaration entity.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the national central bank and foreign exchange administration within the Eurosystem; policy coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor and prudential supervision through the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution under the Code monétaire et financier, Articles L. 411-1 through L. 411-5 and R. 411-1 through R. 411-7.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: CONDITIONAL/MEDIUM for operational treasury disbursements; LOW for capital escrow and statutory capital commitment. A French or EU e-money institution authorized by the ACPR or benefiting from a PSD2/E-Money Directive passport may hold safeguarded funds and execute SEPA/SWIFT payments, but it cannot receive deposits or grant credit. Digital EMIs are not acceptable as sole evidence of banking substance for the SAS capital escrow account and cannot issue documentary credits or bank guarantees; they may be used as execution-only payment rails if a Tier-1 euro settlement bank acts as the underlying treasury and clearing counterparty.

Inbound equity and shareholder advances must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer, with MT202 cover where interbank routing requires it, from the non-resident parent or group treasury center to the SAS IBAN at the receiving Tier-1 bank.
