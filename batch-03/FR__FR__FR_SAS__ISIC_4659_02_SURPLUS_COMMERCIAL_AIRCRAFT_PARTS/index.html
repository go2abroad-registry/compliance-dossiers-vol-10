Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual/domiciliation office is legally acceptable for the registered seat of a French SAS under Articles L.123-11-2 and L.123-11-3 of the French Commercial Code, provided the company subscribes to a licensed domiciliation company service and the domiciliation contract is recorded in the RCS filing. However, for a commercial aircraft rotables and avionics surplus distribution business, the virtual office is only acceptable during the initial incorporation and first operational quarter; it does not satisfy commercial substance requirements for DGAC/FAA/EASA distribution oversight or French customs VAT registration. The company must establish a physically staffed and addressable office, warehouse, or contracted logistics facility in France before invoicing aircraft parts in the EU and must be able to demonstrate dedicated management, inventory control, and trade documentation presence.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SAS (may be €1); however, for this cross-border aviation spares distribution activity a paid-up capital of at least €10,000 is recommended. The full subscribed capital must be deposited in a blocked bank or notarial escrow account before filing and released only upon issuance of the RCS certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification required for the storage facility, covering electrical installations, escape routes, extinguishing systems, and emergency signposting; structural load inspection verifying floor loading capacity appropriate to aircraft parts shelving; workplace clearance issued following an occupational health inspection and the production of the DUERP (document unique d'évaluation des risques professionnels).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member; the euro is freely convertible with unrestricted capital and current-account transactions pursuant to Article 63 TFEU and the French Monetary and Financial Code (CMF). No exchange-control authorization or monetary compensation obligation applies. Profit remittance: qualifying EU/EEA corporate shareholders benefit from the 0% Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption; non-EU shareholders rely on treaty rates (typically 0-30%) or a 25% default withholding, subject to formalities such as the attestation de résidence fiscale and pre-payment return (RFI). Practical hurdles are limited to anti-money-laundering onboarding, tax-residency certification, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and balance-of-payments statistical declarations to the Banque de France for cross-border flows involving non-residents

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, compulsory conversion, or repatriation timeline exists under French external-finance regulations. Export receivables and foreign-currency accounts may be retained offshore or converted freely. A statistical reporting obligation applies where current-account and financial flows with non-residents individually exceed EUR 50,000 or aggregate monthly flows surpass the Banque de France threshold. Aircraft-parts export transactions remain subject to EU dual-use export-control screening and French customs clearance (EORI, DAU/formal entry), but not to FX surrender or conversion ratios

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as the Central Bank and balance-of-payments statistical authority, with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for foreign-exchange legislative policy and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking and e-money supervision

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Operational feasibility: Medium-High for euro-denominated expense payments through ACPR-licensed electronic-money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Treezor, Lydia Payments, Nickel); Low-Critical for aviation-escrow, commercial LC, and trade-finance rails because EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot legally take repayable deposits, issue letters of credit under UCP600, or provide escrow guarantees without breaching the French banking monopoly (CMF arts. L511-1 and L521-3). Offshore digital EMIs (UK-FCA, Lithuanian, or Cypriot authorized) have limited or no EU-passport access to France post-Brexit and lack the correspondent clearing and title-custody infrastructure required for aircraft-parts settlement; they may serve only as ancillary disbursement vehicles, never as the main treasury counterparty. A Tier-1 bank remains mandatory for the capital-blocking certificate and aviation escrow.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. Exemptions may apply under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, including qualifying ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax and anti-abuse conditions. A tax treaty may reduce the rate, commonly to 5%, 10% or 15%, depending on the treaty and ownership percentage.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the electronic return and tax package are generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the following year, subject to the standard additional electronic-filing tolerance where applicable. For a non-calendar year-end, filing is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The final corporate income tax balance is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, with specific rules for 31 December year-ends.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Withholding tax on qualifying payments to non-residents is generally declared and paid within the statutory period applicable to the payment and recipient category, commonly through the relevant French withholding form and electronic payment process. Dividend distributions may require payment of withholding tax within the applicable statutory period following payment or allocation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets, or ownership or control of an entity meeting the threshold, generally triggers the French full transfer-pricing documentation requirement under Article L13 AA of the French Tax Procedures Book. A simplified documentation requirement generally applies below that threshold for taxpayers subject to transfer-pricing scrutiny. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups meeting the EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone regime identical to a separate offshore Economic Substance Regulations code. Substance is enforced through the general anti-abuse doctrine, principal-purpose and treaty anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, permanent-establishment principles and transfer-pricing requirements. A French SAS distributing surplus commercial aircraft parts should maintain French decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, inventory and logistics control, customer and supplier functions, risk management, bank activity, accounting records and evidence that the company performs the functions for which it earns its margin.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax returns

Statutory sanction: Recovery of tax, late-payment interest, administrative surcharges generally ranging from 10% to 80%, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that may include up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, with aggravated cases potentially reaching seven years' imprisonment and EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, tax penalties and civil liability where they personally participated in, authorized or facilitated the conduct. Courts may impose management bans and confiscation. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from judicial control, arrest warrants or criminal proceedings.
Fraudulent insolvency and asset diversion

Statutory sanction: Bankruptcy-related criminal offenses may result in imprisonment, fines, director-management bans and civil claims by the insolvency practitioner or creditors. Exact sanctions depend on the offense and facts.

Director personal liability: Directors may face liability for insufficient assets, wrongful trading-type conduct, management misconduct, restitution and contribution claims. The corporate veil is not automatically pierced; personal liability requires a statutory basis or proven personal fault.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and hidden profit distribution

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, denial of deductions, withholding-tax recovery, interest, penalties for deliberate or fraudulent conduct and possible documentation-related penalties. Significant penalties may apply for failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they knowingly organized fraudulent profit diversion or misused corporate assets. Ordinary unsuccessful pricing judgments do not alone create personal liability.
Customs fraud and export-control violations

Statutory sanction: Customs duties, import VAT, seizure, forfeiture, customs fines and criminal sanctions may apply. Export-control breaches may produce administrative restrictions, criminal penalties and denial of future authorizations, depending on the goods and conduct.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and officers may be prosecuted where they ordered, knowingly approved or negligently enabled unlawful shipments. Travel restrictions, asset freezes or imprisonment depend on judicial action and are not automatic.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing controls

Statutory sanction: Potential criminal money-laundering penalties include imprisonment and fines, with aggravated treatment for habitual, organized or professional conduct. Separate sanctions may arise under financial-sanctions, customs and payment regulations.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and employees may be individually liable for knowing participation, facilitation or deliberate failure to act. Corporate fines, confiscation, professional bans and judicial restrictions may accompany individual sanctions.
Sanctions and embargo violations

Statutory sanction: Asset freezes, payment blocking, administrative enforcement, customs seizure, substantial criminal fines and imprisonment may apply under applicable French and European Union sanctions regimes.

Director personal liability: Individuals who approve or facilitate prohibited transactions may face prosecution, asset restrictions, management bans and imprisonment. A compliance-policy failure alone is not equivalent to criminal intent, but deliberate blindness and serious negligence may be relevant.
Corporate veil abuse and sham-company conduct

Statutory sanction: Possible tax reassessment under abuse-of-law principles, nullification or disregard of transactions, insolvency claims, asset seizure, management bans and criminal fraud or money-laundering sanctions where applicable.

Director personal liability: French law does not automatically pierce the corporate veil. Personal liability may nevertheless arise from misuse of corporate assets, management fault, fraudulent concealment, guarantees, statutory insolvency claims or direct criminal participation. Travel bans and imprisonment require a specific judicial or criminal basis.
Accounting, recordkeeping and beneficial-owner violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, rejection of accounts, tax assessment, penalties for obstruction or missing information, and criminal sanctions for false accounts or related offenses may apply.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and persons responsible for accounting may incur personal civil or criminal liability for knowingly false accounts, obstruction or deliberate record destruction. Sanctions may include management bans and restitution.
