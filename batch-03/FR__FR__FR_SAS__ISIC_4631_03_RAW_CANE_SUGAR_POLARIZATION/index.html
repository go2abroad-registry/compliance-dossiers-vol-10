Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital is required for an SAS; the articles determine the capital amount. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited into an account opened in the company's formation name with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the deposit certificate must be filed. In-kind contributions require valuation by a commissaire aux apports unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain building, planning, and occupancy approvals where construction or change of use is involved. For a warehouse or industrial site, complete fire-risk assessment, emergency access, fire detection and suppression, evacuation, electrical, storage-rack, and structural-load inspections. Consult the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours where required by the project or building authority. Comply with French Labour Code workplace-safety duties, dust and explosion-risk assessment, machinery safety, occupational-health arrangements, and mandatory workplace documentation. A separate public-access fire certificate is required only where the premises qualify as an établissement recevant du public.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital is required for an SAS; the articles determine the capital amount. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited into an account opened in the company's formation name with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the deposit certificate must be filed. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Professional tax filings and payments are handled through the DGFiP professional portal, impots.gouv.fr. Company formation and registration particulars are submitted through the national Guichet unique operated by INPI; customs identification is administered separately by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

Incorporate and register the SAS through the INPI Guichet unique to obtain its SIREN and SIRET identifiers. Contact the competent SIE to establish the applicable VAT regime and obtain or confirm the French VAT identification number, normally FR followed by a two-digit key and the nine-digit SIREN. Register for a professional account on impots.gouv.fr. A foreign taxable person without a French establishment generally applies to the competent non-resident tax service; a non-EU/non-assistance-country entity may need an accredited fiscal representative, subject to statutory exceptions. Obtain an EORI number separately from French customs when required for customs operations. A French establishment of a foreign company follows the applicable French business-registration and tax-registration procedures.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés), generally at the standard 25% rate, subject to applicable qualifications and reduced-rate provisions. An eligible SAS may elect temporarily for taxation under the personal income-tax regime for up to five fiscal years if statutory conditions are met, including limits on age, size, ownership, and qualifying business activity. Certain qualifying family-owned companies may elect a partnership-style regime under separate conditions. France has no US-style check-the-box election; classification and hybrid-mismatch treatment follow French and applicable EU law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts an ordinarily VAT-liable French SAS from registration. The domestic small-business franchise en base regime may apply if its statutory turnover conditions are met; the principal thresholds applicable to goods are EUR 85,000, with a tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500. Thresholds and eligibility rules are subject to legislative change and must be checked for the relevant tax year. A wholesale sugar trader making taxable supplies should confirm its regime with the SIE; qualifying food products may be subject to a reduced rate, commonly 5.5%, depending on the product and statutory conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods may be exempt with credit (zero-rated) under French VAT rules implementing Article 262 of the French Tax Code where the goods are dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the EU and the statutory exporter, transaction, and proof-of-exit conditions are satisfied. Retain evidence that the goods actually left the EU and reconcile it to the sale and customs declaration. Services are not automatically zero-rated merely because the customer is offshore: determine the place of supply under the applicable B2B/B2C rules; qualifying services may be outside French VAT scope, while exemptions with credit apply only where specifically provided by law.

  • Export customs declaration and MRN, with customs exit confirmation or equivalent evidence that the goods left the EU
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, packing list, and transport instructions identifying the goods, buyer, destination, and transaction
  • Bill of lading, CMR, airway bill, or other carrier transport document, together with delivery or receipt evidence where available
  • Proof of payment or bank remittance records where relevant to substantiate the transaction; France imposes no general inward-remittance certificate requirement for VAT zero-rating
  • Product, lot, weight, and shipping records sufficient to reconcile the declared raw cane sugar to the invoice and export movement

France is introducing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction/payment e-reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all in-scope businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; issuance obligations apply from that date to large and intermediate-sized companies and from 1 September 2027 to SMEs and micro-enterprises. In-scope invoices must be transmitted through a registered or approved platform under the applicable French framework. Cross-border transactions and other transactions outside domestic e-invoicing are generally subject to e-reporting where required. France does not generally operate a real-time tax-authority pre-clearance or digital-stamping system for ordinary VAT invoices.

The DGFiP may deny the exemption, assess output VAT, recover improperly deducted input VAT, and charge late-payment interest if export conditions or evidence are deficient. Deliberate or serious breaches may attract tax penalties, including an 80% penalty for fraud or abuse in applicable cases, and criminal consequences for tax fraud. Customs declaration inaccuracies may separately result in customs duties, penalties, or proceedings. Penalties depend on the facts, statutory basis, and available defenses.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both capital and current account transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (Articles 63-65). France applies no foreign exchange control approval regime to trade settlements, profit remittances, or capital movements involving EU Member States and most third countries. Dividend and branch profit repatriation are unrestricted, subject to applicable tax withholding and standard anti-money laundering verification. Payments to non-EU countries are subject only to economic sanctions screening and statistical reporting under French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier). The balance of payments reporting obligation to Banque de France is automatic and bank-mediated, and does not impair convertibility or remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation requirement exists for export proceeds in France. The French Monetary and Financial Code does not impose mandatory conversion ratios or timelines for trade receipts. Exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts with French banks. For statistical balance of payments purposes, banks report cross-border transactions above regulatory thresholds to Banque de France, but the exporter faces no compelled settlement or exchange-rate instruction. Funds may be held, used, or repatriated according to commercial discretion, subject to sanctions and AML/Tracfin screening.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the national central bank and implementing the European Central Bank's monetary policy under the Eurosystem framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. A fintech e-money institution (EMI) licensed in France or an EU/EEA state under PSD2 may lawfully provide cross-border payment accounts to an SAS via the EU passport. However, offshore EMIs without an EEA passport are legally unable to provide payment services or hold IBANs for a French entity. For physical sugar commodity arbitrage involving vessel shipments, Revolving Letters of Credit, and SWIFT MT103 trade payments, most EMIs lack documentary credit (LC) and commodity trade compliance infrastructure. Legal viability is therefore limited to pre-funding, EUR/SEPA transfers, or auxiliary disbursement; a full treasury banking relationship with a tier-1 commercial bank remains mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections from a non-resident shareholder to the French SAS must be wired in EUR via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank transfers) to the entity's French EUR IBAN (e.g., BNP Paribas or Société Générale). No French exchange control authorization is needed for a share capital increase or current-account advance because France applies full capital movement liberalization at the EU level. The bank requires a legal document such as a notarized subscription resolution, shareholder agreement, and updated RCS filing if the injection forms part of a registered capital increase. For shareholder current-account funding, provide a formal loan agreement with clear commercial terms, interest rate, repayment schedule, and purpose. For statistical monitoring, the bank automatically reports cross-border flows to Banque de France; include precise payment details: the SAS legal name, RCS number, transaction reference, contract or invoice number, and economic rationale in the remittance information field. Always use the UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference) for the SWIFT gpi payment tracking.Outbound trade settlements for physical raw cane sugar cargoes should be executed via two main rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 cross-border wire for direct payment against invoice, and (2) commercial letters of credit (LC) issued by the French bank under UCP 600, typically as revolving LC for repeated shipments. The carrier-protected settlement for bulk sugar is an irrevocable LC at sight, payable against presentation of: an original bill of lading, commercial invoice, weight certificate, polarization certificate (pH/ICUMSA), bill of lading statement, certificate of origin, and insurance coverage certificate. Where the counterparty is well-established and the shipment value is lower, a clean SWIFT MT103 with invoice and proof of dispatch is acceptable. Use French RIB or IBAN/BIC for all EUR transfers. Where the trade is denominated in USD, the French bank will route via its correspondent network; incorporate SWIFT gpi and avoid any SDN/OFAC-prohibited banking corridor. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million (annual turnover or total assets) triggers full Master File and Local File documentation; simplified documentation for entities below this threshold but with related-party transactions exceeding EUR 1 million.

Economic substance scope: France applies general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) and substance-over-form doctrine. While no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like UAE, entities must demonstrate real economic activity (employees, premises, decision-making) to avoid challenges under Article 155-A of the French Tax Code (abuse of law) and to benefit from treaty provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (Article 1741 CGI); administrative penalty of 80% of understated tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for corporate tax debts if they participated in fraudulent acts; travel bans and asset freezes possible; criminal conviction may lead to disqualification from management.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, 40% penalty for abuse of law (Article 64 LPF), and potential criminal charges for tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid taxes if they knowingly used the entity as a sham; travel bans and seizure of personal assets may be imposed.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 5% of turnover (Monetary and Financial Code); criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned; professional ban and confiscation of assets; civil liability for damages.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty for lack of documentation (Article 1735 ter CGI), and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-arm's length pricing; potential criminal liability for tax evasion if intent is proven.
