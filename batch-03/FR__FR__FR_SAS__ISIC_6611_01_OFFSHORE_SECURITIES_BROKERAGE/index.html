Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) of the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique; taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr. Corporate income tax and VAT filings are lodged electronically through the 'Espace Professionnel' and the EDI-TDFC protocol. Company legalization is executed through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) operated by the Institut National de la Statistique et des Études Économiques (INSEE), which assigns the SIREN identifier. VAT-number allocation and day-to-day tax administration rest with the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Customs and export formalities (EORI number, export declarations) are handled by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Incorporate the SAS via a notarial deed or signed private deed and file the registration dossier with the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés) through the Guichet Unique portal; INSEE issues the SIREN (9-digit national statistical identifier) and SIRET. Step 2 – Upon initial tax registration of the business commencement, the DGFiP automatically grants the intra-Community VAT identification number (FR + 2-digit control key + 11-digit SIREN), verifiable in the European VIES database. Step 3 – For a non-established foreign entity carrying on taxable activities in France without a permanent establishment, the company must file the dedicated non-resident VAT identification request (formulaire 1586-OGC-SD) with the competent SIE, and appoint a fiscal representative ('représentant fiscal') who is jointly and severally liable for French VAT where required by law. Step 4 – Apply to the DGDDI for an EORI number whenever the entity undertakes any EU customs export or import declaration. Step 5 – Verify within approx. 2–6 weeks that the VAT number is activated and that the entity is correctly registered for the applicable tax regime (real regime, IS, and CET/CFE local levies where relevant).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — An SAS is by statute a commercial corporation subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) on its profits; no pass-through mechanism exists for an SAS. France follows the territoriality principle: an SAS is taxable only on profits generated by French establishments or French-source activities, with foreign-source income generally outside the French IS net unless attributable to a French permanent establishment or specifically caught under article 209-A CGI (non-cooperative States). Standard IS headline rate: 25%; a reduced 15% rate applies to the first €42,500 of annual taxable profit for SMEs meeting article 219-I-b CGI conditions (worldwide turnover below €10 million, share capital fully paid up and held at least 75% by individual shareholders). For VAT purposes the SAS is a taxable person by operation of law (article 256-A CGI) from its first taxable transaction, without any registration threshold.

Available election forms

(i) There is no 'check-the-box' or other entity-classification election under French law: the SAS is definitively a corporation for income tax purposes. (ii) Option for VAT taxation of financial services under article 260-C CGI: a licensed securities broker (société de bourse / investment firm within the meaning of the Code Monétaire et Financier) may elect to subject its security-dealing and brokerage transactions to TVA, thereby restoring the right to deduct input VAT on related costs; the option is global, filed with the SIE, and binding for a minimum period of five years. (iii) Fiscal consolidation election ('intégration fiscale') under article 223-A CGI: available where an SAS is held at least 95% by a French parent company, allowing group-wide set-off of profits and losses for IS purposes. (iv) VAT pro-rata deduction coefficient and the option to attribute actual usage ('affectation réelle') for input VAT recovery under article 271 CGI and BOFiP doctrine. (v) Hybrid mismatch rules transposing EU ATAD (articles 212-bis A to G CGI) contain no elective relief: cross-border hybrid mismatches are automatically denied, and any hybrid arrangement must be reported on the annual corporate tax return (n° 2065-SD). (vi) No micro-enterprise 'franchise en base de TVA' election is available to an SAS, which is always subject to the actual (real) VAT regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For an established French SAS: no economic registration threshold exists — VAT registration is mandatory from its first taxable activity. By contrast, the statutory small-business exemption ('franchise en base de TVA', article 293-B CGI) applies only to non-corporate micro-businesses, with 2025 indexed ceilings of €94,300 for principal supplies of goods and €37,500 for principal supplies of services; a corporate SAS cannot invoke this franchise. For non-established foreign suppliers, a €10,000 EU-wide threshold applies exclusively to intra-EU distance sales of goods and B2C electronic services under the OSS special scheme. For the target activity (offshore securities brokerage), services supplied to customers established outside the EU are 'hors champ' — outside the scope of French VAT — under article 259-A CGI, and services to EU institutional clients are exempt as financial transactions under article 261-C 1° CGI; consequently, no French VAT registration is triggered in most brokerage configurations unless the entity files the article 260-C option or carries other taxable auxiliary services in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: exports of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the European Union qualify for the 0% effective French VAT rate under article 262-I-1° CGI provided that the goods physically leave EU customs territory at the time of supply, as evidenced by the export declaration (DAU) registered in the DGDDI DELTA-X system, the transport contract (bill of lading, CMR, air waybill) and proof of receipt at destination; the evidential file must be retained for at least six years. Offshore services: brokerage, CFD and derivative services supplied to customers established outside the EU have their place of supply outside France under article 259-A CGI and are therefore 'hors champ' (outside the scope) of French VAT — functionally equivalent to zero-rating though invoiced without TVA. Input VAT incurred on costs attributable to such out-of-scope services is recoverable under article 271-I CGI (direct attribution, with application of the deduction coefficient), provided the transactions would give rise to a right of deduction if performed in France; where the broker serves EU taxable persons, the transaction is exempt under article 261-C 1° CGI and input VAT is recovered through the deduction coefficient or by exercising the article 260-C election to restore full deductibility.

  • Bill of Lading — or equivalent transport title (CMR note for road, air waybill for air freight, port terminal receipt for maritime consolidation) evidencing the physical exit of the goods from EU customs territory and identifying the carrier, conveyance, container number, gross weight and destination.

France is mandating full B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting on a phased timetable (as revised by the 2025 Finance Law): from 1 September 2026, all large and medium-sized entities must issue structured e-invoices and e-report their transaction data; from 1 September 2027 the mandate extends to small and micro enterprises. Invoices must be emitted in the hybrid 'Factur-X' format (EN 16931 semantic model embedded in PDF/A-3) or in UBL/CII XML syntax, transmitted through an accredited Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation – PPF). Each e-invoice receives an immutable digital signature/cachet by the PDP before exchange; the PPF operates as central directory and clearing point for B2B flows, with transaction and payment-status data relayed to DGFiP on a periodic (monthly/quarterly) e-reporting cycle. France does not operate a real-time pre-clearance fiscal stamping model (unlike Latin-American CTC regimes); its model is continuous transaction controls coupled with periodic e-reporting. For the securities-brokerage scope, commission notes and fee invoices are subject to the e-invoicing mandate, while exempt securities transactions remain reportable at aggregate level; the prior Chorus Pro obligation continues for invoices involving public-sector counterparties.

The DGFiP and the DGDDI jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims during 'vérification de comptabilité' and customs controls. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full current and capital account convertibility under EU Treaty provisions on free movement of capital (Articles 63–66 TFEU) and OECD Liberalisation Codes. No general exchange control approval is required for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or liquidation proceeds, provided the underlying transactions comply with tax transparency, AML/KYC, and statistical reporting obligations. Non-resident holdings may be subject to declaration thresholds for balance-of-payments statistics and foreign direct investment screening in sensitive sectors; securities brokerage activities are not ordinarily screened. However, the retail CFD/derivatives brokerage model entails payment of client funds to and from segregated client accounts, which must be channelled through authorised credit institutions or payment institutions and may be subject to source-of-funds verification but not exchange approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio applies in France or the euro area. Export receipts may be held in foreign currency accounts freely, subject to balance-of-payments reporting (monthly or quarterly declarations to the Banque de France for direct investment-related flows exceeding EUR 15 million). For a securities brokerage entity, trade settlements and client money transactions are not classified as export proceeds and therefore trigger no conversion or repatriation obligation.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), acting as the French component of the Eurosystem, with foreign exchange policy competence shared with the Directorate General of the French Treasury (Direction générale du Trésor) for capital account and direct investment notifications.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from non-French shareholders to the SAS are executed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the French bank's nostro account in the originator's currency, with the beneficiary account denominated in EUR. The remittance must include the purpose code for direct investment operations (e.g. OECD/BPM6 classification 'Direct investment – Equity capital') and a clear reference to 'Apport en capital' or 'Prime d'émission'. For EU-origin wires, SEPA Credit Transfer may be used for amounts below the EUR threshold; for non-EU jurisdictions, SWIFT is required. The French receiving bank will require the relevant corporate documents (statutes, shareholders' resolution, RCS extract, UBO registry) and will issue a French balance-of-payments declaration to the Banque de France for the transaction if it exceeds EUR 15 million. No exchange control authorisation is required, but the funds must be freed of any suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing.Trade settlements and client money disbursements must be made exclusively through licensed financial institution rails: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for euro-denominated transactions within the SEPA zone; (2) SWIFT MT103 (Single Customer Credit Transfer) for non-euro or non-SEPA cross-border payments, including USD, GBP, JPY and other major currencies; (3) Commercial letters of credit (Documentary Credits) for B2B trade settlements involving physical delivery of securities or collateral, issued under UCP 600 rules and confirmed by the French bank's correspondent network. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. The rate may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France. The EU exemption generally requires qualifying legal-form, subject-to-tax, minimum ownership and holding-period conditions, together with anti-abuse compliance. Dividends paid to French residents are generally subject to a 12.8% income tax withholding mechanism, without prejudice to social levies and final taxation rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the filing deadline is generally the second business day after 1 May of the following year, subject to the annual administrative calendar. Corporate income tax instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, with a final balance generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for 31 December year-ends, the balance is generally due by 15 May.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual deadline follows the financial year-end rule described above. Instalments are generally payable on the 15th day of March, June, September and December, unless a statutory exemption or low-liability threshold applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150,000,000 threshold for the relevant French entity or qualifying group circumstances, generally based on annual turnover excluding tax or gross assets, or where the entity controls or is controlled by an entity meeting the threshold. Large taxpayers must maintain comprehensive transfer-pricing documentation, including a master file and local file, and may have annual reporting obligations. Entities below the threshold must still apply the arm's-length principle and maintain documentation adequate to defend material controlled transactions.

Economic substance scope: France has no single broad regime labelled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless enforced through the French arm's-length principle, anti-abuse doctrine, beneficial-ownership analysis, permanent-establishment rules, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, general anti-abuse rules, principal-purpose analysis under treaties and EU directives, and business-purpose requirements for deductions. A securities brokerage SAS should maintain French decision-making, appropriately qualified personnel, risk control, compliance and AML functions, operational premises or documented outsourced capacity, books and records, client and execution controls, and regulated status proportionate to its activity. A French entity acting only as a conduit for offshore affiliates creates significant reassessment, withholding, permanent-establishment and regulatory risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, surcharges of up to 80% in fraudulent cases, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that may include imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, increased in aggravated cases to up to seven years and EUR 3,000,000, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, tax penalties, civil liability to the company and creditors, and personal collection liability where serious and repeated misconduct has made tax debts unrecoverable. Asset seizure and prohibition from management may apply. Travel bans are not automatic for ordinary tax debt but may arise from judicial measures or criminal proceedings.
Unauthorized Investment Services and Securities Brokerage

Statutory sanction: Regulatory injunctions, suspension or prohibition of activity, administrative fines, restitution and criminal sanctions. Unauthorized provision of regulated investment services may expose responsible persons to imprisonment of up to three years and fines that may reach EUR 375,000, subject to the precise offense and applicable Monetary and Financial Code provision.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may be prosecuted personally, disqualified from management, fined, and ordered to compensate clients. The corporate form does not protect persons who direct or knowingly facilitate unauthorized regulated activity.
AML, Terrorist Financing and Sanctions Screening

Statutory sanction: AMF or other competent-authority enforcement may include reprimand, suspension, withdrawal of authorization and substantial administrative fines. Serious laundering or terrorist-financing conduct may trigger criminal imprisonment and fines under the French Criminal Code, with materially enhanced exposure for organized or terrorist conduct.

Director personal liability: Senior managers, compliance officers and beneficial owners may face personal criminal prosecution, asset confiscation, professional bans and civil liability. Travel restrictions may be ordered by a criminal court or investigating authority; they are not an automatic consequence of every AML breach.
Market Abuse and Misleading Client Conduct

Statutory sanction: AMF administrative sanctions may include fines, publication of the decision, suspension and withdrawal of authorization. Criminal market-abuse offenses may result in imprisonment and substantial fines, subject to the offense, gains obtained and applicable statutory limits.

Director personal liability: Directors, traders, compliance personnel and beneficial owners who authorize, facilitate or knowingly ignore misconduct may be individually sanctioned, disqualified, ordered to disgorge gains and required to compensate clients.
Transfer-Pricing Concealment and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment based on the arm's-length principle, interest, penalties for deliberate breach or fraudulent conduct, documentation-related penalties and possible withholding-tax adjustments. A failure to produce required documentation may result in a specific penalty generally capped by the higher of a statutory fixed amount or a percentage of reassessed profits or controlled transactions, subject to the applicable law.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability, management bans and criminal tax-fraud exposure where the pricing structure is knowingly fictitious or fraudulent. Ordinary transfer-pricing disagreement does not by itself create criminal liability.
Illegal Profit Repatriation and Withholding-Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: Collection of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, interest, surcharges, denial of treaty relief, reassessment of non-deductible expenses and potential penalties for inaccurate or fraudulent declarations. Distributions made without distributable profits may also create corporate and civil consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for wrongful distributions, serious management misconduct and unpaid tax in exceptional cases. Criminal liability may arise if records or declarations were deliberately falsified.
Client-Asset Misappropriation and Segregation Failure

Statutory sanction: Regulatory intervention, restitution, asset freezes, authorization withdrawal, insolvency proceedings and criminal penalties for breach of trust, fraud or misappropriation. Client claims may receive priority under applicable segregation and insolvency rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face criminal prosecution, personal restitution, confiscation, management bans and civil claims. Imprisonment is possible where conduct constitutes fraud, breach of trust or misappropriation.
Shell Company, Nominee Management and Beneficial-Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Refusal of treaty benefits, tax reassessment, beneficial-ownership enforcement, AML sanctions, dissolution or liquidation measures in appropriate cases, asset confiscation and criminal penalties for fraud, money laundering or false corporate records.

Director personal liability: Nominee and de facto directors may be treated as responsible managers and may face criminal prosecution, civil restitution, management prohibition and confiscation. Travel bans may be imposed in criminal proceedings but are not automatic.
Accounting, Corporate Records and False-Instrument Offenses

Statutory sanction: Rejection of accounts, tax reassessment, auditor reporting, civil damages, regulatory sanctions and criminal penalties for forgery, use of forged documents, accounting offenses or obstruction of supervisory authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who approve or knowingly rely on false records may be personally prosecuted and liable for damages, disqualification and imprisonment where the conduct meets the relevant criminal offense.
Permanent Establishment and Place-of-Effective-Management Failure

Statutory sanction: French corporate income tax, payroll and VAT reassessment where applicable, withholding-tax consequences, interest and penalties, together with possible regulatory authorization failures.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil and tax liability for deliberate concealment or serious management misconduct. Criminal exposure generally requires fraud, false declarations or obstruction rather than a simple technical permanent-establishment dispute.
