Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SAS (L.227-2). The articles freely set the capital; any cash portion must be deposited in a blocked bank account at a credit institution or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the deposit certificate is required for registration. Funds are released only after RCS registration or returned if the company is not formed.

Funding & unlock path: Ordinary office occupancy requires compliance with fire-safety rules under the Code du travail and building code for non-ERP premises: emergency exits, fire extinguishers, electrical installation conformity and evacuation planning. If the office is open to the public (ERP, e.g. showroom or service lobby), the building owner's fire-safety attestation must be in place. No separate industrial civil-defence clearance applies.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SAS (L.227-2). The articles freely set the capital; any cash portion must be deposited in a blocked bank account at a credit institution or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the deposit certificate is required for registration. Funds are released only after RCS registration or returned if the company is not formed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); corporate incorporation and registration via Guichet Unique operated by INPI (guichet-entreprises.fr); tax portal: impots.gouv.fr; non-resident taxpayer desks: DRESG (Direction des Résidents à l'Étranger et des Services Généraux).

E-government filing portal

Incorporation of the SAS is filed electronically through the Guichet Unique; the RCS registrar (Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce) issues SIREN/SIRET numbers. DGFiP automatically allocates the French VAT identification number (Numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) upon VAT registration. Foreign entities without a French establishment must apply directly to DGFiP under Article 289 B CGI using Form 3571 (CA20), providing identity, legal form, activity code, and bank coordinates, with mandatory designation of a tax representative (représentant fiscal) where the non-resident has no EU establishment and a French business is carried on.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the SAS is a limited-liability company taxable separately from its shareholders and subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés) under Article 206 CGI at the 25% standard rate, with a 15% reduced rate on taxable profits up to €42,000 for eligible SMEs. Territorial scope: the SAS is fully fiscally resident in France if management and control are exercised in France; profits are taxed on a worldwide basis with treaty relief for double taxation. No tax-exempt zone applies. — Incorporation of the SAS is filed electronically through the Guichet Unique; the RCS registrar (Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce) issues SIREN/SIRET numbers. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SAS is a limited-liability company taxable separately from its shareholders and subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés) under Article 206 CGI at the 25% standard rate, with a 15% reduced rate on taxable profits up to €42,000 for eligible SMEs. Territorial scope: the SAS is fully fiscally resident in France if management and control are exercised in France; profits are taxed on a worldwide basis with treaty relief for double taxation. No tax-exempt zone applies.

Available election forms

Option for fiscal integration (régime d'intégration fiscale, Article 223 A CGI) allowing a 95%+ parent to consolidate subsidiaries' results. Election to be treated as a Jeune Entreprise Innovante (JEI) for payroll tax relief and enhanced loss carry-forward if R&D expenditure exceeds 15% of costs. Research tax credit (CIR) election for eligible software/R&D expenditure. No US-style check-the-box election exists; hybrid mismatch deduction-disallowance and CFC rules under Article 212 bis CGI and Articles 209 B/209 C CGI (ATAD transposition) apply to cross-border hybrid arrangements and controlled foreign structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€37,500 basic annual turnover threshold for service activities (increased limit €41,250); €85,000 basic threshold for goods (increased limit €93,500) under Article 293 B CGI as adjusted by the Loi de Finances pour 2025. Non-established foreign digital-service suppliers are subject to mandatory VAT registration without any threshold under Article 259 A CGI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate applies under Article 262 CGI to exports of tangible goods physically transported outside the European Union. VPN/network-privacy SaaS services supplied to non-EU B2B customers are outside the scope of French VAT under the place-of-supply rule at Article 259-1 CGI; services to non-EU B2C consumers are taxable in the customer's residence country under Article 259 D CGI. Supplies of digital services to EU consumers are taxed in the consumer's member state and reported through the OSS (One Stop Shop) procedure at the destination rate, not zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration Administrative Unique / DAU) or EXA simplification
  • Bill of Lading (connaissement maritime) / Air Waybill (LTA) / CMR road consignment note evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice stating Incoterms, export contract, and value
  • Proof of external transport including carrier receipt and/or customs exit stamp (PTT transit declaration)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (attestation de rapatriement de fonds) or bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds

Mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing via certified Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs) or the Public Facturation Portal (PPF). Reception of e-invoices becomes a legal obligation for all French VAT-registered businesses on 1 September 2026; issuance obligation applies to large enterprises (GE) and mid-caps (ETI) from 1 September 2026 and to SMEs/micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027. E-reporting of cross-border B2B and B2C transaction data to DGFiP follows the same calendar. Real-time fiscal clearance and digital stamping are not currently in force in France; data transmission is structured (UBL, Factur-X, CII) through PDPs/PPF.

Claimed export zero-rating without conclusive transport and payment evidence exposes the taxpayer to retrospective VAT reassessment within the 6-year repose period for VAT (Article L.176 LPF); penalties of 40% of the reassessed VAT for bad faith (mauvaise foi) and 80% for fraud (manœuvres frauduleuses) under Article 1729 CGI; statutory late-payment interest (Article 1727 CGI) accrues and refund rights are immediately suspended; criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI carries a fine up to €3,000,000 and/or 7 years imprisonment, with publication of the tax sanction and prohibition from public tenders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: the euro is freely convertible and France maintains no restrictions on current-account or capital-account transactions, no pre-approval regimes for profit remittance, and no blocked-funds mechanisms. An SAS may freely distribute dividends, pay licensing and service fees, repay intercompany loans and repatriate liquidation proceeds to non-resident shareholders. The only frictions are (i) standard French withholding tax on outbound dividends (25% for corporate non-residents, generally eliminated under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (2011/96/EU) where the qualifying EU parent holds at least 10% of the capital for at least 24 months, or reduced/treaty-exempted), (ii) documentary evidence duty of the paying bank, and (iii) statistical BOP reporting to the Banque de France. Sanctions (EU/EUR restrictions) constitute the only true blocking overlay.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting within the Eurosystem/ESCB statutory framework and under the French Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF), is the central bank and balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical authority for the French jurisdiction. There is no separate foreign-exchange administration or FX licensing body; cross-border capital movements and payments are liberalized under Article 63 TFEU and Directive 88/361/EEC. The Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) co-ordinates restrictive-measure implementation and investment screening, while the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) supervises the credit institutions executing treasury operations.
