Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for a French SAS; the capital is freely fixed in the Statuts. Cash contributions must be fully paid up upon subscription and deposited into a blocked escrow account with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations (CDC) until the Kbis is issued. In-kind contributions exceeding €30,000 or representing more than half of the capital require a Commissaire aux Apports valuation report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: if the premises include a public showroom, obtain the CCDSA commission de sécurité visit and ERP exploitation authorization (5th category); for a purely industrial workshop, maintain a Fire Safety Register, install spark detection and extraction-silo protection, and file the fire-safety declaration with the local SIS (service départemental d'incendie et de secours). Structural clearance: the lease or property deed must evidence structural load capacity for the installed machinery, confirmed by an attestation de solidité structurelle. Occupational clearance: complete and sign the DUERP (Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels), register the employer with CARSAT, certify machine guards per Décret n° 92-767, and arrange periodic verification of wood-dust extraction systems by an accredited inspection body.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for a French SAS; the capital is freely fixed in the Statuts. Cash contributions must be fully paid up upon subscription and deposited into a blocked escrow account with a French bank or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations (CDC) until the Kbis is issued. In-kind contributions exceeding €30,000 or representing more than half of the capital require a Commissaire aux Apports valuation report.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. A French SAS may receive, hold, convert, and remit euros and foreign currencies without a general foreign-exchange licence, mandatory central-bank approval, capital repatriation restriction, or foreign-currency retention limit. Current-account payments, import settlements, export receipts, service fees, royalties, interest, dividends, and intercompany balances are generally transferable through regulated payment institutions or credit institutions, subject to bank AML, sanctions, tax, and source-of-funds controls. Capital movements, shareholder loans, capital contributions, acquisitions, and disposals are also generally unrestricted, subject to EU and French foreign-investment screening where a transaction concerns a protected strategic sector, sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, or statistical reporting. Dividend and liquidation distributions require distributable profits or legally available reserves, valid corporate approvals, accounts supporting the distribution, and compliance with French withholding-tax rules. The ordinary French domestic dividend withholding rate is generally 25%, subject to applicable treaty relief, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption, beneficial-ownership requirements, anti-abuse rules, and documentary procedures. Payments to related parties must also satisfy transfer-pricing, deductibility, interest-limitation, and beneficial-owner requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or fixed deadline requiring a French SAS to convert foreign-currency export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's treasury discretion. Export transactions must be properly documented through commercial invoices, transport records, customs export declarations, customer contracts, and banking references. Banks may request evidence of the underlying trade, payer identity, ultimate beneficial owner, sanctions screening, and economic purpose before crediting or releasing funds. Statistical reporting may apply to cross-border transactions under Banque de France or European statistical-survey requirements, generally through bank reporting or direct declarations when thresholds and reporting notices apply. Cash or certain negotiable monetary instruments physically entering or leaving the European Union at or above EUR 10,000 must be declared to customs. No export-receipt conversion ratio or statutory repatriation timetable applies to ordinary merchandise exports.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, with prudential and payment-services supervision by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution and monetary-policy oversight within the European System of Central Banks led by the European Central Bank. Foreign-exchange activity is principally governed by European Union free-movement-of-capital rules, French anti-money-laundering legislation, sanctions law, customs rules, and payment-services regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for operational payments, collections, cards, and selected foreign-currency flows, but not a complete substitute for a French relationship bank. An electronic-money institution or payment institution licensed in France or another European Economic Area state may provide services in France through passporting, an establishment, or an authorised agent, subject to applicable notification and AML requirements. Offshore providers without an EU licence, passport, French establishment, or authorised agent should not be used for regulated payment services to French customers. EMI balances are safeguarded customer funds and are not bank deposits; deposit-guarantee protection generally does not apply. Providers may restrict regulated goods, marketplace flows, high-volume cross-border receipts, third-party funds, cash activity, sanctioned jurisdictions, and rapid settlement patterns. A fintech account should be paired with a French or EU credit-institution account for capital escrow, tax payments, reserve liquidity, and business continuity.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Certified passport or national identity document; recent proof of residential address; tax-residence declaration and identification number; notarised ownership chart; beneficial-owner declaration; corporate registry extracts for every intermediate entity; director appointment evidence; sanctions and politically exposed person screening record; signed explanation of ownership purpose.

Root cause: Toy manufacturing or distribution activity involving product-safety, import, customs, or regulatory exposure

Large shareholder funding, intercompany loans, rapid capital movements, or funds inconsistent with declared turnover

High-volume marketplace, card, refund, chargeback, or third-party collection activity

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Deposit SAS share capital into a designated capital escrow account at a French bank or eligible notary before incorporation or capital increase. Use a transfer narrative identifying the subscriber, SAS name, capital contribution, and incorporation or capital-increase reference. After receipt of the deposit certificate and registration, release the funds into the operational account. For post-incorporation equity, route funds against executed shareholder or board resolutions, amended statutes where required, subscription forms, and the relevant corporate filing. For shareholder loans, use a signed loan agreement, arm's-length terms, maturity, interest, repayment mechanics, transfer-pricing support, and any applicable French tax or statistical reporting. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT cross-border transfer for other currencies or jurisdictions. France does not operate a universal foreign-exchange purpose-code regime; the bank's payment-purpose field, invoice or contract reference, and accounting classification should accurately state capital contribution, shareholder loan, intercompany funding, or operating receipt. Banks may verify source of funds, source of wealth, UBO identity, sanctions status, corporate authority, beneficial ownership, and consistency with the declared business model before crediting or releasing funds.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro suppliers and SWIFT customer credit transfer for non-SEPA or foreign-currency payments. A SWIFT MT103 is the legacy customer-payment format; banks are migrating to ISO 20022 payment messages, principally pacs.008, so payment instructions should follow the executing bank's current format. For higher-value or higher-risk imports, use documentary commercial letters of credit, standby letters of credit, documentary collections, open-account settlement with trade-credit insurance, or supply-chain-finance programmes. A commercial LC should identify the applicant, beneficiary, issuing and advising banks, amount, currency, expiry, shipment terms, Incoterms, presentation period, required transport and customs documents, insurance requirements, and UCP 600 or other agreed rules. Maintain purchase orders, invoices, transport documents, customs entries, certificates of origin where relevant, product-safety documentation, and proof of receipt. Screen suppliers, banks, vessels, countries, goods, and end users against applicable EU and French sanctions.
