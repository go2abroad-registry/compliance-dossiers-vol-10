Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Clerk's Office of the Commercial Court) responsible for the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); filings are transmitted electronically through the INPI Guichet unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce): Articles L. 210-1 to L. 210-10 (general incorporation rules), Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-32 (Société par Actions Simplifiée), and Articles R. 123-31 to R. 123-159 (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés filing formalities), supplemented by Decree No. 2023-208 on the single electronic filing window (guichet unique).

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic single-window creation declaration (dossier unique de création d'entreprise, CERFA M0) submitted via the official portal, together with the final statuts and constitutive annexes.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS (may be set as low as EUR 1). Cash subscriptions must be paid up to at least 50% at incorporation, with the balance callable within five years. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked account with a licensed French credit institution, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the depository's attestation de dépôt des fonds must be produced at filing. In-kind contributions are conclusively released upon transfer of the contributed asset and require a commissaire aux apports valuation report where statutory thresholds are exceeded.

Funding & unlock path: For leased office premises, verify fire-safety conformity with Articles R. 4227-1 to R. 4227-41 of the French Labour Code (detection, evacuation, extinguishers), obtain an electrical conformity certificate for the fit-out, confirm structural load adequacy via the lessor's lease file, and, if clients are received, classify the premises as an ERP (Établissement Recevant du Public) and complete the corresponding safety commission procedures. Workplace clearance includes registration with the CSE and compliance with general health and safety obligations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — national tax administration responsible for corporate income tax, VAT registration, collection and audit; official taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr. Business registration and TIN procurement are performed through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) operated by Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI); local VAT operational unit: Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE).

E-government filing portal

For a French-established SAS: (1) incorporate via the INPI Guichet Unique; (2) DGFiP automatically allocates the SIREN (9-digit national company TIN), SIRET (14-digit establishment identifier), and the intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 2-character key + 9-digit SIREN (11 characters total); (3) obtain the attestation de numéro de TVA intracommunautaire from DGFiP. For a foreign non-established entity: (1) file Form Cerfa 15856 (Demande d'identification pour l'assujettissement à la TVA) with the competent SIE before making the first French taxable supply; (2) provide constitutive documents, proof of foreign establishment and a powers-of-attorney dossier; (3) appoint an approved VAT fiscal representative (représentant fiscal agréé) if established outside the EU (CGI Art. 289 A); (4) receive a non-established French VAT number in the format FR + 2 alphabetic characters + 9 digits.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French-established SAS: (1) incorporate via the INPI Guichet Unique; (2) DGFiP automatically allocates the SIREN (9-digit national company TIN), SIRET (14-digit establishment identifier), and the intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 2-character key + 9-digit SIREN (11 characters total); (3) obtain the attestation de numéro de TVA intracommunautaire from DGFiP. For a foreign non-established entity: (1) file Form Cerfa 15856 (Demande d'identification pour l'assujettissement à la TVA) with the competent SIE before making the first French taxable supply; (2) provide constitutive documents, proof of foreign establishment and a powers-of-attorney dossier; (3) appoint an approved VAT fiscal representative (représentant fiscal agréé) if established outside the EU (CGI Art. 289 A); (4) receive a non-established French VAT number in the format FR + 2 alphabetic characters + 9 digits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is per se a corporation (Code civil Art. 1843-5; CGI Art. 206) — no US-style check-the-box pass-through election exists; entity-level corporate income tax, VAT and territorial taxes are mandatory. Available statutory elections: (1) fiscal integration (régime d'intégration fiscale) under CGI Art. 223 A for a French parent with 95% or greater ownership of French subsidiaries; (2) election for the normal VAT regime (régime réel) where turnover remains below the franchise exemption threshold; (3) option under CGI Art. 209 quinquies to be taxed on worldwide business profits for companies deriving main turnover abroad (narrowly available and subject to DGFiP conditions); (4) mandatory anti-hybrid qualification rules under CGI Arts. 205-205 C implementing EU ATAD and OECD BEPS Action 2 — no elective hybrid-mismatch treatment is available outside those statutory provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Established French entity: EUR 37,500 annual taxable turnover for services (consulting) with an EUR 41,250 tolerance limit (2025 fiscal thresholds); below the threshold the franchise en base de TVA exempts supplies but blocks input VAT recovery; crossing the threshold makes VAT registration compulsory from the first euro. Non-established foreign supplier: compulsory registration from EUR 0 (no de minimis) for French-taxable B2B supplies unless the French customer is liable under the reverse charge mechanism (CGI Art. 283-2); B2C supply of distance digital services benefits from the EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual distance-sales threshold under CGI Art. 259-0.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Consulting and market-entry advisory services (ISIC 7020) follow the place-of-supply rule under CGI Arts. 259-1 and 259-2: if the business customer is established outside France (EU or third country), the supply is outside the scope of French VAT — effectively zero-rated with the right to deduct input VAT — provided the supplier holds the customer's EU VAT registration number (intra-EU), or a certified foreign tax-residence certificate (non-EU), and can evidence that the service was genuinely rendered to that foreign recipient. Supplies to a French fixed establishment of the foreign customer remain French taxable. Exports of goods to third countries are exempt with deduction under CGI Art. 262-I where customs documentation proves exit from the EU within 12 months; intra-EU movements, if any, require the customer's valid EU TIN and compliance with the French Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens reporting regime.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation / DAU filed electronically via the DELTA ECS platform) evidencing lawful exit of goods from the EU
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport contract demonstrating physical dispatch and destination in a third country
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank certificate or avis de mise à disposition) evidencing receipt of the export consideration in the French entity's bank account
  • EU VAT registration certificate or foreign tax-residence certificate of the non-French customer supporting the place-of-supply analysis under CGI Art. 259-1
  • Signed market-entry advisory engagement contract and delivery report evidencing actual performance of the consultancy for the foreign recipient

France is implementing the e-invoicing and e-reporting reform under the Finance Act of 2024 and Decree No. 2024-255 of 17 March 2024, administered by DGFiP. Domestic B2B invoices must be issued in structured electronic formats (UBL, Factur-X, CII) and routed through an approved Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the DGFiP Public Invoicing Portal (PPF); the mandate applies from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises. Transaction data (e-reporting) must be transmitted to the PPF generally no later than one business day after issuance, requiring API integration between PDPs and the PPF with digital validation of VAT-relevant fields. France does not operate real-time fiscal stamping or continuous transaction control clearance as seen in Italy or Latin America, but applies near-real-time structured electronic transmission without a live taxpayer clearance step.

DGFiP and the French customs authority (DGDDI) may jointly verify zero-rating claims in documentary and post-clearance audits. The ordinary reassessment period is the three-year statute of limitations under LPF Art. L. 169, extended to six years in cases of deliberate tax fraud and further extended or left uncapped in cases of concealed offshore activities or undisclosed foreign flows (LPF Arts. L. 169 B, L. 188 and L. 188 A). Where the exporter fails the substantiation burden under CGI Arts. 259-1 and 262-I, French output VAT is clawed back with: a 40% penalty for omitted or under-declared turnover (CGI Art. 1729-A), an 80% penalty for bad faith (CGI Art. 1729-B), a 100% penalty in cases of abuse of right or organised manoeuvres, monthly late interest of 0.20% per month on VAT reassessed, and criminal sanctions up to EUR 750,000 corporate fine and 5 years' imprisonment for tax fraud or false customs declarations (CGI Arts. 1741-1745; Code des douanes Art. 414).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under a fully liberalized capital and current account regime as a member of the Eurozone and the EU. There are no exchange controls on current account transactions (e.g., trade, services, remittances) or capital account transactions (e.g., FDI, loans, portfolio investments) for residents and non-residents. Profit remittance and dividend distributions are unrestricted, subject to standard corporate tax withholding (if applicable) and anti-money laundering (AML) reporting. The euro is fully convertible; no prior authorization is required for cross-border transfers, but transactions exceeding €10,000 must be reported to the French customs and tax authorities for statistical and AML purposes. Non-resident entities may hold euro-denominated accounts without restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose any mandatory surrender or conversion requirements for export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in designated accounts or convert freely. However, all cross-border transactions must be reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics if they exceed €50,000 per transaction or €500,000 annually per counterparty. No specific timeline for repatriation exists, but proceeds must be declared in the annual financial statements and tax returns.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) in coordination with the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) and the Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. French-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine, and cross-border EMIs like Wise and Revolut Business are viable for operational accounts, offering multi-currency IBANs and low-cost cross-border transfers. However, they may not provide full treasury services (e.g., letters of credit, trade finance) and may have lower transaction limits. Legal constraints: EMIs must be licensed by the ACPR or passport under the EU PSD2; they are subject to strict AML/KYC but are generally more flexible for remote onboarding. For a consultancy with low-risk profile, an EMI is acceptable for day-to-day operations, but a tier-1 bank is recommended for capital escrow or large trade settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound equity injections from foreign shareholders must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code. For France, the appropriate purpose code for FDI is 'S001' (Direct investment) or 'S002' (Real estate investment) under the Banque de France's balance of payments classification. The transfer must be accompanied by a copy of the shareholder resolution and the updated Kbis. Verification: The receiving bank will require the source of funds declaration (e.g., bank statement from the sending bank) and may request the underlying transaction documents. For amounts exceeding €10,000, the bank will report to the French customs and tax authorities. No prior authorization is needed, but the transaction must be declared in the annual financial statements.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For consultancy services, outbound payments (e.g., subcontractor fees, licensing) are typically executed via SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) or SEPA (for EUR-denominated payments within the EU). For larger trade settlements involving goods, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) may be used, but this is less common for service-based consultancies. The bank will require supporting documentation (invoices, contracts) and may apply FX conversion at the prevailing rate. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. Eligible small and medium-sized companies may qualify for a 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to the applicable turnover, capital ownership, and paid-up-capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic withholding rate on dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate shareholder is generally 25%, subject to exemptions and treaty relief. Dividends paid to non-resident individuals are generally subject to 12.8% withholding, subject to applicable rules and treaty relief. The EU Parent-Subsidiary regime may exempt qualifying distributions to eligible EU parent companies, subject to ownership, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, and other statutory conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the fiscal year. For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the electronic filing deadline is generally the second working day after 1 May of the following year, subject to the annually published filing calendar and any administrative extension. The corporate tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following fiscal year-end; for a 31 December year-end, it is generally due by 15 May. Quarterly installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: French full transfer-pricing documentation is generally required for a French entity whose annual turnover or gross assets are at least EUR 400 million, or that meets specified ownership or control connections with an entity meeting the threshold. A separate annual simplified transfer-pricing filing, Form 2257-SD, generally applies to qualifying entities at the lower EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, subject to the statutory related-party and control conditions. Local-file and master-file requirements and filing deadlines should be tested separately against current law.

Economic substance scope: France has no general standalone regime called Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore regimes. French tax authorities may nevertheless challenge artificial arrangements under domestic anti-abuse rules, the general abuse-of-law doctrine, the principal-purpose test where applicable, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, and treaty anti-abuse provisions. A French SAS should be able to evidence real decision-making, personnel or outsourced functions, premises where appropriate, operational capacity, and commercial rationale consistent with its activities and reported profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion or fraudulent return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessments, late-payment interest, and surcharges may apply; fraud-related surcharges may reach 80% under applicable conditions. Serious tax fraud may constitute a criminal offense punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, with higher penalties in aggravated cases, including up to seven years and EUR 3 million, subject to the applicable statutory provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability where they personally participate in or knowingly facilitate an offense. Tax authorities may seek director liability for tax debts in cases of fraudulent maneuvers or serious and repeated breaches that make collection impossible. Imprisonment, fines, asset measures, and restrictions may apply where authorized by law; travel bans are not an automatic consequence.
Transfer-pricing misstatement or undocumented related-party transactions

Statutory sanction: Adjustments may increase taxable profits and trigger tax, interest, and applicable penalties. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation can attract a minimum penalty generally equal to 0.5% of the amount of transactions concerned, subject to a statutory minimum generally of EUR 10,000 per financial year, and may be increased by reference to tax reassessed, subject to statutory limits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for intentional fraud or where statutory conditions for personal liability are met. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments do not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Unlawful dividend distribution or withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: The company may be liable for unpaid withholding tax, interest, and applicable penalties. Unlawful distributions may be subject to recovery and corporate-law remedies; knowingly false treaty claims may also trigger tax-fraud sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil or criminal liability for knowingly authorizing unlawful distributions or fraudulent withholding-tax conduct. Personal liability is not automatic and depends on the statutory cause of action and evidence.
Shell-company, sham-substance, or abuse-of-law arrangement

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard or recharacterize arrangements, deny treaty benefits or deductions, reassess tax, and impose interest and anti-abuse or fraud surcharges where the required conditions are met. Criminal sanctions require the elements of a separate offense.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for their own knowing participation in fraud or other offenses. Corporate separateness is not disregarded merely because the SAS is foreign-owned or has limited operations.
Anti-money-laundering, sanctions, or beneficial-ownership non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Sanctions depend on the conduct and applicable regime and may include administrative measures, asset freezing, substantial criminal fines, and imprisonment. AML obligations depend on whether the entity or its activities fall within a regulated or otherwise covered category.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for their own participation, consent, or legally actionable failure to act. Asset restraint or travel restrictions require competent authority action under applicable law and are not automatic corporate-tax penalties.
