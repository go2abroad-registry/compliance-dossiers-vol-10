Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) held at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce, processed exclusively through the INPI Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Livre II, Titre IV, Chapitre V (Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-20) governing the Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS); general provisions of Articles L. 210-1 et seq.; implementing rules of Articles R. 227-1 et seq.

Formation / constitutional filings

Formulaire unique de création d'entreprise (CERFA M0) submitted electronically via the INPI Guichet Unique (formalites.inpi.fr)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum legal capital for an SAS (€1 or more permitted); however, at least 50% of cash subscription amounts must be paid up upon incorporation under Article L. 227-2 applying Article L. 225-3, with the balance payable within five years; cash funds must be deposited and blocked with a licensed deposit bank, a notaire, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the RCS registration (Kbis) is obtained.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: office and studio premises must comply with Code du travail fire-safety requirements (Articles R. 4227-1 et seq.), including portable extinguishers, emergency exits, fire detection and staff evacuation plans; any area open to the public is additionally subject to ERP clearance under the Code de la Construction et de l'Habitation and periodic commission de sécurité visits. Structural load inspections: obtain the landlord's building-compliance attestation before installing recording equipment, lighting rigs, or heavy server racks; filming sets must be erected only in structurally verified locations. Workplace clearance: prepare and maintain the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP) and register with the occupational health service (CARSAT/PRéSANCE) upon hiring employees.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum legal capital for an SAS (€1 or more permitted); however, at least 50% of cash subscription amounts must be paid up upon incorporation under Article L. 227-2 applying Article L. 225-3, with the balance payable within five years; cash funds must be deposited and blocked with a licensed deposit bank, a notaire, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the RCS registration (Kbis) is obtained.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — official portals: impots.gouv.fr (tax filing, TéléTVA, professional tax account), formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique for company and TIN registration), and pro.dgfip.fr (professional online services); VAT returns and OSS filings are handled through the DGFiP electronic system.

E-government filing portal

For the French SAS (FR_SAS): (1) incorporate via the Guichet Unique, which automatically triggers issuance of SIREN and SIRET identifiers by INSEE; (2) request the intra-Community VAT number (format FR + two-digit key + SIREN) from the DGFiP via the professional online tax account; (3) activate TéléTVA electronic filing for monthly VAT returns. For a foreign entity not established in France: submit Form No. 3557-SD to the competent non-resident corporate tax office (service des impôts des entreprises) to obtain a French VAT ID; if established outside the EU, appoint an accredited fiscal representative (représentant fiscal accrédité) with joint and several liability unless using the Non-Union OSS scheme. For digital micro-drama app distribution, the seller may alternatively register under the OSS/IOSS regimes to declare French VAT on distance sales and B2C electronic services.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (an SAS is mandatorily subject to French corporate income tax — Impôt sur les Sociétés, CGI art. 206 — with no pass-through to shareholders; Territorial_Scope: France-resident entity taxed on worldwide profits, with foreign branch profits excluded under the territoriality carve-out and losses of foreign branches non-deductible; Tax_Exempt_Zone: none, standard metropolitan French tax territory applies; Pass_Through_Eligible: no). — For the French SAS (FR_SAS): (1) incorporate via the Guichet Unique, which automatically triggers issuance of SIREN and SIRET identifiers by INSEE; (2) request the intra-Community VAT number (format FR + two-digit key + SIREN) from the DGFiP via the professional online tax account; (3) activate TéléTVA electronic filing for monthly VAT returns. For a foreign entity not established in France: submit Form No. 3557-SD to the competent non-resident corporate tax office (service des impôts des entreprises) to obtain a French VAT ID; if established outside the EU, appoint an accredited fiscal representative (représentant fiscal accrédité) with joint and several liability unless using the Non-Union OSS scheme. For digital micro-drama app distribution, the seller may alternatively register under the OSS/IOSS regimes to declare French VAT on distance sales and B2C electronic services.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (an SAS is mandatorily subject to French corporate income tax — Impôt sur les Sociétés, CGI art. 206 — with no pass-through to shareholders; Territorial_Scope: France-resident entity taxed on worldwide profits, with foreign branch profits excluded under the territoriality carve-out and losses of foreign branches non-deductible; Tax_Exempt_Zone: none, standard metropolitan French tax territory applies; Pass_Through_Eligible: no).

Available election forms

An SAS has no check-the-box mechanism and cannot elect general pass-through treatment; corporation tax is mandatory. Available elections: (1) reduced corporate tax rate for small and medium enterprises under CGI art. 219 IV-B on the first €42,500 of taxable profit; (2) election for the parent-subsidiary regime (régime mère-fille, CGI art. 145 and 216) for qualifying dividend income; (3) election for tax consolidation (intégration fiscale, CGI art. 223 A) requiring at least 95% ownership in French subsidiaries; (4) election for Young Innovative Company (JEI) tax and social reliefs if R&D expenditure conditions are met; (5) narrow temporary option for income tax (IR) for newly created small SAS under CGI art. 239 bis AA; (6) hybrid mismatch neutralization rules under CGI art. 212 bis implementing ATAD 1 apply mandatorily as a back-stop rather than being elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold: any taxable economic activity carried out in France by an established business mandates VAT registration from the first euro. Non-established suppliers of B2C digital services (including vertical micro-drama streaming via app stores) must register from the first euro under the Non-Union OSS scheme, with platform deemed-supplier liability under CGI art. 283 quinquies for Apple IAP and Google Play Billing sales to French consumers. EU-established distance sellers of goods benefit from the €10,000 EU-wide annual distance-selling threshold (CGI art. 210 sexdecies) before French VAT applies at destination.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: exports outside the EU are zero-rated (exonération, CGI art. 262-I) provided the exporter demonstrates physical removal of the goods from EU territory and retains the prescribed documentary evidence; intra-EU supplies to registered EU businesses may be zero-rated under CGI art. 262-I-1° upon possession of the customer's valid EU VAT number and matching EC sales list filings. Services (vertical micro-drama streaming): digital services follow the place-of-supply rules rather than classic export zero-rating — B2C electronic services are taxed at the customer's location (CGI arts. 259 A and 259 D); sales to non-EU consumers are outside French VAT scope at an effective 0% rate, while sales to EU consumers attract the destination Member State's VAT through the OSS or Non-Union OSS regime.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU — Document Administratif Unique) or comparable export declaration validated via the DELTA electronic customs system for tangible shipments
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note proving physical removal of goods from the EU
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and applicable export permits matching the declared goods and incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence documenting the repatriation of export proceeds within the statutory period; for digital services: platform settlement statements (Apple, Google, Stripe), customer contracts, content licence agreements, IP usage reports, and app download data
  • For intra-EU supplies: VIES validation of the customer's EU VAT number and EC Sales List (Déclaration d'échanges de biens) filed to customs

France is implementing a mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting reform (Ordonnance No. 2021-1190, Décret No. 2022-66 as amended): B2B domestic invoices must be emitted and transmitted through certified Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDPs) or the Public Invoicing Portal (Portail Public de Facturation — PPF) using Factur-X (EN 16931) or structured EDI formats. Roll-out: 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for SMEs/VSEs. E-reporting covers international B2B and B2C transaction data transmitted to DGFiP. France does not require real-time digital stamping of each invoice as in Italy or Latin America; continuous transaction controls are achieved through TéléTVA periodic VAT returns and the central PPF clearance repository. App-store settlement data (Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, Stripe) must be reconciled against OSS filings or national VAT returns.

DGFiP applies strict substantive and documentary scrutiny to export zero-rating claims; failure to substantiate physical export or customer location triggers VAT reassessment, late-payment interest at 0.20% per month (CGI art. 1727), formal deficiencies at 40% (CGI art. 1728 and 1729-1), abusive tax fraud (manoeuvres frauduleuses) at 80%, and criminal sanctions under CGI art. 1741 of up to €750,000 fine (or twice the sums evaded) and five years' imprisonment. The VAT reassessment window extends to the end of the sixth calendar year following the year at issue (art. L.176 LPF), extended to ten years for hidden activity, non-filing, or established fraud (arts. L.180 and L.181 LPF). Fictitious export schemes, fraudulent intra-EU 'carousel' chains, and misuse of offshore-receivable routing additionally trigger joint and several liability for the scheme promoters and loss of the right to claim refunds.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has no general exchange controls or restrictions on conversion between euros and foreign currencies. Current-account payments, including payments for services, royalties, subscriptions, imports, exports, and ordinary profit distributions, are generally freely transferable, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, accounting, and payment-services rules. Capital-account movements, shareholder funding, dividends, intercompany loans, acquisitions, and repatriations are also generally permitted without prior exchange-control approval, but banks may require corporate approvals, beneficial-owner information, source-of-funds evidence, underlying agreements, invoices, tax support, and proof that the distribution is legally available under French corporate law. A French SAS must satisfy statutory accounts, distributable-profit, solvency, withholding-tax, transfer-pricing, and beneficial-ownership requirements before making distributions. Cross-border payments may be delayed or rejected where the payer, payee, bank, intermediary, country, transaction purpose, or goods and services create sanctions, AML, tax-evasion, fraud, or dual-use concerns. Euro payments within SEPA should normally use IBAN and BIC data and are subject to applicable SEPA and payment-services rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a French bank, convert foreign-currency export receipts into euros, or complete conversion within a prescribed period. Export proceeds may normally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the company's commercial discretion. The company must maintain complete commercial, customs, invoice, contract, VAT, accounting, and bank records and provide information required for statistical, customs, tax, AML, sanctions, and banking controls. Foreign-currency balances and transactions must be recorded and valued under applicable French accounting rules. Export transactions may be subject to intra-EU statistical reporting, customs declarations for non-EU trade, VAT evidence, and Banque de France or other statistical reporting requirements where applicable. No universal mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange and payment-services supervision is also allocated to the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, the Autorité des marchés financiers, the European Central Bank, the European Banking Authority, and the French customs and tax administrations as applicable.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to High for routine collection and payment flows, but not a substitute for a French relationship bank where capital deposit, local credit, cash management, trade finance, or complex treasury support is required. An electronic money institution or payment institution established in France or another EEA state may provide services under its authorization and applicable passporting or freedom-to-provide-services arrangements. A non-EEA offshore EMI cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to French customers merely by being offshore; it generally requires an appropriate EU authorization, establishment, passport, or other legally available basis. Customer funds must be safeguarded and cannot be treated as bank deposits unless held by a licensed credit institution. The SAS must verify the provider's authorization, safeguarding model, settlement banks, client-money segregation, execution location, sanctions controls, data-processing arrangements, insolvency protections, transaction limits, and withdrawal rights. Apple, Google, and Stripe settlement accounts remain subject to their own onboarding, reserve, chargeback, payout, platform, consumer-protection, and sanctions controls.

Route equity through the French bank's pre-incorporation capital deposit account where the SAS is being formed, using a payment from an account held in the subscribing shareholder's name. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to High for routine collection and payment flows, but not a substitute for a French relationship bank where capital deposit, local credit, cash management, trade finance, or complex treasury support is required. An electronic money institution or payment institution established in France or another EEA state may provide services under its authorization and applicable passporting or freedom-to-provide-services arrangements. A non-EEA offshore EMI cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to French customers merely by being offshore; it generally requires an appropriate EU authorization, establishment, passport, or other legally available basis. Customer funds must be safeguarded and cannot be treated as bank deposits unless held by a licensed credit institution. The SAS must verify the provider's authorization, safeguarding model, settlement banks, client-money segregation, execution location, sanctions controls, data-processing arrangements, insolvency protections, transaction limits, and withdrawal rights. Apple, Google, and Stripe settlement accounts remain subject to their own onboarding, reserve, chargeback, payout, platform, consumer-protection, and sanctions controls.
