Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP) through the official portal impots.gouv.fr; cross-border and large-taxpayer supervision by the Département des grandes entreprises and the Direction Nationale des Vérifications de Situations Fiscales. Customs and export controls are administered separately by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the formalities portal (guichet unique). After the certificate of incorporation (Kbis) is issued, the local tax office (SIE) automatically allocates the SIREN and VAT identification number (FR + SIREN) without separate application. A non-established foreign entity carrying on a taxable supply in France must file the dedicated non-established taxable person registration form with the DGFIP office competent for non-resident taxpayers, provide proof of foreign incorporation, identification of a local tax representative when required, and state the date of commencement of French transactions. This VAT registration is mandatory before the first taxable transaction in France and is separate from the M0/Kbis incorporation procedure.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation – the SAS is a French-resident juridical person subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on worldwide profits; it is not a fiscally transparent entity. The entity has French territorial tax nexus for VAT, corporate income tax, social security and payroll taxes; no sub-jurisdictional tax distinction exists in France. — A French SAS is registered through the formalities portal (guichet unique). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the SAS is a French-resident juridical person subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on worldwide profits; it is not a fiscally transparent entity. The entity has French territorial tax nexus for VAT, corporate income tax, social security and payroll taxes; no sub-jurisdictional tax distinction exists in France.

Available election forms

French law does not use the check-the-box transparency election. Available elections are: (1) tax consolidation within a French group under article 223 A CGI where the parent holds at least 95% of the subsidiary; (2) the narrow option for income tax treatment under article 239 bis AB CGI available to small innovative or service SAS entities; (3) VAT optioning on equipment sale/lease under articles 260 CA/CB CGI; and (4) mandatory anti-hybrid mismatch and DAC6 disclosures through forms 2257-SD, 10023-SD and 3948-SD where the EMI participates in hybrid or paying-agent arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For French-established small taxable persons, the franchise in base de TVA exemption applies below €85,000 for goods and €37,500 for services. However, an electronic money institution (Établissement de Monnaie Électronique) and any non-French-established supplier are not covered by the franchise; VAT registration is required from the first taxable transaction even without turnover. Where the EMI only supplies VAT-exempt financial payment services under article 261 C CGI, registration may still be mandatory for statutory reporting, EU recapitulative statements and acquisition/import charging purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported to a destination outside the European Union are zero-rated if the exporter proves that the goods physically left the EU. The proof consists of a validated customs export declaration (Déclaration en Douane / DAU) and the original transport documents. Intra-Community supplies to a business customer in another EU Member State are exempt from French VAT under article 262 ter CGI, subject to a valid French SIREN and the customer's EU VAT ID. Offshore business-to-business services to non-EU customers are treated as supplied where the customer is established and therefore fall outside the scope of French VAT, but the EMI must retain documentary evidence of customer location and business purpose. By contrast, electronic money issuance, payment accounts, transfers and the management of digital-wallet reserves are VAT-exempt under article 261 C 1° of CGI; they are not export zero-rated and carry only a pro-rata input VAT recovery right.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / DAU) stamped or electronically validated by the DGDDI evidencing exit from the EU
  • Transport document proving physical export: Bill of Lading for sea freight, CMR or e-CMR for road freight, Air Waybill for air cargo, or shipped-in-bond document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing the foreign exchange repatriation into the French entity's bank account within the statutory period
  • Recapitulative statement of intra-Community sales for EU-destination goods and the customer's EU VAT identification number, where applicable

France mandates structured electronic invoicing under the 2023-2027 reform. Large and mid-size enterprises must issue e-invoices through certified dematerialisation platforms (PDP) or the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) from 1 September 2026; all businesses follow from 1 September 2027. Invoices must use Factur-X, UBL or CII formats and the transmission to the tax authority must occur through APIs conforming to the DGFIP digital interoperability standard. There is no central pre-approval stamping; instead the PPF performs post-transaction structured reporting, validation cross-checks, and invoice status acknowledgement. Each e-invoice must carry a guaranteed digital authenticity sideband including legal signature, audit trail and seamless re-integrity control.

Export documentation must be preserved for at least six years. If zero-rating is applied without valid customs proof of export or without timely repatriation of the foreign sales proceeds, the French tax authority re-characterises the transaction as a domestic taxable sale and claws back the full 20% TVA plus interest at 5.00% per annum. The penalty regime under article 1788 A and 1731 CGI imposes 40% additional assessments for material inaccuracies, 80% for organised fraud, and criminal fines under article 1741 CGI for tax fraud; in cross-border export schemes the customs code also applies penalty duties of up to 100% of the value of the goods. Directors of the SAS may be held personally liable in cases of fraud or gross negligence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full Eurozone member with unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, OECD Capital Liberalisation Codes and the French Code Monétaire et Financier; the Euro is freely convertible and profit remittance is unrestricted provided that statutory distributable-reserve tests, corporate governance documentation and FATCA/CRS tax reporting requirements are satisfied; there are no prior exchange-control authorisations for non-resident equity investment into a French SAS; practical conversion hurdles are limited to AML-driven banking due diligence for large-size share premium receipts and the standard requirement that the capital be evidenced by a notarially-equivalent deed and entered into a dedicated bank account before the RCS corporate registry filing

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio; the mandatory conversion ratio is 0% for Euro-denominated receipts and 0% for foreign-currency trade receivables which may be retained freely on external or domestic EEA accounts; this outcome is mandated by EU capital-movement liberalisation principles and the French General Tax Code reporting framework; only statistical balance-of-payments declaration duties apply to cross-border flows above the Banque de France reporting threshold (EUR 12,500), and no repatriation-maintenance period is imposed on export receivables

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Central Bank and Eurosystem settlement authority), acting jointly with the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution / French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority) and the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) as the statutory foreign exchange administration; the Banque de France is the competent central bank for TARGET2 settlement, domestic payment system oversight and balance-of-payments regulatory reporting, while the ACPR holds prudential and FX-risk supervision over licensed Electronic Money Institutions

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium with legally constrained structuring; a licensed French EMI may use an offshore digital EMI only as a technical white-label distribution rail or as an agent, and cannot use a non-credit-institution as custodian of safeguarded funds because Article L521-3 of the French Code Monétaire et Financier requires that safeguarded funds be held with an EU/EEA-licensed credit institution; an EEA passporting EMI (Ireland or Lithuania) is legally admissible for processing SEPA flows but must be engaged under an outsourcing arrangement compliant with the EBA Outsourcing Guidelines, ACPR position 2020-03 and PSD2/EMD2 agency rules; a UK EMI is post-Brexit a third-country provider requiring licensed agent or branch structures and cannot be the primary Treasury bank; the recommended architecture is a tier-1 French bank for safeguarding and TARGET2 settlement, with an EEA EMI only for niche cross-border merchant-acquiring and faster-disbursement channels
