Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS under the Code de commerce; the capital is freely set in the articles and must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account with a bank authorized by the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations or with a notary, and at least 50% of cash subscriptions must be paid up upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. In-kind contributions are fully paid up upon delivery, and if they consist of real estate or going concern, require the intervention of a notary and a statutory auditor (commissaire aux apports).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification for the registered office/workshop (attestation délivrée par un bureau de contrôle under Article R.123-43 of the French Construction and Housing Code for premises receiving employees); structural load inspection of any equipment storage area; electrical safety verification of the workshop installation by a qualified electrician; the employer must draft and display the single risk assessment document (Document Unique) and comply with the Labour Code obligations on occupational health, prevention and the use of personal protective equipment on client substation sites.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS under the Code de commerce; the capital is freely set in the articles and must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be deposited into a blocked escrow account with a bank authorized by the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations or with a notary, and at least 50% of cash subscriptions must be paid up upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. In-kind contributions are fully paid up upon delivery, and if they consist of real estate or going concern, require the intervention of a notary and a statutory auditor (commissaire aux apports).

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — no national industrial operation permit is required for a SCADA/PLC integration contractor under French law; the company should nonetheless hold a professional electrical qualification certificate (e.g., Qualifelec or OPQIBI) to evidence technical competence for infrastructure works by grid operators such as RTE or Enedis.

Category: Not required for the SCADA integration service activity itself; where the company performs fixed electrical installation works in substations, the works contract will require evidence of professional qualification and compliance with NF C 15-100 standards, rather than a separate operating license.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), including the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE); the national business formalities portal is the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the INPI Guichet unique. Following registration, the Institut national de la statistique et des études économiques (INSEE) assigns the entity a SIREN and establishment-specific SIRET; the SIREN is the principal domestic tax identifier. The company establishes its professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr and contacts its competent SIE for corporate tax and VAT administration. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in France must register with the DGFiP service competent for foreign businesses, provide incorporation, identity, activity, and transaction evidence, and appoint a French VAT fiscal representative where required. Non-EU businesses are generally required to appoint a representative jointly liable for VAT, subject to applicable legal exceptions and mutual-assistance arrangements. An intra-Community VAT number is requested from the competent tax office; the French format is FR followed by a two-character key and the SIREN.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A French SAS is registered through the INPI Guichet unique. Following registration, the Institut national de la statistique et des études économiques (INSEE) assigns the entity a SIREN and establishment-specific SIRET; the SIREN is the principal domestic tax identifier. The company establishes its professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr and contacts its competent SIE for corporate tax and VAT administration. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in France must register with the DGFiP service competent for foreign businesses, provide incorporation, identity, activity, and transaction evidence, and appoint a French VAT fiscal representative where required. Non-EU businesses are generally required to appoint a representative jointly liable for VAT, subject to applicable legal exceptions and mutual-assistance arrangements. An intra-Community VAT number is requested from the competent tax office; the French format is FR followed by a two-character key and the SIREN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A qualifying newly created company may elect, subject to statutory ownership, activity, size, and age conditions, for temporary partnership-style income taxation for up to five fiscal years under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code. This is a limited statutory election, not a general check-the-box regime. No general hybrid-mismatch classification election is available. Eligible companies may separately elect for a French VAT group subject to statutory conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France does not provide a general turnover threshold below which a business making taxable domestic supplies is exempt from VAT registration. The franchise en base is a small-business exemption, not a general registration threshold: the principal thresholds are EUR 85,000 for sales of goods and accommodation and EUR 37,500 for services, with respective tolerance thresholds of EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250. Applicable thresholds and eligibility should be confirmed for the relevant tax year and activity. A business that waives or is ineligible for the franchise, or that carries out transactions requiring registration, must register as applicable. For specified cross-border EU distance sales and electronic services, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold may apply under its conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union may be exempt with the right to deduct input VAT, subject to the applicable French VAT rules and evidence that the goods physically left the EU. Intra-Community supplies may be exempt with deduction where substantive conditions are met, including valid customer VAT identification and proof of transport. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: their place of supply is determined under the applicable B2B or B2C rules; services supplied to a business established outside the EU are generally outside French VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule, subject to exceptions and evidence of customer status and location.

  • Export customs declaration and evidence of exit from the European Union, such as the customs exit confirmation or equivalent electronic customs record
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract or purchase order, and records identifying the goods and transaction
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the shipment, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, or carrier delivery documentation
  • For intra-Community supplies, customer VAT-number verification and evidence of dispatch or transport to another Member State
  • For services treated as supplied outside France, contract, customer business-status evidence, and records supporting the place-of-supply treatment
  • Bank and payment records supporting the transaction where relevant; an inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory export-VAT requirement

France is implementing domestic B2B e-invoicing and transaction/payment e-reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all in-scope businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; issuance obligations begin on that date for large and mid-sized businesses and generally on 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises. In-scope invoices are to be exchanged through an approved private platform (plateforme agréée), with required invoice data transmitted to the tax administration; e-reporting applies to specified transactions outside the domestic e-invoicing mandate. France does not generally require real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of each invoice. Public-sector invoicing is subject to the separate Chorus Pro system.

The taxpayer must retain reliable evidence supporting the export or other exemption and its VAT returns. Unsupported exemption may be denied, with output VAT assessed retrospectively, applicable late-payment interest, and tax penalties. Deliberate breaches may attract increased penalties under the French Tax Code, including penalties of 40% for deliberate failure and 80% in specified cases of fraud or abusive conduct. Criminal sanctions may apply to qualifying tax fraud. France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has full current-account convertibility and generally unrestricted capital-account convertibility under European Union law. A French SAS may receive foreign capital, pay foreign suppliers, repay intercompany debt, distribute dividends, and remit liquidation proceeds without a general foreign-exchange approval or forced conversion requirement. Dividend and interest payments remain subject to French corporate-law approvals, withholding-tax rules, treaty or European Union relief conditions, beneficial-ownership requirements, transfer-pricing rules, anti-abuse provisions, sanctions screening, and sufficient distributable reserves. Foreign investment screening may apply to non-European Union acquisitions or control investments in activities affecting essential infrastructure, energy, national security, or other protected sectors. SCADA and substation automation activities should be screened on a transaction-specific basis before a change of control or qualifying investment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a bank, convert them into euros, or convert a prescribed percentage of receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to ordinary accounting, tax, anti-money-laundering, sanctions, and payment-service controls. There is no general statutory repatriation deadline or mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters must nevertheless comply with customs declaration requirements, maintain commercial and transport records, reconcile receivables under accounting and tax rules, and satisfy any documentary requirements imposed by banks, insurers, lenders, or trade-finance providers. Cash or equivalent monetary instruments transported into or out of the European Union at an aggregate value of EUR 10,000 or more require the applicable customs declaration. Banks may request payment-purpose, contract, invoice, export-declaration, and beneficial-owner information.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank; prudential and payment-services supervision is principally conducted by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, and customs cash-reporting matters are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operating payments and foreign-currency collections, but low to medium as a substitute for a French domestic bank in the incorporation phase or for complex trade finance. A regulated European Economic Area electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, SEPA services, foreign-exchange execution, cards, and cross-border transfers, subject to its license scope, safeguarding model, onboarding policy, and correspondent access. An electronic-money account is not necessarily a bank deposit and generally does not provide a French capital-deposit certificate for SAS incorporation unless the provider is legally authorized and accepted for that purpose. Offshore or non-European Union digital EMIs may face correspondent, licensing, sanctions, data, safeguarding, and French establishment-risk limitations. The company should retain a regulated French or European bank for capital escrow, credit facilities, payroll continuity, and trade-finance capacity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route subscribed equity through a bank account held in the name of the subscriber or clearly documented investor to the French SAS capital-escrow account. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments where available and SWIFT cross-border transfer for non-euro currencies. Include an unambiguous remittance description such as 'capital contribution', 'subscription for share capital', or 'shareholder loan', together with the entity name and corporate reference. Do not combine equity, loan, service fees, or customer receipts in one unexplained transfer. Provide the bank with the subscription agreement, draft or amended articles, shareholder resolution, investor ownership and source-of-funds evidence, originating-account proof, and foreign-investment-screening analysis where relevant. The depositary bank issues the capital-deposit certificate after verification; funds are released after evidence of incorporation and registration, typically including the Kbis extract. For a later capital increase, obtain the applicable corporate approvals and updated depositary documentation before remittance. Reconcile each receipt to the general ledger and preserve bank and corporate records.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro-area suppliers, subject to bank limits and fraud controls. Use SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly represented by MT103 in legacy messaging or the corresponding ISO 20022 payment message, for non-SEPA currencies and jurisdictions. Include the beneficiary's legal name, verified bank-account details, invoice number, contract reference, currency, and payment purpose. For higher-value, documentary, or jurisdictionally sensitive transactions, use a commercial letter of credit issued or confirmed by a bank under UCP 600, with documentary presentation through the banking chain; use documentary collections under URC 522 where commercially appropriate. Apply sanctions, export-control, dual-use, end-user, and goods-classification checks before release. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. A qualifying small or medium-sized enterprise may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to the applicable ownership, turnover and capital-payment conditions; the excess remains taxable at 25%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. A qualifying EU or EEA parent may obtain a domestic exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary regime, commonly where the parent holds at least 10% for the required period and anti-abuse and subject-to-tax conditions are met. Tax treaties may reduce the rate, commonly to 5%, 10% or 15%, depending on the treaty and ownership level.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the annual return is generally due by the third working day after 1 May of the following year. For a financial year ending on a date other than 31 December, the return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The final corporate income tax balance is generally payable by the fifteenth day of the fourth month following the financial year-end, with a special deadline for 31 December year-ends. Instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Local File and Master File obligations generally apply where the French entity or the French tax-consolidated group has annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, or where it is controlled by, controls or belongs to a group including an entity meeting the EUR 400 million threshold. A French entity below that threshold may still be required to provide sufficient transfer-pricing evidence during an audit. An annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies at or above EUR 50 million of turnover or gross assets, or where another statutory group condition is met.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a standalone regime formally equivalent to the United Arab Emirates or certain offshore Economic Substance Regulations. French substance is assessed through general anti-abuse principles, transfer-pricing rules, permanent-establishment rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, the place of effective management, anti-conduit doctrines, controlled-foreign-company rules, interest limitation rules and the general anti-abuse rule. The SAS should maintain French decision-making evidence, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises, technical capability, contract performance records, bank-account control, customer and supplier evidence, and contemporaneous support for all intercompany charges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, a surcharge that may reach 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse, and criminal tax-fraud penalties. Individuals may face imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000; aggravated cases may reach seven years and EUR 3 million. Fines for legal persons may be increased to five times the individual fine where the statutory conditions are met.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may incur criminal liability, civil liability and tax-authority liability where they personally participated, authorised the conduct or committed serious and repeated management misconduct causing non-recovery of tax. Courts may impose management bans, professional disqualification, asset confiscation or a travel restriction where legally justified; travel bans are not automatic.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Profit adjustment, withholding-tax exposure, interest, penalties and transfer-pricing documentation penalties under the French Tax Procedure Code. The amount depends on the nature of the failure, the reassessed profits or undocumented transactions, repeated conduct and cooperation during the audit.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability to the company or creditors and criminal exposure if documentation failure forms part of intentional tax fraud, false accounting, misuse of corporate assets or insolvency misconduct. Ordinary pricing disagreement alone does not automatically create personal criminal liability.
Shell Company, Artificial Residency or Permanent-Establishment Concealment

Statutory sanction: Reallocation of profits, denial of treaty benefits, French corporate income tax, VAT and payroll reassessments, interest and tax surcharges. Artificial arrangements may be challenged under the French abuse-of-law or general anti-abuse framework.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for deliberate misrepresentation, fictitious accounting, insolvency misconduct or tax fraud. Management bans, confiscation and criminal penalties may apply after judicial proceedings; there is no automatic veil piercing solely because the company has foreign shareholders.
Improper Dividend, Royalty or Interest Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, interest, applicable surcharges, denial of treaty relief and potential sanctions for unlawful distributions or misuse of corporate assets. Civil restitution may be ordered for improperly distributed amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore unlawful distributions and may face civil or criminal liability for knowingly approving fictitious accounts, unlawful distributions, misuse of corporate assets or fraudulent withholding-tax treatment.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Beneficial-Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Suspicious-transaction reporting obligations may apply to regulated intermediaries, and money-laundering offences may lead to imprisonment of up to five years and substantial fines, with aggravated penalties in specified circumstances. Failure to register or accurately report beneficial ownership may produce administrative and criminal consequences.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally prosecuted for laundering, complicity, concealment or false declarations. Asset freezing, confiscation, management prohibitions and judicial travel restrictions may be ordered where statutory conditions are satisfied.
Accounting Fraud and Corporate Misuse

Statutory sanction: Civil damages, repayment, shareholder remedies, insolvency proceedings and criminal sanctions for misuse of corporate assets, false accounting or distribution of fictitious dividends. Additional tax reassessment and penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: The SAS's limited liability does not protect directors from their own tortious or criminal conduct. Directors may face restitution, disqualification from management, personal liability for an insufficiency of assets in insolvency where serious management faults contributed to the shortfall, confiscation and imprisonment where applicable.
VAT, Customs and Import Misclassification

Statutory sanction: VAT and customs reassessment, import duties, interest, administrative penalties, seizure and potentially criminal customs or tax proceedings. Fraudulent conduct can attract aggravated tax penalties and criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise for intentional fraud, customs smuggling, false declarations or serious management misconduct. Civil restitution and judicial restrictions may accompany criminal sanctions.
Sanctions, Export-Control and Dual-Use Equipment Risk

Statutory sanction: Administrative measures, transaction prohibitions, asset freezes, fines, licence consequences and criminal penalties may apply under French, European Union and applicable international sanctions regimes. Penalties depend on the relevant measure and conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for intentional or negligent sanctions breaches, complicity or obstruction. Personal fines, imprisonment, confiscation and management prohibitions may apply; a travel ban requires a competent judicial or administrative basis and is not automatic.
