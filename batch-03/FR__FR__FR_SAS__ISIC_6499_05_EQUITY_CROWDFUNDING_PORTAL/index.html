Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), French Public Finances General Directorate; official tax administration portal: impots.gouv.fr; business taxpayer interface and e-filing via 'Espace Professionnel' and Chorus Pro.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS, the statutory TIN (SIREN) and establishment ID (SIRET) are obtained automatically upon company registration with the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CFE) within the jurisdiction of the registered office. The DGFiP subsequently issues the VAT identification number (n° TVA intracommunautaire) through the impots.gouv.fr professional portal after the CFE dossier is transmitted. Non-resident entities engaging in direct taxable supplies without a fixed establishment must complete the simplified registration regime under Article 286 ter of the CGI via the 'Service des impôts des entreprises', submitting proof of legal existence, power of attorney, expected activity, and a French fiscal representative only when required under applicable VAT rules.

The DGFiP subsequently issues the VAT identification number (n° TVA intracommunautaire) through the impots.gouv.fr professional portal after the CFE dossier is transmitted. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a check-the-box classification system. An SAS is automatically subject to entity-level corporation income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) under Article 206-1 of the CGI. Available elections include the option to form a tax consolidation group under Article 223 A CGI where at least 95% control and a minimum one-year inclusion period are met, the option for group subsidiaries to use loss-absorption and integration equalisation, and the mandatory filing of excess-fragmentation and hybrid-mismatch notifications required under EU ATAD as transposed into French domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Under Article 293 B CGI, the French VAT exemption ('franchise en base de TVA') implies mandatory registration once annual turnover exceeds EUR 37,500 for services (the equity crowdfunding portal's core activity) or EUR 85,000 for goods. Increased 'seuil majoré' thresholds of EUR 41,000 for services and EUR 93,500 for goods apply where the immediately preceding year did not exceed the normal threshold. An entity may voluntarily register for VAT from its first taxable transaction even below these thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union may be zero-rated under Article 262-1 CGI if the physical export is proved, the goods leave the EU within the prescribed period, and the exporter retains evidence of the export declaration accepted by customs. For the equity crowdfunding portal's cross-border services, French VAT does not apply as a 0% rate; instead, B2B services supplied to non-EU business customers are outside the territorial scope under Articles 259 A and 259 B CGI, and B2C services supplied to non-EU consumers are generally outside French VAT where the effective use and enjoyment occurs outside the EU, with supporting documentation required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation) accepted and validated by French or EU customs authorities
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, Air Waybill, or equivalent transport document evidencing physical departure of goods from EU/EEA territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank credit advice, or SWIFT MT103 showing the export payment has been received and repatriated

France is implementing mandatory structured electronic invoicing and e-reporting for domestic B2B transactions via accredited dematerialization platforms (PDPs) and the public portal Chorus Pro. Phased entry into force is 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized enterprises and 1 September 2027 for small enterprises and micro-enterprises. E-reporting is mandatory for B2C sales and cross-border transaction data. The regime uses PEPPOL-like standards and API-based transmission to DGFiP, but France does not currently impose continuous transaction control with real-time fiscal clearance or Italian-style digital stamping.

The DGFiP audits export zero-rating claims strictly. Missing or contradictory proof that goods failed to leave the EU, or documentation indicating the exporter is not the true seller in substance, will trigger re-characterisation as a domestic taxable supply and assessment of 20% French VAT. For deliberate misstatements, concealment, or organised fraudulent practice, the taxpayer faces late-payment interest at 0.2% per month plus penalties up to 40% for bad faith and up to 80% for organised fraud, with a ten-year retrospective limitation period in fraud cases and possible criminal sanctions under Article 1741 CGI.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member and the euro is fully convertible for both current-account and capital-account operations. There are no domestic exchange controls restricting inbound or outbound transfers, and profit remittance is unrestricted after ordinary tax and filing obligations are met. Dividends from a French SAS to non-resident shareholders are in principle subject to the 30% domestic withholding provided by Article 119 bis of the Code Général des Impôts, before treaty relief (generally ranging from 0% to 25%) or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption for qualifying corporate shareholders. The principal practical hurdles to capital mobility are the €12,500 per-counterparty monthly balance-of-payments notification obligation to Banque de France, EU sanctions and AML filtering, and the FDI screening regime under Articles L151-1 et seq. of the Code monétaire et financier for activities falling within sensitive sectors or subject to certain foreign government ownership thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. France does not impose surrender of export proceeds, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines; a French SAS may freely retain in any currency all foreign trade proceeds and service revenue. Nonetheless, cross-border cash transactions in excess of €12,500 per counterparty per calendar month must be reported to Banque de France as balance-of-payments statistics, and payments involving a foreign State or non-cooperative jurisdiction may be subject to enhanced documentary orientation. Client funds collected by the crowdfunding platform under Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 are segregated and are not treated as export proceeds for any surrender or conversion purpose.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (central bank and balance-of-payments statistical authority), with prudential supervision of credit institutions and ECSP/PSFP registrations by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); securities and crowdfunding conduct supervision by the Autorité des Marchés Financiers (AMF). The Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) administers exchange-control policy and foreign investment screening under the Code monétaire et financier.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for operational treasury; Low for client-facing escrow under the ECSP/PSFP regime. A non-French EU electronic money institution (EMI) can passport into France under Directive 2009/110/EC and open an operational treasury account for the SAS. However, Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 requires crowdfunding client funds to be safeguarded by one or more credit institutions, or authorised payment/e-money institutions in segregated accounts, and de facto French ACPR guidance and bank counterparty practice require the underlying collection and escrow accounts to sit with a fully licensed French or EU credit institution. Offshore EMIs located outside the EEA cannot lawfully provide account services in France without a French or passporting licence, therefore an offshore EMI is viable only for ancillary disbursement routing, never for client-money custody or regulatory capital deposits.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

A 25% domestic withholding rate generally applies to dividends paid by a French company to non-residents, subject to reductions or exemptions under an applicable tax treaty or EU law. Treaty rates vary by recipient jurisdiction, beneficial ownership, and participation conditions. Dividends paid to eligible EU parent companies may qualify for the Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption, subject to its conditions and anti-abuse rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the filing deadline is generally the second working day following 1 May. The final corporate tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month after year-end; for a 31 December year-end, generally 15 May. Tax instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A French Local File and Master File are generally required for entities or French tax groups meeting the statutory threshold of EUR 400 million in annual turnover or gross assets, or belonging to a group meeting specified EUR 400 million thresholds or control conditions. Separate simplified transfer-pricing reporting obligations may apply to qualifying entities and certain transactions; the applicable threshold and reporting scope should be checked for the relevant year. Documentation must be available to the tax authorities upon request.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime. French tax rules nevertheless permit challenges to artificial arrangements, sham transactions, abuse of law, and arrangements lacking commercial substance. Transfer-pricing outcomes must reflect the actual functions performed, assets used, and risks controlled. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate tax evasion and fraudulent returns

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessments, late-payment interest, and surcharges may apply. Tax fraud may result in criminal penalties, including imprisonment and substantial fines; penalties depend on the offence, amount, and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically liable for company tax debts. Personal criminal liability may arise from their own participation or complicity. Statutory tax collection liability may apply where a director's fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated tax non-compliance makes collection impossible, subject to judicial determination.
Transfer-pricing misstatement and undocumented related-party dealings

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable profits, assess tax, interest, and penalties, and impose specific documentation-related fines under the applicable rules. The amount depends on the breach and statutory calculation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability only where a separate statutory basis or their own criminal conduct is established; a transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not pierce the corporate veil.
Unlicensed regulated crowdfunding activity

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include injunctions, cessation of activity, administrative sanctions, financial penalties, and criminal liability under the applicable financial-services legislation. The precise sanction depends on the activity and authorization breached.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face personal administrative or criminal proceedings for their own participation. Limited liability is not a defense to personal offences.
Money laundering, terrorist financing, and sanctions violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative and criminal sanctions, fines, activity restrictions, and asset-freezing measures may apply under French and EU law. Sanctions and liability are fact-specific and may be severe.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for their own acts or omissions where the statutory elements are met. Corporate status does not shield an individual from personal criminal liability.
Dividend or payment withholding-tax non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The payer may be liable for the unpaid withholding tax, late-payment interest, surcharges, and applicable penalties. Improper treaty relief may be denied and withholding recovered.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically personally liable for withholding-tax debts; personal exposure may arise under statutory collection rules or for their own fraudulent or criminal conduct.
Shell-company or artificial tax arrangement

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny tax benefits, recharacterize transactions, reassess tax, and impose interest and penalties. Fraud, money laundering, or other offences may trigger separate criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Personal liability requires a statutory basis or proof of personal participation in an offence. Fraudulent conduct may support criminal prosecution and, where legal conditions are met, personal collection liability; travel restrictions are not an automatic tax consequence.
