Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Conditionally permissible at formation stage. An SAS may validly establish its siège social at a licensed commercial domiciliation address in accordance with Article L123-11-2 of the Code de commerce. However, for the ACPR authorization of a cross-border money transmission service under the transposed Payment Services Directive (CMF Book VI), the ACPR requires a genuine local establishment in France with physical presence, decision-making and compliance functions, personnel with AML expertise, and full audit-trail and record-keeping capacity at the French office. A purely virtual office without French staff is insufficient to sustain the payment-institution license and must be upgraded to substantive premises prior to the license decision.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A Société par Actions Simplifiée has no statutory minimum share capital. The statutes determine the capital amount. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Funds may be deposited with a French bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the deposit certificate is required for registration. The amount shown is an approximate USD conversion of the standard commercial registration fee of EUR 37.45; publication, beneficial-owner filing, licensing, banking, and professional fees are excluded.

Funding & unlock path: Before opening premises, obtain landlord and local-authority evidence that the premises satisfy applicable building and fire-safety rules. For premises receiving the public, verify ERP classification, evacuation routes, emergency lighting, alarms, extinguishers, accessibility, and, where required, an opening or safety opinion from the competent municipal authority. Complete electrical, fire-equipment, and structural-load inspections where applicable. Establish workplace risk assessment documentation (document unique d'évaluation des risques), occupational-health arrangements, displayed safety instructions, and staff training. Cash-handling sites may require additional physical-security and insurance controls.

Paid-in capital rule: A Société par Actions Simplifiée has no statutory minimum share capital. The statutes determine the capital amount. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Funds may be deposited with a French bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, and the deposit certificate is required for registration. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), acting through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and the non-resident tax services; tax portal: impots.gouv.fr. Customs and export/EORI matters are handled by Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS: incorporate through the guichet unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (INPI/RNE), which issues SIREN and SIRET; DGFiP then allocates the French intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + two-digit key + 11-digit SIREN. For a foreign entity without a French establishment: file Form Cerfa 16503 with the competent SIE/non-resident unit; provide constitutional documents, power of attorney, proof of foreign registration, and EU VAT certificate where applicable; if established outside the EU, appoint a French tax representative under CGI Article 286 A; DGFiP issues the French VAT number and DGDDI issues the EORI number for customs operations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SAS: incorporate through the guichet unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (INPI/RNE), which issues SIREN and SIRET; DGFiP then allocates the French intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + two-digit key + 11-digit SIREN. For a foreign entity without a French establishment: file Form Cerfa 16503 with the competent SIE/non-resident unit; provide constitutional documents, power of attorney, proof of foreign registration, and EU VAT certificate where applicable; if established outside the EU, appoint a French tax representative under CGI Article 286 A; DGFiP issues the French VAT number and DGDDI issues the EORI number for customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box regime. Available elections: (i) option for income tax instead of corporate income tax under CGI Article 239 bis AA for small qualifying SAS during the first five years; (ii) group tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) for French companies held at least 95%; (iii) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under CGI Article 256 C ter; (iv) election for monthly or quarterly VAT returns; (v) election to waive the small-business VAT franchise and remain registered; (vi) specific elections for hybrid mismatch provisions are not applicable under French domestic law absent an EU/ATAD-derived regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for established taxable persons: VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply. Small enterprises may benefit from the franchise en base de TVA exemption (no VAT charged and no registration) up to EUR 37,500 annual turnover for services and EUR 85,000 for goods, with first-year tolerance ceilings of EUR 41,000 and EUR 93,500. For intra-EU B2C distance sales of goods, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold applies before French registration is required. Cross-border money-transfer services are VAT-exempt under CGI Article 261 C regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT under CGI Article 262-I-1° where they are dispatched or transported outside the EU by the seller or on the seller's behalf, with proof of physical departure within 90 days of supply evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration. Services directly related to export logistics and international transport can also be zero-rated. For the target cross-border remittance activity, transfer-of-funds services are exempt from VAT under CGI Article 261 C; they are not zero-rated export services, and input VAT recovery on overheads follows the financial-sector pro-rata deduction rules. B2B services supplied to non-EU-established customers are placed outside France under CGI Article 259 A but remain exempt when they qualify as financial transactions.

  • Accepted customs export declaration (DAU/EXA with MRN)
  • Commercial invoice issued in the seller's name
  • Packing list and transport documents (Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill)
  • Proof of physical departure from the EU (carrier certificate, port or airport stamps)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing settlement
  • EORI number and export licences where the goods are regulated

Mandatory e-invoicing is being phased in under the French e-invoicing reform: all established taxable persons must be able to receive structured electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance obligations apply to large and mid-size enterprises from 1 September 2026 and to SMEs/micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027. Invoices must be transmitted through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the Public Invoicing Portal (PPF) in structured formats compatible with EN16931/UBL; transaction data e-reporting to DGFiP follows the same timetable through PPF APIs or certified PDPs. France does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-invoice authorization; compliance is verified through periodic VAT returns (CA3/CA12) and cross-checked e-reporting data.

If the 0% export exemption is claimed without valid proof of export or within the 90-day departure rule, DGFiP will reinstate French VAT at 20% plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month and penalties under CGI Articles 1729 and 1788-A (40%-80% in cases of bad faith or abusive practices). Customs authorities may impose fines of one to three times the value of the goods for false declarations under the Code des Douanes. The anti-abuse procedure under LPF Article L64 permits recharacterisation of artificial arrangements; serious or fraudulent conduct carries criminal penalties (up to five years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine) and publication sanctions. Input VAT improperly deducted on the VAT-exempt remittance activity is clawed back with interest and penalties, and refunds are suspended until corrected declarations are filed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full capital account convertibility for EU residents and non-residents. Profit remittances are unrestricted post-tax settlement and beneficial ownership verification. Non-resident capital injections require TRACFIN reporting above 10,000 EUR thresholds with source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds applies. Foreign currency receipts must be declared to customs within 30 days and retained in foreign currency accounts subject to tax reporting and AML compliance.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

EMI cross-border viability is feasible under EU PSD2 and MiCA frameworks but requires ACPR authorization for money transmission. Legal constraints include strict AML/KYC obligations and licensing as a payment institution.
