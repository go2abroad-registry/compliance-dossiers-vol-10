Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation period of 30 days for an SAS. A proposed name is checked during preparation and filing, but availability is not a definitive legal clearance and there is no universal reservation extension procedure. Conduct searches through the INPI company and trademark databases, Infogreffe or equivalent registry data, domain registries, and relevant sector registers. A separate trademark objection or infringement issue must be handled before the competent industrial-property or judicial authority.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription; the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Cash contributions are deposited with a qualifying bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, which issues the certificate of deposit. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed and may require a commissaire aux apports report, subject to statutory exemptions.

Funding & unlock path: No industrial civil-defense clearance is ordinarily required for a standard SaaS office. The landlord or operator must address building safety, fire detection, evacuation routes, accessibility, and, where applicable, ERP obligations for premises open to the public. The employer must complete the occupational risk assessment in the DUERP, provide safe working conditions, and arrange legally required inspections. Structural-load, fire, and electrical certifications are required where imposed by the building, lease, works, or premises classification.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full euro convertibility and unrestricted current-account and capital-account liberalization under Articles 63-66 TFEU and Articles L151-1 to L151-7 of the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF). There are no exchange-control barriers to profit remittance, dividend distribution, capital repatriation, or liquidation proceeds payable to non-resident shareholders of a French SAS. The only residual controls apply to foreign direct investment in national-security-sensitive sectors listed in Article R153-1 et seq. CMF, which does not cover ISIC 6201 software publishing. A French SAS distributing dividends to a non-resident corporate shareholder is normally subject to French withholding tax at 25% for non-treaty EEA shareholders, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the beneficial owner is an EU/EAA company holding at least 5% of the SAS capital for an uninterrupted two-year period; interest on shareholder loans is generally exempt. Cross-border transfers equal to or exceeding EUR 1,000 must be effected through a licensed credit institution and are reported for statistical purposes by the intermediary to the Banque de France; no prior administrative authorization is required for a consumer micro-SaaS treasury.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or repatriation deadline for export proceeds under current French law. A French SAS may lawfully retain export receipts in USD, GBP, or any other currency in resident or non-resident correspondent accounts with no obligation to convert into euros. The residual statutory obligation under Article L151-2 CMF is limited to routing cross-border transfers and payments through an approved credit institution or assimilated intermediary. Banque de France Instruction No. 91-01 imposes statistical declaration of each cross-border transaction equal to or exceeding EUR 1,000; for a micro-SaaS collecting export revenues via Paddle or Lemon Squeezy, the declaration is made by the payment institution or the acquiring bank, not by the exporting entity. Direct balance-of-payments filing by the SAS will arise only if the entity is selected in the Banque de France direct corporate survey, which depends on annual cross-border flow thresholds rather than FX holdings.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), a member of the Eurosystem, acting as the national central bank and the competent authority for balance-of-payments statistics and cross-border payment surveillance; foreign exchange policy and exchange-control direction are exercised by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) within the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique; prudential and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions and payment institutions is vested in the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution), with operational suspicious-transaction reporting to Tracfin.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for ACPR-licensed or EEA-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) and digital banks, subject to significant legal constraints. An EMI such as Qonto, Shine, or Pennylane can lawfully hold client funds under the e-money Safeguarding rules, supports fully remote onboarding with video KYC, and provides API-driven multi-currency settlement rails suitable for the Paddle and Lemon Squeezy payout streams. However, an EMI is not a credit institution: client funds are safeguarded rather than deposited, are not covered by the French deposit guarantee fund (FGDR), and e-money issuance presumes an immediate exchange of funds into e-money, making the EMI unsuitable as the long-term home of share capital, VAT reserves, or payroll. An offshore non-EEA EMI without an ACPR passport or French branch registration cannot lawfully open a French-domiciled account or settle balances in the euro system; therefore, the treasury blueprint mandates a licensed Tier-1 bank account as the primary legal entity account and restricts fintech entities to the FX conversion and cross-border collection layer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For initial formation, the non-resident shareholder wires the appel de fonds in euros via SWIFT MT103 to a blocked deposit account (compte bloqué) held either at the chosen French Tier-1 bank or at the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the wire must identify the payer as the exact named shareholder, reference the SAS corporate name and intended SIREN, and carry in Field 70 the indication 'APPORT EN NUMÉRAIRE / CONSTITUTION DE SAS' to allow the bank to issue the statutory deposit certificate (certificat du dépositaire des fonds). After the SAS obtains its Kbis registration, the blocked funds are released to the operational account. For a subsequent capital increase, the same MT103 protocol applies with reference to the notarial or board resolution increasing capital and the corresponding registry filing. French exchange-control neutrality means no prior authorization is required, but the intermediary bank will include the inbound flow in its Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting and the company must preserve the full upstream source-of-funds documentation, including bank statements of the sending account and the shareholders' agreement, to satisfy ongoing AML reviews.All EU-based disbursements for cloud infrastructure, subcontractors, and statutory expenses settle through the SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) scheme using the operational account IBAN, with SEPA Instant available for urgent sub-EUR 100,000 payments. Payments outside the eurozone, including US-based cloud providers and any non-EU suppliers, settle via SWIFT MT103 through the French bank's correspondent network, with strict consistency between the invoice amount, the SWIFT amount, and the payment reference to avoid transfer-pricing or trade-data mismarches; documentary trade instruments are not necessary for SaaS licensing flows. A commercial letter of credit is structurally permissible but economically inappropriate for recurring software subscriptions and is reserved only for exceptional high-value procurement of physical hardware where the beneficiary requires a bank-backed undertaking. For treasury optimisation, the USD and GBP balances created by Paddle and Lemon Squeezy settlements may be held on multi-currency accounts and converted through the bank's spot desk rather than undergoing wasteful double conversion through the EMI layer.Spot FX outright transactions (EUR/USD and EUR/GBP) executed through the Tier-1 bank treasury desk or regulated digital-brokerage channels for immediate conversion of PSP settlement balancesPlain-vanilla FX forward contracts (outright forwards) up to 12 months, documented under the FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) Master Agreement or ISDA, available for hedging anticipated monthly USD and GBP receipts from Paddle and Lemon Squeezy, provided the notional does not exceed forecast commercial exposureNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs), permissible only where the deliverable EUR/USD or EUR/GBP forward market is unavailable or illiquid; not expected to be used for this micro-SaaS treasuryFX swaps (swap de change) for short-term liquidity smoothing where USD settlement float is swapped into euro funding for VAT or payroll cyclesCross-currency swaps (xCCY), admissible for medium-term intercompany shareholder loans and capital injections, subject to MiFID II appropriateness, derivative-clearing eligibility review, and the prohibition of speculative uncovered positionsEuropean-style currency options (call and put structures) permitted for the SAS as an end-user hedging instrument against adverse USD/EUR or GBP/EUR movements, purchased only as protection on identified licensing receivables or supplier payables and never in a naked speculative capacity

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax rate. A 3.3% social contribution may apply to companies meeting the statutory turnover and corporate income tax liability thresholds, producing a potential marginal combined rate of approximately 25.83%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The standard French withholding tax on dividends distributed by a French company to a non-resident recipient is generally 25%, subject to applicable exemptions and tax treaties. A qualifying EU or European Economic Area parent may obtain an exemption under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, commonly requiring at least 10% ownership for the relevant holding period and satisfaction of anti-abuse and beneficial-owner conditions. A 75% rate may apply to certain payments to non-cooperative jurisdictions or territories.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the annual return and tax balance are generally due by the second business day after 1 May of the following year, commonly 15 May. For other year-ends, the return is generally due within three months after year-end. Corporate income tax installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, subject to statutory exceptions and adjustment rules. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Withholding tax is generally declared and paid by the 15th day of the month following the payment, booking or making-available event, depending on the relevant statutory rule.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory full transfer pricing documentation generally applies where the French entity or French tax group has annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, where it is controlled by or controls an entity meeting the threshold, or where it belongs to a group meeting the applicable statutory conditions. Large groups may also have country-by-country reporting obligations, generally where consolidated group revenue reaches EUR 750 million. Below the threshold, French taxpayers must still demonstrate arm's-length pricing and provide simplified or appropriate documentation on request.

Economic substance scope: France has no separate UAE-style economic substance regulation applicable solely because a company receives foreign income. Substance is assessed through general anti-abuse rules, transfer pricing, permanent establishment, beneficial ownership, controlled foreign company, interest limitation, withholding tax and abuse-of-law doctrines. A French SAS should maintain genuine French decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or documented operating arrangements, accounting records, bank control, customer and supplier evidence, and commercial rationale proportionate to its SaaS activity. Artificial conduit arrangements, circular cash flows and shareholder-controlled expenses may be recharacterized or denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, tax fraud penalties, interest and criminal prosecution. Ordinary tax fraud may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated cases potentially carrying up to seven years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions and judicial determination.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, imprisonment, asset seizure and director disqualification. Courts may impose personal tax liability on a manager under Article L.267 of the French Tax Procedures Code where fraudulent or repeated serious failures made collection from the company impossible. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal proceedings.
VAT fraud and digital-platform misreporting

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, late-payment interest, 10% or higher surcharges, possible 40% or 80% tax penalties depending on conduct, payment-platform information matching, and criminal prosecution for aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for deliberate or repeated VAT fraud, tax collection failure, criminal fines, seizure and disqualification. Personal liability requires the applicable statutory or judicial basis and is not automatic merely because a company has unpaid VAT.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to French taxable income, withholding tax reassessment, interest, documentation penalties, and possible application of the French anti-abuse or abnormal-management-act doctrines. The tax administration may reject unsupported deductions and recharacterize transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable where they knowingly orchestrate fraudulent profit shifting, conceal records or facilitate tax fraud. Routine transfer pricing adjustment alone does not automatically create criminal or personal director liability.
Shell company and artificial substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of income, permanent establishment exposure, withholding tax, corporate tax reassessment, abuse-of-law penalties and potential criminal sanctions where concealment or fraud is established.

Director personal liability: Managers may face civil claims, criminal prosecution, asset seizure and disqualification if they knowingly operate an artificial structure for unlawful tax avoidance or concealment. A lack of substance is not by itself a criminal offence but is a major evidentiary risk.
Unauthorized dividend or disguised distribution

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful distributions, corporate tax reassessment, withholding tax, denied deductions, penalties and potential breach of French corporate law rules governing distributions and related-party transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors may owe civil restitution, damages and potentially criminal penalties for unlawful distributions or misuse of corporate assets. Shareholders who knowingly receive unlawful distributions may also be required to repay them.
Money laundering and AML failure

Statutory sanction: Suspicious transaction reporting obligations, account restrictions, confiscation, administrative sanctions and criminal money-laundering penalties. Aggravated money laundering may carry substantial imprisonment and fines, subject to the applicable facts and statutory provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who participate in or knowingly facilitate laundering may face imprisonment, personal fines, confiscation and disqualification. Directors may also face regulatory sanctions for serious governance or compliance failures.
Bribery, corruption and improper commercial payments

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution, imprisonment and substantial fines for active or passive corruption, confiscation of proceeds, procurement exclusion and corporate liability. Exact sanctions depend on the offence and circumstances.

Director personal liability: Individuals may be prosecuted personally regardless of corporate form. Directors may face imprisonment, fines, confiscation, disqualification and civil claims; the SAS may incur separate corporate criminal liability.
Accounting obstruction and record destruction

Statutory sanction: Tax penalties, rejection of accounting records, estimated assessment, criminal accounting or tax-fraud proceedings, and increased penalties for obstruction or deliberate concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines, imprisonment, civil damages and disqualification where they ordered, approved or knowingly tolerated destruction or falsification of records.
Foreign exchange, sanctions and restricted-party breach

Statutory sanction: Payment blocking, asset freezing, administrative and criminal penalties, confiscation, contractual termination and regulatory enforcement under applicable French and EU sanctions rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal criminal exposure where they knowingly authorize or negligently facilitate prohibited transactions. Corporate veil protection does not shield intentional sanctions violations.
