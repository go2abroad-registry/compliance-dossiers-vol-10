Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises operated by the INPI (Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle), forwarding the dossier to the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) kept at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered office's court district (for Paris: Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce de Paris).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce) Articles L.227-1 to L.227-4 (SAS constitution and capital), L.227-5 to L.227-19 (governance and powers of the President), and R.224-3 (RCS filing formalities); implemented jointly with Ordinance No. 2023-393 on the single business formalities portal (guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises).

Formation / constitutional filings

Formulaire M0 (CERFA n° 11680) - Declaration of Creation of a Commercial Company (Déclaration de création d'une société), filed electronic-only through the INPI guichet unique portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 21 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for a French SAS; the articles freely fix the amount, which must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be released on incorporation to at least 50% of nominal value where capital equals or exceeds EUR 37,000, otherwise to at least one-fifth under the SARL regime (Art. L.223-7), with the balance payable within five years. Paid-in cash must be deposited in a blocked account (compte bloqué) at a licensed French credit institution; the bank's attestation de dépôt de fonds is filed with the RCS dossier and funds are released only after registration. In-kind contributions require a statutory auditor's valuation report (commissaire aux apports) unless waived by unanimous shareholders.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: conformity certificate delivered by the local Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) upon inspection of the production facility, including emergency exits, extinguishing systems, and storage of lithium batteries/electron components. Structural clearance: load-bearing verification certificate issued by a licensed civil engineering office confirming floor capacity for the ventilation test benches and machining equipment. Occupational clearance: inspection by DREETS Île-de-France covering CE-marked machinery, electrical installation conformity, machine guarding, and air quality in assembly and test rooms; approval of the CSE (Comité Social et Économique) health and safety rules and the unique risk assessment document (document unique d'évaluation des risques) required under the Code du travail.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE); non-established businesses generally deal with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). The principal online portal is impots.gouv.fr, including the professional area (Espace professionnel).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the business formalities portal operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). Following registration in the Registre national des entreprises and, where applicable, the Registre du commerce et des sociétés, it receives a SIREN and establishment-level SIRET. The competent SIE assigns or confirms the corporate tax account and French VAT identification number, generally in the format FR plus a two-digit key and the nine-digit SIREN. The company must activate its DGFiP professional account and comply with electronic filing and payment requirements. A foreign entity without a French establishment that makes transactions requiring French VAT registration applies to the SIEE, providing formation documents, proof of business activity, identification and transaction information. Such a taxable person generally has no French VAT turnover threshold. A non-EU-established business may be required to appoint a French VAT fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual assistance arrangement or statutory exception removes that requirement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A qualifying newly formed company may elect temporary partnership-style income taxation under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code, generally for no more than five financial years, subject to ownership, activity, size and age conditions; the election is unavailable to a company whose shares are listed. This is not a US-style check-the-box election. France has no general elective hybrid-mismatch classification regime; anti-hybrid rules implementing EU ATAD provisions may deny deductions or require income inclusion in specified cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to an ordinary taxable business, including a non-established taxable person, once it makes transactions for which French VAT registration is required. Eligible France-established small businesses may use the VAT exemption regime (franchise en base) subject to applicable turnover limits; the generally applicable thresholds are EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for services, with separate prior-year/current-year rules and activity-specific exceptions. Thresholds and eligibility should be checked for the relevant tax year.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the EU may be exempt with the right to deduct input VAT under Article 262 I of the French Tax Code, provided the statutory exporter and documentary conditions are met and exit from the EU customs territory is evidenced. A supply of services is not automatically a zero-rated export: its VAT treatment depends on place-of-supply rules, customer status and any applicable exemption or special rule. Services whose place of supply is outside France are generally outside the scope of French VAT, subject to reporting and evidence requirements. Input VAT refund or deduction requires valid supporting records and a direct link to taxable or otherwise credit-preserving activities.

  • Export customs declaration and MRN, together with electronic confirmation of exit from the EU customs territory where available
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract or purchase order identifying the goods, parties, price and destination
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR, carrier receipt or proof of delivery
  • Evidence identifying the exporter of record and linking the declared goods to the invoiced supply
  • For services, contract, customer VAT or business-status evidence where relevant, deliverables and records supporting the place-of-supply treatment
  • Bank remittance records where available as corroboration; an inward remittance certificate is not a general French statutory condition for export exemption

France does not generally operate a real-time VAT invoice clearance or digital-stamping system. Under the enacted domestic B2B reform, businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance obligations apply from that date to large and intermediate-sized enterprises and from 1 September 2027 to SMEs and micro-enterprises. Covered domestic B2B invoices must be exchanged through a registered private platform (plateforme agréée) or the public platform designated by law, with required invoice data transmitted to the tax administration. E-reporting applies to specified B2C and cross-border transactions. These rules do not make export customs declarations subject to VAT invoice pre-clearance.

France imposes no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The exporter must retain reliable records supporting export, transport, customer, invoicing and VAT-return treatment and produce them during a DGFiP audit. If exemption or deduction is unsupported, the administration may assess output VAT, deny or recover input VAT deductions, charge late-payment interest and apply tax penalties. Depending on the breach and intent, penalties may include the 5% penalty for specified invoicing or reporting failures, 40% for deliberate non-compliance, 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or concealed activity, and criminal sanctions for tax fraud. Applicable rates depend on the facts, statutory provision and procedure; a good-faith correction may affect penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility of the euro under Articles 63-66 TFEU. There are no exchange-control approvals, currency licences, surrender obligations, or quantitative restrictions on euro or foreign-currency payments. For the French SAS manufacturing ICU ventilators, practical frictions are not convertibility but (i) balance-of-payments statistical declaration to Banque de France for France-versus-foreign transactions of EUR 50,000 or more per settlement; (ii) French foreign-investment screening requiring prior Treasury authorisation where a non-EU/EEA investor acquires control of, or more than 25% of the voting rights in, a French entity active in a sensitive sector listed in Article R. 151-3 CMF (which may include critical public-health manufacturing); and (iii) tax friction on profit remittance, namely the French statutory dividend withholding tax of 25% for corporate non-residents, reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or to treaty-reduced rates, plus the 30% flat tax applied to distributions beneficially held by individuals. None of these hurdles prevents euro-denominated profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. France imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory euro conversion ratio, and no time limit for retaining export receipts abroad. Proceeds from ICU ventilator government contracts may be kept in EUR or foreign currency in any licensed bank account, including non-resident accounts. Compliance is limited to (i) monthly balance-of-payments declaration to Banque de France for export settlements of EUR 50,000 or more, filed by the SAS or its reporting bank with supporting invoices and export customs documentation; and (ii) the French bank's standard documentary verification of the underlying commercial transaction under AML/KYC rules. LC proceeds are credited net of commissions without any forced conversion.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Eurosystem member) is the central bank responsible for monetary stability, balance-of-payments statistics, and oversight of French payment and settlement systems, including TARGET2 participation. Foreign-exchange control and capital-movement regulation are administered through the Ministry of the Economy and Finance (Direction Generale du Tresor) under the Code monetaire et financier, while banking prudential and AML/CFT supervision is delegated to the ACPR (Autorite de Controle Prudentiel et de Resolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Medium for small-value operational disbursements and FX conversion; Low for capital escrow, large trade settlement, and government LC operations. Legal constraints: ACPR-licensed electronic-money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business, Wise) are not credit institutions under Directive 2013/36/EU; they may not take deposits, grant credit, issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, or provide regulated account-pledge or escrow services. A French capital escrow for a SAS must be held with a credit institution or notaire. EMIs also lack the native SWIFT-correspondent and Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting interface expected by French authorities, and their transaction ceilings are generally inadequate for multi-million-euro government tenders. They should be used only as a supplementary payment wallet, never as the primary treasury bank.
