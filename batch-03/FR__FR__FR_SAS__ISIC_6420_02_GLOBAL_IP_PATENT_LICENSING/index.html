Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent clerk of the commercial court (greffe du tribunal de commerce) maintains the Trade and Companies Register (Registre du commerce et des sociétés, RCS); applications are filed through the INPI-operated Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, including Articles L. 210-1 et seq. (commercial companies), L. 227-1 et seq. (SAS), L. 123-1 et seq. (Trade and Companies Register), and R. 123-53 et seq. (registration particulars). SAS governance is principally set by its articles, subject to mandatory statutory rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic application for registration in the RCS (demande d’immatriculation au RCS) submitted through the Guichet unique, supported by the signed SAS articles (statuts) and required evidence.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation; the balance must be paid within five years after registration. Cash contributions must be deposited before registration with an authorized depositary, such as a bank, notary, or the Caisse des dépôts et consignations, and a deposit certificate must be obtained. In-kind contributions are fully contributed at incorporation; an independent contribution auditor is generally required unless a statutory exemption applies. The USD filing-fee figure is an approximate conversion of the RCS registration charge; additional beneficial-owner filing, publication, and service charges may apply.

Funding & unlock path: No industrial fire, structural-load, or occupational operating clearance is ordinarily required solely for a non-industrial IP company. Any occupied premises must comply with applicable building, fire-safety, and workplace health and safety rules; premises open to the public may require ERP authorization and fire-safety review, and industrial installations may require ICPE approvals.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation; the balance must be paid within five years after registration. Cash contributions must be deposited before registration with an authorized depositary, such as a bank, notary, or the Caisse des dépôts et consignations, and a deposit certificate must be obtained. In-kind contributions are fully contributed at incorporation; an independent contribution auditor is generally required unless a statutory exemption applies. The USD filing-fee figure is an approximate conversion of the RCS registration charge; additional beneficial-owner filing, publication, and service charges may apply.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable to a France-based SAS conducting only ownership and cross-border sub-licensing of trademarks and patents. If the company later operates a regulated industrial facility, the competent authority is generally the Prefect, with technical review by the Regional Directorate for Environment, Planning and Housing (DREAL).

Category: No sector-specific manufacturing or operating license is ordinarily required for a pure intellectual-property holding and licensing business.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), France - the online portal is impots.gouv.fr; for corporate tax and VAT registration, the relevant local office is the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and for non-residents the Direction des résidents à l’étranger et des services généraux (DRESG).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity creating a French SAS must apply for a French tax identification number (numéro d’identification) through the DGFiP business formalities portal ('Formalités En Ligne' / guichet-entreprises.fr). For VAT purposes, the SAS obtains a French intra-community VAT number from the SIE upon submission of the bylaws, proof of registration at the RCS (extrait Kbis), proof of registered office or designated fiscal representative (for non-EU principals), identity documents of the legal representative, and a copy of the share capital evidence. A non-EU entity without a French permanent establishment must appoint a fiscal representative approved by the DGFiP for VAT and tax filings. The SIE then issues a TIN letter ('Avis de situation') and an EORI number where customs activities are anticipated.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - an SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the standard 25% rate and to VAT as a taxable person in France. The entity is within the territorial scope of French tax on its French-source and worldwide income as determined by article 209 of the French General Tax Code (CGI). The SAS cannot benefit from pass-through taxation except in narrow statutory exceptions not applicable to a trading IP HoldCo; however, the parent-subsidiary regime (article 145 CGI) and French tax consolidation (article 223 A CGI) are available at the entity level. — A foreign entity creating a French SAS must apply for a French tax identification number (numéro d’identification) through the DGFiP business formalities portal ('Formalités En Ligne' / guichet-entreprises.fr). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - an SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the standard 25% rate and to VAT as a taxable person in France. The entity is within the territorial scope of French tax on its French-source and worldwide income as determined by article 209 of the French General Tax Code (CGI). The SAS cannot benefit from pass-through taxation except in narrow statutory exceptions not applicable to a trading IP HoldCo; however, the parent-subsidiary regime (article 145 CGI) and French tax consolidation (article 223 A CGI) are available at the entity level.

Available election forms

France does not operate a check-the-box election. Available elections relevant to an IP HoldCo include: election into the French VAT group (assujetti unique) under article 256 C III of the CGI; option for simplified or actual VAT settlement regimes under articles 287 and 287-0; election for VAT payment based on proceeds collected rather than invoiced (article 269); option for capital gains rollover relief under article 238 quindecies on IP transfers; election under EU ATAD 2 hybrid mismatch provisions (article 212 bis CGI) to neutralize or disallow deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; and election for the tax neutrality treatment of certain intangible contributions under article 210 A CGI.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established SAS, there is no fixed VAT registration threshold; VAT applies from the first taxable transaction. The 'franchise en base de TVA' exemption is available only to small enterprises with an annual turnover of EUR 85,000 (principal sales of goods) or EUR 37,500 (services), and such businesses may opt for real VAT payment. A non-resident supplier with no establishment in France is required to register from the first French supply unless the transaction is covered by the OSS/IOSS scheme; the intra-EU distance sales threshold is EUR 10,000 for total cross-border supplies of goods and services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under article 262-I-1° CGI applies to supplies of goods physically exported outside the European Union by or on behalf of the supplier. For an IP licensing enterprise, zero-rating is available for cross-border licensing and assignment of patents, trademarks, and underlying intangible rights supplied to a non-French customer under article 262-I-2° CGI, provided the place of supply is not in France under articles 259 A, 259 B, and 259 C CGI, and the recipient is a taxable person established outside the EU. The licensor must hold documentary evidence that the intellectual property rights are legally enforceable in the destination country and that the actual use and exploitation of the IP occurs outside France.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU - Document Administratif Unique) or an EXL electronic export declaration for goods exports
  • Bill of Lading, CMR road consignment note, or air waybill evidencing physical movement of goods outside the EU
  • Commercial invoice issued by the French SAS
  • Customs exit proof validated by French customs or the customs office of exit from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/bank transfer reference showing payment in foreign currency from the non-resident licensee
  • Signed patent/trademark license agreement, proof of IP ownership/registration (INPI records), and a rationale demonstrating the place of actual IP use outside France

France is implementing a mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting regime under the 2023 Finance Act. From 1 September 2026, all taxable persons must be able to receive electronic invoices through a registered identity provider or through the public PPF (Portail Public de Facturation); from 1 September 2027, all taxable persons must issue and transmit e-invoices and transaction data to a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP). The reform imposes structured invoice data (Factur-X or cross-industry invoice formats) and continuous digital audit (via Fichier des Écritures Comptables) under article 289 bis CGI. There is no pre-clearance or digital stamping for standard invoices, but all invoices are subject to real-time or near-real-time transmission obligations once the platform is engaged.

The DGFiP applies strict audit scrutiny on export zero-rating claims. Where the 0% VAT rate has been improperly claimed, the tax authority assesses the unpaid VAT plus late payment interest at 0.20% per month. The French Tax Procedure Code (LPF) allows law adjustment within the normal limitation period ending on 31 December of the third year following the relevant tax year, extended to six years in cases of hidden activity or tax fraud. Penalties are 40% for gross negligence (manquement délibéré) and 80% for fraudulent maneuvers or carousel-type abuse (mauvaise foi); criminal tax fraud under article 1741 CGI carries imprisonment up to three years (five years if aggravated) and fines up to EUR 75,000 (EUR 500,000 for certain cross-border VAT fraud). In addition, customs law penalties may apply to missing export evidence or false declarations, including VAT reassessment and confiscation of reimbursements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member: the euro is fully convertible and capital movements are liberalised under Article 63 TFEU and Articles L151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are free from exchange-control approval. Royalty inflows from foreign sub-licensees and dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders are not exchange-controlled, but the intermediary French bank must perform the statutory balance-of-payments declaration to the Banque de France for cross-border settlements above €75,000 (Article R152-8 CMF) with monthly aggregate reporting and underlying invoices retained for five years. Dividend remittances may attract French withholding tax at 30% or a reduced treaty/Parent-Subsidiary rate; royalty payments to non-resident licensors may attract withholding tax under Article 182 B CGI with treaty relief, yet neither event requires central-bank authorisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable and not imposed in France. There is no statutory surrender obligation, no repatriation window and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or licensing proceeds; the SAS may freely retain and convert foreign-currency receipts at market rates. The only mandatory compliance is the Banque de France statistical cross-border settlement declaration where the monthly transaction value reaches €75,000, which is filed by the collecting French bank on the company's behalf, and the company must maintain the underlying licence contracts, royalty statements and transfer-pricing documentation as evidence of economic substance.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as attached prudential supervisor and the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) as the foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational disbursement accounts, Low as a full-service master treasury bank. An EU-authorised Electronic Money Institution or payment institution passporting into France (e.g., a French PSD2-licensed EMI or a Luxembourg/German EMI using the single passport) may lawfully hold safeguarded client funds and process SEPA and SWIFT-style royalty transfers. However, such institutions cannot grant credit, cannot open a capital-escrow account in the same legal form as a credit institution, are not covered by the French FGDR deposit guarantee on the same terms, and generally cannot provide contracted FX forward/swap lines or documentary credit facilities. The SAS should therefore use a tier-1 credit institution as the master treasury bank and engage an EMI only for a segregated disbursement channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Pre-registration equity capital is deposited in a French bank blocked account (compte bloqué) in the name of the future SAS; the depositary issues an attestation de blocage and the funds are released to the operating account upon RCS registration. Post-incorporation equity or shareholder-loan injections are received by SWIFT MT103 with purpose code CAPC (or the equivalent bank remittance code) referencing the extraordinary general meeting resolution and the French registration number. Inbound royalty collections from EEA sub-licensees are cleared by SEPA Credit Transfer using the ISO 20022 purpose code ROYA (royalties) or LIC (licence fees); non-EEA and non-EUR receipts use SWIFT MT103 with field 70 stating the licence agreement reference, patent or trademark registration number, royalty period and amount, and, where French VAT is chargeable, the matching VAT invoice or reverse-charge reference. The receiving French bank generates the Banque de France statistical declaration for monthly cross-border settlements at or above the €75,000 threshold.Euro disbursements to EEA beneficiaries use SEPA Credit Transfer (standard or instant) with full remittance information. Non-EEA and non-euro disbursements are executed by SWIFT MT103 through the French bank correspondent network with complete beneficiary identification in field 59 and the applicable purpose code LIC or ROYA. Where the SAS secures a counterparty's payment obligation or conditions a milestone payment under a patent acquisition, a Commercial Letter of Credit may be issued under UCP 600 via MT700, or a Standby Letter of Credit may be accepted as collateral. Euro legs clear through TARGET2/Euro1 using the bank's BIC; non-euro legs clear over SWIFT with standard value dating (typically T+2 for USD). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for operational disbursement accounts, Low as a full-service master treasury bank. An EU-authorised Electronic Money Institution or payment institution passporting into France (e.g., a French PSD2-licensed EMI or a Luxembourg/German EMI using the single passport) may lawfully hold safeguarded client funds and process SEPA and SWIFT-style royalty transfers. However, such institutions cannot grant credit, cannot open a capital-escrow account in the same legal form as a credit institution, are not covered by the French FGDR deposit guarantee on the same terms, and generally cannot provide contracted FX forward/swap lines or documentary credit facilities. The SAS should therefore use a tier-1 credit institution as the master treasury bank and engage an EMI only for a segregated disbursement channel.
