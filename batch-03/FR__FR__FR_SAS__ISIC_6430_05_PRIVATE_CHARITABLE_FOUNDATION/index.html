Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), acting through the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). The principal digital portal is impots.gouv.fr. Company formation and statutory business registration are filed through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI); INSEE allocates the SIREN identifier. The French VAT identification number is issued by the DGFiP and generally has the format FR followed by a two-digit control key and the nine-digit SIREN.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS must be formed and registered through the Guichet unique. The registration process transmits corporate data to the competent registries, INSEE and the tax administration. The resulting SIREN identifies the legal entity and the SIRET identifies each establishment. The company must provide its articles of association, registered-office evidence, identity and authority documents for representatives, beneficial-owner information, business activity description, bank and accounting information where requested, and the intended tax and VAT treatment. The company must submit the appropriate tax registration information to its SIE, including the commencement date, expected taxable activities, accounting period, VAT regime and intra-Community transaction status. The SIE issues or confirms the French VAT number where the entity conducts taxable transactions or elects a VAT regime. A foreign entity without a French establishment generally applies for a French VAT number through the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères or the competent DGFiP service. An entity established in another EU Member State generally does not require a French fiscal representative solely for French VAT registration; a non-EU entity normally requires an accredited French fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement removes that requirement. EU-established taxable persons may also use the applicable French OSS regime for qualifying cross-border B2C transactions, but OSS does not replace a French VAT registration when domestic registration is otherwise required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on its taxable profits. The legal form does not create charitable or foundation tax exemption. A charitable or grant-making purpose may support specific non-profit or public-interest exemptions only where the entity satisfies the applicable statutory conditions, including non-lucrative management, absence of private profit distribution and qualifying activities. Grants and donations may be outside the scope of VAT or exempt depending on their legal and economic character, but investment management, commercial services and other taxable activities remain subject to the applicable tax rules.

Available election forms

An SAS may elect temporarily for partnership-style income taxation under the French corporate tax rules if it satisfies the statutory conditions, including qualifying ownership and management requirements, a qualifying commercial, industrial, craft or professional activity, and the applicable age and size limitations. The election is generally limited to five fiscal years and must be made within the prescribed period to the tax administration; it is not a general permanent pass-through election and may be unsuitable or unavailable for a charitable endowment model. There is no United States-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive framework and may deny deductions, impose inclusion or restrict relief for hybrid arrangements; they are statutory anti-avoidance rules rather than elective entity-classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France has no single general VAT registration threshold for all taxable persons. A taxable business carrying out transactions subject to French VAT may be required to register from the first taxable transaction, particularly where it is established outside France or does not qualify for the domestic small-business exemption. The franchise en base de TVA may apply to eligible French-established businesses below the applicable annual turnover thresholds. The principal current thresholds are generally EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and certain accommodation or food activities and EUR 37,500 for services and professional activities, with tolerance thresholds generally of EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250 respectively. Thresholds and sector-specific rules must be verified for the relevant tax year. Intra-Community acquisitions, imports, exports, reverse-charge transactions and transactions subject to special regimes can create registration or reporting obligations independently of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods shipped from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT under Article 262 of the French Code général des impôts, provided the exporter or an authorized intermediary can demonstrate that the goods physically left the European Union and that the transaction, exporter status and customs formalities are properly documented. The customs export declaration, MRN and electronic confirmation of exit are the principal evidence. Intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-identified customer in another EU Member State may qualify for the separate intra-Community supply exemption if transport, customer VAT identification, recapitulative reporting and documentary conditions are satisfied. Services are not generally described as zero-rated exports. B2B services supplied to a business established outside France are normally located outside France under the place-of-supply rules, while qualifying services supplied to EU business customers are generally subject to customer-state reverse charge. Special rules apply to immovable-property services, passenger transport, admission, events, telecommunications, electronically supplied services, financial services and other categories. A charitable or grant-making purpose does not itself establish export VAT exemption.

France is implementing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction-data reporting in phases. All French VAT-taxable businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Mandatory issuance is scheduled from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and certain larger groups and from 1 September 2027 for small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises, subject to statutory transitional rules. Invoices must generally be transmitted through an approved private electronic invoicing platform or the public platform designated by the French system, using structured or hybrid formats and required data fields. E-reporting applies to specified B2C and cross-border transactions. France does not generally operate a universal real-time invoice-clearance or pre-issuance digital-stamping system for ordinary commercial invoices. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. Records, invoice authenticity, integrity and readability must be maintained for the statutory retention period, generally ten years for invoices.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement for export VAT relief. French VAT exemption depends principally on the legal conditions and documentary proof of export or the applicable place-of-supply rule. If the tax administration rejects the exemption, it may assess the VAT that should have been charged, applicable late-payment interest, and penalties. Deliberate or materially negligent misstatements may attract tax penalties, including a 40% surcharge for deliberate failure and an 80% surcharge for fraudulent manoeuvres or abusive conduct, subject to the applicable statutory facts and procedural safeguards. Late-payment interest is generally calculated at the statutory monthly rate applicable to the assessment period. False customs declarations may trigger customs reassessment, customs penalties, loss of preferential treatment and, in serious cases, criminal liability under French and Union customs law. Fraudulent VAT claims, fabricated export evidence or unjustified input-VAT refunds may result in repayment of the refund, denial of input-tax deduction, tax penalties, recovery proceedings and criminal prosecution. Documentary records should be retained for the applicable accounting and tax limitation periods, commonly ten years for invoices and supporting commercial records.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a Eurosystem member, operates a fully convertible currency regime; the euro is freely transferable and there are no exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions for EEA/OECD counterparties. Capital movements are liberalised pursuant to Article 63 TFEU and Articles L151-1 to L153-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code. No prior approval is required for routine capital injections, grant disbursements, or profit remittances; residual restrictions apply only to inward direct investments from non-EEA investors in regulated or strategic sectors and to exceptional safeguard measures. Profit remittances by a French SAS are lawful once tax compliance is satisfied; dividends paid to non-resident shareholders may be subject to withholding tax in the range of 25%-30% unless reduced by a double-tax treaty or exempt under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive. For a private charitable foundation, the distribution of grants is not a profit remittance but a non-commercial transfer made in the public interest, and should be documented as such to avoid recharacterisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender or statutory conversion ratio on export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained freely in euros or foreign currency and may be used for offshore disbursements without Bank of France approval. However, French resident entities must report cross-border transactions to Banque de France for balance-of-payments purposes when the aggregate value of their monthly transactions with non-resident counterparties exceeds €12,500. The declaration must be filed electronically through the Banque de France BOP reporting channel within 20 business days after the end of the calendar month in which the transactions occurred. Export proceeds held on accounts with foreign correspondents must also be reflected in the resident entity's balance-of-payments position reporting.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) is the central bank and the designated balance-of-payments statistics authority for France. Foreign-exchange and capital-movement policy is implemented by the Banque de France in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) of the Ministry of Economy and Finance, while the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) is the consolidated banking supervisor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Medium for auxiliary settlement flows; Low for core endowment custody and capital escrow. French or EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) authorised by ACPR under PSD2 may provide euro IBANs, SEPA payment accounts, multi-currency wallets, and cross-border collections. Their principal legal constraint is that they cannot grant credit, are not deposit-taking credit institutions, and cannot offer the same statutory protection as a bank operating escrow account. EMIs such as Qonto, Wise Business, Airwallex, and Currencycloud may be used as pre-funded operating sub-accounts for grant disbursements, provided that the main treasury account is held with a Tier-1 credit institution. Non-resident directors or indirect UBOs detected through a multi-layer corporate chain, creating opacity risks.

Regular grant disbursements to beneficiaries in high-risk jurisdictions or to natural persons, which could trigger misuse-of-banking-products or money-laundering red flags.

A complete grant disbursement register must be maintained and made available to the bank's AML and compliance units upon request.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound endowment contributions should be effected by SWIFT MT103 from the donor's bank to the French Tier-1 bank using the receiving entity's BIC/IBAN. For euro payments within SEPA, a SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) may be used and must include the ordering customer's name and address, the foundation's full legal name, and a purpose reference identifying the transaction as a charitable endowment contribution. Where a payment category is required, the ISO 20022 Category Purpose Code 'CHAR' is appropriate, supplemented by a free-text reference such as 'gift/endowment contribution'. Because France does not require exchange-control approval, the principal compliance requirement is Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting: all inbound capital flows must be aggregated when the monthly cross-border total exceeds €12,500 and declared through the electronic BOP channel. The receiving bank will also perform sanctions screening and source-of-funds verification prior to crediting the escrow account.Cross-border grant disbursements and trade settlements should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers with full beneficiary name, IBAN/account number, BIC, address, and purpose code. Euro payments within the SEPA zone are cleared as SEPA Credit Transfers (SCT) without FX conversion; large or non-EEA payments require correspondent banking or TARGET2 settlement where the beneficiary bank is directly TARGET2-connected. For commercial letters of credit, the French issuing bank operates through SWIFT MT700; amendments are transmitted by MT707, confirmations by MT710, and documentary collections by MT400/MT910 with MT202 cover when required. The foundation may use the same SWIFT rails to make grant payments directly to foreign beneficiaries; no FX surrender or conversion applies because the underlying activity is non-exporting and all disbursements are charitably motivated.Outright deliverable FX forwards with an authorised credit institution, limited to known grant or endowment currency exposures and documented under an ISDA 1998 FX or 2002 Master Agreement.FX swaps (swap de change) used to convert and re-convert liquidity between the euro functional currency and grant payout currencies.Cross-currency swaps referencing EUR and USD, CHF, or GBP for long-term endowment liabilities or multi-year grant commitments.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currencies when the primary currency is not deliverable onshore; permitted only for hedging identifiable exposures and never for speculative positions.Plain vanilla FX options (European-style calls or puts) purchased with a one-off non-leveraged premium, provided the bank's suitability assessment confirms the foundation has adequate derivative knowledge or the services of a dedicated investment adviser.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium for auxiliary settlement flows; Low for core endowment custody and capital escrow. French or EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) authorised by ACPR under PSD2 may provide euro IBANs, SEPA payment accounts, multi-currency wallets, and cross-border collections. Their principal legal constraint is that they cannot grant credit, are not deposit-taking credit institutions, and cannot offer the same statutory protection as a bank operating escrow account. EMIs such as Qonto, Wise Business, Airwallex, and Currencycloud may be used as pre-funded operating sub-accounts for grant disbursements, provided that the main treasury account is held with a Tier-1 credit institution. UK-licensed EMIs no longer benefit from EU passporting after Brexit and require either a French/EEA licence or an ACPR branch authorisation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on net taxable profits; 15% reduced rate applies to the first EUR 42,500 of annual taxable profit for qualifying small companies (turnover ceiling EUR 10 million, 75% individual ownership, fully paid-up capital).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident beneficial owners (Article 119 bis 2 CGI); dividends qualifying under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive and Article 145 CGI (minimum 10% shareholding held for at least 2 years) are exempt at source (0%).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full Master File and French Local File (Article L.13 AA Code des procédures fiscales) are mandatory when the French entity's annual turnover or gross assets exceed EUR 400,000,000, or when the entity is controlled by, or controls, a group exceeding that threshold; country-by-country reporting applies to consolidated groups with EUR 750,000,000 or more of annual revenue; simplified documentation applies to medium-sized groups.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime comparable to ESR jurisdictions; substance is enforced through the general anti-abuse rule (abus de droit, Article L.64 LPF), the ATAD GAAR (Article 205 A CGI), CFC provisions (Article 209 B CGI), treaty anti-abuse clauses, and the requirement of a genuine economic purpose. A private charitable endowment managed by an SAS must maintain a French governing board, local investment and grant-approval processes, minutes, and complete records at its registered office; otherwise the tax administration may disregard the entity, reclassify the endowment's investment income as commercial profit, and apply the 80% abuse penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Concealment

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years under Article 1741 CGI, increased to EUR 2,000,000 and 7 years when committed in an organised group or through offshore concealment; civil tax penalties of 40% (bad faith) or 80% (fraudulent manoeuvres) plus 0.20% monthly late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: The president and directors face criminal liability for fraud; the tax authorities may impose joint personal liability under Article L.267 LPF for taxes arising from fraudulent conduct, order freezing of personal assets, prohibitions on managing commercial entities, travel bans, and imprisonment upon conviction.
Misuse of Charitable Endowment Regime

Statutory sanction: Withdrawal of the charitable exemption with retroactive effect, reclassification of all accumulated income as commercial profit taxable at 25% or 15%, application of the 80% bad-faith penalty under Article 1729 b CGI, restitution of tax credits, and dissolution proceedings by the competent prefecture or public prosecutor.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for restitution of misappropriated assets, disqualification from managing any foundation or company, personal risk of the action en comblement du passif under Article L.651-2 Code de commerce if mismanagement caused the entity's insolvency, and criminal conviction for abus de confiance (up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine).
AML / Beneficial Ownership / TRACFIN Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fixed administrative fines up to EUR 20,000 per missing beneficial-ownership filing; AML penalties for legal entities up to EUR 375,000 per violation (up to EUR 5,000,000 for aggravated money laundering); prohibition of banking payment rails (Bank Wire, Grant Distribution) and loss of the required statutory licence.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to criminal prosecution for money laundering (up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine), travel bans, asset freezes, and forfeiture of personal property; repeated AML breaches trigger automatic disqualification from corporate office.
Transfer Pricing Abuse and Artificial Offshore Structures

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of profits under French arm's-length rules with 40% bad-faith penalty, 80% abuse-of-law penalty where the arrangement lacks genuine commercial purpose, 0.20% monthly late-payment interest, fines of EUR 15,000 per missing transfer-pricing document, and denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised or benefited from the abusive scheme may be joined to the tax debt under Article L.267 LPF, ordered to replenish corporate assets personally, subjected to international travel bans, and imprisoned upon criminal conviction for tax fraud; extradition risk applies where the arrangement involves non-cooperative jurisdictions.
