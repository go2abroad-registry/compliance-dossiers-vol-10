Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS; the capital is freely fixed in the articles of association. Cash contributions must be paid up in accordance with the articles and deposited into a blocked escrow account at a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts before registration; a capital deposit certificate (attestation de dépôt de fonds) is mandatory for the M0 filing.

Funding & unlock path: Occupational clearance: the leased office must be fit for category ERP (Espace Recevant du Public) or ERT (Espace de Travail) occupancy with valid fire-safety certificates and compliance with French accessibility rules. Workplace arrangements must satisfy the Labor Code (Code du travail) requirements on lighting, ventilation, hygiene, and electrical safety. No civil-defense industrial clearance applies because the activity does not involve classified sites or dangerous substances.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for an SAS; the capital is freely fixed in the articles of association. Cash contributions must be paid up in accordance with the articles and deposited into a blocked escrow account at a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts before registration; a capital deposit certificate (attestation de dépôt de fonds) is mandatory for the M0 filing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French tax administration, with main portal at impots.gouv.fr; corporate registration and SIREN issuance are channelled through the Institut national de la statistique et des études économiques (INSEE) via the Guichet Unique des formalités des entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must first register with the Guichet Unique to obtain a SIREN/SIRET if it creates a French permanent establishment or subsidiary. A French SAS is a separate legal entity and must file an incorporation dossier for automatic assignment of SIREN, APE/NAF code, and a VAT identification number. For direct cross-border VAT registration without a French establishment, the foreign taxable person must apply to the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) using Form 3555-SD, indicating no French fixed establishment; VAT registration is generally granted within two to four weeks and the VAT ID is issued by the DGFiP. There is no statutory minimum capital requirement for a SAS and non-resident ownership is unrestricted, but the manager (président) or a tax representative may be required for non-EU persons.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must first register with the Guichet Unique to obtain a SIREN/SIRET if it creates a French permanent establishment or subsidiary. A French SAS is a separate legal entity and must file an incorporation dossier for automatic assignment of SIREN, APE/NAF code, and a VAT identification number. For direct cross-border VAT registration without a French establishment, the foreign taxable person must apply to the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) using Form 3555-SD, indicating no French fixed establishment; VAT registration is generally granted within two to four weeks and the VAT ID is issued by the DGFiP. There is no statutory minimum capital requirement for a SAS and non-resident ownership is unrestricted, but the manager (président) or a tax representative may be required for non-EU persons.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is automatically classified as a corporation subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) under Article 206 of the CGI. No general 'check-the-box' regime exists under French domestic law. Available elections include: (i) option for tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under CGI Articles 223 A to 223 W for 95%-owned subgroups; (ii) option for the parental territoriality regime (régime mère-fille) and similar tax treatment; (iii) option for VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 256 C III (permitted since 1 January 2023); and (iv) elections relevant to hybrid mismatch rules under Article 212 bis CGI transposing ATAD, including adjustments where a foreign entity is treated as transparent. French law also permits a small enterprise election for share-ownership plans but does not permit an SAS to pass through its income to shareholders other than in exceptional jointly owned vehicle cases, which are not available to this entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established taxable person, there is no general de minimis registration threshold: any economic activity carried out in France requires VAT registration. For non-established suppliers of digital and e-services to French consumers, the EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual distance-sales threshold applies; above this, OSS (One-Stop Shop) registration is available. For purely French domestic distance sales of goods by EU taxable persons, the threshold is EUR 35,000. Since this entity is a French SAS, it is required to hold a French VAT number irrespective of turnover, and should charge French VAT on B2C supplies of email infrastructure services in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, zero-rating applies under Article 262 CGI when the goods physically leave the European Union and are evidenced by a customs export declaration (DAO/ENS). For services, including email infrastructure and newsletter hosting, French VAT is not charged on supplies of services to a customer established outside the European Union when the services are effectively used and enjoyed outside the EU (Article 259 A and 259 C CGI). For EU B2B supplies, the place of supply is the customer's establishment and VAT is reverse-charged in the customer's Member State. A French SAS may also zero-rate export-related services directly linked to exported goods, but ordinary SaaS services are generally outside the scope of French VAT for non-French customers rather than 'zero-rated'.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / DAU-ENS with MRN)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (or CMR for road transport)
  • Proof of settlement (Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing FX repatriation)

France is progressively mandating electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions: mandatory e-invoicing and structured e-reporting begins 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for SMEs. Invoicing must be transmitted through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the public Portal Public de Facturation (PPF); no continuous transaction-control or real-time fiscal clearance (visa or digital stamping) is currently enforced for ordinary VAT invoices. For export/zero-rated transactions, the classical paper/electronic invoice requirements of Article 289 CGI still apply, and e-reporting of B2B international transactions is required once the measure is in force.

The DGFiP rigorously audits export zero-rating claims and may re-invoice VAT plus interest if the documentary evidence is incomplete or false. Fraudulent or abusive zero-rating exposed to penalties under Articles 1728 and 1729 CGI: late-payment interest (0.20% per month) and an additional penalty of 40% for bad faith or 80% for deliberate tax evasion or artificial arrangements; criminal sanctions under Article 1741 CGI may include a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment of up to five years, with publicity of the conviction. A retrospective clawback period of ten years applies for hidden or fictitious transactions (Article L. 169, Livre des procédures fiscales).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full euro convertibility under Article 63 TFEU. Current-account payments, commercial settlements, and profitremittance transactions are unrestricted and require no Bank of France exchange-control permit. Capital-account transactions, including euro share-capital injections, shareholder loans, and treasury transfers, are generally free for a non-sensitive French SAS active in email/newsletter infrastructure software. A cross-border direct investment in France may, however, require prior authorization under Decree 2019-1590 if it falls within a strategic sector; ISIC 6201 bulk transactional email and newsletter hosting is not a designated sensitive sector. Profit remittance hurdles are tax-oriented, not exchange-control-oriented: a French distributing SAS must prove the distributor decision, the existence of distributable profits under Article L232-11 of the Commercial Code, the beneficial ownership of the shareholder, and the applicable withholding-tax or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief. No central-bank permission is required for dividend transfers to non-residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no forced conversion ratio for export proceeds or cross-border digital-service receivables. The SAS may retain export receivables in foreign currency or convert them at market rates on a voluntary basis. French credit institutions are, however, obligated under the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting framework to classify and transmit cross-border payment information, including a BOP purpose code and the economic nature of the underlying transaction, usually when an individual operation reaches or exceeds the EUR 50,000 regulatory statistical-reporting threshold. The company should retain customer contracts, commercial invoices, SWIFT confirmations, and evidence of service delivery so that the bank can correctly code the flows and defend any audit inquiry.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (central bank of France; General Directorate of Operations and Statistics), in concurrence with the Direction Generale du Tresor on capital-movement and investment screening, and the ACPR (Autorite de Controle Prudentiel et de Resolution) for banking and prudential supervision; monetary policy authority remains with the European Central Bank within the euro area.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for auxiliary collection/disbursement rails, Low-Medium for core treasury concentration. An offshore digital EMI that is outside the EEA cannot lawfully passport payment or e-money services into France; it must be licensed or passported in France/EEA under PSD2 and the French Monetary and Financial Code. Regulated EEA EMIs can be used for customer pay-ins such as Stripe Billing settlement, virtual-account segmentation, and fast international payouts. Legal constraints: EMI balances are safeguarded funds, not bank deposits; they are not covered by the French deposit-guarantee fund, and the EMI cannot provide credit, term deposits, or structured treasury products. For a low-risk SaaS treasury, the prudent board policy is to cap idle balances at an EMI at approximately two times monthly operating expenses and to wire residual liquidity to a Banque de France-supervised credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound euro capital subscriptions must clear via SEPA or SWIFT direct to the SAS IBAN, but for a completed share-capital increase the funds should first be credited to a temporarily blocked bank account held in the name of the SAS or deposited with a French approved financial institution until the legal formalities are completed. The SWIFT MT103 should identify the ordering shareholder, the SAS beneficiary, and in Field 70 a clear legal reference such as /APPT/ Apport en capital, under a French BOP/NC-ECO purpose code identifying equity shares, not ordinary services. The bank will require an authenticated corporate resolution of the SAS approving the capital increase, supporting documentation of the contribution, inbound source-of-funds evidence, UBO confirmation for the shareholder, and any necessary Form 5000/5001 if treaty relief is claimed. For future director or shareholder liquidity injections, the routing should use a separate loan agreement and label the payment as Avance/Compte courant d'associe with arm's length interest terms to prevent recharacterization by the French tax administration. No French exchange-convertibility authorization is needed for a non-sensitive SaaS investment.For trade settlements and treasury outflows, use SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA suppliers, SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for time-critical European disbursements, and SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer for non-EEA suppliers or multi-currency operational payments. Each instruction must include the original invoice number and a precise remittance narrative. A documentary commercial letter of credit is not market standard for pure SaaS or email-infrastructure services; if the company later imports hardware, servers, or network equipment, an irrevocable documentary credit under UCP600 may be opened at a French confirming bank via SWIFT MT700, payable against transport documents and a clean set of invoices. Outbound dividends and director fees should be transmitted by SEPA/SWIFT only after formal corporate decisions and, where applicable, tax withholding or treaty-relief documentation has been presented to the paying French bank.
