Cash contributions may be paid up within five years, provided that at least one-half of each cash contribution is deposited upon subscription/formation, and in-kind contributions must be fully transferred at the moment of subscription.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to industrial infrastructure. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP); online portal: impots.gouv.fr (professional space); SIRENE/INSEE for SIREN/SIRET issuance.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must first register with the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS) via a French commercial court to obtain a SIREN (9-digit legal entity identifier) and SIRET (14-digit establishment identifier). For non-resident entities without a French establishment but liable for VAT, registration is made directly with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) using form Cerfa 11908 (VAT registration) and supporting documents: certificate of incorporation, proof of legal representative identity, and a power of attorney if an agent is used. Upon validation, a French VAT number (FR + 2 check digits + SIREN) is issued. The process is typically completed within 2–4 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities must first register with the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS) via a French commercial court to obtain a SIREN (9-digit legal entity identifier) and SIRET (14-digit establishment identifier). For non-resident entities without a French establishment but liable for VAT, registration is made directly with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) using form Cerfa 11908 (VAT registration) and supporting documents: certificate of incorporation, proof of legal representative identity, and a power of attorney if an agent is used. Upon validation, a French VAT number (FR + 2 check digits + SIREN) is issued. The process is typically completed within 2–4 weeks.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No general check-the-box regime. Specific elections include: (i) option for tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for groups with ≥95% ownership; (ii) option for the parent-subsidiary regime (régime des sociétés mères et filiales) under Articles 145 and 216 CGI allowing 95% dividend exemption; (iii) election to be taxed as a partnership (société de personnes) is not available for SAS; (iv) possible to opt for VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under Article 256 C CGI for closely linked entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for foreign entities making taxable supplies in France. For domestic small businesses, simplified VAT regime thresholds: 85,000 EUR for goods and 37,500 EUR for services under the franchise en base scheme; however, non-resident entities with a fixed establishment or making distance sales above 10,000 EUR to French consumers must register immediately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the EU are zero-rated under Article 262 I CGI. Services supplied to non-EU customers are zero-rated under Article 259 A CGI if the customer is established outside the EU and the service is not related to immovable property in France. For B2B services, the place of supply is the customer's location, so no French VAT applies (reverse charge).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation, DAU) lodged with French customs (Douane)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement proving payment
  • Commercial invoice with VAT number and export mention
  • Proof of exit of goods from EU territory (certified by customs)
  • Contract or purchase order for services
  • Proof of customer's non-EU establishment (e.g., VAT number, certificate of incorporation)

France mandates electronic invoicing (Facturation électronique) progressively: from July 2024, all companies must accept e-invoices; from July 2025, large and mid-sized companies must issue e-invoices; from January 2026, all companies must issue e-invoices. The system requires transmission through a Chorus Pro portal or an approved platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire, PDP) with real-time clearance and digital stamping. Non-compliance penalties: 15 EUR per invoice, capped at 15,000 EUR per calendar year.

French tax authorities (DGFiP) conduct rigorous audits for export zero-rating. Penalties for fraudulent claims include: retrospective assessment of the evaded VAT plus a 40% surcharge for deliberate breach (Article 1729 CGI), 80% in cases of fraudulent intent, and criminal penalties under Article 1741 CGI (up to 5 years imprisonment and 500,000 EUR fine). Additional penalties for failure to provide documentation: 50 EUR per missing document. Clawback period: 3 years (normal) or 10 years for fraudulent activity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and generally applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility under European Union law. There are no general French foreign-exchange controls, capital repatriation restrictions, mandatory resident foreign-currency surrender rules, or routine central-bank approval requirements for inbound equity, shareholder loans, dividends, royalties, service fees, or other lawful cross-border payments. Transactions remain subject to bank customer due diligence, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing rules, sanctions screening, beneficial-owner verification, tax documentation, transfer-pricing support, withholding-tax analysis, and possible reporting obligations. Dividend distributions by a Société par Actions Simplifiée require distributable profits, approved financial statements, applicable statutory-reserve compliance, and corporate approvals. Payments to related non-residents must be commercially supported and arm's-length. French withholding tax, treaty relief, European Union exemptions, and anti-abuse rules must be assessed before remittance. Banks may delay or reject transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions, opaque ownership, unexplained source of funds, unusual payment chains, or insufficient commercial evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a French bank, convert export receipts into euros, or convert them within a prescribed period. Export proceeds may generally be retained in euro or foreign-currency accounts, including accounts outside France, subject to accurate accounting, tax reporting, customs and trade documentation, sanctions compliance, and bank-specific transaction monitoring. Payments received through SEPA, SWIFT, card-acquiring, or other regulated channels must be reconcilable to invoices and contracts. Cash transactions and cross-border cash movements remain subject to European Union and French declaration and anti-money-laundering rules. Export-control, customs, VAT, and Intrastat obligations may apply independently of foreign-exchange rules.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework. Foreign-exchange and payment-services supervision is supplemented by the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution), the French Treasury (Direction générale du Trésor), and customs authorities where trade reporting is relevant.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for payment collection, SEPA payment initiation, direct-debit processing, card acquiring, and treasury sub-accounts, provided the provider is authorized under the Payment Services Directive or Electronic Money Directive and is passported or otherwise lawfully operating in France. An offshore digital EMI without French or European Economic Area authorization, passporting, or an authorized local partner cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to the French SAS. An EMI is not a credit institution and generally cannot accept repayable deposits or provide a conventional bank overdraft. Customer funds must be safeguarded rather than treated as bank deposits. The company should verify authorization in the European Banking Authority or relevant national register, safeguarding structure, SEPA reachability, French customer-service capability, sanctions controls, data-processing arrangements, insolvency protection, account-closure rights, and access to cash-management APIs. A regulated EMI account should not be the sole account for payroll, tax, capital funding, or critical liquidity.

The SAS should avoid leveraged or opaque products that are disproportionate to its operating exposures.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium to High for payment collection, SEPA payment initiation, direct-debit processing, card acquiring, and treasury sub-accounts, provided the provider is authorized under the Payment Services Directive or Electronic Money Directive and is passported or otherwise lawfully operating in France. An offshore digital EMI without French or European Economic Area authorization, passporting, or an authorized local partner cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to the French SAS. An EMI is not a credit institution and generally cannot accept repayable deposits or provide a conventional bank overdraft. Customer funds must be safeguarded rather than treated as bank deposits. The company should verify authorization in the European Banking Authority or relevant national register, safeguarding structure, SEPA reachability, French customer-service capability, sanctions controls, data-processing arrangements, insolvency protection, account-closure rights, and access to cash-management APIs. A regulated EMI account should not be the sole account for payroll, tax, capital funding, or critical liquidity.
