Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For an SAS, the law imposes no minimum capital; the capital is fixed in the articles. If cash is subscribed, the full subscribed amount (or the shareholder-defined instalments expressly accepted in the articles) must be deposited into a French bank blocked account or with Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations before filing. The blocked sum is released to the President immediately after issuance of the Kbis. Non-cash contributions are valued by a commissaire aux apports unless exempted, and are paid in full upon contribution.

Funding & unlock path: Before operations, obtain the fire safety attestation from the local sécurité civile/SDIS or the competent commission de sécurité where the premises qualify as ERP, a structural load survey/attestation certifying the load capacity of any storage areas, an electrical installation verification certificate, and an occupational health and safety clearance from DREETS under the French Labour Code. The site must be registered with the relevant authorities and a workplace health and safety file established before storage or distribution of APIs begins.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), including the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères for non-established businesses. Official portals: https://www.impots.gouv.fr and https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Customs and EORI matters are administered by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI) through https://www.douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is incorporated through the one-stop business formalities portal operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). The registration produces a SIREN identifier for the legal entity, a SIRET identifier for each establishment, and a Kbis extract. The DGFiP then assigns or confirms the French VAT identification number, generally formatted as FR followed by two check digits and the nine-digit SIREN. The company must register with the competent SIE, provide its articles of association, Kbis, registered-office evidence, beneficial-owner information, business activity description, bank details, expected turnover, intra-Community transaction profile, and evidence supporting the pharmaceutical wholesale activity. A foreign entity carrying on taxable transactions in France must apply to the competent French tax office or the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères for a French VAT number. An entity established in the European Union generally may register directly without appointing a French fiscal representative. A non-EU entity generally must appoint an accredited French fiscal representative unless an applicable legal or administrative exception applies. Importers and customs representatives should obtain an EORI number from the DGDDI before importing or exporting APIs. VAT registration may be required even where the domestic turnover franchise threshold is not exceeded, including for imports, intra-Community acquisitions, and transactions for which the business elects or is required to charge French VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

An SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). It may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime if statutory conditions are satisfied, including generally that the company is newly formed, unlisted, carries on an eligible commercial, industrial, artisanal, agricultural, or professional activity, remains below the applicable employee and turnover or balance-sheet limits, and obtains the required shareholder approval. The election is limited in duration and cannot normally be renewed after its statutory period. An SAS does not have a general U.S.-style check-the-box election. France has no general elective hybrid-mismatch classification regime; anti-hybrid rules under the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance framework may deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences where statutory conditions are met. VAT grouping, tax consolidation, and other group regimes are separate elections subject to their own eligibility requirements and do not change the SAS's basic legal-entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For businesses eligible for the French domestic VAT franchise, the ordinary annual turnover threshold is generally EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and related activities, including wholesale distribution, with a higher tolerance threshold generally of EUR 93,500. The corresponding service thresholds are generally EUR 37,500 and EUR 41,250. Thresholds and sector-specific rules must be checked for the relevant tax year. These thresholds do not eliminate registration requirements arising from imports, intra-Community acquisitions, distance sales, reverse-charge obligations, voluntary taxation, or other taxable transactions. A French SAS conducting pharmaceutical API wholesale activity should normally obtain a VAT number at commencement where it imports, exports, or trades intra-Community, irrespective of domestic turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to a destination outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT with the right to deduct input VAT where the supplier is established or VAT-registered as required, the goods are actually dispatched or transported outside the EU, the supplier is identified as exporter or otherwise satisfies the applicable export conditions, and the export is supported by customs evidence showing exit from the EU. The export exemption is not available merely because an invoice states that the transaction is an export. For intra-Community supplies to a VAT-identified customer in another EU Member State, the separate intra-Community supply exemption applies only if the statutory conditions are met, including valid customer VAT identification, transport to another Member State, and required reporting. Services supplied to a business customer established outside France are generally outside the scope of French VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule rather than technically zero-rated; the supplier must retain evidence of the customer's business status and establishment. Services connected with goods, transport, customs, and regulated pharmaceutical activities require transaction-specific place-of-supply analysis.

  • Electronic export customs declaration containing the consignor, consignee, commodity classification, customs value, origin, procedure, and movement reference number
  • MRN and electronic exit confirmation or equivalent customs evidence, including the Export Accompanying Document and IE-599 or successor exit message where applicable
  • Commercial invoice identifying the goods, quantities, prices, Incoterms, VAT treatment, exporter, and consignee
  • Packing list and transport document, such as bill of lading, air waybill, rail consignment note, or road consignment note
  • Proof of transport and delivery outside the European Union, including carrier or freight-forwarder evidence
  • Import customs evidence or destination-country customs records where necessary to corroborate exit and delivery
  • Valid EORI information and, where applicable, customs power of attorney and export authorization records
  • For regulated pharmaceutical APIs, applicable product, export, quality, controlled-substance, and customs licensing records
  • Banking and payment records supporting the commercial transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a standard French VAT export document

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting rather than a current universal real-time clearance model. All French businesses must be capable of receiving compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Large and medium-sized businesses must issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises generally must issue them from 1 September 2027. Electronic invoices must use an approved structured or hybrid format and be transmitted through an approved private electronic invoicing platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire, PDP), subject to the final operating rules. B2C and cross-border transaction data will generally be reported electronically under the e-reporting framework. France does not currently require a universal pre-clearance stamp or real-time government validation for every invoice. Businesses must retain invoices and supporting VAT records for the statutory retention period and ensure reconciliation between invoices, customs declarations, VAT returns, Intrastat declarations, and accounting records.

France has no general foreign-exchange control requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. The exporter must nevertheless retain reliable commercial, customs, transport, payment, and accounting evidence and comply with applicable anti-money-laundering, sanctions, customs, and pharmaceutical controls. If export exemption is incorrectly claimed, the DGFiP may assess the unpaid VAT retrospectively, together with late-payment interest and tax penalties. Deliberate or materially negligent inaccuracies may attract penalties commonly ranging from 40% for deliberate failure to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, subject to the applicable facts and statutory limits. A VAT credit or refund obtained without entitlement may be clawed back with interest and penalties. False customs declarations may trigger customs duties, import VAT, customs fines, seizure, and other sanctions. Serious intentional tax fraud may additionally result in criminal prosecution, including imprisonment and substantial fines; aggravated tax-fraud cases may carry significantly higher penalties. Export documentation must therefore be complete, internally consistent, and capable of being matched to the customs exit record, invoice, logistics chain, VAT return, and accounting ledger.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible across current and capital account operations. France imposes no exchange controls on trade or financial flows, subject to the EU free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU) and the OECD Code of Liberalisation. Profit remittance to foreign parents is unrestricted, but dividends to non-EU shareholders incur a 25% withholding tax (12.8% plus social levies), reduced under applicable tax treaties; qualifying EU parents benefit from the Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU) at 0% withholding. Capital inflows require no prior authorization. Any cross-border transfer exceeding EUR 7,500 must be reported to the Banque de France via the intermediary bank for balance-of-payments statistics.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio apply in France. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained, converted, or used for payments at the SAS's discretion. The sole statutory obligation is the Banque de France declaration regime (Articles R152-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code): each transfer between France and a non-resident exceeding EUR 7,500 must be declared through the banking intermediary. For operational efficiency, the entity should invoice in EUR or USD and convert via treasury hedging rails rather than rely on forced conversion timelines.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as national competent authority for foreign-exchange data collection and balance-of-payments reporting under Articles L141-6 and R141-7 of the French Monetary and Financial Code, acting within the euro-area framework of the European Central Bank; the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) administers exchange-control and foreign-investment screening.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury function. French or EEA-authorised e-money institutions and payment institutions cannot issue documentary letters of credit or provide the credit facilities required for pharmaceutical API trade flows, and French tier-1 banks treat EMI accounts as non-qualifying for capital and creditor-protection purposes. Non-EU digital EMIs (notably post-Brexit UK e-money firms) may not operate in France without an EEA passport or local agency; even where passporting exists, the high-risk pharma classification confines EMIs to ancillary collections and payables automation. All principal liquidity and trade-finance rails must remain with tier-1 credit institutions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for primary treasury function. French or EEA-authorised e-money institutions and payment institutions cannot issue documentary letters of credit or provide the credit facilities required for pharmaceutical API trade flows, and French tier-1 banks treat EMI accounts as non-qualifying for capital and creditor-protection purposes. Non-EU digital EMIs (notably post-Brexit UK e-money firms) may not operate in France without an EEA passport or local agency; even where passporting exists, the high-risk pharma classification confines EMIs to ancillary collections and payables automation. All principal liquidity and trade-finance rails must remain with tier-1 credit institutions.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard French corporate income tax rate; 15% reduced rate applies to the first EUR 38,120 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs with annual turnover below EUR 10 million, and a 3.3% social surcharge applies on additional CIT payable by groups with turnover above EUR 7.63 million and tax liability exceeding EUR 763,000).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic dividend withholding tax under Article 119 bis of the French General Tax Code (CGI) for dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders; reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the shareholder holds at least 10% of the capital for 2 years; treaty rates may reduce the rate to 10%, 5%, or 0%, depending on the residence treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million in annual turnover or total assets (or control by an entity satisfying this threshold) triggers the mandatory French Master File, Local File, annual transfer-pricing declaration (Form 2257-SD), and notification of transfer-pricing documentation (Form 2258-SD); upon a written tax-administration request, the documentation must be supplied within 30 days.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute of the UAE type; however, economic substance is decisive under Article 57 CGI transfer-pricing recharacterisation, Article 209 B deemed abandonment of profit, the abuse-of-law doctrine under Articles L64/L64A of the Book of Tax Procedures, and the OECD Principal Purpose Test in DTAs. A French API wholesale SAS must maintain real premises, qualified personnel, inventory storage, logistical decision-making, and supporting contracts to preserve DTA and EU directive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Fraudulent Filing / Concealment of Profit

Statutory sanction: Administrative surcharge up to 80% of the tax under-assessed (40% in cases of deliberate misstatement, 10% for simple late filing), plus monthly late-payment interest at 0.20%; criminal conviction for tax fraud can lead to imprisonment up to 5 years and criminal fines determined under Article 1741 CGI.

Director personal liability: Directors may be declared jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt and penalties under Article 1743 CGI; travel bans, management disqualification, freezing of personal assets, and imprisonment are possible upon criminal conviction.
Shell Company / Abuse of Law (Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: The French tax administration may disregard the arrangement as an abuse of law, re-attribute income to the true economic operator, impose the 80% abuse-of-law penalty on the reassessed tax, and deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly signed the sham transactions may be subject to the personal recovery of taxes and penalties as well as criminal prosecution for participating in the fraudulent scheme; civil courts may pierce the veil and order restitution.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Intra-Group Margin Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer; the tax administration may reconstruct the arm's-length margin and re-assess CIT; failure to respond to the documentation request within 30 days exposes the company to an automatic fine under Article 1740 A of the CGI and, in aggravated cases, the 40% or 80% surcharge with late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false transfer-pricing certificates or concealing documentation risk management disqualification and joint civil liability; egregious cases are prosecuted together with tax fraud, which may result in travel bans and imprisonment.
Anti-Money Laundering and Abusive Financial Routing (AML/CFT)

Statutory sanction: Violations of the French Monetary and Financial Code may expose the company to fines up to EUR 1 million and imprisonment up to 5 years for named officers; the competent authority may order freezing of bank accounts, confiscation of the fraudulent proceeds, and liquidation proceedings; customs penalties can be added for false import declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to criminal imprisonment, personal fines, travel bans, and civil damages; French courts may impose asset-freezing orders and enter judgments directly against personal holdings where the corporate structure is used as a vehicle for money laundering.
Unlicensed or Non-Compliant Pharmaceutical Wholesale Operations

Statutory sanction: ANSM may suspend or withdraw the wholesale authorisation, order immediate closure of the storage site, and impose administrative fines; criminal penalties include imprisonment and fines for endangering public health; tax deductions relating to prohibited activities are disallowed.

Director personal liability: Directors of an unlawful pharmaceutical wholesale operation may be barred from commercial activity, held personally liable for public-health damage, and subject to criminal penalties including imprisonment; the corporate veil is pierced where distribution was carried on without the statutory licence.
