UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier), including Articles L.561-2 et seq. and R.561-1 et seq., governs AML/CFT obligations and beneficial-owner identification by covered entities. Commercial Code Articles L.561-46 and R.561-55 et seq. govern beneficial-owner declarations for companies registered in France. A SAS must identify and declare each natural person who ultimately owns, directly or indirectly, more than 25% of its share capital or voting rights, or otherwise exercises control; where no such person can be identified, the legally designated senior managing person is reported. The filing is made through the company formalities system for entry in the RNE and relevant register records. Access to beneficial-owner information is restricted under the post-CJEU framework; competent authorities and covered obliged entities have statutory access, while other access depends on applicable French rules and a demonstrated legitimate interest.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), with registration completed by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce / Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) of the registered office.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.227-1 to L.227-30 (Société par Actions Simplifiée), Article L.210-2 (lawful corporate purpose), Article L.210-6 (legal personality from registration), Article L.227-9 (freedom of organization), Articles R.227-1 et seq., and Articles R.123-30 to R.123-39 (registration in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés).

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de constitution de société (electronic formalities dossier for an SAS), supported by the signed statuts and statutory annexes; registration is evidenced by the official Extrait Kbis.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SAS has no statutory minimum share capital; the capital is freely fixed in the statuts and must be fully subscribed. The statuts set the liberation terms, but the Greffe requires proof of the actual availability of the subscribed cash capital before registration; funds are deposited in a blocked account at a French credit institution or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and released only upon RCS registration and issuance of the Kbis. In-kind contributions must be fully paid in at incorporation and require a valuation by a Commissaire aux apports unless the shareholders validly waive the report.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty. Principal digital portals are impots.gouv.fr for tax accounts, filings, payments and VAT administration, and formalities.entreprises.gouv.fr through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) for business registration. The relevant tax office for a French SAS is generally the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) assigned to its registered office; non-established businesses are generally administered by the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS must complete incorporation formalities through the INPI Guichet unique. Following registration in the Registre national des entreprises, the entity receives a SIREN number for the legal entity and a SIRET number for each establishment. The entity then activates its professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr and identifies its competent SIE. If the SAS conducts taxable transactions, it applies for or confirms a French VAT identification number through the competent SIE. The French VAT number generally has the format FR followed by two check characters and the nine-digit SIREN number. A non-established foreign entity with French VAT obligations must register with the SIEE, provide corporate documents, proof of business activity, identification of legal representatives, banking and address information, and appoint a French tax representative where required. EU-established businesses are generally not required to appoint a French tax representative; non-EU businesses generally must appoint an accredited French tax representative unless an applicable exemption or mutual-assistance arrangement applies. An EORI number must be obtained from French customs for customs operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A qualifying SAS may elect temporarily for taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu) if statutory conditions are satisfied, including being newly created for no more than five years at the time of election, carrying on an eligible commercial, industrial, craft or professional activity, having fewer than 50 employees, meeting the applicable turnover or balance-sheet limits, and not being listed. The election generally requires shareholder approval and is limited to five financial years; it is not freely revocable during that period. There is no US-style federal check-the-box election. French and EU anti-hybrid-mismatch rules under the French implementation of ATAD restrict deductions, impose inclusion outcomes or deny benefits for arrangements involving hybrid entities, hybrid instruments, imported mismatches or reverse hybrids. These rules do not create a general entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A French SAS carrying on taxable economic activity is generally required to account for VAT unless it qualifies for the franchise en base de TVA or another exemption. For domestic services, the principal franchise threshold is EUR 37,500 of annual turnover, with a tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250 under the applicable current rules. For trading and other goods activities, the principal threshold is EUR 85,000, with a tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500. Thresholds and the operation of the franchise regime are subject to statutory updates. The franchise does not generally eliminate VAT obligations for intra-EU acquisitions, imports, reverse-charge transactions, or certain cross-border services. A French SAS in an AI subtitle, transcription, dubbing or voice-cloning business should obtain a VAT number where its transactions are taxable, where the franchise is unavailable, or where intra-EU and international reporting obligations require identification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exports of goods from France to a destination outside the European Union, the transaction is generally exempt from French VAT with the right to deduct input VAT under Article 262 I of the French Tax Code, provided the supplier or an authorized intermediary establishes that the goods physically leave the EU and that the exporter satisfies the documentary and procedural requirements. The principal evidence is a validated electronic customs export declaration and an exit result or equivalent customs proof linked to the export movement. For services, France generally applies place-of-supply rules rather than a universal export zero-rating rule. B2B services supplied to a business established outside France are generally located where the customer is established and therefore are outside the scope of French VAT, subject to customer-status and anti-abuse evidence. Certain services connected with goods, transport, rights, events or immovable property have specific place-of-supply rules. AI transcription, subtitle production, dubbing and voice-cloning services require analysis of the customer status, contractual deliverables, exploitation rights, licensing elements and actual place of use; invoices must state the applicable exemption or reverse-charge basis and retain sufficient customer-location evidence. Input VAT refunds require valid VAT invoices, a direct link to taxable or qualifying export activities, proper accounting and timely VAT return or refund claims.

  • Electronic customs export declaration containing the applicable customs procedure and exporter data
  • Export Movement Reference Number (MRN), customs release data and electronic exit confirmation or equivalent proof of exit from the European Union
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms and destination
  • Packing list and transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, road consignment note or courier proof, where applicable
  • Evidence of delivery or receipt outside the European Union where customs exit evidence alone is insufficient
  • EORI number and any required export authorization, license, origin document or special customs certificate
  • Accounting records linking the export invoice, customs declaration, transport movement and VAT return
  • For services treated as outside the scope of French VAT: customer business identification, contractual documentation, proof of customer establishment outside France, service-delivery records and payment evidence
  • Bank or payment evidence is supporting substantiation and is not, by itself, a substitute for customs exit evidence for exported goods

France does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system. Electronic invoicing is being introduced progressively: all businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; large and intermediate businesses must issue compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; small and medium-sized businesses and micro-enterprises must generally issue them from 1 September 2027. Electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions subject to French VAT will use an approved private platform or the public invoicing platform designated under the reform. Businesses will also submit e-reporting data for relevant B2C and international transactions, including certain payment data. Mandatory invoice content includes supplier and customer identification, valid VAT numbers where applicable, invoice date and number, description, taxable amount, VAT rate and amount, exemption or reverse-charge wording, and required payment information. France does not generally require a digital tax stamp or pre-clearance for ordinary VAT invoices, although customs, payment-service, platform and sector-specific data requirements may apply.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. French foreign-exchange controls generally do not impose a universal mandatory repatriation period, although banking, anti-money-laundering, accounting, sanctions and customs controls may require transaction evidence. Export VAT exemption or input VAT recovery may be denied or clawed back if physical export, customer status, place of supply, customs exit, contractual performance or accounting linkage is not substantiated. Reassessed VAT is generally accompanied by late-payment interest, currently calculated under the applicable statutory monthly rate, and administrative penalties may include a 10% surcharge for late payment, 40% for deliberate failure, 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law, and up to 100% for opposition to audit. False documents, concealed transactions or organized VAT fraud may also trigger criminal prosecution, including imprisonment and substantial fines under the French Tax Code. The taxpayer must preserve invoices, customs records, transport evidence, contracts, customer-status evidence, payment records and VAT returns for the applicable statutory retention and audit periods.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France has full capital and current account convertibility under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The euro is fully convertible; no foreign-exchange licensing or central bank approval is required for genuine commercial transactions or profit remittance. Profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders is generally unrestricted, provided the Societe par Actions Simplifiee (SAS) has satisfied its corporate tax filing obligations, any applicable withholding tax has been settled, and the cross-border capital flow is reported for statistical purposes. However, transactions exceeding EUR 500,000 or equivalent must be declared to Banque de France under the balance-of-payments reporting regime. Additionally, dealings with jurisdictions listed as non-cooperative by the Financial Action Task Force may attract enhanced review but do not constitute a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds is imposed on French resident exporters. Exporters may retain receipts in foreign currency or convert them into euros at their discretion. For balance-of-payments compliance, all export transactions (and related financial settlements) exceeding EUR 500,000 per transaction or EUR 15,000,000 cumulative per company per year, as applicable under Banque de France thresholds, must be reported through the monthly or annual declaration process. There is no statutory conversion ratio or timeline for conversion of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the European Central Bank Single Supervisory Mechanism for banking supervision and domestically enforcing the French Monetary and Financial Code, including foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments reporting requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility for operational payment processing, but low feasibility as a replacement for a tier-1 banking relationship. Wise and Stripe each operate in France through ACPR-regulated payment institutions or as agents of licensed payment institutions, or hold an EU passport via another EEA regulator. These digital EMIs are legally permitted to hold client funds, execute SEPA and SWIFT payments, issue corporate cards, and manage payouts. However, they cannot lawfully provide credit products, cannot host capital-escrow accounts for statutory purposes, and are generally insufficient for complex treasury operations, such as FX hedging or large balance-of-payments reporting. Offshore non-EEA EMIs without a French or EU passport are not permitted to provide payment accounts to French entities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital or shareholder loans must be routed through the French corporate bank account using either SEPA Credit Transfer (for EU/EEA shareholders) or SWIFT MT103 (for non-EU shareholders). The wire must carry an unambiguous purpose code, such as EQUI (equity capital) or LOAN (shareholder loan), together with the SAS legal name and SIREN number. For amounts exceeding EUR 500,000 or equivalent, the company's legal representative must file the cross-border balance-of-payments declaration with Banque de France via the bank or the dedicated online portal. To satisfy bank verification, the company must provide the notarized deed of incorporation, minutes of the shareholders' meeting approving the capital increase, a signed 'compte courant d'associe' agreement for shareholder loans, and proof of identity of the capital provider. No prior administrative authorization is required for euro-zone capital movements.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements should use SEPA Credit Transfer (XML pain.001) for EU/EEA beneficiaries and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA beneficiaries, with clear invoice and contract references in the remittance information field. For large or milestone-based payments to non-EU post-production houses, a Commercial Letter of Credit issued under ICC Uniform Customs and Practice (UCP 600) through a tier-1 French bank is permissible, especially where the beneficiary requires documentary assurance against presentation of proof of delivery, invoices, and inspection certificates. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for operational payment processing, but low feasibility as a replacement for a tier-1 banking relationship. Wise and Stripe each operate in France through ACPR-regulated payment institutions or as agents of licensed payment institutions, or hold an EU passport via another EEA regulator. These digital EMIs are legally permitted to hold client funds, execute SEPA and SWIFT payments, issue corporate cards, and manage payouts. However, they cannot lawfully provide credit products, cannot host capital-escrow accounts for statutory purposes, and are generally insufficient for complex treasury operations, such as FX hedging or large balance-of-payments reporting. Offshore non-EEA EMIs without a French or EU passport are not permitted to provide payment accounts to French entities.
