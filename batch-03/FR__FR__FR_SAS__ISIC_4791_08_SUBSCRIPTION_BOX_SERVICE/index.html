Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); official portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; corporate registration via the Guichet Unique (procedure.gouv.fr) administered in coordination with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce

E-government filing portal

A non-resident founder forms the SAS by drafting and filing articles of incorporation through the Guichet Unique, obtaining a SIREN/SIRET from INSEE; the competent SIE then allocates the corporate tax account reference (numéro de gestion) and issues the intra-Community VAT identification number (TVA intracommunautaire) following the Article 286-ter CGI registration filing. A foreign entity not incorporated in France but carrying on taxable French activities must apply for French VAT registration using the simplified non-resident application (form n°3559 / MO/CA) with the Non-Resident SIE of Joinville-le-Pont; a fiscal representative under Article 289-A CGI is mandatory for non-EU tax-resident operators unless a European scheme exception applies. Newly incorporated SAS entities must also transmit a tax-status attestation to the DGFiP within 15 days of registration per Article 302 septies-A CGI.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-resident founder forms the SAS by drafting and filing articles of incorporation through the Guichet Unique, obtaining a SIREN/SIRET from INSEE; the competent SIE then allocates the corporate tax account reference (numéro de gestion) and issues the intra-Community VAT identification number (TVA intracommunautaire) following the Article 286-ter CGI registration filing. A foreign entity not incorporated in France but carrying on taxable French activities must apply for French VAT registration using the simplified non-resident application (form n°3559 / MO/CA) with the Non-Resident SIE of Joinville-le-Pont; a fiscal representative under Article 289-A CGI is mandatory for non-EU tax-resident operators unless a European scheme exception applies. Newly incorporated SAS entities must also transmit a tax-status attestation to the DGFiP within 15 days of registration per Article 302 septies-A CGI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not maintain a general check-the-box entity classification regime; an SAS is mandatorily taxed at entity level under Impôt sur les Sociétés. Available statutory elections include: (i) fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 223-A CGI, enabling an SAS group head holding at least 95% of French subsidiaries to consolidate their results into a single corporate income tax return; (ii) election for centralised group VAT registration and filing; (iii) option to apply the monthly normal VAT settlement regime in lieu of the automatic simplified regime; (iv) hybrid mismatch provisions (Articles 212-II and 212-IX CGI, implementing the ATAD) provide no elective classification but impose a documentary justification obligation to avoid deduction disallowance where a payment has not been taxed or deducted in the counterparty jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a taxable person established in France, VAT liability arises on the first taxable transaction, subject to the franchise en base exemption at an annual goods turnover below €85,800 (Article 293-B CGI; threshold indexed by decree; services threshold: €37,500). Above these ceilings, VAT registration is mandatory. For intra-EU distance sales of subscription boxes to consumers in other Member States, registration is due in the destination State once the annual cross-border turnover exceeds €10,000, unless the operator opts to register the OSS; a non-established foreign seller has no de minimis threshold and must register from the first taxable supply located in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of subscription-box goods to non-EU destinations qualifies for the 0% French VAT rate under Article 262-I-1° CGI, provided the goods physically leave the EU customs territory and the exporter or its customs agent holds a valid export declaration (DAU/MRN). Deliveries to EU B2B customers are zero-rated under Article 258-A CGI only when the customer's EU VAT number is validated and transport to the destination Member State is evidenced. Exports to the French overseas territories and départements d'outre-mer, which lie outside the EU VAT customs territory, may also be exempted on the same evidence basis. The burden of proof rests entirely on the subscription-box operator, and the DGFiP together with the Customs Authority (DGDDI) require a complete documentary chain.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Document Administratif Unique / DAU) bearing the MRN
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing international carriage outside the EU
  • Transport contract and carrier booking confirmation showing dispatch from France
  • Proof of third-country customs entry or verified offloading at destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing settlement of the export proceeds

France mandates a phasing-in electronic invoicing regime: from 1 September 2026 all taxable persons must be able to receive structured e-invoices, large and mid-size enterprises must issue via a certified Dématérialisation Partner (PDP) or the public Invoice Platform (PPF), and from 1 September 2027 all small businesses must also issue e-invoices. There is no per-invoice digital stamping or real-time fiscal clearance; instead, the requirement is structured transmission of invoice lifecycle events and monthly e-reporting of B2C and international transaction data through secured APIs to the PPF. Failure to transmit exposes the supplier to fines of up to €15,000 per obligation under Articles 173 and 1737 CGI, plus loss of input VAT deduction where the invoice does not satisfy Articles 289 requirements.

Export zero-rating claims are audited through the French statutory verification procedure (vérification de comptabilité) with a standard three-year reassessment period (Articles L. 169 and L. 176 LPF), extended to six years in cases of concealed activity. If zero-rating is disallowed, the DGFiP reassesses French VAT at 20% plus monthly late interest of 0.20% (Article 1727 CGI) and penalty of 10% for late declaration, 40% for deliberate under-declaration (Article 1729-a), and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres (Article 1729-b); defective invoice documentation can additionally trigger fines of up to €15,000 per document under Article 1737 CGI and loss of input VAT deduction. Where customs fraud is established, Article 414 of the French Customs Code provides fines of one to three times the evaded VAT and imprisonment of up to three years, and the clawback may be reinforced by a supplementary assessment period of up to ten years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies the EU free movement of capital under Article 63 TFEU; the euro is fully convertible for current and capital transactions. No prior exchange-control authorization is required for ordinary cross-border payments in and out of France, except for measures imposed by EU sanctions, counter-terrorist financing, and anti-money-laundering legislation. Current account convertibility is unfettered: trade, service, royalties, and payment-card settlement flows can circulate freely. Capital account convertibility is likewise unrestricted, but a French resident company must report material cross-border capital, loan, and foreign-exchange transactions to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics. Profit remittance hurdles for a French SAS are procedural rather than FX-related: distributable profits must satisfy legal-reserve and dividend-payment requirements under the French Commercial Code; a dividend paid to a non-resident shareholder may attract French withholding tax unless reduced by an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive; and the paying bank may require the shareholder's tax-residence certificate or an IFU form before releasing the remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose mandatory surrender or mandatory conversion of foreign-currency export proceeds into euro. A French SAS may retain export receipts in a foreign-currency account, or 'compte en devises', at a French or EEA credit institution. There is no statutory 30-day surrender timeline and no minimum conversion ratio. French exchange control only requires commercial documentation and Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting when cross-border transactions reach applicable reporting thresholds. The company should ensure that export invoices, customs declarations, and bank settlement records are complete and consistent, and that the account is designated as foreign-currency-denominated if such receipts are expected.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR). The foreign-exchange administration function is exercised by the Direction Générale du Trésor at the French Ministry of the Economy, Finance and Sovereign, Industry and Digital Sovereignty, subject to European Union treaty obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Overall feasibility for a French SAS: Medium. An ACPR-regulated electronic-money institution or payment institution such as Qonto or Finom can be useful for day-to-day EUR collections, Stripe payout reception, and SEPA payments. However, such EMIs are not full credit institutions and cannot provide capital-escrow services, commercial credit, documentary letters of credit, margin-based FX derivatives, or full cross-border treasury clearing. Offshore EMIs domiciled outside France/EEA are generally not viable as primary treasury or escrow accounts because they lack the PSD2 'single passport' and are not recognized as eligible receiving institutions by French banks for capital-deposit certificates. An EU-licensed EMI with a valid passport may hold segregated client funds, but it cannot be a substitute for a supervised French credit institution when the SAS requires trade finance, FX hedging, or correspondent-banking liquidity infrastructure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions into the French SAS should be routed via SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR from an EEA shareholder, or via SWIFT MT103 in non-EUR from a non-EEA shareholder. The payment instruction must identify the beneficiary by legal name, French IBAN, SIREN/RCS registration number, and must state the purpose as 'Souscription au capital social / augmentation de capital' rather than a generic remittance. The bank-specific SWIFT/OECD balance-of-payments purpose code for equity capital contribution must be used, together with the subscription deed and, where funds are deposited before RCS registration, a bank certificate of deposit (attestation de dépôt des fonds). The supporting verification file must include the shareholders' meeting minutes or sole-shareholder decision, printed subscription form, proof of bank funds deposit, updated bylaws, and the RCS/Kbis extract showing the registered capital increase.For recurring subscription-box merchandise imports, the preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for direct post-shipment cross-border supplier payments; for EUR suppliers inside SEPA, a standard SEPA Credit Transfer is sufficient. For higher-value bulk orders with open-account risk, the French SAS may use an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (commercial LC) opened by its French bank and transmitted by MT700/MT707, with the seller presenting compliant transport and invoice documents. Documentary collections with drafts on an approval basis are also available. All outbound payments must reference the supplier invoice number, purchase order, Incoterms 2020 rule, customs/EORI numbers, and the relevant commodity description for ISIC 4791 subscription-box merchandise. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal penalties including fines up to 80% of evaded tax, imprisonment up to 5 years, and repayment of evaded taxes with interest

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability, fines, and disqualification from corporate offices
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as disregarded entity, back taxation, penalties up to 80% of additional tax

Director personal liability: Personal liability for fraudulent management, fines, and possible imprisonment
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, penalties of 80% of additional tax due, and interest charges

Director personal liability: Personal liability if directors knowingly approved non-compliant arrangements
AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1 million or 5% of annual turnover, criminal penalties, asset freezes

Director personal liability: Personal fines and imprisonment for willful non-compliance
