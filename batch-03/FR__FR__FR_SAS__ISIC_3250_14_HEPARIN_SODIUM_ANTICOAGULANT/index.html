Name reservation / lock period

In France there is no administrative pre-reservation of a corporate name. If the RCS registrar (Greffe) rejects the filing due to the name, the burden falls on the applicant to publish an amendment. The applicant may appeal the registrar's refusal before the judge charged with supervision of the RCS (juge chargé du suivi du registre du commerce et des sociétés) within 10 days of notification. Alternatively, the applicant may refile the M0 form with an amended name. Conflicts with prior rights may be challenged before the civil courts under trademark law; no administrative extension of validity is available for a non-reserved name.

Competent corporate registry

The National Register of Enterprises is administered through the INPI electronic one-stop shop (Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises); the relevant Trade and Companies Register is maintained by the competent commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce).

Governing statute

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash may be deposited into a blocked account with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the deposit certificate supports registration and funds are released after presentation of the Kbis extract. In-kind contributions must be fully contributed and may require a commissaire aux apports report, subject to statutory exemptions.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain required building and premises approvals, SDIS fire-safety review or opinion, emergency-access and evacuation compliance, fire detection and suppression certification, hazardous-material storage controls, and electrical and pressure-equipment inspections. Complete the French occupational-risk assessment document, chemical-exposure controls, machine and ventilation conformity, employee training, occupational-health arrangements, and workplace-opening notifications or consultations required for the site.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the online portal impots.gouv.fr; business registration is administered through the Répertoire National des Entreprises (RNE) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For an SAS incorporated in France, statutory identifiers (SIREN, SIRET, SIRENE registration) are obtained automatically at incorporation via the formalities portal. Corporate income tax registration is automatic with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). A separate French VAT identification number (n° d'identification TVA intracommunautaire) is issued by DGFiP after notification of the SIREN; a distinct EORI number is required for customs purposes only if the entity undertakes customs operations. Foreign entities carrying on taxable supplies in France without a permanent establishment must register directly with DGFiP under the non-resident simplified VAT registration procedure.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: The SAS is a capital company (société de capitaux) and is irrebuttably subject to French corporate income tax (IS) at the entity level; no pass-through treatment is available. Territorial scope applies to foreign permanent establishments unless a worldwide group election is made. — For an SAS incorporated in France, statutory identifiers (SIREN, SIRET, SIRENE registration) are obtained automatically at incorporation via the formalities portal. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The SAS is a capital company (société de capitaux) and is irrebuttably subject to French corporate income tax (IS) at the entity level; no pass-through treatment is available. Territorial scope applies to foreign permanent establishments unless a worldwide group election is made.

Available election forms

France does not offer a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Available elections include: (i) option for tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) for a 95%+ French parent; (ii) election for the régime des sociétés mères (parent-subsidiary dividend exemption); (iii) election for VAT grouping (assujettis unis); (iv) election to waive VAT exemption on qualifying leased property. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD 1 and 2 apply per CGI Article 212 bis and impose entity characterization based on foreign law, but no separate hybrid election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for established businesses: any French SAS supplying goods or services must be VAT-registered from inception. For non-established EU suppliers, an annual distance-selling threshold of €35,000 applies to goods sold to French consumers; for non-EU suppliers, no threshold applies once a taxable supply occurs (importation is always taxable).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to a destination outside the EU are zero-rated for French VAT under CGI Article 262-I. The supplier must prove that the goods physically left EU territory within 90 days of dispatch. Services directly related to exported goods (e.g., loading, transport, handling) may also be zero-rated under Article 262-II. For pharmaceutical products, export zero-rating is unconditional on the nature of the goods, provided the export is genuine and documented. The export must occur under customs control, evidenced by an accepted customs export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document – DAU via DELTA)
  • Commercial invoice meeting VAT invoicing requirements
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) with carrier and shipping dates
  • Proof of international transport (CMR note, railway bill, etc.)
  • Evidence of exit from the EU customs territory (customs exit confirmation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT proof of payment for the exported goods

France is transitioning to mandatory electronic invoicing under the 2020 Finance Act: all established businesses must be able to receive e-invoices by 1 July 2026 and issue e-invoices by 1 July 2026 (large enterprises) and 1 January 2027 (SMEs). Invoices are transmitted through certified Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDP) or the public portal Chorus Pro. No real-time fiscal stamping is required; however, a real-time 'e-reporting' data transmission of transaction data to DGFiP is mandatory. Customs exports are cleared electronically via the DELTA system.

Fraudulent or insufficiently documented export zero-rating triggers: (i) immediate VAT clawback plus 0.40% monthly late payment interest; (ii) 50% penalty for lack of bona fide provided documentation and 80% penalty for intentional concealment or abusive situations; (iii) criminal penalties under CGI Article 1741 (five years imprisonment and €500,000 fine); (iv) retrospective reassessment within a six-year statute of limitation. DGFiP audits export proofs strictly and requires consistent documentation between customs declarations, commercial contracts, and payment flows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has no general exchange control over current-account or capital-account transactions. The SAS may generally receive foreign capital, convert currencies, pay suppliers, remit dividends, repay shareholder funding, and transfer liquidation proceeds, subject to bank anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions, tax, customs, foreign-investment-screening, and beneficial-ownership controls. Dividend and other profit remittances require distributable profits, approved financial statements, corporate approvals, and compliance with applicable French withholding tax, treaty, or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions. Cross-border capital movements involving sanctioned persons, restricted jurisdictions, controlled goods, or regulated pharmaceutical materials may be blocked or require governmental authorization. Certain foreign-investment transactions in strategic pharmaceutical activities may require prior French screening or notification, depending on the investor, activity, control rights, and transaction structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to a domestic bank, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no universal statutory deadline for converting export receipts. Export proceeds may remain in euros or foreign currency, subject to contractual banking arrangements, accounting, tax, customs, sanctions, and transaction-reporting obligations. Banks may request invoices, customs declarations, transport documents, end-customer information, and an economic-purpose explanation before crediting or converting funds. The company must preserve records supporting export, VAT, customs, transfer-pricing, and payment compliance. Cross-border cash movements and certain statistical or balance-of-payments information may be reportable under applicable French, European Union, or Banque de France frameworks.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange policy is governed principally by European Union and euro-area rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment accounts and international collections; low to medium for regulated pharmaceutical trade, capital-deposit functionality, documentary letters of credit, and complex treasury. An EU electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide payment accounts, SEPA transfers, foreign-exchange execution, and selected international payment services, but it is not equivalent to a French credit institution and may not accept statutory SAS share capital deposits, provide credit, issue all forms of commercial letters of credit, or support every SWIFT and correspondent-banking service. The EMI must be authorized under the applicable EU passporting framework, conduct full KYC and beneficial-owner verification, apply sanctions and transaction monitoring, and comply with safeguarding requirements. Offshore non-EU digital EMIs may require an EU or French authorization, local regulated partner, or reliance on a permitted cross-border-services regime. The SAS should maintain a regulated French or EU bank as its primary account for capital, payroll, taxes, regulated trade, and contingency liquidity.

The SAS should maintain a regulated French or EU bank as its primary account for capital, payroll, taxes, regulated trade, and contingency liquidity.
