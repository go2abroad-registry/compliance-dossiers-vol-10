Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS; the share capital is freely fixed in the articles. All shares must be subscribed in full. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Cash subscription funds must be escrowed in a blocked French bank account opened for the company in formation until the RCS registration certificate is issued.

Funding & unlock path: No fire brigade approval or civil-defense clearance is required for a purely administrative and commercial operation. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), through the official impots.gouv.fr portal. VAT registrations and filings for foreign businesses are handled by the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered for tax purposes through its business formalities filing via the competent Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI); registration results in allocation of a SIREN and establishment-specific SIRET, and the company obtains its French VAT number from DGFiP when VAT registration is required. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in France must apply to the SIEE for French VAT registration before commencing those supplies, provide its formation and tax-registration documents, identify its French activities and expected transactions, and appoint a French fiscal representative if required. Non-EU entities generally must appoint a fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement provides an exemption. VAT registration is conducted through the applicable DGFiP process; the VAT number is generally FR plus a two-character key and the entity's SIREN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). It may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax rules under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code if the statutory conditions are met, including qualifying ownership, size, age, and business-activity conditions; the election is limited to five fiscal years and is not a US-style check-the-box election. No general check-the-box or elective hybrid-mismatch classification regime applies. French tax residence and source rules determine the taxable scope; the French territorial system does not itself make a SAS tax-exempt.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business, the domestic small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base) generally uses annual turnover thresholds of EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and certain accommodation or food activities and EUR 37,500 for services, subject to statutory rules and applicable threshold changes. These are exemption thresholds, not a general VAT-registration threshold. A non-established business making taxable supplies in France generally has no domestic turnover threshold and must register when required under the applicable place-of-supply and reverse-charge rules. The EU-wide threshold for qualifying cross-border intra-Community distance sales and certain electronic services is EUR 10,000, subject to the relevant EU rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the EU may be exempt from French VAT with a right to deduct input VAT, provided the supplier can substantiate export and meet the statutory conditions, including the applicable time limit for export and evidence of exit from the EU customs territory. Intra-Community supplies are subject to separate exemption rules, including a valid customer VAT number and proof of transport. Services are not automatically zero-rated because a customer is offshore; their VAT treatment depends on place-of-supply rules, customer status, and any applicable exemption. For B2B services supplied to a taxable customer established outside France, the general place-of-supply rule commonly places taxation where the customer is established, subject to exceptions.

  • Export customs declaration with the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and customs confirmation of exit from the EU
  • Commercial invoice identifying the goods, buyer, destination, and applicable VAT-exemption basis
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the mode of shipment, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR, or carrier delivery confirmation
  • Purchase order, sales contract, delivery records, and evidence linking the exported goods to the relevant invoice
  • Proof of payment or settlement records where available; France does not impose a general inward-remittance certificate requirement for export VAT exemption

France is introducing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction e-reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all businesses must be able to receive e-invoices; issuance obligations begin on that date for large and intermediate-sized businesses and generally on 1 September 2027 for small and medium-sized businesses. Covered invoices must use a compliant format and be transmitted through a DGFiP-authorized partner dematerialization platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire, PDP), directly or through the public invoicing portal where available. Relevant transaction and payment data must be reported under the e-reporting rules. France does not generally require real-time pre-clearance or digital tax stamping of each VAT invoice.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The supplier must retain sufficient evidence that the goods left the EU and that the transaction qualifies for exemption. Unsupported or incorrectly claimed export exemption may result in reassessment of output VAT, late-payment interest, and tax penalties; penalties depend on the nature and degree of fault and may be increased for deliberate conduct or fraudulent manoeuvres. Input VAT refunds may be denied or recovered where entitlement or supporting evidence is insufficient. Customs, VAT, and accounting records should be retained for the applicable statutory periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The French franc legacy controls have been superseded by the euro regime, which is fully convertible under Article 63 TFEU and the OECD Codes of Liberalisation. France applies no material exchange-control restrictions to current-account trade payments for battery-grade lithium carbonate imports; the capital account is likewise liberalized for EU and third-country investors, with no prior authorization required for non-sensitive wholesale distribution activities. Profit remittance of dividends to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted from a treasury perspective; any levy (e.g., 30% flat tax or treaty-reduced withholding) is a fiscal matter and not a liquidity-clearing constraint. The only compliance layer is statistical: cross-border transactions are automatically reported to the Banque de France by the instructing bank for balance-of-payments purposes, and foreign direct investment inflows (≥10% voting rights in the SAS) trigger a mandatory 'Investment in France' external-finance notification (déclaration d'investissement direct étranger en France) within 20 days of the operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose any statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation quota on export proceeds; a French resident company may lawfully retain export receivables in EUR or convertible foreign currency. The only regulatory obligations are (i) routing all export receipts through an authorised bank, (ii) accurate encoding of the transaction for the Banque de France balance-of-payments return, and (iii) declaration of foreign commercial debt (dette extérieure) when the outstanding balance of supplier credits or advances granted by non-residents exceeds the regulatory notification threshold (absolutely €1.0 million) on a monthly basis. The subject SAS is an importer of lithium carbonate; hence the export-surrender dimension is not applicable to its primary procurement flows.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) acting within the Eurosystem, with delegated authority from the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor, Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty) for external financial relations, balance-of-payments statistics and cross-border payment surveillance. The ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) supervises licensed banks and e-money institutions; the Banque de France operates the foreign-exchange information system and the SARFT-style reporting portal for cross-border fund movements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for domestic euro payments and administrative treasury, but Low for cross-border documentary trade flows. A French ACPR-licensed Electronic Money Institution (e.g., Qonto, Treezor, Mangopay) may lawfully open a payment account for the SAS, issue IBANs, execute SEPA and SWIFT MT103 transactions, and provide expense-card infrastructure; however, an EMI is prohibited from extending credit, granting overdrafts, issuing commercial letters of credit (MT700), or providing trade-finance instruments. A non-EU offshore EMI without a French passport or an ACPR branch licence has no legal standing to maintain an operational payment account for a French resident SAS under PSD2 and could not serve the cross-border procurement rails of battery-grade lithium carbonate. The recommended structure is therefore a full-service commercial bank for primary clearing, with an EMI optionally layered for FX card and payroll services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-EU shareholder invests into the SAS through a SWIFT MT103 wire to the BNP Paribas or Société Générale account with purpose code 'CAPITAL INCREASE / FONDS PROPRES' and reference to the shareholders' resolution. The bank encodes the inflow as euro-area external liability in the Banque de France balance-of-payments return; for a foreign direct investment involving ≥10% of the capital or voting rights, the company must deliver the 'Investment in France' declaration (DIDE) to the Banque de France within 20 working days. Capital must be credited as paid-in capital and shown in the corporate accounts under 'Capital social' to prevent recharacterisation as shareholder debt; the bank's credit advice should be preserved as the treasury evidence of the contribution. If the contribution is made in foreign currency, the French receiving bank converts it at the ECB fixing applied to the account contract; any settlement-risk protection requires the SWIFT ordering bank to use a direct correspondent of the French beneficiary institution.For the purchase of battery-grade lithium carbonate powder from China, the recommended clearing rail is an irrevocable documentary letter of credit: the French issuing bank (e.g., Société Générale or BNP Paribas) transmits a SWIFT MT700 through its China correspondent to the supplier's advising bank, with settlement at sight against compliant presentation (commercial invoice, full-set original ocean bill of lading, certificate of quality attesting to purity ≥99.5% Li₂CO₃, packing list, rules of origin certificate). Where open-account settlement is commercially necessary, the company instructs a SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'IMPORT GOODS / METALS AND MINERAL WHOLESALE' and a precise payment narrative matching the customs import declaration; all such payments must be routed through the French bank's compliance-approved trade desk, with invoice, bill of lading, and customs entry attached as supporting evidence inside the bank's trade surveillance system. SEPA Credit Transfer is available only for intra-EEA euro supplier payments and is not applicable to Chinese counterparties.EUR/USD deliverable FX forward contracts (ISDA 2002 French Master Agreement, EMIR-cleared where required; actual physical delivery of EUR and USD at maturity).FX swaps (Cambiste deposits/reverse deposits or classic swap-cambiste) for short-term treasury liquidity management up to 12 months.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS/CRS) to fix EUR-USD conversion costs on medium-term supplier credit lines or trade payables.Vanilla EUR/USD option strategies — bought calls, collars, and zero-premium risk reversals to cap USD depreciation against the euro without speculative features.FX spot forward conversion lines under the bank's daily treasury facility, including standing instruction hedging for monthly trade settlements.NDFs are permissible only for the non-euro trading currencies of the underlying supply chain (e.g., USD/CNY or USD/CLP) where the local currency has no deliverability; NDF contracts with a euro leg are not accepted by French credit institutions because the euro is freely deliverable and such contracts would constitute non-genuine risk-only instruments.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for domestic euro payments and administrative treasury, but Low for cross-border documentary trade flows. A French ACPR-licensed Electronic Money Institution (e.g., Qonto, Treezor, Mangopay) may lawfully open a payment account for the SAS, issue IBANs, execute SEPA and SWIFT MT103 transactions, and provide expense-card infrastructure; however, an EMI is prohibited from extending credit, granting overdrafts, issuing commercial letters of credit (MT700), or providing trade-finance instruments. A non-EU offshore EMI without a French passport or an ACPR branch licence has no legal standing to maintain an operational payment account for a French resident SAS under PSD2 and could not serve the cross-border procurement rails of battery-grade lithium carbonate. The recommended structure is therefore a full-service commercial bank for primary clearing, with an EMI optionally layered for FX card and payroll services.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million (annual turnover or total assets of the French entity, or of the consolidated group to which it belongs)

Economic substance scope: France does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but enforces economic substance through the substance-over-form principle, the General Anti-Abuse Rule (abus de droit under Article L.64 LPF), and BEPS-related principal purpose tests. A French SAS carrying on battery-grade lithium carbonate procurement must maintain genuine substance — real employees, physical premises, and actual commercial decision-making — to sustain tax residence, support permanent establishment allocation, and preserve treaty benefits. Artificial or conduit structures lacking commercial reality are recharacterized and denied tax benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Non-Filing (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI, tax fraud is punishable by up to five years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 75,000; aggravated circumstances carry increased penalties. Tax authorities may reassess profit with a 40% bad-faith penalty plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: A director, whether de jure or de facto, may be prosecuted personally; the corporate veil can be pierced through civil liability for unlawful management (action en comblement du passif), and courts may impose travel restrictions, asset freezes, and confiscation of personal property.
