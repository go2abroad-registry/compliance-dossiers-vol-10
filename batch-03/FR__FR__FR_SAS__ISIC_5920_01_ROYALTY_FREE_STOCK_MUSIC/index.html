Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French tax administration under the Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; business registration portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; e-invoicing platform portal: impots.gouv.fr/professionnel/echange-de-donnees (Portail Public de Facturation).

        
E-government filing portal
A French SAS is incorporated online via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, which automatically notifies DGFiP; the company then receives its SIREN and SIRET identifiers and its intra-community VAT number (format FR with a 2-digit control key) issued by the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Foreign entities without a French establishment must apply directly to DGFiP's non-resident tax office (Service des Impôts des Non-Résidents) using the dedicated French VAT identification form, providing proof of foreign TIN, articles of association, and confirmation of the foreign principal place of business; a French VAT ID is required for taxable transactions, for intra-EU acquisitions where the reverse charge does not apply, and for reclaiming French VAT under the EU refund procedure.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A French SAS is incorporated online via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, which automatically notifies DGFiP; the company then receives its SIREN and SIRET identifiers and its intra-community VAT number (format FR with a 2-digit control key) issued by the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Foreign entities without a French establishment must apply directly to DGFiP's non-resident tax office (Service des Impôts des Non-Résidents) using the dedicated French VAT identification form, providing proof of foreign TIN, articles of association, and confirmation of the foreign principal place of business; a French VAT ID is required for taxable transactions, for intra-EU acquisitions where the reverse charge does not apply, and for reclaiming French VAT under the EU refund procedure.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
France does not apply check-the-box elections; legal personality is driven by the entity form. An SAS is by default subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level, with no statutory pass-through election. Optional elections available include: (i) group tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A of the CGI for a French parent holding at least 95% of subsidiaries; (ii) election for the simplified VAT regime (régime réel simplifié) instead of the normal VAT regime for small businesses; (iii) the option to apply the margin scheme for qualifying second-hand goods and works of art; and (iv) the option to treat certain rental operations as taxable for VAT. Hybrid mismatch rules, based on the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive, are implemented in French domestic law and apply to cross-border arrangements regardless of any check-the-box election.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
20%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
EUR 85,000 annual turnover for main business activities of supplying goods; EUR 37,500 annual turnover for service providers and for intellectual property licensing or commercial soundscape licensing (digital audio subscription and royalty-free stock music license sales). These thresholds apply to French-established small enterprises under the franchise en base de TVA. Foreign suppliers selling goods or services to French consumers via electronic interfaces must register from the first euro unless using the OSS or IOSS scheme; a French SAS performing taxable transactions in France generally cannot benefit from a turnover threshold and must register immediately for VAT, irrespective of whether the customer is a business or private individual.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods exported to non-EU destinations are zero-rated for French VAT, provided that the vendor holds satisfactory documentary evidence of physical export outside the EU, such as a customs export declaration, within the prescribed time frame. Services physically performed outside the EU, including digital audio content and license subscription services to non-EU customers, are outside the territorial scope of French VAT and are treated as taxable at 0% for refund purposes only if the supplier can demonstrate that the place of supply is correctly attributed to the non-EU customer (B2B or B2C rules). For the specific business of royalty-free stock music sales and audio sample subscriptions, zero-rating is available for (i) electronic downloads and royalties to non-French non-EU customers, (ii) rights to use music incorporated into tangible manufactured goods that are physically exported, and (iii) B2B transactions where French VAT is ultimately reverse-charged to the EU customer's VAT ID in a destination-member-state tax code. The issuer must retain evidence of destination and customer tax status, including the customer's EU VAT ID, IP address logs, subscription contracts, and bank settlement details.

France has a phased mandatory e-invoicing regime for domestic B2B transactions, under which invoices must be issued in a structured electronic format, transmitted through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or an approved plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire (PDP), and electronically reported to DGFiP. Real-time fiscal clearance is not currently required in France, but e-invoicing data are transmitted no later than data-receipt deadlines set by the PPF and are automatically cross-checked with VAT returns and customs declarations. Digital stamping, blockchain hash, or continuous controls (such as the Romanian / Italian equivalent) are not applied. For non-resident suppliers subject to French VAT, VAT returns must be filed electronically and payments settled via impots.gouv.fr; mandatory e-invoicing obligations apply to taxpayers established in France while OSS returns are filed electronically in the member state of establishment.

      
False or abusive use of export zero-rating and fraudulent VAT recovery triggers clawback of the underpaid VAT, plus the full amount of the sums improperly deducted, and additional penalties under Articles 1729, 1731, 1788 and 1788 A of the CGI. A 'manquement délibéré' (deliberate breach) entails a 40% penalty plus late-interest indemnities at the monthly rate of 0.20%; 'manœuvres frauduleuses' (fraudulent manoeuvres) or abuse of law entail an 80% penalty, and criminal sanctions may be incurred under the French Customs Code and the CGI for customs declaration falsification and tax fraud, with fines of up to EUR 75,000 (or three times the tax evaded) and imprisonment of up to 7 years for categories of aggravated tax fraud. The customs administration may also perform retrospective verification and impose non-collection penalties for missing or erroneous export evidence. If export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days under FX regulations, the tax advantage is automatically unwound and the VAT becomes due, with interest and penalties accruing from the original due date.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
France applies full euro-area convertibility with no exchange-control authorisation required for current-account or capital-account transactions. Dividend distributions, royalty payments, and profit remittances from a French SAS are freely transferable abroad provided the underlying corporate documentation, tax compliance, and anti-money laundering source-of-funds checks are satisfied. Withholding tax may apply unless reduced under an applicable income tax treaty, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, or the EU Royalty/Interest Directive, subject to effective-beneficiary and substance requirements. Intra-group financing and direct investment must be reported to the Banque de France for statistical and balance-of-payments purposes.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
France maintains no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and imposes no mandatory conversion ratio or repatriation timeline. Export receivables may be retained in foreign currency, converted freely, or used offshore, provided that the underlying transactions are accurately identified in the company's books and any statistical balance-of-payments declarations required by the Banque de France are filed.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem and jointly with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking licensing and prudential supervision; the Direction Générale du Trésor retains policy competence for external financial and exchange-control legislation.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
An EU-authorised electronic money institution or payment institution may passport into France under the PSD2 freedom-to-provide-services regime and can offer ancillary receiving, payout, or currency-conversion services. However, offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA are legally constrained from taking deposits or issuing electronic money in France without an ACPR-authorised branch or a French licensed banking partner. For treasury-grade clearing and irrevocable settlement of trade payments and capital flows, direct account relationships with French tier-1 banks remain the primary rail; fintechs are suitable only as secondary toggles or expense-disbursement channels. Overall feasibility rating: Medium for EU-passporting EMIs; Low for non-EEA offshore EMIs.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Unexplained or mismatched source of funds for the initial capital injection or subsequent intercompany funding.
        
Root cause: Unexplained or mismatched source of funds for the initial capital injection or subsequent intercompany funding.

        
Defense evidence: Provide (i) notarised board resolution authorising the capital increase and specifying bank account details; (ii) the transferor's bank statements covering the full funding chain for at least 12 months; (iii) source-of-wealth declaration signed by the beneficial owner; (iv) audited financial statements or a certified balance-sheet of the parent entity; (v) commercial contracts proving future royalty-free music licensing revenues; (vi) tax residence certificates of all direct shareholders; and (vii) a written explanation of the business purpose and expected transaction volume.

      

      
      

        Beneficial ownership opacity, nominee-shareholder flags, or a mismatch between the filing at the Registre National des Bénéficiaires Effectifs and bank interviews.
        
Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity, nominee-shareholder flags, or a mismatch between the filing at the Registre National des Bénéficiaires Effectifs and bank interviews.

        
Defense evidence: Provide (i) current French RBE extract; (ii) a recent Kbis company extract; (iii) updated articles of association of the French SAS; (iv) a full corporate ownership chart with personal identification documents and an address-verification document for each natural-person beneficial owner; (v) passports of directors and authorised signatories; (vi) signed disclosure declarations that no undisclosed nominee or trust arrangement exists; and (vii) independent professional references from an accountant, lawyer, or notary.

      

      
      

        Perceived high-risk payment corridors, crypto-related counterparties, or royalty collection from online platforms with geographically fragmented buyer bases.
        
Root cause: Perceived high-risk payment corridors, crypto-related counterparties, or royalty collection from online platforms with geographically fragmented buyer bases.

        
Defense evidence: Provide (i) the licensing agreement, streaming-platform terms, and distribution contracts for the royalty-free stock music catalogue; (ii) a detailed breakdown of expected counterparty jurisdictions and payment methods (Stripe, PayPal, SWIFT settlements); (iii) sanctions-screening results confirming that no counterparty is in a proscribed jurisdiction; (iv) a compliance manual describing end-customer due diligence for sub-licensees; and (v) monthly or quarterly board reporting confirming the absence of mixing personal and corporate funds.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound equity injections and intra-group capital flows should be executed through SWIFT MT103 wires from the shareholder's bank account in the identical legal name, using a unique end-to-end transaction reference and the ISO 20022 category purpose code 'INTC' for intra-company financing or 'CAGR' for a capital increase where the receiving bank accepts that code. The remittance field must state the legal basis as a paid-in capital contribution under the French SAS articles of association or the notarised capital-resolution deed. Before credit is applied, the French bank will normally require copies of the board resolution, the shareholder registry, the beneficial-owner declaration, and a completed source-of-funds questionnaire. For each disbursement, provide invoices, licensing download logs, or contractual milestone certificates, and verify that payees are not subject to European, OFAC, UN, or national sanctions. Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A reduced 15% rate may apply to qualifying small companies on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to revenue, capital ownership, and fully paid-up capital conditions.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to domestic exemptions and tax treaties. A qualifying EU parent company may obtain a 0% exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, commonly requiring at least 10% ownership, a qualifying legal form and subject-to-tax status, a minimum holding period or commitment, and anti-abuse compliance.

    

    

      
Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a financial year ending on a date other than 31 December, the return is generally due within three months after the year-end. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, or by 15 May for a 31 December year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million turnover or gross assets generally triggers the full French transfer-pricing documentation obligation, including Local File and Master File requirements, subject to additional statutory control and group conditions. A simplified documentation regime and annual transfer-pricing summary declaration may apply from EUR 50 million turnover or gross assets, including for certain entities belonging to qualifying groups. Thresholds and filing obligations must be tested for the French entity and relevant group relationships.

      
Economic substance scope: France has no single UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime applicable to all companies. Substance is nevertheless required to support beneficial ownership, treaty access, deductibility, transfer-pricing outcomes, anti-abuse analysis, controlled foreign company rules, hybrid mismatch rules, and the general anti-abuse doctrine. A French SAS should maintain real decision-making, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational capacity, adequate premises or infrastructure, financial capability, books and records, board or management evidence, and genuine business functions in France. A royalty-free stock-music marketplace should document ownership or licensing rights, content curation, platform operations, customer support, pricing, marketing, and risks actually performed in France.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns
        
Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, surcharges of up to 80% in aggravated cases, and criminal tax-fraud penalties that may include imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with higher penalties for aggravated or organized fraud.

        
Director personal liability: Directors or de facto managers may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, management bans, and civil liability. Under Article L267 of the French Tax Procedures Book, a director may be held jointly and severally liable for tax debts where fraudulent maneuvers or serious and repeated non-compliance made collection impossible. Travel restrictions are not automatic and require a specific legal or judicial basis.

      

      
      

        Artificial Profit Shifting and Transfer-Pricing Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment, secondary adjustment, denial of deductions, withholding-tax reassessment, interest, penalties, and potential documentation penalties. A 40% penalty may apply for deliberate breaches and an 80% penalty for fraudulent maneuvers or abuse of law.

        
Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise for deliberate participation in fraudulent arrangements, concealment, or serious repeated non-compliance. Ordinary TP adjustment alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, loss of deductions, tax reassessment, anti-abuse penalties, withholding-tax exposure, and possible permanent-establishment or place-of-effective-management disputes.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil or criminal exposure where they knowingly implement sham arrangements, falsify records, or organize insolvency. Management bans may be ordered in insolvency or criminal proceedings; travel bans are not automatic.

      

      
      

        Beneficial Ownership and Treaty Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: Denial of reduced withholding, collection of the 25% domestic dividend or applicable royalty rate, interest, penalties, and possible application of principal-purpose or anti-abuse rules.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for intentional misrepresentation, forged residence certificates, or fraudulent withholding reporting. Contractual reliance on a foreign recipient's certificate does not protect a payer that ignored obvious abuse indicators.

      

      
      

        Withholding-Tax Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, late-payment interest, applicable surcharges, and potential penalties for inaccurate or missing declarations.

        
Director personal liability: Personal liability may follow from fraudulent concealment, repeated serious failures, or misuse of corporate assets. Failure caused solely by an arguable legal interpretation does not automatically create personal liability.

      

      
      

        Accounting and Corporate Record Falsification
        
Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, accounting and corporate criminal sanctions, fines, imprisonment in serious cases, and possible prohibition from managing a company.

        
Director personal liability: Directors, officers, accountants, and beneficial owners who knowingly participate may be prosecuted personally. Corporate limited liability does not shield intentional criminal conduct.

      

      
      

        VAT and Digital Platform Misclassification
        
Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, interest, penalties, joint-liability exposure in certain platform situations, and possible failure-to-register or invoicing penalties.

        
Director personal liability: Personal exposure may arise for deliberate VAT fraud, carousel arrangements, false invoices, or repeated serious failures. Directors may also face management bans or insolvency-related liability.

      

      
      

        Intellectual Property Royalty Mischaracterization
        
Statutory sanction: Withholding-tax reassessment, deduction denial, transfer-pricing adjustment, copyright-related liability, and penalties for inaccurate declarations.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for knowing infringement, fraudulent licensing evidence, or deliberate tax misclassification. Commercial disputes alone generally remain corporate liabilities.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering and Payment Fraud
        
Statutory sanction: Account freezing, asset confiscation, regulatory reporting, criminal fines, and imprisonment. Money-laundering offenses may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with higher sanctions for aggravated conduct.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face personal criminal liability, confiscation, management bans, and judicial restrictions. Travel bans may be imposed by a court or investigating authority where legally justified, but are not automatic.

      

      
      

        Insolvency Misconduct and Asset Diversion
        
Statutory sanction: Extension of insolvency proceedings, contribution to company debts, clawback of transactions, bankruptcy offenses, fines, and imprisonment.

        
Director personal liability: Managers may be ordered to contribute to liabilities, disqualified from management, and prosecuted for misuse of corporate assets or bankruptcy offenses. Limited liability does not protect directors from wrongful trading or asset diversion.

      

      
      

        Failure to Maintain Statutory Registers and Beneficial-Owner Information
        
Statutory sanction: Administrative and criminal penalties, inability to substantiate tax positions, tax reassessment, and possible restrictions on corporate filings or banking relationships.

        
Director personal liability: The legal representative may be personally prosecuted for knowingly false declarations or obstruction. Civil veil piercing is fact-dependent and not automatic.
