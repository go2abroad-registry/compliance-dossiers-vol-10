Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), administered through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); official portal: impots.gouv.fr and the formalities single window at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity with a French permanent establishment, French VAT liability, or other taxable presence must register through the formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr single window (Guichet Unique) to obtain a French SIREN/SIRET number and, where applicable, an intra-Community VAT identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) in the format FR[2-digit key][SIREN]. Non-established foreign companies without a French establishment that trigger French VAT obligations must file an application directly with the local SIE using the dedicated non-established operator form (Cerfa 1547 for VAT and corporate registration), and must appoint a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal, joint and several liability) if established outside the EU/EEA and not covered by a convention. Establishment formalities include submission of constitutional documents, appointment of a legal representative, proof of foreign tax residence, and, for non-EU entities, proof of an EU permanent establishment or designation of a VAT agent. Registration triggers assignment of a French EDI (identifiant de dépôt) in the national directory and enrolment for VAT returns, corporate tax filings via EFI (Espace Fiscal de l'Entreprise), and social contribution declarations where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope: a French SAS is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level on its worldwide profits as a French resident, with foreign-source income relieved through treaty and domestic territorial provisions; France applies territoriality for certain foreign activities through the parent-subsidiary regime (régime mère-fille) and foreign branch exemption principles, but the SAS itself is a French tax resident taxed at entity level under CGI Article 209.

Available election forms

French law does not provide a US-style check-the-box regime; entity classification follows legal form and mandatory corporate tax liability. Limited statutory elections include: (1) under CGI Article 239 bis A, a newly created SAS may elect, within its first five fiscal years and subject to eligibility conditions, to be taxed on a pass-through partnership basis (régime des sociétés de personnes); (2) under CGI Article 223 A, a 95% French-parent group may elect fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) to consolidate results of French subsidiaries; (3) election to apply the small-business reduced corporate tax rate under CGI Article 219 requires no formal opt-in but requires periodic qualification; (4) hybrid mismatch and anti-avoidance limitations under CGI Article 212 bis and the ATAD implementation deny deduction or recharacterize payments in cross-border hybrid arrangements; no election is available to escape the French territorial VAT system or to convert the SAS into a transparent vehicle beyond the narrow 239 bis A window.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Non-established sellers: no exemption threshold, VAT registration required from the first taxable transaction in France. EU distance selling of goods by non-established EU sellers: EUR 10,000 Union-wide distance-sales threshold; exceeding it, or opting into the OSS (One-Stop Shop), triggers French VAT liability, with OSS registration allowing simplified foreign declaration. Domestic small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA): EUR 91,900 (goods and certain supplies) and EUR 36,800 (services) annual turnover thresholds under CGI Articles 293 B and 293 B ter, excluding this scope from statutory VAT registration; sellers above these limits are mandatorily registered with a French VAT number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to: (1) export of goods dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU, provided the exporter holds proof of physical exit from EU customs territory within the statutory period (CGI Article 262-1-1°); (2) intra-Community supplies of goods to a taxable person identified with a valid EU VAT number in another Member State, including transport documentation to the Member State of destination (CGI Article 262 ter 1°); (3) services directly linked to export of goods and services involving third-party international transport where VAT applies at the destination and evidence of export is retained; (4) digital B2B services supplied to a non-French taxable person or to a non-EU recipient are outside French VAT scope under place-of-supply rules rather than zero-rated, requiring evidence of the customer's business status, IP location, and customer registration details. The claim requires commercial, transport, and sometimes customs-triangulation evidence; absence of such proof shifts the supply to standard-rate French VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / ENS via the DELTA-X or DSL system) bearing the exporting trader's EORI number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from France/EU to the non-EU destination
  • CMR international road consignment note, rail waybill, or other transport document showing the exact route and exit from EU territory
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract identifying the foreign buyer and delivery terms
  • Proof of payment and inward remittance documentation confirming receipt of proceeds from the export sale
  • EU VAT number validation certificate (VIES confirmation) and delivery records for intra-Community dispatches

Mandatory e-invoicing for all B2B transactions is phased in under the French e-invoicing reform delegated to DGFiP: large enterprises from 1 September 2026, and mid-sized and SMEs from 1 September 2027, using structured electronic invoices (FACTUR-X / EN 16931) transmitted through registered Partner Dematerialization Platforms (PDP) to the public invoice central directory (Annuaire et Répertoire de facturation). B2G invoicing via Chorus Pro is already mandatory. There is no current French real-time per-invoice fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime equivalent to Latin American clearance models; instead, France mandates e-reporting (e-reporting des transactions) of B2B and B2C payment data to DGFiP from the same 2026/2027 phased dates. In parallel, taxable persons must retain transaction-level records, including payment-confirmation data, to support the French VAT inspection powers and to evidence place-of-supply for digital services under Articles 58 and 259 of Council Directive 2006/112/EC.

DGFiP audits export zero-rating claims within the standard three-year limitation period (CGI Article L 169 LPF), extended to six years for undisclosed activities and up to ten years for concealed operations or cross-border fraud. Fraudulent or unjustified zero-rating and export VAT claims are requalified as domestic taxable supplies with retroactive VAT, input VAT credit clawbacks, and denial of deduction rights. Penalties include: 40% of the under-assessed VAT for deliberate misconduct (manquement délibéré, Article 1728), 80% for abuse of right or repeated concealment (Article 1729), monthly late-payment interest of 0.2% (intérêts de retard, Article 1731), and criminal sanctions for VAT fraud (escroquerie fiscale) under Article 1741 CGI with imprisonment of up to five years, a fine of EUR 750,000, and loss of certain civil or professional rights; in aggravated fraud cases involving organized crime, the maximum penalty reaches seven years' imprisonment and a EUR 1,000,000 fine, alongside unlimited retroactive reassessment and joint liability for the result of the fiscal representative or the exporter's legal representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member and applies full convertibility on both capital and current account transactions under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. There are no material foreign exchange convertibility restrictions on deposits, equity contributions, dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily tax-driven: dividends paid by a French SAS to non-residents may attract French withholding tax at the applicable domestic rate or a reduced treaty rate, but no exchange control authorization is required for the remittance. Non-resident investors may trigger a statistical statement to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments purposes, and certain investments in strategic digital or communications technologies may be screened under the French foreign direct investment regulations, but this does not affect the underlying FX convertibility of the French euro.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratios. Export receipts may be retained in original foreign currency accounts, converted to euro at a licensed bank, or redeployed freely. The only standard requirement is the Banque de France’s balance-of-payments statistical reporting framework for cross-border payments and direct investment flows above administrative thresholds. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation and no percentage requirement for conversion into euro.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the delegated prudential supervisor for banks and electronic money institutions; in the euro area, the European Central Bank (ECB) exercises direct monetary authority and participates in bank supervision under the Single Supervisory Mechanism.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. A fully licensed French or EEA-registered electronic money institution (EMI) with a valid PSD2 passport can support cross-border collections and disbursements, including payment waterfall structures around Apple IAP and Paymentwall. However, an offshore, non-EEA EMI without an ACPR registration or passporting notification will not be viable because it cannot lawfully provide payment accounts in France, cannot hold safeguarding accounts with a French credit institution, and will be rejected by the French banking and clearing infrastructure for a Medium-Risk Enhanced profile. An EEA EMI may act as an auxiliary rail, but a Tier-1 French bank relationship remains the foundational treasury mandate.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a French SAS receiving equity or shareholder loans, the compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103, or a SEPA/Target2 credit transfer if euro-denominated, from the foreign investor’s bank to the SAS’s French IBAN. The payment reason must be expressed using an ISO 20022 external purpose code, such as 'CAPH' for a capital increase or 'INTC' for an intra-group transfer; for operational funding, 'GDDS' for goods and services may apply. The receiving French bank will verify that the remitter’s name matches the registered shareholder, that the board resolution and contribution agreement are in the corporate file, and that the source of funds is transparent. No French exchange-control approval is required, but the transaction may be subject to the Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration threshold and, where the investor is non-EEA and the activity touches communications infrastructure, to foreign direct investment screening by the Direction Générale du Trésor.Clearing rails for trade settlements include SWIFT MT103 for direct correspondent-bank transfers, MT700/MT710 for documentary commercial letters of credit, and SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for euro payments within the EEA. Outbound payments will be released only when the underlying invoice, contract, or trade document matches the beneficiary name, bank account, and stated purpose. For Apple IAP and Paymentwall revenue settlement, the treasury must rely on direct merchant-payout ACH or SEPA settlement reports rather than documentary credits, and reconcile every settlement file against the SAS’s bank statement under French accounting and tax verification rules. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. A fully licensed French or EEA-registered electronic money institution (EMI) with a valid PSD2 passport can support cross-border collections and disbursements, including payment waterfall structures around Apple IAP and Paymentwall. However, an offshore, non-EEA EMI without an ACPR registration or passporting notification will not be viable because it cannot lawfully provide payment accounts in France, cannot hold safeguarding accounts with a French credit institution, and will be rejected by the French banking and clearing infrastructure for a Medium-Risk Enhanced profile. An EEA EMI may act as an auxiliary rail, but a Tier-1 French bank relationship remains the foundational treasury mandate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400,000,000 (group turnover or total assets) triggers mandatory Master File and Local File; below threshold, simplified documentation required if cross-border transactions exceed €1,000,000.

Economic substance scope: France applies substance-over-form and anti-abuse rules (e.g., GAAR, abuse of law doctrine). No specific ESR regime, but real economic activity (employees, premises, decision-making) is required to benefit from treaty provisions and to avoid recharacterization of transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% of the understated tax (80% if abuse of rights), plus late interest of 0.2% per month. Criminal fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (increased to €1,000,000 and 7 years for aggravated cases).

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the tax debt if they participated in the fraud (piercing the corporate veil). Travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment are possible. Civil restitution of unpaid taxes and damages.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, 80% penalty for abuse of law, and potential nullification of the entity for tax purposes. Fines up to €1,000,000 for corporate entities.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for the resulting tax liabilities and penalties. Travel bans and restrictions on leaving France may be imposed during investigations. Criminal prosecution for tax fraud if intent is proven.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering) Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1,000,000 (or 10% of annual turnover for legal entities). Criminal penalties for money laundering: up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine for individuals; €1,000,000 for companies.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines and imprisonment if they knowingly fail to comply with AML obligations. They may also be subject to professional bans and travel restrictions. Civil liability for damages caused by non-compliance.
