Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions under EU Treaty provisions (Articles 63-66 TFEU) and the French Monetary and Financial Code. Capital account convertibility is unfettered for remittances related to equity investments, profits, loans, and service payments. Profit remittance is permitted subject to standard corporate formalities (proof of distributable reserves, audited accounts when applicable, and satisfaction of French withholding tax clearance if non-treaty relief applies). No material residual convertibility hurdlers for an SAS owned by non-residents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender or forced conversion timeline for export proceeds. Export receipts in euros or foreign currencies may be retained onshore or offshore, subject to the Banque de France's balance-of-payments reporting obligations if the flow exceeds EUR 750,000 or forms a trade receivable that must be reported indirectly through the banking channel. No mandatory conversion ratio to EUR applies.

Central bank supervision: The Banque de France exercises monetary authority and balance-of-payments oversight; the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) prudentially regulates credit institutions and payment service providers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for entirely digital onboarding, but subject to significant legal constraints. Offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) wishing to service a French establishment must be passported or licensed under the ACPR/Régime du passeport européen. Remote KYC is permitted under French law if the solution is ACPR-compliant and captures biometric or equivalent assurance; however, a purely offshore EMI not holding an EU payment institution license cannot legally clear euro-denominated mass transactions on behalf of a France-based SAS. Practical viability without a domestic EMI agent or authorized partner is Low. Stripe and PayPal, when used as collection gateways, must be configured as merchants of the French entity, not as substitutes for the depository banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Wire transfers denominated in EUR directly into the French SAS euro account via SEPA CT. Non-EUR transfers use SWIFT MT103 with clearly described purpose codes.
