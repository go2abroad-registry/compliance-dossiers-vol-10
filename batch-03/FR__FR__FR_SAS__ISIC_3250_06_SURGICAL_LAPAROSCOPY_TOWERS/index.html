Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); professional portal: impots.gouv.fr; business formalities single window: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique); customs authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) with DELTA H1 export clearance.

E-government filing portal

Incorporate the SAS via the Guichet Unique (single electronic window) by filing French articles of association (statuts), appointing a president, declaring the registered office in France and opening a French corporate bank account; the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) issues the K-bis extract with SIREN/SIRET and NAF code 3250B; the DGFiP automatically attributes the French intra-community VAT number (FR + 11-digit SIREN-based key), reflected on the taxpayer identification notice and in the VIES registry; for immediate import/export operations the SAS must also obtain an EORI number from the DGDDI. A foreign entity without a French establishment that makes direct taxable supplies into France must file a VAT identification request with the competent SIE under Article 289 A CGI and appoint a fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) when not established in the EU; the medical device activity must additionally be notified to ANSM under Article L.5211-3-2 of the French Public Health Code for CE-marked surgical endoscopy equipment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the SAS is by default subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés 'IS') at the standard rate of 25% on its French-attribute profits, applying the French territoriality principle whereby foreign permanent establishment profits are generally exempt under Article 209-I CGI; no general pass-through regime applies to the SAS.

Available election forms

France does not apply a US-style check-the-box regime. Available permanent elections include: (i) the Article 239 bis AB CGI option for a newly created SAS to be taxed as a transparent partnership under personal income tax for up to five fiscal years; (ii) the Article 223 A CGI election by a French parent SAS for tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) of qualifying French subsidiaries; (iii) the Article 209 A CGI election for worldwide taxation for five years (instead of territoriality); (iv) the VAT franchise option for small businesses. Hybrid mismatch arrangements are restricted: ATAD/ATAD 2 rules transposed in Articles 209 and 212 bis CGI disallow deductions for hybrid financial instruments, hybrid entities and double-dip arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Goods: EUR 91,000 annual taxable turnover for the franchise en base de TVA, with a EUR 101,000 upper limit not to be exceeded; services: EUR 40,000 (upper limit EUR 44,000). A purely exporting medical-device manufacturer should register voluntarily at inception to recover input VAT on components, R&D and overheads; registration is mandatory from the day the threshold is exceeded.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of surgical laparoscopy towers and rigid endoscopes to non-EU destinations are exempt with credit (0% VAT) under Article 262-I-1° CGI provided title and possession are transferred to the non-EU purchaser and the goods physically leave EU customs territory unchanged; intra-EU dispatches are exempt under Article 262 ter CGI where the customer's intra-community VAT number is declared and the recapitulative statement is filed under Article 289 bis CGI; direct services tied to offshore installation, repair or technical support of medical devices supplied to a non-EU B2B recipient may be zero-rated under Article 259 A CGI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation DAU via DELTA H1 ECS, bearing the MRN)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank confirmation of foreign-currency repatriation from the non-EU purchaser)
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List and, if applicable, Certificate of Origin / EUR.1 movement certificate
  • Intra-community sales list / recapitulative statement (Article 289 bis CGI) for EU dispatches

France mandates structured e-invoicing and e-reporting under the 2023 Réforme de la facturation électronique: from 1 September 2026 all VAT-registered businesses must receive electronic invoices, and large/mid-sized entities must issue them through a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenariale (PDP) or the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) using Factur-X / UBL / CII formats via API; SMEs follow on 1 September 2027. Transaction and payment data (e-reporting) is transmitted to the DGFiP in near real-time, complemented by the annual FEC file. For exports, fiscal clearance is electronic through the DELTA H1 (ECS) declaration with a Movement Reference Number (MRN); France does not apply physical fiscal stamping or QR-code clearance, but the DGDDI runs real-time risk scoring with the crédit d'enlèvement procedure for goods leaving French customs territory.

Export proof documentation must be retained for at least 6 years for tax purposes (Article L102 B Livre des procédures fiscales) and for the applicable customs period (Article 65 Code des douanes). Where the 0% rate is claimed on missing, false or manipulated documentation, the DGFiP re-invoices VAT at 20% and applies monthly late-payment interest of 0.20% (Article 1727 CGI); a deliberate misstatement triggers the 40% bad-faith penalty (Article 1729 CGI), or 80% for manœuvres frauduleuses. Criminal customs sanctions (Code des douanes, Articles 414 et seq.) include confiscation of goods, fines of up to twice the goods' value, and imprisonment up to 10 years for organized fraud; joint and several liability applies under Article 283-2 CGI for circular MTIC-type fraud, and flagrant cases are referred to the Commission des Infractions Fiscales for criminal tax prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current-account and capital-account operations. France has accepted Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement and applies the EU free movement of capital regime under TFEU Articles 63-66 and Directive 88/361/EEC. The EUR is freely convertible; no prior administrative authorization applies to inbound or outbound payments, dividend and profit remittance, repayment of shareholder advances, or liquidation distributions. Residual compliance gates are: (1) EU/UN financial sanctions and asset-freeze orders; (2) French AML obligations under CMF Article L561-1 et seq.; (3) Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical declarations for cross-border transactions reaching EUR 750,000 or more per transaction; and (4) the EUR 10,000 cash-movement declaration under CMF Article L152-1 and EU Regulation 2018/1672. Profit remittance from the French SAS to an EU parent is exempt from withholding tax under EU Directive 2011/96/EU where the parent holds at least 10% for 24 months; dividends to non-EU residents suffer a 25% French withholding rate, subject to treaty reduction. No practical hurdle exists for a manufacturing entity with legitimate medical-device operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France eliminated mandatory surrender of export proceeds in 1989 as part of the liberalization of exchange controls. There is no statutory conversion timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. The exporter may retain USD, CNY, or JPY balances in a French or EEA authorized credit institution; the annual statutory accounts must be kept in EUR and foreign-currency balances are revalued at the ECB reference rate at year-end. Only statistical reporting to the Banque de France may apply when the monthly or per-transaction cross-border payment threshold is exceeded.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as the national central bank, exercising foreign-exchange administration jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté Industrielle et Numérique. Monetary policy for the eurozone is delegated to the European Central Bank (ECB). The operative legal source is the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium as the sole bank, acceptable only as a complementary disbursement channel. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Treezor, Bunq, Revolut Business, and Wise Business can operate in France under the EMI passport regime of CMF Article L525-1 et seq. and may open a professional account remotely for a French SAS. However, EMIs are legally and practically unsuitable as the primary treasury bank for this issuer because: (1) they cannot issue or confirm documentary credits under ICC UCP 600 since they lack banking-partner credit-intermediation powers; (2) they act through bank sponsors, inserting an intermediation layer and settlement counterparty into the SWIFT MT103 chain; (3) electronic money floats must be safeguarded, not held freely, making working-capital sweeps less efficient; (4) they generally do not provide the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting service, leaving the statutory declaration burden on the client; (5) foreign-currency accounts and natural FX positions are limited. Recommended structure: BNP Paribas or Société Générale as the relationship bank for LC and wires; a single EMI (e.g., Qonto) for expense cards and low-value disbursements capped at EUR 10,000 per transaction, swept daily to the operational account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder transfers the initial subscription and any subsequent equity contributions via SWIFT MT103 or, for EEA-origin EUR, via SEPA Credit Transfer. The payment must identify the beneficiary as FR_SAS with the remittance reference Souscription au capital social - FR_SAS, and include the ISO 20022 purpose code for equity (e.g., Equi). All funds must be blocked on a dedicated share-capital escrow account (compte séquestre) held with a French credit institution or a notaire until the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce issues the certificate of incorporation or the capital-increase filing is registered. For transactions at or above EUR 750,000, the French receiving bank must report the flow to the Banque de France under the balance-of-payments survey within the statutory monthly deadline; there is no prior-approval requirement. Same-day EUR finality is achieved through TARGET2. Where a non-EU investor acquires control of the French manufacturing entity, an assessment under the French foreign-investment screening regime (CMF Article L151-3; Décret 2019-1590) must be performed; the manufacture of surgical endoscopes and laparoscopy towers generally does not fall within the protected sectors unless the activity includes R&D on critical technologies such as AI-driven surgical imaging or cybersecurity of connected devices, in which case a pre-closing authorization from the DG Trésor may be required.Settlement rails ranked by operational preference: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments to EEA component suppliers, executed through STEP2/TARGET2 with one-business-day finality and no correspondent charges; (2) SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer for hard-currency payments to non-EEA suppliers, notably USD and CNY for Japanese CMOS sensors and German optical modules, with two-day value dating and full BPSS compliance; (3) SWIFT MT700 irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued by the French relationship bank for bulk procurement of 4K CMOS sensors, rigid endoscope lenses, and LED light-source modules where the supplier requires LC-at-sight or 60-day tenor, with confirmation added by the beneficiary's advising bank under ICC UCP 600; (4) documentary collections via MT400 series as a fallback for medium-risk suppliers with an established shipping record; (5) internal disbursement controls requiring dual-director signature authorization for any wire above EUR 10,000 and a 30-day payment calendar aligned with the LC tenor. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium as the sole bank, acceptable only as a complementary disbursement channel. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Treezor, Bunq, Revolut Business, and Wise Business can operate in France under the EMI passport regime of CMF Article L525-1 et seq. and may open a professional account remotely for a French SAS. However, EMIs are legally and practically unsuitable as the primary treasury bank for this issuer because: (1) they cannot issue or confirm documentary credits under ICC UCP 600 since they lack banking-partner credit-intermediation powers; (2) they act through bank sponsors, inserting an intermediation layer and settlement counterparty into the SWIFT MT103 chain; (3) electronic money floats must be safeguarded, not held freely, making working-capital sweeps less efficient; (4) they generally do not provide the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting service, leaving the statutory declaration burden on the client; (5) foreign-currency accounts and natural FX positions are limited. Recommended structure: BNP Paribas or Société Générale as the relationship bank for LC and wires; a single EMI (e.g., Qonto) for expense cards and low-value disbursements capped at EUR 10,000 per transaction, swept daily to the operational account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax rate. A qualifying small company may benefit from a 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to applicable ownership, turnover, and paid-in-capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident. The rate may be reduced or eliminated under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, subject to beneficial-ownership, minimum holding, holding-period, anti-abuse, and other conditions. French domestic participation-exemption rules generally do not themselves eliminate withholding tax on payments to a non-resident shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the annual return is generally due on the second working day after 1 May of the following year. For another year-end, filing is generally due within three months after the year-end. The corporate income tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, or by the 15th day of the fifth month for a 31 December year-end. Quarterly instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer-pricing documentation, including local-file and master-file requirements, generally applies where the French entity or relevant group meets the EUR 400,000,000 statutory threshold based on turnover or gross assets, or where the entity belongs to a group meeting the applicable threshold. A separate annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration generally applies at the EUR 150,000,000 threshold or where the entity belongs to a group meeting that threshold. Currency: EUR.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a standalone regime titled Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to certain offshore jurisdictions. Substance is nevertheless required under French transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership standards, anti-abuse provisions, the principal-purpose test in treaties, controlled-foreign-company rules, interest limitation rules, and the general abuse-of-law doctrine. The French SAS should maintain genuine French functions, personnel, premises, decision-making, assets, risks, regulatory capability, and commercial rationale proportionate to its manufacturing and medical-device activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, administrative penalties generally ranging from 40% to 80% of the evaded tax, and criminal tax-fraud sanctions. Natural persons may face imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, increased in aggravated cases to up to seven years and EUR 3,000,000. Corporate fines may be multiplied under French criminal-law rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur personal criminal liability, tax-solidarity liability for fraudulent or repeated non-payment, civil claims, management bans, and asset seizure. Travel restrictions are not automatic and require a specific legal or judicial basis.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, interest, transfer-pricing documentation penalties, penalties for inadequate or missing documentation, and potential 40% or 80% tax penalties where conduct is deliberate or fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they knowingly approve fraudulent arrangements, obstruct an audit, or organize insolvency. Normal arm's-length adjustments generally remain liabilities of the SAS.
Shell company and artificial substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, withholding-tax reassessment, transfer-pricing adjustment, abuse-of-law recharacterization, interest, and penalties. Transactions may be disregarded or reattributed to the entity performing the actual functions.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for breach of management duties and criminal liability where the structure is used to commit fraud. Corporate separateness is not normally disregarded solely because ownership is foreign.
Beneficial-ownership and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of reduced treaty or EU rates, application of domestic withholding tax, repayment of underwithheld tax, interest, and anti-abuse penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable where they knowingly certify false residence, ownership, or beneficial-ownership facts. The SAS may remain jointly responsible for unpaid withholding tax.
Withholding-tax failure

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, late-payment interest, and applicable filing or tax penalties. Treaty relief may be denied if procedural or substantive requirements are not met.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal liability for deliberate non-remittance or fraudulent withholding arrangements and may be subject to criminal proceedings in serious cases.
Accounting and corporate-record falsification

Statutory sanction: Tax penalties, rejection of accounts, criminal accounting offenses, confiscation, and potential imprisonment and fines under French criminal law.

Director personal liability: Directors and accountants who participate in falsification may be prosecuted personally. Civil claims may include restitution and damages; management bans may apply.
AML, sanctions, and suspicious payment activity

Statutory sanction: Transaction blocking, account restrictions, administrative sanctions, confiscation, criminal fines, and imprisonment depending on the offense. AML reporting failures may trigger professional and regulatory penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal criminal exposure for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or negligent governance. Travel bans may be imposed only by competent authorities under applicable proceedings.
Medical-device regulatory and customs non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Product seizure, recalls, market withdrawal, customs reassessment, administrative fines, suspension of operations, and criminal sanctions for serious unsafe-product or customs offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal criminal or civil liability for knowingly placing non-compliant or unsafe devices on the market. Product-liability exposure may extend to bodily injury and consequential damages.
Undercapitalization and fraudulent insolvency

Statutory sanction: Insolvency proceedings, clawback of transactions, repayment orders, asset recovery, management bans, and possible criminal bankruptcy sanctions.

Director personal liability: Courts may order contribution to insufficient assets, impose personal bankruptcy restrictions, or prohibit management. Corporate veil piercing is exceptional but personal liability can arise from a director's management fault.
Failure to preserve tax and transfer-pricing evidence

Statutory sanction: Documentation penalties, adverse inference, rejection of deductions, transfer-pricing reassessment, withholding-tax exposure, and applicable late-payment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil or administrative liability for systematic governance failures and personal liability where non-preservation is intentional obstruction or part of a fraud.
