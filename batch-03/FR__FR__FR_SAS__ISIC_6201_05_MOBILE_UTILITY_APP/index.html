Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) – Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), with automatic transmission to the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés, RCS) and to INSEE for SIREN/SIRET assignment.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.227-1 to L.227-32 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); Articles L.210-1 et seq. and R.123-1 et seq. (RCS registration); Decree No. 2023-179 of 6 March 2023 establishing the INPI Guichet Unique for business formalities.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de Création d'une Personne Morale (Form M0), filed exclusively through the INPI Guichet Unique; legal existence is evidenced by the Extrait Kbis issued by the RCS greffe.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 15 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SAS has no statutory minimum capital (Article L.227-2, Code de commerce); the Articles freely fix the capital (in practice €1 or more). At least 50% of the nominal value of each cash subscription must be released at incorporation, with the balance payable within 5 years; in-kind contributions must be fully released at formation. Cash funds must be deposited and blocked in a dedicated French bank account or with a notary (attestation de dépôt des fonds) until the RCS delivers the Kbis. 100% non-resident ownership is permitted.

Funding & unlock path: For leased office premises: verify the landlord's fire safety compliance certificate and ERP classification (category 5 if applicable), electrical installation conformity certificate, and compliance with workplace health-and-safety rules under Articles L.4111-1 et seq. of the Code du travail; for remote-first app development, no physical operational clearance is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

French Directorate General of Public Finances (Direction générale des finances publiques, DGFiP), administered through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official impots.gouv.fr portal. Business formation and registration data are submitted through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS obtains its statutory identifiers through the INPI Guichet unique registration filing. The filing generates a SIREN number for the legal entity, a SIRET number for each establishment, and an activity code. The entity applies for corporate income tax and VAT accounts with the competent SIE, generally using the initial tax registration information and the VAT registration request. The French VAT identification number is issued by the DGFiP and normally has the format FR followed by a two-character check key and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity with a French fixed establishment registers through the relevant SIE after obtaining the applicable French establishment identifiers. A non-established foreign taxable person making taxable French supplies must generally register with the Non-Resident Tax Service (Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères) unless the transaction is subject to domestic reverse charge or is reported through an applicable EU One Stop Shop scheme. EU-established businesses may use the Union OSS for qualifying cross-border B2C digital services; OSS does not replace domestic registration for supplies outside its scope.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) by default. An eligible SAS may elect temporarily for income tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, IR) if statutory conditions are satisfied, including qualifying ownership, size, age, activity, and non-listed status requirements; the election is time-limited and must be notified within the statutory period. There is no general US-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules apply to arrangements producing deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes; they operate as statutory restrictions and do not constitute an elective entity-classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a France-established business eligible for the domestic VAT franchise for services, the ordinary prior-year turnover threshold is generally EUR 37,500 and the current-year tolerance threshold is generally EUR 41,250, subject to statutory category rules and legislative change. For sales of goods, the ordinary and tolerance thresholds are generally EUR 85,000 and EUR 93,500. These thresholds do not provide a general exemption for non-established suppliers making taxable French supplies. A supplier established in one EU Member State may generally apply the EUR 10,000 EU-wide threshold for qualifying intra-EU B2C telecommunications, broadcasting, and electronically supplied services, after which destination-country VAT applies; the supplier may elect to apply destination-country taxation from the outset and use OSS.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the EU may be VAT-exempt with the right to deduct input VAT where the supplier proves that the goods left the EU customs territory and satisfies invoicing, accounting, and customs requirements. The exemption is supported by customs exit records, including the electronic export declaration and exit results. Intra-EU supplies of goods may qualify for the separate intra-Community exemption when the customer has a valid VAT number in another Member State, the goods are dispatched or transported to that Member State, and the supplier retains required evidence and reporting. Services supplied to a business customer are generally taxed at the customer's location under the B2B place-of-supply rule and may be reported without French VAT under reverse charge; this is generally a place-of-supply outcome rather than a French zero-rated export. Electronically supplied B2C services are taxed in the customer's Member State under destination-based rules, with OSS available where applicable. Input VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices, a direct business connection, proper accounting, and timely VAT return or refund-claim support.

  • Electronic customs export declaration and customs export movement reference
  • Electronic confirmation that the goods exited the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, and VAT treatment
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, or carrier delivery record
  • Packing list and, where applicable, export licence or other regulatory authorization
  • Proof of payment or commercial settlement where relevant to the audit file
  • For intra-EU supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number, transport evidence, and required EC Sales List reporting
  • For digital services, customer-location evidence, billing records, IP or payment data where relevant, and OSS records if the Union OSS is used

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing for domestic B2B transactions between French-established taxable persons and mandatory electronic transaction reporting for specified cross-border B2B and B2C transactions. The current statutory timetable provides mandatory receipt capability for all businesses from 1 September 2026, mandatory issuance for large and medium-sized businesses from 1 September 2026, and mandatory issuance for small and micro-enterprises from 1 September 2027, subject to any legislative amendments or approved extensions. Invoices will generally be exchanged through an approved private electronic invoicing platform (plateforme agréée) or the public platform framework designated by law; structured data and required transaction fields must be transmitted to the DGFiP. France does not currently operate a universal real-time invoice-clearance requirement for ordinary domestic invoices. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. Electronic invoicing does not generally replace OSS reporting for qualifying cross-border B2C digital services.

France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline of 180 days for export VAT relief. Export evidence must nevertheless be retained and produced to establish that goods actually left the EU and that the VAT exemption conditions were met. If the exemption or deduction is unsupported, the DGFiP may assess the unpaid VAT, deny or claw back input VAT, charge late-payment interest, and impose penalties. The penalty may generally be 10% for certain late or incomplete compliance failures, 40% for deliberate failure or deliberate inaccuracy, and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres, abuse of law, or concealed activity, subject to the applicable facts and statutory provisions. False customs declarations may trigger customs penalties and, in serious cases, criminal proceedings. Statutory limitation periods commonly allow reassessment within three years for VAT, subject to extended periods for fraud, concealed activity, or criminal conduct. A prudent audit file should preserve customs exit evidence, invoices, transport records, customer-status evidence, digital-service location evidence, VAT returns, OSS returns, and payment records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the euro framework (Article 63 TFEU; Directive 88/361/EEC; Articles L151-1 to L165-2 CMF). No prior exchange-control authorization is required for profit repatriation, dividend distributions, or loan repayments by a French SAS to non-resident shareholders; however, dividend and interest outflows may trigger withholding tax (30% default under Article 119 bis CGI, reduced to 5–15% or 0% under applicable DTT and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive) and are reportable on the Banque de France balance-of-payments (BOP) statistical declaration. Foreign direct investment in 'strategic sectors' (Article R151-3 CMF), including dual-use or critical-infrastructure data activities, requires prior approval from the Ministry of Economy; a mobile utility app (ISIC 6201) with no critical-infrastructure nexus does NOT trigger this control. Bank financing from non-EU lenders is subject to arm's-length substance requirements under Article 155-A CGI to prevent recharacterization of interest as deemed dividends, and to automatic BOP reporting thresholds (€15 million direct investment).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds or App Store/Google Play settlement revenues. Receipts may be held in EUR or any freely-convertible foreign currency in resident or non-resident accounts; the only ongoing obligation is statistical declaration to the Banque de France via the bank's automated BOP monitoring system (monthly reporting of cross-border payments, with direct investment transactions requiring a dedicated declaration when exceeding €15 million per calendar year).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) — Direction Générale des Relations Financières avec l'Étranger, operating within the Eurosystem/ESCB framework and coordinating with the European Central Bank and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) on external financial relations and direct investment screening under Articles L151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for day-to-day treasury operations using EU-licensed EMIs and fintech banks (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business Luxembourg, Airwallex via EU passporting), which offer remote onboarding, multi-currency IBANs, and SWIFT/SEPA API rails; however, such EMIs have LOW viability for the mandatory blocked capital escrow account required during SAS incorporation, because the French Commercial Code (Article L227-2 CGI-compliant deposit rules) requires funds to be deposited with a credit institution or a notary (Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations) capable of issuing a legal attestation de dépôt des fonds. Offshore non-EU EMIs (e.g., Singapore/HK-based) have LOW viability as primary settlement banks because Apple and Google payout contracts typically require a Eurozone IBAN, and Banque de France AML expectations favor licensed French/EU institutions for the €10,000+ receipt threshold.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pain.001.001.03) for EU/EEA shareholders, or (ii) SWIFT MT103 (field 70 remittance info, field 72 bank-to-bank) for non-EEA shareholders, with ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (Equity) or 'CAPC' (Capital Contribution) and French BOP narrative 'Apport en capital social – prise de participation directe'. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax on taxable profits. Qualifying small and medium-sized enterprises may benefit from a 15% rate on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profits, subject to ownership, turnover and paid-up-capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic French withholding tax rate on dividends and similar distributions paid to non-residents is generally 25%, subject to exemptions and reduced treaty rates. A qualifying EU parent may obtain exemption under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring a qualifying participation, minimum holding period and anti-abuse compliance. French resident individuals are generally subject to a 12.8% income tax withholding component, separate from social levies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million turnover or gross assets, or specified group, ownership or related-party conditions connected with an entity meeting that threshold, generally trigger the full French Master File and Local File documentation framework under Articles L13 AA and L13 AB of the French Tax Procedures Code. An annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration, Form 2257-SD, generally applies at a lower EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold or where specified related-party or group conditions are met.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a single standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to certain offshore regimes. Substance is enforced through the arm's-length principle, beneficial-ownership requirements, the general anti-abuse rule, the principal-purpose test, controlled-foreign-company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation provisions and permanent-establishment doctrine. A French SAS should maintain real decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational control, premises or operational infrastructure, appropriate capitalization, functional risk control, IP governance and contemporaneous business purpose. An app business should specifically document product development, software ownership, user acquisition, platform agreements, payment flows, customer support and allocation of Apple and Google platform fees.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax filings

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment, late-payment interest, a 40% penalty for deliberate conduct and an 80% penalty for fraudulent conduct or certain abusive arrangements. Criminal tax fraud may carry imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000; aggravated cases may carry up to 7 years and EUR 3 million, with higher statutory limits in specified circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors or de facto managers may incur civil tax liability under Article L267 of the French Tax Procedures Code where serious and repeated misconduct makes collection impossible. Criminal liability may include imprisonment, fines, confiscation, management bans and professional disqualification. A travel ban is not automatic but may be ordered by a court or investigative authority where legally justified.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and disguised distribution

Statutory sanction: Taxable-income adjustment, denial of deductions, withholding tax on recharacterized distributions, late-payment interest, 40% or 80% penalties depending on conduct, and transfer-pricing documentation penalties where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability for breach of management duties, tax recovery proceedings in cases of serious and repeated misconduct, and criminal exposure if the arrangements form part of intentional tax fraud.
Shell company, fictitious substance and permanent-establishment concealment

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the entity or arrangements, denial of treaty benefits, French permanent-establishment assessment, corporate tax reassessment, withholding tax, VAT consequences, interest and aggravated anti-abuse penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil and criminal liability for fraudulent use of the company, fictitious accounting or tax evasion. Courts may order management bans, asset confiscation and imprisonment where the underlying conduct constitutes an offence. A shell company is not independently unlawful if it is dormant and accurately disclosed, but false substance representations are high-risk.
Anti-money laundering and beneficial-ownership violations

Statutory sanction: Corrective orders, administrative fines, criminal penalties for laundering or related offences, confiscation of proceeds and enhanced penalties where organized crime or aggravated circumstances apply. Beneficial-owner registration failures may result in formal notices, fines and restrictions on corporate filings.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be personally prosecuted for knowing participation, concealment or facilitation. Sanctions can include imprisonment, fines, confiscation and management disqualification. Travel restrictions may be imposed only through applicable judicial or investigative procedures.
VAT and digital-platform reporting failures

Statutory sanction: VAT assessment, interest, fixed or percentage-based penalties, denial of input VAT, joint-liability exposure in specified fraud situations and possible criminal sanctions for deliberate VAT fraud.

Director personal liability: Personal liability can arise for intentional fraud, serious management misconduct or obstruction of tax collection. Directors may also face criminal prosecution where VAT evasion is deliberate and material.
Corporate distributions and unlawful profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: Restatement as unlawful distribution or hidden distribution, repayment claims, withholding tax assessment, interest, penalties, possible breach of French corporate-law rules and, in serious cases, misuse-of-corporate-assets or bankruptcy-related offences.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to restore improperly distributed assets and may be personally liable for management misconduct. Intentional misuse of company assets can result in imprisonment, fines, management bans and disqualification.
Accounting, record-retention and obstruction violations

Statutory sanction: Rejection of accounts, assessment based on external evidence, non-cooperation penalties, tax reassessment, interest, aggravated penalties and criminal sanctions for forgery, obstruction or fraudulent accounting.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally prosecuted for falsification, obstruction or deliberate destruction of evidence and may face fines, imprisonment, confiscation and management disqualification.
