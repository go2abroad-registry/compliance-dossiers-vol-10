Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet unique operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), transmitted to the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce for registration in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.227-1 to L.227-44 (SAS); L.210-1 to L.210-10 (general company law); L.210-6 (legal personality from registration); associated R.123 registry filing provisions.

Formation / constitutional filings

Articles of Association (Statuts) and the standard RCS incorporation dossier (online M0 form via the formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr one-stop portal).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 12 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The SAS has no legislated minimum share capital. The Statuts must fix a capital that is fully subscribed at incorporation. For cash contributions, at least 50% of the nominal amount must be paid up on subscription; the remaining amount may be called within five years. Paid-up cash contributions must be evidenced by a bank deposit certificate or notary sequestration until registration.

Funding & unlock path: Workplace fire-safety certification covering fire extinguishers, emergency exits, alarm systems and electrical installations; structural load verification for racking or mezzanines; occupational health-and-safety checklist under the French Labour Code, including prevention of musculoskeletal risks and access for disabled persons where the premises are open to staff or third parties.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP). The principal online portal is impots.gouv.fr; foreign businesses generally deal with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) for French tax registration and VAT matters.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the applicable company-formation formalities and receives a SIREN and SIRET from the national business-registration system; it must then complete tax formalities with the DGFiP and obtain a French VAT identification number where required. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in France should apply to the SIEE for VAT registration before commencing those supplies, providing evidence of legal existence, constitutional documents, business activities, identity and authority of representatives, and details of French taxable transactions. A non-EU-established business generally must appoint a French fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual-assistance arrangement or statutory exception removes that requirement. EU-established businesses generally do not require such a representative. A VAT number does not itself establish a permanent establishment or determine corporate-tax residence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Eligible qualifying small companies may elect temporarily for taxation under the personal income-tax regime, subject to statutory conditions, shareholder and activity tests, and a limited election period. This is not a US-style check-the-box election. France has no general domestic check-the-box mechanism for classifying foreign entities; foreign-entity treatment follows French tax-law characterization and applicable treaty rules. Hybrid mismatch rules may deny deductions or require inclusion under applicable French and EU anti-hybrid legislation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,000 annual domestic turnover for supplies of goods and certain comparable activities, and EUR 37,500 for services under the domestic small-business VAT exemption thresholds, subject to statutory conditions and applicable tolerance thresholds. These thresholds generally do not protect a non-established business making taxable supplies in France: registration and VAT accounting may be required from the first taxable transaction. Special EU cross-border SME arrangements and transaction-specific rules may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to a destination outside the EU may be exempt with deduction (effectively 0% VAT) where the supplier establishes that the goods were dispatched or transported outside the EU and satisfies documentary and invoicing requirements. Intra-EU supplies are governed by separate rules and generally require dispatch to another Member State, a valid customer VAT identification number, and supporting evidence. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: place-of-supply rules generally determine treatment, with B2B services commonly taxed where the business customer is established and statutory exceptions applying. Returned-goods logistics require transaction-specific analysis; a return or re-export does not by itself establish eligibility for export exemption or import relief.

  • French/EU export customs declaration and customs-office export certification or equivalent electronic exit confirmation
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and records identifying the goods and linking the shipment to the sale or return transaction
  • Transport evidence, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier delivery and exit records
  • Customer order, contract, return authorization, and warehouse or inventory records supporting the goods' movement and ownership
  • Banking or payment records where relevant to substantiate the transaction; an Inward Remittance Certificate is not a generally prescribed French export-VAT document

France does not generally operate real-time VAT invoice clearance or mandatory digital stamping. Domestic B2B e-invoicing is being phased in: from 1 September 2026, all businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices, while large and intermediate-sized businesses must issue them; SMEs and micro-enterprises are scheduled to issue from 1 September 2027. In-scope invoices must use an authorized platform and compliant formats. E-reporting applies to specified transactions, including relevant B2C and cross-border transactions, under the phased implementation timetable. Foreign businesses should verify whether their French transactions fall within the domestic mandate.

France imposes no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The zero value above denotes that no 180-day or other general deadline applies, not a required repatriation period. Unsupported or false export exemption claims may result in reassessment of VAT, late-payment interest, and tax penalties; penalties depend on the nature, amount, and intent of the breach and may be higher for deliberate or fraudulent conduct. Input VAT deductions and refunds may also be denied or recovered. Customs and tax records should be retained under applicable French statutory requirements and produced on audit.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France grants full legal convertibility for current and capital transactions; the EUR is freely transferable and no prior central-bank approval applies to equity subscriptions, dividend remittances, or liquidation proceeds. The French foreign-investment screening regime (Decree No. 2019-1590, Articles L151-3 et seq. CMF) covers only strategic sectors, and ISIC 5229 reverse logistics is outside the sensitive list, so no prior investment authorisation is required. Practical profit-remittance hurdles are documentary: positive distributable reserves, a valid corporate distribution resolution, FATCA/CRS self-certification, and the applicable non-resident dividend withholding tax (25–30%) unless reduced by tax treaty or exempted under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (parent holding ≥10% for at least 24 months).

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender or conversion ratio; foreign-currency receivables may be retained in onshore devisen-denominated accounts without central-bank instruction. Statutory reporting alone binds the operator: any resident entity with aggregate cross-border transfers (receipts and payments) of at least EUR 750,000 in a calendar month must electronically declare them to the Banque de France through the MERIDIA channel within 20 business days after month-end (Articles L.141-1 to L.165-2 CMF). Returned-merchandise reversal flows collected from foreign merchants are coded as services or re-export flows for balance-of-payments statistics; no forced conversion applies to such receipts.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) is the central monetary and exchange-control authority; statistical surveillance of cross-border capital movements is administered jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the Code monétaire et financier and Articles 63–66 TFEU (free movement of capital).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility as an ancillary payment and wiring rail, Medium as the sole treasury custodian. Airwallex (expressly targeted as a payment rail) operates under an EU electronic-money institution licence (Bank of Lithuania as home supervisor), passported into France under PSD2/EMD2 and supervised by the ACPR for payment services; it offers multi-currency SWIFT, SEPA and Local ACH rails with segregated client funds and competitive FX pricing. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a French credit institution and cannot provide a regulated capital escrow (compte séquestre), payroll/VAT-linked settlement with full banking liability, overdraft facilities, or treasury credit; French tax practice requires the payroll/VAT account to be held at an ACPR-licensed credit institution. Optimal architecture: Airwallex for cross-border supplier, merchant and refund payments, and a tier-1 French bank for statutory escrow, share-capital and tax settlement accounts.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Opacity of non-resident ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO chain through foreign holding vehicles or trusts)

Defense evidence: Current RBE extract (déclaration des bénéficiaires effectifs), certified corporate ownership and tracing chart, passport copies and three-month proof of address of each natural-person UBO, statutory declaration that no nominee/fiduciary arrangement exists, and, if a trust or fund sits in the chain, the trust deed, register of beneficiaries and the reporting required by Article 1649-AB CGI.
Sanctions or integrity screening hits (OFAC, EU restrictive measures, Russian/Ukrainian sanctions lists) on a non-resident director or shareholder with a common name

Root cause: Sanctions or integrity screening hits (OFAC, EU restrictive measures, Russian/Ukrainian sanctions lists) on a non-resident director or shareholder with a common name

Defense evidence: Full identity set (passport, national identity card), utility bills covering the last six months, a certified sanctions-screening report from a recognised data provider (World-Check/Refinitiv), an explanatory letter, and confirmation that neither the beneficial owners nor the transaction counterparties are designated persons, enabling the bank's compliance committee to close the false-positive match.
Source-of-funds opacity for an inbound equity injection aggregating EUR 750,000 or more in one calendar month

Root cause: Source-of-funds opacity for an inbound equity injection aggregating EUR 750,000 or more in one calendar month

Defense evidence: Six months of bank statements of the remitting shareholder, the board-approved or notarised capital-subscription resolution, proof of the upstream liquidation event (if the shareholder is a holding vehicle), the investor's certificate of tax residence, and the Banque de France MERIDIA balance-of-payments declaration receipt evidencing that the transfer was reported.
Perceived high-risk activity: reverse logistics of returned merchandise possibly associated with counterfeit goods, stolen inventory or IP infringement

Root cause: Perceived high-risk activity: reverse logistics of returned merchandise possibly associated with counterfeit goods, stolen inventory or IP infringement

Defense evidence: Brand-authorised reverse-logistics agreements (marketplace and merchant contracts), the SAS's EORI and French customs registrations, VAT registration certificate, RMA traceability workflow, anti-counterfeiting and goods-disposal SOPs, insurance certificates, K-bis description confirming ISIC 5229, and brand licenses covering quality grading and refurbishment.
PEP status or inconsistent tax domiciliation of a non-resident manager or beneficial owner

Root cause: PEP status or inconsistent tax domiciliation of a non-resident manager or beneficial owner

Defense evidence: Signed PEP self-declaration of each manager and UBO, French tax-residence certificate (attestation de résidence fiscale) or Article 1975-A CGI certificate, evidence of substance in France (registered office, lease, payroll/URSSAF account, operational warehouse), and proof of genuine economic activity reconciling the manager's tax residency with the SAS's management seat.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity requires no FX authorisation: the non-resident shareholder transmits EUR via SWIFT MT103/MT202 (TARGET2 euro clearing) to the SAS's euro account at a French tier-1 bank. The bank will credit the funds as share capital only after verifying the share-subscription resolution, updated articles and K-bis, RBE extract and remitter identity; funds intended as equity must not be routed as shareholder current-account flows. The SAS must file the Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration via MERIDIA when the aggregate monthly external flow equals or exceeds EUR 750,000, using the inward-direct-investment transaction code and the ISIC 5229 activity tag; the wire's remittance-information field should reference 'CAPITAL_SUBSCRIPTION_FR_SAS_ISIC5229_[DATE]_[EUR]' and the remitter's LEI.Treasury clearing stack by counterparty geography: (1) intra-EEA suppliers, merchants and carriers settle via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant on the EPC clearing perimeter, using the French IBAN with structured remittance references (invoice and RMA number); (2) non-EEA and non-EUR payments (United Kingdom, Hong Kong, United States, China) settle via SWIFT MT103 (gpi with UETR) through the French correspondent-banking network for freight, grading fees and merchant refunds; (3) large-scale re-export of graded returned goods settles under commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) or documentary collections (URC 522) with inspection certificates and customs declarations (EXA/Intrastat). Every outward transaction must be supported by a commercial invoice bearing the ISIC 5229 service description and must be included in the monthly Banque de France declaration where the EUR 750,000 aggregate threshold is breached; goods flows are captured in Intrastat declarations. Consumer refunds are executed exclusively through SEPA mandates with reverse-logistics refund reference numbers, never via cash or bearer instruments.Deliverable foreign-exchange forward contracts (change à terme) in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP and EUR/CNH provided by French credit institutions under the FBF/ISDA master agreement; no product-level authorisation is required, but EMIR margin rules apply to non-financial counterparties above the relevant clearing thresholds.Non-deliverable forwards (NDF) as permitted overlays for restricted or illiquid currencies (e.g., CNY, INR) where onshore physical delivery is unavailable; the NDF is cash-settled in EUR and may hedge reverse-logistics collection and refund flows only where matched to an identifiable cross-border settlement exposure.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD and EUR/CNH) aligning external treasury exposures (capital injection, long-term receivables, repatriation programmes) to EUR liabilities; permitted with tier-1 EU banks under the ISDA/FBF protocol, with variation margin collateralised where the aggregate notional exceeds EMIR thresholds; physically settled FX swaps are excluded from the EMIR clearing obligation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2022; reduced 15% rate applies to qualifying SMEs on the first €42,500 of taxable profit if turnover < €10 million and capital held >=75% by individuals or qualifying entities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% standard domestic withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents (Article 119 bis CGI). EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption applies for EU parent companies holding >=10% of SAS for at least 2 years; treaty rates may reduce to 0-15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 3 months of fiscal year-end. For a 31 December year-end, the deadline is the second working day following 1 May of year N+1. Balance of CIT due by 15 May (or the 15th of the fourth month following year-end) for 31 December year-end; advance payments due quarterly.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed simultaneously with the CIT return 2065, within 3 months of fiscal year-end (second working day after 1 May for 31 December year-end).

Section 1 - Group identification Name, country, and tax ID of ultimate parent and principal group entities. French SAS reports foreign parent details. Foreign parent does not file 2257 unless it has a French PE. Failure to file 2257-SD: €150,000 fine (Article 223 quinquies B CGI). Failure to provide TP documentation upon audit: €10,000 per fiscal year; €50,000 for failure to respond to formal request within 30 days. Reassessment of profits with 40% penalty for abuse.
Section 2 - Intercompany transactions Nature, amount, and counterparties of controlled transactions (services, goods, royalties, interest). Disclose all transactions between SAS and foreign related parties, including reverse logistics service fees and cost recharges. Failure to file 2257-SD: €150,000 fine (Article 223 quinquies B CGI). Failure to provide TP documentation upon audit: €10,000 per fiscal year; €50,000 for failure to respond to formal request within 30 days. Reassessment of profits with 40% penalty for abuse.
Section 3 - Transfer pricing methods Selected OECD method (CUP, resale price, cost plus, TNMM, profit split) and justification. Non-resident parent's TP policy should align; French SAS must document its own functional profile. Failure to file 2257-SD: €150,000 fine (Article 223 quinquies B CGI). Failure to provide TP documentation upon audit: €10,000 per fiscal year; €50,000 for failure to respond to formal request within 30 days. Reassessment of profits with 40% penalty for abuse.
Section 4 - Documentation availability Confirmation that Master File and Local File are available within 30 days of tax audit request. Foreign parent must cooperate to provide group Master File; French SAS retains Local File. Failure to file 2257-SD: €150,000 fine (Article 223 quinquies B CGI). Failure to provide TP documentation upon audit: €10,000 per fiscal year; €50,000 for failure to respond to formal request within 30 days. Reassessment of profits with 40% penalty for abuse.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File mandatory for legal entities or groups with annual turnover or gross assets >= €400 million. Annual transfer pricing declaration (form 2257-SD) mandatory for entities with turnover >= €50 million. Currency: EUR.
