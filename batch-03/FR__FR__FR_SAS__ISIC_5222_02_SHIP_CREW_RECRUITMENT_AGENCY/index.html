Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office (bureau en domiciliation) is generally permissible for the registered office of a French SAS, subject to the provision of a statutory domiciliation contract and compliance with the 'Option 4' domiciliation rules under Article L123-11-2 of the French Commercial Code. However, for the maritime crew recruitment agency scope (ISIC 5222), the French Directorate of Maritime Affairs (Direction des Affaires Maritimes) will, in the exercise of its licensing power for 'recruitment and placement of seafarers', typically require demonstrable physical operational presence in France, including an office suite for seafarer interviews and document handling. Therefore, virtual office status is only recommendable as a temporary initial seat, not as a permanent substantive presence for the licensed activity.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SAS has no statutory minimum capital under French law; however, share subscription must be in full and paid in at least 1 EUR per share. Funds may be held in escrow or a blocked bank account until the company is duly registered and the RCS certificate has been issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and structural load inspections must be obtained from the local fire safety/prevention commission for the office premises; if the office is located in a building open to the public, an access to work certificate is required. In all cases, the employer must obtain a workplace clearance from CARSAT and perform risk assessments for repeated movements and visual ergonomics, and fire drill records must be retained.

Paid-in capital rule: SAS has no statutory minimum capital under French law; however, share subscription must be in full and paid in at least 1 EUR per share. Funds may be held in escrow or a blocked bank account until the company is duly registered and the RCS certificate has been issued.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies a liberal foreign-exchange regime. The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions, tax, customs, and statistical-reporting requirements. A French SAS may generally receive foreign capital, pay foreign suppliers, remit dividends, repay shareholder loans, and transfer profits abroad without a general central-bank approval or mandatory conversion into euros. Profit distributions remain subject to French corporate-law requirements, including approval of annual accounts, availability of distributable reserves, preservation of share capital, and applicable withholding-tax, treaty, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse rules. Banks may require evidence of the underlying transaction, source of funds, tax residence, ultimate beneficial ownership, corporate approvals, and compliance with sanctions screening. Foreign direct investment screening may apply to a non-EU investment in a sensitive French sector; ordinary maritime crew recruitment and staffing activities are not automatically within a sensitive sector, but the precise activity, technology, data, defense, port, or critical-infrastructure exposure must be reviewed before closing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement for exporters to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, no mandatory export-receipt conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring conversion of foreign-currency receipts into euros. Export receipts may remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's discretion, subject to bank documentation, sanctions controls, tax accounting, customs evidence, and applicable Banque de France or European statistical reporting. Banks may request invoices, contracts, bills of lading, crew-management agreements, customer identification, and proof of services for compliance and transaction-monitoring purposes. Any reporting obligation is transaction- or institution-specific and should not be confused with a currency-surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank framework; foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments reporting is supplemented by the French Treasury Directorate-General (Direction générale du Trésor), the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR), and applicable European Union regulations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment accounts, foreign-currency collections, payroll disbursements, and low-to-medium-volume cross-border transfers; low as a complete substitute for a French relationship bank where the SAS requires capital deposit certification, domestic credit facilities, commercial letters of credit, maritime-sector underwriting, or complex trade documentation. An offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to French customers unless it is authorised in the European Economic Area or operates through a valid passporting or local-authorisation arrangement. The provider must be an authorised electronic-money institution, payment institution, bank, or permitted agent. Client-money safeguarding, AML/KYC, sanctions screening, PSD2 requirements, operational-resilience rules, data-protection obligations, transaction limits, correspondent-bank dependencies, and account-closure risk must be assessed. An EMI account should not be represented as a French bank deposit account unless the provider is a licensed bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Non-resident or multi-layered ownership structure, nominee shareholders, unexplained holding companies, or inability to identify every ultimate beneficial owner.

Defense evidence: Current articles of association; certificate of incorporation and registry extract; complete ownership chart to natural persons; beneficial-owner declaration; passports and residential-address evidence for directors, signatories, shareholders, and ultimate beneficial owners; board or shareholder resolutions; explanation of the ownership rationale; tax-residence information; and certified translations where requested.

Root cause: Unclear source of initial capital, shareholder loans, or subsequent capital injections.

Defense evidence: Bank statements showing accumulation of funds; audited or management accounts; shareholder net-worth and funding declaration; executed capital-increase or shareholder-loan agreement; board and shareholder approvals; transfer instructions identifying the remitter; tax filings or financial statements; and evidence that the remitting account is controlled by the disclosed investor.

Large or repetitive incoming payments that do not match declared revenue, invoices, or contractual cash-flow expectations.

Root cause: Large or repetitive incoming payments that do not match declared revenue, invoices, or contractual cash-flow expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route capital to an account held in the exact legal name of the French SAS through a regulated bank or authorised payment institution. For formation capital, use the selected bank's capital-deposit account and obtain the deposit certificate required for registration; release funds to the operational account after incorporation and satisfaction of the bank's conditions. For a subsequent equity injection, use a documented capital increase, share-premium contribution, or other legally characterised contribution approved by the competent corporate body. For shareholder debt, execute a written loan agreement and classify the transfer consistently in the accounting records. Use SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008 through a regulated correspondent chain, with the correct ordering customer, beneficiary, ultimate beneficial owner where required, and transparent payment narrative. France has no universal FX purpose-code regime for ordinary capital transfers; identify the transfer as equity contribution, share premium, shareholder loan, or other legally accurate purpose and provide the corporate resolution, investment agreement, source-of-funds evidence, remitter ownership evidence, and tax documentation requested by the receiving bank. Verify receipt against the sending-bank confirmation, reconcile bank charges and FX conversion, and retain records for AML, accounting, tax, and statistical-reporting purposes.Use SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area; use SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 cross-border payment messages for non-SEPA or foreign-currency payments. For supplier, shipowner, and crewing-service settlements, retain the underlying contract, invoice, purchase order, vessel or crew reference, beneficiary verification, sanctions-screening result, and payment approval. Commercial letters of credit may be issued, advised, confirmed, amended, or settled through the banking network using documentary-credit messaging, including SWIFT MT700 for issuance and applicable presentation, amendment, and settlement messages; the bank will apply the governing UCP 600 terms where incorporated and may require collateral or credit capacity. Documentary collections, open-account payments, bank guarantees, and prepayment may be used subject to counterparty, sanctions, fraud, and trade-document controls. Payroll payments should be made from the SAS account using validated beneficiary data, documented employment or service records, and a controlled multi-approval process. Apply EU and French sanctions, AML, tax, employment, VAT, and accounting requirements to every payment; do not split transactions to avoid bank controls or reporting thresholds.Deliverable forward exchange contracts with an authorised bank or regulated investment firm, supported by an identifiable underlying exposure, documented hedge purpose, approved counterparty limit, maturity schedule, valuation, collateral terms where applicable, and accounting treatment.Currency swaps and cross-currency swaps with an authorised bank or regulated investment firm for documented funding, liquidity, or currency-mismatch management, subject to counterparty credit limits, EMIR obligations where applicable, confirmation, collateral, valuation, and reporting requirements.Non-deliverable forwards may be used for currencies subject to local non-deliverability or settlement restrictions, provided the instrument is legally available in the relevant currency, entered into with an authorised counterparty, and supported by documented exposure, ISDA or equivalent terms, EMIR compliance, and applicable market-conduct controls.Foreign-exchange options and collars may be used for hedging identifiable receivables, payables, payroll, or funding exposures, subject to suitability, governance, premium and valuation controls, and the applicable MiFID II, EMIR, accounting, and disclosure requirements.Spot foreign-exchange conversions through regulated banks or payment institutions for ordinary operational settlement; maintain a written treasury policy distinguishing hedging from speculative trading and prohibiting unapproved leveraged or naked positions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 50 million annual net turnover or gross fixed assets for the French entity triggering Local File obligation; EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue for Master File obligation.

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime; however, the general abuse-of-law doctrine (Article L.64 of the Book of Tax Procedures) and anti-fragmentation rules require actual business substance, fixed place of business, and management control. A company organized purely for treaty-shopping or tax avoidance will be re-characterized with severe penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of up to €75,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (increased to €100,000 and 7 years for aggravated fraud, organised gang, or concealment); civil tax penalty of 80% of the evaded tax plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted personally, subject to travel bans, asset seizure, and disqualification; where the corporate veil is pierced, they face civil restitution for the tax deficiency and damages.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative adjustment with a 5% fine on the reassessed transfer-pricing adjustment (capped at €100,000) for missing documentation; 40% negligence penalty or 80% concealment penalty plus 0.20% monthly interest on the tax re-assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve artificial pricing may be subject to criminal conspiracy charges, personal fines, and civil liability; travel bans and seizure of assets may be imposed where fraud is established.
Lack of Economic Substance / Tax Abuse (Abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of the arrangement under Article L.64 with an 80% penalty; the arrangement may be set aside and taxes recalculated; in criminal cases, up to €50,000 fine and 5 years imprisonment for abuse in fraudulent intent.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for the tax consequences, and may be subject to criminal sanctions, personal bankruptcy, travel restrictions, and management disqualification.
AML Compliance Failure (Anti-Money-Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines by ACPR up to €5 million or, if greater, twice the benefit derived; criminal penalties under the Monetary and Financial Code: up to 5 years' imprisonment and €375,000 fine for natural persons, and up to €1 million fine for legal persons.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal prosecution, personal fines, asset seizure, travel bans, and prohibition from managing financial institutions; civil liability for damages to third parties.
