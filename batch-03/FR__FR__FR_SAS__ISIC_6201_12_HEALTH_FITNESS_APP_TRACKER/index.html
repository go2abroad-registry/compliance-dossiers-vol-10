UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: Code monétaire et financier, Articles L.561-2-2, L.561-4-1, L.561-5, L.561-5-1, L.561-6, L.561-10, L.561-15, L.561-32 and L.561-46; Articles R.561-1 to R.561-22 and R.561-55 to R.561-63; Code de commerce, Articles L.233-3 and R.123-123; Décret n° 2024-951 du 23 octobre 2024 relating to the register of beneficial owners and the national register of enterprises; Directive (EU) 2015/849, as amended by Directive (EU) 2018/843, as implemented in French law. A SAS must identify and declare each natural person who ultimately owns or controls it, including persons holding directly or indirectly more than 25% of capital or voting rights and persons exercising control by other means; if no such person can be identified, the senior managing official is reported under the statutory fallback rule.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A SAS must have a registered office in France under Code civil Article 1837 and Code de commerce Articles L.123-11 and following. Domiciliation with an authorized commercial domiciliation company is generally permitted if the provider is authorized and the domiciliation agreement satisfies statutory requirements. A director's personal residence may be used subject to applicable statutory conditions and lease, condominium, planning, and landlord restrictions. A virtual office is not sufficient if it is merely a mail-forwarding address without a valid domiciliation arrangement. For a fitness biometric tracking and wellness telemetry application, a virtual office may be acceptable for incorporation and ordinary corporate administration, but it is inadequate as the sole operational substance where the company performs research and development, employs personnel, stores equipment, processes health-related data, contracts with healthcare or payment partners, or requires regulatory, banking, tax, or consumer-facing verification. The company should maintain accessible books and records, a genuine management location, documented decision-making, appropriate cybersecurity controls, and operational evidence proportionate to activity.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS; the articles may fix capital at €1 or higher. For cash contributions, at least 50% of the nominal amount must be paid up on incorporation, with the balance payable within 5 years; in-kind contributions must be fully released at formation. Funds are typically deposited in a French bank account or with a notary in escrow, but no statutory escrow account is mandatory.

Funding & unlock path: No separate civil-defense permit is required for a non-industrial service company. If a dedicated office or laboratory space is leased, the employer must comply with fire-safety standards, electrical installation conformity, and general workplace health-and-safety requirements; a local fire-certificate may be required for any space open to the public, but not for a typical office.

Exports of goods outside the EU are zero-rated under Article 262-I CGI; B2B digital services are outside French VAT where the recipient is outside France (Article 259 A CGI), subject to proof of customer establishment and commercial documentation; B2C digital services for foreign customers must be verified to avoid French VAT exposure.

  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Airway Bill evidencing departure from the EU

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully convertible currency regime for both current-account and capital-account transactions in accordance with the EU free movement of capital under TFEU Article 63. There is no exchange-control licensing, no surrender requirement, and no prior authorisation for ordinary profit remittances, dividends, royalties, trade payments, or cross-border capital movements to EU and non-EU counterparties. Residual obligations consist of statistical balance-of-payments reporting by French banks to the Banque de France, mandatory declaration of fund transfers exceeding EUR 10,000 between France and non-EU countries (CMF Articles R. 152-1 to R. 152-3), and annual tax reporting of foreign bank accounts and foreign financial assets (Ficoba/IFU forms). Profit repatriation by a French SAS faces no material legal hurdle beyond standard AML-KYC verification and the routing of non-EU payments through an authorized financial intermediary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export proceeds in France or the Eurozone; export receivables may be retained in foreign currency, held in authorised multi-currency accounts, or converted at freely negotiated market rates. For a health-and-fitness digital app, revenue settlements from Apple App Store and Google Play are classified as service export or platform-settlement flows, not physical export goods, and are therefore outside any merchandise export surrender framework. Compliance requirements are limited to: (i) documentation and declaration of any outward transfer above EUR 10,000 to a non-EU destination; (ii) filing of internal corporate accounting entries reflecting unrealised FX gains and losses under IFRS 9; and (iii) maintaining accurate source documentation for the platform settlements supporting tax and transfer-pricing positions.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for eurozone monetary policy; together with the Direction Generale du Tresor (DG Tresor) of the French Ministry of Finance and the Autorite de Controle Prudentiel et de Resolution (ACPR) for prudential supervision and enforcement of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monetaire et Financier, CMF), notably Articles L. 611-1, R. 151-1 et seq. and R. 152-1 et seq.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium. Fully authorised EU electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions such as Wise, Revolut Business, Qonto, and Shine operate in France either under an ACPR licence or an EU passport in accordance with PSD2/PSD3, and they are feasible for day-to-day operational disbursement, staff expenses, and multi-currency FX conversion. However, an EMI is not legally qualified to hold blocked share capital for a French SAS and cannot issue the mandatory certificat de depot de fonds or attestation de blocage required for incorporation purposes, nor can it act as an issuing or confirming bank for documentary trade instruments such as commercial letters of credit. The practical blueprint therefore reserves the capital-escrow and trade-finance layer to a tier-1 credit institution and uses the EMI exclusively as a secondary operating rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions are routed via SWIFT MT103 (for non-SEPA or foreign-currency transfers) or SEPA Credit Transfer (ISO 20022 pacs.008.001.02) when the paying bank and beneficiary are both in EUR with SEPA reachability. The transfer must credit the French IBAN of the blocked capital-escrow account and must carry the applicable Banque de France nature-of-payment code for equity subscription or capital contribution, together with the full legal name of the beneficiary FR_SAS and the UBO identifiers required by the receiving bank. For transfers from non-EU territories equal to or exceeding EUR 10,000, the instruction must reference the mandatory declaration de transfert de fonds for Banque de France statistics. The receiving institution will retain the funds in the escrow account and issue the attestation de depot de fonds or certificat du depositaire required for registration of the SAS; funds may be released only upon presentation of the duly registered Kbis and statuts of the incorporated entity. Verification protocols include EU/OFAC/UN sanctions filtering, intermediary-bank KYC, fraud screening, and validation of the underlying subscription contract before final crediting.The clearing architecture comprises four layers: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EUR-denominated operating disbursements within the EU; (ii) SWIFT MT103 standard customer credit transfers and MT202 beneficiary transfers for high-value or extra-EEA payments, populated with the required purpose code and full remittance information in field 70; (iii) documentary trade instruments issued through the relationship bank's trade-finance desk, principally irrevocable at-sight commercial letters of credit under UCP 600 and standby letters of credit used for international procurement of hardware and third-party manufacturing services; and (iv) euro high-value clearing via the Target2/T2 settlement infrastructure operated through Banque de France for any treasury liquidity repositioning.
