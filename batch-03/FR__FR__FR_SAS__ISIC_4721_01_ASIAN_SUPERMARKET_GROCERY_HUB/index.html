Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a eurozone member with fully convertible current and capital account operations. There are no general foreign-exchange controls on current transactions, including profit remittances by non-resident shareholders, provided the distribution complies with French company law and the applicable tax residence/tax treaty requirements. Capital movements are free within the EU and subject only to EU anti-money-laundering and national security screening for foreign direct investment. Profit remittances are not subject to exchange-control approval, but French withholding tax may apply on dividends unless reduced or eliminated by the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or a bilateral tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement under French or EU law. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted to euro freely. Reporting obligations are limited to Bank of France balance-of-payments surveys when monthly transactions exceed EUR 50,000 in aggregate, and to customs/statistical declarations for intra-EU and third-country goods movements.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting under the European Central Bank and the eurosystem framework, with foreign-exchange regulatory authority delegated under the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Electronic-money institutions with a valid French or EEA passport (e.g., Qonto, Shine, or similar licensed EMIs) may provide agile operational EUR accounts and online payment rails. However, they are legally prohibited from offering credit or deposit products, and their capacity for non-EU remittance clearing and documentary trade finance is limited. For cross-border Asian supplier settlements and LC operations, a tier-1 credit institution is mandatory.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard CIT; 15% reduced rate on first €42,500 taxable profit for SMEs (group turnover < €10M); 3.3% social contribution on CIT exceeding €763,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% domestic withholding tax for non-resident corporations; 30% flat withholding for non-resident individuals; 0% under EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if holding ≥10% for ≥24 months; treaty rates commonly 0–15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal years ending 31 Dec: file by the second working day after 1 May. For other fiscal years: file within three months of the close of the period, unless administrative extension is granted.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Transfer-pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) is mandatory only if the French entity shows annual turnover (excl. VAT) ≥ €400 million or total balance sheet ≥ €40 million. Smaller entities must nevertheless complete the annual simplified transfer-pricing return (form n°2257-A-SD).

Economic substance scope: France has no dedicated domestic 'Economic Substance Regulations' but applies ATAD/anti-abuse principles and 'abus de droit' (Art. L.64 of LPF). The SAS must have real commercial substance: local inventory, sales staff, procurement decisions, and operational risk in France. A paper presence with outsourced management and nominal cash registers can be recharacterized as abuse and the tax base ignored.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Tax recapture plus late interest (0.20% per month) and penalties up to 40% (bad faith) or 80% (fraud); criminal fine up to €500,000/seven years imprisonment under Article 1741 CGI.

Director personal liability: The SAS president may be criminally sanctioned for 'abus de bien social' (Art. L.242-6 C. com.): five years imprisonment, €375,000 fine, and civil restitution to the company; under tax law the director may be jointly liable for penalties under a finding of false accounting.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Application of Art. L.64 LPF to disregard the arrangement and re-tax the French entity on re-determined profit; 40%/80% penalty; DAC6 disclosure penalties up to €100,000 for failure to report cross-border arrangements.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: director and controlling shareholder are jointly and severally liable for corporate liabilities (Art. L.651-2 C. com. in insolvency); individual travel ban and professional order sanction are possible when management intentionally caused the lack of substance.
AML / Merchant Compliance

Statutory sanction: ACPR administrative fine up to €5 million or 10% of turnover; criminal money-laundering sanction up to €750,000 and five years imprisonment under Art. 324-1 C.P.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines and disqualification from management; if the corporate veil is pierced, the travel ban can be imposed and personal assets seized as proceeds of money laundering.
Withholding Tax Default / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the payments as dividends under Art. 111(a) CGI; additional 40% penalty and late interest; 30% withholding may be reassessed jointly against the distributing company.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately omit withholding may be held personally liable under Art. L.262 LPF for the unpaid amount; French tax authority can serve a travel ban ('avis à tiers détenteur') against management and obtain temporary passport surrender during criminal prosecution.
