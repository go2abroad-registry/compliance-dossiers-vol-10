Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered locally through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Tax accounts and filings are generally managed through the DGFiP professional portal, impots.gouv.fr. Company formation and registration formalities are filed through the national business formalities portal operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the INPI business formalities portal. Upon registration, it receives a SIREN identification number and a SIRET for each establishment, recorded in the Registre national des entreprises and generally also in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés. The competent SIE handles corporate tax and VAT account activation. A French VAT number consists of the prefix FR, a two-digit control key, and the SIREN. A foreign taxable person without a French establishment applies to the DGFiP for VAT registration before carrying out transactions requiring French VAT reporting; a non-EU business generally appoints a French fiscal representative unless an applicable statutory exception or mutual-assistance arrangement applies. EU-established businesses are generally not required to appoint a representative but may need a French tax representative or agent for procedural purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying newly created SAS may elect temporary partnership-style taxation under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code, generally for up to five fiscal years, subject to ownership, activity, age, size, and other statutory conditions. This is not a U.S.-style check-the-box election. No general hybrid-mismatch election changes a SAS's French tax classification; anti-hybrid rules may deny deductions or require income inclusion in applicable cross-border arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold applies to a French-established taxable business that must charge VAT: registration and liability depend on the nature and location of taxable transactions. Eligible small businesses may use the franchise en base de TVA. For 2025, the principal domestic thresholds are generally EUR 85,000 for sales of goods and accommodation and EUR 37,500 for services, with higher tolerance thresholds generally EUR 93,500 and EUR 41,250, respectively; statutory eligibility and activity-specific rules must be checked. Non-established businesses generally do not benefit from a domestic turnover threshold for taxable transactions requiring French VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union may be exempt with credit (effectively zero-rated) under the French VAT rules implementing Article 262 of the EU VAT Directive, provided the goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU and the exporter retains reliable evidence of export. Intra-EU supplies are governed by separate rules and are not exports for this purpose. Services are not automatically zero-rated as exports: the place-of-supply rules determine whether French VAT applies. A supply to a non-EU business may be outside the scope of French VAT under the general B2B rule, subject to exceptions; services connected with French immovable property and other special-category services may remain taxable in France. Input VAT recovery depends on the applicable deduction rules and substantiation.

  • Export customs declaration and proof of exit from the EU customs territory, including the export accompanying document or electronic exit confirmation where available
  • Commercial invoice identifying the parties, goods, value, and relevant VAT treatment
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the route, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier proof of delivery
  • Purchase order, contract, and correspondence supporting the export transaction and the identity of the customer
  • Proof of payment where relevant to substantiate the transaction; an inward remittance certificate is not a generally prescribed French VAT export document

France is implementing domestic B2B electronic invoicing and e-reporting in phases. Under the enacted timetable, all businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; large and intermediate-sized businesses must issue them from that date, while SMEs and micro-enterprises must issue them from 1 September 2027. Domestic B2B invoices must use an authorized platform or the public invoicing portal under the applicable system. E-reporting applies to specified transactions, including relevant B2C and cross-border transactions. France does not generally require real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of every invoice. Businesses must comply with statutory invoice-content, retention, and transmission requirements.

France imposes no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline of 180 days for export proceeds. Export exemption claims may be denied or reversed if the taxpayer cannot establish that the goods left the EU or that the transaction satisfies the applicable conditions; resulting VAT, late-payment interest, and penalties may be assessed. Under the French Tax Code, penalties can include a 40% increase for deliberate breach and an 80% increase in specified cases of fraudulent activity or abuse, in addition to statutory interest; the applicable assessment depends on the facts and procedure. False documents or fraudulent conduct may also trigger criminal liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account items, in line with Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU). For French resident entities, cross-border transactions with non-residents must be reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred abroad provided the origin of funds is documented and banks file any necessary declarations to TRACFIN (French FIU). There are no exchange controls, but the flow of physical currency, asset transfers, and payments exceeding certain thresholds require administrative declarations to customs or tax authorities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender requirement for export proceeds. Exporters may retain EUR or foreign currency balances in with any authorised bank or financial institution. Nonetheless, under EU Regulation 184/2005 (transposed by the Banque de France), all payment receipts from non-residents must be reported for balance-of-payments statistics via the resident bank. Specific direct-investment inflows and outflows above €15 million per annum must be reported quarterly; no foreign-exchange conversion or repatriation is mandated for goods, services, or intangibles.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) and the European Central Bank (ECB) under the French Foreign Exchange Control regime administered jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury). The legal framework is codified in the French Monetary and Financial Code (CMF, Book V).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for consolidated operational payments and foreign-currency collection, but Low for capital escrow, custody, or investment-settlement accounts. Legal constraints under ACPR and the E-Money Directive (2009/110/EC): EMIs cannot grant credit, hold client securities, or provide portfolio-management services. For an MFO with discretionary investment advisory activity, EMIs must not substitute for a licensed investment firm; they may be used only as a payment-rail interface, with mandatory safeguarding of funds in a low-risk account at a credit institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €50 million consolidated revenue for local file; €750 million for master file

Economic substance scope: Limited applicability under French DAC6 and ESR for holding or IP entities; pure advisory services generally do not trigger full ESR

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to file corporate tax return

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine €750-€7,500 or 0.5% of tax per month late (max 25%) plus interest; possible criminal prosecution

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for tax debts; possible imprisonment for willful non-compliance or fraud
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 80% of understated tax or more; restitution of tax plus interest

Director personal liability: Personal civil and criminal liability; possible disqualification from management
Understatement of taxable profits

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion penalties: fines up to 500% of tax evaded, imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Imprisonment, fines, and personal restitution obligations
Shell company or artificial structures

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions; penalties up to 80% of tax; criminal sanctions

Director personal liability: Personal liability for tax, possible jail time for fraudulent conduct
