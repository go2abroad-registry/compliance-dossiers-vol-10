Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS not making a public offering; the statuts fix the share capital. A minimum of 50% of cash contributions must be deposited and blocked in escrow with a notary or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations before filing, with the balance payable within 5 years. In-kind contributions must be fully paid up at incorporation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — France. Professional portal: impots.gouv.fr. Mandatory business formalities portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises). Customs single window for exports: SOPRANO/DELTA (Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the SAS formation dossier (statuts, registered office, manager appointments) through the Guichet unique portal, which automatically registers the entity with the RCS (Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce) and INSEE; INSEE issues the SIREN/SIRET identifiers. Step 2: DGFiP assigns the intra-community VAT number (FR + 11 digits or letters) upon completion of the INSEE registration; the number is published in the VIES directory. Step 3: Foreign parents without a French establishment must appoint a fiscal representative for VAT purposes (art. 289 A CGI); a non-resident contractor performing French construction works must register with the SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) of the first French construction site before commencing work. The SAS receives the extrait KBIS as proof of legal existence and tax identity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (SAS is automatically subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) under art. 206 CGI. Territorial scope: profits of the French head office and attributable French permanent establishment activities are taxable in France; foreign-source profits of foreign permanent establishments are excluded under the territoriality principle. Available election forms

French law provides no check-the-box election; entity classification follows the statutory corporate form. Available elections: (i) small-business simplified real regime (régime réel simplifié); (ii) fiscal integration election under art. 223 A CGI where the SAS is at least 95% owned by a French-resident parent; (iii) option for group-wide worldwide profit consolidation within art. 209 quinquies CGI (where applicable); (iv) VAT group election (art. 256 C bis CGI); (v) ATAD hybrid mismatch neutralization rules (art. 212 bis CGI) apply automatically without elective designation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Statutory VAT registration is mandatory regardless of turnover for non-EU contractors performing construction works in France. For French-resident suppliers, the franchise en base de TVA (art. 293 B CGI) sets de minimis thresholds of EUR 91,900 (supply of goods) and EUR 36,800 (supply of services), with tolerance limits of EUR 101,000 and EUR 39,100; these thresholds are exceeded by the forecast hyperscale data center EPC revenue, and the SAS must therefore register for VAT with the DGFiP from its first taxable transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to exports of movable goods physically dispatched or transported to a destination outside the EU (art. 262-I-1° CGI) and to services directly connected with those goods — including loading, transport, and works on movable property (art. 262-I-3° CGI). The supplier must prove that the goods left the customs territory within 90 days of transfer. For the data center EPC contract, services relating to French immovable property (installation, mechanical and electrical works on the building) are deemed supplied in France under art. 258-A CGI and remain taxable at 20%; only equipment physically installed outside France or offshore pre-commissioning engineering services performed and consumed abroad may qualify for zero-rating or a foreign place-of-supply treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration: electronic Single Administrative Document (DAU) filed through DELTA/ECS, including the MRN (Movement Reference Number) and export summary declaration; the MRN must be traceable in the French and EU customs databases.
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Airway Bill: commercial transport document evidencing physical departure from the EU, matching the declared exporter, consignee, value, and goods description of the customs and VAT documentation.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: banking credit advice or SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from the non-resident principal; retained with the VAT file for the statutory 6-year record retention period.

Mandatory structured e-invoicing and e-reporting under Ordonnance 2021-1190 and the Finance Act 2024 reform. Deadline: large enterprises and ETIs by 1 September 2026; SMEs/TPE by 1 September 2027. Domestic B2B invoices must be transmitted in a structured digital format (Factur-X, UBL, CII) via a certified PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) or the public portal (PPF); invoice data must be transmitted within one working day of issuance and e-reporting data within 30 days of month-end. France does not currently apply government digital stamping or per-invoice real-time clearance (unlike Italy/Hungary); compliance is monitored through the PPF, the PSTAI standard, and the mandatory reliable audit trail (piste d'audit fiable). Customs export declarations must be filed separately in the DELTA single window.

Export zero-rating is scrutinized by the DGFiP audit services (DVNE and SIE refund desk) against customs database correlations. A missing MRN, inconsistent transport documents, or absence of payment proof within the 180-day documentary production window triggers reinstatement of 20% VAT ex officio, plus: (i) late-payment interest at 0.20% per month (art. 1727 CGI); (ii) penalties from 10% (late declaration, art. 1728 CGI) through 40% (bad-faith understatement, art. 1729 CGI) to 80% (fraudulent maneuvers, art. 1729 CGI); (iii) criminal sanctions under art. 1741 CGI, including up to EUR 500,000 fine and 5 years' imprisonment; (iv) joint and several liability of the main EPC contractor for VAT, penalties, and interest of its subcontractors under art. 283-2 CGI. VAT refund claims may be suspended until documentary compliance is fully restored.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member and applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Article 63 TFEU. There is no exchange-control authorisation, no surrender obligation, and no restriction on holding or remitting dividends, loan repayments, royalties, or liquidation proceeds to non-resident shareholders of a French SAS. The operative constraints are documentary: cross-border financial flows equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 per transaction are declared monthly through the resident bank to the Banque de France, and non-EU investments must be pre-notified under Decree No. 2019-1590 where the target falls within a protected sector. A hyperscale datacenter mechanical-and-cooling EPC contractor sits outside the defence/security and critical-technology categories (subject to a case-by-case check where the scope includes cybersecurity, classified data processing, or critical infrastructure hosting), so pure construction activity requires no prior ministerial authorisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The French and EU regimes impose no statutory repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to surrender export receipts. A French SAS may lawfully retain, convert, or disburse USD/CNY/EUR export and milestone proceeds in commercial bank accounts; the only duty is the Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration of cross-border settlements above EUR 12,500, filed by the bank on a monthly basis. This permits the EPC contractor to match USD-denominated capex procurement against USD-denominated receivables without forced conversion to EUR.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), the Republic's central bank and head of the French balance-of-payments reporting regime, acting within the Eurosystem/ECB framework; banking licensing and AML/CFT supervision are exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), while foreign-investment screening and economic-dossier review sit with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury).

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate (Article 219-I CGI); reduced 15% rate applies to the first €42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs meeting Article 44 sexies-0 A conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For dividends paid by a French SAS to a non-resident corporate beneficiary, the statutory withholding tax rate is 25% under Article 119-2 CGI. For non-resident individuals, a 30% flat withholding rate generally applies (including the 17.2% social levies on dividends treated as capital income). Mixed and treaty situations may reduce the rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year closing on 31 December, the return must be filed no later than the second working day following 1 May (generally 3 May, extended to the next business day when a holiday intervenes). For a fiscal year ending other than 31 December, the filing deadline is the last day of the third month following the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The mandatory Master File and Local File in France apply where the French entity, or the group to which it belongs, has annual turnover or balance-sheet total of at least €400 million (Article L.13 AA LPF); the Country-by-Country Report threshold is €750 million consolidated group turnover. Even below €400 million, a prudent SAS exposed to cross-border EPC procurement must prepare contemporaneous industry, functional and pricing evidence for audit defense.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the offshore sense, but the substance doctrine is embedded in the French abuse-of-law provision (Article L64 LPF), the OECD/IF principal-purpose test, and the EU ATAD general anti-abuse rule. For a French SAS engaged in hyperscale data-center electromechanical EPC, economic substance requires proper local board decisions, actual technical staff, project-management oversight, bank accounts and risk control in France. Absence of substance may invalidate treaty relief, cause the 80% abuse penalty, and trigger the recharacterisation of inter-company arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Concealment

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI: up to 5 years' imprisonment and a €500,000 fine in aggravated cases; automatic 80% fraud surcharge; monthly 0.20% late-payment interest; exclusion from French public contracts; Corporate veil may be pierced to reach personal assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally direct or approve the concealment are criminally liable and may be sentenced to imprisonment, fined, stripped of management rights, subjected to travel bans, and ordered by the criminal court to pay civil restitution of the defrauded tax debt and penalties under Article L.267 LPF.
Artificial Transfer Pricing / Sham Intercompany Contracts

Statutory sanction: Tax recharacterisation at arm's length and reassessment; 40% bad-faith penalty (or 80% for abuse of law); transfer-pricing documentation deficiency penalty of 5% of the reassessed transferred profit, minimum €10,000; denial of expense deduction when the arrangement lacks economic substance.

Director personal liability: Directors signing fraudulent transfer-pricing declarations may face criminal prosecution for fraud, civil restoration of adjusted tax, and judicial disqualification; a French court may apply veil-piercing where the SAS is a mere conduit for the non-resident controller.
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: RBE non-compliance is punishable by criminal fine up to €20,000 for the company and up to €10,000 per legal representative; concealment of beneficial ownership may be treated as money laundering; administrative penalty up to 80% of the tax due in the case of an artificial scheme; statutes require identifying the beneficial owner before payment of dividends.

Director personal liability: Company officers are subject to criminal imprisonment for deliberate non-disclosure, personal monetary fines, travel bans prohibiting exit from French territory while under investigation, and civil liability for all tax and penalties resulting from the undisclosed ownership structure.
Treaty Shopping and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Denial of the DTA reduced rate or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption; statutory 25% withholding tax applied retroactively; abuse-of-law surcharge of 80%; no tax-refund availability; the intermediary is often ignored for French tax purposes.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly execute treaty-shopping structures can be prosecuted for aggravated tax fraud, personally ordered to pay withholding tax and 80% penalties, subjected to travel bans during proceedings, and, in severe criminal cases, sentenced to imprisonment.
Late Filing / Omission of Transfer-Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: 10% late-filing surcharge; 40% penalty for inaccurate returns; 0.20% monthly interest on unpaid CIT; if documentation is missing, 5% of the reassessed transferred profit with a minimum €10,000 penalty; potential tax base estimation by the tax administration.

Director personal liability: Where non-filing is deliberate concealment, directors may be criminally prosecuted for tax evasion; the DGFiP may invoke individual liability for the company's tax debt if the director committed a serious anomaly in the management of the company and caused its insolvency.
Non-Cooperative Jurisdiction Transactions

Statutory sanction: Mandatory tax disallowance of the expense; 33.33% or higher withholding on royalties and certain interest payments (up to 75% in some ETNC cases); 80% penalty for abusive offshore arrangements; unreported ETNC payments create a presumption of fiscal concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct such payments may be prosecuted for fraudulent management and tax evasion, subjected to civil compensation to the French treasury, travel bans, judicial control, and imprisonment of up to five years; courts may order freezing of personal bank accounts and passport surrender.
