A French SAS must have a registered office in France under the Commercial Code. A virtual office is permissible only through a compliant domiciliation arrangement with an eligible provider, or at the legal representative's residence subject to statutory conditions and time limits. Name reservation / lock period

France does not generally provide a statutory company-name reservation mechanism with a fixed 30-day reservation period. Before filing, conduct searches through the INPI database, Registre National des Entreprises, RCS, EUIPO, domain registries, and relevant trademark databases. If a name is rejected or the filing is suspended, obtain the registrar's written reason, correct the articles or filing data, and resubmit through the INPI single window. Where a formal administrative decision is issued, request reconsideration from the competent registry or INPI authority and, if necessary, file an administrative challenge before the competent administrative court within the applicable deadline, normally two months from notification. A separate action may be required before the commercial court for third-party corporate-name or unfair-competition disputes. Use a new name if the objection concerns prior rights, public-order restrictions, or regulated activity.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy and Finance, France – portal: https://www.impots.gouv.fr; business tax files and VAT management handled by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) of the entity's registered situs; corporate formalities filed through the single portal Guichet Unique (https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) operated by INPI; INSEE issues the SIREN/SIRET identifiers; cross-border and non-resident matters are handled by the Direction des Non-Résidents et des Services Généraux (DVNI).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the SAS incorporation deed and legal representative appointment via the Guichet Unique; INSEE automatically assigns the 9-digit SIREN and the 14-digit SIRET establishment number. Step 2: The DGFiP activates the French VAT account upon incorporation; no separate domestic VAT certificate is issued because the SIREN doubles as the domestic VAT identifier. Step 3: Submit to the SIE a request for an intra-Community VAT number (format FR + 2-digit checksum + SIREN) for intra-EU trade, zero-rated dispatches, DEB/DES statistical declarations, and export VAT refund claims. Step 4: Appoint a French tax domicile (siege social or dedicated fiscal address) and register the foreign ultimate beneficial owner(s) with the RCS and the 'bénéficiaire effectif' filing; non-resident shareholding is authorized without any minimum capital for an SAS. Step 5: Open a French business bank account and communicate IBAN details to the SIE for potential credit transfers of VAT refunds. Step 6: For the highway/bridge EPC business scope, no additional construction-specific tax ID is required; the SAS must however enroll for the mandatory monthly or quarterly CA3 TVA returns and, because it executes public infrastructure works, file the specific turnover declarations required by Article 287 CGI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – an SAS is a capital company subject to French corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) under Article 206(1) CGI; it is not a pass-through entity and has unrestricted legal personality; for EPC contracts performed abroad, the limited territoriality principle of Article 209 CGI applies so that foreign-source profits of a foreign branch or fixed base may be exempt from French IS if subject to foreign corporate income tax; for all French-source EPC revenues the SAS is taxed at the standard IS rate (25% since 2022) plus the 3.3% social contribution (Article 235 ter ZC CGI) on distributions; the SAS cannot elect US-style pass-through taxation.

Available election forms

France does not have a check-the-box election; the SAS classification is statutory. Available elections: (a) Tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for groups with at least 95% direct/indirect French-incorporated subsidiaries, enabling offset of EPC losses across group members; (b) Option for income-tax transparency (Art. 239 bis AB CGI) for a newly created SAS with fewer than 50 employees, annual revenue below €10 million, capital held at least 50% by individuals, and company age below 5 years – these conditions are unlikely to be met by a highway infrastructure EPC contractor; (c) Option for the territorial parent-company regime of Article 209 CGI for foreign EPC branch profits subject to foreign CIT; (d) Option under Article 269 CGI to charge VAT on receivables (régime des débits) rather than on cash receipt (régime des encaissements), subject to DGFiP acceptance; (e) Option for VAT grouping (assujettis unis) under Article 256 C III CGI effective from 1 January 2023 where the SAS is part of a closely-linked but legally independent French group; (f) The hybrid-mismatch rules of Article 212 bis CGI (ATAD 2 implementation) impose mandatory anti-abuse recharacterization and do not provide any favorable elective mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

ZERO – no turnover threshold for a French-established SAS: registration is compulsory immediately upon commencement of activity (Article 256 CGI). For non-established EU suppliers, the €10,000 annual cross-border B2C distance-selling threshold applies to goods and electronically supplied services (Article 258 A and 259 B CGI), above which French VAT and OSS registration are mandatory; for non-EU suppliers there is no threshold. Where construction services related to French immovable property are provided by a non-established foreign contractor, French VAT is payable by the French recipient under the reverse-charge mechanism (Article 283-2 CGI), so the foreign supplier generally does not need French registration solely for that supply. This French-established SAS is therefore VAT-registered without any threshold and at all times after incorporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

(i) Exports of goods outside the EU: exempt with full input VAT credit ('zero-rating') under Article 262(I) CGI, provided the goods physically leave the EU; the exit must be evidenced by a validated customs export declaration, generally within 90 days from the transfer of power of disposal, per BOI-TVA-CHAMP-30-20-20-10. (ii) Intra-EU dispatches of goods to a customer holding a valid intra-Community VAT number: exempt under Article 262-0 CGI subject to the EC Sales List (recapitulatif des clients assujettis) and DEB. (iii) Works, services and construction output relating to immovable property located outside France: outside the territorial scope of French VAT under Article 259 CGIE (place-of-supply rule), subject to documentary proof of the foreign location of the infrastructure and of the effective performance of the EPC services abroad. (iv) In the mixed EPC contract for highway/bridge infrastructure, the French supplier must make an economic apportionment between the exported or foreign-situated supplies (exempt/out-of-scope) and any supply physically carried out or delivered in France (20% TVA); invoice wording must explicitly mention the zero-rating/exemption article invoked. Claims to French VAT refunds on inputs (export refund or FCTVA mechanism) are admissible where the credit exceeds the debit on the CA3 return and are substantiated by supporting customs and banking records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/EAD) validated via the DELTA/GUNEXIT French customs IT system evidencing exit of goods from the EU customs territory
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading (ocean), CMR waybill (road), or Air Waybill (air) showing consignee abroad and exit route
  • Commercial invoice and detailed export packing list
  • Inward remittance certificate: French bank credit advice (avis de crédit) confirming SWIFT MT103 settlement under the sovereign Letter of Credit and reconciliation with the exporter's books
  • Intra-EU dispatch: valid customer intra-Community VAT number captured in the supplier's validation log, DEB/DEB-MIG declaration, and EC Sales List
  • EPC-specific evidence: infrastructure contract, certificate of final acceptance (réception définitive), quantity survey reports, site works certificates, and the foreign sovereign authority's acceptance minutes demonstrating performance outside France
  • Supporting logistics trail: GUNEXIT exit certificate, container tracking manifest, CMR receipts signed by the foreign consignee, and bank foreign-exchange records

Mandatory structured electronic invoicing (facturation électronique) under Articles 289 bis CGI and ordonnance n° 2022-1087 as amended by ordonnance n° 2024-557: from 1 September 2026, all French-established taxable persons must be able to receive e-invoices through an accredited Dematerialization Partner Platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire – PDP) or the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation – PPF), and large/mid-cap issuers (grandes entreprises and ETI) must issue e-invoices; from 1 September 2027, issuance obligation extends to all PME and micro-enterprises. Invoices must use formats aligned with the EN 16931 semantic model (Factur-X, UBL, CII), and each PDP transmits e-invoicing and e-Reporting data (transaction, payment, VAT breakdown) to DGFiP within one calendar day. This is a post-transaction data-reporting regime rather than a real-time pre-clearance or digital-stamping system; for the EPC contractor, all progress payment claims (situations de travaux), invoices, credit notes, and retention releases must be issued as structured e-invoices from the applicable mandate date, and each invoice must include the crypto-stamped audit trail generated by the PDP.

Export zero-rating and out-of-scope claims are scrutinized through the DGFiP tax audit (vérification de comptabilité) and the anti-fraud intelligence unit (DNRED); if the exporter fails to produce the customs and banking documents listed above, the VAT is retroactively re-invoiced at 20% (clawback) on the full gross value of the supply. Penalties escalate in graduated tiers: (a) late-payment monthly interest of 0.20% (Article 1727 CGI); (b) negligence penalty of 40% on the insufficiently declared amount (Article 1729(a) CGI); (c) bad faith or lawful-abuse penalty of 80% (Article 1729(b) CGI); (d) tax evasion (fraude fiscale) constitutes a criminal offense punishable by a fine of up to €500,000 and 7 years' imprisonment under Article 1741 CGI, with publication sanctions and a 5-year professional disqualification for the EPC contractor; (e) accomplices and principals involved in missing-trader/carousel fraud are jointly and severally liable for VAT under Article 283(4) CGI; (f) fraudulent export refunds trigger a 100% recovery order through coercive collection (avis à tiers détenteur) and may cause suspension of future VAT refunds for a period of up to 60 days pending enhanced pre-refund checks; cross-border coordination through Eurofisc and Council Regulation (EU) No 904/2010 enables multi-state clawback assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no general French foreign-exchange approval requirement for inbound equity, shareholder loans, ordinary trade payments, dividends, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds. Remittances remain subject to corporate-law requirements, distributable-profit and solvency tests, withholding-tax analysis, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-owner verification, anti-money-laundering controls, sanctions screening, and documentary requirements imposed by the servicing bank. Cross-border payments involving sanctioned persons, restricted jurisdictions, dual-use goods, or regulated public-procurement transactions may require enhanced review, licensing, or refusal. French residents and entities may hold foreign-currency accounts and foreign accounts, subject to applicable tax and reporting obligations. Payments in euro within the Single Euro Payments Area are generally processed through SEPA; international payments may use correspondent banking and SWIFT.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline and no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may generally be retained in euros or foreign currency, credited to French or permitted foreign accounts, and converted at the company's discretion. Banks may request invoices, customs or transport records, contracts, payment purpose information, and evidence of economic rationale under anti-money-laundering, sanctions, tax, and transaction-monitoring procedures. Cash movements of EUR 10,000 or more into or out of the European Union must be declared to customs by the carrier. Bank reporting may apply to cross-border payment statistics and balance-of-payments data; such reporting is not a currency-conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange policy is governed principally by European Union law and the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payments and collections; Low to Medium for the full treasury stack. A French SAS may use an EU-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution operating under passporting or local authorisation, subject to the provider's country, customer, currency, and transaction-risk policy. An EMI safeguards customer funds but does not provide a bank deposit, ordinary credit facility, capital-escrow functionality, or assured access to correspondent-bank services. Offshore digital EMIs may be unsuitable where the company requires French share-capital deposit certification, sovereign or bank-issued letters of credit, construction-project finance, guarantee lines, controlled disbursement, cash pooling, or high-value infrastructure payments. The provider must be verified in the relevant EBA or national regulatory register, and its passporting status, safeguarding model, sanctions controls, safeguarding-bank arrangements, French tax reporting capability, and permitted services must be documented.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payments and collections; Low to Medium for the full treasury stack. A French SAS may use an EU-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution operating under passporting or local authorisation, subject to the provider's country, customer, currency, and transaction-risk policy. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate shareholder, subject to domestic exemptions and applicable tax treaties. An EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive exemption may apply to qualifying EU or EEA corporate shareholders meeting ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax and anti-abuse conditions. The domestic rate for certain individual recipients is different and must not be applied to corporate shareholders without analysis.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million of annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets generally triggers the full French Local File and Master File documentation requirement under the current threshold rules. A separate annual Form 2257-SD transfer-pricing declaration generally applies at the EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, including certain group-threshold situations. Documentation may be requested below these thresholds where the tax administration challenges the pricing.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime. Substance is assessed through French transfer-pricing rules, the principal-purpose and abuse-of-law doctrines, beneficial-ownership analysis, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, withholding-tax anti-abuse provisions and general anti-avoidance principles. A French SAS should maintain French decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capacity, premises or operational infrastructure, project records, contract-management capability, banking and accounting controls, and demonstrable functions and risks consistent with its infrastructure EPC activities. Circular pass-through invoicing, conduit financing, artificial management fees and unsupported foreign-parent allocations are high-risk.
