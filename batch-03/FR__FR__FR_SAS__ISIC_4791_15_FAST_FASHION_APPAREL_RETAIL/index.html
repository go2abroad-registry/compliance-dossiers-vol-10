Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member state and applies full capital-account liberalization pursuant to EU Treaty provisions on the free movement of capital (Article 63 TFEU) and French Monetary and Financial Code Articles L.221-1 and R.221-1 et seq. Current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, royalties, management fees) are freely convertible without prior authorization. Profit remittance by a French SAS is unrestricted; no exchange-control approval is required for dividend distributions, loan repayments, or intercompany payments to non-residents. Residual procedural obligations are confined to statistical balance-of-payments reporting through the Banque de France's direct reporting system for transactions exceeding EUR 750,000 (monthly declaration) or thresholds set for monetary financial institutions. Cross-border capital movements of any amount are not subject to convertibility restriction, and France maintains no multiple-currency practices or surrender requirements. Transfer-pricing documentation must contemporaneously support the commercial substance of any intercompany treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists under French law or Eurosystem regulations. Export receipts are fully fungible in EUR and may be retained offshore or repatriated at the entity's discretion. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no mandatory holding period, and no requirement to liquidate foreign-currency balances into EUR. For inward collection of export receivables denominated in foreign currency, the SAS must ensure that booking to its French bank account is accompanied by the appropriate customs declaration (DAE/DX) and the provision of the Single Administrative Document reference to support the economic nature of the flow. Balance-of-payments declarative thresholds (EUR 750,000 per transaction for direct investment flows and EUR 50,000 per calendar year for cross-border current-account flows reported by non-intermediated entities) remain applicable for statistical, not control, purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France and Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); external operations oversight jointly administered with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) under Article L.151-3 of the French Monetary and Financial Code

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High feasibility subject to strict ACL constraints. ACPR-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Lydia, Nickel, Treezor (backed by Société Générale), and Mangopay may open accounts for a French SAS remotely and provide cross-border payout rails, multi-currency virtual IBANs, and BNPL/settlement integrations. Post-Brexit UK-licensed EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise) must rely on French branching or EEA passporting; unlicensed offshore EMIs without a) an ACPR or EEA passport, b) a local registered branch, and c) access to TARGET2/SEPA are legally non-viable as primary settlement banks because they cannot provide a valid French IBAN for direct-debit (SEPA Core) or immediate transfer clearing. Digital EMI accounts are acceptable only as collection/settlement sub-accounts; all statutory filings, VAT settlements, and payroll must route through a French account held by a credit institution or licensed EMI with a French CFONB/IBAN allocation. A formal contractual overlay with the underlying licensed sponsor bank is required to secure DGS/ FGDR deposit protection limits and to prevent unregulated re-hypothecation of client funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital injection channels for the French SAS include (1) share-capital increase via non-resident subscription, (2) shareholder current-account advances (avance en compte courant d'associé), and (3) formal intercompany loans. Routing must be executed via SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code aligned to EU Balance of Payments statistics: 'Equity Capital' (category 528) for share subscriptions, or 'Financial Credit and Adjustments' / 'Other Investment – Loans' (category 751) for shareholder advances or intercompany loans. The transfer must include (i) the ISIN of the shares where applicable, (ii) full legal name and registration number of the French SAS, (iii) the beneficial-payer identification, and (iv) a reference to the constitutive documents or the loan agreement accepted by the bank. For amounts exceeding EUR 750,000, prior notification to Banque de France (direct reporting circuit) or, for amounts over EUR 1 million, an ex-post statistical declaration is required. The bank will map the code to the CBP (Catégorie de Balance des Paiements) table; incorrect purpose codes trigger mandatory verification and potential 72-hour holding until documentary confirmation. To facilitate smooth clearing, the inbound transfer should be addressed to the SAS's French IBAN with the note 'Capital subscription / apport en nature' and reference the specific notarial or statutory auditor certificate (rapport du commissaire aux apports) where capital is injected in kind.Outbound trade settlements for fast-fashion apparel procurement must use (1) SWIFT MT103 single-payment transfers for supplier invoices up to EUR/USD thresholds, (2) bank payment obligations under documentary letters of credit (UCP600) for high-value textile shipments, and (3) SEPA Instant Credit Transfers (TARGET Instant Payment Settlement – TIPS) for EU-subzone suppliers. For Asian suppliers (Bangladesh, Vietnam, China), the preferred rail is a clean MT103 with ICC Uniform Rules for Collections (URC 522) in case of documents-against-payment terms, or a confirmed irrevocable commercial LC issued by the French bank and advised through a Chinese or Hong Kong correspondent. Each outbound MT103 must incorporate the correct 2-digit purpose code from the EU Harmonised List (e.g., 'Trade in Goods – General Merchandise' for invoice settlement) and the UETR (Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference). Funds settlement within SEPA can be completed via SEPA Credit Transfer (single or batch); with TARGET2 for high-value EUR settlements. USD settlement must occur via CHIPS Fedwire and correspondent accounts; commercial LC reimbursement occurs under SWIFT MT700/MT750. The treasury must also reconcile against Klarna and Shopify Payments settlement files: BNPL flows are cleared via the acquiring bank (e.g., via Adyen, Mollie, or Klarna's own SEPA infrastructure), and each settlement entry must be mapped to the SAS's accounting system to avoid double-funding. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). A qualifying small company may apply the 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, with the excess taxed at 25%, if its turnover does not exceed EUR 10 million, its share capital is fully paid up, and at least 75% is continuously owned by individuals or qualifying companies meeting the statutory ownership test.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The domestic withholding rate on dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident legal entity is generally 25% of the gross distribution, subject to exemptions and treaty relief. A qualifying EU parent may obtain exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive and French implementing rules, subject to ownership, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse conditions. Rates for non-resident individuals differ and must be determined under the applicable domestic rules and treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer-pricing documentation generally applies where the French entity or qualifying French tax group has annual turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, or meets specified ownership or control links with an entity meeting that threshold. The annual Form 2257-SD summary obligation generally applies at a lower threshold, including where the French entity or qualifying group has turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 50 million, or meets specified links to an entity meeting that threshold. Related-party transaction reporting and documentation duties may also arise under other statutory rules. Thresholds and group aggregation must be tested for each financial year.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a general standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to certain offshore jurisdictions. Nevertheless, French tax law and treaties apply substance-sensitive rules, including beneficial-ownership requirements, the general anti-abuse rule, abuse-of-law doctrines, transfer-pricing rules, controlled-foreign-company rules, interest-deduction restrictions, and substance and principal-purpose tests under applicable treaty and EU measures. A French SAS should document its personnel, decision-making, premises, functions, assets, risks, and commercial rationale consistently with its claimed tax position.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax fraud and concealed turnover

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, and penalties that may reach 80% in aggravated cases. Tax fraud may also constitute a criminal offence punishable, in serious cases, by up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, with higher penalties possible in specified aggravated circumstances, including certain organized or offshore arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur criminal liability where they personally participated in or knowingly facilitated fraud. A court may impose personal fines and imprisonment. Separate statutory procedures may make a director jointly and severally liable for tax debts in cases of fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated breaches that rendered collection impossible. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax sanction.
Transfer-pricing misstatement and undocumented related-party charges

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable profit, impose late-payment interest and applicable tax penalties, and apply the statutory transfer-pricing documentation fine, generally at least EUR 50,000 per audited year and potentially a higher transaction-linked amount subject to statutory limits.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal or civil consequences if they knowingly participate in false accounting, tax fraud, or asset diversion. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments do not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Improper treaty or EU withholding-tax exemption

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent may be assessed for under-withheld tax, interest, and applicable penalties. The recipient may be required to repay an improperly obtained refund; fraud or deliberate misrepresentation may trigger aggravated tax and criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: A director or responsible officer may be personally exposed for knowing participation in fraudulent claims or deliberate misstatements. Personal liability is not automatic for a good-faith documentation or interpretive error.
Shell-company, artificial-conduit, or abusive arrangement

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny treaty or tax benefits, recharacterize transactions, reassess tax, and impose interest and applicable penalties. The general anti-abuse or abuse-of-law rules may apply depending on the facts and statutory test.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur liability for personal participation in fraud, misrepresentation, or asset diversion. A French SAS's limited liability is not displaced merely because it has limited substance; veil-piercing or personal debt liability requires a separate legal basis.
Money laundering and unlawful movement of criminal proceeds

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is a criminal offence punishable in general cases by up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with higher penalties for specified aggravated circumstances. Other offences, confiscation, and professional sanctions may also apply.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly participate may face criminal prosecution, imprisonment, fines, and confiscation. Directors do not incur automatic personal liability solely by holding office. Travel restrictions may be imposed only through an applicable judicial or procedural measure, not as an automatic corporate-tax penalty.
Failure to file, pay, or retain required tax records

Statutory sanction: Late-filing surcharges, late-payment interest, tax reassessment, and applicable recordkeeping or documentation penalties may apply. Persistent non-compliance can increase audit and enforcement exposure.

Director personal liability: The SAS is ordinarily liable for its tax debts. A director may be held personally liable under a specific statutory procedure for fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated tax-law breaches that make collection impossible, or under ordinary civil or criminal law for personal misconduct.
