Competent corporate registry

The competent local commercial and companies registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce), with applications submitted through the national Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by INPI.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), principally Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-20 and R. 123-1 et seq.; SAS formation and governance are governed by the Code and the company's articles of association.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic application for registration in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS), submitted through the Guichet unique, supported by the signed articles of association and required declarations.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. Cash contributions must be paid up at least 50% on incorporation; the balance is payable within five years after registration. Cash funds are deposited with a qualified depositary, such as a bank or notary, and released after registration. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed at incorporation; an auditor for contributions (commissaire aux apports) is generally required unless the statutory exemption conditions are met and the shareholders unanimously elect the exemption. The USD fee figure is an approximate conversion of standard registry and beneficial-ownership filing charges; publication and ancillary charges are additional and may vary.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupation, verify planning and building-use permissions, fire-safety requirements applicable to the premises, and electrical and machinery conformity. A building permit or change-of-use approval may be required depending on the site. The employer must assess occupational risks, prepare and update the DUERP, provide required worker training and protective measures, and arrange statutory inspections of relevant equipment. No universal separate fire or structural-load certificate applies to every workshop; requirements depend on premises classification, works, and equipment.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. Cash contributions must be paid up at least 50% on incorporation; the balance is payable within five years after registration. Cash funds are deposited with a qualified depositary, such as a bank or notary, and released after registration. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has no general foreign-exchange control regime restricting ordinary current-account payments, trade receipts, dividend payments, interest, royalties, or service fees. The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, customs, beneficial-ownership, and foreign-investment rules. A French SAS may generally hold foreign-currency accounts, receive foreign investment, make cross-border payments, and remit distributable profits without prior central-bank approval. Dividend remittances require legally available distributable profits, required corporate approvals, appropriate withholding-tax analysis, and supporting accounting and tax documentation. Banks may require evidence of the underlying transaction, source of funds, tax residence, beneficial ownership, and sanctions screening before executing transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds to France, surrender them to a bank, convert them into euros, or convert a prescribed percentage. No general statutory conversion deadline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts, subject to contractual bank terms, accounting records, customs documentation, sanctions controls, tax rules, and applicable statistical or regulatory reporting. EU and French sanctions, dual-use restrictions, customs rules, and anti-money-laundering controls may restrict specific counterparties, jurisdictions, goods, or payment routes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, as the French central bank within the Eurosystem; the European Central Bank is the competent monetary authority for the euro area. The French Treasury Directorate General of the Treasury and French customs authorities administer applicable external-payment reporting, trade, and foreign-investment controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for operating payments, collections, multicurrency balances, cards, and payment-service connectivity, subject to provider eligibility and risk appetite. An electronic-money institution or payment institution authorised in France or another EEA state may passport services into France, but it is not a bank unless separately licensed as a credit institution. Customer funds are generally safeguarded rather than covered by the French deposit-guarantee regime applicable to eligible bank deposits. Offshore digital EMIs without an appropriate EEA authorisation, passport, or French establishment cannot lawfully provide regulated payment or e-money services to French customers merely through an online interface. Fintech accounts may be unsuitable as the sole capital-deposit account for SAS formation or as the only account for regulated or higher-risk flows. Providers may impose transaction, country, currency, marketplace, chargeback, and reserve restrictions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Unclear ownership chain, nominee shareholders, bearer-like arrangements, or inability to identify the ultimate beneficial owner.

Defense evidence: Current French company extract from the Registre National des Entreprises or equivalent registration evidence; articles of association; shareholder register; ownership chart to the ultimate natural persons; beneficial-owner declaration; passports or national identity documents; residential-address evidence; director and authorised-signatory details; and a signed ownership and control certification.
Foreign shareholder, parent, director, or beneficial owner from a higher-risk, sanctioned, restricted, or opaque jurisdiction.

Root cause: Foreign shareholder, parent, director, or beneficial owner from a higher-risk, sanctioned, restricted, or opaque jurisdiction.

Defense evidence: Full corporate registry extracts for each foreign entity; constitutional documents; tax-residence certificates where relevant; certified identification; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds memorandum; audited accounts or bank statements; transaction rationale; sanctions-screening results; ownership-chain evidence; and written explanation of all jurisdictions involved.
Capital injection inconsistent with the shareholder profile, company business plan, or stated economic activity.

Defense evidence: Executed subscription or capital-increase documents; shareholder or board resolutions; bank transfer evidence; proof of source of funds; financial statements or tax returns of the investor; investment agreement; capitalization table; business plan; expected transaction volumes; and confirmation that funds are intended for share capital, share premium, shareholder loan, or another correctly documented category.
Newly incorporated SAS with no operating history, no French commercial footprint, or projected international volumes disproportionate to the business.

Root cause: Newly incorporated SAS with no operating history, no French commercial footprint, or projected international volumes disproportionate to the business.

Payments involving high-risk countries, sanctioned parties, restricted goods, dual-use items, or unusual intermediaries.

Incomplete tax, accounting, or corporate compliance information, including missing annual accounts, tax identifiers, or beneficial-owner filings.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use an account held in the SAS's name or, during formation, a compliant capital-deposit account opened for the company under formation. Route funds through SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments or SWIFT cross-border transfer for non-SEPA currencies. Identify the remitter as the shareholder or documented lender and use accurate payment references such as share capital subscription, share premium contribution, or shareholder loan; France does not use a universal statutory foreign-exchange purpose-code system. For a capital contribution, provide the bank and depositary with the subscription form, shareholder or board resolutions, articles, capitalization table, proof of remitter ownership, source-of-funds evidence, and formation or capital-increase documents. Do not mix share capital, share premium, shareholder loans, customer receipts, and operating funds without accounting and contractual classification. The bank may request an LEI, SIREN, VAT information, beneficiary-owner evidence, and supporting documents under AML rules. Verify beneficiary name, IBAN, BIC, currency, fees, intermediary-bank requirements, sanctions status, and receipt reconciliation. Record the transaction in the statutory accounts and preserve transfer evidence.For euro suppliers within SEPA, use SEPA Credit Transfer with beneficiary-name and IBAN verification and invoice-based approval. For non-SEPA suppliers, use SWIFT customer credit transfer, commonly documented through an MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, with accurate originator, beneficiary, purpose, invoice, goods description, and intermediary-bank information. Use commercial documentary letters of credit where supplier, country, or performance risk justifies them; the bank may issue an import LC subject to agreed rules such as UCP 600, with documentary examination, margin, collateral, and sanctions review. Alternative instruments include documentary collections, bank guarantees, and open-account payments with dual approval. Match invoices, purchase orders, customs records, shipping documents, and payment amounts; screen counterparties and banks; retain evidence of import or export compliance; and avoid third-party payments unless contractually justified and approved.
