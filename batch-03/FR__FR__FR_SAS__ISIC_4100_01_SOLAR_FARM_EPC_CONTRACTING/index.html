Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office in France and provide a valid address capable of receiving official notices. A domiciliation company may be used if it is authorized under the Commercial Code and the domiciliation agreement satisfies statutory requirements. A simple virtual mailbox, accommodation without lawful consent, or non-operational address is insufficient. For utility-scale photovoltaic EPC activities, a virtual office may be acceptable for incorporation and administrative headquarters only; it is not a substitute for operational premises, project offices, equipment storage, site safety arrangements, employees or subcontractor management where required by contracts, labor law, insurance, tax, environmental or construction rules. Construction activity may require professional competence, appropriate liability insurance, sector registrations and project-specific permits, including environmental, planning, grid-connection and electrical compliance approvals.

Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory company-name reservation mechanism with a standard 30-day reservation period. Conduct an INPI name search, RNE/RCS search, trademark search through INPI and EUIPO, domain-name search and sector-specific regulatory review before filing. If the Guichet unique, INPI, greffe or other competent authority rejects or suspends the filing, obtain the written reason, correct the name or supporting documents where possible, and submit the requested modification through the portal within the stated cure period. A formal challenge may be brought before the competent administrative or judicial authority depending on the contested act; a refusal based on a corporate-register filing issue may require referral to the competent commercial court or registry judge, while a trademark or administrative decision follows its specific appeal route. The filing does not create trademark protection, and separate trademark registration should be considered.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy and Finance; taxpayer portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; business formalities portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; non-resident business tax office: Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign entity: (i) register the French SAS or foreign branch through the Guichet unique (INPI) for RCS/INSEE registration; (ii) obtain SIREN (9 digits) and SIRET (14 digits) from INSEE; (iii) file VAT registration with the competent SIE/SIEE using Cerfa M0/M1 or electronic procedure; (iv) receive VAT number FR + 2-digit key + SIREN. Non-EU entities may register directly without a fiscal representative for VAT since 1 January 2022. For a non-resident without French establishment, file form 11963 or via the impots.gouv.fr professional space; appoint a tax representative only if required for specific regimes.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the standard rate of 25% (2025). A reduced rate of 15% applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs. Worldwide taxation with territorial exemption for foreign branch profits under Article 209 A CGI. No pass-through default; no tax exemption zone. — For a foreign entity: (i) register the French SAS or foreign branch through the Guichet unique (INPI) for RCS/INSEE registration; (ii) obtain SIREN (9 digits) and SIRET (14 digits) from INSEE; (iii) file VAT registration with the competent SIE/SIEE using Cerfa M0/M1 or electronic procedure; (iv) receive VAT number FR + 2-digit key + SIREN. Non-EU entities may register directly without a fiscal representative for VAT since 1 January 2022. For a non-resident without French establishment, file form 11963 or via the impots.gouv.fr professional space; appoint a tax representative only if required for specific regimes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the standard rate of 25% (2025). A reduced rate of 15% applies to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs. Worldwide taxation with territorial exemption for foreign branch profits under Article 209 A CGI. No pass-through default; no tax exemption zone.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box. Available elections: (i) partnership tax regime (régime des sociétés de personnes) for qualifying SAS under Article 239 bis AB CGI; (ii) tax consolidation group (intégration fiscale) under Articles 223 A et seq. CGI for 95%-owned subsidiaries; (iii) VAT group (assujettissement unique) under Article 256 C CGI since 1 January 2023; (iv) option for VAT on real estate transactions; (v) hybrid mismatch rules under Article 205 B CGI and ATAD2 apply automatically; no elective hybrid mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for B2B taxable supplies; VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction. Micro-enterprise franchise en base thresholds (2025): EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services (tolerance EUR 93,500 / EUR 41,250). These thresholds do not apply to an EPC contractor making mandatory B2B supplies. Intra-EU acquisitions and reverse-charge services require registration irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: exports outside the EU are exempt under Article 262-I CGI, with proof of exit from EU customs territory. Services: B2B services located outside France under Article 259-1 CGI are outside the scope of French VAT; offshore services to non-EU recipients are zero-rated. For EPC solar farm construction in France, domestic supplies are standard-rated at 20%; export zero-rating applies only to separable exported goods or services supplied to non-French recipients.

  • Customs export declaration (DAU / EX-A) validated by French customs
  • Bill of lading, CMR, or air waybill
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of payment / inward remittance certificate (bank SWIFT/MT103) where applicable
  • Transport and insurance documents
  • For services: contract, invoice, proof of recipient's non-EU establishment, and evidence of payment

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting reform: from 1 September 2026, all VAT-registered companies must be able to receive electronic invoices; large and mid-sized companies must issue e-invoices. From 1 September 2027, all companies must issue e-invoices. Invoices must transit through a registered PDP (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) using Factur-X, UBL or CII formats; e-reporting of B2C and international transactions to DGFiP. Chorus Pro mandatory for B2G. No Italian-style real-time clearance per invoice, but centralized directory and reporting obligations.

French tax authority may audit within 3 years (10 years for concealed activity or unreported foreign accounts). If export zero-rating is unsubstantiated, output VAT at 20% is reassessed with late-payment interest of 0.20% per month. Penalties: 40% for deliberate breach, 80% for fraudulent breach under Article 1729 CGI; criminal penalties under Article 1741 CGI up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 500,000 fine (or 5x proceeds). Joint and several liability may apply to recipients and managers. France has no general FX repatriation deadline; exchange controls abolished.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility. As a eurozone Member State, France applies unrestricted current and capital account convertibility under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. No capital controls apply to inbound equity or shareholder loans, dividend or profit repatriation, or repayment of intercompany advances. Non-EU investments are screened only for (i) public-order and sensitive-sector authorization (Articles L151-3 to L151-8 CMF) and (ii) EU/UN/OFAC sanctions; neither imposes convertibility or permit constraints on remittance flows. For a French SAS, profit extraction occurs via dividends or interest, which are subject to standard French taxation: CIT at 25% (or 15% on the first EUR 42,500 for qualifying small businesses), 12.8% withholding under the Prélèvement Forfaitaire Unique, a 0% rate under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU parent shareholders, or reduced treaty rates for non-EU shareholders upon submission of a beneficial-ownership certificate. No FX conversion permits, no repatriation licences, and no minimum capital stay period are applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender and no forced conversion. France imposes no statutory export-proceeds repatriation deadline and no mandatory conversion ratio into euros. Export receipts may be held in EUR or in any fully convertible currency. Nevertheless, each cross-border remittance (EUR 15,000 or above in equivalent, and all direct-investment transactions regardless of amount) must be reported electronically by the applicable French bank to the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistics system (Article R152-3-1 CMF; EU Regulation (EC) No 184/2005). The exporter is required to place the correct Banque de France 'motif monétaire' code on the wire instruction, using the category for merchandise exports or for construction/engineering services as applicable, and to retain underlying export contracts and proforma invoices in the compliance file. Foreign-currency balances retained at year-end must be revalued at the ECB/closing spot rate for corporate tax and IFRS purposes, with unrealised FX gains or losses being taxable or deductible as applicable.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) acting as the micro-prudential banking and AML/CFT supervisor, and the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) exercising foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments regulatory competence under Articles L151-3 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium - legal constraints apply. ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and EU-passported payment institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business, Payoneer) may legally open a EUR corporate account for the SAS and provide IBANs, SEPA/SCT, SWIFT MT103 receivables, and retail-grade FX conversion. However, EMIs cannot issue commercial Letters of Credit, open standby credits, grant credit facilities, act as escrow or security agent, or deal in FX derivatives; such activities are reserved to credit institutions under CMF Articles L511-1 and L522-1. EMI usage is therefore viable only for pass-through operational collections and sub-contractor disbursements up to a practical guideline of EUR 500,000 per month. Cross-border milestone payments backed by LC undertakings and escrow arrangements must be routed through a Tier-1 credit institution. The recommended treasury architecture is hybrid: Tier-1 bank for LC/escrow and project finance flows, plus an EMI for operational low-value FX-sensitive payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: contributing shareholders credit the SAS EUR IBAN (FR76 + BBAN) via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COV from the remitting bank (e.g., BNP Paris BIC BNPAFRPP). The payment instruction must state the purpose as 'AUGMENTATION DE CAPITAL' for equity or 'APPORT EN COMPTE COURANT D'ASSOCIÉ - CONTRACT N°...' for shareholder loans, and carry the Banque de France balance-of-payments motif code corresponding to direct investment (equity) or intercompany loan, using the receiving bank's published 'motif monétaire' list. Supporting executed board resolutions and share subscription documents must be transmitted to the bank via its trade confirmation interface. EU/EEA-sourced funds require no exchange-control authorisation; non-EU funds are sanctions-screened and reported to the DG Trésor/Banque de France. A capital increase must first be placed in a segregated notarial or depositary 'Compte Séquestre' (compte de blocage), followed by issuance of the attestation de dépôt des fonds, filing of RCS formalities, and only then release to the operational account. The receiving bank automatically reports all transfers of EUR 15,000 or more to the Banque de France, and an attestation de transfert is retained for tax and audit evidence.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 single commercial payment executed by the Tier-1 bank for milestone disbursements to sub-contractors and equipment suppliers. For trade settlements requiring bank undertakings, use SWIFT MT700 (issuance of irrevocable documentary credit), MT707 (amendment), MT710/MT720 (third-bank advice and transferable credit), all governed by ICC UCP 600, with MT798 pre-advice and MT752/MT754 for acceptance or negotiation. Euro-denominated EEA payments are cleared via SEPA Instant or SEPA Credit Transfer (IBAN only, BIC no longer required domestically). Non-EEA contract payables (e.g., PV module suppliers in Asia) require the MT103 to include the Banque de France balance-of-payments motif code for goods or construction services, and must comply with EU Regulation (EU) 2015/847 on information accompanying transfers (funds traceability). All outbound cross-border transfers of EUR 15,000 or more are auto-reported to the Banque de France. Back-to-back LCs, trust-receipt loans, and forfaiting are admissible treasury tools provided each documentary draw satisfies UCP 600 and the bank's compliance sign-off; payments to sanctioned jurisdictions require prior DG Trésor licensing where licences are obtainable.FX Outright Forward (Achat/Vente à Terme) in EUR/USD, EUR/CNY and EUR/INR under ISDA 2002 or an AMF French Master Agreement, covering up to 12 months of firm EPC receivables and payablesCross-Currency Swap (XCCY/CCS) aligning EUR treasury debt service with PPA-indexed or milestone-based project revenues over the full construction and defects-liability termPlain Vanilla FX Options (EUR call and put) for asymmetric hedging of tender-pipeline exposures, priced at AMF-regulated market levelsFX Swap (Swap de Devises) for temporary conversion of milestone USD inflows into EUR while preserving currency exposure within defined prudential limits

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium - legal constraints apply. ACPR-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) and EU-passported payment institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Revolut Business, Payoneer) may legally open a EUR corporate account for the SAS and provide IBANs, SEPA/SCT, SWIFT MT103 receivables, and retail-grade FX conversion. However, EMIs cannot issue commercial Letters of Credit, open standby credits, grant credit facilities, act as escrow or security agent, or deal in FX derivatives; such activities are reserved to credit institutions under CMF Articles L511-1 and L522-1. EMI usage is therefore viable only for pass-through operational collections and sub-contractor disbursements up to a practical guideline of EUR 500,000 per month. Cross-border milestone payments backed by LC undertakings and escrow arrangements must be routed through a Tier-1 credit institution. The recommended treasury architecture is hybrid: Tier-1 bank for LC/escrow and project finance flows, plus an EMI for operational low-value FX-sensitive payments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 (group consolidated turnover) for full Master File and Local File; simplified documentation required for entities with turnover below this threshold but above EUR 50 million.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but applies general anti-abuse rules (e.g., Article 155-A of the French Tax Code) and substance-over-form principles. A French SAS must demonstrate real economic activity (employees, premises, decision-making) to avoid challenges under the anti-abuse doctrine and to benefit from treaty or EU directive relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty of 80% of the evaded tax (or 40% if no deliberate intent), plus interest at 0.20% per month. Criminal prosecution may result in fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they have committed fraud or used the company as a shield. Civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment (up to 5 years) are possible.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 40% penalty on additional tax assessed. The company may be deemed a sham and dissolved.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for taxes and penalties if they knowingly facilitated the artificial arrangement. Travel bans and restrictions on leaving France may be imposed.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 5% of turnover for serious breaches. Criminal penalties include imprisonment up to 5 years and fines up to €375,000.

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment and fines, if they knowingly participate in or fail to prevent money laundering. Civil liability for damages and travel bans may also apply.
