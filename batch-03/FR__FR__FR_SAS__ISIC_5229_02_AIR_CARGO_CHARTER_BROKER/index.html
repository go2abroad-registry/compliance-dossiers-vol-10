Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office in France under the Commercial Code, including Articles L123-11 and following and Articles R123-167 and following. A domiciliation company may be used if it is authorized under applicable French rules and provides the required domiciliation agreement and documentation. A virtual office is not a substitute for operational substance. For an air-cargo charter broker, it is generally acceptable for the registered office to be a compliant domiciliation address during the start-up phase, but the company should maintain accessible records, a functioning telephone and email presence, competent management, contracts, accounting records, bank controls, sanctions procedures, and documented brokerage operations in France. Banking, tax, customs, aviation, and AML counterparties may reject a purely nominal office.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) or Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE), using the official impots.gouv.fr portal. Customs matters are administered separately by the Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI), through the French customs online systems.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS must register through the business formalities single window operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). The filing transmits corporate information to the competent registries and tax authorities. The entity receives a SIREN identification number, a SIRET number for each establishment, and, where liable to VAT, a French intra-Community VAT identification number in the format FR followed by a two-digit validation key and the nine-digit SIREN. The SAS must provide its registered-office details, legal-form information, beneficial-owner information, activity description, commencement date, accounting period, bank details where requested, and tax-option information. A non-resident entity without a French establishment applies to the SIEE for French tax registration and a VAT number using the applicable foreign-entity registration process and supporting documents, including constitutional documents, tax-residence evidence, activity and supply details, identification of the French tax representative or intermediary where required, and evidence of taxable French transactions. EU-established businesses generally do not need a fiscal representative for French VAT; non-EU businesses may require one unless an applicable legal exemption or mutual-assistance arrangement applies. Registration is not itself an authorization to conduct regulated air-transport or customs activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS). An eligible SAS may elect taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu, or IR) for a maximum period of five financial years if statutory conditions are satisfied, including limits relating to workforce, turnover or balance-sheet size, age of the company, ownership, and qualifying operating activity. The election requires notification to the competent tax authority within the prescribed period and is generally not a check-the-box election. French law does not provide a general United States-style check-the-box classification election for SAS entities. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules may deny deductions or impose inclusion consequences; they do not create a general hybrid-election mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the French domestic VAT franchise regime, the ordinary annual turnover threshold is generally EUR 37,500 for services, including most brokerage and intermediary services, and EUR 85,000 for goods and certain commercial activities. Tolerance thresholds are generally EUR 41,250 for services and EUR 93,500 for goods. A taxable business may voluntarily register below these thresholds. The thresholds do not eliminate registration obligations for particular cross-border transactions, domestic taxable supplies by non-established businesses, intra-Community acquisitions, imports, or transactions subject to reverse charge. A French SAS conducting taxable domestic brokerage services should obtain VAT registration before commencing taxable activity where required, rather than relying solely on the franchise threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT under the French implementation of the EU export rules, provided the exporter can establish that the goods left the EU customs territory and satisfies the documentary and reporting requirements. The usual evidence includes an accepted electronic export declaration and customs confirmation of exit. For air-cargo charter brokerage, the brokerage or intermediary service is normally analyzed under the place-of-supply rules rather than treated automatically as a zero-rated export of goods. Business-to-business services supplied to a customer established outside France may be outside the scope of French VAT under the principal B2B place-of-supply rule, subject to the nature of the service and anti-abuse provisions. Certain transport-related and intermediary services may qualify for a specific exemption only where the statutory conditions are met. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, a direct business purpose, proper accounting records, and evidence that the underlying transactions are taxable, exempt with deduction rights, or outside the scope with a deduction entitlement.

  • Electronic export declaration filed through the French customs DELTA system or the applicable EU customs system, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs confirmation of exit from the European Union, such as the electronic exit result or equivalent customs exit evidence
  • Commercial invoice, contract or purchase order, packing or cargo documentation, and transport document such as an air waybill or equivalent
  • Evidence linking the exporter, goods, consignee, transport movement, and declared transaction
  • Accounting records and VAT invoice records supporting the claimed exemption or deduction
  • For services or brokerage outside the scope of French VAT: customer identification, customer VAT or tax status where relevant, contract, service description, place-of-supply analysis, invoice wording, and payment evidence
  • Bank payment evidence where relevant to commercial substantiation; France does not generally require an inward-remittance certificate as a universal statutory condition for export VAT exemption

France does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system. The electronic invoicing reform requires all French-established businesses to be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Issuing obligations for domestic business-to-business invoices are phased in from 1 September 2026 for large and intermediate enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises. Invoices must generally be transmitted through an approved private electronic-invoicing platform or an authorized public channel, with structured data reported to the French tax administration. Electronic reporting of relevant business-to-consumer and cross-border transaction data is also being phased in. Existing rules still require invoice integrity, authenticity, legibility, sequential numbering, mandatory VAT particulars, retention, and electronic archiving. There is no general mandatory digital tax stamp for ordinary VAT invoices.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export VAT treatment. The taxpayer must retain reliable evidence of the export or applicable place-of-supply treatment and cooperate with DGFiP and DGDDI audits. If export exemption or input-VAT recovery is unsupported, the administration may reassess output VAT, deny or claw back input-VAT deductions or refunds, and charge late-payment interest generally at 0.20% per month, subject to applicable statutory rules. Tax penalties may include 10% for late or deficient payment, 40% for deliberate failure, 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, and potentially 100% for obstructing an audit, with increased consequences for concealed activity or serious fraud. Criminal prosecution may apply to deliberate tax fraud. The normal reassessment period is generally three years for VAT, with extended periods in specified fraud, concealed-activity, or criminal circumstances. Any refund obtained through false export evidence may be recovered with interest and penalties, and customs violations may additionally trigger customs debt, administrative fines, seizure, and loss of customs simplifications.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies the EU framework for free movement of capital and payments. The euro is freely convertible, and there are no general exchange controls or routine approval requirements for a French SAS to make or receive cross-border commercial or investment transfers. Current-account payments, dividends, and capital movements are generally transferable, subject to corporate-law formalities, tax compliance, sanctions, anti-money-laundering controls, and bank documentation. Dividend remittances require distributable profits, valid corporate approval, and applicable withholding-tax and treaty analysis. Banks may request contracts, invoices, beneficial-ownership information, and source-of-funds evidence, and may delay or reject transactions under compliance policies. Certain cross-border financial transactions may be subject to statistical reporting under French rules; the company and its bank should confirm applicable reporting obligations for the transaction and counterparty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender or convert export proceeds into euros and no general mandatory conversion ratio or export-receipt conversion deadline. Export proceeds may be retained in permitted domestic or foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank terms, accounting, tax, sanctions, and applicable reporting requirements. Reconcile receipts to invoices and retain supporting documents. Cash or certain bearer instruments crossing the EU external border at EUR 10,000 or more are subject to declaration requirements; this is distinct from ordinary bank transfers.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France is France’s central bank and part of the Eurosystem. The European Central Bank sets euro-area monetary policy. The Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR), within the Banque de France, supervises authorized French banks and certain financial institutions. The Direction générale du Trésor administers relevant financial regulations and external-financial reporting requirements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro collections and payments through an appropriately authorized EU payment institution or electronic-money institution; low as a substitute for a full-service French corporate bank where cash, credit, trade finance, or complex foreign-currency services are needed. Verify the provider’s authorization and passporting status in the relevant official register. An EMI account is not a bank deposit and does not provide deposit-guarantee protection. Offshore providers do not remove French AML, sanctions, tax, accounting, or reporting obligations; banks and counterparties may restrict cargo-charter activity or high-risk routes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million in annual turnover or total assets for mandatory Local File and Master File under Article L13 AA LPF; Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Below the threshold, Article 57 CGI still requires arm's-length pricing and the tax administration may request simplified documentation in an audit.

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation, but the abuse-of-law doctrine under Article L64 LPF, the general anti-abuse rule under Article 205-A CGI, and the OECD principal purpose test require the SAS to show actual personnel, premises, commercial functions, and decision-making in France. A shell company used to channel air cargo charter brokerage commissions through France without substance is recharacterized, and the 80% abuse penalty applies.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale – Article 1741 CGI)

Statutory sanction: Recovery of evaded tax plus 40% bad-faith or 80% fraudulent penalty under Article 1729 CGI; late-payment interest at 0.20% per month; criminal fine up to €1,000,000 and imprisonment up to seven years in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false returns may be jointly and severally liable for the tax debt under Article L267 LPF, subject to asset seizure, professional interdiction up to fifteen years, travel bans, and custodial sentences; limited liability is pierced where the director personally participated in fraudulent acts.
