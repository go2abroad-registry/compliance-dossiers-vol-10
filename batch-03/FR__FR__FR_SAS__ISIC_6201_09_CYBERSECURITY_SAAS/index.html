Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for SAS; capital is freely set in the articles. Cash contributions must be deposited with a bank or notary before incorporation, and at least one-half of cash contributions must be paid up upon incorporation (balance within five years). In-kind contributions must be fully delivered at subscription.

Funding & unlock path: Office-only operations do not require a special pre-occupancy fire certificate for private premises; however, the employer must maintain evacuation procedures, accessible fire extinguishers, and conduct a workplace risk assessment. Employer obligations include displaying emergency instructions and, from 11 employees, establishing a CSE (Comité Social et Économique).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; business portal: pro.impots.gouv.fr; general portal: impots.gouv.fr; customs portal: douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entity establishing a French SAS: (1) file incorporation through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); (2) DGFIP automatically attributes SIREN and SIRET; (3) VAT registration occurs together with company registration, resulting in an intra-Community TVA number in the Sirene registry. For a foreign seller with no French fixed establishment, use the Union OSS/Non-Union OSS or IOSS portals for eligible distance sales and digital services; for pure B2B operations, apply for simplified French VAT registration at the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and obtain a France-specific TVA number. Non-EU sellers without a fixed establishment must ordinarily appoint a DGFIP-accredited fiscal representative under Article 289 A of the French General Tax Code (CGI) before registering for VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election. The default for an SAS is mandatory corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Specific elections include: (i) option for income-tax treatment under Article 239 bis AB CGI for qualifying small/young companies; (ii) election to form a French tax consolidation group (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI where at least 95% of capital is held; (iii) hybrid-mismatch outcomes are not elective and follow the EU ATAD transposition rules under Article 212-0 et seq. CGI, requiring documentation of any mismatch treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established company, no turnover threshold exists; VAT registration is mandatory before carrying on any taxable activity. For an EU seller making intra-EU distance sales of goods to French consumers, the applicable cross-border distance-sales registration threshold is EUR 10,000 per calendar year (option to register below this threshold exists). For a non-EU seller supplying digital or B2C services into France, there is no monetary threshold; registration is required through the Non-Union OSS scheme or direct French VAT registration. For B2B supplies, a non-established foreign seller of services to a French taxable customer is not required to register in France because the place of supply is at the customer's location and reverse-charge applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods qualify for the 0% export rate (exemption with right to input VAT deduction) when the goods are physically delivered to a destination outside the EU; proof of dispatch and EU exit is mandatory. Offshore services and cross-border digital services supplied to a non-resident business customer are treated as made outside France under the B2B place-of-supply rule in Article 259-A CGI; therefore they are outside French territoriality rather than formally zero-rated. For a cybersecurity SaaS product supplied to a non-EU enterprise, no French VAT should be applied if the customer provides a valid foreign VAT/TIN and the substantive B2B tests are met; the supplier must retain service contracts, customer tax status evidence, and use-case documentation to support the territorial analysis. Services supplied to non-taxable overseas customers may qualify as outside scope or zero-rated only where specific CGI provisions apply and the supplier can substantiate offshore consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DELTA electronic export log / DAU-ENS) accepted by Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI)
  • Transport document proving physical exit from France and the EU: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order, and bank financing evidence such as an Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 payment record

France is not a real-time fiscal stamping jurisdiction. Under the French e-invoicing reform (2022 administrative simplification law), B2B e-invoicing is mandatory for established taxable persons through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) connected to the public Portal Public de Facturation (PPF); B2C and international transaction data are subject to mandatory e-reporting. The rollout is phased: large and mid-sized entities must issue e-invoices from 1 September 2026 and small businesses from 1 September 2027. Standardised formats include Factur-X, UBL, CII, Peppol BIS; API connectivity is required. No DGFIP clearance stamp is applied to individual invoices; transmission is periodic, accompanied by audit-trail retention requirements. OSS/IOSS filings use separate European Commission portal infrastructure.

DGFIP and French Customs can retrospectively deny export or outside-scope treatment where supporting documentation is missing, forged, or otherwise insufficient. Reassessment triggers VAT clawback plus delayed-payment interest and the application of penalties under French tax law: 40% bad-faith penalty, 80% penalty for organised or fraudulent conduct, and criminal sanctions under Article 1741 CGI up to EUR 75,000 fine and five years' imprisonment. A false customs declaration can also lead to suspension or revocation of AEO/customs standing and exclusion from simplified OSS/IOSS schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully and freely convertible under EU law (Articles 63-66 TFEU); France maintains no exchange-control licensing regime for current-account items (SaaS licensing fees, hosting fees, service remuneration) or capital-account items (equity injections, shareholder loans, distributions). No profit-remittance authorization, conversion licence, or transfer restriction applies to OECD/EU counterparties. The de facto hurdles are limited to (i) the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical declaration ('motif'-coded cross-border payment reporting performed by the resident bank for transactions above EUR 12,500), (ii) French dividend withholding tax applicable to foreign shareholders (12.8% flat tax for individuals; 25% corporate withholding under Article 119 bis CGI, both reduced by applicable double-tax treaties), and (iii) the arm's-length and substance requirements applicable to intercompany treasury balances. None of these hurdles limits convertibility of the euro.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France enforces no export-proceeds surrender or forced conversion requirement: the mandatory conversion ratio is 0% and no statutory surrender timeline exists. Export receipts (SaaS subscription and cybersecurity service revenue) may be retained, pooled, and settled in any freely convertible currency. However, under EU monetary statistics Regulation (EC) No 184/2005 and the Banque de France balance-of-payments instruction, the resident exporter must ensure its intermediary bank reports cross-border receipts exceeding EUR 12,500 within five business days of month-end using the 'motif' (purpose-of-payment) taxonomy. Supporting documentary evidence (electronic invoices, licensing agreements) must be retained for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the European System of Central Banks; foreign-exchange statistics and balance-of-payments compliance are co-administered with the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) and the Direction Générale du Trésor of the French Ministry of Economy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

High for subscription-collection operations: regulated EU EMIs with an ACPR passport (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Memo Bank) and EU-passported digital banks (Revolut, N26) provide competitive IBAN-based SEPA C2B/B2B rails. Low for treasury-critical functions: EMIs are not full credit institutions and cannot (i) hold capital-escrow balances for SAS statutory capital-blocking certificates, (ii) file the Banque de France 'motif' balance-of-payments statistical declarations on behalf of the client, or (iii) execute USD/EUR/GBP FX hedging products. Non-EU/offshore EMIs (UK, US, HK licence) have no legal viability in France without an ACPR/ECB passport under PSD2; unlicensed firms engaging in French payment services commit the criminal offence of unlawful exercise of payment services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-loan flows are cleared by SWIFT MT103 over Target2 (EUR) or correspondent nostro rails (USD/GBP) into the SAS's French beneficiary account (IBAN FR76...; BIC of the receiving Tier-1 bank). The remitting bank must encode the Banque de France balance-of-payments 'motif' for direct investment ('Investissements directs - apport en capital') in SWIFT field 77B/remittance information; the receiving bank verifies against the statutory declaration threshold (EUR 12,500) and files the aggregated declaration within five business days of month-end. The founder must pre-deliver to the bank (a) the capital-increase deed and depositary fund-blocking certificate, (b) the shareholder resolution, and (c) a source-of-funds pack from the remitting institution; proceeds are released to the operational account only after the capital increase is registered with the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce.For cloud infrastructure (AWS/Azure/GCP), subcontractors, and software licences, the clearing rails are (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border non-EU settlements, with MT202 COV as cover when a correspondent intermediary is used; (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Direct Debit Core/B2B for EU supplier payables and payroll, settled on Target2/TIPS; (iii) commercial card rails (Visa/Mastercard virtual corporate cards) for recurring platform purchases; and (iv) standby letters of credit (SBLC) or UCP 600 documentary credits only when counterparties in physical-infrastructure jurisdictions require bank payment undertakings, which is commercially atypical for SaaS treasury.
