Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), France / French Tax Authority. VAT registration and filings are managed by the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) local tax office; corporate registrations occur through the Guichet Unique des Formalités (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); e-invoicing will be cleared through the French Public Billing Portal (PPF) and certified dematerialisation platforms (PDPs).

E-government filing portal

For a French-established SAS, incorporation via the Guichet Unique automatically generates the SIREN/SIRET business identifiers and the French intra-Community VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) by INSEE and DGFiP. The SAS must subsequently register with the SIE for corporate income tax and VAT liabilities. For a non-resident entity performing taxable French supplies without a French permanent establishment, registration is effected by submitting form 1656 or the simplified VAT registration form to the local SIE, after appointing a tax representative when required for non-EU sellers; the authority issues a French VAT number (FR + 11 digits). Any cross-border goods operator must also obtain an EORI number (FR prefix) for customs/export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not employ a US-style check-the-box election. An SAS is subject to corporation tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) by default at 25% for standard profits. However, a small SAS may opt for transparency and be taxed as an income-tax partnership (régime des sociétés de personnes under CGI Art. 239 bis D) provided it has fewer than 5 employees, turnover below €10,000,000, and satisfies shareholder eligibility conditions. An SAS can also elect to form a French VAT grouping under Article 257 C CGI, to join a fiscal consolidation regime for IS (intégration fiscale), and, for cross-border structures, must observe France's anti-hybrid mismatch rules (CGI Art. 212 bis) transposing ATAD – no elective hybrid mismatch exit is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business supplying goods such as automotive aftermarket parts, the franchise de base de TVA (VAT exemption threshold) is €85,000 of annual turnover for supplies of goods, with a tolerance band up to €93,500. Once turnover exceeds the €93,500 tolerance, VAT registration becomes mandatory. For intra-EU distance sales of goods, a pan-European €10,000 threshold applies for the EU OSS regime; above that, VAT registration is required in each destination Member State or via the Union OSS. Non-established foreign sellers making taxable French sales have no general EU turnover threshold and are required to register for French VAT from the first transaction in France unless an OSS/import regime applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to supplies constituting an export to a destination outside the EU (CGI Art. 262 ter I) and to intra-Community dispatches (CGI Art. 262 ter II). For exports of goods, the seller must evidence the goods physically left France and the EU within the statutory timeframes (customs summary declarations and proof of departure). For intra-EU supplies, a valid EU VAT number of the acquirer, an EC Sales List, and the dispatch/transport evidence are required. Offshore services (including certain digital/e-commerce services) supplied to an economic operator located outside the EU are zero-rated under CGI Art. 259 A where the effective use and enjoyment of service are outside the EU. The taxpayer must retain contemporaneous evidence including contracts, payment documents, and certification of the customer's business status.

  • Combined customs export declaration (Déclaration Unique d'Exportation / DAU) of the good leaving France/EU
  • International transport documentation: Bill of Lading (B/L), Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or postal export label
  • Commercial invoice issued by the exporting SAS
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank settlement attestation proving receipt of foreign currency or euros from the foreign buyer
  • Proof of the customer's overseas taxable status (VAT registration certificate or equivalent)
  • EC Sales List / Intrastat declaration for intra-Community dispatches, where applicable
  • EORI number and customs export movement identification (MRN) confirmation

France has implemented a phased mandatory e-invoicing reform aligned with the E-invoice Directive. Real-time fiscal clearance is not yet active, but mandatory structured e-invoicing (Factur-X, UBL, CII) and electronic transmission (EDI) of transaction data are required through certified dematerialisation platforms (PDPs). For VAT purposes, the reform is effective from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-size taxpayers and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises. The tax administration receives invoice data through the Public Invoicing Portal (PPF); no physical stamping or real-time pre-clearance is required, though eligible PDPs must transmit automatically to the PPF.

Export zero-rating is examined strictly by DGFiP and Customs (DGDDI). Failure to substantiate the export within the legal periods triggers retrospective VAT assessment at the 20% standard rate, plus late-payment interest (0.20% per month) and penalties. Deliberate concealment or misuse of zero-rating may attract the 80% penalty under CGI Art. 1729, possible criminal liability for tax fraud under CGI Art. 1741 (up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of €75,000, or 500,000€ for organised fraud), plus a 5-year statutory limitation (10 years for hidden/fraudulent activity) and potential denial of refund claims. Directors of a French SAS may be held personally liable for fraudulent export filings.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a Eurozone member, maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for all EUR transactions and for cross-border dealings with non-EU counterparties, subject only to EU financial sanctions and anti-money-laundering filters. Profit remittance of distributable reserves by the SAS to foreign shareholders is unrestricted, though dividend payments attract domestic withholding tax at 30% unless reduced under a DTT (e.g., US-France 0% or 15% under entitlement to benefits). No prior exchange-license authorization is required for online retail export proceeds or supplier payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No legal obligation to surrender, repatriate, or convert export proceeds exists because the EUR is the lawful currency of the receiving company; a French resident SAS may freely retain foreign currency balances. All cross-border payments routed through resident credit institutions are captured at the transactional level for the Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting (BASE-EC), with the intermediary bank acting as declarant; no affirmative filing by the SAS is required for individual export receipts below EUR 50,000, while larger flows are aggregated and reported by the PSP.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) acting as the monetary and financial stability authority, with prudential supervision of credit institutions and electronic money institutions delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution); the Direction Générale du Trésor administers balance-of-payments statistics and investment controls under the Article 63 TFEU free-movement-of-capital framework.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2022

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% on gross French-source dividends paid to non-residents under CGI Article 119 bis 2. A 0% rate applies under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive when the EU corporate shareholder holds 5% or more of the capital and has held it for at least 24 months.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a fiscal year ending 31 December, the return must be filed by the second business day after 1 May, typically 2 or 3 May. For a non-calendar fiscal year, filing must occur within three months after the fiscal year-end. CIT instalments are due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December; the balance is payable with the annual return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 (net consolidated turnover excluding VAT, whether of the French head company or of the foreign parent group); a simplified documentation obligation applies to entities conducting cross-border transactions with related parties exceeding EUR 7,000,000 or transacting with entities located in tax havens.

Economic substance scope: France has no Economic Substance Regulation analogous to offshore ESR regimes. Nevertheless, the general anti-abuse doctrine (abus de droit, Article L.64 LPF) and the EU ATAD anti-abuse provisions deny tax treaty or directive benefits to structures lacking real economic substance. An SAS or non-resident PE must demonstrate actual business premises, employees, common governance, and substantive decisions made in France. Artificial ownership and profit allocation without operational substance will be recharacterized, with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude Fiscale

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax fraud under Article 1741 CGI is punishable for natural persons by up to 5 years' imprisonment and a EUR 75,000 fine; aggravated fraud can raise the sanction to 7 years' imprisonment and EUR 250,000 fine. The tax administration may apply a deliberate-failure penalty of 40% or a fraud penalty of 80% on the reassessed tax, plus late-payment interest at 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: The president of an SAS may be held personally liable under Article L.267 LPF for tax debts caused by fraudulent manoeuvres or repeated failures to pay. A criminal conviction may result in imprisonment, personal fines, commercial management bans, and a travel ban preventing the director from leaving French or EU territory.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the French taxable base to the arm's length result plus sanctions: 10% penalty if the omission is simple, 40% in bad faith, 80% for tax fraud, applied with 0.20% monthly late-payment interest. France also applies a 5% penalty on the amount of hidden distributions or profits painstakingly disguised intercompany transfers.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign manipulated filings or direct intercompany invoicing policy may be subject to personal tax liability under L.267 LPF and could be joint and severally liable for penalties where fraudulent acts are demonstrated. Criminal liability under Article 1741 also extends to active directors.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement (Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: Article L.64 LPF permits the tax authority to ignore the artificial transaction and tax the underlying economic reality. The taxpayer faces a 80% penalty for abus de droit in addition to reassessed tax and late-payment interest. Companies using abusive cross-border arrangements may also incur a penalty of 10% of the arranged amount under Article 1729(6) CGI.

Director personal liability: The director may be made personally liable for the resulting tax debt under Article L.267 LPF if the arrangement is an intentional sham. Criminal prosecution for fraud is possible, with imprisonment, fine, prohibition on managing a company, and a travel ban as potential ancillary measures.
Failure to Disclose Beneficial Ownership / AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Non-disclosure of beneficial owner information is punishable by a fine of up to EUR 15,000, and for legal persons up to EUR 75,000. Failure to register a commercial business (immatriculation au RCS) may result in rejection of the corporate tax return and legal standing issues. Payment intermediaries may freeze funds and report suspicious activity to TRACFIN.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly provide false beneficial ownership information may be held personally liable for fines and are exposed to criminal sanctions for false certificates. Personal assets can be attached after repeated non-compliance; travel bans and imprisonment can follow where AML notification violations accompany tax fraud.
