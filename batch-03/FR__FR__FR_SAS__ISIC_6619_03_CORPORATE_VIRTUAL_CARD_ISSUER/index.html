Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), including the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and, for non-established businesses, the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). Online tax services are provided through impots.gouv.fr; company formation and registration filings are submitted through the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate or register the Société par Actions Simplifiée through the INPI Guichet unique. 2. Obtain the company SIREN and SIRET identifiers from the Registre national des entreprises. 3. Open the professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr using the legal entity details and registration certificate. 4. File the relevant corporate tax and VAT registration information with the competent SIE. 5. The DGFiP assigns the French VAT identification number, generally formatted as FR followed by two check characters and the nine-digit SIREN. 6. A foreign entity without a French establishment must register with the SIEE where French VAT obligations arise; a non-EU entity may require an accredited French tax representative unless an applicable legal or administrative exception applies. 7. A regulated card issuer must separately obtain the applicable payment-services or electronic-money authorization from the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR); tax registration does not authorize payment activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A qualifying closely held SAS may elect temporary income-tax treatment under the statutory option applicable to certain newly formed companies, generally for a maximum of five fiscal years and subject to ownership, activity, age, and revenue conditions. There is no general United States-style check-the-box election. France applies statutory anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implementing EU ATAD requirements; those rules restrict deductions, deny duplicate deductions, or impose inclusion where relevant and are not elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the French domestic VAT exemption regime, the principal service threshold is EUR 37,500 of prior-year turnover, with a current-year tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250; the principal goods threshold is EUR 85,000, with a current-year tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500. Thresholds do not override mandatory registration for specified cross-border transactions, acquisitions, imports, or transactions outside the exemption regime. Financial services, payment transactions, credit, and certain related services may be VAT-exempt under Article 261 C of the French General Tax Code; taxable technology, management, platform, card-program, and ancillary services must be analyzed separately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT when the supplier holds the export customs declaration and evidence that the goods exited the EU, subject to the applicable French General Tax Code conditions. Services are generally governed by place-of-supply rules rather than a generic export zero rate: B2B services supplied to a business established outside France are commonly outside the scope of French VAT, while specified financial and payment services may be VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated. A corporate virtual-card issuer must distinguish exempt payment execution or credit-related services from taxable software, program-management, data, support, and other ancillary services. VAT refunds require valid invoices, customs evidence where applicable, proof of business use, and reconciliation to accounting and VAT returns.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and customs MRN or equivalent export reference
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent evidence that the goods left the European Union
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and transport document such as bill of lading, air waybill, or consignment note
  • Proof of delivery or destination evidence where required by the transaction and audit circumstances
  • Customer and supplier VAT identification data, contractual documentation, and VAT return reconciliation
  • Inward remittance evidence may support commercial substantiation but is not generally a universal statutory condition for French export VAT exemption

France has no general real-time invoice-clearance requirement equivalent to a continuous transaction-control model. Domestic business-to-business e-invoicing is being phased in through approved or registered platforms: receipt capability is scheduled for all businesses from 1 September 2026; issuance is scheduled from 1 September 2026 for large and intermediate enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises, subject to applicable implementation changes. Transaction data reporting (e-reporting) applies to relevant business-to-consumer and cross-border transactions. Public-sector invoices must generally be submitted through Chorus Pro. Invoices must satisfy French VAT content, authenticity, integrity, and archiving requirements; France does not generally require a government digital stamp for each invoice.

France has no general foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. The DGFiP may assess unpaid VAT, interest for late payment, and penalties where export conditions or exemption evidence are not substantiated. The principal penalty may be 40% for deliberate failure and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law, subject to statutory qualification and procedural safeguards; a 10% surcharge may apply for late payment or certain filing failures. Fraudulent VAT refund claims can be denied, clawed back with interest and penalties, and referred for criminal prosecution. Tax fraud may result in imprisonment and substantial fines, with enhanced sanctions in aggravated cases and potentially higher corporate penalties. Records, customs evidence, invoices, contracts, payment records, and VAT reconciliations should be retained for the applicable French accounting and tax limitation periods.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates within the Eurozone, ensuring full convertibility and free movement of the Euro for capital and current account transactions. Non-Euro currency transactions are subject to mandatory reporting to the Banque de France under EU Regulation 2018/1672. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are permitted without prior authorization provided they comply with corporate tax residency rules and do not exceed declared dividends; large or unusual remittances require supporting documentation to prevent capital flight classification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

French exporters must repatriate export proceeds within six months of shipment date. Mandatory conversion to Euro occurs at the official ECB reference rate. All receipts require prior customs declaration via Douane and subsequent reporting to tax authorities (DGFiP) via the e-BAC system. Banks enforce surrender through account verification and may impose penalties for non-compliance.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

30% under Article 119-bis-2 CGI for dividend distributions to non-resident shareholders, reduced to 0% for qualifying corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (direct or indirect holding of at least 10% for at least two years); most tax treaties provide a reduced rate of 15% for portfolio dividends and 5% for direct investment dividends.
