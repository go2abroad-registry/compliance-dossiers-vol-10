Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The articles must state the capital amount and share terms. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription, and the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Cash contributions are generally deposited into a blocked account or with a notary before incorporation, against a deposit certificate; funds are released after issuance of the registration extract. Legal-publication fees, beneficial ownership fees, notarial fees, and bank charges are excluded from the stated filing fee. The USD amount is an indicative conversion of the applicable French registration charge and must be verified at filing.

Funding & unlock path: No site-specific fire, structural-load, or occupational clearance is required for a domiciled office-only SPV beyond the obligations of the premises operator and landlord. Any occupied office or industrial premises must comply with applicable French workplace, accessibility, fire-safety, electrical, and building requirements. Industrial premises may require DREAL or Préfecture review, fire-service consultation, and occupational-risk documentation before use.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The articles must state the capital amount and share terms. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription, and the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Cash contributions are generally deposited into a blocked account or with a notary before incorporation, against a deposit certificate; funds are released after issuance of the registration extract. Legal-publication fees, beneficial ownership fees, notarial fees, and bank charges are excluded from the stated filing fee. The USD amount is an indicative conversion of the applicable French registration charge and must be verified at filing.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable to a passive private-equity acquisition SPV with no manufacturing, storage, treatment, or physical industrial operations. If the company later operates an industrial site, the relevant authority is the Préfecture through the Direction régionale de l'environnement, de l'aménagement et du logement (DREAL).

Category: No industrial or manufacturing operating permit is required for the stated PE syndicate SPV activity. Regulated financial activities, investment services, fund management, or public solicitation would require separate analysis and potentially authorisation from the Autorité des marchés financiers or the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full convertibility for current-account transactions under IMF Article VIII; EUR payments are unrestricted. Capital-account operations are free within the European Economic Area and generally liberalized with non-EEA counterparties, subject only to EU/UN financial sanctions, anti-money-laundering verification, and prior authorization for foreign direct investment in sensitive sectors under Articles L.151-3 et seq. and R.151-3 of the Code monétaire et financier. Profit remittance by an SAS to foreign shareholders is freely executable without FX license, but is subject to French corporate-law solvency restrictions on distributions, standard AML/KYC evidence, and either domestic withholding tax relief under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive or a double-tax treaty, or the applicable statutory withholding tax where no relief applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds. Exporters may receive and retain export receipts in any currency in authorized foreign-currency accounts held with a French credit institution or an EU/EEA passporting bank. There is no deadline for repatriation and no conversion ratio. The collecting credit institution must report cross-border flows to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics; the exporter must retain invoices, customs declarations, and shipping evidence. For a private-equity buyout SPV without export activity, these rules are not applicable.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting under Articles L.141-1 to L.165-2 of the French Code monétaire et financier, and in coordination with the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR) for banking supervision and the Eurosystem/European Central Bank for monetary policy matters. There is no separate French foreign-exchange administration; residual controls are embedded in prudential, statistics, sanctions, and foreign-direct-investment rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary payment/operating channel, but Low as the sole treasury rail for a leveraged buyout SPV. ACPR-licensed domestic electronic-money institutions and payment institutions, such as Qonto, Shine, or Lydia, can onboard a French SAS and provide EUR IBANs, card controls, and payment-initiation services; they cannot operate a regulatory securities escrow, settle syndicated loan funding, or provide credit. Offshore digital EMIs may legally provide e-money/payment services in France only under an EEA passport under the Payment Services Directive and E-Money Directive; an entity outside the EEA without French registration is not viable for French corporate account opening. Transactional constraints: escrow agents, notaries, and financing banks generally reject EMI accounts for closing wires, equity contributions, and buyout disbursements; therefore the SPV must maintain a Tier-1 bank relationship for all high-value capital flows.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opacity of beneficial ownership chain involving offshore holding companies or managed professional entities.

Root cause: Opacity of beneficial ownership chain involving offshore holding companies or managed professional entities.

Defense evidence: Complete BO registry extract for each entity in the ownership chain; ultimate natural-person UBO identification documents; notarized/certified trust deed or LLP/limited partnership constitutional deed if used; corporate organizational chart with shareholding percentages; tax-residency certificates; legal opinion from French counsel confirming the economic rationale of the chain.
Source of leverage and subscription funds inconsistent with SPV purpose or insufficient source-of-wealth documentation.

Root cause: Source of leverage and subscription funds inconsistent with SPV purpose or insufficient source-of-wealth documentation.

Defense evidence: Executed share-subscription agreements, capital-call notices, and LPA commitment schedules; sponsor audited financial statements or investor fund statements; bank statements showing the provenance of funds; promissory note or term-sheet for the acquisition facility; independent lender legal opinion confirming bona fide lending and non-fronting.
Adverse media, sanctions exposure, or nexus with restricted Russian/Belarusian/Crimean or other sanctioned persons.

Root cause: Adverse media, sanctions exposure, or nexus with restricted Russian/Belarusian/Crimean or other sanctioned persons.

Defense evidence: Independent commercial sanctions-screening report covering the SPV, shareholders, UBOs, directors, and counterparties; negative-news resolution memorandum prepared by the sponsor; written clearance opinion from French external counsel; board resolution confirming the beneficial owners are not designated persons; declaration of no nexus to restricted industries.
PEP status of a shareholder, director, or beneficial owner.

Root cause: PEP status of a shareholder, director, or beneficial owner.

Defense evidence: Written PEP-status declaration; source-of-wealth disclosure with supporting asset and tax records; audited financial statements; notarized identity and address proof; employer/business reference; explanation of the transaction history and relationship to the buyout transaction.
Apparent mismatch between stated ISIC 6499 buyout-SPV scope and requested payment rails or transaction counterparties.

Root cause: Apparent mismatch between stated ISIC 6499 buyout-SPV scope and requested payment rails or transaction counterparties.

Defense evidence: Detailed business plan and acquisition pipeline; executed or draft SPA and equity/investment agreement; projected cash-flow waterfall; transaction counterparty KYC pack; legal opinion that the SPV does not perform regulated banking, financing, or crowd-lending activity; board resolution limiting activity to buyout holding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for an inbound equity subscription into the French SAS requires an SWIFT MT103 for non-EUR funding or a SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR funding from the investor’s account. France does not operate a statutory Chinese-style purpose code, but the remittance information field should clearly state 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - SAS' together with the reference to the subscription agreement, capital-call notice, and share-allocation certificate. For correspondent-bank screening, the payer should use the ISO 20022 purpose code most consistent with the payment, as agreed in the account mandate; the key compliance element is the evidentiary bundle, not a single code. Verification at the receiving French bank will include the BO declaration, proof of identity of the transferor, source of funds, board authorization, constitutional documents, and confirmation that the capital injection is recorded in the SPV’s audited ledger. For amounts that constitute French cross-border financial operations requiring balance-of-payments statistics, the receiving bank files the report with the Banque de France; the SPV must keep the original capital-contribution deed and bank confirmation.For buyout consideration, debt-repayment, and acquisition-related trade settlements, the principal clearing rails are: SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier or registered shareholder payments; SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV for high-value nostro-covered euro or foreign-currency clearing; SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR disbursements inside EEA below high-value thresholds; commercial letters of credit under UCP600 for documented trade flows; and demand guarantees under URDG758 when escrow or warranty deposits are required. All outbound wires must reference the relevant SPA, facility agreement, or invoice, and must conform to the two-person signature mandate registered with the Tier-1 bank. The SPV should confirm that the final beneficiary, intermediary bank, and underlying transaction survive sanctions screening before release.Deliverable OTC FX forwards in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CNY with ACPR-licensed or EEA passported bank counterparties under the French FBF master agreement.FX swaps for structural restructuring of acquisition cash flows and bridge-to-equity timing gaps.Plain-vanilla FX options, including European calls and puts, limited to economically hedged exposure under the buyout pipeline.Cross-currency swaps for swap of acquisition-facility interest streams into EUR or sponsor reporting currency, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with appropriate collateral annex.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with restricted convertibility, such as offshore CNH, only to the extent the SPV has a verifiable underlying payment obligation and the bank accepts non-deliverable settlement.
