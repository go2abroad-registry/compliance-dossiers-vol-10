Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), administered by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce, with filings processed through the Guichet Unique des Entreprises operated by INPI (Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle), which also transmits to INSEE (SIREN/SIRET), URSSAF and the DGFiP.

Governing statute

Code de commerce (France), Articles L.210-1 to L.210-9 (commercial companies), Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 to R.227-14 (Société par Actions Simplifiée), Articles L.232-1 et seq. (accounts), Article R.123-1 et seq. (RCS registration) and Article L.225-3 principles extended by practice to SAS cash contributions.

Formation / constitutional filings

Dossier de création d'entreprise / constitution de société (single-window incorporation dossier) comprising the 'statuts constitutifs', attested copy of the 'attestation de dépôt des fonds', 'déclaration de non-condamnation', proof of legal notice publication, and beneficial-owner declaration ('déclaration des bénéficiaires effectifs'); the resulting title of registration is the 'Extrait Kbis'.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 30 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The SAS has no statutory minimum share capital (Article L.227-2 of the Code de commerce); capital is freely fixed in the statuts. Subscription may be in cash or in kind (in-kind contributions valued by a 'commissaire aux apports' where value exceeds EUR 30,000 and exceeds half of the share capital). Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked escrow account ('compte séquestre' / 'compte bloqué') with a credit institution, notary or avocat, evidenced by an 'attestation de dépôt des fonds'; funds are released to the company only after RCS registration. Shares are fully paid up at issuance in standard practice; a minimum of 50% paid-up on subscription applies only where the statuts adopt the SA-style rule.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-safety and structural compliance: premises subject to the 'règlement de sécurité contre les risques d'incendie et de panique' (Arrêté du 25 juin 1980 for ERP; Arrêté du 31 décembre 1980 for ERT); a fire-safety certification or 'avis favorable' from the Commission Centrale/Départementale de Sécurité is mandatory for public-access premises; structural load inspections are required for server-room installations and any modification to load-bearing elements under the Code de la construction et de l'habitation (Article L.112-1 et seq.). Workplace clearance: the employer must maintain a 'Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels' (DUERP, Article R.4121-1 of the Code du travail), affiliate all employees to a Service de Prévention et de Santé au Travail (SPST), organise statutory training on electrical and ergonomic hazards, and comply with the Registre de Sécurité and evacuation-drill obligations (Articles R.4227-28 to R.4227-39).

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing board is competent: game engine software development under ISIC 6201 constitutes a tertiary/digital activity. Any residual facility-related oversight falls to the DREAL (Direction Régionale de l'Environnement, de l'Aménagement et du Logement) and, for public-access premises, to the Commission de Sécurité of the department and the SDIS (Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours).

Category: None required — no 'autorisation d'exploitation' or manufacturing permit applies. The only facility-level authorisations potentially applicable are the 'autorisation de construire' / 'permis de construire' (Code de l'urbanisme, Article L.421-1) and the fire-safety clearance for an 'Établissement Recevant du Public' or 'Établissement Recevant des Travailleurs'.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), principally through the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Tax and VAT services are accessed through impots.gouv.fr; company formation is registered through the national formalities portal, formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Register the SAS through the national business-formalities portal to obtain its SIREN and SIRET identifiers. The competent SIE assigns or confirms the corporate income tax account and, where required, the French VAT number, generally in the format FR plus a two-character key and the nine-digit SIREN. A foreign entity making taxable supplies in France must apply to the DGFiP for VAT registration before the relevant transactions; an entity established outside the EU generally appoints a French fiscal representative unless an applicable exemption or mutual-assistance arrangement applies. EU-established entities are generally not required to appoint one. Registration does not itself establish that a transaction is taxable or that VAT is recoverable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). An eligible qualifying SAS may elect temporary taxation under the personal income-tax regime for up to five fiscal years, subject to statutory ownership, activity, age, size, and shareholder-consent conditions. The election is not a US-style check-the-box election; France has no general entity-classification election equivalent to the US rules. No general hybrid-mismatch election changes the SAS's classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general domestic turnover threshold applies to a French-established SAS required to charge VAT on taxable transactions. The French small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base) may apply below statutory turnover limits, which vary by activity and are subject to current-year rules; it does not apply as a general registration threshold to non-established taxable persons. Cross-border EU distance sales and certain electronically supplied services are subject to separate rules, including the EU-wide EUR 10,000 threshold for specified intra-EU B2C sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the EU may be exempt from French VAT with a right to deduct input VAT when statutory conditions are met and the supplier can substantiate that the goods left the EU. The supplier must retain a valid export declaration and evidence of exit, together with the invoice and transport or commercial records. Services are not zero-rated merely because the customer or payment is offshore: treatment depends on the place-of-supply rules, customer status, service category, and any applicable exemption. B2B services supplied to a taxable customer established outside France are generally outside French VAT under the general place-of-supply rule, subject to exceptions.

  • Export declaration and customs exit confirmation, ordinarily evidenced by the export MRN and electronic exit result
  • Sales invoice and records identifying the goods, purchaser, destination, and export transaction
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the shipment, such as a bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or carrier proof of delivery
  • Commercial and accounting records supporting the transaction and the link between the goods supplied and the goods exported

France is implementing mandatory domestic B2B e-invoicing and transaction/payment e-reporting through approved platforms (plateformes de dématérialisation partenaires, PDP), with data transmission to the tax administration. The statutory rollout begins on 1 September 2026: all in-scope businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from that date; issuance obligations apply from that date to large companies and intermediate-sized enterprises and from 1 September 2027 to SMEs and micro-enterprises. The regime is not a general real-time pre-clearance or digital-stamping system. Applicable format, platform, transmission, and e-reporting requirements depend on transaction and taxpayer category; public-sector invoicing is subject to separate Chorus Pro requirements.

France imposes no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated within 180 days. DGFiP may deny the export exemption or recover VAT where export or transaction conditions are not substantiated, with interest and applicable penalties. Late-payment interest is generally 0.20% per month. Tax increases may include 10% for specified filing failures, 40% for deliberate non-compliance, and 80% in cases such as fraudulent activity or abuse of law, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards. False customs declarations may also attract separate customs penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains a fully convertible currency regime for both capital and current accounts under EU free movement of capital rules and post-1990 liberalization. The euro is freely convertible with no prior approval required for most cross-border transactions. Capital account convertibility is unrestricted for legitimate corporate purposes including equity injections, intercompany loans, and profit remittances. Current account transactions such as trade settlements are similarly liberalized. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is permitted without restriction for EU-established entities, subject only to standard corporate tax withholding (12.8% plus social charges) and potential CFC rules if the recipient jurisdiction applies preferential taxation. All transactions exceeding €10,000 trigger mandatory statistical reporting via the Banque de France 'M' report for monetary and balance-of-payments purposes. No capital controls or exchange restrictions apply to gaming-related treasury flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds is required under French law. Export receipts must be declared to customs authorities and recorded in corporate accounting systems for tax and statistical compliance. Companies may repatriate funds through normal banking channels without statutory timeline or conversion ratio mandates. Failure to declare may result in tax penalties or customs fines, but voluntary surrender is not enforced. For statistical reporting purposes, exports above applicable thresholds require the 'M' report submission to the Banque de France. Gaming anti-cheat engine settlements qualify as standard commercial exports with no additional surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (as the national central bank and foreign exchange authority) and European Central Bank (for eurozone monetary policy coordination)

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to 40% of evaded tax (80% for abuse of rights), plus interest; criminal penalties up to €500,000 and 5 years imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for corporate tax debts if they participated in fraudulent acts; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of transactions, denial of treaty benefits, 80% penalty for abuse of law, and potential criminal charges

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; possible ban on managing companies
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 5% of turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines and imprisonment if knowingly involved; professional disqualification
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profits, 40% penalty on understated profits, and interest; potential double taxation

Director personal liability: Directors can be held liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; criminal liability in cases of fraud
