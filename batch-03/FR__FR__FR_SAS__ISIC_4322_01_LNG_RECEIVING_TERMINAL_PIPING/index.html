Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés) and VAT (TVA); e-portal: impots.gouv.fr; company law and registration formalities: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises, operated by INPI); customs and export control: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI); non-resident taxpayer liaison: Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE).

E-government filing portal

French-established SAS: incorporation is filed through the Guichet Unique; INSEE automatically attributes the SIREN (9-digit national tax identification number) and SIRET; the DGFiP allocates the intra-Community VAT identification number (TVA intracommunautaire) on the same registration file; no separate TIN application is required. Non-resident entities: must register for French VAT and tax remittance through the SIEE using Form CERFA 14175 (or standard registration Form CERFA 14171 via the Guichet Unique for entities establishing a permanent establishment); non-EU resident suppliers without a fixed establishment must appoint a VAT fiscal representative (garant) approved by the DGFiP under CGI Art. 289 A. VAT ID format: 'FR' plus 2-digit key plus 9-digit SIREN (11-character intra-Community ID).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. A French SAS is a separate legal entity mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (IS) under CGI Art. 205, with no pass-through or check-the-box option. France applies the territoriality principle of CGI Art. 209-I: the SAS is taxed on French-source active business profits unless it holds an official authorization to elect worldwide taxation; CIT standard rate 25%, reduced to 15% on the first €42,000 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs (CGI Art. 219). No Tax_Exempt_Zone status applies to this metropolitan-France LNG piping activity. — French-established SAS: incorporation is filed through the Guichet Unique; INSEE automatically attributes the SIREN (9-digit national tax identification number) and SIRET; the DGFiP allocates the intra-Community VAT identification number (TVA intracommunautaire) on the same registration file; no separate TIN application is required. Non-resident entities: must register for French VAT and tax remittance through the SIEE using Form CERFA 14175 (or standard registration Form CERFA 14171 via the Guichet Unique for entities establishing a permanent establishment); non-EU resident suppliers without a fixed establishment must appoint a VAT fiscal representative (garant) approved by the DGFiP under CGI Art. 289 A. VAT ID format: 'FR' plus 2-digit key plus 9-digit SIREN (11-character intra-Community ID).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope. A French SAS is a separate legal entity mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (IS) under CGI Art. 205, with no pass-through or check-the-box option. France applies the territoriality principle of CGI Art. 209-I: the SAS is taxed on French-source active business profits unless it holds an official authorization to elect worldwide taxation; CIT standard rate 25%, reduced to 15% on the first €42,000 of taxable profit for qualifying SMEs (CGI Art. 219). No Tax_Exempt_Zone status applies to this metropolitan-France LNG piping activity.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists in French tax law; classification follows the registered legal form, and an SAS is always IS-taxable (the income-tax option of CGI Art. 239 bis AA is reserved to SARL/EURL only). Available statutory elections: (1) fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under CGI Art. 223 A, allowing a 95%-parent to consolidate French subsidiary results; (2) worldwide aggregation election (régime du bénéfice mondial) under CGI Art. 209 quinquies and global imputation election (régime de l'imputation globale) for foreign branch profits, each requiring DGFiP authorization; (3) hybrid mismatch alignments under ATAD (Directive 2016/1164) implemented via CGI Art. 212 bis, including structured-arrangement rebuttal elections; (4) VAT simplification options including OSS/IOSS registration for intra-EU and non-EU distance sales and the reverse-charge self-invoicing mechanism for inbound B2B construction services.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis registration threshold: any French-established taxable person must register for VAT from the first euro of taxable turnover. Small-business VAT exemption (franchise en base de TVA, CGI Art. 293 B) is available below €36,800 annual turnover for services and €91,900 for goods, but this is an exemption, not a registration threshold. Non-established foreign suppliers have no registration threshold; intra-EU distance sales and B2C digital/telecom/broadcasting services trigger registration via the OSS when cross-border turnover exceeds €10,000 per EU-wide calendar year; B2B installation works and cryogenic piping services performed at an LNG terminal in France trigger French VAT registration or reverse charge with no turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating (exonération avec droit à déduction) applies under CGI Art. 262 ter-I-1° to goods physically exported or consigned outside the EU customs territory within 90 days of supply. Art. 262 ter-II extends 0% treatment to services directly linked to exported goods, including transport, loading, handling, and works performed on goods. Services rendered to non-EU customers are outside the scope of French VAT where effectively used outside France (CGI Art. 259-1°). For this LNG receiving terminal piping scope: fabricated cryogenic pipe and insulation materials exported from France qualify as zero-rated goods; on-site installation and welding services performed within France on French immovables are taxable in France at 20% (CGI Art. 259-2°), while installation and welding performed outside the EU fall outside French VAT territoriality. Claim requires documented proof of physical exit from EU customs territory (ECS), a direct nexus between the exported goods and the alleged zero-rated supply, and, for services, evidence of effective use outside France.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane – DAU/ECS) lodged electronically with DGDDI and accepted with validated exit
  • Bill of Lading (or Through Bill of Lading / CMR / air waybill) evidencing physical departure from the EU customs territory to the non-EU destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank documentary evidence, SWIFT MT103/MT202, proving payment and repatriation of the export proceeds to the French entity)

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting reform (Finance Act 2023 Art. 91; CGI Arts. 289 septies to 289 quindecies): from 1 September 2026 all French taxable persons must receive structured electronic invoices, and large and mid-size enterprises must issue them; from 1 September 2027 PME and micro-enterprises must issue them. Invoices must be exchanged through certified Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires (PDP) or the public Chorus Pro portal using structured formats (Factur-X, UBL, CII). Transaction data (e-reporting for B2C and intra-EU) is transmitted in real time to the DGFiP through PDP APIs; mandatory invoice data fields follow CGI Art. 289. France does not operate an Italian-style pre-clearance stamping system; enforceability rests on the mandatory PDP/API digital pipeline, the Fichier des Écritures Comptables (FEC) audit extract, and penalties for e-invoicing non-compliance.

DGFiP may retrospectively claw back the zero-rated exemption and deny input VAT deduction where export documentation is absent, incomplete, or fraudulent; reassessed VAT attracts late-payment interest (intérêts de retard, CGI Art. 1727) and the new 30% increase per Finance Act rules where applicable. Penalties under CGI Art. 1729 are 40% for manquement délibéré (deliberate inaccuracy) and 80% for manœuvres frauduleuses or abus de droit (fraudulent maneuvers or abuse of law). Sham export arrangements may be recharacterized under LPF Art. L64, exposing the company to the 80% penalty plus loss of the exemption; falsified customs declarations additionally trigger customs fines under Code des douanes Arts. 414 and 426 (fine up to 250% of the evaded duties and possible seizure of goods) and criminal tax prosecution under CGI Art. 1741 (up to €500,000 fine and five years imprisonment for tax fraud). The reassessment limitation period extends to six years for undisclosed or falsely documented transactions (LPF Art. L. 169) and up to ten years where criminal fraud is established; corporate directors may incur joint and several liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully liberalised current and capital account regime within the eurozone and vis-à-vis non-EU states, consistent with Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the OECD Code of Liberalisation of Capital Movements. The euro is freely convertible, and no exchange control approval is needed for trade settlement, dividends, capital contributions, or liquidation proceeds in a French SAS. Profit remittance hurdles are not exchange-control-driven; they arise from (i) corporate-law solvency checks on distributions under Article L.232-11 of the French Commercial Code, (ii) tax documentation and withholding tax for non-resident shareholders under Article 119(2) of the CGI (reduced by tax treaty), and (iii) banks' AML-based due diligence requiring clean UBO and sanction-screen status. No routine FX quota, no surrender requirement, and no convertibility restriction should be expected.

Export proceeds surrender rules

French law does not impose a statutory surrender obligation, a mandatory conversion ratio, or a prescribed repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Export invoices may be settled in EUR or any freely convertible currency, and the proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency account at an authorised intermediary bank. However, under the French balance-of-payments regulations, any cross-border remittance of EUR 10,000 or more requires statistical reporting to the Banque de France by the transmitting bank or the entity concerned; the underlying export invoice, customs declaration, and proof of delivery must be retained for at least ten years to support the transaction, and failure to report may result in administrative fines but does not invalidate the payment or transfer title.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the national competent authority for foreign exchange equilibrium, balance-of-payments reporting, and prudential oversight under Articles L.151-1 through L.165-1 and R.151-1 et seq. of the French Code monétaire et financier (CMF).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: LOW-MEDIUM. French-licensed or European-passthrough electronic money institutions and fintech banks, such as Qonto, Pliant, or Mano, can open operational payment accounts, accept SEPA/SEPA Instant credits, and provide basic FX rail for small-to-mid ticket disbursements. Their legal constraint is that an EMI is not a credit institution and therefore cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, provide trade finance guarantees, or hold escrow balances for a capital increase requiring bank attestation. Additionally, many EMIs have low subledger transaction caps for high-value cross-border wiring and lack direct SWIFT membership, causing them to rely on sponsor banks with added compliance friction. For ISIC 4322 LNG-terminal piping contracting, use an EMI for short-term euro float only; run capital escrow and trade settlement through a full-service tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires a SWIFT MT103 or SEPA credit transfer in EUR, initiated by the foreign shareholder's credit institution, into the SAS's dedicated Capital Escrow Account at a tier-1 French bank. The wire must carry an explicit balance-of-payments purpose code used by the Banque de France, typically the category corresponding to equity participation or shareholder current account (the bank will map the transaction to category such as '1120' acquisition of participations or '7000' loans/current-account advances), together with a clear narrative such as 'APPORT DE CAPITAL SOCIAL - FRANCE SAS - LNG PIPING PROJECT'. Pre-funding verification requires a notarised or legalised capital increase deed, board resolution, Kbis extract, UBO declaration, evidence of source of funds from the sending bank, and CRS/FATCA tax attestations. The receiving bank will file the balance-of-payments report with Banque de France, and the escrowed funds become available after AML/Sanctions clearances; ensuring accurate purpose-code classification and maintaining full underlying documentation avoids frozen funds and reclassification risk. Non-EU parent wires may be in USD and settled through a US correspondent bank using an MT103 cover; the French bank will require the USD,EUR conversion to be made only through its own treasury or a pre-approved FX line.For procurement of cryogenic stainless steel pipes, valves, insulation, and welding consumables, the primary clearing rails are (i) SEPA credit transfer for EUR payments within the EEA, (ii) SWIFT MT103 for direct vendor wires outside the EEA, and (iii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit opened at a tier-1 bank in favour of suppliers, issued via SWIFT MT700 and governed by UCP 600. Settlement under an LC occurs against presentation of an original bill of lading, signed inspection certificate, packing list, commercial invoice, and, where applicable, EU dual-use export-control documentation. The French bank will require an underlying trade-invoice and contract consistent with the SAS's declared ISIC 4322 activity; the contractor's own import and export must be matched with the Single Administrative Document and TARIC controls. For high-value disbursements, an advance-payment bond or performance bond in favour of the supplier is commonly used; require national bank sanctions screening on the beneficiary bank and ensure each MT103 references the project ID and purpose code to ease Banque de France post-reporting.Plain-vanilla forward foreign exchange contracts (EUR/USD, EUR/GBP) for firm and forecast trade payables or receivables with a maturity not exceeding 12 months.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD and EUR/GBP) to hedge principal and interest flows on trade financing and capital expenditure.European-style FX options, both calls and puts, for procurement contracts with uncertain timing or quantities, settled by physical delivery.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) limited to currencies that are non-fully convertible or have no deliverable onshore FX market, provided the counterparty and settlement currency are consistent with EU sanctions regulations and the bank's internal policy.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) in EUR/USD where the underlying debt or commercial obligation is itself linked to a defined project contract or fixed asset.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: LOW-MEDIUM. French-licensed or European-passthrough electronic money institutions and fintech banks, such as Qonto, Pliant, or Mano, can open operational payment accounts, accept SEPA/SEPA Instant credits, and provide basic FX rail for small-to-mid ticket disbursements. Their legal constraint is that an EMI is not a credit institution and therefore cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, provide trade finance guarantees, or hold escrow balances for a capital increase requiring bank attestation. Additionally, many EMIs have low subledger transaction caps for high-value cross-border wiring and lack direct SWIFT membership, causing them to rely on sponsor banks with added compliance friction. For ISIC 4322 LNG-terminal piping contracting, use an EMI for short-term euro float only; run capital escrow and trade settlement through a full-service tier-1 bank.
