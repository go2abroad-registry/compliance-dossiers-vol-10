Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du tribunal de commerce (local commercial court registry), with electronic intake through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) Guichet unique at www.formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title II, Chapter VII, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-2 and L.227-9; Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 on formation; Decree No. 2021-300 on the single business registration portal.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration unique d'immatriculation (single registration application) together with the statuts (articles of association), filed pursuant to Articles R.123-1 et seq. of the Commercial Code.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 7 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS. The capital is freely fixed in the statuts; in-kind contributions must be fully released at subscription, while cash contributions must be paid in at least 50% at subscription and the balance within five years. No mandatory state escrow account applies, but cash funds must be deposited in a bank blocked until registration unless the statutes provide otherwise.

Funding & unlock path: No fire-safety operating license is required for ordinary office or warehouse space; however, if the premises are open to a workforce, the employer must prepare the unique risk-assessment document (DUERP), verify electrical and fire-safety conformity under the Code du travail, and, for relevant racking or mezzanine structures, secure structural load inspection by a qualified controller. Where the premises constitute an ERP (établissement recevant du public), a fire-safety commission visit may apply.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS. The capital is freely fixed in the statuts; in-kind contributions must be fully released at subscription, while cash contributions must be paid in at least 50% at subscription and the balance within five years. No mandatory state escrow account applies, but cash funds must be deposited in a bank blocked until registration unless the statutes provide otherwise.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for pure commercial wholesaling of prilled urea and potassium chloride; no industrial licensing body is triggered. If the entity later installs large-scale physical storage subject to ICPE thresholds, the competent authority is the Préfet of the department, supported by the DREAL.

Category: Not required — no operating permit or manufacturing license is necessary for an unregulated wholesaling activity under French commercial law; RCS registration is the sole statutory status.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), operating under the French Ministry of Economy and Finance; public portal at www.impots.gouv.fr; corporate formalities portal at www.formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SAS or a French branch of a non-resident entity must first register the legal entity or branch with the French Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through the online formalities portal, obtaining a SIREN and SIRET number. Thereafter, the DGFiP automatically or on application issues a French VAT number in the format 'FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN'. Non-EU entities without a permanent establishment in France that carry out taxable supplies in France must appoint an accredited fiscal representative (représentant fiscal accrédité) for VAT and obtain the VAT ID through that representative. Application is made to the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope — A foreign-owned SAS or a French branch of a non-resident entity must first register the legal entity or branch with the French Trade and Companies Register (RCS) through the online formalities portal, obtaining a SIREN and SIRET number. Thereafter, the DGFiP automatically or on application issues a French VAT number in the format 'FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN'. Non-EU entities without a permanent establishment in France that carry out taxable supplies in France must appoint an accredited fiscal representative (représentant fiscal accrédité) for VAT and obtain the VAT ID through that representative. Application is made to the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No discretionary check-the-box election is available for an SAS; it is mandatorily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level. Available statutory mechanisms include: election for consolidated French tax group filing (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for entities at least 95% controlled by a French head company; election to form a single-tax VAT group (assujetti unique) under Article 256 C bis CGI; and application of the EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules with possible offsetting adjustment elections where recognised by the French tax authorities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For non-established foreign suppliers, there is no registration threshold: any taxable supply made in France triggers a VAT registration obligation. For established French domestic businesses, the small-business exemption threshold is EUR 85,000 of annual turnover for supplies of goods (and EUR 183,300 for certain supplies of services), but this relief is not available to foreign persons without a fixed establishment in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union may be zero-rated for VAT under Article 262-I of the French General Tax Code (CGI), provided the supplier holds evidence that the goods left the EU territory. Offshore supplies of services to non-resident counterparties may be zero-rated or outside scope under Articles 259 and 259 B CGI where the place of supply rules apply. Fertilizer and potash sales to non-EU buyers qualify for 0% VAT if the export evidence and commercial documentation are complete and consistent.

  • Customs Export Declaration (déclaration en douane - DAU)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (B/L, CMR, AWB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice / Sales Contract
  • Proof of transport and insurance
  • Dual-use / restricted goods declarations if applicable
  • Intrastat declaration (for intra-EU transactions)

France has a phased mandatory B2B e-invoicing and e-reporting regime. From 1 September 2026, all large enterprises must issue structured electronic invoices (Factur-X, UBL or CII) through Chorus Pro or a certified private dematerialisation platform (PDP); from 1 September 2027, all VAT-registered businesses must comply. Invoice transaction data and payment status data must be transmitted to DGFiP in near real time for fiscal clearance and pre-filling of VAT returns. Physical stamping is not used, but digital fiscal data transmission is mandatory; operators not using a certified PDP must connect directly to Chorus Pro.

The French tax administration may refuse the export VAT exemption and reassess VAT at 20% where the exporter fails to produce the required export proof or where documents are false or knowingly irregular. Deliberate tax fraud (manquement délibéré) triggers an 80% administrative penalty under Article 1731 CGI plus late-payment interest (intérêt de retard), and in criminal cases fines and imprisonment under the tax fraud provisions may apply. Any VAT recovered or deducted on export transactions may be clawed back retrospectively with penalties where the export zero-rating conditions are found not to have been satisfied.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both current and capital account. France maintains no distinction between current and capital account for exchange-control purposes, and there is no prior exchange authorization for cross-border payments by an SAS resident in France. Profit and dividend remittance is unrestricted: an SAS may distribute dividends in EUR or in an agreed foreign currency via SWIFT/SEPA after a shareholders' resolution; the distribution is subject to the standard French withholding tax (generally 25% for non-resident corporate shareholders, or treaty-reduced rates). Cross-border financial flows are monitored for statistical purposes: the servicing French bank reports all flows to the Banque de France under the balance-of-payments framework (EU Regulation 184/2014 and the national nomenclature of international transactions), assigning the applicable purpose code; no prior approval is required. The key convertibility-linked constraints for a physical urea and potash wholesale charter vehicle are not FX restrictions but EU trade measures: (i) under Council Regulation (EU) 833/2014 as amended by the 16th sanctions package (Council Regulation (EU) 2025/395 of 24 February 2025), the purchase and import of potash (CN 3104/3105) originating in Russia or exported from Russia is restricted to an annual quota until 10 July 2026, with a full import prohibition thereafter; (ii) imports of certain nitrogenous fertilizers (e.g., urea ammonium nitrate solutions, solid urea) from Russia may be subject to definitive EU anti-dumping or countervailing duties; (iii) payments relating to sanctioned persons, vessels, or charter parties are prohibited unless covered by a license. These items are managed through customs and compliance screening rather than through FX controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no holding period for export receipts. An EU-resident exporter may retain EUR or foreign-currency balances in any bank account in France or abroad, may entertain netting arrangements, and may freely use forwards or other hedging instruments. The only applicable obligations are (i) the standard monthly balance-of-payments statistical declaration submitted by the intermediary bank to the Banque de France, and (ii) sanctions and anti-dumping verification for cargoes originating in or transiting territories subject to EU restrictive measures (in particular Russian/Belarusian potash and urea).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) as the French central bank and monetary authority; ACPR (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution) as the banking, e-money and payment-institution licensing and AML/CFT supervisory authority; Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) at the Ministry of Economy and Finance for foreign investment review (IEF) and external financial relations. The applicable legal framework is the Code monétaire et financier (CMF). Article L.521-1 CMF enshrines the principle that financial relations between France and foreign countries are free, subject to the safeguards in Articles L.511-1 et seq. and L.561-1 et seq. (AML/CFT).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility for operational EUR/USD settlement (SEPA, SWIFT MT103 outbound, multi-currency wallet) through ACPR-licensed French electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Mangopay) or through EU-passported EMIs under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe, Airwallex EU). Legal constraints: (i) a non-EEA EMI cannot lawfully provide payment accounts in France unless it establishes a French-licensed entity or operates through a ACPR-approved e-money agent; (ii) an EU EMI passporting into France must complete the ACPR notification procedure; (iii) EMIs are not credit institutions and are not permitted to issue or confirm documentary credits (MT7xx), grant commodity trade finance, or provide FX forwards or swaps, which limits the utility of this route for ISIC 4669 physical commodity clearing. Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident UBO with no French or EEA tax residence controlling a newly registered French SAS (typical third-country direct-investment vehicle)

Root cause: Non-resident UBO with no French or EEA tax residence controlling a newly registered French SAS (typical third-country direct-investment vehicle)

Defense evidence: Kbis excerpt (issued within 3 months); signed Statuts; UBO declaration filed at the Greffe (Registre des bénéficiaires effectifs); passport and proof of address of the UBO; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds statement; parent-company audited financial statements (or 6 months bank statements if unaudited); group organizational chart; detailed business plan describing the fertilizer wholesale and charter activity; list of first eight target customers and suppliers; signed AML questionnaire of the bank.
Commodity flows involving Russia, Belarus, or sanctioned ports (potash, urea, NPK) — elevated sanctions and PEP/reputational risk

Root cause: Commodity flows involving Russia, Belarus, or sanctioned ports (potash, urea, NPK) — elevated sanctions and PEP/reputational risk

Defense evidence: Sanctions screening output on all counterparties, beneficial owners, vessel owners, and charterers; certificates of origin (non-Russian or non-Belarusian origin); Bill of Lading and cargo survey reports (SGS, Intertek); full AIS voyage history of the vessel; where the cargo is Russian potash within the authorized quota, the relevant quota certificate and DGDDI customs entry; written compliance policy confirming EU/OFAC/UN sanctions screening at 100% of transactions; contractual warranties from the supplier excluding sanctioned supply chains; evidence that the French importer is registered with the relevant customs and fertilizer product regulations.
Physical chartering business with no material substance (no operational office, no staff, no warehouse) in France — trade-based money laundering risk

Root cause: Physical chartering business with no material substance (no operational office, no staff, no warehouse) in France — trade-based money laundering risk

Settlement to third parties or to accounts in jurisdictions with no nexus to the underlying cargo

Root cause: Settlement to third parties or to accounts in jurisdictions with no nexus to the underlying cargo

Defense evidence: Fully reconciled invoices matched to LC terms; approved payee list signed by two authorized signatories; treasury policy on dual-control payments; explanation of the commercial reason for each destination country; documentary evidence of physical cargo movement (warehouse receipts, port discharge reports, weight certificates, mate's receipts); any intermediary mandates or sub-charter agreements.
High-frequency USD/EUR conversion without a documented hedging policy — bank risk committee may classify the SAS as a quasi-financial entity

Root cause: High-frequency USD/EUR conversion without a documented hedging policy — bank risk committee may classify the SAS as a quasi-financial entity

Defense evidence: Written treasury policy approved by the SAS board including FX exposure limits, hedging horizon, and permissible instruments; FBF Master Agreement (Contrat-Cadre) signature sheet; mandate for forward foreign exchange transactions; description of the natural hedge between USD-linked purchase prices and EUR-linked sales; evidence of the capital injection and shareholder loan structure confirming medium-term solvency; line-item confirmation that the SAS does not carry speculative open FX positions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a capital contribution, share premium, or shareholder loan from a non-resident parent into the French SAS: (i) from the EEA, use a SEPA Credit Transfer; from outside the EEA, use SWIFT MT103; (ii) instruct the remitting bank to identify the transfer as a capital operation in the Remittance Information field (e.g., 'Capital increase - SAS [name]' or 'Shareholder loan agreement dated [date]') and to apply the standard Banque de France balance-of-payments category for direct investment creation/capital increase; for a loan, the intercompany loan category applies; (iii) the French receiving bank requires the corporate supporting file (Kbis, signed shareholders' resolution or president's decision, notarized or witnessed certificate of deposit for capital, copy of the loan agreement for shareholder debt) and screens the funds under the ACPR AML framework; (iv) no exchange authorization is required under Article L.521-1 CMF. Where the contribution exceeds EUR 1,000,000, the bank will conduct enhanced source-of-funds verification (audited accounts of the transferor, chain-of-funds statements), and the receipt must be reported in the monthly BOP statement. Capital increases must be reflected at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce within one month; the escrow account release is executed upon presentation of the certificate of registration (certificat d'immatriculation/kbis).Clearing rails for physical urea and potash charter transactions: (i) SWIFT MT103 for open-account settlement of supplier invoices, charter hire, port costs, and surveyor fees, with complete narrative (contract reference, invoice number, Incoterms, BOP purpose code) and sanctions screening on beneficiary, intermediary banks, and underlying vessel; (ii) SWIFT MT700 for issuance/amendment of irrevocable documentary credits under UCP600, where the French bank is the issuing bank and the beneficiary is the fertilizer producer or trading house; the LC proceeds settle via TARGET2 for EUR or via Fedwire/CHIPS correspondent rails for USD; (iii) SWIFT MT760 for standby LCs guaranteeing charterer performance or demurrage obligations; (iv) SWIFT MT202/MT202 COV for bank-to-bank cover payments in correspondent banking. For EUR-denominated LC settlement, TARGET2 provides same-day finality; for USD settlement, the clearing path is the correspondent account at the New York Fed/bank. The French exporting entity may also request the beneficiary to provide certificates of origin, phytosanitary documents (where required), and vessel pre-arrival notices to release the LC documents.Deliverable outright FX forwards (forward foreign exchange contracts) in EUR/USD and EUR/GBP, executed under the FBF 2018 Master Agreement (Contrat-Cadre relatif aux opérations de change) with the account bank; these are the standard hedging instruments for the expected USD-billed cargo periods.FX swaps (change à terme / swap de change) EUR/USD to convert temporary foreign currency liquidity and match the timing of charter payments versus sales proceeds.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD) for longer-dated hedging of committed cargo purchase programs, subject to an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or the FBF swap annex, with appropriate EMIR documentation for a non-financial counterparty (NFC) below the clearing threshold.OTC EUR/USD FX options (European call/put and zero-cost collars) for asymmetric risk protection, permissible for French non-financial corporates as treasury instruments and settled through the FBF or ISDA framework.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in restricted third-country currencies (e.g., COP, EGP, PKR, NGN) where the cargo is sourced from a producer country whose currency is not freely deliverable; settlement is in USD or EUR under an ISDA-based confirmation, with the French bank acting as pricing counterparty and permissibly structured under EU EMIR rules.Cross-commodity and freight-linked derivatives (FFA - Forward Freight Agreements) commonly used alongside the FX book to fix the charter legs in USD; these are permissible as commodity derivatives under EMIR for the SAS in its capacity as physical charterer.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility for operational EUR/USD settlement (SEPA, SWIFT MT103 outbound, multi-currency wallet) through ACPR-licensed French electronic money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Mangopay) or through EU-passported EMIs under PSD2 (e.g., Wise Europe, Airwallex EU). Legal constraints: (i) a non-EEA EMI cannot lawfully provide payment accounts in France unless it establishes a French-licensed entity or operates through a ACPR-approved e-money agent; (ii) an EU EMI passporting into France must complete the ACPR notification procedure; (iii) EMIs are not credit institutions and are not permitted to issue or confirm documentary credits (MT7xx), grant commodity trade finance, or provide FX forwards or swaps, which limits the utility of this route for ISIC 4669 physical commodity clearing. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2022

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under CGI Article 119 bis for dividends paid to non-resident corporate shareholders; 30% flat levy applies to non-resident individual shareholders (12.8% income tax plus 17.2% social levies); reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the EU parent holds at least 5% for 24 months; DTA rates generally range from 0% to 15%

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: In EUR: full Master File and Local File obligations arise where the French entity's annual turnover or gross assets exceed EUR 400 million; Country-by-Country Reporting arises where the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750 million; even below these thresholds, the French tax administration may require transfer pricing information under Article L.13 B of the French Tax Procedures Code upon audit request

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulation, but the EU ATAD General Anti-Abuse Rule, French abuse-of-law doctrine (Article L.64 of the Tax Procedures Code), and the OECD Multilateral Instrument Principal Purpose Test require genuine commercial substance, local decision-making, personnel, physical activity and real risk absorption; an invoicing-only fertilizer conduit with no trading substance can be recharacterized and denied treaty benefits

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Administrative reassessment with 40% bad-faith penalty or 80% fraudulent-maneuver penalty; criminal conviction under CGI Article 1741 carries up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 75,000 fine, increased to seven years and EUR 250,000 when committed by an organized group; confiscation of proceeds and publication orders may also be imposed

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally prosecuted personally, ordered to pay civil restitution, subjected to asset freezes and travel bans, disqualified from management, and held jointly liable for corporate tax debts under Article L.267 of the Tax Procedures Code where their serious fault caused non-collection of tax
Shell Company and Abusive Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the whole arrangement under Article L.64 of the Tax Procedures Code with an 80% abuse-of-law penalty; possible denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; additional 100% penalty where the abuse is proven to be adopted with fraudulent intent

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing on grounds of fictitious company or alter ego; directors face personal civil liability for restitution, criminal fines, travel bans, professional disqualification and, in insolvency, activation of commercial liability for fraudulent mismanagement
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Arm's length reallocation of profit, reassessment of CIT and withholding taxes, late-payment interest at 0.20% per month, and administrative penalties for failure to present transfer pricing documentation, potentially reaching 5% of reassessed amounts or additional flat fines; deliberate non-compliance attracts the 80% penalty

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved artificial intra-group pricing may be held personally liable for the resulting tax loss, subjected to fines, ordered to compensate the company, and prosecuted under abuse-of-law rules where the arrangement concealed fraud
AML and Fictitious Payment Structuring

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under French Penal Code Articles 324-1 and 324-2 carries up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine for individuals; aggravated money laundering carries up to ten years and EUR 750,000; legal entities face multiplied fines, dissolution and confiscation orders; false currency declarations trigger separate customs monetary penalties

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal conviction, confiscation of illicit assets, travel bans, withdrawal of passport, disqualification from company management and personal civil liability in subsequent insolvency or shareholder proceedings
