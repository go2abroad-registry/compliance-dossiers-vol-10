Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation; the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must generally be fully contributed. Cash contributions are deposited into a restricted account with a bank, notary, or qualifying payment institution, which issues a certificate of deposit. The deposit is released after registration.

Funding & unlock path: No general pre-opening occupational clearance is required for a standard wholesale office or warehouse. The employer must prepare and update the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels, provide occupational training and personal protective equipment where required, and comply with workplace health and safety rules.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

DGFiP (Direction Générale des Finances Publiques) - French tax administration; professional online portal: impots.gouv.fr (espace professionnel / Pro TVA); single company-registration one-stop-shop operated by INPI: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; customs and EORI authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via douane.gouv.fr / import-export.gouv.fr, using the DELTA system for export declarations.

E-government filing portal

1) File incorporation of the SAS through the Guichet unique (single business-creation portal) with the M0/standard forms; the dossier is routed electronically to the commercial registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce), INSEE, URSSAF and DGFiP. 2) The greffe delivers the Kbis extract evidencing legal existence. 3) INSEE assigns the SIREN (9-digit), SIRET (14-digit) and APE/NAF code (for this scope: 46.46Z - wholesale of pharmaceutical and medical goods, including ophthalmic goods). 4) DGFiP issues the French intra-community VAT number (n° TVA intracommunautaire, format FR-XX-123456789) upon completion of the VAT/IS registration data; for an export-oriented wholesale SAS, VAT identification is mandatory from the first taxable transaction even when turnover is below the franchise threshold. 5) Apply separately to the DGDDI for an EORI number (FR + SIREN), required to lodge customs export declarations; EORI issuance normally takes 2-10 working days. 6) No French fiscal representative is needed because the SAS is a locally established taxable person; a foreign non-established entity selling in France would require VAT registration and, where applicable, a fiscal representative under Article 289 A CGI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation | Territorial_Scope: a French SAS is by law subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) under Article 206(1) CGI and cannot elect pass-through treatment; US-style check-the-box does not exist. France applies the territoriality principle: profits from businesses carried on in France are taxable in France, while profits from foreign permanent establishments are exempt unless an approved worldwide/consolidated election applies. For eligible small companies, a reduced IS rate of 15% applies to the first EUR 42,500 of annual taxable profit, with the standard rate of 25% thereafter.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election and no elective hybrid-mismatch rule for an SAS under French law; ATAD hybrid mismatch rules (Article 212 bis CGI) apply mandatorily. Available statutory elections include: (i) French VAT grouping ('assujetti unique') under Article 256 C CGI for financially, economically and organisationally linked French entities; (ii) French fiscal integration (intégration fiscale) under Article 223A CGI, electable by a French head company (SAS qualifying) that holds at least 95% of French subsidiaries; (iii) election for 'bénéfice mondial' or 'bénéfice consolidé' (worldwide or consolidated IS base) subject to ministerial approval under Article 209 CGI; (iv) option between the simplified real regime ('régime réel simplifié') and normal real regime ('régime réel normal') for VAT/IS reporting; (v) the parent-subsidiary regime ('régime mère-fille', Articles 145 and 216 CGI) is electable for qualifying shareholdings to exempt 95% of dividends; (vi) for SNC partnerships, an option for IS taxation exists under Article 206(2) CGI but is not available for a SAS, which is mandatorily IS-taxable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies unrestricted capital and current account convertibility as a core principle of EU law under Articles 63-65 TFEU on free movement of capital and payments. No exchange control authorisation is required for ordinary trade payments, export proceeds conversion, equity injections, shareholder loans, or profit repatriation. A French SAS may freely remit dividends abroad once the corporate decision is properly adopted and the distributable accounting result exists; withholding tax exposure is managed through the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for qualifying EU corporate shareholders and through applicable double tax treaties for non-EU shareholders. Restrictions exist only in narrowly defined public-policy areas: EU and national financial sanctions, asset freezes, anti-money-laundering reporting, and the obligation to channel cross-border transactions through authorised banking intermediaries. In practice, French banks execute foreign exchange freely at market rates without central bank authorisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed repatriation timeline for export proceeds in France. Export receipts arising from the international wholesale of acetate eyeglass frames may be settled in EUR or any convertible currency and retained on the company's bank accounts. The only administrative duty is statistical balance-of-payments reporting: the servicing bank reports cross-border payment flows to the Banque de France, and a resident company must submit a direct declaration where a cross-border financial transaction reaches or exceeds EUR 750,000 or where the Banque de France designates the company as a direct declarant. All export receipts must remain traceable through underlying invoices, customs declarations, and SWIFT/SEPA payment evidence.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the monetary authority and central administrator of the French balance-of-payments reporting regime; banking prudential supervision is exercised by the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution), with foreign-exchange policy coordination performed by the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) within the Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility, limited to routine treasury operations including SEPA/SWIFT payments, FX execution, and multi-currency account management. French or EEA-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) are authorised under PSD2 and supervised by the ACPR; a licensed EMI may open remote accounts for a French SAS, hold client funds under safeguarding rules, and provide API-driven payables and receivables platforms. Legal constraints: an EMI may not grant credit, issue commercial letters of credit, provide import/export trade finance, or act as a depository for share capital escrow accounts. Safeguarding obligations restrict the deployment of client funds to a narrow range of low-risk liquid assets. Therefore, the EMI rail is viable only as a secondary operational account and cannot replace the Tier-1 bank relationship required for capital deposits, documentary credit, and full cross-border liquidity management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity contributions are transmitted by foreign shareholders to France through SWIFT MT103 international wires with an explicit purpose code identifying the remittance as an equity injection (ISO 20022 purpose EQUI or GFIN) or as a shareholder current account advance (ADVC or INTE). All injections are documented by board resolutions, subscription deeds, and the bank's certificate of funds deposit where capital is being constituted or increased for the SAS. For cash share capital, funds must first be directed to the blocked capital escrow account and are released to the free operational account only after the company is registered or the capital increase is legally completed. Any non-EU remittance must pass through the French banking intermediary, which applies sanctions and reputation risk filters, verifies the remitter and the underlying transaction, and files the balance-of-payments declaration with the Banque de France where thresholds are met. EUR funds are typically available on a T+0 or T+1 basis via SEPA/Target2, and USD or other currency wires on a T+1 or T+2 basis through the correspondent banking network.Outbound settlements against suppliers of ophthalmic frames and accessories are executed through SWIFT MT103 international telex transfers for non-EU suppliers, SEPA Credit Transfers for EU/EEA suppliers, and SEPA Instant Credit Transfers for urgent euro disbursements. High-value or order-backed purchases are settled by commercial documentary letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 and amended by MT707, or by documentary collections under MT740/MT750 where the bank intermediates documents of title. Standby letters of credit and bank guarantees support advance payment obligations to Asian acetate frame manufacturers. The treasury function operates dual-approval payment workflows in the bank's e-banking portal, transmits structured remittance data referencing invoices, customs references and transport documents, and ensures clearing in EUR via Target2 or in major convertible currencies via the bank's correspondent network. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400 million: mandatory Local File and Master File preparation applies when the French entity or its foreign parent has annual revenue or total balance-sheet assets exceeding €400 million. If below this threshold, full documentation is not automatic, but DGFiP may still require basic transfer-pricing evidence under Article L 13 B LPF where cross-border related-party transactions are significant.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate sector-specific Economic Substance Regulations comparable to UAE; however, anti-abuse rules under Article 64 LPF (abus de droit) and the anti-artificial arrangement doctrine require genuine economic substance. An SAS registered in France but without local premises, employees, bank accounts headed in France, or real management and decision-making is vulnerable to redomiciliation at its place of effective management, denial of treaty benefits, CFC recharacterization, and application of the 80% abuse-of-law penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude Fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 1741 CGI, fraud is punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of €500,000. When committed in an organized group or through offshore concealment, penalties increase to 7 years and €3,000,000. Tax penalties of 80% under Article 1729-1 CGI plus late-payment interest (0.2% per month) are also imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations may be jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax and penalties; criminal courts can order confiscation of assets, personal restitution, travel bans (interdiction de sortie du territoire), and imprisonment. The limited liability of the SAS is pierced where personal criminal intent or fraudulent manoeuvring is established.
Abuse of Law / Artificial Arrangement (Abus de Droit)

Statutory sanction: Under Article L 64 LPF, DGFiP may ignore the artificial arrangement and apply an 80% abuse-of-law penalty, or a 40% bad-faith penalty, on the additional tax assessed. The issuer is also exposed to a formal judicial sanction publishing the decision and to possible criminal tax-fraud referral if concealment is proved.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed or acquiesced to the artificial chain face personal liability for the 80% penalty, professional interdiction from managing a French company, and civil restitution. A travel ban may be imposed while the criminal inquiry is ongoing, and a conviction for fraud can result in up to 5 years' imprisonment.
Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Article L465-1 CMF, money laundering is punishable by 5 years' imprisonment and a €375,000 fine; aggravated circumstances (professional habit, organised gang) raise penalties to 10 years and €750,000. Administrative sanctions by ACPR or the relevant authority can reach €100 million or 10% of annual turnover, plus an order to cease the business.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal conviction, confiscation of assets, prohibition from operating a commercial activity, and travel bans pending trial. The SAS's limited liability does not shield directors from criminal AML liability; each director can be separately prosecuted even when acting in the corporate name.
