Name reservation / lock period

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent legal registry is the local Commercial Court Registry (Greffe du tribunal de commerce) maintaining the Registre du commerce et des sociétés, with filings submitted through the INPI Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.210-1 to L.210-9 and R.210-1 to R.210-26 on commercial companies; Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 to R.227-2 on sociétés par actions simplifiées; Articles L.225-3 and L.225-14, as applied to SAS, on capital contributions and release of cash subscriptions; Articles L.123-1 to L.123-12 on the Trade and Companies Register.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of company formation through the INPI Guichet unique, accompanied by signed SAS articles of association, capital deposit certificate, beneficial-owner declaration, and supporting identity, address, management, and premises documents. The electronic filing replaces the former paper M0 declaration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 115 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation; the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with a bank, notary, or qualifying deposit institution before filing, and the deposit certificate must be included in the incorporation dossier. In-kind contributions are generally fully contributed at incorporation, subject to statutory valuation rules and any applicable auditor exemption.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain building and premises approvals where construction or change of use requires them. Complete fire-risk assessment, emergency exits, extinguishing equipment, alarm systems, electrical inspection, and periodic workplace safety inspections under the French Labour Code and applicable building rules. An établissement recevant du public requires ERP fire-safety and accessibility approval. Industrial premises may require ICPE inspection and Préfecture authorization; there is no universal standalone occupational operating license for ordinary electronic assembly.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation; the balance must be paid within five years of registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with a bank, notary, or qualifying deposit institution before filing, and the deposit certificate must be included in the incorporation dossier. In-kind contributions are generally fully contributed at incorporation, subject to statutory valuation rules and any applicable auditor exemption.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

SAS companies are taxed at the entity level under the standard corporate income tax (IS) regime. Limited check-the-box style elections are not available; however, hybrid mismatch rules under EU ATAD and specific regimes for holding companies or micro-entreprise (for small turnover) may apply.

20%

36,300 EUR (turnover threshold in the preceding 12 months for most activities)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT rate applies to exports of goods outside the EU territory and certain offshore services. Eligibility requires the goods to leave French territory and proof of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane d'Exportation - DAU)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment received

Mandatory real-time e-invoicing (facturation électronique) for all B2B transactions through the French government's PEP platform. Digital stamps (horodatage) are required, with API integrations for automated clearance and reporting to tax authorities.

Fraudulent claims of zero-rating incur penalties of up to 80% of the evaded VAT plus interest and late payment surcharges. Retrospective clawback of input VAT credits if export documentation is insufficient or fraudulent, potentially leading to criminal sanctions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a full member of the euro area and operates an open capital account under Article 63 TFEU and the Code monétaire et financier (CMF) Articles L151-1 et seq. and L524-1 et seq. Current-account transactions, including export proceeds, service fees, royalties and intercompany management fees, are fully convertible with no foreign-exchange permit required and no restriction on invoicing currency. Capital-account inflows (equity subscriptions, shareholder advances and loans) are free of exchange-control approval; the only limits are (i) cash-declaration obligations, (ii) anti-money-laundering/TRACFIN declaratons by the banking sector, and (iii) the IEF prior-authorisation regime where a non-EU investor acquires control, directly or indirectly, of a French entity active in listed sensitive sectors such as defence, critical infrastructure, artificial intelligence or public-health and essential medical-supply activities. A greenfield French manufacturing SAS for electronic patient monitors is not itself barred, but if the non-EU parent later acquires control of an existing French healthcare/medical-data company, an IEF filing may be required. Profit remittance by dividend is freely permitted and the funds are transferable on a same-day basis; France levies a 25% withholding tax on dividends to non-French tax-resident corporate shareholders (subject to the Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief at 0% for a 10%+ 24-month holding and to applicable tax-treaty reductions), while interest and royalties on cross-border payments are generally payable gross subject to treaty/domestic withholding conditions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion ratio in France or anywhere in the euro area. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency in designated devises accounts, converted at market rates, or settled in EUR without any ceiling. The continuing compliance obligations are: (i) the €10,000 physical-cash movement declaration to French Customs/TRACFIN under Regulation (EU) 2018/1672 and CMF Article L152-7 (not applicable to bank transfers), and (ii) statistical balance-of-payments reporting of cross-border flows to the Banque de France via the FIDE teleprocedure or bank-conducted surveys, normally executed by the bank for transactions above the statistical threshold. For the medical-device exporter, the key proof vehicle is the French Customs export declaration (DAU via DELTA G/C) and the Intrastat/Extrastat filings, which together evidence the trade nature of the proceeds and support the bank's 'use of funds' questionnaires.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) — the French central bank responsible for balance-of-payments statistics, foreign-exchange reporting and payment-system oversight; prudential supervision of credit institutions and payment/electronic-money institutions is delegated to the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution); the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) is the competent authority for foreign-investment screening (IEF) and monetary-policy/FX coordination within the Eurosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for operational cross-border payments and FX conversion, but LOW as the sole banking relationship and NOT ACCEPTABLE for the capital formation escrow account. Under PSD2 transposed in CMF Articles L522-1 et seq. and L525-8 et seq., EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Ebury, Qonto/Revolut group entities) may lawfully open e-money accounts for a French SAS, hold safeguarded client funds, and provide foreign-exchange and low-value cross-border disbursement rails, provided they are duly licensed in France or passporting from another EEA state and recorded by the ACPR in the payments register. However, French AML regulations require a credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts to issue the attestation de dépôt des fonds for the constitution of a SAS, and statutory auditors/administrations will treat an EMI account as supplementary, not primary. Post-Brexit UK-only EMIs can no longer passport into France and must operate through a licensed EEA entity or a French branch, which makes the legal structure more consequential. For the low-risk medical-device profile, an EMI is viable for: (i) FX conversion between EUR/USD/CNY where a Tier-1 bank cannot price competitively, (ii) sub-€50,000 supplier payments, and (iii) e-commerce/marketplace settlements; it must be excluded from dividend distributions, the capital escrow and any trade-finance instrument requiring a confirming bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the initial constitution of the French SAS, cash share capital must be deposited with an établissement de crédit or the Caisse des Dépôts, and the bank issues an attestation de dépôt des fonds (deposit certificate) that accompanies the formation dossier at the RCS; the funds remain blocked until registration of the company. The inbound remittance from the foreign parent should be a SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for nostro-cover associated flow) with (i) the parent named as originator with its full corporate name/address, (ii) the beneficiary named 'FR_SAS en formation' with the French IBAN (FR76 crate pattern) and BIC, (iii) remittance field 70 reciting 'SOUSCRIPTION CAPITAL SOCIAL — [amount EUR] — DECISION DU [date]', and (iv) no cash or third-party funds. After registration, subsequent equity injections are routed by direct SWIFT MT103 to the operational account using balance-of-payments direct-investment code D.31 and the label 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION / AUGMENTATION DE CAPITAL ou APPORTS EN COMPTE COURANT D'ASSOCIE', accompanied by the shareholders' resolution and, where the contribution is a compte courant d'associé, a written shareholder-loan agreement bearing an arm's-length interest rate to avoid recharacterisation under French thin-capitalisation rules (CGI Article 212). All inbound flows must be supported by the bank's source-of-funds package: parent bank statement evidencing the legal origin of money, the capital increase/contribution resolution, and the updated RBE/Kbis of the French entity; EU-sourced capital is free of prior authorisation, while non-EU investors must verify that the IEF prior-authorisation regime does not apply to the particular formation (greenfield versus acquisition-of-existing health activities).EUR-denominated disbursements to suppliers and service providers must use the SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) or SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) rails, which clear via T2 / TARGET Services and require only the IBAN and BIC of the creditor — no SWIFT MT messages and no correspondent bank charges. Non-EUR trade settlements (USD, CNY, GBP) are executed via SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) and, for nostro cover or chain transactions, MT202 COVER; for high-value imports of electronic components for patient-monitor assembly from Asia, the recommended rail is an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit issued/confirmed under UCP 600 (SWIFT MT700/MT707; confirmation by BNP Paribas, SG or CIC at the exporter's request), with the document set comprising commercial invoice, combined-transport Bill of Lading, packing list, certificate of origin, CE/quality certificates and insurance certificate/cargo policy; the issuing bank examines compliance and pays only against the conforming presentation. For open-account post-shipment settlements, MT103 plus three-way reconciliation to invoice, packing list and delivery proof is the prevailing protocol; banks may apply a first-transaction scrutiny or payment-blocking right, and first-time counterparties are subject to the French 'Vigilance' anti-fraud payment-securitisation modules. Collection business (documents against payment / acceptance) under URC 522 is permissible for moderate-value repeat transactions, and Bank Payment Obligations (BPO, e.g., via SWIFT Trade Services Utility) may supplement the LC rail if both bank networks support them; all disbursements must be evidenced by the underlying vendor invoice, proof of shipment and the company's approved disbursement authority matrix.Plain Vanilla Forward FX (Change à terme ferme) — maturity up to 24 months, documented under an FBF/ISDA OTC Derivatives Master Agreement or the bank's master agreement; permitted without margin for a commercial UNDERLYING where the hedge ratio matches the forecast export/import exposure.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) — permissible for currencies lacking full onshore convertibility for the French entity (offshore CNH, INR, BRL), provided the hedge is backed by a physical trade disbursement or receipt and the counterparty is a licensed credit institution or eligible bank; the NDF may be used for USD/CNH protection of the Asian component-sourcing leg.FX Swaps (devise-against-devise swap, change à terme calendaire) — spot versus forward contract combining a spot exchange and a reverse forward, commonly used to convert USD sales receipts into EUR for French payroll and operating costs, authorised where the transaction matches the company's cash-flow cycle.Cross-Currency Swaps (XCCY — EUR/USD, EUR/CNH) — permitted under French law and EMIR uncleared-margin rules where the entity's gross notional stays below the clearing thresholds; documented by ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a Credit Support Annex for variation margin and supported by an internal treasury policy confirming a non-speculative rationale.Plain Vanilla Currency Options (calls/puts) — legally permissible and executable with Tier-1 French banks for hedging currency risk on receivables/payables; the premium may be expensed in accordance with IFRS hedge accounting (IAS 39/IFRS 9) and is bounded by the underlying exposure to satisfy the prudential ban on purely speculative uncovered option strategies.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for operational cross-border payments and FX conversion, but LOW as the sole banking relationship and NOT ACCEPTABLE for the capital formation escrow account. Under PSD2 transposed in CMF Articles L522-1 et seq. and L525-8 et seq., EEA-licensed electronic-money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Ebury, Qonto/Revolut group entities) may lawfully open e-money accounts for a French SAS, hold safeguarded client funds, and provide foreign-exchange and low-value cross-border disbursement rails, provided they are duly licensed in France or passporting from another EEA state and recorded by the ACPR in the payments register. However, French AML regulations require a credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts to issue the attestation de dépôt des fonds for the constitution of a SAS, and statutory auditors/administrations will treat an EMI account as supplementary, not primary. Post-Brexit UK-only EMIs can no longer passport into France and must operate through a licensed EEA entity or a French branch, which makes the legal structure more consequential. For the low-risk medical-device profile, an EMI is viable for: (i) FX conversion between EUR/USD/CNY where a Tier-1 bank cannot price competitively, (ii) sub-€50,000 supplier payments, and (iii) e-commerce/marketplace settlements; it must be excluded from dividend distributions, the capital escrow and any trade-finance instrument requiring a confirming bank.
