Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. As a euro-area member state, France applies the TFEU Article 63 prohibition on restrictions on capital movements between Member States and third countries; no exchange-control permits are required for lease receivables, vendor payments, or equity injections. Current-account transactions (colocation service fees, bare-metal hosting payments, management fees) are completely liberalized under Articles L.151-1 and L.151-2 of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier, CMF). Capital-account flows (capital contributions, shareholder loans, guarantees, security deposits) are similarly free but trigger statistical declaration to the Banque de France when the transaction exceeds EUR 15,000. Profit remittance is unrestricted for an SAS subject to standard corporate formalities: approval of distributable reserves by the shareholders, satisfaction of the legal reserve requirement, and, where the recipient is a foreign parent, dividend withholding at 25% unless reduced by an applicable double-tax treaty (e.g., Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU for EU parents). Payments to French-listed non-cooperative tax jurisdictions (ETNC) under Article 2384 A CGI face a punitive 75% withholding tax and enhanced documentation; the data center colocation model is otherwise free of convertibility hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender, repatriation deadline, or forced conversion ratio for export proceeds. A French SAS may retain foreign-currency lease income (USD, GBP, CHF) in domestic or offshore foreign-currency accounts without conversion to EUR. The only compliance obligation is statistical: under the Banque de France balance-of-payments regime (CMF Articles R.151-1 et seq. and EU Regulation (EC) No 184/2005), each cross-border transaction above EUR 15,000 must be reported through the instructing credit institution, which files the external transactions declaration (déclaration de balance des paiements). There is no requirement to liquidate USD or GBP balances within any statutory window.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the French Foreign Exchange Administration for balance-of-payments statistics and external financial relations, with banking prudential supervision and AML/CFT enforcement delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), and exchange-rate policy competence retained at the European Central Bank (ECB) for euro-area monetary policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High for domestic euro treasury operations but legally constrained for core banking functions. PSD2-licensed and ACPR-supervised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and EU-passported challenger banks (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Wise Business, Revolut Business) can efficiently execute monthly SEPA ACH debits, virtual IBAN allocation, and FX conversion at transparent rates, and are viable for low-risk operational disbursement accounts. Legal constraints under the E-Money Directive 2009/110/EC and CMF Articles L.525-1 et seq.: EMIs may not grant credit, may not hold client funds as deposits, and must safeguard all e-money funds in segregated accounts at a credit institution or central bank, thereby falling outside the FGDR (French deposit guarantee) scheme. Consequently, an EMI is unsuitable as the sole treasury rail for capital escrow, securitized security deposits, cross-border equity injections, or documentary credit operations. The strategic design therefore mandates a Tier-1 bank as the primary clearing anchor with one or two EMIs as secondary collection acceleration rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: route all inbound equity injections via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank) to the SAS EUR account at the designated Tier-1 French bank. The remittance information field (Field 70) must state 'Fonds apport en capital de la SAS' together with a reference to the capital increase resolution. The receiving French bank will map the flow to the Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose code for equity participation under the capital-transactions classification (category 3D), and will automatically file the mandatory declaration for any amount exceeding EUR 15,000. Where the remitting shareholder is treaty-eligible, the bank may request a certificate of tax residence for withholding-treaty application; the SAS treasury must retain the shareholder resolution, updated RCS filing (dépôt modificatif), and proof of registration of the capital increase as verification for the bank's KYC file. SEPA Credit Transfer is acceptable only for EEA-origin euro injections; SWIFT remains the preferred rail for non-EEA funds to preserve the audit trail through Target2 and central bank reporting.Primary rail for recurring euro-denominated lease costs and operating expenses: SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) via the Target2 ecosystem. Tenant rent collections should be automated through the SEPA Direct Debit (SDD) core scheme, with virtual IBAN segmentation to reconcile individual colocation units. For cross-border vendor payments in USD, GBP, or CHF: SWIFT MT103 with SWIFT gpi for same-day tracking and irrevocable settlement. High-value infrastructure procurements (transformers, uninterruptible power supplies, generators, cooling systems) should be executed through documentary Letters of Credit under ICC UCP 600, with deferred-payment tenors matched to lease receivables, and Incoterms 2020 allocation negotiated at shipment. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 in annual turnover or balance-sheet total; groups with consolidated turnover of EUR 750,000,000 or more must additionally file country-by-country reporting.

Economic substance scope: France does not maintain a dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) register, but the general anti-abuse rule, Article 205 A CGI, and OECD BEPS 12 principles require a French SAS to have real premises, operating employees, and actual decision-making capacity. A colocation SAS must exercise active control over server contracts, power supply, cooling, and client onboarding, and cannot be a mere invoice-passer or subscribing shareholder conduit without triggering recharacterization or denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Profit Suppression

Statutory sanction: Automatic tax assessment with 40% penalty for deliberate misstatement, increased to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights; monthly late-payment interest at 0.2%; criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 and five years' imprisonment for fraude fiscale.

Director personal liability: Directors who ordered or approved the concealment can be prosecuted personally; courts may impose travel bans, freezing orders, restitution of unpaid taxes, and civil liability for the company's tax shortage.
Shell Company / Abuse of Rights

Statutory sanction: Application of the abuse-of-rights procedure (LPF Article L64) with an 80% penalty on the re-qualified profit, plus recovery of the underlying tax; criminal prosecution for money laundering of the illicitly shifted profit is also available.

Director personal liability: De facto managers and directors may be jointly and severally liable under LPF Articles L266-L267 for the unpaid tax and penalties; travel bans and imprisonment may apply upon criminal conviction.
AML/CFT and Sanctions Exposure

Statutory sanction: AML violations carry administrative fines up to EUR 5 million or twice the value of the proceeds for non-financial professionals; failure to report suspicious transactions to Tracfin triggers further sanctions; EU and French asset-freeze rules may lead to criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors can be sentenced for money laundering (five years' imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine), face quartering of personal assets, and be prohibited from managing companies; travel bans are commonly imposed in cross-border laundering cases.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Correction of French taxable profit to the arm's-length position; 10% transfer-pricing penalty under Article 1731bis CGI, plus 40% penalty for absence of documentation; double taxation and late-tax interest may also be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors signing inadequate transfer-pricing documentation can be held civilly liable for the additional tax; if pricing is set with entities in tax-haven countries, joint and several liability for the hidden profits and criminal sanctions may follow.
