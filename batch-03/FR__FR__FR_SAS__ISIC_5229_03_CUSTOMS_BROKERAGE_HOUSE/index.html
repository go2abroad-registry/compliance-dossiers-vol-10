Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Name reservation / lock period

France has no general statutory nationwide company-name reservation period equivalent to a 30-day reservation system. A proposed name is normally submitted with the incorporation filing through the INPI Guichet unique; legal protection principally arises upon registration and is subject to prior rights. If rejected, obtain the written reason or filing deficiency notice, verify availability and regulated-word restrictions, correct the name or supporting documents, and resubmit through the Guichet unique within the stated correction period. If the decision is an appealable administrative decision rather than a curable filing deficiency, submit a reasoned prior administrative appeal to the issuing authority and, where appropriate, a hierarchical appeal; judicial review may be brought before the competent administrative or commercial court depending on the nature of the decision. Trademark infringement, unfair competition, or company-name disputes should be addressed before the competent judicial court. A new name should be filed immediately if the commercial launch cannot await dispute resolution.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SAS has no mandatory minimum share capital under Article L.227-2 of the Commercial Code; the capital is freely fixed in the Statuts. Where cash contributions are made, the full amount must be subscribed and each cash contribution deposited in a French bank blocked account or with a notary acting as escrow agent; funds are released only upon production of the Kbis registration certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: premises open to employees only are treated as non-ERP, but a simple attestation of conformity with Article R.123-54 of the Construction Code and a fire extinguisher servicing record must be kept; if any client-facing open area exists, the premises must be classified as an Établissement Recevant du Public (ERP) 5th category, requiring a prior safety visit and favorable opinion from the Municipal Fire Safety Commission (Commission de Sécurité Incendie). Occupational clearance: registration of employees under the Déclaration Préalable à l'Embauche (DPAE), health and safety risk assessment document (DUERP), and check of electrical installation conformity issued by a certified electrician.

Paid-in capital rule: SAS has no mandatory minimum share capital under Article L.227-2 of the Commercial Code; the capital is freely fixed in the Statuts. Where cash contributions are made, the full amount must be subscribed and each cash contribution deposited in a French bank blocked account or with a notary acting as escrow agent; funds are released only upon production of the Kbis registration certificate.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP) – Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; business tax portal: impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel). Customs and indirect duties: Direction générale des douanes et droits indirects (DGDDI), portal: douane.gouv.fr. The DGDDI is the competent authority for customs brokerage, customs representation, EORI issuance, and excise under the Union Customs Code (UCC).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate a French SAS or register a foreign company branch via INPI Guichet unique; obtain SIREN/SIRET from INSEE. Step 2: File VAT registration with the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) using form 13993 or via impots.gouv.fr; receive French VAT number (FR + 2 alphanumeric key + 9-digit SIREN). Step 3: Non-EU established entities may need a French fiscal representative unless a mutual assistance exemption applies; EU-established entities can register directly. Step 4: Obtain EORI from DGDDI via douane.gouv.fr for customs operations. Step 5: For customs brokerage, be established in the EU, declare activity to DGDDI, provide a guarantee, and elect direct or indirect representation; indirect representation creates joint and several customs liability. Step 6: Maintain authorized economic operator (AEO) status where beneficial.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at the standard rate of 25% (reduced 15% up to €42,500 for qualifying SMEs). Not a pass-through entity by default; no territorial tax exemption zone. French tax resident companies are taxed on worldwide income; non-resident companies are taxed on French-source income. Territorial scope: metropolitan France including Corsica; overseas territories generally outside domestic VAT and corporate tax regimes unless specific rules apply.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election. Available French elections include: (i) tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Art. 223 A CGI for French subsidiaries 95% owned, with a 5-year irrevocable election; (ii) parent-subsidiary regime under Arts. 145 and 216 CGI for qualifying dividends; (iii) optional partnership tax regime for newly created SAS under Art. 239 bis AB CGI if statutory conditions are met (not listed, less than 5 years, etc.); (iv) VAT group (assujetti unique) under Art. 256 C CGI since 1 January 2023; (v) hybrid mismatch and anti-abuse rules under ATAD2. Entity classification generally follows legal form, not elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General French VAT registration: no threshold for non-established taxable persons or cross-border B2B services subject to French VAT. Domestic franchise en base thresholds: €37,500 for services (tolerance €41,250) and €85,000 for goods (tolerance €93,500). Customs brokerage B2B services typically require immediate VAT registration due to place of supply in France, import VAT deferment, and the obligation to issue invoices with French VAT or apply the reverse charge. Intra-EU acquisitions or imports also trigger registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility: (i) Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU are exempt under Art. 262-I CGI, provided goods leave the EU and proof of exit is retained. (ii) Services directly connected with export/import operations may be exempt under Art. 262-II CGI, including certain customs brokerage, forwarding, and ancillary services linked to goods under export/import customs procedures. (iii) International transport services are exempt under Art. 262-II-2° CGI. (iv) B2B services to non-EU customers may be outside the scope of French VAT under Art. 44 VAT Directive, but customs brokerage related to French import may remain taxable in France. (v) Zero-rating requires timely, verifiable documentary evidence; otherwise French VAT at 20% applies. France imposes no general FX repatriation deadline; export proceeds may be held abroad.

B2G e-invoicing is mandatory via Chorus Pro. B2B/B2C e-invoicing and e-reporting mandate under French Finance Law 2024/2026: phased implementation via approved partner platforms (Plateformes de Dématérialisation Partenaires – PDP) and the public invoicing portal (Portail Public de Facturation – PPF). Non-compliance penalties under Art. 1788 D CGI: €15 per invoice up to €15,000 per year; €250 per e-reporting omission up to €15,000 per year.

French tax and customs authorities conduct retrospective audits. VAT assessment period: 3 years (until the end of the third year following the taxable event); extended to 10 years for concealed activity, offshore accounts, or fraudulent export zero-rating. Penalties under CGI: 10% for late payment; 40% for deliberate breach; 80% for aggravated fraud (false documents, fictitious invoices, concealment). Interest: 0.20% per month. Retrospective clawback: VAT zero-rating is denied if proof of export is not produced; output VAT is reassessed with penalties and interest. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility for EUR and other freely convertible currencies within the framework of EU capital movements rules (Arts. L112-1 et seq. of the Code monétaire et financier). Capital inflows (equity increases, shareholder loans, M&A) require no prior FX approval for non-sanctioned counterparties; however, any financial transaction or payment to or from non-EEA residents exceeding €15,000 must be reported via the banking intermediary using the Banque de France / DG Trésor statistical code. Profit remittance by a SAS is fully permitted, but non-resident beneficiaries are subject to default 25% withholding tax on dividends unless reduced under an applicable double-tax treaty; treasury planning should obtain a tax residency certificate and treaty-position memo before outbound payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no general percentage-based surrender requirement for French resident companies receiving export receipts in foreign currency. However, every export payment must be collected in France through an authorized intermediary bank (credit établissement) and repatriated to the company's account within statutory payment terms of the underlying contract; the proceeds may be either converted to EUR or credited to a foreign-currency account. A zero percent mandatory conversion ratio applies in practice, and for statistical purposes the receiving bank records the transaction under the BDF external reporting code. In the specific customs-brokerage context, settlement of import duties and VAT must be eventually made in EUR via the DGDDI collection mechanism (Délégation de Domidiation / télé-réglement), so treasury should maintain adequate EUR liquidity to cover customs debt surge.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France, BdF) in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) for external financial relations, with prudential oversight exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) and customs-oriented exchange rules administered by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM for core French customs-brokerage treasury. Legal constraints: an EEA-licensed Electronic Money Institution (EMI) can maintain a euro-denominated payment account for the SAS via passporting under PSD2, and can process cross-border MT103-equivalent transfers in certain supported corridors. However, the DGDDI will not accept EMI references for customs debit authorisations (DDN), and many French counterparties (commercial ports, freight handlers, and VAT collector accounts) require direct debiting through a full ACPR-licensed bank. Recommended approach: use a fintech EMI as a lightweight secondary rail for disbursements below compliance thresholds, while mandatorily holding the primary treasury accounts and customs guarantees with a Tier-1 French bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM for core French customs-brokerage treasury. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés) under Article 219 CGI for fiscal years opened on or after 1 January 2022; the reduced SME rate is 15% on the first EUR 38,120 of taxable profit; groups with turnover of at least EUR 1 billion are subject to an additional 3% contribution resulting in an effective headline ceiling of approximately 28% on taxable profit above EUR 2.5 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax under Article 119 bis 2 CGI on dividends paid by a French SAS to non-resident corporate shareholders; not applicable where the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive transposed into domestic law provides a 0% rate for a qualified EU parent holding at least 5% for 24 months; dividend distributions to non-resident individuals are subject to the 12.8% flat tax (prelevement forfaitaire unique) plus social levies in the absence of a more favourable treaty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000: full transfer pricing documentation (Master File and Local File) is mandatory for any French legal entity, including an SAS, whose annual turnover (excluding VAT) or gross assets equal or exceed EUR 400 million; the requirement also applies where the SAS holds at least 50% of, or is at least 50% held or controlled by, an entity meeting that threshold, or where the French entity belongs to a group of companies with aggregate turnover above EUR 400 million and the French entity's own turnover is at least EUR 50 million. The threshold is found in Article L.13 AA of the French Book of Tax Procedure (LPF). Penalty for failure to supply or disclose the documentation: 5% of the reassessed amount when a transfer pricing adjustment is made, or a fixed sanction of EUR 10,000 per missing document when no adjustment is proposed.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filings; however, the doctrine of substance over form is embedded in the French abuse of law concept (abus de droit, Article L64 LPF) and in the OECD principal purpose test incorporated into French treaty practice and the BEPS MLI. A French SAS customs brokerage must demonstrate real commercial substance: a permanent registered office, effective management located in France, operational employees, physical premises, accounting and customs records, and the valid customs brokerage licence; artificial letterbox arrangements with foreign control are recharacterised under Articles 57 and 209 B CGI, triggering the 80% anti-abuse penalty and denial of double taxation treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and concealment of taxable revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI: criminal fine of EUR 750,000 (raised to EUR 3,000,000 for aggravated or organised fraud) and imprisonment of up to 7 years; the tax administration applies the 80% fraud penalty under Article 1729 CGI in addition to the evaded tax and monthly late payment interest at 0.20%; the court may order confiscation of the fraud proceeds, publication of the judgment in newspapers and posting on the company website, and prohibition from carrying on the customs brokerage activity.

Director personal liability: The President and directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt under Article L267 LPF where fraudulent manoeuvres contributed to the non-payment; criminal conviction exposes directors to personal imprisonment and fines, travel bans (interdiction du territoire) for non-French nationals, disqualification from managing commercial companies for up to 15 years, and an action for misuse of corporate assets (abus de biens sociaux) with a penalty of 5 years imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine.
Shell company / artificial arrangement and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test and the abuse of law doctrine; 80% penalty under Article 1729-0 bis CGI on the recharacterised profits; reassessment of the full French corporate income tax with 0.20% monthly late interest; revocation or suspension of the customs brokerage licence under Article 413-1 of the French Customs Code; confiscation of the assets of the artificial structure; additional customs fines of up to twice the evaded duties.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: shareholders and de facto directors are treated as legal managers and may be personally liable for the unpaid corporate taxes and customs duties; directors face criminal prosecution for the offence of false customs declarations with imprisonment of up to 3 years; the court may impose travel bans, freezing orders on personal assets, and prohibition from exercising a commercial profession for up to 15 years.
AML-TRACFIN reporting deficiency and money laundering facilitation

Statutory sanction: French Monetary and Financial Code Article L561-3: administrative fines of up to EUR 15,000 for failure to report suspicious transactions; money laundering offence under Articles 324-1 et seq. of the French Penal Code: imprisonment of 5 years and a fine of EUR 375,000; habitual or organised money laundering increases the sanction to 10 years imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine; seizure and confiscation of the brokerage accounts, vehicles and instrumentalities of the offence; suspension of the customs broker authorisation.

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to personal criminal prosecution for money laundering; the court may impose a travel ban, a prohibition on exercising a customs or financial profession for up to 15 years (Article 131-27 Penal Code), confiscation of personal bank accounts and assets, and civil liability for client losses; foreign directors may face extradition requests and an entry ban on French territory.
Transfer pricing manipulation and sham intercompany rebilling

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under Article 57 CGI reincorporating the excessive payments into the French taxable base; 40% penalty for deliberate misstatement under Article 1729 CGI; 80% penalty for an abusive arrangement under Article 1729-0 bis CGI; late-payment interest of 0.20% per month; failure to produce transfer pricing documentation results in a penalty of 5% of the reassessment, with a minimum of EUR 10,000 per missing document; the customs brokerage licence may be suspended for violations of customs declaration rules.

Director personal liability: Officers having designed and executed the artificial rebilling structure face an action for civil liability under Article 1843 of the Civil Code to recover the tax shortfall; criminal liability under Article 1741 CGI attaches where the pricing scheme was a deliberate fraud on the French Treasury; the court may order personal fines, imprisonment, travel bans, seizure of benefits and gains, and disqualification from corporate management.
