Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official impots.gouv.fr portal. Corporate registration data and business identifiers are also coordinated through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is incorporated through the Guichet unique with submission of its articles, registered office details, directors, beneficial-owner information, activity description, and supporting identification documents. The INPI process transmits the filing to the competent registries and tax authorities. The entity receives a SIREN number for the legal entity and a SIRET number for each establishment. The DGFiP allocates the French intra-Community VAT identification number, generally consisting of the country prefix FR, a two-digit key, and the nine-digit SIREN number. The SAS must provide its legal, banking, activity, premises, and expected turnover information to the SIE and request VAT activation where required. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies through the designated non-established-business tax service of the DGFiP for French VAT registration, submitting its certificate of incorporation, foreign tax registration, constitutional documents, proof of business activity, identity of authorized representatives, and evidence of taxable transactions. Businesses established in another EU Member State may use the applicable Union OSS regime for eligible intra-Community distance sales and specified B2C services, but OSS does not replace ordinary French VAT registration where French domestic transactions, imports, stock, or establishment-related obligations require it.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A qualifying closely held SAS may elect temporary taxation under the income-tax partnership-style regime under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code, generally for a maximum period of five fiscal years and subject to conditions concerning ownership, voting rights, size, age, and operating activity. The election is not a general United States-style check-the-box election and is not available solely by filing a classification form. France has anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implementing the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive, including limitations on deductions and denial of duplicate deductions or deductions without corresponding inclusion; these rules do not provide a general elective hybrid classification. VAT franchise, real-tax, and simplified reporting regimes are compliance regimes rather than entity-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the French VAT franchise regime, the principal annual turnover threshold is EUR 37,500 for services and non-commercial activities and EUR 85,000 for goods, accommodation, and certain mixed activities. Increased thresholds are generally EUR 41,250 for services and EUR 93,500 for goods. A fulfillment, kitting, and packaging operator must classify each transaction based on its economic substance; services involving customer-owned goods generally follow the services thresholds, while sales of packaged or assembled goods may follow the goods thresholds. Exceeding the applicable threshold or voluntarily opting for ordinary VAT taxation triggers VAT charging, filing, invoicing, and deduction obligations. Imports, intra-Community acquisitions, domestic stock, and other transaction-specific rules can create registration obligations independently of the franchise thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the EU may be invoiced without French VAT where the goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU by the supplier or by the customer acting under the applicable conditions, and the supplier retains prescribed evidence of export and exit from the EU customs territory. The export must be reported under the correct customs procedure and linked to the commercial invoice and transport records. Intra-Community supplies of goods to a VAT-identified customer in another EU Member State may qualify for VAT exemption, subject to valid customer VAT identification, transport evidence, recapitulative reporting, and transaction-level documentation. B2B services supplied to a business established outside France generally follow the customer-location rule and are outside the scope of French VAT rather than technically zero-rated exports; the supplier must retain evidence of the customer's business status and location. Services directly connected with export transactions may qualify for specific VAT exemptions where statutory conditions are met. Export VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices, business-use evidence, deductible-tax eligibility, customs and transport records where relevant, and timely submission through the applicable French or EU refund procedure.

  • Electronic export declaration filed through the applicable French customs system, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent electronic proof that the goods exited the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, Incoterms, and VAT treatment
  • Packing list and detailed description of the kitted or packaged goods
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, or courier export record
  • Warehouse, fulfillment, assembly, and stock-movement records linking the exported goods to the customer order
  • Proof of delivery or receipt outside the EU where available and relevant
  • Customer order, contract, and purchase documentation
  • Evidence of customer location and, for intra-Community supplies, the customer's valid VAT identification number
  • Accounting records, VAT returns, export sales ledger, and reconciliation between invoices, customs declarations, and transport documents
  • Bank payment records or other commercial payment evidence where relevant; French law does not generally require an inward-remittance certificate as a universal export zero-rating condition

France is introducing mandatory domestic B2B electronic invoicing and transaction reporting in phases. From 1 September 2026, all businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices; large and medium-sized enterprises are generally required to issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from that date. Small and micro-enterprises are generally required to issue them from 1 September 2027. Electronic invoices must be exchanged through an approved private platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire, or PDP) or the designated public infrastructure as legally available. France uses a structured exchange and data-reporting model rather than a universal real-time pre-clearance system for every invoice. E-reporting of specified B2C and foreign transactions is phased with the same timetable. Invoices must contain mandatory French and EU VAT particulars, including supplier and customer identification, VAT numbers where applicable, invoice date and unique sequential number, taxable amount, rate, VAT amount, currency, payment terms, and applicable reverse-charge or exemption references. Electronic customs systems and EDI/API interfaces may be required for export declarations and customs data, but customs data exchange is distinct from VAT invoice clearance.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Export zero-rating or exemption may be denied and French VAT reassessed where the supplier cannot prove dispatch or transport outside the EU, customs exit, customer status, transaction substance, or accurate reporting. The reassessed VAT is generally accompanied by late-payment interest, ordinarily calculated at 0.20% per month under the French Tax Code, subject to applicable statutory rules. Tax penalties may include a 10% surcharge for late or deficient payment, a 40% surcharge for deliberate failure, and an 80% surcharge for fraudulent conduct or abusive manoeuvres, with specific rules and procedural safeguards applying. Incorrect invoices may expose the issuer to liability for VAT shown or improperly deducted. In addition, input VAT refunds may be rejected or clawed back, and customs duties or import VAT may be reassessed where customs declarations are inaccurate. Serious intentional fraud may trigger criminal proceedings, fines, and imprisonment under applicable French law. The taxpayer must preserve reliable records for the applicable statutory retention period, generally at least six years for tax documentation, with longer periods potentially applying to accounting, customs, or commercial records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. The euro is freely convertible, and no prior foreign-exchange approval is required for capital contributions, trade settlements, or profit remittances by an SAS. Dividend and profit remittance hurdles are documentary only: distributable profits must be evidenced by filed annual accounts, the paying bank may request a French tax residence certificate, and reduced treaty-withholding relief requires the appropriate attestation from the French and foreign tax authorities. There are no quantitative remittance caps, but all reportable cross-border payments must be identified to Banque de France, generally through the authorized bank's balance-of-payments declaration system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no compulsory conversion ratio in France. Export receivables may be retained indefinitely in any convertible currency or in EUR, with a mandatory conversion ratio of 0%. Export transactions are not subject to repatriation deadlines, but the receiving bank must capture the transaction for French balance-of-payments statistical reporting where the monthly or annual reporting thresholds apply.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting in coordination with the Direction Generale du Tresor for balance-of-payments and foreign-exchange statistical administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. French and EEA-licensed electronic money institutions are suitable supplementary rails for cross-border FX, low-value disbursements, and vendor payments, provided they hold a valid ACPR authorization or a PSD2 passport into France. Offshore non-EEA digital EMIs without an EU passport cannot lawfully service the French market as a primary bank, cannot issue French IBANs, and cannot be used for the SAS's statutory corporate account; they may only function as payment-orchestration or virtual-account technology layers through an authorized partner bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed exclusively through cleared and fully identified bank channels. For EEA or SEPA shareholders, use EUR SEPA Credit Transfer to the SAS's French IBAN. For non-SEPA shareholders, use SWIFT MT103 with the French receiving bank as beneficiary bank, including a genuine remitter, the SAS's legal name and Kbis-matched beneficiary identifier, and the French balance-of-payments purpose classification for direct investment or equity participation. The remittance reference must state 'Capital subscription - SAS [name] - transaction number'. The bank will require the capital subscription deed and, where funds are first placed in escrow, the depository certificate before releasing funds to the operational account. No anonymous cash or third-party pass-through settlement is acceptable.Trade payments for fulfillment and kitting assembly should use: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR payments to EU suppliers; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA or non-EUR disbursements, with beneficiary name, IBAN, and remittance details matching the underlying purchase order and commercial invoice; and (iii) irrevocable import documentary credits governed by ICC UCP 600 for high-value packaging machinery, tooling, or bulk material purchases where the supplier requires a bank payment undertaking. Supporting documents must include purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, proof of delivery or CMR, and, where requested, final-consignee certificates for dual-use or controlled logistics inputs. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax under the French General Tax Code; qualifying small and medium-sized enterprises may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to ownership and turnover conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident legal entity are generally subject to 25% withholding tax under domestic law, subject to treaty relief and exemptions. A qualifying EU parent company may obtain exemption under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive implementation where the minimum holding, holding-period, subject-to-tax, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. Dividends paid to non-resident individuals are generally subject to a 12.8% domestic withholding rate, with separate social-contribution rules potentially relevant to French residents.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and tax package are generally due within three months after the fiscal year-end, or by the second business day following 1 May for a financial year ending on 31 December. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end; for a 31 December year-end, the balance is generally due by 15 May.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Installments are generally due on the 15th day of March, June, September, and December. The last installment may be adjusted under the statutory annual installment rules. The balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month after the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 threshold for the principal large-enterprise transfer-pricing documentation obligations under French law, based on turnover or gross assets, or where the enterprise is controlled by, controls, or belongs to a group containing an entity meeting the statutory threshold. A Local File and Master File may also be required under specific group and transaction circumstances. Country-by-Country Reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a separate UAE-style standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime. Substance is nevertheless material under the French general anti-abuse rule, beneficial-ownership principles, EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive measures, controlled-foreign-company rules, interest limitation rules, treaty anti-abuse provisions, and the principal-purpose test. A French SAS performing fulfillment, kitting, packaging, or 3PL functions should maintain real personnel, premises or operational capacity, inventory and process controls, commercial risk evidence, decision-making records, and contemporaneous intercompany pricing support. Artificial arrangements lacking commercial substance may be recharacterized, denied treaty benefits, or challenged under abuse-of-law rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, penalties generally ranging from 40% to 80% of the evaded tax, and criminal sanctions. Ordinary tax fraud may result in up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine for an individual; aggravated cases may result in up to seven years' imprisonment and a EUR 3 million fine, subject to statutory aggravating conditions. Corporate criminal fines can be increased under the rules applicable to legal persons.

Director personal liability: The managing director or de facto manager may incur personal criminal liability, civil liability for misconduct in management, and tax-director liability where deliberate or fraudulent conduct caused non-payment. A court may impose asset seizure, disqualification from management, or judicial travel restrictions where legally justified; travel bans are not automatic. The corporate veil is not pierced merely because the SAS owes tax.
VAT fraud and carousel fraud

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, repayment of improperly claimed credits, 40% or 80% penalties depending on conduct, late-payment interest, joint and several recovery in qualifying cases, and criminal prosecution for fraud, forgery, or money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and finance officers may face criminal liability, personal tax-recovery proceedings for fraudulent conduct, management bans, asset seizure, and imprisonment where the evidence establishes intentional participation or knowing facilitation.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of French taxable income, withholding-tax exposure, interest limitation or deduction denial, penalties for inadequate documentation, and possible 40% or 80% penalties where the conduct is deliberate or abusive. The French tax administration may apply the arm's-length principle and abuse-of-law provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for deliberate management misconduct, fraudulent tax conduct, or knowingly approving unsupported transactions. Routine transfer-pricing errors do not automatically create personal criminal liability.
Shell company, artificial substance, and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, withholding at domestic rates, reassessment under the general anti-abuse rule, penalties of up to 80% in serious abusive cases, interest, and potential criminal consequences if false records or fraudulent declarations are used.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil claims, tax-recovery actions, criminal prosecution for fraud or money laundering where applicable, management disqualification, and seizure of proceeds. No automatic veil piercing or automatic travel ban follows solely from insufficient substance.
Anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing violation

Statutory sanction: Money laundering may result in imprisonment and fines, with aggravated penalties for organized or repeated conduct. Proceeds may be confiscated, and the company may face criminal fines, dissolution, prohibition from conducting activities, or exclusion from public contracts where ordered by a court.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face imprisonment, personal fines, confiscation, professional disqualification, and judicial travel restrictions. Liability requires personal participation, authorization, complicity, or legally established management fault; corporate status does not immunize an individual.
Undeclared foreign accounts and cross-border reporting

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, tax reassessment, late-payment interest, enhanced penalties for concealed assets, and potential criminal tax-fraud proceedings where deliberate concealment is established.

Director personal liability: The individual who owns, controls, or deliberately administers the undisclosed account may be personally assessed and prosecuted. Directors are not automatically liable for accounts belonging solely to the company unless they participated in or authorized the concealment.
Customs, import, and fulfillment documentation fraud

Statutory sanction: Recovery of duties and import VAT, customs fines, seizure or forfeiture of goods, suspension of customs simplifications, and criminal prosecution for smuggling or document fraud in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and customs representatives may incur personal liability where they knowingly submit false declarations or instruct unlawful import practices. The company may also face joint liability, loss of customs authorizations, and exclusion from trusted-trader programs.
Accounting, corporate governance, and insolvency misconduct

Statutory sanction: Accounting sanctions, rejection of deductions, civil claims, insolvency clawback, management bans, contribution to insufficient assets, bankruptcy-related penalties, and criminal sanctions for concealment or fraudulent bankruptcy.

Director personal liability: SAS officers and de facto managers may be ordered to contribute to company liabilities where management misconduct caused an asset shortfall and may face personal civil restitution, disqualification, asset seizure, or imprisonment for intentional insolvency offenses.
