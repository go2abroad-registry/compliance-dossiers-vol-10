Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent local Registry of the Commercial Court (greffe du tribunal de commerce), with company-formation formalities submitted through the national Guichet unique managed by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code, including Articles L. 210-1 et seq. (commercial companies), L. 227-1 to L. 227-20 (SAS), and R. 123-1 et seq. (commercial and companies register formalities). The SAS is governed principally by its articles of association, subject to mandatory statutory rules.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company-formation formalities (formalités de création) filed through the Guichet unique, including the declaration of creation and filing of the signed articles of association; the registry records the company in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 23 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A SAS has no statutory minimum share capital; €1 is legally possible, subject to commercial and financing needs. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years after registration. Cash contributions must be deposited before filing with an eligible bank, notary, or other legally authorized depositary; the funds are released upon presentation of evidence of registration. Contributions in kind are generally subject to valuation by a commissaire aux apports unless a statutory exemption applies.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupancy, obtain any required building or planning approvals and comply with the applicable fire-safety rules for the building’s classification, including ERP requirements if the premises receive the public. Confirm structural capacity for equipment and storage through competent design review where relevant. Implement French Labour Code workplace risk prevention, occupational-health arrangements, fire precautions, emergency procedures, and required workplace inspections. Additional site-specific clearances may apply to classified installations, pressure equipment, chemical storage, or other regulated hazards.

Paid-in capital rule: A SAS has no statutory minimum share capital; €1 is legally possible, subject to commercial and financing needs. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years after registration. Cash contributions must be deposited before filing with an eligible bank, notary, or other legally authorized depositary; the funds are released upon presentation of evidence of registration. Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Mandatory Environmental Clearance

There is no general French industrial licensing board or universal manufacturing operating permit. The competent authority for an ICPE installation is generally the Prefect of the department, with technical processing by the regional DREAL or, in specified territories, the relevant environmental inspection service. Medical-device market-access oversight is exercised by the Agence nationale de sécurité du médicament et des produits de santé (ANSM), alongside the applicable EU conformity-assessment and notified-body framework.

Category: No general operating-license title applies solely because the company manufactures IVD equipment. Depending on site activities and thresholds, the facility may require an ICPE declaration, registration, or environmental authorization under the French Environmental Code. In-vitro diagnostic devices placed on the EU market must comply with Regulation (EU) 2017/746 (IVDR), including applicable conformity assessment, CE marking, and registration and vigilance obligations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — French Public Finances Directorate; taxpayer portal impots.gouv.fr; customs authority Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) via pro.douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the SAS with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) through the CFE (Centre de Formalités des Entreprises) to obtain the 9-digit SIREN and 14-digit SIRET. Step 2: File French VAT (TVA) registration form Cerfa N°15417*03 with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); for non-established foreign investors the application is handled by the Non-Resident Tax Department (Service de l'impôt des non-résidents) which issues the FR intra-Community VAT identification number. Step 3: Non-EU persons without an EU establishment must appoint a DGFiP-approved fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) for VAT compliance unless the non-Union OSS or EU VAT refund regime applies. Step 4: Apply to the DGDDI for an EORI number (pro.douane.gouv.fr) before first customs clearance. Step 5: Activate the SIE account on impots.gouv.fr and update the ERP master data with SIREN, SIRET, TVA intra-communautaire and EORI identifiers. Typical completion time for the French SAS is 4 to 6 weeks from formation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the SAS is compulsorily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the standard 25% rate (reduced 15% rate up to EUR 42,500 net taxable profit for SMEs with turnover ≤ EUR 10 million) on all profits subject to French domestic territoriality rules. No shareholder-level pass-through is available to an SAS under French domestic law and no US-style check-the-box regime exists. Territorial scope: France applies a worldwide taxation model for resident companies with foreign tax credit and branch loss mechanisms; certain activities implemented in overseas collectivities (COM) may benefit from territorial exemption regimes, but the mainland IVD manufacturing activity remains fully within the mainland French tax net.

Available election forms

France does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid pass-through election. The material elections and mechanisms available to the SAS are: (i) intégration fiscale (group tax consolidation) under CGI Art. 223 A, allowing a 95%-owned parent SAS to consolidate French subsidiaries' results; (ii) the parent-subsidiary (mère-fille) participation exemption on qualifying dividend income (CGI Art. 145); (iii) the dividend and capital-gain holding-period tracking rules; (iv) ATAD 1/2 anti-hybrid recharacterization elections implemented under LOI 2019-1479 and CGI Art. 212 bis, which permit the recharacterization of hybrid instrument payments and entity mismatches to eliminate double non-taxation; (v) the EU OSS non-Union registration election for distance/electronic B2C supplies within the EU; and (vi) the customs authorized economic operator (AEO) certification elections for simplified export clearances. None of these mechanisms converts the SAS into a transparent entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France applies full euro-zone capital account liberalization; the euro is fully convertible for current account and capital transactions. Profit remittances by a French SAS to non-resident shareholders are freely permitted, subject to the prior completion of statutory filing obligations, corporate income tax settlement, and the submission of Form 2561 or the report of payment of dividends to the tax administration. No prior exchange control authorization is required for dividend distribution or loan repayments, provided the transaction is routed through a licensed credit institution and the beneficial ownership documentation complies with French AML/KYC rules. For inbound capital injections by non-resident shareholders, the euro-denominated contribution requires no Bank of France foreign-exchange approval except in sectors subject to prior authorization under Decree No. 2019-1590 (foreign investment screening), which does not apply to IVD manufacturing absent technology-control triggers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no export proceeds surrender requirement. Exporters are entitled to retain all proceeds in foreign currency or euro accounts and are not compelled to convert receipts within any statutory timeline. However, proceeds must be declared in aggregate via the annual 'déclaration de change' under Article L. 152-1 of the Monetary and Financial Code where cumulative transactions exceed EUR 10,000 per calendar month; banking counterparties require standard underlying commercial documentation (invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration) under French anti-money laundering obligations.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France and Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) acting under the European Central Bank's Single Supervisory Mechanism; foreign exchange policy administered by the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is Limited. French-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine, and Treezor may provide operational euro accounts and payment initiation services, but are not viable for holding client escrow, trade-finance documentary credits, or large capital-injection settlement because they are not credit institutions, do not provide commercial loans or import/export letters of credit, and are subject to stricter activity limits under Article L. 521-3 of the Monetary and Financial Code. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled outside the EEA cannot legally provide payment services or hold accounts for a French SAS without a French or EU passporting licence, and would trigger French AML compliance rejection; their viability is rated Low for treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection routing compliant with French monetary regulation requires: (i) classification of payment purpose code as 'subscription of shares' or 'contribution to share capital' under the IMF BPM7 current-transfer classification, using SWIFT MT103 with Field 70 purpose 'CAPCONTRIB' or 'SUSB' and French Bank of France counterparty code 22019; (ii) funds remitted to the SAS's dedicated capital-escrow account (compte bloqué) at BNP Paribas or Société Générale; (iii) completion of the Banque de France financial reporting obligation for foreign direct investment through the 'Téléprocédure FDI' portal within 20 days after the transaction if the investor is non-EU or the transaction meets the EUR 15,000,000 threshold; (iv) verification of the declarant legal status and anti-money-laundering SOF (source of funds) documentation; (v) only after the full credit is verified and notarized by the bank, the notary's certificate of release (mainlevée de blocage) permits escrowed funds to be transferred to the operational account. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate in metropolitan France and overseas departments, subject to applicable territorial rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident corporate recipient, subject to domestic exemptions, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive implementation, tax treaties, and anti-abuse rules. A qualifying EU or EEA parent may obtain exemption where statutory ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. French domestic withholding on dividends paid to non-resident individuals is generally 12.8%, subject to treaty and status rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For financial years ending on dates other than 31 December, the electronic annual return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. For a 31 December year-end, the return is generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the following year. The corporate income tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following the year-end; for a 31 December year-end, the balance is generally due by 15 May. Instalments are generally payable by 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, subject to statutory exceptions and adjustments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 for the principal full transfer-pricing documentation obligation where the French entity's turnover or gross assets meet the statutory threshold, where it belongs to a group meeting the threshold, or where another statutory control or group condition applies. A separate annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration generally applies at the lower EUR 150,000,000 threshold or where other statutory conditions are met. The Local File and Master File must be prepared and maintained in accordance with French requirements and OECD principles.

Economic substance scope: France has no single, generally applicable economic-substance regulation equivalent to the UAE ESR regime. Substance is assessed through the French abuse-of-law doctrine, general anti-abuse rules, beneficial-owner analysis, permanent-establishment principles, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules, anti-hybrid rules, treaty principal-purpose tests, and the requirement that expenses correspond to a real service and serve the company's business interest. The SAS should maintain French decision-making, qualified personnel, premises or operational capacity, technical control, inventory and risk management, IP functions, banking records, and contemporaneous evidence proportionate to its IVD manufacturing and distribution activities. A contract, invoice, or legal ownership record alone is insufficient where conduct and functions contradict the asserted arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Tax recovery with interest and penalties; fraudulent tax evasion may result in imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with aggravated cases potentially reaching seven years and EUR 3,000,000, subject to the applicable facts and statutory provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, civil liability, and joint and several liability for tax debts where their fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated non-compliance made recovery impossible. Courts may impose professional prohibitions, asset seizure, and, where legally ordered, travel restrictions. The corporate veil is not automatically pierced solely because of control or ownership.
VAT Carousel, Missing Trader, or False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, denial of input VAT, repayment of refunds, late-payment interest, penalties generally ranging from 40% to 80% depending on intent and conduct, and possible criminal prosecution for aggravated fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly organize or approve fraudulent VAT arrangements may face criminal fines, imprisonment, management prohibitions, and personal tax-debt liability under applicable French procedures.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to arm's-length profit, withholding-tax consequences, interest, penalties for deliberate or fraudulent conduct, documentation penalties, and possible application of the French abuse-of-law or general anti-abuse rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed where they knowingly approve sham transactions, materially false documentation, or repeated non-compliance that prevents tax recovery. Ordinary disagreement over an arm's-length method does not by itself create personal criminal liability.
Shell Company, Beneficial Ownership, or Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or domestic exemption, withholding-tax reassessment, abuse-of-law penalties, interest, and potential criminal consequences where concealment, falsification, or laundering is established. A shell company is not unlawful solely because it has limited operations; unlawful consequences depend on purpose, conduct, and applicable anti-abuse rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may face civil recovery, criminal prosecution, asset confiscation, management bans, and personal tax liability where they knowingly participate in concealment or abusive arrangements.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Regulatory, administrative, and criminal sanctions may include fines, confiscation, account restrictions, business prohibitions, and imprisonment depending on the underlying conduct. French financial institutions may freeze or reject transactions and file suspicious-transaction reports with TRACFIN.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal criminal and civil liability for knowing participation, facilitation, or grossly deficient controls. Travel restrictions, asset freezing, and imprisonment may apply under the relevant criminal provisions.
Customs, Import Valuation, and Origin Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Recovery of customs duties and import VAT, customs penalties, seizure or forfeiture of goods, loss of simplified customs authorizations, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent customs declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible customs officers may be liable where they knowingly authorize fraudulent declarations or fail to maintain required controls. Personal exposure can include fines, asset seizure, professional disqualification, and imprisonment in aggravated cases.
Medical-Device Regulatory and Product-Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative orders, withdrawal or recall, suspension or prohibition of placing products on the market, destruction or seizure of products, civil damages, and criminal penalties under applicable EU and French medical-device laws.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal and civil exposure for knowing distribution of unsafe or non-compliant devices, falsified technical records, obstruction of regulators, or failure to act after a safety signal. Corporate limited liability does not protect deliberate misconduct.
Accounting, Corporate-Law, and Insolvency Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Nullity of distributions, restitution, damages, management bans, bankruptcy-related sanctions, criminal fines, and imprisonment for abuse of corporate assets, fraudulent accounting, or bankruptcy offenses.

Director personal liability: Managers may be ordered to contribute to all or part of an insufficient insolvency estate where management faults contributed to the shortfall. Criminal courts may impose imprisonment, fines, confiscation, and director disqualification.
