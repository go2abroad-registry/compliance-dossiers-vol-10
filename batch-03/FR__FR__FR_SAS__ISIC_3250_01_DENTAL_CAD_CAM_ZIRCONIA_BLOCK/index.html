Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), acting through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); corporate and VAT registration are initiated through the Guichet Unique operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI). Electronic portals: impots.gouv.fr (tax filing and VAT returns), formalites.entreprises.fr (entity and TIN registration), and douane.gouv.fr (customs and export declarations).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS obtains its statutory tax identification number (numéro SIREN/SIRET) automatically upon company incorporation via the Guichet Unique, which transmits the registration file to the INSEE and the DGFiP. The VAT intra-Community identification number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire, format FR + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN) is issued ex officio by the DGFiP after the INSEE registration is completed. A foreign entity without a French establishment but carrying out taxable transactions in France must apply directly to the DGFiP for a TIN/VAT number under Article 286 ter of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI); a non-established operator must in principle appoint an accredited fiscal representative in France under Article 289 A CGI, unless an EU simplification applies, and must provide proof of its foreign registration, identity documents of the legal representative, and a description of the intended French activities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SAS is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, Article 206-1 CGI) at entity level. It is not pass-through eligible by default, and its French corporate tax residence entails taxation of worldwide profits subject to tax-treaty relief and to the territoriality carve-out for foreign permanent establishments in certain treaty situations. France has no general check-the-box regime; the civil-law classification of the SAS as a limited-liability company is determinative. — A French SAS obtains its statutory tax identification number (numéro SIREN/SIRET) automatically upon company incorporation via the Guichet Unique, which transmits the registration file to the INSEE and the DGFiP. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A French SAS is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, Article 206-1 CGI) at entity level. It is not pass-through eligible by default, and its French corporate tax residence entails taxation of worldwide profits subject to tax-treaty relief and to the territoriality carve-out for foreign permanent establishments in certain treaty situations. France has no general check-the-box regime; the civil-law classification of the SAS as a limited-liability company is determinative.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists for French entities. Available elective mechanisms include: (1) French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI, allowing a French parent holding at least 95% of a French subsidiary to consolidate results, available to an SAS forming a group; (2) the option for the personal-income-tax/partnership regime under Article 239 bis AB CGI, available in narrow conditions only to SARL-type companies and certain family/SME entities, and not generally available to an SAS with non-individual shareholders; (3) the option for corporate income tax for entities that would otherwise be taxed as partnerships; (4) the VAT group (assujetti unique) election under Article 256 C CGI once the relevant administrative authorisation is obtained; and (5) hybrid-mismatch filing and documentation obligations under Articles 212 bis B and 212 bis C CGI, which require calibrated elections and contemporaneous transfer-pricing evidence for cross-border related-party transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold triggers VAT registration in France: any person independently carrying out a taxable economic activity is liable for VAT from the first taxable transaction. The statutory small-business exemption (franchise en base de TVA, Article 293 B CGI) applies only to established taxable persons whose preceding calendar-year turnover does not exceed EUR 91,900 for supplies of goods or EUR 36,800 for services (with reduced thresholds where the activity comprises a service component above EUR 10,000); a manufacturing SAS selling dental zirconia blocks to dental laboratories and professionals will not benefit from the exemption if it opts to invoice VAT or if it exceeds the threshold, and in any event the exemption is not available for enterprises formed as SAS unless they are in their first year of activity and meet the statutory ceilings.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible movable goods physically leaving France and the European Union are exempt from French VAT with credit for input VAT under Article 262-I CGI (true export). To claim the exemption, the SAS must prove: (i) transfer of the power to dispose of the goods in its capacity as supplier; (ii) actual and decisive removal of the goods from EU customs territory within the statutory or administrative timeframe, supported by a customs export declaration (régime douanier 1000) accepted by French customs via DELTA-X and the official exit certificate (preuve d'exportation, typically the Bill of Lading, airway bill, or CMR coupled with an electronic exit record); (iii) compliance with the 90-day administrative deadline for the goods to leave the Union; and (iv) retention of a complete documentary file in France. If the dental zirconia blocks are sold to a taxable customer established in another EU member state, the transaction is classified as an intra-Community supply exempt under Article 262 ter I CGI, which additionally requires the customer's valid VAT number, the inclusion of the sale in the EC Sales List (déclaration d'échanges de biens), and delivery to the customer in that member state.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation, DELTA-X format, customs procedure code 1000) accepted by French customs and the export exit record
  • Bill of Lading (connaissement), airway bill, CMR, or multimodal transport document showing the goods leaving France/EU territory
  • Commercial invoice with full Incoterms and export references, together with the purchase order or sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank credit evidence (SWIFT MT103/MT950 or attestation de domiciliation bancaire) proving settlement of the export payment
  • Proof of arrival or customs entry in the destination country, where available, and VAT registration data of the customer for intra-EU supplies

France is transitioning to mandatory B2B electronic invoicing and e-reporting through the Reforme de la Facturation Electronique. The implementation timeline is phased: large and mid-sized enterprises must issue and receive structured e-invoices from 1 September 2026, and SMEs from 1 September 2027. Electronic invoices must be transmitted through an immatriculated Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or through the free Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) using structured formats such as Factur-X, UBL, or CII/EN 16931. Transaction and invoice data are compulsorily transmitted to the DGFiP for VAT control, thereby creating a continuous transaction control environment comparable to real-time clearance, although France does not use a fiscal stamping or pre-clearance system. Customs export operations are declared in real-time via DELTA-X through the Guichet Unique National (GUN), and intra-EU goods flows require DES/Intrastat declarations.

The DGFiP may reopen the export exemption position for the standard three-year prescription period, extended to six years where the taxpayer has failed to file returns or has engaged in an activity designed to dissimulate taxable operations. Any deficiency in the export proof file triggers the reversal of the zero rating and the assessment of the 20% TVA on the export sales, plus intérêts de retard (0.20% per month). If the deficiency is held to result from bad faith or abusive behaviour, a major penalty of 40% or 80% is applied under Article 1729 CGI, and fraudulent export schemes can lead to the 80% fraud penalty together with criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI, carrying a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and five years' imprisonment for legal representatives found personally responsible. In parallel, the customs administration may apply Code des Douanes penalties, including confiscation and fines equal to one to two times the value of the proceeds. The input VAT allocated to the zero-rated exports is correspondingly clawed back, and any artificial arrangements at the level of the export customer or intermediary may be recharacterised under abuse of law rights (abus de droit) and subjected to the same sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a Eurozone member and an IMF Article VIII participant, therefore the euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no blanket administrative approval requirement for cross-border payments. Profit and dividend remittances from a French SAS are operationally free after the legal corporate formalities, audited financial statements and tax clearances; the bank will only apply sanctions screening, AML/KYC checks, and the compulsory balance-of-payments statistical reporting. For non-EEA capital transactions, France retains the right to impose temporary safeguard measures or request statistical declarations on large transactions, and investment in sensitive defence/security sectors may require prior authorisation under the French foreign-investment screening regime, but these do not represent normal convertibility restrictions on dental consumables manufacturing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds and no forced conversion into euro. Exporters are free to maintain foreign-currency settlement accounts and retain export earnings. However, all French resident exporters and, in substance, their financing banks are subject to Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting: any cross-border payment or collection reaching or exceeding EUR 150,000 per transaction requires an individual declaration, while transactions between EUR 10,000 and EUR 150,000 are reported on an aggregated basis through the intermediary. The exporter must retain supporting documentation (export declaration, commercial invoice, transport documents, cash receipt evidence) for a minimum of five years; failure to furnish accurate declarations can give rise to administrative fines and delays in clearing inbound export remittances.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating as the national central bank within the Eurosystem and in conjunction with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking supervision and compliance; exchange-control and balance-of-payments reporting duties are exercised by Banque de France under the French Code monétaire et financier and EU treaty provisions on the free movement of capital.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium only where the electronic-money or payment institution is EEA-licensed under PSD2 and registered in France with ACPR or is a properly appointed agent of an ACPR-registered institution; in such cases a virtual IBAN and digital treasury dashboard can be used for collection of dental-lab receivables and supplier payments. Low for offshore, non-EEA EMIs or 'quick-IBAN' providers, as they cannot maintain a direct Banque de France relationship, cannot open true French current accounts, and are generally unwilling to support documentary trade or FX hedging. Therefore fintech EMIs are acceptable only as a supplementary receivables-collection rail and must never be the sole repository for the French SAS corporate treasury, capital escrow, or VAT/tax settlement amounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the French SAS must be executed by a priority SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 cover) with the French IBAN and the investing bank's BIC embedded. The payment must be accompanied by a purpose code corresponding to 'Investissement direct – capital et réserves' according to the Banque de France balance-of-payments nomenclature, and the receiving bank must hold the following verification batch: the constitutional corporate deeds of the SAS, the capital-subscription resolution, the French bank or notary deposit certificate, the origin-of-funds declaration, the personal identification of beneficial owners, and a tax-status statement confirming no inconsistent profit-shifting. Receipts greater than EUR 150,000 automatically generate a Banque de France BOP report, and wire clarity must be maintained by avoiding reference to general fiduciary accounts or crypto-linked channels. When the capital is received in a foreign currency, Banque de France permits credit to a euro-denominated account only after conversion by the receiving bank, which must retain the FX contract for audit trail.Outbound settlement from the French operational account should prioritise the following clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for T/T wires to non-SEPA destinations and for settlement against dental laboratory invoices; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer via TARGET2 for intra-EU payments, with no purpose code but structured creditor reference; (3) documentary commercial LC operations through SWIFT MT700/MT710/MT720 channels under UCP600 for substantial buyer-initiated imports or exports; (4) local clearing for high-volume low-value payments in markets where B2B customers require ACH/Paynet-style rails. Each disbursement must be tied to an underlying invoice, customs declaration, or freight document, and must pass in real time through the bank's sanctions and counterparty filter. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if annual turnover ≥ €50 million or if related-party transactions exceed €1 million; Master File required if group consolidated turnover ≥ €750 million.

Economic substance scope: France applies anti-abuse rules (e.g., substance-over-form, GAAR) and requires genuine economic activity for tax residency and treaty benefits. No separate ESR regime, but French tax authorities scrutinize shell companies and artificial arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: 40% penalty on understated tax (80% for fraud), plus 0.40% monthly interest; criminal prosecution possible with fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for corporate tax debts if they participated in fraudulent acts; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, denial of treaty benefits, 100% penalty on unreported income, and potential criminal charges for abuse of law.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unpaid taxes if they knowingly used the entity as a sham; travel bans and seizure of personal assets.
AML / Financial Fraud

Statutory sanction: Fines up to €1 million for companies, imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors can face criminal liability, including imprisonment and personal fines; travel bans and prohibition from managing companies.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty on additional tax, and interest; possible double taxation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant TP policies; personal fines and potential criminal prosecution.
