Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A SAS must have a registered office in France. A virtual address alone is not evidence of operational substance and may be inadequate for banking, tax, licensing, or project-contract requirements. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum paid-in capital is required for an SAS unless the articles provide otherwise; however, the entire share capital must be fully subscribed and, if paid in cash, deposited and blocked in escrow with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the company's registration is evidenced by the issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation (Kbis extract).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification must be obtained from the Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours (SDIS) for all temporary construction installations, welding and compressor facilities; structural load and trench-support inspections must be certified by a licensed engineering office in compliance with the Code du travail and the ICPE safety study; occupational health clearance from the Service de Prévention et de Santé au Travail and CARSAT is mandatory before deployment; a racking and storage load plan, confined-space rescue plan, and the pipeline automatic welding qualification record are also required as a condition of the physical operating license.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France does not operate a domestic check-the-box election. A French SAS is statutorily subject to corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, Article 206 CGI) with entity-level taxation. Available statutory elections include: (i) fiscal consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for groups holding ≥95% of shares; (ii) the 2025 reduced IS rate of 25% versus the standard 25% general rate, with the reduced rate retained for eligible gains; (iii) election under Article 239 quater B CGI to maintain the taxable quality of a conversion; and (iv) hybrid mismatch countermeasures under EU ATAD/Article 209 B CGI containing elective alignment provisions for cross-border loss grants. Substantial participation exemption may be elected under long-term capital gains rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Standard franchise en base exemption thresholds: EUR 85,000 annual turnover for sale of goods and EUR 37,500 for supply of services (2025/2026 fiscal thresholds). However, no registration threshold applies to an SAS undertaking cross-border gas pipeline works, intra-Community acquisitions, or services under Article 259 B CGI; VAT liability arises immediately from the first taxable transaction, irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating on exports is available under Article 262-1-1° CGI when (i) goods are physically transported from EU territory to a destination outside the EU, normally being dispatched within 30 days of supply, (ii) the exporter holds a definitive customs export declaration (ACC), and (iii) economic title and real goods-control pass to a non-EU purchaser. For services, the zero rate applies when services are directly and primarily related to exported goods and the export-eligibility evidence is held, or when services are performed outside France under Article 259-1 CGI (e.g., pipeline engineering, site welding, and commissioning works physically delivered abroad). A SAS performing zero-rated cross-country oil and gas pipeline services must maintain at export the pro-forma duty of transport, shipment, and final foreign landing documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

Mandatory e-invoicing under France’s Yréis reform for large and mid-size enterprises from 1 September 2026 and for all SMEs from 1 September 2027. All B2B invoices must be exchanged through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) using the Factur-X hybrid format, with near-real-time transmission of e-reporting data to the DGFiP. Construction and cross-border pipeline EPC contracts must transmit transactional data via the Chorus Pro public API or an authorized PDP gateway, including digital stamping of invoice sequence numbers; foreign suppliers without a French establishment must meet the e-reporting data flows for goods and services supplied into France.

The DGFiP audits export files under the Département de Vérification Nationale et Internationale. Failure to substantiate within the statutory 30-day export loss or by lacking legally required documents triggers re-invoicing of VAT at the standard 20% rate, plus monthly late interest assessed at 0.2% per month. Negligent inaccuracy attracts a 40% penalty (Article 1729 a CGI); intentional misreporting or falsified export evidence is subject to an 80% penalty (Article 1729 b CGI), a fine of up to EUR 750,000, and five years’ imprisonment for forgery or customs evasion, and retrospective clawback of any input tax credits previously claimed.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is an EU euro-area jurisdiction with substantially free current-account and capital-account convertibility. There is no general French requirement to obtain exchange-control approval before receiving foreign investment, paying suppliers abroad, repatriating dividends, servicing shareholder loans, or converting euros into foreign currency. Transfers remain subject to EU and French sanctions, anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, tax, customs, anti-corruption, foreign-subsidy, and sector-specific controls. A French SAS may remit dividends only from distributable profits after approval of annual accounts and allocation of reserves, subject to applicable withholding-tax rules, treaty relief, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive conditions, beneficial-ownership requirements, and anti-abuse rules. Interest, royalties, management fees, and intercompany payments require arm's-length support, appropriate tax documentation, transfer-pricing records, and withholding-tax analysis. Banks may delay or reject transactions lacking a clear commercial purpose, complete beneficial-owner evidence, source-of-funds evidence, sanctions screening, or documents supporting an oil-and-gas infrastructure project. Cross-border pipeline activity may additionally require export-control, dual-use, energy-sanctions, public-procurement, anti-bribery, and foreign-investment screening analysis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no universal deadline requiring conversion of foreign-currency export receipts into euros. Export receipts may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's discretion, subject to contractual, tax, accounting, sanctions, and bank-reporting requirements. The company must maintain accurate commercial invoices, customs evidence, transport and delivery records, customer contracts, and reconciliations between exports and receipts. Banque de France may require balance-of-payments or international-services data from relevant reporting entities, and banks may request transaction-purpose information under AML and payment-transparency rules. French residents must generally report foreign bank or payment accounts to the French tax administration with the annual income-tax return where the statutory reporting conditions apply. Physical cross-border movement of cash or bearer negotiable instruments of EUR 10,000 or more is subject to EU and French customs declaration rules. No general export-proceeds repatriation deadline applies, but contractual payment terms, tax-accounting cut-off rules, customs requirements, and sectoral sanctions must be observed.

Central bank supervision: European Central Bank for euro monetary policy and payment-system oversight; Banque de France for French banking supervision functions delegated by the European Central Bank, foreign-exchange statistics, balance-of-payments reporting, and certain payment-system matters; Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution for prudential supervision of French credit institutions and payment institutions; Direction Générale du Trésor for French financial and foreign-exchange regulatory policy; and Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects for cross-border cash and certain trade-control reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine EUR collections and payments, and Low to Medium for capital escrow, sovereign-project proceeds, large construction flows, foreign-currency liquidity, documentary trade finance, and project guarantees. An electronic-money institution or payment institution authorised in France or another EEA state may provide payment services under its licence and valid passporting or French establishment arrangements. Customer funds must be safeguarded and are not equivalent to insured bank deposits. An EMI generally cannot provide a bank deposit, regulated lending, capital escrow accepted by a notarial depositary, commercial-letter-of-credit issuance, or unrestricted derivatives dealing unless separately authorised or partnered with an appropriately licensed institution. Offshore non-EEA digital EMIs cannot rely on EU passporting and may require a French or EEA licensed entity, agent, branch, or correspondent arrangement. The SAS should use a French or EEA-regulated bank as primary treasury bank and treat an EMI as a controlled secondary rail after confirming licensing, safeguarding, sanctions screening, settlement finality, data protection, outsourcing, transaction limits, and account-closure procedures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route subscription capital and shareholder loans from an account held in the legal name of the investor or lender to the SAS's French bank account, using SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments or SWIFT cross-border transfer using the bank's current ISO 20022 payment format and, where still supported, MT103 messaging. For incorporation or a capital increase, deposit funds into a designated capital escrow account and obtain the depositary certificate before registration or filing of the capital increase. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for routine EUR collections and payments, and Low to Medium for capital escrow, sovereign-project proceeds, large construction flows, foreign-currency liquidity, documentary trade finance, and project guarantees. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) applicable from 2022; reduced rate of 15% on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit per annum for qualifying SMEs with turnover below EUR 10 million; the former 3.3% social surcharge on large companies has been abolished.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under Article 119 bis 2 of the French General Tax Code (CGI) on dividends distributed by a French SAS to non-resident corporate shareholders; no domestic withholding tax applies to qualifying EU parent companies under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive; treaty-reduced rates commonly range from 0% to 15%.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

EFI-registered filers benefit from a sliding deadline extending into late June depending on the professional online filing category.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million: mandatory Local File and Master File documentation under Article L.13 AA of the French Tax Procedures Code (LPF), triggered when annual net turnover or total assets reach EUR 400 million or when the entity is controlled by or controls a company reaching that threshold; below the threshold, the taxpayer must still be able to justify intercompany pricing upon request and bears the burden of proof after a formal rectification notice.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply the zero-tax-jurisdiction Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) model; economic substance is enforced through the general anti-abuse rule (abus de droit, Article L.64 LPF), the doctrine of abnormal acts of management (acte anormal de gestion), substance-over-form jurisprudence, and the BEPS MLI Principal Purpose Test. The French SAS executing cross-border oil and gas pipeline EPC work must maintain genuine operational substance — physical premises, technical staff, active management decisions in France and real execution capability for pipeline projects — to access treaty benefits and EU Directives; a paper-only conduit is recharacterized, treaty benefits are denied, and fraud penalties of up to 80% apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under Article 57 CGI with automatic application of the minimum 5% margin on the adjusted amount; 40% bad-faith penalty or 80% fraudulent-manoeuvre penalty under Article 1729 CGI; late-payment interest at 0.20% per month; criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Article 1741 CGI with a fine of up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals, increasing to EUR 3 million and 7 years in aggravated cases; corporate fines are multiplied by five.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts when fraud or bad-faith management caused the evasion; personal criminal conviction carries imprisonment, asset freezes, travel bans (interdiction de territoire), disqualification from managing French companies, and the corporate veil is pierced for abuse of corporate assets (abus de bien social) or patrimonial confusion.
Shell Company / Abusive Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the scheme under the abuse-of-law procedure (Article L.64 LPF); the tax administration disregards the sham entity and taxes the real beneficial owner; 80% penalty for abusive maneuvers under Article 1729 CGI; denial of treaty and Directive benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; additional wage and social security adjustments for fictitious employment.

Director personal liability: Managers risk civil liability under Article 1857 of the Civil Code for corporate debts upon insolvency, personal criminal prosecution for fraudulent misrepresentation, judicial travel bans, confiscation orders, and disqualification from holding directorship positions; where the shell is used to conceal ownership, the directors are treated as the true taxpayers.
Money Laundering and Corruption in Pipeline Contracts

Statutory sanction: Corporate criminal fine for corruption of up to EUR 1 million per offence, multiplied by five for legal persons; administrative AML sanctions for regulated entities up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual turnover; asset confiscation; debarment from public contracts; compulsory compliance orders issued by the French Anti-Corruption Agency (AFA).

Director personal liability: Directors personally face up to 10 years' imprisonment and fines up to EUR 1 million for corruption and influence peddling; judicial travel bans and freezing orders are routinely imposed; the corporate shield is lifted for laundering of corruption proceeds, and directors are exposed to civil restitution for resulting damage to the company and third-party creditors.
