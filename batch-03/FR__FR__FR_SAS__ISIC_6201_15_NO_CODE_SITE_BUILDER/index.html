Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry) of the department of the registered office; electronic filing routed exclusively through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises operated by INPI (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce): Articles L.227-1 to L.227-29 (Société par Actions Simplifiée), R.123-1 et seq. (Registration with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés), L.210-2 (Legal announcement), and L.561-46 (Beneficial ownership declaration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts (Articles of Association) filed as part of the single electronic M0 dossier, accompanied by the Déclaration des Bénéficiaires Effectifs (DBE), proof of capital deposit, and the legal-publication certificate.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS (Art. L.227-2 Code de commerce). The Articles set EUR 1,000 (1,000 shares of EUR 1.00). For capital below EUR 50,000, cash contributions must be fully paid up on subscription; funds must be blocked in a French escrow bank account (compte bloqué) or held by a notary, and released only upon presentation of the K-bis registration extract.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises must comply with ERP 5th-category (Type W) fire-safety provisions under Code de la construction et de l'habitation, Art. R.123-1 et seq.; periodic fire-extinguisher and emergency-exit inspections; electrical installation verification by a qualified body; workplace health, air quality, and sanitation per Code du travail, Art. L.4222-1 to L.4222-6; CARSAT prevention visit for office workplaces.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for an SAS (Art. L.227-2 Code de commerce). The Articles set EUR 1,000 (1,000 shares of EUR 1.00). For capital below EUR 50,000, cash contributions must be fully paid up on subscription; funds must be blocked in a French escrow bank account (compte bloqué) or held by a notary, and released only upon presentation of the K-bis registration extract.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Relance; primary portals: impots.gouv.fr (taxpayer account, VAT returns, e-procedures), formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (single business registration gateway), and the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) portal operated through impots.gouv.fr for non-Union and Union schemes.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident entity: (1) verify whether a permanent establishment or French establishment exists; if not, appoint a tax representative for VAT (Art. 289 A CGI) unless the OSS non-Union scheme is used; (2) submit Form 1477-SD to the local SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) to obtain a French intra-Community VAT number (FR + SIREN) and, where necessary, a SIRET registration via the formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr portal; (3) for B2C electronic services to French consumers, elect the non-Union OSS scheme on the digital OSS portal to avoid separate French VAT registration; (4) once registered, file monthly or quarterly CA3/CA12 returns online and appoint a direct-debit account in France. For a French SAS: incorporation filing via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr generates automatic INSEE registration (SIREN/SIRET), a K-bis extract, and the intra-Community VAT number; no separate DGFiP enrollment is required beyond SIE assignment.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SAS is automatically subject to impôt sur les sociétés (IS, corporate income tax) on its worldwide profits (Art. 209 CGI), with territoriality only applicable to non-resident enterprises taxed on French-source income. Standard IS rate is 25%; eligible small companies with turnover ≤ €10 million, fully paid-up capital, and ≥75% individual ownership may apply a 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit. No pass-through treatment applies by default; the SAS is a separate legal taxpayer. — Non-resident entity: (1) verify whether a permanent establishment or French establishment exists; if not, appoint a tax representative for VAT (Art. 289 A CGI) unless the OSS non-Union scheme is used; (2) submit Form 1477-SD to the local SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) to obtain a French intra-Community VAT number (FR + SIREN) and, where necessary, a SIRET registration via the formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr portal; (3) for B2C electronic services to French consumers, elect the non-Union OSS scheme on the digital OSS portal to avoid separate French VAT registration; (4) once registered, file monthly or quarterly CA3/CA12 returns online and appoint a direct-debit account in France. For a French SAS: incorporation filing via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr generates automatic INSEE registration (SIREN/SIRET), a K-bis extract, and the intra-Community VAT number; no separate DGFiP enrollment is required beyond SIE assignment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — a French SAS is automatically subject to impôt sur les sociétés (IS, corporate income tax) on its worldwide profits (Art. 209 CGI), with territoriality only applicable to non-resident enterprises taxed on French-source income. Standard IS rate is 25%; eligible small companies with turnover ≤ €10 million, fully paid-up capital, and ≥75% individual ownership may apply a 15% reduced rate on the first €42,500 of taxable profit. No pass-through treatment applies by default; the SAS is a separate legal taxpayer.

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box classification regime; entity status is fixed by commercial law and EU anti-hybrid rules (ATAD, Arts. 212 bis and 212 bis-2 CGI). Available elections: (1) fiscal integration (régime d'intégration fiscale) under Art. 223 A CGI for a 95%-owned French group, allowing one tax payer; (2) option under Art. 239 bis AB CGI for qualifying small SAS/SARL owned by natural persons to be taxed as pass-through at income tax (IR) for a renewable 5-year period, subject to activity and ownership conditions (commercial/digital activities may qualify); (3) election for simplified real-regime VAT under the franchise en base thresholds; (4) option for prepayment/mensualized VAT payment; (5) group OSS registration for intra-Community distance sales. Hybrid mismatch elections are limited to electing application of anti-abuse safe harbors; no pure flow-through check-the-box is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For French-established businesses, the franchise en base exemption applies up to €91,900 (goods) / €36,800 (services) for 2024, with tolerance up to €101,000 / €39,100; above these turnover thresholds, compulsory VAT registration applies. For non-established foreign suppliers of digital services, no registration threshold exists — immediate French VAT (or non-Union OSS election) is required on B2C supplies. For EU-established distance sellers of goods, an EU-wide €10,000 annual cross-border distance sales threshold applies for use of the Union OSS, after which French VAT applies. A no-code website builder (ISIC 6201, digital services) in France is above the €36,800 service threshold at any meaningful scale and must register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU are zero-rated under Art. 262 CGI, provided the exporter proves physical dispatch and the goods leave EU customs territory within the statutory deadline; a Déclaration d'Exportation (DE) with customs exit confirmation is required. Intra-EU supplies of goods are exempt with credit under Art. 262 ter CGI only if the customer communicates a valid EU VAT number, the goods are dispatched to the EU buyer, and the recapitulative statement (état récapitulatif) is filed. For services: B2B supplies where the customer is established outside France are taxed in the customer's country under the general place-of-supply rule (Art. 259-1 CGI), and French VAT is not chargeable if the customer provides a foreign VAT registration; B2C supplies of digital services (no-code website building) to consumers outside the EU are outside French VAT scope under Art. 259 A CGI, while B2C digital supplies to EU consumers (non-established in France) fall under OSS. Zero-rating/export substantiation requires contemporaneous documentary evidence of foreign receipt and actual cross-border movement or offshore service performance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation filed via DELTA-X / DES with customs exit certification)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport evidence (for goods exports)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT / bank credit confirmation showing payment from foreign purchaser)
  • Commercial invoice stating 'Exportation TVA non applicable — Art. 262 CGI' or 'Reverse charge — Art. 283 CGI'
  • Contract or purchase order identifying the foreign customer, service description, and place of establishment
  • Proof of delivery or electronic service delivery log (IP address, server location, service access records)
  • Intra-EU supplier's recapitulative statement (état récapitulatif, ESA) and the customer's validated EU VAT number
  • Certificate of origin (EUR.1 or REX) where customs preference is claimed

France mandates e-invoicing and e-payment data reporting (e-reporting). Under the 2024 Finance Act timetable, mandatory e-invoicing in B2B and mandatory transmission of B2C transaction/payment data to DGFiP applies from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and mid-size companies, and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro enterprises (all VAT-registered businesses). Invoices must be issued and transmitted through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) in structured formats: Factur-X (hybrid), UBL 2.1, or CII (Cross-Industry Invoice). Transmission must be near-real-time, generally within one business day, to enable DGFiP continuous transaction controls and tax clearance. France does not use physical digital fiscal stamps or fiscally sealed cash registers; the clearance is digital-timestamped through the PPF/PDP network with a unique invoice identity, archiving, and audit trail.

The DGFiP rigorously audits export zero-rating claims and can claw back all underpaid VAT where documentary proof is missing or falsified. Civil penalties under Art. 1729 CGI: 40% increase for bad faith (mauvaise foi) and 80% increase for fraudulent maneuvers or abusive schemes. Late-payment interest under Art. 1727 CGI accrues at 0.20% per month from the due date. Missing or fraudulent export documentation also triggers penalties under Arts. 1731 bis and 1788 bis CGI (per-document fines). Criminal tax fraud under Art. 1741 CGI is punishable by up to €500,000 fine and five years imprisonment, plus publication of the judgment; directors of an SAS can be held personally liable. The DGFiP's three-year reprise (assessment window) extends to six years for non-declared or disguised activity and ten years for offshore concealment or serious tax evasion. Refunds of falsely zero-rated VAT are rejected outright and may trigger a full comprehensive audit (vérification de comptabilité) with extended audit powers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under the European Union's free movement of capital and current account, with full convertibility for EUR. No foreign exchange controls, withholding or authorization are applied to current account payments (dividends, royalties, service fees) for non-residents, provided underlying tax compliance (e.g., pre-clearance for anti-abuse checks) is satisfied. Capital account operations (equity injection, loans) are unrestricted within the EU and require administrative declaration to the Banque de France for statistical purposes only when the amount exceeds €15,000 per month. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal, typically requiring proof of distributable reserves and adherence to anti-money laundering (AML) source-of-funds verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements. Exporters may retain accounts in foreign currency, but must report export proceeds to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes if the transaction value exceeds €12,500 on a single operation or is the subject of a prior declaration. No mandatory conversion ratio into EUR applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) operating under the regulatory supervision of the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), and in coordination with the European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary and foreign exchange policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. EU-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) holding an ACPR or other EU competent-authority passport can legally offer the SAS cross-border collection and payment accounts. Non-EU offshore EMIs (e.g., UK, US, Singapore) have limited viability because they cannot provide a French IBAN or access SEPA clearing rails without establishing a EU branch or relying on a correspondent partner bank, and may introduce additional AML/KYC friction. Legal constraints under the ACPR and French Monetary and Financial Code (Code Monétaire et Financier) require EMIs to be authorised and within the EU/EEA for unrestricted single-euro payments area (SEPA) participation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax. A 3.3% social contribution on corporate income tax may apply to companies whose corporate income tax liability exceeds EUR 763,000 and which do not qualify for an exemption, producing an effective rate of approximately 25.83%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French SAS to a non-resident shareholder, subject to exemptions, European Union rules, tax treaties, and anti-abuse provisions. Dividends paid to an eligible EU or EEA parent may qualify for a domestic participation exemption where the statutory ownership, holding-period, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. A qualifying parent generally must hold at least 10% of the distributing company's capital, directly or through an eligible arrangement, and satisfy the required holding-period conditions or provide the required commitment.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, filing is generally due by the second business day following 1 May of the following year. For other year-ends, filing is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. The electronic deadline may be subject to the administrative filing calendar and applicable extensions. Corporate income tax installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end, or by 15 May for a 31 December year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million of annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets, or membership in a group meeting the statutory threshold, generally triggers full transfer-pricing documentation requirements, including Local File and Master File content. A transfer-pricing declaration on Form 2257-SD generally applies from EUR 50 million of annual turnover or gross assets, or where the taxpayer belongs to a group meeting the statutory conditions. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France has no standalone economic-substance regime identical to the United Arab Emirates ESR regime. Substance is nevertheless material under French anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership analysis, treaty entitlement, permanent-establishment principles, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules, and the general anti-abuse doctrine. The SAS should maintain French decision-making, personnel or outsourced operational capability, premises or operational infrastructure where commercially required, functional control over software and customer activities, bank-account control, board records, contracts, and evidence of actual services. A conduit entity with no genuine functions, risks, assets, or decision-making may lose treaty benefits and deductions and may create tax reassessment, permanent-establishment, or withholding exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Ordinary tax fraud may carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, together with tax reassessment, interest, penalties, and potential corporate fines. Aggravated conduct, including organized schemes or substantial offshore concealment, may carry up to seven years of imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, civil tax liability under statutory management-liability rules, personal contribution claims, asset recovery, professional disqualification, and in serious cases travel restrictions or pre-trial detention. Corporate veil protection does not shield intentional personal participation in fraud.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit diversion

Statutory sanction: The French tax administration may reassess taxable profit, deny deductions, impose withholding tax, interest, and penalties, apply the transfer-pricing documentation penalty, and recharacterize transactions under anti-abuse principles.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal exposure where they knowingly approve fictitious or abusive transactions, conceal records, or organize insolvency to defeat tax recovery. Civil liability may arise for mismanagement and criminal liability may arise if the conduct constitutes fraud or falsification.
Permanent-establishment concealment

Statutory sanction: The administration may assert a French permanent establishment, allocate profits to France, reassess corporate income tax and payroll-related liabilities, impose withholding obligations, interest, and penalties, and challenge treaty protection.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for intentional concealment, false filings, or failure to maintain statutory records. Criminal liability generally requires an additional fraudulent act rather than mere technical disagreement over permanent-establishment characterization.
Illegal dividend or disguised distribution

Statutory sanction: Consequences may include repayment of unlawful distributions, corporate-law sanctions, denial of deductions, dividend recharacterization, withholding tax, tax penalties, and potential insolvency or misuse-of-corporate-assets proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors approving unlawful distributions may owe restitution, damages, and insolvency contributions and may face criminal sanctions for misuse of corporate assets or presentation of misleading accounts. Travel restrictions or imprisonment depend on the separate criminal offense and judicial order.
Money laundering and proceeds concealment

Statutory sanction: Money laundering may carry up to five years of imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 500,000; aggravated money laundering may carry up to ten years of imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 750,000, subject to statutory conditions. Assets may be frozen or confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who knowingly participate may face criminal prosecution, confiscation, asset freezing, professional bans, civil restitution, and judicial travel restrictions. The SAS may incur corporate criminal fines and confiscation.
Anti-money-laundering and beneficial-ownership failure

Statutory sanction: Sanctions may include administrative fines, injunctions, account or business restrictions, reporting failures, and criminal consequences where the conduct facilitates laundering, fraud, or false declarations. The applicable regime depends on whether the SAS is an obligated entity under French AML legislation.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives may face personal fines or criminal liability for knowingly false beneficial-owner declarations or intentional participation in laundering. Civil liability, director disqualification, and asset recovery may follow.
VAT fraud and payment-platform concealment

Statutory sanction: Consequences include VAT assessment, late-payment interest, surcharges commonly ranging from 10% to 80% depending on conduct, denial of input VAT, joint-liability measures in certain cases, and criminal fraud sanctions for deliberate evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for deliberate fraudulent VAT conduct, false records, or insolvency-related misconduct. Corporate limitation does not protect intentional personal criminal conduct.
False accounting and document destruction

Statutory sanction: The company may face accounting-law sanctions, tax reassessment, evidentiary presumptions against it, fines, and criminal penalties where false accounts or destruction of evidence forms part of fraud, bankruptcy, or money-laundering conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors, accountants, and de facto managers may face imprisonment, fines, director disqualification, civil damages, restitution, and liability for insolvency shortfalls. Judicial travel restrictions may be imposed where legally justified.
Bribery, corruption, and improper commercial payments

Statutory sanction: French corruption offenses may carry substantial imprisonment and fines, corporate criminal liability, confiscation, exclusion from public procurement, and tax denial of illicit payments. Exact sanctions depend on public or private bribery, domestic or foreign conduct, and aggravating circumstances.

Director personal liability: Directors and employees may be personally prosecuted, fined, imprisoned, disqualified, and subject to confiscation and civil restitution. Corporate limited liability does not protect a knowing participant.
Sanctions and export-control breach

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include criminal fines, imprisonment, asset freezes, confiscation, payment blocking, administrative enforcement, and loss of authorization, depending on the applicable EU and French sanctions regime.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally prosecuted for intentional or grossly negligent violations. Civil damages, confiscation, travel restrictions, and imprisonment may apply where the statutory offense is established.
