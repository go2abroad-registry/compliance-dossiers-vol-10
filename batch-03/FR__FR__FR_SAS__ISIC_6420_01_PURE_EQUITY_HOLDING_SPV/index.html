Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) operated by INPI (Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle) through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); local documentary control exercised by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the competent arrondissement.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II: Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (formation and legal personality), L.123-1 et seq. (Registre National des Entreprises) and L.227-1 to L.227-30 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); Code monétaire et financier Article L.561-45 (register of beneficial owners).

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration d'immatriculation (Formulaire M0) filed electronically via the Guichet Unique, accompanied by signed statuts, capital blockage certificate, legal announcement proof, and identity documents, resulting in inscription at the RNE/RCS and issuance of the extrait Kbis.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 16 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legal minimum share capital for an SAS (may be as low as EUR 1); the capital fixed in the statuts must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked funds account with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations before registration and are released only upon issuance of the Kbis extract.

Funding & unlock path: Not required for a passive holding SPV. If commercial premises are occupied, the following may apply: fire safety conformity attestation (ERP/IGH rules), electrical and structural safety certificates, waste collection contracts, and workplace health-and-safety compliance checks by DREETS.

Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked funds account with a French credit institution or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations before registration and are released only upon issuance of the Kbis extract.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for a pure equity holding SPV; no industrial licensing board competence is engaged. If the company later acquires operational assets, the residual competent authorities would be the DREETS (labor and company inspections) and the Préfecture through DREAL (environmental matters).

Category: No operating permit required: the passive holding of subsidiary shares and receipt of corporate dividends (ISIC 6420 / NAICS 551112) is exempt from French sectoral licensing.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP); online portal: impots.gouv.fr; local operational service for enterprises: Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE); central e-invoicing/e-reporting platform: Chorus Pro.

E-government filing portal

For a French-established SAS, incorporation is completed through the Guichet unique (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés), which triggers allocation of a SIREN/SIRET identification number by INSEE; the SIREN is the statutory corporate tax identification number, and the intra-Community VAT number is subsequently assigned by DGFiP. For a foreign entity not established in France, registration for French VAT requires a specific procedure: an EU-established taxable person must file Form 14264 (Cerfa n° 14264*02) with the competent SIE, provide proof of identity and foreign tax residence, and may use the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for distance sales or pan-EU services; a non-EU established taxable person must additionally appoint a tax representative established in France and approved by DGFiP, file Form 14264, and submit the foreign registration certificate, constitutional documents, and certified powers of attorney.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French-established SAS, incorporation is completed through the Guichet unique (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés), which triggers allocation of a SIREN/SIRET identification number by INSEE; the SIREN is the statutory corporate tax identification number, and the intra-Community VAT number is subsequently assigned by DGFiP. For a foreign entity not established in France, registration for French VAT requires a specific procedure: an EU-established taxable person must file Form 14264 (Cerfa n° 14264*02) with the competent SIE, provide proof of identity and foreign tax residence, and may use the EU One-Stop-Shop (OSS) for distance sales or pan-EU services; a non-EU established taxable person must additionally appoint a tax representative established in France and approved by DGFiP, file Form 14264, and submit the foreign registration certificate, constitutional documents, and certified powers of attorney.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) is by statutory design subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at entity level; it cannot elect pass-through treatment. No US-style check-the-box election exists under French domestic law. Elective mechanisms available to an SAS include: election into French tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under CGI Article 223 A if held at least 95% by a French parent; election for VAT grouping (assujetti unique) under CGI Article 256 C quater; and the automatic parent-subsidiary regime for 95% exempt intercompany dividends. Hybrid mismatch adjustments are mandatory and non-elective as transposed under EU ATAD, without taxpayers being able to choose fiscal transparency or opaque treatment against the French statutory classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration turnover threshold applies to foreign taxable persons carrying on taxable supplies in France; registration is compulsory irrespective of turnover. For established micro-businesses, the French registration exemption and VAT franchise thresholds are EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and EUR 37,500 for supplies of services (2025 thresholds, subject to franchise en base conditions). The pre-2021 intra-EU distance-sales threshold for France was EUR 35,000 but has been repealed since 1 July 2021; distance sales are now taxable at destination and may be reported through the Union One-Stop-Shop without separate French VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under CGI Article 262-I, exports of goods dispatched or transported outside the EU are exempt and effectively zero-rated upon submission of proof of export; the seller must hold a valid export declaration (DAU) and cover transport documentation proving the goods left France or the EU within the statutory timeframes. Intra-Community supplies to a taxable person with a valid EU VAT number are exempt under CGI Article 262 ter, subject to the buyer communicating a valid VAT identification number and to the seller keeping an EC sales list. Services exported to non-EU B2B customers fall outside French VAT where the place of supply is deemed to be outside the EU under CGI Articles 259 and 259 A/B. For a pure equity holding SPV, dividends and share-sale proceeds are not export transactions for VAT purposes, but if the SPV incurs upstream or downstream supplies, the standard export substantiation rules apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / CAA Electronique) lodged through the French customs electronic clearance system
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (CMR, air waybill, rail consignment note, postal receipt)
  • Commercial invoice issued by the French supplier
  • Proof of payment and inward remittance certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds on French territory
  • Buyer's EU VAT identification number and EC sales list for exempt intra-Community supplies

France mandates structured electronic invoicing under the 2026-2027 reform. Large enterprises and intermediate-sized enterprises (ETI) must issue and receive e-invoices from 1 September 2026, while small and medium enterprises (PME/TPE) must comply from 1 September 2027. Suppliers must transmit standardized invoice data in compliance with Factur-X, UBL, or CII formats to Chorus Pro or a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP). B2B e-invoices require digital signature, certification, and near-real-time transmission for VAT audit. France does not currently operate a continuous transaction control model, but the reform is based on a clearance/e-reporting architecture requiring structured VAT data transmission to the fiscal authorities, with penalties for non-compliant issuance.

Export zero-rating is heavily audited by DGFiP and French customs. Where the exporter cannot produce satisfactory export documentation, the VAT originally zero-rated is clawed back, together with reversal of any input VAT credited to the exporter. Fraudulent claims may trigger penalties of 40% for bad-faith understatements and 80% for manoeuvres frauduleuses or abus de droit, plus late-payment interest of 0.20% per month and, under CGI Article 1741, criminal exposure for tax fraud, including imprisonment and fines. The supposed exporter may also be held jointly and severally liable for customs and VAT debts if transports or repatriation undertakings have been made without lawful substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a full capital-account convertibility regime for the euro and all freely convertible currencies, in force since the 1990 'liberté des changes' liberalisation and consistent with EU capital movement rules. There is no exchange-control authorization, no prior licensing, and no profit-remittance approval required for an SAS to distribute dividends or repay shareholder advances, provided corporate law solvency tests (legal reserve of 5% of share capital, net assets above total capital) and the 30% withholding or treaty-reduced mechanism are respected. Practical constraints exist only for counterparties resident in FATF non-cooperative jurisdictions, triggering reinforced due diligence under Article L561-10 of the French Monetary and Financial Code (CMF) and potential bank refusal of the MT103 clearing channel. Outbound direct investments from France are free except for the post-transaction statistical declarations to BdF and, where the SPV invests into a strategic-sector French target in the future, prior authorisation under French FDI screening rules (Decree 2019-1590).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to a pure-equity holding SPV because the SAS has no trading or export turnover. As a general French rule, there is no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts: the surrender obligation was abolished in 1990 (CMF Art. L221-2). Exporters may keep non-euro proceeds indefinitely in approved foreign-currency accounts, provided the resident corporate entity reports each cross-border receipt and disbursement equal to or exceeding EUR 15,000 to Banque de France's balance-of-payments statistical department, either directly or through the banking intermediary, using the standard 'movements de fonds' declaration.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) is the national central bank; the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) governs banking licensing and AML conduct; the Direction Générale du Trésor (DGT) within the Ministry for the Economy and Finance administers foreign-exchange regulatory policy and foreign-investment screening.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary payment sub-accounts, Low to Medium as primary treasury rail. ACPR-licensed or EU-passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine, and Revolut Business may lawfully open payment accounts, execute SEPA and SWIFT transfers, and offer simple FX conversion under PSD2; however, EMIs cannot hold client funds beyond one business day, cannot grant credit or intraday liquidity, and are not permitted to act as custodian for the proceeds of capital subscriptions or dividend distributions where the SPV itself is the account counterparty. The legal boundary is set by CMF Articles L522-1 et seq. Consequently, a licensed credit institution remains mandatory for primary treasury purposes; an EMI may serve only as a disbursement sub-account for low-value operating expenses, and its use will nonetheless trigger enhanced due diligence under CMF Article D561-4-1.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: for intra-EEA contributions use SEPA Credit Transfer; for non-EEA and non-euro injections use SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary account title exactly matching the SAS registered name and RIB, and with field 72 completed with the purpose descriptor 'Apport en capital – Souscription d'actions' and the French balance-of-payments statistical code 22000 ('Direct investment – Equity capital'). The receiving bank is legally obliged to report to Banque de France any cross-border movement of EUR 15,000 or above (or aggregated daily flows exceeding that threshold) under the balance-of-payments declaration regime; the SPV must simultaneously file a notarial capital subscription statement and update the KRS/RBE within one month. For inbound injections over EUR 150,000, banks will additionally require written confirmation of the corporate interest, the passport of the ultimate investor, and an opinion of counsel on the tax classification (equity vs debt) to prevent disguised financing. Verification is completed by cross-matching three elements: the SWIFT GPI tracking receipt, the MT103 value-date confirmation, and the notary's certificate confirming completion of the capital increase.Primary rails: (1) SEPA Credit Transfer for euro disbursements to EEA beneficiaries, including instant SEPA Credit Transfer for amounts up to EUR 100,000 per transaction; (2) SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA or non-euro payments, with MT202/MT202COV used when the SPV clears through a correspondent bank to confer transparency on the beneficiary; (3) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710, UCP600 rules) where the equity holding finances an underlying trading subsidiary that requires bank-confirmed settlement; (4) SEPA Direct Debit or TIPI filings for statutory payments to the French tax authority (DGFiP). Outbound dividend distributions or shareholder loan repayments must carry the tax reference (form 2561 or treaty certificate) and the payment code in the instruction message, and the SPV must maintain a full format log to evidence the economic rationale under transfer-pricing and anti-abuse documentation.Outright FX Forward (marché de change à terme) up to 18 months on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/JPY, traded OTC with a licensed French bank under a signed ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or French FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) Master Agreement.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) for restricted or non-deliverable currencies such as offshore CNY (EUR/CNH) or RUB, used only when the SPV receives economic exposure from a foreign subsidiary and cannot settle in that currency onshore.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (XCCY) for the re-denomination of intra-group shareholder loans from EUR to USD, including fixed-rate to floating-rate legs where appropriate.FX Swap (swap de change) for temporary liquidity bridging before a capital increase is completed, with maturity not exceeding 90 days to avoid reclassification as a financial transaction.Vanilla European FX Options (call/put) under the same ISDA/FBF frame, purchased or sold only when the SPV can evidence a real pre-existing exposure through audited subsidiary dividend forecasts or acquisition commitments; all trades are subject to EMIR transaction reporting, and clearing thresholds are assessed annually.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% on French-source dividends paid to non-residents, subject to domestic exemptions, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, applicable tax treaties and anti-abuse rules. Qualifying EU or EEA corporate shareholders may obtain an exemption where statutory ownership, subject-to-tax, holding-period and anti-abuse requirements are satisfied.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the fiscal year. For a fiscal year ending on 31 December, the normal deadline is the second business day following 1 May of the following year. Electronic filing and payment are mandatory for companies subject to corporate income tax.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally filed with the corporate income tax return or by the applicable annual deadline prescribed for the relevant form.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million of annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets, or membership in or control of an enterprise meeting the statutory threshold, generally triggers full French transfer-pricing documentation obligations, including a Local File and Master File. A separate annual transfer-pricing statement may apply from EUR 50 million of annual turnover or gross assets or equivalent group-status conditions. Material controlled transactions must be documented even below these thresholds under the arm's-length principle.

Economic substance scope: France has no single comprehensive ESR regime equivalent to certain offshore substance regimes. Substance is nevertheless assessed through the general anti-abuse rule, principal-purpose and treaty anti-abuse standards, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled foreign company rules, interest-limitation rules, transfer-pricing rules, anti-hybrid rules and the abuse-of-law doctrine. A pure equity holding SAS should maintain French decision-making, appropriate directors, documented board deliberations, a French registered office, accounting and tax records, bank-account controls, evidence of investment monitoring and commercially credible functions. A conduit, nominee arrangement or entity lacking genuine control over dividends, financing or investments may lose treaty and participation-exemption benefits.
