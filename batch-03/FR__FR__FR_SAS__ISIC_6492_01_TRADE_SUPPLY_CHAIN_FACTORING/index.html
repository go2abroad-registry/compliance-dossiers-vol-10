Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The articles determine the capital amount and payment terms. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des finances publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; online tax portal: www.impots.gouv.fr; business formalities portal: www.formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; INSEE assigns SIREN/SIRET.

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities register with the guichet unique des formalités d'entreprises (INPI) via formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Upon incorporation or registration, INSEE assigns a SIREN (9 digits) and SIRET (14 digits). The VAT identification number is issued as FR + 2-digit key + SIREN. Non-resident entities not established in France may register directly with the DGFiP for French VAT if they carry out taxable transactions in France or are required for intra-EU acquisitions; a fiscal representative is generally mandatory for non-EU established taxable persons unless the reverse-charge mechanism applies. A French SAS must file its statutes, beneficial owner declarations, and identity documents through the guichet unique. Registration is complete upon receipt of SIREN/SIRET and VAT number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available. A French SAS is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the standard rate of 25% (plus applicable social contributions). Available statutory elections include: (i) tax consolidation regime (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI, subject to 95% ownership conditions; (ii) VAT group (assujettissement unique) under Article 256 C CGI; (iii) option to tax otherwise exempt financial services; (iv) no hybrid or transparent entity election for a SAS. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are implemented in Articles 205 A and following CGI, with linkage and deduction denial rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT exemption threshold (franchise en base de TVA): EUR 85,000 for supplies of goods and related activities; EUR 37,500 for other services, including most taxable factoring, debt collection, and management services. For intra-EU distance sales and certain cross-border B2C services, the EU-wide threshold is EUR 10,000. Factoring and financial services may be VAT-exempt under Article 261 C CGI where they constitute negotiation or collection of receivables, but debt collection and outsourced management are generally taxable at 20% unless the place of supply is outside France under Article 259 B CGI.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 262-I CGI, exports of goods outside the EU are exempt (zero-rated) if the exporter provides proof of export. Intra-EU supplies are zero-rated under Article 262-I when the customer is a VAT-registered business and the goods are transported to another Member State. For services, exemption or zero-rating applies under Articles 259, 259 A, and 262-I CGI where the service is supplied to a non-resident or is directly related to an export transaction. Factoring of export receivables may be exempt as a financial service under Article 261 C CGI if it constitutes negotiation or collection of receivables; otherwise it is taxable at 20% unless the place of supply is outside France under Article 259 B CGI.

France mandates B2B domestic e-invoicing and e-reporting under the 2024 Finance Act. From 1 September 2026, all VAT-registered businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; large and mid-sized companies must issue them. From 1 September 2027, SMEs and micro-enterprises must issue electronic invoices. Invoices and reports are transmitted through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a certified private platform (Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire - PDP). The model combines structured invoice transmission, lifecycle status management, and periodic e-reporting for B2C and international transactions; it is not a pure real-time clearance regime. Chorus Pro remains mandatory for B2G invoices. API interoperability and data specifications are prescribed by the DGFiP.

French VAT audits (contrôle fiscal) may retrospectively reassess zero-rated exports for up to 3 years, or 10 years in cases of fraud or hidden activity. Penalties include: 5% late payment interest; 10% surcharge for late filing; 40% penalty for failure of proof or bad faith; 80% penalty for aggravated fraud under Article 1729 CGI. Criminal penalties under Article 1741 CGI: up to 5 years imprisonment and EUR 500,000 fine; aggravated organized fraud: up to 10 years and EUR 2,000,000 fine. Customs penalties under the Code des douanes apply for false export declarations. Joint and several liability may attach to fiscal representatives and directors. Clawback of refunded VAT plus interest and costs.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full convertibility for both capital and current accounts under EU law and IMF Article VIII obligations. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders face no statutory barriers but require compliance with AML directives, beneficial ownership registers, and CRS reporting for cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds at official rates. All export transactions above €10,000 require customs declaration via Douane. Banks financing such transactions demand supporting documents including commercial invoices and transport documents; repatriation must occur promptly to support Banque de France balance-of-payments statistics.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

EMI authorization under PSD2 and potential MiCA framework permits limited cross-border e-money services, yet integration with licensed banking rails remains mandatory for trade factoring due to capital adequacy and AML requirements. Legal constraints include prohibition on unlicensed payment initiation and strict monitoring obligations.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (plus 3.3% social surcharge for companies with taxable profits exceeding €2,290,000, producing an effective headline rate of approximately 25.83%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% under CGI Article 119 bis-2 for dividends or distribution of profits paid to non-resident shareholders in the absence of a treaty or EU relief; reduced to 0% for EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive qualifying shareholders (minimum 5% holding for two years) and to 0%–15% under French double tax treaties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Full transfer pricing documentation (Master File, Local File, Country-by-Country Report) is mandatory when the French entity or the worldwide multinational group generates consolidated annual revenue or total assets equal to or exceeding EUR 400,000,000; entities qualifying as SMEs (headcount under 250 and annual turnover not exceeding EUR 50,000,000) benefit from a general exemption, and all taxpayers may still be required to file Form 2257-SD when specifically requested.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime, but requires genuine economic substance through the abuse-of-law (abus de droit) doctrine, the anti-fraud procedural rules of the LPF, and French CFC legislation; a French SAS engaged in trade factoring or reverse factoring must prove real local management, staff, IT infrastructure, contractual conclusion and risk-bearing in France, and the legal and factual substance of each structured supply-chain finance arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed Factoring / Illicit Banking Operations

Statutory sanction: Civil nullity of the factoring agreements; administrative injunction by the ACPR; criminal fine up to EUR 375,000 for individuals and EUR 1,875,000 for legal persons, with imprisonment up to three years.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may be declared individually liable for the civil restitution of all monies advanced, be subject to a prohibition from managing a business for up to ten years, and may face travel bans issued by the commercial court in insolvency proceedings.
Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Declarations

Statutory sanction: 40% to 80% administrative penalty on the rectified tax pursuant to Article 1728/1729 of the CGI, plus late-payment interest; criminal conviction can lead to a fine of EUR 500,000 (up to EUR 2,500,000 for legal persons) and five years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false declarations or dispose of assets to defeat tax collection become jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt; criminal courts may impose a travelling restriction ban (interdiction du territoire) for fraud offences and an exclusion from corporate management (interdiction de gérer) for up to fifteen years.
Abuse of Law / Artificial Shell Structure

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the abusive arrangement under Article L.64 of the LPF, recharacterisation of the whole transaction, collection of the omitted tax plus 80% penalty, and in aggravated cases criminal prosecution for tax evasion with up to five years imprisonment and EUR 500,000 fine.

Director personal liability: The managing parties may be jointly and severally liable for the recomputed tax debt; the court may issue an insolvency disqualification (faillite personnelle) and impose a prohibition against directing or managing any legal person for the duration of the case.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative sanctions by ACPR/TRACFIN up to EUR 5,000,000 or 10%% of annual turnover, criminal sanctions of five years' imprisonment and EUR 500,000 fine (individuals) or EUR 2,000,000 fine (legal persons), freezing of assets, and cancellation of the ACPR licence.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors and senior managers may be imprisoned for up to five years and fined EUR 500,000; they may also be personally ordered to pay the full amounts laundered, prohibited from exercising a trade, expelled from office, and made subject to a European or international travel ban for felony-related activities.
