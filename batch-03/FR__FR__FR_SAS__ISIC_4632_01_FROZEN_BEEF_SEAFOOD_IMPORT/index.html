Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum for SAS. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and applies EU rules on free movement of capital and payments. There is no general exchange control, mandatory conversion into euros, or prior authorization for ordinary cross-border commercial payments or capital contributions. Current-account payments, dividends, and capital transactions may generally be made through regulated payment providers, subject to sanctions, AML/CFT, tax, corporate-law, bank documentation, and applicable statistical-reporting requirements. Dividend remittances require distributable profits, duly approved accounts and resolutions, and withholding-tax analysis; treaty or EU relief may require evidence of residence, beneficial ownership, and satisfaction of anti-abuse conditions. Interest, royalties, service fees, and shareholder-loan payments require substantiation, transfer-pricing support where applicable, and withholding-tax review. Banks may request source-of-funds, transaction-purpose, invoice, customs, and beneficiary information and may delay or reject transactions under their compliance controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory deadline requiring export proceeds to be repatriated and no mandatory surrender or conversion ratio for ordinary commercial export receipts. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to bank terms, sanctions, AML/CFT controls, accounting and tax rules, and any applicable statistical-reporting obligations. The company should retain contracts, invoices, transport and customs records, and bank confirmations to substantiate receipts.

Central bank supervision: The European Central Bank (ECB) sets euro-area monetary policy. Banque de France is France's national central bank and performs Eurosystem and payment-system functions. The Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR), within Banque de France, supervises French banking and insurance institutions. The French Treasury (Direction générale du Trésor) administers relevant national financial and monetary rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for routine euro collections and payments, subject to the provider's French or EEA authorization, permitted services, country coverage, and risk appetite. An electronic money institution is not a bank; safeguarded client funds are not necessarily covered by a deposit-guarantee scheme. Confirm legal-entity onboarding, French IBAN and payment functionality, foreign-currency availability, payment limits, direct-debit support, and access to SWIFT before reliance. An EMI should not be assumed to provide a French statutory capital-deposit certificate, revolving trade finance, documentary letters of credit, or bank credit. Do not use an offshore provider to circumvent sanctions, AML controls, or a bank's onboarding decision.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route subscriptions and shareholder funding from an account in the contributor's own name to the designated French bank capital-deposit account or, after registration, the SAS's operational account. Identify the payment as share capital, share premium, or shareholder loan and include the subscriber or lender name, SAS name, and supporting reference. Supply the bank with subscription documents, incorporation evidence, ownership information, source-of-funds evidence, and any requested tax-residence or regulatory forms. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments where available and SWIFT for other currencies or non-SEPA origins. Do not rely on a universal French bank purpose-code system; provide a precise narrative and transaction documents. Reconcile receipt to corporate records and obtain the capital-deposit certificate where required for formation.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated EEA payments where supported, and SWIFT customer credit transfers (commonly documented as MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent) for other cross-border payments. Match beneficiary name, account, bank identifiers, currency, invoice number, contract, shipment, and payment terms. For supplier risk mitigation, request a documentary commercial letter of credit issued or confirmed by a bank, subject to credit approval, collateral, and applicable ICC rules such as UCP 600 where incorporated. Documentary collections may be considered where appropriate. Retain invoices, purchase orders, transport and customs documents, sanctions-screening evidence, and payment confirmations. Agree allocation of charges and FX conversion terms in advance; reconcile bank fees and short payments. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% for non-resident corporate shareholders under Article 119 bis CGI; 12.8% for non-resident individuals plus applicable social levies where France has taxing rights. Treaty rates may reduce the domestic rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a 31 December fiscal year-end: file by the second business day after 1 May. For other fiscal year-ends: file no later than the third business day following the expiry of three months after the closing date. E-filing extensions apply automatically.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 2051 (Form 2065, first page) Company identification, SIREN, APE/NAF code 4632Z, legal form SAS, registered office, fiscal year opening and closing dates, and place of tax residence. A French SAS, even if wholly foreign-owned, files as a French resident entity and must enter its French registered address. Non-resident shareholders do not file this form; the SAS itself is the taxpayer. No daily surcharge for CIT; late payment interest accrues at 0.20% per month. Late filing after formal notice attracts a 10% majoration under Article 1728 CGI; deliberate concealment carries 40%; fraudulent declarations or abuse of law carry 80%. Failure to file a nil return triggers a minimum EUR 150 penalty. Additional penalties may be imposed for missing transfer pricing documentation and for non-cooperation with an audit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 annual turnover or total assets in France triggers mandatory Master File and Local File documentation under Article L.13 AA/AB LPF. EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers French Country-by-Country Reporting under Article 223 quinquies C CGI.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a separate Economic Substances Regulations code, but the substance-over-form doctrine and anti-abuse rules require genuine commercial substance for any French SAS or permanent establishment. The French entity must demonstrate local management, operational staff, cold-chain infrastructure, meaningful purchasing and logistics activities, and assumption of commercial risk. Absent substance, tax authorities invoke Article L.64 LPF, Article 57 CGI, and treaty restriction under the BEPS MLI principal purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent return

Statutory sanction: 80% tax penalty under Article 1729 CGI plus late-payment interest; criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI with a fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment for five years, increased to EUR 1,000,000 and seven years for organised or aggravated fraud. The tax administration may recover the full concealed tax base and social security contributions.

Director personal liability: Directors who participated in preparing false declarations are jointly liable for the company's tax debt and penalties under Article L.267 LPF. Conviction can lead to travel bans, asset seizure, commercial disqualification, and imprisonment. Civil restitution orders may attach personal wealth if the corporate veil is pierced.
Shell company or abuse of law

Statutory sanction: Abuse of law under Article L.64 LPF triggers an 80% penalty, reassessment at the level of the real beneficial owner, and denial of treaty benefits under the principal purpose test. The transaction is recharacterised, and additional withholding tax becomes immediately payable.

Director personal liability: Directors of the artificial entity may be jointly liable for reassessed taxes and penalties under Article L.267 LPF. If they caused or knew of the sham, they may also face criminal sanctions, personal civil damages, travel restrictions, and confiscation orders.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Reassessment under Article 57 CGI to real arm's-length price, plus penalties: 40% for deliberate mispricing and up to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres. Failure to provide transfer pricing documentation within 30 days of request incurs a fine up to EUR 150,000 or 5% of the reassessed result, whichever is higher, under Article 1735 bis CGI.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed or approved artificial transfer prices can be personally held liable for the resulting tax deficiencies under Article L.267 LPF. Egregious conduct may trigger criminal tax fraud prosecution, professional bans, and seizure of personal assets.
AML and suspicious payment flows

Statutory sanction: Money laundering under Article 324-1 of the French Criminal Code is punishable by up to five years imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine for natural persons; aggravated or organised laundering carries ten years and EUR 750,000. Customs fraud penalties may add one to three times the evaded duties. Administrative sanctions from ACPR and TRACFIN may include licence withdrawal and financial fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who structured or knowingly tolerated money laundering risk personal prosecution, travel bans, asset confiscation, and disqualification from French corporate management. Civil restitution can attach personal assets, and the corporate veil is pierced for deliberate AML non-compliance.
