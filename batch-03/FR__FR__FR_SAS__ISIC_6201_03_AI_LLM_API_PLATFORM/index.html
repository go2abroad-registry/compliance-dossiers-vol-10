Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) of the Tribunal de Commerce having territorial jurisdiction over the registered office; electronic filings are made through the Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises, operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-30 and R. 227-1 et seq. governing the Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS); Article L. 210-2 (nationality); Articles L. 225-218 et seq. (special provisions applicable to simplified companies); Code civil Article 1841 et seq. for corporate constitution.

Formation / constitutional filings

Declarative incorporation file lodged via the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr), comprising the signed Articles of Association (statuts), the INSEE company creation data set, the legal notice publication certificate, the capital deposit certificate, and the beneficial owner declaration; the resulting official registration proof is the K-bis extract.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital under Article L. 227-2 of the Code de commerce; shareholders may fix nominal capital as low as EUR 1. All cash contributions must be fully paid upon constitution and deposited with a French credit institution, the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary as depositary; the written depositary certificate (attestation de dépôt des fonds) must be attached to the Articles at the time of signature.

Funding & unlock path: No civil-defense fire certificate is required for purely office-based digital activity; the premises must nevertheless comply with workplace safety regulations of the Code du travail, including electrical installations checks, fire extinguisher maintenance, and emergency evacuation planning. If the company opens a customer-facing showroom or training space qualifying as an Établissement Recevant du Public (ERP), a fire safety commissioning checklist (commission de sécurité) and periodic controls apply under the Code de la construction et de l'habitation.

Category: None required under French law for the supply of AI/LLM API services; the RCS registration (K-bis) constitutes the sole pre-authorization for the business activity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), through the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and the official tax portal impots.gouv.fr. Business formation and statutory registration are filed through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI).

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is incorporated through the INPI Guichet unique. The filing generates the SIREN and SIRET identifiers and transmits the formation data to the DGFiP. The company must identify its competent SIE, provide its articles of association, registration extract, registered-office evidence, directors' and beneficial-owners information, business activity, bank details, and expected taxable turnover. The DGFiP assigns or confirms the corporate income-tax account and, where applicable, the French VAT identification number in the format FR plus a two-digit key and the nine-digit SIREN. VAT registration, intra-Community VAT activation, and filing-frequency requests are handled through the SIE and the relevant DGFiP forms and online services. A non-established foreign entity carrying out taxable French activities must register with the appropriate non-resident or local SIE before commencing transactions requiring French VAT compliance; an EU-established business may generally use the VAT One Stop Shop for eligible B2C intra-EU supplies, but the OSS does not replace a French VAT registration where French domestic taxable transactions, imports, stock, or other registration triggers exist.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) by default. A qualifying SAS may elect temporarily for income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu, IR), generally for a maximum of five financial years, if statutory conditions are satisfied, including qualifying ownership, business activity, size, age, and shareholder requirements. The election must be notified within the statutory period and is not a general check-the-box election. France does not provide a broad United States-style entity-classification election. Tax-transparent treatment may apply to qualifying partnerships or other legally distinct forms, but not automatically to an ordinary SAS. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement applicable EU and OECD-based limitations on deduction, exemption, and imported mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For French domestic businesses supplying services, the VAT franchise threshold is generally EUR 37,500 of prior-year turnover, with a current-year tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250, subject to statutory updates and sector-specific rules. The principal thresholds for goods and accommodation are generally EUR 85,000 and EUR 93,500. Exceeding the applicable threshold, electing for VAT, making transactions requiring registration, or conducting specified intra-Community or import transactions can trigger registration. Cross-border EU SME exemption rules may additionally require an EU-wide turnover limit of EUR 100,000 and compliance with the relevant national threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods dispatched or transported from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with credit for input tax, provided the supplier establishes exportation and exit from the EU through customs records and transport evidence. Intra-Community supplies of goods may qualify for exemption where the customer has a valid VAT number in another EU Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier satisfies recapitulative and evidentiary requirements. For AI inference, software, hosting, and API services, a B2B supply is generally located where the business customer is established or has the relevant fixed establishment; a supply to a customer established outside France is therefore commonly outside the scope of French VAT rather than a French zero-rated export. B2C digital services are generally taxed at the consumer's location and may require OSS or local registration. The supplier must verify customer status, contractual recipient, establishment, service nature, payment trail, and technical delivery evidence.

  • Electronic export declaration and customs exit confirmation, including the Movement Reference Number (MRN) or equivalent customs evidence
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and VAT treatment
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, airway bill, consignment note, carrier confirmation, or equivalent proof of dispatch and delivery outside the European Union
  • Packing list and, where applicable, import or destination customs documentation
  • Proof of contractual export responsibility and evidence linking the goods to the customs declaration
  • For intra-Community goods supplies, the customer's valid EU VAT identification number, transport evidence, and recapitulative statement records
  • For offshore or out-of-scope services, contract, customer establishment evidence, VAT-number validation where applicable, service-delivery records, invoices, and payment evidence
  • Bank payment and accounting records sufficient to reconcile the transaction; a separate inward-remittance certificate is not generally required under French law for ordinary export VAT exemption

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting for domestic B2B transactions. From 1 September 2026, all French businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices, and large enterprises and certain intermediate-sized enterprises must issue them; SMEs and micro-enterprises are generally subject to issuance from 1 September 2027. The timetable is subject to legislative implementation and permitted postponements. Invoices will be exchanged through an approved private platform or the designated public infrastructure, with structured data transmitted to the DGFiP. Transaction reporting (e-reporting) applies to relevant B2C and cross-border transactions. France does not generally require real-time pre-clearance of every invoice before issuance; digital transmission, structured-data compliance, authenticity, integrity, auditability, and reporting requirements apply. A service provider operating an AI API platform must retain valid invoice data and evidence of customer status, place of supply, VAT number, reverse-charge treatment, and export or out-of-scope treatment.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement for ordinary export receipts. Export VAT exemption is denied or clawed back where customs exit, transport, customer status, place of supply, or transaction records are insufficient. The DGFiP may assess unpaid VAT, applicable late-payment interest, and penalties; a 40% penalty may apply for deliberate failure or serious negligence, and an 80% penalty may apply for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of law, subject to statutory conditions. False invoices, fabricated export evidence, concealment, or intentional VAT fraud may also create criminal exposure, including fines and imprisonment under applicable law. Assessments generally include retrospective recovery for open tax years, and input VAT refunds may be rejected or recovered where the underlying zero-rating or taxable-business nexus is unsubstantiated. Records must be preserved for the applicable French tax and accounting retention periods and must be available in an auditable form.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is part of the euro area and does not operate exchange controls for current or capital account transactions; currency convertibility is full and unrestricted in law, subject only to EU measures, financial sanctions, and anti-money-laundering procedures. The euro is freely convertible and cross-border payments in EUR, USD, GBP, and CHF can be made without prior central-bank approval. Profit remittance by a French SAS by way of dividends, management fees, or royalties is legally unrestricted, but in practice requires distributable profits, approved corporate accounts, a valid shareholder decision in the form required for an SAS, French tax residence evidence of the recipient, treaty or EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive analysis for withholding tax, and standard CRS/FATCA reporting by the paying bank. Capital contributions from non-resident shareholders are permitted without exchange approval and must be fully documented to satisfy KYC, balance-of-payments, and AML requirements. USDC stablecoin inflows are not subject to exchange control but attract enhanced due diligence and, where relevant, CASP licensing and MiCA compliance in the European Union.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France maintains no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no forced repatriation timeline. A French SAS may receive foreign-currency export revenue into an authorized EU/EEA bank account or convert it into euros at any time. For balance-of-payments statistics, all cross-border transfers must pass through an authorized financial intermediary, and the SAS must provide the bank with correct transaction coding under the BPM6 service category applicable to computer services, cloud infrastructure, and API/LLM usage fees, together with any threshold-based REX declarations to the Banque de France. There is no obligation to invoice exports in a particular currency, but USD-invoiced AI/LLM API sales must be booked accurately for French VAT, CIT, and transfer-pricing purposes.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, in coordination with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) as the foreign-exchange policy authority and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the prudential and AML/CFT banking supervisor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility is High for collecting digital API receivables through a licensed EU/EEA payment or e-money institution, Medium for using such institutions as the main treasury settlement account, and Low for a non-EEA offshore EMI as the primary banking layer. Stripe, operating through an EU payment-institution licence and passporting into France under PSD2, is a highly effective collection rail for AI/LLM token usage, card payments, and SEPA Direct Debit, but it is not a bank and does not provide deposit protection, capital escrow, FX hedging, or high-value trade settlement. Fully digital EU EMIs and neobanks may open accounts remotely with reasonable AML/KYC standards, but they impose lower transaction ceilings and are less suitable for large equity injections and intercompany treasury flows. A non-EEA offshore digital EMI established in the UK, Singapore, or the United States generally cannot lawfully provide payment accounts to a French resident without obtaining French or EU payment-service authorisation; any such arrangement requires a formal French regulatory legal opinion to prevent unlicensed payment activity under the French Monetary and Financial Code.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity or intercompany funding from non-resident shareholders must be routed by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer or SWIFT MT202 COV interbank cover into the French operating or capital-escrow account of the SAS at a Tier-1 bank. The payment message must reference the SIREN/SIRET identifier and the corporate purpose, such as subscription of new shares, capital increase, or shareholders’ current account. The bank will classify the flow for Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting as direct investment equity, other investment, or intercompany loan, and the SAS must contemporaneously file the shareholder resolution, capital-increase deed, subscription receipt, UBO declaration, CRS/FATCA self-certifications, and source-of-funds evidence. To avoid French tax re-characterization, all received amounts must be posted to the correct share-capital, share-premium, or shareholders’ current-account ledger and reported consistently to the French tax administration.For payments to non-resident suppliers, contractors, and shareholders, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 customer transfers for high-value or cross-currency disbursements, SEPA Credit Transfers for EUR-denominated payments within the EU/EEA, and SWIFT MT202 COV for interbank cover of large instructions. Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600, documentary collections, and bank payment obligations are acceptable for physical hardware, data-centre equipment, and infrastructure purchases; digital service and API supplier fees may be settled through card acquiring via Stripe, SEPA Direct Debit, or SWIFT. All outbound payments must be supported by invoices, purchase orders, service contracts, and, where relevant, VAT or Intra-EU reporting; dividend distributions require evidence of distributable reserves, approval of the shareholders, and the correct French withholding-tax treatment.
