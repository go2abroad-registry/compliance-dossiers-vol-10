Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS, incorporation through the Guichet Unique des formalités d'entreprises (formerly CFE) enables issuance of SIREN/SIRET identifiers; the DGFiP then allocates the French VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire FR XXXXXXXXX) for taxable activities. A foreign entity not yet incorporated may apply directly to the local SIE for VAT registration and, if no EU establishment, must appoint a fiscal representative accountable for VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or pass-through election under French law; an SAS is mandatorily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). Available elections include: (i) fiscal integration under Article 223 A CGI for reorganised French groups; (ii) option for simplified withholding-like regimes is not applicable; (iii) hybrid mismatch elections not generally elective but ATAD-aligned rules apply automatically under Articles 212 bis, 209 B, and the French anti-hybrid statutory provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 91,900 annual taxable turnover for goods supply/main business (Article 293 B CGI) for the franchise en base exemption; mandatory VAT registration applies upon exceeding this threshold or on making taxable intra-Community/export transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods shipped outside the European Union qualify for 0% VAT where the goods are transferred from France and evidenced by a customs export declaration (DAU) accepted by French customs. Intra-Community supplies of goods to EU purchasers with a valid VAT identification number also qualify for zero-rating. Offshore services supplied to a non-EU recipient are generally outside the scope or zero-rated if the recipient is a taxable person established outside France/EU, subject to the place-of-supply rules under Articles 259 A/259 B CGI.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane – DAU/DELTA)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (CMR / Air Waybill)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of physical shipment out of EU and arrival evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment proof of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping currently enforced. France mandates structured electronic invoicing for all domestic B2B transactions under the Factur-X standard through the Public Invoicing Portal (PPF) and certified dematerialization platforms (PDPs), with phased obligations beginning 1 September 2026 for reception and 1 September 2027 for issuance; API reporting (e-reporting) is transmitted automatically to the DGFiP via the PPF.

French customs and the DGFiP may retroactively reassess VAT at standard rates if documentary proof of export is incomplete or inconsistent, recovering the tax under Articles 272 and 178 A CGI plus late payment interest and penalties. Failure to substantiate zero-rated exports results in a clawback of exempt treatment. Fraudulent claims attract a 40-80% tax penalty and, under Article 1741 CGI, potential criminal prosecution including imprisonment, alongside the blocking of any refund claim until evidence is produced.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area member; the euro is fully convertible on current and capital accounts. The EU Treaty guarantees free movement of capital; no exchange controls exist for bona fide commercial or investment transactions. Profit remittance from a French SAS to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted provided dividends are declared from audited accounts and tax reporting obligations (e.g., Form 2561 for distribution, EU Interest and Royalties Directive, if applicable) are satisfied. Payments must pass through a licensed banking channel, subject to AML/KYC due diligence and sanctions screening. Restrictions apply only to sanctioned countries and to transactions involving terrorist financing, money laundering, or tax evasion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds in France. Exporters are free to retain balances in foreign currency and repatriate the equivalent of export receivables at commercially agreed dates. However, Banque de France requires statistical reporting of cross-border flows for balance-of-payments purposes (via 'EC' survey forms for flows above EUR 1 million per transaction aggregate), and all settlements payable to a resident export company must be routed through a French-licensed bank or an EU-authorized payment institution that reports to the appropriate national central bank. Export contracts may be denominated in any currency, and there are no maturity limits on foreign currency deposits.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor, DGT); prudential and anti-money laundering supervision is exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR), an independent authority attached to the Banque de France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for transaction flows, but subject to legal constraints: authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, or Qonto may open euro and foreign-currency accounts with SEPA/SWIFT IBANs under ACPR or ECB licensing. They facilitate small-value trade settlements and offer mid-market FX conversion with reduced fees. However, EMIs cannot offer credit facilities, cash-deposit services, or formal escrow functions; balances are not covered by the French Deposit Guarantee Fund; and high-value capital injections or dividend distributions will trigger enhanced compliance review. For a manufacturing SAS, an EMI is recommended as a secondary liquidity vector only; the primary operating and capital account should be held with a tier-1 French licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All cross-border euro or foreign-currency injections into the SAS must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 credits to the French operating account. The required transactional purpose code under the EU Balance of Payments statistical system is 'INCA' (direct investment in kind) or 'CANT' (capital contribution); in practice, French banks require the narrative 'Capital contribution to SAS Company' and the Banque de France code 'D.8' (equity capital). Direct cross-border capital contributions exceeding EUR 1,000,000 must be reported to the Banque de France through the resident company's monthly 'Liasse de Couverture' filing. The beneficiary bank will demand the final share-capital deposit certificate, notarised minutes of the shareholders' resolution, and a signed conformity declaration for FX statistics before releasing funds for operational expenditure.Settlements to non-EU materials suppliers should be executed by SWIFT MT103 with the appropriate ICC trade purpose code (e.g., '151' for goods, '152' for services). For high-value structured procurement, a documentary Letter of Credit under UCP 600 issued by a French confirming bank is recommended, with the SAS as applicant and the overseas supplier as beneficiary; settlement occurs upon presentation of compliant shipping documents. For EU intra-zone payments, SEPA Instant Credit Transfer and SEPA Direct Debit are suitable for routine invoices.
