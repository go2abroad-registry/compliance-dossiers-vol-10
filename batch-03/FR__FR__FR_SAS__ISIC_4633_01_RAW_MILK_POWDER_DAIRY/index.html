Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) and Direction Régionale des Douanes et Droits Indirects for customs/VAT on import-export; official portal: impots.gouv.fr and pro.douane.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an SAS in France must (1) file Articles of Association with the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (now through formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) to obtain an INSEE identification (SIREN/SIRET); (2) apply for a French VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) via the Service des Impôts des Entreprises for the registered office using Form 326-PFG for regular filing or Form 3526 for EU sales; (3) submit a non-EU entity's ultimate parent, director/manager identification and a copy of a bank account certificate; (4) await delivery of the FR SIREN two-digit key and a provisional French VAT registration certificate; (5) for import/export activity, obtain an EORI number (FR followed by SIREN) through pro.douane.gouv.fr. Liquidation of minor errors requires a rectification filing with DGFiP; mandatory designation of a tax representative for non-EU principals applying for VAT refunds or direct customs clearance.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity establishing an SAS in France must (1) file Articles of Association with the Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (now through formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) to obtain an INSEE identification (SIREN/SIRET); (2) apply for a French VAT number (TVA intracommunautaire) via the Service des Impôts des Entreprises for the registered office using Form 326-PFG for regular filing or Form 3526 for EU sales; (3) submit a non-EU entity's ultimate parent, director/manager identification and a copy of a bank account certificate; (4) await delivery of the FR SIREN two-digit key and a provisional French VAT registration certificate; (5) for import/export activity, obtain an EORI number (FR followed by SIREN) through pro.douane.gouv.fr. Liquidation of minor errors requires a rectification filing with DGFiP; mandatory designation of a tax representative for non-EU principals applying for VAT refunds or direct customs clearance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box pass-through election exists under French domestic law; an SAS is per se subject to corporate income tax (IS). Available elections include: (1) option for normal corporate income tax if a fiscal integration regime applies under Article 223 A CGI, allowing a tax group with a 95%+ parent; (2) election under Article 239 bis A CGI to be treated as a tax-transparent partnership under strict statutory and joint-ownership conditions (limited to unlisted SCS/SCA, not SAS); (3) option for VAT franchise not available to an SAS because it exceeds the small-enterprise thresholds by statute; (4) hybrid mismatch design is constrained by DAC6 reporting and Article 212 bis CGI — no unilateral check-the-box, but upstream foreign entities may file form 8832 in their own jurisdiction to alter French branch/conduit result.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No minimum registration threshold for an SAS performing taxable wholesale supplies in France; a compulsory registration is due from the first taxable transaction. The domestic franchise exemption may apply only to entity types demonstrating a tax-exclusive turnover below EUR 85,000 for supply of goods (gemeine rule) or EUR 37,500 for services, and does not apply to an SAS conducting merchant wholesale of dairy powders where the economic activity is taxable by default; a foreign company with no fixed establishment is entitled to distance-sales registration only above EUR 35,000 B2C threshold for another EU seller, but a French-domiciled SAS has no beneficial threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies under Article 262-I-1° CGI to tangible goods physically dispatched or transported outside the European Union by or on behalf of the seller, evidenced by customs declaration; zero-rating extends to transport services directly linked to the export (Article 262-I-2°) and to intra-Community supply of goods if the purchaser possesses a valid EU VAT ID and the goods leave the seller's territory (Article 262-I-3°). For cross-border services supplied to a non-taxable EU customer or offshore principal, the place of supply determines whether VAT is due; an exporter must substantiate the actual removal of goods with transport contracts, loading risk and delivery terms (CIF/FOB), and for dairy commodity trading, the zero-rating cannot be claimed for goods retained in an EU customs warehouse or consumed in France.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation via DELTA-X, EMCS/GCC) proving customs release for exit from the EU customs territory
  • Bill of Lading (Connaissement) or CMR / AWB citing the French exporter of record, chapter 1–ISIC 4633 goods description, gross weight and destination outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Attestation de Change / Banque de France certificate) evidencing that export payment was received in an authorized French bank account in convertible currency
  • Commercial invoice bearing the buyer's identity, incoterms and exact milk-powder specification (HS 0401/0402)
  • Transport document (for non-EU cargo: airway bill or seaway bill; for EU route to non-EU: transit transport document T1 or electronic exit summary ECS-D)
  • Register of export proofs and copy of the buyer's written confirmation of receipt at the declared non-EU port of destination

Mandatory electronic invoicing for all French B2B transactions under Law 2022-1157 (Facturation électronique) enforced from 2024 for large taxpayers and from 2026 for all taxable persons, transmitted through certified dematerialization platforms (PDP) or the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF). Each invoice must satisfy prescribed structured data fields (factur-X/UBL) and be reported in near-real-time to DGFiP using a certified API (no digital stamping applies); customs export declarations are cleared via the DELTA/GCC system integrated with the Single Window, and a failed or altered fiscal data transmission leads to rejection of the invoice and a 15 EUR per-invoice penalty (Article 1737 CGI).

Fraudulent or substantively defective export zero-rating triggers immediate VAT reversal with statutory interest and penalties: (1) 5% per quarter of the principal amount under Article 1731 CGI; (2) 80% fraudulent-claims penalty under Article 1737 CGI where the exporter issued false customs declarations or forged bills of lading; (3) administrative confiscation of exported goods (Article 414-3 Customs Code); (4) personal liability under Article 1753-bis CGI against the managing shareholder/director for concealment of grossly inflated export invoices; (5) an importer/exporter CSCE deregistration and six-month customs bank guarantee upon repeated violations. The recovery window is extended to six years in tax fraud cases, and a criminal customs offence entailing up to 30,000 EUR fine per declaration is counted, with clawback of the VAT refund plus 40% late-payment surcharge.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro (EUR) is fully and freely convertible within France and the euro area. No prior foreign-exchange approval is required for current-account transactions (trade in goods and services, income distributions and profit remittances) or for inward direct investment. Capital-account operations are liberalized subject to prudential monitoring; the only binding requirements are statistical reporting obligations to the Banque de France for cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 50,000 per calendar month (balance-of-payments reporting), and EU/UN sanctions screening of all counterparties. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted once corporate formalities (board approval, distributable reserves, withholding tax analysis under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive) are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no export proceeds surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion requirements. Exporters may hold foreign currency proceeds freely in euro or foreign-currency accounts with authorized intermediaries. However, the Banque de France requires: (a) declaration of all cross-border settlements exceeding EUR 50,000 on a monthly aggregated basis via the authorized intermediary bank; (b) substantiation of export receipts by commercial invoices, transport documents, and the related EUR1/ATR or customs export declarations; (c) compliance with EU sanctions and anti-money-laundering screening on the paying bank and the underlying trade file.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting in conjunction with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy and Finance) for foreign exchange control policy and balance-of-payments reporting; EU-level competence under the Single Euro Payments Area and the free movement of capital principle (TFEU Art. 63).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for collection-side receipts and disbursements, but LOW for end-to-end treasury autonomy. Legal constraints: an offshore (non-EEA) digital EMI may not provide payment services in France unless it holds an EU/EEA licence passported under PSD2 into the French market via the ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) filing, or establishes a French branch. A UK-based EMI post-Brexit lacks passporting rights and would require full ACPR licensing or reliance on a French partner bank. EEA-licensed EMIs may operate through the single-passport regime but are subject to: (i) safeguarding of client funds in segregated accounts; (ii) SCA/PSD2 authentication; (iii) prohibition on granting credit; (iv) ACPR registration as a payment services agent/principal where local distribution occurs. For commodity trade treasury involving commercial LC operations, an EMI cannot substitute for a licensed credit institution; EMIs are therefore acceptable only as auxiliary rails for small-balance collections, not for documentary credit or escrow functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the shareholder remits the subscribed capital either (i) to a notary-sealed Capital Escrow Account of the SAS opened in France before incorporation, or (ii) directly to the SAS's operational account after K-bis registration, in each case by SWIFT MT103/TT. The wire must use ISO 20022 purpose-code 'EQUI' (or 'INCA' for capital increase) and include the SAS's name, LEI and the reference of the capital subscription resolution. Bank verification requires the notary certificate of deposit (attestation du dépositaire des fonds), the deed of incorporation, a statement of no registration within eight days, and the UBO declaration. Funds must be free of any third-party encumbrance; for loans a shareholder loan agreement and interest-rate conditions aligned with French thin-capitalization transfer pricing rules are required. For non-EU remitters, requisite supporting record: source-of-funds evidence (audited accounts, dividend resolution of the remitting entity, or disposal contract).Primary rails for a French SAS paying foreign milk-powder suppliers and logistics parties: (a) SWIFT MT103/MT202 single-payment TT wires in EUR/USD on a maturity-matched basis; (b) Documentary Letters of Credit under UCP 600 (sight or usance) issued by the French bank in favour of suppliers, with negotiation under the supplier's corresponding bank; (c) Standby LCs for advance-payment performance security; (d) Documentary Collections under URC 522 (Documents against Payment / Documents against Acceptance) as the intermediate control option; (e) domestic SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for euro-denominated payments within the EEA; and (f) supply-chain finance programmes (approved payables financing) for recurring imports. A UK-based EMI post-Brexit lacks passporting rights and would require full ACPR licensing or reliance on a French partner bank. EEA-licensed EMIs may operate through the single-passport regime but are subject to: (i) safeguarding of client funds in segregated accounts; (ii) SCA/PSD2 authentication; (iii) prohibition on granting credit; (iv) ACPR registration as a payment services agent/principal where local distribution occurs. For commodity trade treasury involving commercial LC operations, an EMI cannot substitute for a licensed credit institution; EMIs are therefore acceptable only as auxiliary rails for small-balance collections, not for documentary credit or escrow functions.
