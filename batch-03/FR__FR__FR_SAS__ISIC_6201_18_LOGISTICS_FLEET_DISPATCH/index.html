Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS; shareholders freely determine the capital in the Statuts. Capital must be fully subscribed. Monetary contributions must be deposited into a blocked bank account until registration; the Statuts may allow payment of up to 50% on formation, with the remainder due within five years. In-kind contributions exceeding €7,500 in value require a statutory auditor (Commissaire aux Apports) unless an exemption applies.

Funding & unlock path: For the office premises, obtain a fire safety certificate from the municipal Commission de Sécurité before occupation. The building must comply with the Code du travail workplace safety standards and the load-bearing structural requirements of the Code de la Construction et de l'Habitation. No specific occupational license is required for software-development employees, but a health and safety assessment must be documented.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). The principal online portal is impots.gouv.fr. Corporate formation and registration data are submitted through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), with SIREN and SIRET numbers issued through the national business register infrastructure.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is incorporated and declared through the INPI Guichet unique. The filing generates the entity's SIREN and SIRET identifiers and communicates formation data to the DGFiP. The SAS must identify its registered tax office, submit its corporate tax and VAT registration information, and activate its professional account on impots.gouv.fr. The French VAT identification number is generally formatted as FR followed by a two-character validation key and the nine-digit SIREN. A non-resident entity without a French fixed establishment must apply to the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE), provide its incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, foreign tax registration evidence, business description, contracts and invoices, identity and authority documents for representatives, and banking and contact details. The entity must distinguish between transactions requiring French VAT registration, transactions subject to customer reverse charge, and transactions reportable through an applicable One Stop Shop regime. EU businesses may generally avoid French VAT registration for B2B services where the customer accounts for VAT under the reverse-charge mechanism, but registration remains necessary for French taxable supplies not covered by reverse charge, domestic imports, local stock, or other French VAT liabilities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS) on its taxable profits. A qualifying SAS may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu, or IR) for up to five financial years, subject to statutory ownership, activity, size, age and shareholder requirements; the election must be made within the prescribed period and is generally irrevocable once the election period has expired. A qualifying family-owned company may have separate eligibility rules, but those rules do not ordinarily apply to a standard SAS. France has no general United States-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the EU ATAD II framework and may deny deductions, impose inclusion, or neutralize imported mismatch outcomes; they do not create a general elective hybrid classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 37,500 annual turnover for the ordinary French VAT franchise threshold applicable to services, including many SaaS and electronically supplied service activities. A tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250 may apply where the preceding-year threshold was not exceeded, subject to current statutory conditions. Separate thresholds apply to goods and certain regulated activities. The threshold does not eliminate VAT obligations for transactions subject to special place-of-supply rules, imports, intra-Community acquisitions, domestic reverse-charge obligations, or elective taxation. A French-established SAS exceeding the applicable threshold, or voluntarily opting for VAT taxation, must register and charge or account for VAT as required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from France or another EU Member State to a destination outside the EU may be invoiced without French VAT where the supplier, its customs representative, or the customer completes the export formalities and the supplier retains reliable evidence that the goods left the EU customs territory. The treatment is technically a VAT exemption with the right to deduct rather than a domestic 0% rate. SaaS and other services supplied B2B to a customer established outside France are generally taxed according to the place-of-supply rules and may be outside the scope of French VAT or subject to customer reverse charge, rather than being treated as exported goods at 0%. B2C electronically supplied services may be subject to VAT in the customer's country and may require OSS or local registration. Evidence should include customer status and location, contractual scope, service delivery records, invoices, payment records, IP or access records where relevant, and any required reverse-charge wording.

  • Export customs declaration and Movement Reference Number (MRN), with electronic confirmation of exit from the EU customs territory
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, destination and applicable VAT exemption basis
  • Transport evidence appropriate to the mode of transport, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, carrier certificate or freight-forwarder exit evidence
  • Sales contract, purchase order and packing list where relevant
  • Proof of delivery or destination outside the EU where customs exit evidence alone is insufficient
  • For services, customer VAT or business-status evidence, customer establishment and location records, contract, invoice, service logs and proof of payment
  • Intra-Community supply evidence, where applicable, including the customer's valid EU VAT number, transport evidence and required recapitulative statement; an inward remittance certificate is not a general French VAT export requirement

France does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system or mandatory digital stamping regime for ordinary private-sector invoices. Domestic B2B e-invoicing reform is being phased in: all businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; large and medium-sized businesses must issue domestic B2B electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; and small and medium-sized businesses must issue them from 1 September 2027, subject to applicable legislative and regulatory implementation. Electronic invoices must be transmitted through an approved private platform or the public invoicing portal infrastructure designated by law. B2C and cross-border transactions may require electronic transaction reporting (e-reporting) under the phased regime. Public-sector invoices are generally submitted through Chorus Pro. Current invoices must contain mandatory French VAT particulars and be retained in an auditable, authentic and integral form; no general pre-clearance API approval is presently required.

France has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. Export VAT exemption or deduction may be denied or retrospectively clawed back if the supplier cannot prove export, misstates the customer or destination, issues false documentation, or fails to satisfy invoicing and record-retention requirements. The DGFiP may reassess unpaid VAT, deny related input VAT credits, charge late-payment interest generally at 0.20% per month, and impose penalties commonly ranging from 10% for certain filing or payment failures to 40% for deliberate failure and up to 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres or concealed activity, subject to statutory facts and procedure. Serious fraud may trigger criminal sanctions. Ordinary VAT reassessment periods generally extend through the end of the third year following the year in which the tax became payable, with longer periods available in specified fraudulent, concealed or criminal circumstances. Export evidence, customs data, contracts, invoices and accounting records must be retained for the applicable French statutory period, commonly at least ten years for business accounting records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on both the current account and the capital account. France maintains no exchange-control licensing or prior-authorisation regime for ordinary treasury operations; Article L. 151-1 et seq. of the Code monetaire et financier confirms the free movement of capital. The practical constraints are documentary and statistical: cross-border financial transactions involving non-EEA counterparties are subject to Banque de France balance-of-payments declarations through the servicing bank, and cash or asset movements of EUR 10,000 or more must be reported. Profit remittance hurdles relate to corporate law and tax rather than convertibility: dividends must be paid from distributable reserves under Articles L. 232-11 and L. 232-11-1 of the Code de commerce; the statutory 25% withholding tax under Article 119-2 CGI may be reduced to 0% for qualifying EU/EEA parent companies meeting the 10% holding and two-year condition, or under an applicable double-tax treaty; and payments to residents of non-cooperative states under Article 238-A CGI face enhanced due diligence, non-deductibility, and high withholding rates. A French SAS with non-resident shareholders must therefore prepare a distribution-capacity certificate, a tax-residency certificate for the beneficial owner, and treaty-reduction evidence before any upstream dividend remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France, as a Eurosystem member state, imposes no mandatory surrender, no conversion ratio, and no repatriation window for export proceeds. SaaS and logistics-telematics service exports are classified as invisible current-account transactions and may be invoiced and retained in EUR, USD, or any freely convertible currency without prior approval. The relevant obligation is statistical only: non-EEA payments exceeding EUR 10,000 must be captured in the bank's monthly balance-of-payments declaration to the Banque de France, and the SAS must retain the underlying commercial evidence (signed platform agreement, invoices, payment settlement records, and usage logs) for at least five years for inspection by the ACPR, the Direction Generale des Finances Publiques, and customs authorities. No currency surrender certificate or central-bank approval is needed for the collection of subscription fees from foreign fleet customers.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting with the Eurosystem and the European Central Bank, is the central bank; foreign-exchange administration and balance-of-payments reporting functions are exercised through the French Ministry of Economy and Finance, specifically the Direction Generale du Tresor. Prudential and AML/CFT supervision of credit institutions is conducted by the ACPR (Autorite de Controle Prudentiel et de Resolution) within the Banque de France.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High for operational disbursement rails and receivables collection; Low for capital escrow and commercial LC issuance. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions such as Qonto, Shine, and Treezor, which are ACPR-supervised, can onboard the SAS remotely and provide EUR IBANs, SEPA Instant Credit Transfers, virtual cards, and some SWIFT outbound coverage. They are not credit institutions and therefore cannot provide the deposit-guarantee status, the capital-subscription attestation, or the commercial letter-of-credit facilities expected by a Bank of France branch and foreign investors. An offshore digital EMI that is not established and authorised in the EEA cannot lawfully hold the company's treasury as a French payment account and is highly likely to be rejected by the statutory auditor and the RCS. Stripe Billing, while compatible with the target payment rails, should be used strictly as a merchant collection gateway with automatic daily sweeps to an ACPR-licensed bank; material liquidity must not be parked with a non-credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for capital subscriptions requires a French blocked account at an ACPR-licensed bank or a notary-led deposit arrangement. The shareholder sends EUR via SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA transfers or SEPA Credit Transfer for EEA transfers to the blocked account, with the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' and clear remittance text such as 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - SAS [name] - [RCS pending] - UBO [name]'. The receiving bank verifies the ordering party, the source of funds, sanctions lists, and beneficial ownership; where the deposit is made before RCS registration, the bank or notary issues the deposit certificate required for the company formation file. After the Kbis is issued, the escrowed funds are released to the operational account. For shareholder current-account advances, the purpose code 'LOAN' or the French commercial label 'apport en compte courant d'associe' must be used; the loan agreement must respect the Article 39-6 CGI interest-rate cap on shareholder advances, must be at arm's length, and must not exceed the debtor's distributable-safety thresholds if repaid. All non-EEA inbound receipts equal to or exceeding EUR 10,000 are automatically reported to the Banque de France through the bank's balance-of-payments declaration; no exchange-control approval is required, but the company must retain the share-subscription bank statements, SWIFT UETR, corporate resolutions, and source-of-funds files for five years.For EEA and French disbursements, the primary rail is SEPA Credit Transfer under ISO 20022 pain.001, which covers payroll, hosting fees, telematics data licences, subcontractor fees, and subscription refunds. For non-EEA beneficiaries, the standard rail is SWIFT MT103 carrying a UETR, with exact beneficiary name, IBAN or account number, and a precise purpose text such as 'ROYALTY - TELEMATICS SOFTWARE' or 'CLOUD HOSTING FEES'. Where the SAS procures physical fleet-hardware components from non-EU suppliers in large amounts, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit under UCP 600 issued by BNP, Societe Generale, or CIC and confirmed by the seller's bank is the legally appropriate clearing instrument; at-sight LCs are preferred for high-volume telematics device purchases. Before any outbound transfer to non-cooperative jurisdictions, the company must run restrictive-measures screening against EU, UN, and French Treasury lists and assess Article 238-A CGI non-deductibility and withholding consequences. Any outbound non-EEA transfer of EUR 10,000 or more flows into the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical report and must be supported by contract, commercial invoice, and receipt of service evidence.Deliverable FX spot contracts and outright forwards in EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, and EUR/CHF executed with ACPR-licensed credit institutions under an ISDA or French master agreement, used solely to hedge forecast commercial cash flows.Vanilla European-style FX options in EUR/USD for protection against adverse movements in USD-denominated subscription revenue and vendor payments, with no speculative leverage.Cross-currency interest rate swaps between EUR and USD, subject to EMIR clearing and margining thresholds, portfolio reconciliation, and reporting to an EU-registered trade repository, with documented hedge effectiveness.Non-deliverable forwards in restricted-delivery currencies such as CNY or INR with a permitted credit-institution counterparty, where the SAS can evidence actual commercial exposure in those currencies and where the instrument is not entered for speculative or excessive leverage purposes.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium-High for operational disbursement rails and receivables collection; Low for capital escrow and commercial LC issuance. EEA-licensed electronic money institutions such as Qonto, Shine, and Treezor, which are ACPR-supervised, can onboard the SAS remotely and provide EUR IBANs, SEPA Instant Credit Transfers, virtual cards, and some SWIFT outbound coverage. They are not credit institutions and therefore cannot provide the deposit-guarantee status, the capital-subscription attestation, or the commercial letter-of-credit facilities expected by a Bank of France branch and foreign investors. An offshore digital EMI that is not established and authorised in the EEA cannot lawfully hold the company's treasury as a French payment account and is highly likely to be rejected by the statutory auditor and the RCS. Stripe Billing, while compatible with the target payment rails, should be used strictly as a merchant collection gateway with automatic daily sweeps to an ACPR-licensed bank; material liquidity must not be parked with a non-credit institution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine of 50% of understated tax (for gross negligence) or 80% for abuse of law; criminal penalties up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes if they committed fraud or serious misconduct; travel bans and imprisonment possible.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, penalty of 80% for abuse of law, and potential criminal charges.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly liable for taxes and penalties; may face travel restrictions and criminal prosecution.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 5% of turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned; may be banned from managing companies.
