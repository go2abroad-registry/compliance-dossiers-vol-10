Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

INPI — Guichet Unique des Formalités d'Entreprises (single statutory online filing portal), with final RCS registration and issuance of the Kbis extract performed by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce within the jurisdiction of the company's registered office.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 (Société par Actions Simplifiée regime), read together with Articles L.123-1 et seq. and R.123-1 et seq. (registration with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés); implemented through the INPI Guichet Unique pursuant to Décret n° 2023-160 of 26 February 2023.

Formation / constitutional filings

Formulaire M0 — Déclaration d'immatriculation au Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS), accompanied by signed articles of association (statuts), capital-blocking attestation, legal-notice publication certificate, beneficial-owner declaration (DBE), and supporting identity and domiciliation dossiers.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 16 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital applies to a SAS (Article L.227-1 Code de commerce); capital is freely fixed in the articles, with a nominal low amount customary (e.g., EUR 1 to EUR 1,000). Any cash subscription must be deposited on an escrow suspense account ('compte de dépôt de fonds en attente') opened in the name of the company 'en formation' and remain blocked until RCS registration; the bank's attestation de blocage de fonds must accompany the M0 filing. In-kind contributions must be valued and annexed to the articles or, where statutory thresholds are exceeded, reviewed by a statutory contribution appraiser (commissaire aux apports).

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety — any showroom or boutique premises qualify as ERP type M, 5th category (under 300 m²), requiring a simple fire-prevention visitor's certificate and operational safety measures before opening; storage areas above ICPE thresholds (e.g., large-volume high-racking warehouses) would require a declaration or authorization from the prefecture. Workplace clearance under the Code du travail includes electrical installation verification (bilan de conformité), fire drills, first-aid equipment, and submission of the DUER to the occupational-health service; no civil-defense clearance is required for pure online retail.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies to a SAS (Article L.227-1 Code de commerce); capital is freely fixed in the articles, with a nominal low amount customary (e.g., EUR 1 to EUR 1,000). Any cash subscription must be deposited on an escrow suspense account ('compte de dépôt de fonds en attente') opened in the name of the company 'en formation' and remain blocked until RCS registration; the bank's attestation de blocage de fonds must accompany the M0 filing. In-kind contributions must be valued and annexed to the articles or, where statutory thresholds are exceeded, reviewed by a statutory contribution appraiser (commissaire aux apports).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – impots.gouv.fr; company registration and tax ID procurement via Guichet des Formalités des Entreprises (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must register with the French company formalities portal to obtain a SIREN/SIRET number. Where it has no permanent establishment in France but makes taxable supplies in France, it must separately register for French VAT with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Non-EU entities generally must appoint a tax representative. VAT registration is evidenced by a French intra-community VAT number (FRXXXXXXXXX) and requires filing Form 1542-SD, supported by constitutional documents, foreign VAT status proof, and a description of French transactions.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity must register with the French company formalities portal to obtain a SIREN/SIRET number. Where it has no permanent establishment in France but makes taxable supplies in France, it must separately register for French VAT with the competent Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE). Non-EU entities generally must appoint a tax representative. VAT registration is evidenced by a French intra-community VAT number (FRXXXXXXXXX) and requires filing Form 1542-SD, supported by constitutional documents, foreign VAT status proof, and a description of French transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' election exists in France. An SAS is mandatorily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) and cannot elect pass-through treatment. Certain partnerships may elect for corporate income tax, but an SAS has no such option. Hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD are implemented and do not create elective neutralization mechanisms for an SAS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For an established French business selling goods (e.g., vintage luxury resale), the franchise en base exemption applies below €85,000 annual turnover, with a €93,500 tolerance; mandatory VAT registration is required once turnover exceeds the applicable threshold. A non-established foreign entity has no threshold and must register for French VAT as soon as it carries out any taxable supply in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to non-EU destinations qualify for the French VAT zero rate (exonération) under Article 262-I of the Code Général des Impôts where the goods are sold to an overseas buyer, physically leave French and EU customs territory, and are covered by a customs export declaration. Evidence must be retained proving irrevocable export within generally accepted deadlines (usually 90 days from supply). Intra-EU dispatches are exempt under Article 262-ter if the buyer holds a valid EU VAT number and the supplier files a recapitulative statement; they are not zero-rated for export. Distance sales to EU consumers are not export zero-rated and are instead subject to the OSS scheme where applicable.

  • Customs export declaration (Déclaration en Douane / DAU / ENS with MRN)
  • Transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR)
  • Commercial invoice and underlying sales contract or purchase order
  • Packing list and proof of export provenance
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank statement evidencing payment and FX repatriation

Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is being phased in: from 1 September 2026 for large and medium enterprises and from 1 September 2027 for small enterprises. E-invoices must be transmitted in a structured electronic format via a certified Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) to the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF). E-reporting of transaction data to the DGFiP is mandatory, with API/JSON structured formats specified. No fiscal stamping regime exists; the clearance mechanism relies on near-real-time structured data transmission to the State platform.

Unsubstantiated export zero-rating claims are reclassified by the DGFiP, triggering VAT reassessment plus interest de retard (0.20% per month). Deliberate inaccuracies attract a 40% bad-faith penalty under Article 1729 of the CGI; fraudulent schemes attract an 80% penalty and potential criminal prosecution. Retrospective clawback may extend over the standard 3-year tax limitation period, extended to 10 years in cases of fraud. Directors may also face personal liability and fines for participation in fraudulent zero-rating arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility. France, as a Eurozone member, applies Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union on the free movement of capital and payments and has abolished all exchange controls (Code monétaire et financier Art. L.151-1). The euro (EUR) is the sole legal tender. Current account transactions (commercial settlements, dividends, royalties, interest, commissions) and capital account transactions (equity contributions, shareholder loans, securities, real estate) are fully convertible without prior licensing or rationing. Profit remittance from a French SAS is unrestricted under French corporate law: dividends may be paid following the shareholders' meeting only if the distributable reserves exceed the share capital plus the legal reserve (Code de commerce Art. L.232-11), and the distribution is subject to the dividend withholding tax regime unless treaty relief applies. The remaining operational hurdles are (i) the mandatory intermediation of payments through a licensed credit institution or ACPR-licensed EMI (monopole bancaire) and (ii) statistical reporting to the Banque de France: the intermediary bank is obliged to capture the SAS's cross-border flows for the balance-of-payments survey when any single transaction reaches or exceeds EUR 750,000, and a direct-investment declaration is required where the inbound foreign investment meets the EUR 15,000,000 or 10% voting-rights threshold under the IEF regime. No administrative authorization applies to the profit distribution itself for a non-resident-owned SAS.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable — no surrender regime exists. French and EU law impose no statutory timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds, whether derived from goods (vintage luxury resale) or services. The export proceeds may be freely retained in foreign currency on French or foreign bank accounts, and invoices may be denominated in any freely convertible currency. The only binding obligations are informational: (i) all export settlements must transit through a licensed financial intermediary (bank or, where technically permitted, an ACPR-licensed EMI); (ii) a cross-border flow equal to or exceeding EUR 750,000 in a single transaction (or aggregated flows to the same non-resident counterparty above the Banque de France reporting thresholds) must be captured in the balance-of-payments statistical survey via the intermediary bank; (iii) no exchange-control certificate or licence is required. Foreign-currency accounts (comptes en devises) are freely opened by the SAS with domestic banks, and conversion into EUR may be executed at market rates on a spot or forward basis.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) — central bank of the French Republic and the competent authority for monetary operations, balance-of-payments statistics, and cross-border financial reporting. The foreign exchange administration function is shared with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury, Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty) for foreign direct investment screening (régime IEF) under Articles L.151-1 et seq. of the Code monétaire et financier, and with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the prudential licensing and AML/CFT supervisory authority for credit institutions and Electronic Money Institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: HIGH — with legal constraints. ACPR-licensed or EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) — Qonto, Shine, Treezor (Banking-as-a-Service), Mangopay (marketplace payout), and Wise Business — are fully viable for the operational euro rails of the luxury-resale SAS under Code monétaire et financier Art. L.521-1 et seq. and Art. L.525-4 (safeguarding of e-money in segregated accounts). Legal constraints: (1) the EMI must hold an ACPR licence or a valid EU passport; offshore and non-EEA EMIs cannot onboard the French SAS directly and must operate through a licensed partner bank; (2) an EMI may not open a true escrow account for third-party consignment funds beyond its safeguarded e-money model, and may not grant loans, issue letters of credit, or discount trade bills; (3) Stripe and Escrow_Pay settlement flows must be reconciled daily against the EMI or bank ledger; (4) the treasury policy should cap the e-money float held with any single EMI at EUR 1,000,000, with all excess balances swept automatically to the Tier-1 bank; (5) the recommended dual-rail architecture is a Tier-1 bank for capital/escrow and supplier documentary payments, an EMI for daily SEPA Instant collections and commission payouts, and Escrow_Pay as the buyer-facilitation escrow light intermediary for the platform transactions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Undisclosed or opaque third-party consignors: the bank flags incoming secondhand luxury goods from multiple unrelated suppliers combined with payouts to non-contractual beneficiaries, triggering a trade-based money laundering (TBML) or fraudulent-consignment alert.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

(1) Share capital: the SAS's founders or the parent company subscribe by SEPA Credit Transfer for EU-resident subscribers or SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA subscribers; funds must be credited to a temporary blocked account (compte bloqué) opened in the name of the SAS 'en formation', held with the notary, a bank, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations. The wire's Field 70 (Remittance Information) or the bank's purpose-code registry must bear the OECD/BPM6 purpose code 'EQSH — Equity Shares' or the French balance-of-payments category 'Investissements directs — prises de participation'. The depositary then issues the attestation de dépôt des fonds, which is filed at the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce with the Kbis registration application. (2) Paid-in capital: although the SAS has no statutory minimum, a benchmark of EUR 50,000 is economically advisable to establish a viable merchant-acquiring limit and an acceptable bank risk score. An inbound foreign investment exceeding EUR 15,000,000 in aggregate, or carrying more than 10% of voting rights in a French target with turnover above EUR 25,000,000, requires an IEF declaration to the Direction Générale du Trésor (non-strategic retail sector); a EUR 50,000 injection is exempt. (3) Shareholder loans (comptes courants d'associés): the wire must use the BPM6 purpose code 'LI — Loans between direct investors'; a formal loan agreement must specify principal, EUR interest rate, duration, and repayment schedule, with the interest rate not exceeding the French usury ceiling (Art. L.314-1 C. conso.) to avoid reclassification as a disguised distribution. (4) Verification: each inbound wire must match the remitter against the signed shareholder register, and the beneficiary bank must complete compliance review of the Kbis extract, the UBO declaration, and the board resolution authorizing the capital increase or loan. (5) Any inbound financing from a non-EEA parent must be denominated in EUR, USD, or GBP and is eligible for the treasury ring-fenced liquidity pool, with the intent recorded in the board minutes.Clearing rails: (a) EU supplier payments — SEPA Credit Transfer executed via the ISO 20022 pain.001.001.03 message with purpose code 'GOOD' (purchase/sale of goods), using SEPA Instant (SCT Inst) for same-second settlement up to EUR 100,000 where the supplier's bank is reachable, otherwise standard SEPA CT with same-day or D+1 settlement; (b) non-EU supplier payments — SWIFT MT103 wire transfer routed through the correspondent-bank network (CHIPS or Fedwire for USD, CHAPS for GBP, TARGET2 for EUR), with purpose code 'MRCH' or 'GOOD' and the beneficiary bank identified by SWIFT BIC; (c) high-value or documentary transactions above EUR 100,000 — Commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP 600 via SWIFT MT700, with the French bank as issuing bank and the foreign supplier's bank negotiating against the shipping documents and authentication certificates; (d) customer refunds and card chargebacks — routed through Stripe's card network (Visa/Mastercard) using an explicit refund reference, and via SEPA CT for bank-to-bank refunds; (e) consignor payouts — under the Escrow_Pay model, the escrow provider collects buyer funds and disburses to the SAS upon delivery confirmation, after which the SAS pays each consignor its net commission by SEPA CT on a T+1 basis; the bank requires a monthly consignor-payout register demonstrating the commercial rationale for every beneficiary; (f) all outbound disbursements must be pre-funded in the dedicated operational account to avoid overdraft, and the treasury policy sets a maximum outgoing exposure of EUR 1,000,000 per counterparty per month.FX Outright Forwards — OTC with ACPR-authorized banks, documented under an ISDA 2002 French-law master agreement or the bank's standard dealing terms; maturities up to 12 months; the SAS as an end-user is exempt from EMIR mandatory clearing below the EUR 8 billion gross notional threshold, but every trade must be reported to a registered trade repository (e.g., DTCC Data Repository or UnaVista).Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) — permitted for restricted or exotic currencies (e.g., offshore CNY, KRW, INR) with full cash settlement in EUR or USD at maturity, executed under the bank's standard NDF terms and margin requirements.Cross-Currency Swaps (XCCY) — for matched funding of a USD or GBP working-capital pool, exchanging EUR floating-rate (€STR) against USD SOFR or GBP SONIA on a quarterly basis; generally available for notional amounts from EUR 5,000,000 and used to hedge balance-sheet exposure from the cross-border inventory acquisition pipeline.Vanilla European FX Options (Call/Put) — OTC premium-bearing strategies, including simple or zero-cost collars, to cap adverse EUR/USD and EUR/GBP moves on anticipated receivables; execution requires an ISDA Credit Support Annex (CSA) for margining or an upfront cash-collateral arrangement.FX Spot and Overnight Swap — same-day or T+2 settlement executed through the corporate bank's FX desk under an FX Master Agreement, used for the daily conversion of multi-currency settlement balances into EUR and for managing short-term liquidity in the operational account.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: HIGH — with legal constraints. ACPR-licensed or EU-passported Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) — Qonto, Shine, Treezor (Banking-as-a-Service), Mangopay (marketplace payout), and Wise Business — are fully viable for the operational euro rails of the luxury-resale SAS under Code monétaire et financier Art. L.521-1 et seq. and Art. L.525-4 (safeguarding of e-money in segregated accounts). Legal constraints: (1) the EMI must hold an ACPR licence or a valid EU passport; offshore and non-EEA EMIs cannot onboard the French SAS directly and must operate through a licensed partner bank; (2) an EMI may not open a true escrow account for third-party consignment funds beyond its safeguarded e-money model, and may not grant loans, issue letters of credit, or discount trade bills; (3) Stripe and Escrow_Pay settlement flows must be reconciled daily against the EMI or bank ledger; (4) the treasury policy should cap the e-money float held with any single EMI at EUR 1,000,000, with all excess balances swept automatically to the Tier-1 bank; (5) the recommended dual-rail architecture is a Tier-1 bank for capital/escrow and supplier documentary payments, an EMI for daily SEPA Instant collections and commission payouts, and Escrow_Pay as the buyer-facilitation escrow light intermediary for the platform transactions.
