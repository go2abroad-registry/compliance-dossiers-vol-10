Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – French Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; administrative portal: impots.gouv.fr; French corporate formalities portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS, incorporation through the Guichet Unique automatically generates the SIREN/SIRET numbers and the French intra-Community VAT number (FR followed by a two-digit key and the SIREN) allocated by DGFiP after the business commencement declaration. A foreign non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in France must file a VAT identification request with the DGFiP (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) on form n° 3517-SD (Cerfa 14795), stating the responsible partner / fiscal representative; if the foreign entity is not established in the EU and does not have a reciprocal VAT agreement, a fiscally accredited representative in France is mandatory. Supporting documents include a certificate of incorporation, proof of the foreign equivalent VAT ID, proof of the actual business activity and bank identification, and evidence of French or EU place-of-supply involving French VAT.

A foreign non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in France must file a VAT identification request with the DGFiP (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) on form n° 3517-SD (Cerfa 14795), stating the responsible partner / fiscal representative; if the foreign entity is not established in the EU and does not have a reciprocal VAT agreement, a fiscally accredited representative in France is mandatory. Supporting documents include a certificate of incorporation, proof of the foreign equivalent VAT ID, proof of the actual business activity and bank identification, and evidence of French or EU place-of-supply involving French VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law does not provide a pass-through or check-the-box election for an SAS; entity-level corporate income tax under CGI Articles 206 and 223 onwards applies by default. Available elections are: (i) parent-subsidiary tax consolidation (régime d'intégration fiscale) for at least 95% owned French subsidiaries, renewed for a 5-year period (Art. 223 A CGI); (ii) ministerial-authorization elections for worldwide business profit ('bénéfice mondial') or consolidated profit ('bénéfice consolidé'), which are rarely granted; (iii) no elective hybrid-mismatch adjustment – anti-hybrid rules under Article 212 bis CGI operate mandatorily to deny deductions to the extent of untaxed or double-deducted hybrid arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For established French taxable persons: €91,900 annual turnover for supplies of goods (tolerance up to €101,000) and €36,800 for services including leasing of movable tangible property (tolerance up to €39,100); for non-established foreign suppliers there is no de minimis threshold and VAT registration is compulsory from the first euro of taxable supply in France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT on exports outside the EU under CGI Art. 291(1) where the goods are physically transported out of the EU by the supplier or for the supplier's account within the legal time limits and supported by transport/customs evidence. For services: 0% applies when the place-of-supply rules place the service outside France (e.g., B2B customer established outside the EU) or where the service is directly linked to exported goods and is performed for an exporter; a container leasing arrangement will qualify only if the use and documentation demonstrate a direct, genuine link to the physical export of the container or to the export voyage, with the lessee located outside France or the EU, and all services are performed for business purposes.

  • Customs Export Declaration under EU UCC (Document Administratif Unique / export summary declaration verified by French customs)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent negotiable transport document (sea waybill, CMR, AWB)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 showing the export proceeds credited to the French bank account within the repatriation deadline
  • Commercial invoice and packing list identifying the supplier, customer, goods and lease periods
  • Transport contract, booking confirmation and departure/arrival port records evidencing movement outside the EU customs territory
  • Container lease agreement and container tracking/return data demonstrating cross-border use and physical exportation for FCL container leasing

France has adopted a mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting framework ('facturation électronique') for all established French taxable persons using certified Partner Dematerialization Platforms or the PPF. Deferred dates: 1 September 2026 – all taxable persons must be able to receive structured e-invoices, and large taxpayers must issue e-invoices; 1 September 2027 – SMEs/ETIs must issue e-invoices. There is no real-time tax clearance or digital stamping; e-invoice data and transaction 'e-reporting' are transmitted to DGFiP periodically through API-based platforms in a structured XML/E-invoice format.

The DGFiP and French Customs may audit export transactions and claw back zero-rated VAT where documentary evidence is incomplete or the economic reality contradicts the claimed export. Penalties include monthly late-payment interest of 0.2% (CGI Art. 1727), accuracy/restitution penalties of 10% in good faith, 40% for deliberate inaccuracy, and 80% for abusive/maneuver-based fraud (CGI Art. 1729), plus 5% per month late-filing penalties (CGI Art. 1728) and potential criminal liability for tax evasion (CGI Art. 1741). Claims for refund may be denied and the fiscal representative or management may be held jointly and severally liable in cases of fraudulent export zero-rating schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

As a eurozone member, France maintains full convertibility of the euro for both current account and capital account transactions under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier). Profit remittance by a French SAS to non-resident shareholders is free and does not require prior administrative authorisation, subject to anti-money laundering (AML) checks, tax compliance validation, and the availability of distributable reserves. There are no foreign exchange allocation or rationing mechanisms. Cross-border capital movements above notification thresholds must be reported to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistical purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France does not impose any mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds. As France is part of the eurozone, export receipts may be retained in EUR or held in foreign currency accounts with French authorised credit institutions. There is no statutory timeline or conversion ratio for export proceeds. However, French resident companies must ensure that cross-border payments and receipts are declared by the reporting bank to the Banque de France through the Balance of Payments reporting system (REP) when individual transactions exceed the applicable reporting threshold (generally EUR 10,000 or more, as defined by Banque de France instruction).

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French central bank), with foreign exchange regulatory coordination by the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) within the Ministry of Economy and Finance; banking and payment supervision is exercised by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational payment accounts and collection services, Low for capital escrow and borrowing relationships. Electronic money institutions (EMIs) duly authorised by the ACPR or passporting from another EEA jurisdiction may provide payment accounts and execute SEPA and cross-border wire transfers. However, EMIs generally cannot provide commercial lending, foreign exchange hedging, documentary credits, or capital-escrow services requiring deposit-taking authorisation. Legal constraints: an EMI cannot accept deposits from the public; it may only issue electronic money and provide payment services. Container leasing cash flows are low-risk, which favours EMI onboarding for routine collection, but treasury requirements for liquidity clearing and FX hedging will require a full bank relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from non-resident shareholders must be routed through standard SWIFT wire transfers (MT103) to the designated EUR operational account or capital escrow account maintained with a French licensed bank. For balance-of-payments statistical classification, the remittance purpose should be declared with the harmonised BOP purpose code for direct investment equity capital. The bank will typically request the code 'S110' (equity capital between direct investors) or 'S120' for shareholder loans. The bank must verify the source-of-funds and perform sanctions screening prior to credit. After receipt, the SAS must complete the legal capital increase formalities within the prescribed period, register the share issuances, and file the new statutory documents with the RCS. Under French law, funds received for capital must be blocked in an attorney's or escrow account if the incorporation is not yet effective; post-incorporation, equity capital can be credited directly to the operational account with the capital increase filed at a later date.For international container fleet acquisitions, maintenance payments, and lease-related disbursements, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers and commercial letters of credit (L/C) issued by the French bank in favour of suppliers or lessors. Within the eurozone, SEPA Credit Transfers are mandatory for EUR-denominated payments. For non-EUR trade settlements, the French bank will execute SWIFT wires, confirming available cover and applying FX conversion at the bank's contracted rate. Large container purchases from Asia may involve an irrevocable documentary credit generated in the seller's jurisdiction under UCP 600 and the bank's international trade desk. Acceptable supporting documents include purchase orders, commercial invoices, bills of lading, container quality certificates, and insurance policies. All outbound disbursements must be reported through the Banque de France REP for statistical purposes when crossing the reporting threshold.Deliverable foreign exchange forward contracts (plain vanilla forwards) between EUR and traded currencies, such as USD, CNY, GBP, and HKD, executed with the account bank under ISDA or French master agreement terms.Foreign exchange swaps (swap de change) for short-term management of cross-currency liquidity mismatches between lease collections and fleet disbursements.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-term hedging of lease liabilities or asset financing denominated in foreign currency.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies where delivery is restricted in France, subject to the bank's risk appetite, underlying exposure documentation, and compliance with EU EMIR reporting requirements.Currency options (European-style) when the treasury policy permits option-based hedging, subject to board-approved risk management mandate and ACPR-compliant suitability evaluation.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Qualifying small and medium-sized companies may apply 15% to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, with the balance taxed at 25%, subject to statutory conditions including turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up capital, and at least 75% individual ownership or qualifying ownership.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For dividends paid to a non-resident entity, the domestic rate is generally 25% of the gross distribution, subject to applicable exemptions and tax treaties. Dividends paid to non-resident individuals are generally subject to 12.8% withholding, subject to treaty relief and applicable rules. A qualifying EU parent may be eligible for the domestic parent-subsidiary exemption, subject to statutory conditions, anti-abuse rules, and required formalities.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

File within three months after the close of the financial year. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the statutory filing date is generally the second business day following 1 May. DGFiP generally grants an additional 15 calendar days for electronic filing. IS installments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December, subject to statutory exceptions; the balance is generally payable by the 15th day of the fourth month after year-end, or by 15 May for a 31 December year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million: French transfer-pricing documentation and filing obligations generally apply to a French entity or French permanent establishment meeting the statutory turnover or gross-asset threshold, or belonging to a group meeting the relevant threshold, subject to the statutory related-party conditions. Form 2257-SD is generally required for entities within the relevant reporting scope. Separate country-by-country reporting thresholds and rules apply.

Economic substance scope: France has no general regime equivalent to the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations. French tax authorities may nevertheless challenge arrangements under transfer-pricing rules, the general or specific anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership principles, permanent-establishment rules, and requirements that contractual allocations reflect actual functions, assets, risks, and decision-making. A French container-leasing SAS should retain evidence of personnel, premises, control of leasing and financing risks, asset management, and genuine business decision-making appropriate to its functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax filings

Statutory sanction: Tax surcharges may reach 80%, in addition to tax and interest. Tax fraud may also result in criminal prosecution; under the French Tax Code, penalties can include imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000, with higher penalties possible in aggravated cases, subject to the applicable statutory provisions.

Director personal liability: A director is not automatically liable for company tax debts solely by holding office. Personal criminal liability may arise from the director’s own participation or complicity. Statutory tax-debt liability may be imposed in specified cases, including fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated breaches that make collection impossible. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable profits and impose tax, late-payment interest, and applicable surcharges. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation may attract a penalty generally subject to a statutory minimum and a maximum of 5% of the relevant transfer-pricing adjustments, with the applicable amount determined under the French Tax Code.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal exposure for their own fraud or criminal conduct and, in specified statutory circumstances, for company tax liabilities. Ordinary documentation failure does not by itself pierce the corporate veil.
Undisclosed permanent establishment or mischaracterized French activity

Statutory sanction: The authority may assess French tax on attributable profits, interest, surcharges, and applicable filing penalties. Criminal sanctions require conduct meeting the relevant criminal-law elements and are not automatic consequences of a classification dispute.

Director personal liability: Personal liability is not automatic; it may arise from individual participation in fraud or under specific statutory rules concerning recovery of tax debts.
Distribution of unlawful dividends or concealed profit extraction

Statutory sanction: The distribution may be recharacterized for tax purposes, deductions may be denied, and tax, interest, and surcharges may apply. Corporate-law remedies may include repayment of unlawful distributions and director liability under applicable company-law rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for breaches of law, the company’s articles, or management duties. Criminal liability depends on the specific offense and the director’s participation; imprisonment and travel bans are not automatic.
Money laundering and sanctions evasion

Statutory sanction: French criminal penalties depend on the offense and facts; money laundering can carry imprisonment and substantial fines, with enhanced penalties for aggravating circumstances. Asset freezing, confiscation, and other sanctions may also apply.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly participate may be prosecuted personally. Corporate office alone does not create criminal liability; civil restitution, imprisonment, and travel restrictions require an applicable legal basis and competent-authority process.
