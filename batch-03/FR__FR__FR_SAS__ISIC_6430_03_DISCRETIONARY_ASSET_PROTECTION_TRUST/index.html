Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Administrative domiciliation by a registered domiciliation company is expressly permitted for a French SAS under Article L.123-11-1 of the French Commercial Code and may satisfy the initial registration address. For a high-risk discretionary trust administration business under ISIC 6430, a purely virtual office is not acceptable as a final operational substance model. French regulators and the licensing authority expect a genuine establishment in France, including a fixed office, qualified local management, physical or securely accessible records, a resident compliance officer, and documented management autonomy in respect of the trust assets for which the company acts as fiduciary administrator.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area jurisdiction with full current-account convertibility and no general exchange-control approval for ordinary commercial payments, dividend distributions, interest payments, service fees, or capital repatriation. Capital movements are generally liberalized under European Union law and French monetary and financial law. Banks may require evidence of the legal basis, beneficial ownership, source and destination of funds, tax compliance, underlying contracts, and corporate approvals. Outbound distributions remain subject to French corporate-law solvency and distributable-profit rules, withholding-tax analysis, transfer-pricing requirements, anti-abuse provisions, beneficial-ownership verification, and applicable treaty or European Union relief conditions. A French SAS administering irrevocable offshore asset-protection trusts presents elevated scrutiny because trustees, settlors, protectors, beneficiaries, and controlling persons may create complex beneficial-ownership and tax-transparency issues. The proposed activity must not involve unauthorized payment services, deposit-taking, portfolio management, fiduciary activities reserved to regulated professionals, concealment of assets, evasion of French trust reporting, or circumvention of sanctions and anti-money-laundering controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the central bank and no mandatory conversion ratio or universal repatriation deadline for export receipts. Export proceeds may generally remain in euros or foreign currency, subject to the contractual banking arrangements and ordinary accounting, tax, customs, and anti-money-laundering controls. The company must retain commercial invoices, customs evidence, transport documentation, payment records, and proof of the underlying transaction. Foreign-currency balances and transactions must be recorded and translated in accordance with French accounting rules. Banks may require transaction-purpose information and may apply internal monitoring or reporting procedures. Sector-specific rules, sanctions restrictions, cash-reporting requirements, and statistical reporting obligations can apply. No export-receipt rule should be treated as permitting the company to hold or move client trust assets through its own operating account.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the European Central Bank; foreign-exchange and customs reporting may also involve the French Ministry for the Economy and Finance, the Directorate-General of Customs and Indirect Taxes (DGDDI), and the Directorate-General of the Treasury.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for ordinary corporate treasury and Low for discretionary offshore trust administration. An offshore digital electronic-money institution cannot be assumed to provide lawful French deposit-taking, payment services, safeguarding, fiduciary custody, or client-money services. The provider must be authorized under the applicable European Union regime, passported or otherwise permitted to serve France, and capable of supporting the relevant currencies, sanctions screening, safeguarding model, account nomenclature, and regulatory reporting. An EMI account is not a bank deposit and generally cannot replace a regulated client-money, escrow, trust, or custody account. Use of an offshore EMI may trigger enhanced scrutiny, transaction delays, account closure, and inability to evidence segregation of trust assets. Payment initiation, money remittance, account information, custody, investment, or asset-management functions may require separate authorization. The SAS should use a regulated French or European banking institution for core operating, capital, and any legally required segregated accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder equity through a French or European regulated bank into the SAS corporate account or a transaction-specific capital account, using SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments and SWIFT cross-border payment messaging for non-SEPA currencies. The remittance package should include the subscription agreement, shareholder or board resolutions, capital increase documentation, bank-origin evidence, ultimate-beneficial-owner data, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth evidence, tax-residence information, and the correct payment narrative. The receiving bank should confirm whether any Banque de France statistical reporting, foreign-direct-investment reporting, or other regulatory filing is required; reporting thresholds and classifications must be assessed transaction by transaction. Shareholder loans require a written agreement, arm's-length terms, transfer-pricing analysis, withholding-tax review, and accounting treatment. Do not route trust or beneficiary assets as paid-in capital, shareholder loans, revenue, or intercompany funds. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €50 million annual turnover threshold triggers mandatory Local File and Master File documentation

Economic substance scope: France implements EU Economic Substance Directive; ESR applies to holding, IP holding, finance, and certain service entities; requires adequate substance in France including qualified employees, core income-generating activities, and physical presence

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to 80% of evaded tax, imprisonment up to 5 years, and full restitution of unpaid taxes plus interest

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability, fines, and possible imprisonment; corporate veil may be pierced leading to personal restitution obligations
Shell Company / Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, denial of all tax benefits, penalties up to 500% of tax advantage obtained, and potential criminal sanctions

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for damages and possible disqualification from management positions
AML / Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €1 million or 5% of annual turnover, criminal prosecution, and revocation of trust administration license

Director personal liability: Individual directors may face personal fines, imprisonment for willful non-compliance, and travel bans
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: TP adjustments increasing taxable base, penalties of 80% of understated tax, daily interest, and potential criminal referral

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or ratified non-compliant arrangements face personal liability for additional taxes and fines
