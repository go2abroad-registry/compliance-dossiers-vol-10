Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) operated by INPI — formalities submitted through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

Name reservation / lock period

French law does not provide for a separate company-name reservation procedure; the denomination is cleared and frozen only when the full incorporation file is submitted through the Guichet Unique and accepted for registration at the RNE. If the RNE registrar rejects the denomination as identical or confusingly similar to a prior registration or protected trademark, the applicant must refile a corrected dossier through the same portal. No statutory extension of a reservation is available because no reservation is granted. The rejection of the incorporation filing can be challenged before the competent tribunal judiciaire (commercial chamber) under the general civil procedure rules and Code de commerce Articles R.123-4 et seq.; a third party claiming prior trademark rights may bring injunctive relief under Code de la propriété intellectuelle Articles L.713-2 et seq. and L.716-4-6, and an unfair competition action (concurrence déloyale) may be initiated before the commercial court. A formal appeal against an administrative refusal by INPI can be filed with the administrative court (tribunal administratif) within two months of notification.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), including the Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE) and, for non-established foreign businesses, the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). The principal digital portal is impots.gouv.fr, with business tax administration conducted through the espace professionnel.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS obtains its SIREN and SIRET identifiers through registration with the competent business formalities authority via the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). Following registration, the entity is assigned a French tax account by the DGFiP and applies for a VAT identification number through the competent SIE, generally using the registration and tax-account information supplied in the incorporation and tax-registration filings. The VAT number is validated through the VIES system for intra-Community transactions. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the SIEE or other competent DGFiP service using the applicable foreign-business registration process, supplies constitutional documents, proof of business activity, identification of representatives, bank and customer information where requested, and appoints a French fiscal representative only where required under the applicable EU or non-EU rules. Registration is required before conducting transactions for which French VAT is due or before making intra-Community acquisitions or supplies requiring identification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A qualifying SAS incorporated for fewer than five years may elect temporarily for income-tax treatment under the French pass-through regime, subject to statutory conditions concerning size, ownership, activity, and shareholder composition; the election is limited in duration and must be exercised with the DGFiP within the prescribed period. A qualifying family-owned SAS may also elect for the family-company partnership regime where the statutory requirements are met. France does not provide a general United States-style check-the-box election. French anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directives and can deny deductions, impose inclusion, or otherwise neutralize arrangements involving hybrid instruments, entities, or imported mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business, VAT registration is generally required when it carries out taxable transactions outside the applicable franchise en base de TVA. For services, the principal franchise thresholds are EUR 37,500 for prior-year turnover and EUR 41,250 for current-year turnover; for goods, accommodation, and food sales, the principal thresholds are EUR 85,000 and EUR 93,500 respectively. Threshold rules and amounts are subject to legislative change. Voluntary registration is permitted, and registration may be required irrespective of turnover for intra-Community transactions, imports, exports, reverse-charge transactions, or transactions for which the business elects to charge VAT. A French SAS providing professional legal services should obtain a VAT number where its transaction profile requires it, even if a small-business exemption might otherwise be available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT where the supplier, or an authorized intermediary acting for the supplier, can substantiate that the goods physically left the EU customs territory and satisfy the statutory export conditions. The supplier must retain customs evidence and link the export movement to the invoice and accounting records. Intra-Community supplies of goods may be exempt where the customer is VAT-identified in another EU Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier holds the required VAT number and transport evidence. For cross-border legal and litigation services, the result is generally determined by the place-of-supply rules rather than by a conventional export zero rate: B2B services supplied to a customer established outside France are generally located where the customer is established and are invoiced without French VAT, subject to evidence of customer status and applicable reverse-charge rules. B2C services, services connected with French proceedings or immovable property, and other special-category services may remain subject to French VAT.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and export accompanying or exit confirmation, including the Movement Reference Number where applicable
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and VAT exemption basis
  • Transport evidence, such as bill of lading, airway bill, CMR consignment note, courier proof of delivery, or equivalent carrier documentation
  • Packing list and, where relevant, import clearance or destination-country customs evidence
  • Proof linking the goods, invoice, customs declaration, and accounting records

France does not currently operate a universal real-time invoice-clearance system for ordinary domestic business-to-business transactions. Mandatory electronic invoicing is being phased in under the French Finance Act framework: all French-established businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance obligations apply from 1 September 2026 to large and mid-sized businesses and from 1 September 2027 to small and micro-enterprises not otherwise covered earlier. Invoices must pass through an approved private platform or the public invoicing platform designated by the applicable framework, with structured data transmission and e-reporting for specified cross-border and business-to-consumer transactions. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. VAT invoices must contain the statutory particulars, including supplier and customer identity, VAT numbers where required, invoice date and number, taxable amount, VAT rate or exemption basis, and reverse-charge wording where applicable. There is no general digital tax stamp requirement for ordinary invoices.

The DGFiP may deny the VAT exemption or refund and reassess VAT where the supplier cannot prove export, intra-Community transport, customer VAT status, or the applicable place of supply. Reassessed VAT may include late-payment interest and tax penalties, commonly 5% for certain late-payment situations, 10% for late filing or payment, 40% for deliberate failure or serious non-compliance, and up to 80% for fraudulent maneuvers or abuse of rights, subject to the facts and statutory procedures. Incorrect invoices may trigger VAT liability, correction obligations, and refusal of the customer's deduction. Fraudulent customs declarations may result in customs duties and taxes, customs fines, seizure or confiscation, and criminal prosecution. False documentation, fictitious exports, or knowingly abusive VAT refund claims may also create personal criminal exposure for responsible officers and may lead to recovery of improperly refunded amounts.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under a fully convertible Eurozone regime. Capital account transactions are highly convertible with minimal regulatory approvals. Current account transactions, including profit remittances and dividends, face no convertibility barriers beyond standard tax compliance, beneficial ownership verification, and SIREN/SIRET reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion requirements for export proceeds under EU directives. Exporters must file customs declarations and tax returns within 30 days of receipt. Free convertibility applies; ratios are market-driven with no statutory mandates.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax rate; an additional 3.3% social contribution on corporate income tax may apply to companies meeting the statutory turnover threshold, producing an effective rate of approximately 25.83% on the relevant tax amount.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax is generally imposed on dividends and other distributed income paid by a French company to a non-resident shareholder, subject to domestic exemptions and treaty relief. A qualifying EU parent may obtain a 0% exemption under the French implementation of the Parent-Subsidiary Directive, generally requiring the minimum participation, holding-period, legal-form, subject-to-tax, and anti-abuse conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the return is generally due on the second business day following 1 May of the following year. The final corporate income tax balance is generally due by the fifteenth day of the fourth month following year-end, subject to the specific statutory calendar and electronic filing rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Corporate income tax instalments are generally due on the fifteenth day of the relevant months in February, May, August, and November. The final balance is generally due by the fifteenth day of the fourth month following the financial year-end, with special timing for 31 December year-ends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million threshold for the principal French master-file and local-file documentation obligation, generally based on the French entity's turnover or gross assets, or on specified group and controlled-entity circumstances under French law. A separate annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration generally applies to taxpayers meeting the lower EUR 50 million threshold or other statutory group conditions. The precise filing obligation must be tested against the entity's ownership, group, and transaction profile.

Economic substance scope: France has no single standalone economic substance regulation equivalent to a dedicated offshore ESR statute. Substance is enforced through transfer-pricing rules, the beneficial-ownership requirement, the general anti-abuse rule, the principal-purpose test in treaties, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, permanent-establishment principles, abuse-of-law doctrine, and business-purpose requirements. The SAS should maintain French decision-making capacity, appropriate personnel or outsourced operational capability, premises or operational infrastructure proportionate to its activities, contemporaneous records, client and matter files, bank-account control, genuine litigation services, and commercially supportable intercompany arrangements. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, 40% or 80% tax penalties depending on conduct, and potential criminal tax-fraud sanctions including imprisonment and substantial fines. Criminal consequences depend on the facts, intent, amount, and prosecution decision.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face personal criminal liability, civil tax liability for fraudulent or seriously negligent conduct, asset recovery, director disqualification, and possible restrictions on leaving France during criminal proceedings. Imprisonment is possible for aggravated tax fraud; a corporate veil is not a protection against personal participation in the offense.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions, recharacterization of income, withholding-tax assessments, penalties for inadequate or missing documentation, and possible penalties based on transaction amounts or corrected taxable income. The French tax authority may apply the arm's-length principle and assess interest and aggravated penalties where intentional evasion is established.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil liability for tax losses caused by intentional misconduct or serious management errors and criminal liability where transfer-pricing manipulation forms part of fraudulent tax evasion. Individual exposure is heightened where directors approved false records or knowingly implemented sham arrangements.
Shell company, artificial substance, or treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, withholding-tax reassessment, application of the principal-purpose test or abuse-of-law doctrine, corporate tax reassessment, interest, penalties, and possible permanent-establishment or transfer-pricing adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil liability, criminal exposure for fraudulent declarations or money laundering where applicable, director disqualification, asset-freezing measures, and travel restrictions during investigations. Imprisonment may arise from associated fraud or laundering offenses.
Unauthorized practice of the legal profession

Statutory sanction: Criminal and professional sanctions may apply for unauthorized practice, including fines, injunctions, prohibition from continuing the activity, invalidity or unenforceability risks for certain arrangements, and referral to competent professional or judicial authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors and individuals personally presenting themselves as French-qualified lawyers may face direct criminal liability, civil claims from clients, professional bans, damages, and potential imprisonment where the conduct constitutes a criminal offense. Limited liability does not shield the individual perpetrator.
Client-money, IOLTA, and escrow misuse

Statutory sanction: Restitution, civil damages, professional disciplinary action, account restrictions, regulatory reporting, and potential criminal prosecution for breach of trust, fraud, or money laundering. The exact sanction depends on whether the SAS is a regulated avocat structure and on the source and use of the funds.

Director personal liability: Directors, signatories, lawyers, and de facto managers may be personally liable for restitution and damages and may face criminal prosecution, professional disqualification, asset seizure, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for embezzlement, breach of trust, or laundering.
Anti-money-laundering and terrorist-financing failure

Statutory sanction: Regulatory enforcement, fines, supervisory measures, account restrictions, professional discipline, confiscation, and criminal money-laundering or terrorist-financing sanctions. Suspicious transactions must be reported to TRACFIN where the entity is within the relevant regulated-profession perimeter.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation, facilitation, concealment, or serious compliance failures, together with asset confiscation, director bans, travel restrictions, and imprisonment under the applicable criminal provisions.
Fictitious invoices, VAT fraud, and sham legal services

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, denial of input VAT, corporate tax adjustments, 40% or 80% penalties, late-payment interest, invoice-related fines, and criminal fraud or false-document sanctions where intentional conduct is proven.

Director personal liability: Individuals who authorize or knowingly use false invoices may be personally prosecuted for tax fraud, forgery, fraud, or money laundering and may face restitution orders, asset seizure, travel restrictions, director disqualification, and imprisonment.
Bribery, influence peddling, and judicial corruption

Statutory sanction: Severe criminal fines, confiscation, exclusion from public contracts, corporate compliance obligations, dissolution or prohibition measures in extreme cases, and imprisonment under French anti-corruption and criminal law.

Director personal liability: Directors, employees, lawyers, and intermediaries may be personally prosecuted regardless of whether the payment was booked as a fee, success fee, commission, donation, or client expense. Personal fines, confiscation, disqualification, travel restrictions, and imprisonment may apply.
Failure to maintain statutory books and beneficial-owner information

Statutory sanction: Accounting sanctions, tax reassessment, penalties for inaccurate corporate filings, beneficial-owner compliance enforcement, audit adjustments, and potential criminal consequences for false statements or concealment.

Director personal liability: The president and de facto managers may face personal civil and criminal liability, including damages, disqualification from management, asset seizure, travel restrictions, and imprisonment where false statements, fraud, or laundering is established.
