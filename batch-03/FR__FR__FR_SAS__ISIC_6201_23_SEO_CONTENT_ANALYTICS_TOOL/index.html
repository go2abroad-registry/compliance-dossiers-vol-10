Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry) keeping the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS); electronic filings are submitted via the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises operated by INPI at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr and are routed to the competent registry based on the registered office location.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title II, Chapter VII — Articles L.227-1 to L.227-32 (Société par Actions Simplifiée regime); Articles L.225-127 to L.225-144 (share subscription and capital payment rules applicable via Article L.227-2); Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10 (general formation provisions); Decree No. 2007-431 of 25 March 2007; Articles R.123-1 and R.123-30 et seq. (RCS immatriculation).

Formation / constitutional filings

Dossier unique de demande d'immatriculation (single-window creation file) composed of the executed Statuts (Articles of Association), attestation de dépôt des fonds (capital deposit certificate issued by a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts), attestation de parution d'un avis de constitution au Journal d'Annonces Légales, the declaration of beneficial ownership (bénéficiaires effectifs), and the lease or attestation of the registered office (attestation de jouissance).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 17 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum statutory capital for an SAS; the articles set the capital freely. The full capital must be subscribed before registration and at least 50% of the nominal value of cash contributions must be paid up at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years (Article L.225-129 alt. via L.227-2). Cash must be escrowed in a blocked account opened in the company's name at a French bank, Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, or a notary's account; the deposit certificate is filed with the RCS and funds are released only upon registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: for private office premises not open to the public, comply with the Code du travail workplace safety provisions (Articles R.4227-1 to R.4227-50 — extinguishers, alarm and evacuation procedures). If part of the premises is open to the public (ERP 5th category), a declaration to the local Mairie and a fire safety commission visit are required before occupancy. Occupational clearance: draft and register the Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP), arrange workplace medical fitness visits for employees, obtain the lessor's structural load confirmation, and verify electrical installation compliance certificates for the leased office; employer registration with URSSAF is mandatory once staff are hired.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum statutory capital for an SAS; the articles set the capital freely. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) via the Impôts.gouv.fr professional portal

E-government filing portal

A French SAS obtains its statutory identity numbers (SIREN, SIRET, and registration with the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés) through the statutory formalities portal. The entities.gouv.fr / formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr single window files the incorporation dossier, which is transmitted to the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) and the Commercial Court Registry. VAT identification (numéro d'identification à la TVA) is subsequently issued by the DGFiP upon confirmation of the RCS registration. A foreign entity not yet established in France but making taxable supplies must apply directly to the DGFiP Service des Impôts des Entreprises Étrangères (SIEE) using the standard VAT registration form, appointing a tax representative (représentant fiscal) if required by Article 289 CGI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law does not provide a check-the-box election. An SAS is automatically subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) on its worldwide profits if its place of effective management is in France. Available statutory elections include: (i) group tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for French-resident parent companies holding at least 95% of subsidiaries; (ii) election to apply a reduced corporate income tax rate for small-company profits under Article 219 CGI; (iii) option for non-resident-company permanent establishments to file separate French branch accounts; and (iv) anti-hybrid elections and notifications required under Article 212 bis CGI as transposing ATAD rules on hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available under Article 262-I-1° CGI for goods physically transported outside the European Union by the seller or on its behalf, provided the exporter holds evidence of the transport and the customs export declaration. Services qualify for zero-rating under Article 259-1 CGI where they are B2B services supplied to a customer established outside the EU, including software-as-a-service, data analytics, and SEO content analytics tools, provided the services are not physically performed in France. The supplier must retain documentary proof of customer identity, non-EU establishment, and the reality of the cross-border supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document issued through DELTA/ECS)
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill evidencing departure from France
  • Commercial Invoice and export sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence from the non-EU customer
  • Récapitulatif d'expédition / Export Summary Listing (Article 302 CGI supporting records)

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating exposes the taxable person to retrospective VAT assessment, late-payment interest equal to the ECB rate plus 5 percentage points (or 0.20% per month), a 40% negligence penalty, an 80% bad-faith penalty, and criminal sanctions for customs and tax fraud (Article 1741 CGI, Customs Code Article 414). The DGFiP may disallow the exemption if evidence of export is not produced within a reasonable deadline, typically 180 days, and may claw back the exempt VAT plus penalties and interest. Negligence in substantiation that results in an under-declaration may also trigger an ordinary tax audit with an extended three-year or six-year statute of limitations in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates a fully liberalized foreign exchange regime aligned with EU free movement of capital principles. Current account transactions, including profit remittances to non-resident shareholders, are freely convertible without restriction. Capital account transactions by French residents require mandatory declaration to the Banque de France for amounts exceeding €100,000 per transaction or €1 million annually. Profit remittances are unrestricted for EU-established entities provided full tax compliance, beneficial ownership verification, and AML/KYC standards are satisfied.

Export proceeds surrender rules

French exporters are not subject to mandatory surrender or central bank conversion of export proceeds. Repatriation is permitted freely, with proceeds retainable offshore or returned to France. All export transactions above €15,000 require customs declaration; FX transactions above applicable thresholds must be reported to the Banque de France for statistical purposes only.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France
