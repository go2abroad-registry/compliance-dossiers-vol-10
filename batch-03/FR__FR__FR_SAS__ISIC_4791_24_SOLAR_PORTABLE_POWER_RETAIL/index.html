Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – primary tax authority and taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; customs and export excise administration: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) – portal: douane.gouv.fr (DELTA/GUN e-customs clearance system)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must (i) register the SAS with the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE) via the Institut National de la Statistique et des Études Économiques (INSEE) to obtain the obligatory identification numbers SIREN/SIRET; (ii) obtain the French VAT intra-Community number ('numéro TVA intracommunautaire' = FR + two-digit automated key + SIREN) by filing form CERFA for VAT registration with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) having territorial jurisdiction, or, where no French fixed establishment is created, with the DGFiP non-resident taxation unit (Service des impôts des particuliers non-résidents / unité TVA des non-résidents); (iii) alternatively, a non-established EU or non-EU e-commerce seller may cover qualifying distance sales and imports of consignments not exceeding EUR 150 through the Union One-Stop-Shop (OSS) or Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) regimes, thereby avoiding a full French VAT registration; (iv) for intra-Community zero-rated supplies, the SAS must present the customer's valid EU VAT identification number and file the EC Sales List (état récapitulatif de chiffre d'affaires) in the VAT return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation – the Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) is compulsorily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at its own statutory entity level; headline IS rate 25% for fiscal years opened from 1 January 2022, with the reduced 15% rate applicable to the first EUR 42,500 of annual taxable profit for eligible SME taxpayers (turnover below EUR 10,000,000 and paid-up capital held at 75% by individuals); no automatic pass-through is permitted for an SAS.

Available election forms

France does not apply a US-style 'check-the-box' system; however, (i) a small SAS satisfying the statutory conditions (no more than five shareholders, at least 50% of rights held by individuals, turnover ≤ EUR 10,000,000) may elect for partnership-class fiscal transparency for a maximum of five fiscal years under Article 239 bis AA of the Code Général des Impôts (CGI), with the election filed with the DGFiP before the end of the opening fiscal year; (ii) transparent partnerships may conversely elect corporate taxation under Article 206-3 CGI; (iii) hybrid entity mismatches are neutralized by the French hybrid mismatch rules transposing EU ATAD (Articles 209 VIII, 210, 238 bis CGI), and a foreign hybrid branch/entity is treated as French-taxable only to the extent its profits are not deductible elsewhere; (iv) group consolidation (intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI is available as a statutory election to a 95% parent for income tax pooling of French subsidiaries.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France applies no domestic 'small trader' franchise threshold to non-established sellers: a non-established taxable person must register from the first taxable transaction or apply the IOSS for imported consignments ≤ EUR 150 / Union OSS for remote B2C supplies. For taxable persons established in another EU Member State, French VAT accounting is triggered by the EU-wide EUR 10,000 annual distance-sales threshold (intra-Community distance sales of goods and B2C electronically supplied services) once exceeded in France. Domestic 'franchise en base' thresholds (EUR 85,000 for sales of goods / EUR 85,000 for retail distance sales where the franchise has been reformed; EUR 37,500 for services) apply only to French-established small businesses and are not relevant to an SAS carrying on commercial e-commerce.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (exonération) is available strictly for (i) direct exports of tangible goods physically shipped or transported by the seller or its forwarding agent to a destination outside the European Union, under Article 262-I CGI, subject to proof of physical removal from EU customs territory within the statutory time limits; (ii) intra-Community supplies of goods dispatched to a customer identified with a valid EU VAT number and taxable in another Member State, under Article 262 ter-I CGI (subject to EC Sales List filing and the 'assujetti revendeur' rules); and (iii) services directly connected to exported movable goods (transport, handling, carriage and ancillary services) treated as territoriality-excluded under Articles 259-A/B CGI. Solar portable power equipment sold via the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS): imported consignments ≤ EUR 150 benefit from a VAT-remitted-at-import flow, the seller collecting French-appropriate import VAT in the IOSS return and no separate export zero-rating being claimed at dispatch; above EUR 150, normal customs import VAT and the use of the free circulation customs declaration apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Exportation – DAE) lodged electronically in the DELTA/GUN system, including export customs procedure code and destination country
  • Commercial invoice issued under Article 242 nonies A CGI with mandatory VAT details
  • Bill of Lading (B/L), CMR consignment note, Air Waybill (AWB), or postal/courier shipment proof evidencing physical dispatch from France/EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment proof (attestation de rapatriement des recettes d'exportation) evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a bank
  • Packing list, transport insurance certificate, and, where applicable, the customer's valid intra-Community VAT number plus the EC Sales List and Intrastat supplementary declarations

France is implementing the statutory e-invoicing/e-reporting reform (Finance Act 2023, end-of-2022 ordonnance): from 1 September 2026 all B2B transactions involving large and mid-sized French taxable persons must issue structured e-invoices (Factur-X/CII/UBL or EDI) cleared through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a Partner Dematerialisation Platform (PDP) delivering validation and data transmission to DGFiP; from 1 September 2027 the obligation extends to SMEs and micro enterprises. B2C and cross-border sales require e-reporting (données de transaction) to the PPF in near-real time via API, including the customer's country, payment details and VAT accounting codes. No blockchain-style digital stamping is used; compliance relies on structured-data electronic clearance, digital signature integrity, and archiving of invoice datasets for a 6-year period. For B2C e-commerce of portable power products, the VAT invoice/credit note dataset (invoice number, issue date, supplier/customer identifiers, VAT rate, VAT amount, payment method) must be transmitted continuously.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to joint DGFiP/DGDDI audit scrutiny: if documentary evidence of physical exportation or intra-Community dispatch is insufficient, the exempt VAT is reversed ('rappels de TVA'), with monthly late-payment interest of 0.20% (intérêt de retard, Article 1727 CGI) and the full statutory penalty regime: 10% for untimely filing (Article 1728), 40% for intentional inaccuracy (Article 1729-a), 80% for corrective dissimulation or fraudulent activity (Article 1729-b) or for invocation of fraud/abus de droit; false or fictitious export declarations also trigger criminal customs fines and penalties under Articles 413 quinquies and 414 of the Code des Douanes (exclusion from export privileges, amende of up to EUR 50,000 and imprisonment up to three years, confiscation of goods and means of transport); the retroactive clawback period is the standard 3-year tax reprise period (Article L. 176 of the Livre des Procédures Fiscales and Article 354 of the Code des Douanes), extended to six years in cases of undisclosed EU or third-country arrangements, and increased export refund/restitution claims may be prosecuted as customs fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible on current account and capital account under Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Articles L151-1 et seq. of the Code monétaire et financier. France maintains no exchange control approval for inbound or outbound remittances for a French-resident SAS. Practical hurdles are limited to (i) statistical balance-of-payments declarations to the Banque de France for cross-border transactions above EUR 750,000; (ii) customs declarations for physical movements of cash or equivalents of EUR 10,000 or more; and (iii) dividend withholding tax of 25% on outbound profit distributions under Article 119-bis of the French General Tax Code, reduced or eliminated under applicable double tax treaties or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (participation of at least 5% held for 24 months). No central bank 'fair return' or capital authorization certificate applies to ordinary equity injections or trade-related payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France imposes no mandatory surrender, no compulsory conversion ceiling, and no statutory repatriation deadline on export proceeds. Export contracts may be invoiced and settled in EUR or any freely negotiable currency, and foreign-currency receipts may be retained indefinitely in French bank accounts. All commercial and financial transactions between a French resident and a non-resident exceeding EUR 750,000 must be reported statistically to the Banque de France through the presenting bank, using the official balance-of-payments nomenclature; cash movements of means of payment equal to or exceeding EUR 10,000 must be declared to customs at the border.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (French central bank operating within the Eurosystem of the European Central Bank); prudential supervision and AML/CFT licensing are performed by the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); balance-of-payments statistics and financial relations with foreign countries are administered by the Banque de France and the Direction Générale du Trésor (DG Trésor) under the Code monétaire et financier.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-to-High for ACPR-licensed French electronic money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, MANSA) that offer SEPA/Instant SEPA, SWIFT multi-currency accounts, and remote onboarding; viability is conditional on their inability to provide escrow blocking, documentary trade finance, bilateral FX/derivatives credit lines, or complex treasury architecture, hence a Tier-1 bank relationship remains the anchor. UK-based and offshore EMIs lost EU passporting rights after Brexit; they may operate only through an EU-licensed branch, under PSD2 'agency' arrangements, or on a restricted 'reverse solicitation' basis, and they should be limited to ancillary collection/payout flows rather than primary treasury settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The shareholder capital contribution is routed by SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated remittances or by SWIFT MT103 with the mandatory Unique End-to-end Transaction Reference (UETR) for non-EUR transfers, using the Banque de France balance-of-payments purpose code for direct investment (code 'EC', apport en capital) and an unambiguous beneficiary reference citing 'FR_SAS libération du capital social'. The French receiving bank must be a licensed credit institution; funds are first credited to a blocked capital escrow account (compte d'apport), held until the constitutional or capital-increase formalities are completed, then released to the operational account upon presentation of the notary certificate of deposit and the updated K-bis extract. For the SAS, the capital amount is freely set in the articles and cash contributions must be released in accordance with the statutory regime applicable to simplified joint-stock companies. Any single transaction at or above EUR 750,000 must be covered by the Banque de France statistical declaration submitted through the presenting bank; the bank also verifies the source of funds and the tax residence of the contributor for French withholding reporting.Disbursements for the procurement of portable battery generators and solar panels, predominantly from Asian manufacturers, are executed through (i) SEPA Credit Transfer or Instant SEPA for EUR payments up to EUR 100,000; (ii) SWIFT MT103 for international wire transfers in USD, EUR, or CNY with the correct Banque de France balance-of-payments code for imports of goods and the underlying commercial invoice attached; (iii) documentary irrevocable Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707 in accordance with UCP 600) for high-value or credit-based orders, issued or confirmed by the French bank with the supplier's bank as advising/negotiating bank and documents comprising commercial invoice, packing list, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, and inspection certificates; (iv) documentary remittance (documents against payment / documents against acceptance) for lower-risk, low-value repeating suppliers; and (v) direct RMB disbursements through the French bank's cross-border RMB clearing arrangement or CIPS-correspondent channel provided the beneficiary maintains an onshore RMB account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: Medium-to-High for ACPR-licensed French electronic money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, MANSA) that offer SEPA/Instant SEPA, SWIFT multi-currency accounts, and remote onboarding; viability is conditional on their inability to provide escrow blocking, documentary trade finance, bilateral FX/derivatives credit lines, or complex treasury architecture, hence a Tier-1 bank relationship remains the anchor. UK-based and offshore EMIs lost EU passporting rights after Brexit; they may operate only through an EU-licensed branch, under PSD2 'agency' arrangements, or on a restricted 'reverse solicitation' basis, and they should be limited to ancillary collection/payout flows rather than primary treasury settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400,000,000 — full Master File and Local File are compulsory where the French company or the consolidated group reaches this annual turnover threshold; a simplified transfer pricing declaration (Form 2257-SD) is required for every French entity with cross-border related-party transactions of at least EUR 100,000 per year; a Country-by-Country Report is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: France enforces economic substance through the general anti-abuse rule (abus de droit, Article L. 64 LPF), the sham-entity doctrine (société fictive) and the concept of gestion de fait; treaty relief is denied where the SAS lacks genuine management and operational substance or where the principal purpose of the structure is treaty shopping (PPT under the Multilateral Instrument); France transposed the EU minimum taxation directive (Pillar Two, Directive 2022/2523) applying a 15% global minimum top-up tax to low-margin groups; consequently, a solar portable power e-commerce SAS must maintain real substance in France: permanent premises, operational staff, local decision-making and French-side logistics and inventory.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 CGI: fine up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; aggravated cases (international dimension, organised group) carry fines up to EUR 3,000,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years, together with publication of the judgment, confiscation of assets, exclusion from public tenders and denial of tax treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: The SAS president or acting manager who orchestrates the fraud is personally criminally liable; under Article L. 267 LPF the administration may hold the de facto manager jointly liable for the company's tax debts; the court may impose a travel ban (opposition à sortie du territoire), attachment of personal assets and an interdiction de gérer for up to 15 years.
