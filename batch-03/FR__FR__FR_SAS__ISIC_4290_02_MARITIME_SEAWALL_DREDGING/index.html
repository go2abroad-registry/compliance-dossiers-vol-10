Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must have a registered office in France under Commercial Code Articles L123-11 et seq. and may use a domiciliation company or, subject to statutory conditions, the director's premises. A virtual office is not prohibited for registration, but the domiciliation provider must be authorized and the arrangement must support receipt of official correspondence, document retention, and access to corporate records. For marine dredging, seawall, breakwater, and EPC operations, a virtual address alone is commercially and regulatory insufficient. The company should maintain demonstrable French operational substance, including management control, project files, technical personnel or contractors, insurance, banking records, equipment or vessel arrangements, safety documentation, and environmental and maritime permits. The registered office must not be used to misrepresent a port facility, shipyard, construction site, or permanent operating establishment.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital is required for a SAS. For cash contributions, at least 50% of the subscribed amount must be paid upon incorporation and the balance within five years of registration. Capital may be deposited with an eligible French bank, notary, or other authorized depositary before filing; the deposit certificate is normally required where cash capital is paid through a bank. Contributions in kind generally require an auditor's report unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied.

Funding & unlock path: There is no universal standalone occupational operating clearance for an SAS. Obtain site-specific fire-safety approval where premises qualify as an établissement recevant du public or another regulated facility, and coordinate fire prevention with the municipality and departmental fire and rescue service (SDIS). Verify structural, geotechnical, lifting, quay, and temporary-works integrity through qualified engineers; comply with the Labour Code, risk assessment document (DUERP), occupational-health obligations, maritime and port safety rules, Construction and Public Works safety requirements, worker training, personal protective equipment, and mandatory inspections of lifting and other work equipment.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital is required for a SAS. For cash contributions, at least 50% of the subscribed amount must be paid upon incorporation and the balance within five years of registration. Capital may be deposited with an eligible French bank, notary, or other authorized depositary before filing; the deposit certificate is normally required where cash capital is paid through a bank. Contributions in kind generally require an auditor's report unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; online portal: impots.gouv.fr; business formalities portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

The SAS must be incorporated through the Guichet Unique des Formalités des Entreprises (GUE) at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, which transmits the registration to the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) and assigns the statutory identifiers: SIREN (9-digit), SIRET (14-digit), and the n° TVA intracommunautaire (French VAT number bearing 'FR' + 2-digit key + SIREN). Non-resident shareholders may establish the SAS with no minimum capital (Article L.227-1 Code de commerce) and must file Articles of Association. VAT registration (n° d'identification TVA) is centralized via the DGFiP's Service de la Sécurisation des Systèmes d'Information (SSSI) / Direction des grandes entreprises (DGE) for large taxpayers; a specific form 1542-SD (in French, 'déclaration d'identification à la TVA' via portail) is filed, or SIREN auto-creates the intra-EU VAT number. Cessation/change formalities are also filed through the GUE.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is automatically subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) at the entity level over its entire taxable income; no pass-through/check-the-box election exists for SAS under French law. Available elections include: (i) option for fiscal integration (régime d'intégration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI for a group of at least 95%-owned French subsidiaries; (ii) option for the parent-subsidiary regime (régime mère-fille, Article 145 CGI) to exempt 95% of dividends received; (iii) option for the special limited tax regime for maritime activities (régime fiscal spécial des sociétés de navigation maritime) where applicable; (iv) for a newly commercial SAS, an initial 3-year IS exemption in assisted zones (e.g., port zones) under territorial aid rules; (v) hybrid mismatch adjustments are imposed mandatorily under Article 212 bis CGI implementing ATAD 1&2 - no elective opt-in, the primary/secondary linking rules are applied ex officio against deductible payments; (vi) at the entity level, the SAS may elect the simplified VAT reporting regime (régime réel simplifié) or form the TVA group (assujettis uniques) under Article 256 C quater CGI.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. A French SAS may generally receive, hold, convert, transfer, and repatriate foreign currency without a general exchange-control approval or capital-control filing. Cross-border transactions remain subject to anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing controls, sanctions screening, beneficial-owner verification, tax reporting, payment-transparency requirements, customs and trade controls, and any applicable foreign-direct-investment or regulated-sector approvals. Dividends, interest, royalties, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds may generally be remitted to non-residents after satisfaction of corporate-law approvals, withholding-tax rules, transfer-pricing requirements, accounting evidence, creditor-protection rules, and applicable tax-treaty conditions. Banks may request contracts, invoices, board resolutions, source-of-funds evidence, tax residency certificates, and proof of the economic purpose before executing or releasing a payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring conversion of foreign-currency export receipts. The SAS may retain export proceeds in euros or foreign currency, subject to its bank-account terms, accounting records, tax rules, sanctions controls, customer due diligence, customs documentation, and contractual requirements. Export transactions may require customs declarations and statistical reporting, including Intrastat reporting where applicable. Cash payments and certain cross-border payment arrangements are subject to separate French and European restrictions.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, within the Eurosystem, with foreign-exchange and financial-sanctions policy administered under the applicable European Union framework and by the French Directorate-General of the Treasury

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for an EU-authorised electronic-money institution or payment institution operating in France under passporting or French authorisation; Low for an offshore digital EMI without European Economic Area authorisation or a compliant French access model. An EMI safeguards customer funds but does not provide ordinary bank deposits unless separately licensed as a credit institution. Availability of French IBANs, SEPA participation, SWIFT access, client-money segregation, safeguarding, payment-services permissions, transaction limits, and local compliance support must be verified. An EMI is not a substitute for a French bank for statutory capital-blocking evidence, certain public-sector collections, project guarantees, escrow arrangements, credit facilities, or banking products requiring a credit institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €50 million consolidated group turnover

Economic substance scope: France implements OECD BEPS standards and requires economic substance for certain intra-group structures; operational activities such as maritime dredging require demonstrable local economic presence

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal sanctions including fines up to €5 million or 80% of the unreported income, and imprisonment for up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for criminal offenses, facing fines and imprisonment; civil claims for restitution of evaded taxes
Late or Non-Filing of Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of €60 minimum and 0.5% per month of delay up to a maximum of 5% of the tax due, plus interest on unpaid taxes

Director personal liability: Directors can incur personal liability for the penalties and may face additional sanctions for repeated offenses
Transfer Pricing Violations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to 5% of the tax adjustment amount, potential denial of deductions, and double taxation corrections

Director personal liability: Willful non-compliance may result in personal criminal liability and disqualification
Shell Company or Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the entity, disallowance of tax benefits, fines up to €10 million or 5% of turnover, and potential criminal penalties

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil leading to personal civil and criminal liability, including possible imprisonment for fraudulent establishment
