Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The competent local commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce), which maintains the Trade and Companies Register (Registre du commerce et des sociétés, RCS); filings are submitted through the INPI-operated Single Window (Guichet unique des formalités d’entreprises).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-20 and R. 123-1 et seq.; applicable company-law provisions also appear in the Civil Code and the Monetary and Financial Code.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company-creation formalité submitted through the INPI Single Window for registration in the RCS, including the required beneficial-ownership declaration.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 18 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital above EUR 1 applies to a SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription; the balance must be paid within five years after registration. Cash contributions are deposited with an authorized bank, notary, or other permitted depositary, which issues a deposit certificate. The funds are released after RCS registration. Contributions in kind are generally subject to a contribution auditor unless a statutory exemption applies. The USD filing-fee figure is an indicative conversion of French-euro registry charges; actual charges vary and should be confirmed at filing.

Funding & unlock path: No industrial fire, structural-load, or workplace clearance is required solely for an office-based e-commerce business. Customer-accessible premises may be subject to rules for establishments open to the public (ERP), including fire-safety and accessibility requirements; warehouses, workplaces, and installations must comply with applicable building, occupational health and safety, fire-protection, and insurer requirements.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministry of Economy and Finance; online portals: impots.gouv.fr and formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; EU VAT validation via VIES.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS, the SIREN/SIRET registration is automatic upon incorporation through the Guichet Unique; the VAT intra-community number (TVA) is delivered by DGFiP to the local Entreprise Tax Office and must be shown on all invoices. A foreign entity holding French taxable operations must first appoint a tax representative established in France, then file an application with the DGFiP office (non-resident taxpayer unit) using an available French VAT registration form, together with evidence and a copy of the foreign registration document; the DGFiP issues a French VAT number, and the foreign entity remains subject to the same reporting obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SAS, the SIREN/SIRET registration is automatic upon incorporation through the Guichet Unique; the VAT intra-community number (TVA) is delivered by DGFiP to the local Entreprise Tax Office and must be shown on all invoices. A foreign entity holding French taxable operations must first appoint a tax representative established in France, then file an application with the DGFiP office (non-resident taxpayer unit) using an available French VAT registration form, together with evidence and a copy of the foreign registration document; the DGFiP issues a French VAT number, and the foreign entity remains subject to the same reporting obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in France. The SAS is per se subject to corporate income tax under Article 206(1) CGI. Available elections include: (i) régime de l'intégration fiscale (group tax consolidation) for French subsidiaries held at least 95%; (ii) VAT grouping election (assujettis uniques) for entities under common control; (iii) election for the option taxable on dividend income under the parent-subsidiary regime; and (iv) anti-hybrid rules, including ATAD-aligned provisions, may require alignment of entity character with the jurisdiction of incorporation, without any elective hybrid conversion.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established business, there is no general threshold; any person making regular taxable supplies is obliged to register and obtain a VAT number. A small business exemption (franchise en base de TVA) applies where annual taxable turnover remains below EUR 91,900 for sales of goods and EUR 39,100 for services (2025 limits), except that e-commerce distance sales and cash-on-delivery retail channels are not automatically covered where the seller is established abroad; non-EU foreign sellers must register from the first transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods outside the European Union qualify for VAT at 0% (exonération à l'exportation) where tangible property is dispatched or transported outside the EU, demonstrated by an electronic customs export declaration (mention 'exportation', code 00) and supporting transport documents. Purely offshore services to non-EU clients may be qualified as zero-rated under the French territoriality provisions of Article 259 CGI; the supplier must retain contractual evidence and proof of place of establishment of the recipient. Sellers must hold the export documentation within the second month following the sale to maintain the zero rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane / CPD)
  • Bill of Lading (Maritime, Airwaybill, or CMR international consignment note)
  • Commercial Invoice with incoterms and valuation
  • Evidence of dispatch from France or another EU state outside the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt proving payment from the non-EU buyer

France is rolling out mandatory e-invoicing for all VAT-registered B2B transactions through the Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or an approved Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) platform from 1 September 2026 for large enterprises and 1 September 2027 for SMEs; e-reporting of transaction data to DGFiP is mandatory. Invoices must include a digital signature, QR code or an approved fiscal encryption, and all issued invoices must be transmitted in structured XML or Factur-X format. Real-time fiscal clearance at the time of each B2B sale is implemented through PPF; for B2C mail-order sales, the transaction data is reported at the same cadence and must preserve the full audit trail.

The French tax administration may reverse the zero rating and reclaim VAT plus statutory accruals where the export evidence is incomplete, incorrectly reported, or where a fraudulent export arrangement is found. Penalties under Article 1729 CGI amount to 40% of the evaded VAT for bad-faith inaccuracies and 80% for manoeuvres frauduleuses (fraudulent schemes); delay interest accrues at 0.20% per month. Convictions may also carry criminal fines up to EUR 500,000 and prison sentences for tax fraud, and customs penalties for false customs declarations are imposed under the Code des Douanes. Export VAT refunds claimed without legally valid proof are treated as unjustified VAT recoveries and clawed back through a formal assessment procedure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Euro is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions under EU capital movement liberalization (Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Art. 63). No exchange controls or approval requirements for profit remittance, dividends, or repatriation of capital. However, all cross-border transfers exceeding €10,000 must be reported to the French customs and tax authorities for statistical and anti-money laundering (AML) purposes. Profit remittance is subject to standard corporate income tax (25% standard rate) and withholding tax on dividends (30% flat rate unless reduced by tax treaty). No restrictions on conversion of EUR to foreign currency for legitimate business purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds into EUR. French law (Code Monétaire et Financier) does not impose any requirement to repatriate or convert foreign currency earnings. However, all export transactions must be declared to the Banque de France for balance of payments statistics via the 'Déclaration de Transaction' (DT) system if the transaction value exceeds €12,500. The declaration is electronic and must be filed within 20 days of the transaction. No conversion ratio applies; exporters may retain foreign currency in accounts abroad or in France.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF) as national central bank; European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy and eurozone FX operations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Airwallex is a viable option for cross-border payments and multi-currency accounts. It operates in France under the EU Payment Services Directive (PSD2) via an e-money license obtained from the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR) or through passporting from another EU member state. Airwallex can provide local IBANs in EUR and support COD settlement via local logistics partners. However, for capital escrow purposes (e.g., shareholder loans or equity injections), a traditional bank account is often required by investors or legal counsel. Fintech EMIs are generally rated 'Medium' for core treasury operations due to limited credit facilities and potential regulatory constraints on large-value transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity injection or shareholder loans from non-resident shareholders, use SWIFT wire transfers with proper purpose codes. For equity: use purpose code 'S301' (Investment in equity capital) or 'S302' (Investment in mutual funds) under the IMF Balance of Payments Manual. For loans: use 'S310' (Other investment - loans). The transfer must be accompanied by a copy of the shareholder resolution and the bank may require a certificate of the transaction's economic substance. For amounts above €10,000, the bank will report to the Banque de France. Ensure the beneficiary account is a French IBAN (e.g., FR76...). The bank will verify the source of funds and may request a declaration of the transaction's purpose.For trade settlements (e.g., paying suppliers in emerging markets), use SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers or Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for documentary credit. MT103 is suitable for low-value, high-frequency payments; LC is recommended for high-value transactions to mitigate counterparty risk. For COD logistics remittances, use local payment rails via Airwallex or partner banks to settle with local logistics providers. Ensure all outbound payments are accompanied by appropriate purpose codes (e.g., 'S200' for goods trade, 'S300' for services). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. Qualifying small and medium-sized companies may apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit if, among other conditions, annual turnover excluding VAT does not exceed EUR 10 million, fully paid-up share capital is held at least 75% by individuals or qualifying entities, and the applicable ownership conditions are met.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Generally 25% on dividends paid by a French company to a non-resident, subject to domestic exemptions and treaty relief. A reduced or zero rate may apply under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive if all ownership, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, anti-abuse, and procedural conditions are met. Payments to residents of non-cooperative jurisdictions may be subject to a 75% rate, subject to statutory exceptions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Generally EUR 150 million: French transfer-pricing documentation obligations apply to entities meeting the statutory turnover or gross-asset threshold, or belonging to a group meeting the relevant threshold, subject to the precise conditions in the Tax Code. The annual summary return (Form 2257-SD) has its own statutory scope and thresholds; confirm the applicable threshold and filing conditions for the relevant tax year.

Economic substance scope: France does not have a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. French tax law nevertheless applies substance-over-form principles, anti-abuse rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, permanent-establishment principles, and transfer-pricing rules. Arrangements lacking genuine functions, assets, risks, decision-making, or commercial purpose may be challenged under applicable domestic law, treaties, or EU anti-abuse rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, late interest, and penalties that may reach 80% in specified cases. Criminal tax fraud may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine for an individual, increased in specified aggravated cases to seven years and EUR 3 million; statutory multipliers and additional consequences may apply to legal entities.

Director personal liability: A director who personally participates in fraud may face criminal prosecution and personal penalties. A director may also be held liable for tax debts under statutory conditions, including fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated tax non-compliance that makes collection impossible. Imprisonment is not automatic; travel restrictions require a separate legal basis or order.
Late, incomplete, or inaccurate corporate tax compliance

Statutory sanction: Generally a 10% filing surcharge, potentially 40% after formal notice and 80% in specified serious cases, plus late-payment interest generally at 0.20% per month; additional tax and specific filing penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: The SAS is primarily liable. Personal director liability may arise under statutory conditions for fraudulent conduct or serious and repeated non-compliance that prevents collection, or under criminal law for personal participation.
Transfer-pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Taxable-profit adjustments, interest, and penalties may apply. Failure to provide required documentation may trigger a penalty generally set at the greater of 0.5% of the amount of transactions for which information was not provided and 5% of the corresponding transfer-pricing adjustment, subject to a statutory minimum and maximum. The applicable rules and caps must be checked for the tax year and circumstances.

Director personal liability: The company is primarily liable. Directors may face personal consequences for deliberate fraud or personal participation; ordinary documentation failure does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Undeclared French permanent establishment or mischaracterized e-commerce activity

Statutory sanction: Assessment of French corporate tax and related taxes, interest, surcharges, and potentially criminal penalties where fraud is established. Treaty outcomes depend on the relevant treaty and actual facts.

Director personal liability: Company liability is primary. Directors may be personally exposed for knowing participation in evasion or where statutory conditions for personal tax-debt liability are met.
Improper dividend, royalty, or interest withholding

Statutory sanction: The payer may be assessed for unpaid withholding, interest, and penalties. Treaty benefits may be denied under domestic anti-abuse rules, the applicable treaty, or the principal-purpose test where relevant.

Director personal liability: The SAS remains responsible as withholding agent. Personal liability is not automatic but may arise from intentional evasion, personal criminal participation, or applicable statutory director-liability rules.
Shell-company, sham-management, or abuse-of-law arrangement

Statutory sanction: Benefits may be denied and tax reassessed under applicable abuse-of-law, treaty, beneficial-ownership, or transfer-pricing rules, with interest and penalties; criminal sanctions require the relevant criminal elements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where they knowingly participate in fraud or satisfy statutory conditions for liability. A shell characterization alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Anti-money-laundering and proceeds-of-crime exposure

Statutory sanction: Money laundering is punishable under French criminal law by up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine for an individual, with higher penalties in aggravated cases; legal entities may face statutory fines and additional sanctions. Separate offences and sanctions may apply to the underlying conduct.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly commit or facilitate money laundering may be prosecuted personally. Corporate status does not shield a participating director; travel bans or other judicial measures are fact-specific and are not automatic.
