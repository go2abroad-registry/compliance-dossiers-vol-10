Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), administered through the official impots.gouv.fr portal and the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Foreign businesses without a French establishment generally deal with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). Company formation and statutory business registration are filed through the Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI), which interfaces with the DGFiP.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is formed through the INPI Guichet unique. Following acceptance, the company receives a SIREN and SIRET identifier from the Institut national de la statistique et des études économiques (INSEE). The DGFiP then assigns or confirms the French intra-Community VAT identification number, generally formatted as FR plus a two-character key and the nine-digit SIREN. The company must activate its professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr, identify its competent SIE, and register for applicable corporate income tax, VAT, payroll, and local tax obligations. A foreign entity without a French establishment must assess whether it is required to obtain a French VAT number; where registration is required, it files with the SIEE and submits constitutional documents, tax residence evidence, details of French taxable activities, representative information where applicable, and supporting transaction documentation. EU-established businesses may use the applicable EU administrative channel where legally available. A fiscal representative may be required for a non-EU business making taxable supplies in France, subject to applicable legal exceptions and reverse-charge rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is ordinarily subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, or IS). A qualifying closely held SAS may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu, or IR) under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code, subject to statutory ownership, activity, age, turnover, employee, and shareholder requirements. The election is generally limited to five financial years and is not equivalent to a US check-the-box election. France has no general check-the-box regime for entity classification and no general election that converts a French SAS into a fiscally transparent entity. VAT options may include opting into VAT where the business qualifies for the domestic VAT franchise, but cross-border place-of-supply and reverse-charge rules remain applicable. French anti-hybrid-mismatch rules implement the EU ATAD framework and may deny deductions or require inclusion; they are not elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For ordinary domestic services, the French VAT franchise threshold is generally EUR 37,500 of prior-year turnover, with an in-year tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250, subject to statutory amendments and sector-specific rules. A business within the franchise generally does not charge VAT but may elect to become VAT-liable. The threshold does not eliminate registration or reporting requirements for transactions for which VAT identification is independently required, including certain intra-Community transactions, acquisitions, imports, and reverse-charge transactions. B2B services supplied to a business customer established in another EU Member State generally follow the customer-location rule and reverse charge, with no domestic turnover threshold governing the customer's VAT accounting obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods dispatched from France to a destination outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption with the right to deduct input VAT where the supplier proves exportation and exit from the EU customs territory. Required evidence generally includes the export declaration, customs exit result, transport documentation, commercial invoice, and a clear link between the goods, purchaser, and export transaction. Intra-Community supplies of goods may be exempt where the customer is identified for VAT in another EU Member State, the goods are transported to that Member State, and the supplier retains required transport evidence and files the applicable European sales-list information. B2B services supplied to a customer established outside France are generally taxed at the customer's place of establishment and are outside the scope of French VAT rather than technically zero-rated, subject to proof of customer status and establishment. Virtual CFO, bookkeeping, and advisory services may require careful analysis of customer location, fixed establishments, B2C rules, and any professional-services exception. Export treatment does not apply merely because payment is received from abroad or because the supplier uses a foreign bank account.

  • Electronic export customs declaration and Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Customs exit confirmation or equivalent proof that the goods left the European Union
  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, and export destination
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note, courier record, or other independent transport evidence
  • Packing list and, where relevant, customs transport or transit documentation
  • Purchase order, contract, delivery terms, and evidence linking the exported goods to the invoice
  • For intra-Community supplies, customer VAT identification number, transport evidence, and European sales-list filing evidence
  • For services, contract, customer business-status evidence, customer establishment or VAT identification evidence, invoices stating the applicable reverse-charge or outside-scope treatment, and records demonstrating the place of supply
  • Bank payment evidence may support the audit file but is not a general French substitute for customs exit evidence and there is no general French statutory inward-remittance certificate requirement

France is implementing a phased mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction-data reporting system. From 1 September 2026, all French-established businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices, while mandatory issuance obligations initially apply to large companies and certain larger groups. From 1 September 2027, mandatory issuance extends to small and medium-sized enterprises and micro-enterprises, subject to the final statutory timetable. Invoices must generally be exchanged through an approved private platform (plateforme agréée) or the public invoicing platform designated by law, using prescribed structured or hybrid formats. The system is not a universal real-time invoice-clearance model: invoice data and, where applicable, payment, transaction, and international transaction data are reported to the tax administration through the approved platform framework. Public-sector invoices continue to use Chorus Pro. Businesses must maintain compliant invoice numbering, mandatory invoice particulars, VAT rates, customer VAT identification data where required, audit trails, and electronic retention records.

France has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. Export proceeds remain subject to ordinary accounting, anti-money-laundering, banking, transfer-pricing, and tax-record requirements. If export exemption or an equivalent VAT treatment is unsupported, the DGFiP may reassess VAT, deny or claw back input-VAT recovery, charge late-payment interest, and impose tax penalties. A 40% surcharge may apply for deliberate failure or deliberate underassessment, and an 80% surcharge may apply in cases involving fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, subject to statutory conditions. Late-payment interest is generally calculated at the statutory monthly rate applicable for the relevant period. Serious intentional tax fraud may also trigger criminal proceedings, including imprisonment and substantial fines; customs violations may generate additional customs penalties, forfeiture, or seizure. Repeated or materially deficient evidence may result in retrospective reassessment for the audited periods, limitation-period extensions in cases of fraud, and denial of VAT refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for ordinary current-account and capital-account transactions. France generally imposes no exchange-control approval for ordinary inbound or outbound commercial payments, dividends, interest, loan repayments, capital contributions, or repatriation of proceeds. Transfers remain subject to sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing controls, tax compliance, beneficial-owner identification, documentation of economic purpose, and applicable foreign-investment screening. Dividends require distributable profits, approved financial statements, shareholder approval, and withholding-tax analysis. Interest and related-party payments require transfer-pricing support and applicable withholding-tax and interest-limitation analysis. Payments involving sanctioned jurisdictions, sanctioned persons, dual-use goods, or restricted sectors may require blocking, licensing, enhanced diligence, or governmental notification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated to France, surrendered to a central bank, or converted into euros within a prescribed period. No general mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export receipts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts, subject to the company’s banking arrangements, accounting requirements, tax reporting, sanctions controls, and contractual obligations. Banks may request export contracts, invoices, customs evidence, shipping documents, and payment-purpose information. Banque de France balance-of-payments or statistical reporting may apply to qualifying cross-border transactions or reporting populations; the company must follow current Banque de France reporting instructions and bank-specific data requests.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, within the Eurosystem and under the monetary policy framework of the European Central Bank. France applies the European Union and euro-area framework for payments, capital movements, foreign exchange, anti-money-laundering controls, and balance-of-payments reporting.

Do not mislabel equity as a service payment or trade receipt. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate. Eligible small and medium-sized companies may generally apply a 15% rate to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to statutory ownership, turnover, paid-up capital and other eligibility conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% French withholding tax generally applies to dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident recipient, subject to domestic exemptions, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, and applicable tax treaties. A qualifying EU or EEA parent may generally claim an exemption where the statutory holding-period, legal-form, subject-to-tax, minimum ownership and anti-abuse conditions are met. The French recipient-level withholding tax rate and filing mechanics must be verified for the payment date and recipient status.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return is generally due within three months after the end of the financial year. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the usual deadline is the second business day following 1 May of the following year. The electronic tax package and payment obligations must be checked against the date displayed in the professional tax account and applicable annual administrative calendar. A company with a non-calendar year-end must apply the deadline corresponding to that year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 150 million. French companies or French tax groups generally fall within the mandatory comprehensive transfer-pricing documentation regime when they meet the statutory turnover or gross-assets threshold, or when they are controlled by or control an entity meeting the relevant threshold, or when they fall within another statutory triggering condition. Large groups may also have a country-by-country reporting obligation at the EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold. Companies below the comprehensive threshold remain subject to the arm's-length principle and must be able to justify related-party pricing upon request; a simplified documentation regime may apply to certain entities.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a standalone regime identical to the United Kingdom or certain other jurisdictions' Economic Substance Regulations. French tax authorities may nevertheless disregard artificial arrangements and deny treaty or domestic benefits under the general anti-abuse rule, abuse-of-law doctrine, principal-purpose analysis, beneficial-ownership requirements, controlled foreign company rules, permanent-establishment principles and transfer-pricing rules. Substance must be demonstrated through French personnel, premises, decision-making, functions, assets, risks, commercial purpose, governance records, contracts, banking activity and contemporaneous evidence. A virtual CFO consultancy should separately document the actual personnel performing advisory and bookkeeping services, client contracts, professional qualifications, data-security controls, management decisions, service delivery records and allocation of profits and risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate income tax evasion and fraudulent accounting

Statutory sanction: Administrative recovery of tax, late-payment interest, surcharges generally ranging from 10% to 80% depending on conduct, and potential criminal prosecution. Tax fraud may carry imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 500,000; aggravated circumstances can increase exposure to up to 7 years' imprisonment and a fine of up to EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions and judicial assessment.

Director personal liability: The SAS generally provides limited liability, but directors and de facto managers may incur criminal liability, personal fines, imprisonment and civil liability. A director may also face a court-ordered contribution to unpaid tax where serious and repeated management misconduct made collection impossible. Travel restrictions or passport measures are not automatic tax penalties but may arise from criminal judicial measures, enforcement proceedings or flight-risk determinations.
Failure to file, late payment and obstruction of tax audit

Statutory sanction: Tax assessment by estimation or ex officio assessment, 10% late-filing or late-payment surcharge, increased surcharges for deliberate or fraudulent conduct, 0.20% monthly late-payment interest, recovery costs and separate penalties for missing information returns or records.

Director personal liability: The company remains primarily liable. Directors may face personal criminal or civil exposure where they intentionally obstruct the tax authority, falsify records, organize evasion or commit serious management misconduct. Personal asset recovery is exceptional and requires a statutory or judicial basis.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income under the arm's-length principle, interest and surcharges, possible recharacterization of hidden distributions, withholding tax, and documentation penalties generally based on EUR 10,000 or a statutory percentage of covered transactions, depending on the breach and applicable legal provision.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for intentional fraud, abuse of corporate assets, false records or serious management misconduct. Ordinary pricing disagreement without fraudulent conduct normally remains a company-level tax liability.
Undeclared permanent establishment or hidden French activity

Statutory sanction: Retrospective corporate income tax, VAT, payroll and social-security assessments, withholding tax, late-payment interest, surcharges and possible criminal fraud proceedings where concealment is deliberate.

Director personal liability: Foreign directors and local managers may be investigated for tax fraud, illegal employment or management misconduct. Personal liability depends on participation, control, intent and the specific enforcement proceeding; limited liability does not protect deliberate unlawful conduct.
Improper dividend, interest or royalty withholding

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax from the French payer, interest, surcharges, penalties for inaccurate or missing reporting and potential denial of treaty relief. The domestic rate may apply where treaty documentation and statutory conditions are not met.

Director personal liability: The company is normally the withholding debtor. Directors may incur personal criminal or civil exposure for intentional concealment, false certificates, fraudulent distributions or serious management misconduct.
Abusive shell company or artificial substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or domestic benefits, abuse-of-law reassessment, recharacterization of income or distributions, withholding tax, corporate tax, VAT and penalties. An inactive or low-substance company is not automatically unlawful, but artificial arrangements may be disregarded.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may be personally liable for fraud, abuse of corporate assets, money laundering or false accounting where they participated in the arrangement. Civil restitution, management bans and criminal measures may be imposed by a court; travel bans are case-specific and not automatic.
Money laundering, terrorist financing and AML non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Criminal money-laundering penalties can include imprisonment of up to 5 years and a fine of up to EUR 375,000, with aggravated forms carrying higher penalties. Regulated professionals may also face disciplinary sanctions, administrative fines, suspension or withdrawal of authorization. The exact regime depends on the person's regulated status and conduct.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners and employees who knowingly participate may face personal prosecution, confiscation, fines and imprisonment. Civil restitution and asset-freezing measures may apply. Travel restrictions may be ordered in criminal proceedings where legally justified.
Unlicensed accounting or regulated professional activity

Statutory sanction: Potential criminal and professional sanctions under French rules governing the accounting profession, including fines, imprisonment where the statutory offence is established, injunctions, cessation of activity and liability for resulting tax or accounting harm. Advisory services must be carefully distinguished from reserved accounting acts.

Director personal liability: The individuals performing or directing the unauthorized activity may be personally prosecuted. The SAS does not shield directors or service providers from criminal responsibility for unauthorized professional practice, false professional titles or fraudulent representations.
VAT fraud and false invoicing

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, recovery of improperly claimed credits, interest, surcharges generally up to 80% for fraudulent conduct, invoice penalties, joint liability in qualifying cases and criminal tax-fraud exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face personal criminal sanctions and civil liability where they organized or knowingly approved false invoicing or VAT fraud. Asset confiscation and judicial travel restrictions may apply in criminal proceedings.
Corporate asset misuse and unlawful distributions

Statutory sanction: Repayment, nullity or recovery of unlawful distributions, corporate-law sanctions, tax recharacterization as salary or hidden distribution, withholding tax, social charges, fines and potential criminal prosecution for abuse of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal restitution, fines, management bans and imprisonment under applicable corporate criminal provisions. Limited liability does not protect deliberate misuse of company assets.
Failure to preserve accounting, tax and beneficial-ownership records

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines, rejection of deductions or VAT credits, estimated assessment, audit penalties, anti-money-laundering sanctions and adverse evidentiary treatment. French accounting and tax records generally require multi-year retention, commonly at least 10 years for accounting records, with the applicable rule depending on the record type.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for intentional destruction, obstruction, false records or AML breaches. Criminal exposure depends on the record, the statutory obligation and the director's knowledge and participation.
