Statutory Name Clearance & Restricted Words

Official search database

Guichet unique électronique of INPI (https://formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr) combined with the Registre National des Entreprises (RNE), INPI trademark database (data.inpi.fr), and Infogreffe (https://www.infogreffe.fr) for pre-filing availability checks.

Name reservation / lock period

French law does not provide a standalone pre-registration name reservation; the name is cleared at the moment of RCS filing through the Guichet unique and the commercial court registry. If the registry rejects the dénomination, the legal representative receives written reasons. The applicant may correct the name, add a distinguishing token, attach the trademark owner's written consent, and refile through the Guichet unique. Refusals based on public-order grounds or third-party rights may be submitted for judicial determination to the President of the competent Tribunal de commerce in référé; procedural refusals by the Guichet unique may be challenged before the administrative court. A fresh review period begins upon each corrected filing, and all French translations and authenticated governmental documents must be current, generally within 90 days.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce (Commercial Court Registry) maintaining the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) in the department of the registered office; all incorporation filings are submitted electronically through the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI) single window at formalites.entreprises.data.gouv.fr.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce): Articles L. 123-1 to L. 123-9 (RCS registration and Extrait Kbis), L. 210-1 to L. 210-10 (general company provisions), L. 227-1 to L. 227-33 (Société par Actions Simplifiée constitution and corporate governance), R. 123-1 to R. 123-19 (registry filing procedure); supplemented by Law No. 2019-486 (PACTE Act), the Guichet unique implementing decree, and the Civil Code provisions on electronic signatures.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'entreprise (société) submitted via the mandatory Guichet unique electronic dossier, together with the signed Articles of Association (statuts constitutifs), the blocked-capital certificate, and the publication certificate; the operative registration is the inscription au Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés evidenced by the Extrait Kbis.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS; the capital may be fixed at EUR 1. However, Article L. 227-3 of the Code de commerce requires that shares be fully paid up at subscription. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and may be released only after delivery of the Extrait Kbis; in-kind contributions must be appraised by a commissaire aux apports unless unanimously waived, where an individual contribution exceeds EUR 30,000 or the total value of in-kind contributions exceeds half of the share capital.

Funding & unlock path: Workplace opening declaration (déclaration d'ouverture d'un établissement) under the Labor Code; fire-safety certification and evacuation arrangements reviewed by the service départemental d'incendie et de secours (SDIS) where the premises receive personnel; structural load inspection reports for silos, gantries and truck-loading platforms issued by an approved technical control bureau (Apave, Bureau Veritas, DEKRA, Socotec or equivalent); dust-hazard management plans for bulk handling points; and, where the workforce exceeds 50 employees, creation of the Comité Social et Économique (CSE). All premises must meet building-code accessibility and electrical-safety standards before operational clearance is granted.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS; the capital may be fixed at EUR 1. However, Article L. 227-3 of the Code de commerce requires that shares be fully paid up at subscription. Cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked bank account or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations and may be released only after delivery of the Extrait Kbis; in-kind contributions must be appraised by a commissaire aux apports unless unanimously waived, where an individual contribution exceeds EUR 30,000 or the total value of in-kind contributions exceeds half of the share capital.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Direction Régionale de l'Environnement, de l'Aménagement et du Logement (DREAL) and the Préfecture du département; jurisdiction is triggered only if fixed bulk-storage silos, transhipment stations or mechanical processing installations are established. Pure wholesale distribution of Portland clinker does not require an industrial manufacturing permit.

Category: None required for pure wholesale trading under ISIC 4663; optional operational authorizations include a port concession agreement or temporary occupation authorization (autorisation d'occupation du domaine public portuaire) for dock-side unloading, an Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) customs certificate for simplified import procedures, and a town-planning permission for any commercial warehouse footprint.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) – Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr; company/registration portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet unique); customs interface: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) with DELTA/PRO.DOUANE; VAT number verification: VIES (europa.eu/vies).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SAS is incorporated online at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; INSEE issues SIREN/SIRET; the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) assigns the VAT identification number (numéro d'identification TVA) and the EORI number is obtained from DGDDI for customs filing of bulk-clinker exports. A non-EU seller without a French establishment that becomes liable for French VAT must register through the SIE and appoint an accredited fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) before invoicing; intra-EU acquisition checks require a valid VIES-validated VAT number.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned SAS is incorporated online at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; INSEE issues SIREN/SIRET; the local Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) assigns the VAT identification number (numéro d'identification TVA) and the EORI number is obtained from DGDDI for customs filing of bulk-clinker exports. A non-EU seller without a French establishment that becomes liable for French VAT must register through the SIE and appoint an accredited fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) before invoicing; intra-EU acquisition checks require a valid VIES-validated VAT number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Under Art. 239 bis AA CGI, an eligible newly-created SAS may elect (before the end of its first fiscal year) to be taxed on a pass-through basis for up to five years; a French parent SAS may elect fiscal integration (Art. 223 A CGI) for 95%+ owned French subsidiaries; the mère-fille participation regime is available; no US-style check-the-box classification exists in France — entity tainting follows the commercial form, the statut de lieu de direction, and ATAD 2 anti-hybrid rules (Art. 209, 212 bis CGI).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic franchise en base de TVA exemption for small established suppliers of goods below EUR 85,000 annual turnover (tolerance EUR 93,500), reduced by the 2025 Finance Act; non-established foreign suppliers have no franchise — VAT registration is compulsory for any taxable supply made in France and for all intra-EU acquisitions above EUR 10,000 (OSS distance-sales threshold); import VAT is due on every physical importation of portland clinker into France.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of bulk portland clinker/cement to non-EU destinations are exempt from French VAT (Art. 262-I-1° CGI) where the supplier proves that the goods left the EU under an accepted electronic export declaration (E-DAU/ECS exit confirmation) within the statutory proof window (normally 90 days from the taxable event, flexibly extended for long-haul bulk maritime voyages); the buyer must be a non-taxable/third-country party or the supply must qualify as an exempt intra-Community dispatch (Art. 262 ter CGI) with a VIES-validated customer VAT number.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Déclaration Administrative Unique (DAU/E-DAU) filed in the DGDDI DELTA/PRO.DOUANE system with accepted exit-confinement confirmation for the non-EU port of discharge
  • Bill of Lading: full set of original or authorised sea waybill/telex-release B/L evidencing bulk clinker laden on the named ocean vessel and destined for a third-country port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank/SWIFT advice (MT103 or LC settlement) evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in EUR against the invoice, cross-referenced with the Banque de France balance-of-payments filing

Mandatory e-invoicing for domestic B2B supplies phased-in: large/mid-size enterprises from 1 September 2026, SMEs/micro from 1 September 2027; invoices must be sent through a registered Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) using structured Factur-X, UBL or CII formats; e-reporting of transaction and payment data flows to the DGFiP via the PDP/API network; no per-invoice 'fiscal stamp' or Italian-style SDI real-time clearance; cross-border/export transactions are covered by e-reporting; retail cash-register software must be certified under the 2018 anti-fraud law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro, which is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. A French SAS may generally receive foreign currency, convert funds, pay foreign suppliers, remit dividends, repay shareholder or bank debt, and make cross-border investments without a general foreign-exchange approval or mandatory conversion into euros. Transfers remain subject to bank anti-money-laundering, sanctions, beneficial-ownership, tax, accounting, and payment-screening controls. Dividend and interest remittances must be supported by valid corporate approvals, distributable profits or a legally valid financing basis, withholding-tax analysis, treaty documentation where relevant, and evidence that the transaction is not a disguised distribution or unlawful return of capital. Cross-border payments may also trigger statistical reporting, VAT, customs, Intrastat, balance-of-payments, or tax-reporting obligations depending on the transaction and counterparty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no general mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the company's discretion, subject to ordinary accounting, tax, customs, sanctions, AML, and bank reporting requirements. There is no general French deadline requiring export receipts to be repatriated or converted. Banks may request commercial invoices, transport documents, customs declarations, contracts, and proof of economic purpose before crediting or transferring funds. Cash physically transported across the European Union external border at or above EUR 10,000, or its equivalent, is subject to applicable EU and French customs declaration rules. Unusual payment patterns, third-party settlements, over-invoicing, unexplained prepayments, or transactions involving sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions may be delayed, rejected, or reported to TRACFIN.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, acting within the Eurosystem, together with the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty and the Directorate-General of Customs and Indirect Taxes for applicable trade, statistical, and cash-reporting controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment accounts and collections, low as a sole treasury bank. An electronic-money institution or payment institution authorised in France or another EEA state may provide payment services in France under applicable authorisation and passporting rules, subject to safeguarding, AML, sanctions, account-usage, and transaction-monitoring controls. An EMI account is not a bank deposit and generally does not provide deposit-guarantee protection. An offshore non-EEA digital EMI cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services into France solely through an offshore platform without an applicable French or EEA authorisation, branch, agent, or other lawful market-access basis. Fintechs may impose limits on bulk payments, trade finance, cash deposits, sanctioned jurisdictions, commodity trades, third-party receipts, and high-value international wires. A regulated French or EEA bank should remain the primary account for capital injections, payroll, taxes, customs, letters of credit, and material supplier settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route paid-in capital through a French bank capital account or another bank-approved account structure designated for the incorporation or capital increase, using a transfer reference that identifies the subscriber, the SAS, and the capital contribution. Provide the executed subscription documentation, corporate approvals, source-of-funds evidence, ownership information, and bank-requested purpose description. After registration or the relevant capital-increase formalities, obtain the bank's release confirmation and transfer funds to the operational account. Shareholder loans, intercompany funding, and additional paid-in capital should be supported by separate agreements and accounting classifications. Use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated EEA payments where available; use SWIFT for non-SEPA currencies or jurisdictions. Preserve MT103 or equivalent payment confirmations, remitter and beneficiary details, UETR, invoice or agreement references, and reconciliation evidence. Do not route capital through personal accounts, unrelated third parties, anonymous payment services, or unapproved offshore wallets.Use SEPA Credit Transfer for eligible euro supplier payments and SWIFT MT103 for non-SEPA or foreign-currency payments. For new, high-value, or higher-risk suppliers, use an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit issued or confirmed by a regulated bank, with compliant presentation of commercial invoices, bills of lading, certificates of origin, insurance documents, inspection certificates, and other stipulated documents. Documentary collections may be used where the counterparty and jurisdiction are sufficiently established. Payment instructions must match the legal contracting party, verified bank account, invoice, goods, Incoterms, vessel, port, and customs documentation. Apply dual approval, callback verification for changed bank details, sanctions screening, segregation of duties, payment-limit controls, and three-way matching between purchase order, receipt or shipping evidence, and invoice.
