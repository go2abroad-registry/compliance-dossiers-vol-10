Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital for an SAS; capital amount is freely set in the articles. However, at least 50% of cash contribution must be fully paid up on subscription at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. No statutory escrow account is mandatory; funds may be deposited with a notary or with the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations if formation is completed without a notarial deed.

Funding & unlock path: Where the company occupies an office, the workplace must obtain a fire safety certificate (avis favorable de la commission de sécurité) if open to the public, as well as structural and electrical load clearances. For an office-only digital operation, no special occupational clearance is required beyond standard labor law compliance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de l'Industrie. Online portal: impots.gouv.fr; one-stop business registration portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet Unique). Export customs authority: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a French-established SAS: (1) file incorporation documents (statuts, registered office proof, identification of legal representative) through the Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; (2) INSEE automatically issues the 9-digit SIREN, the 14-digit SIRET and the RCS registration (Registre du commerce et des sociétés), generally within 1–5 business days; (3) the DGFiP automatically allocates the intra-Community VAT number (FR + 11 digits), normally transmitted with the INSEE registration; if not received within 15 days, apply via the DGFiP online service 'Demande d'attribution d'un numéro de TVA intracommunautaire'; (4) non-EU foreign entities without a French establishment must appoint a fiscal representative approved by the DGFiP and register through the SIE (Service des Impôts des Entreprises) covering the representative's address; processing takes 4–8 weeks. The FR_SAS, being incorporated in France, does not require a representative.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - SAS is mandatorily subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on its profits, with France's territoriality principle limiting non-resident branches to French-source business profits. Pass-through status is not available; there is no French equivalent of the US check-the-box election, and a SAS cannot elect transparent treatment.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box exists in French law. The available elections are: (i) intégration fiscale (CGI Art. 223 A et seq.) - a SAS holding at least 95% of a subsidiary may elect group consolidation; (ii) option for the parent-subsidiary dividend exemption regime under CGI Arts. 145 and 216 (minimum 5% participation and 2-year holding period); (iii) waiver of the franchise en base to charge VAT (CGI Art. 293 B III) in order to recover input VAT; (iv) PE attribution rules are governed exclusively by applicable double tax treaties; (v) hybrid mismatch outcomes are imposed unilaterally by EU ATAD 2 (Directive (EU) 2016/1164, as transposed) and DAC 6 reporting, not by elective check-the-box mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Franchise en base de TVA (CGI Art. 293 B): €37,500 (main) / €41,000 (tolerance) annual revenue for service businesses; €85,000 (main) / €91,900 (tolerance) for goods businesses; non-established foreign suppliers have no threshold. For the SAS, VAT registration is required in practice from the first day: (i) the core supplies to Apple and Google are intra-EU B2B digital services taxable in Ireland under the reverse charge, requiring a valid French VAT ID and VIES status; (ii) input VAT on app development, hosting and operating overhead is recoverable only if the business is registered, so the franchise waiver (CGI Art. 293 B III) is the recommended election; (iii) B2C consumer sales through app stores are not treated as the SAS's taxable supplies because the stores are deemed suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies can be zero-rated or outside French VAT scope where the place of supply is not France: (1) B2B digital voice-streaming services supplied to Apple Distribution International Ltd (VAT IE9700053D) and Google Commerce Ltd (VAT IE6388047V), both established in Ireland, are taxable in Ireland under the reverse charge (CGI Art. 259-1; Directive 2006/112/EC Arts. 44 and 196), so no French VAT at 20% is due; (2) services supplied to business customers established outside the EU are entirely outside French VAT scope; (3) B2C sales to French/EU end-users made through the Apple App Store and Google Play are deemed supplies by the platforms (art. 14a VAT Directive), so the SAS charges no French VAT on those sales. To sustain the zero-rated/outside-scope treatment, the SAS must maintain two non-contradictory items of evidence of the customer's location (IP address, billing address, BIN of payment card, mobile country code, bank details), valid VIES-confirmed VAT IDs of the Irish customers, signed marketplace agreements, invoices mentioning 'autoliquidation par le preneur' or 'hors champ de TVA', and app-store payout statements.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration en Douane - DELTA/DXE) - relevant only if physical goods are bundled; not applicable to pure digital voice-streaming services
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill - applicable only to physical export shipments; not applicable to digital services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit evidence - App Store Connect Payout Reports and Google Play payout confirmations evidencing remittance of net proceeds after 15%-30% platform commission
  • B2B service agreement with Apple Distribution International Ltd (VAT IE9700053D) and Google Commerce Ltd (VAT IE6388047V), confirming the deemed-supplier marketplace structure
  • Reverse-charge tax invoices issued to Apple/Google under CGI Art. 259-1, stating 'Autoliquidation par le preneur' and displaying the SAS's French VAT ID and the Irish VAT IDs of the customers
  • Digital export substantiation file: IP address logs, billing/postal address data, payment method BIN data, mobile country codes and app-store transaction logs maintained per EU Implementing Regulation (EU) 2013/1042

France has enacted a mandatory e-invoicing/e-reporting reform: from 1 September 2026 all businesses must receive structured e-invoices and large enterprises/ETIs must issue them; from 1 September 2027 SMEs must also issue. Invoices must be issued, transmitted and archived in Factur-X, UBL or CII format through an accredited Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP) or the public Portail Public de Facturation (PPF); transaction and payment data (e-reporting) are transmitted to DGFiP on the statutory transmission calendar. France does not operate a real-time per-invoice clearance or digital stamping system, but the automated PDP-to-PPF transmission creates an effective near-real-time audit trail. B2B invoices to Apple/Google fall within the e-invoicing scope; consumer sales via app stores are invoiced by the app stores themselves as deemed suppliers, with no separate e-invoicing obligation on the SAS for those end-consumer sales.

If the deemed-supplier/reverse-charge structure fails documentation audit or is recharacterized as artificial (e.g., Apple/Google marketplace arrangement set aside as abusive), DGFiP will reassess 20% VAT on gross revenue, add late interest (CGI Art. 1727: 0.20% per month) and apply a 40% penalty for understatement (CGI Art. 1729) or 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres (manœuvres frauduleuses). Inadequate export/outside-scope documentation triggers invoice-formality fines under CGI Art. 1737 (up to €750 per invoice under the e-invoicing mandate) and a 50% documentary-inaccuracy assessment where an exemption is unjustified. Deliberate use of false export documents constitutes criminal tax fraud under CGI Art. 1741, punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and a €500,000 fine, with publication of sanctions. Recovery is enforced by avis de mise en recouvrement, tax liens, and abus de droit recharacterization under LPF Art. L.64 with the 80% penalty applicable to connected parties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro. Current-account convertibility is unrestricted, subject to ordinary bank compliance, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, tax reporting and documentation of the underlying transaction. Capital-account movements are generally unrestricted within the European Union and under applicable treaty and OECD principles. A French SAS may receive foreign shareholder capital, intercompany funding and trade receipts in euros or foreign currencies and may remit dividends, interest, royalties, service fees and loan principal to non-residents, subject to corporate-law approvals, distributable-profit limitations, withholding-tax analysis, treaty eligibility, transfer-pricing support, beneficial-owner verification, anti-abuse rules, sanctions screening and bank payment controls. France does not impose a general foreign-exchange licence, mandatory profit-repatriation timetable, capital repatriation approval or routine conversion requirement. Cash transfers involving sanctioned jurisdictions, restricted persons, dual-use goods, designated sectors or unusual structures may be blocked, reported or require a specific legal analysis. Banks may request contracts, invoices, board resolutions, tax evidence, source-of-funds records and proof of economic purpose before executing or releasing a payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts, be converted voluntarily, or be applied to permitted treasury and settlement purposes. The company should retain invoices, transport or service-delivery evidence, customer contracts, payment confirmations and accounting records. Cross-border payment and balance-of-payments information may be collected or reported by banks, the Banque de France or other competent authorities under applicable statistical, prudential, tax, AML and sanctions frameworks. Cash or equivalent movements of EUR 10,000 or more into or out of the European Union are subject to customs declaration rules; this does not apply to ordinary bank transfers but remains relevant to physical cash and certain bearer instruments.

Central bank supervision: European Central Bank for euro-area monetary policy and payment-system oversight; Banque de France for French central-banking functions, payment-system operations, balance-of-payments reporting and certain regulatory duties; French Treasury Directorate General (Direction générale du Trésor) for French external-financial policy and applicable capital-movement controls; Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution for supervision of French credit institutions and payment institutions; Autorité des marchés financiers for financial-market and investment-services supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment accounts and operating collections; low to medium for initial SAS share-capital deposit escrow. An electronic-money institution or payment institution authorised in France or another European Economic Area state may provide payment services under its licence and applicable passporting or local-registration framework, but it is not a French bank and generally cannot provide a statutory bank capital-deposit certificate unless the receiving institution and formation professional accept the arrangement. Safeguarding, client-money segregation, transaction limits, restricted activities, safeguarding-bank dependencies, sanctions screening and onboarding restrictions apply. Offshore digital EMIs outside the European Economic Area cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to French customers without an applicable French or EEA authorisation, passport, local establishment or other valid exemption. The SAS should maintain a domestic bank account for statutory capital, payroll, tax, regulated collections and payment-continuity purposes, with an EMI used as a supplementary rail after legal and contractual verification.

Use a regulated EEA bank or authorised payment institution with a French or euro-area account capable of receiving shareholder funds. The remitter should be the identified shareholder or documented lender, and the payment reference should identify the legal basis, such as SAS share-capital subscription, share-premium contribution or shareholder loan. For incorporation, route subscribed share capital to a designated capital-deposit account and obtain the deposit certificate required for registration; release the funds to the SAS operational account after registration and delivery of the registration evidence. For post-incorporation funding, use executed subscription or loan documents, board or shareholder approvals where required, transfer-pricing support for related-party debt, and a reconciled funds-flow schedule. Provide the bank with UBO information, source-of-funds evidence, tax-residence data, expected payment volumes and relevant contracts before remittance. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium for payment accounts and operating collections; low to medium for initial SAS share-capital deposit escrow. An electronic-money institution or payment institution authorised in France or another European Economic Area state may provide payment services under its licence and applicable passporting or local-registration framework, but it is not a French bank and generally cannot provide a statutory bank capital-deposit certificate unless the receiving institution and formation professional accept the arrangement. Safeguarding, client-money segregation, transaction limits, restricted activities, safeguarding-bank dependencies, sanctions screening and onboarding restrictions apply. Offshore digital EMIs outside the European Economic Area cannot lawfully provide regulated payment services to French customers without an applicable French or EEA authorisation, passport, local establishment or other valid exemption. The SAS should maintain a domestic bank account for statutory capital, payroll, tax, regulated collections and payment-continuity purposes, with an EMI used as a supplementary rail after legal and contractual verification.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, 'IS') for fiscal years opened from 1 January 2022; qualifying SMEs with turnover not exceeding €10,000,000 benefit from a 15% reduced rate on the first €42,000 of net profit; large groups additionally pay a 3.3% social contribution on the IS where the annual IS liability exceeds €763,000, producing an effective marginal rate of 25.825%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% gross withholding on dividends distributed by a French SAS to non-resident corporate shareholders under Article 119 bis 2 CGI; 12.8% flat-rate withholding (prélèvement forfaitaire unique) for non-resident individuals; 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds at least 10% of the SAS for 24 months and is actually subject to corporate tax without an artificial arrangement; treaty-resident corporate shareholders typically qualify for 0–15% treaty rates, frequently 5% where a 10% or 25% holding test is met.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400,000,000 (French-consolidated annual turnover or total balance-sheet assets) per Articles L.13 AA and L.13 AB CGI, triggering mandatory Local File, Master File, and Form 2257 disclosure; Country-by-Country Report required where the group reports consolidated revenue of at least €750,000,000; all cross-border controlled transactions must satisfy the arm's length standard under Article 57 CGI regardless of size.

Economic substance scope: France does not impose Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but applies a comprehensive substance doctrine: corporate tax residence is determined by the siège de direction effective (effective place of management) under Article 4 CGI, and Articles 209 and 238 CGI deny deductions or recharacterize structures lacking genuine local business purpose. The EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) CFC rules, the general anti-abuse doctrine of Article L.64 of the Livre des procédures fiscales, and the BEPS MLI principal purpose test require the SAS to demonstrate real local staff, premises, commercial decision-making, and actual voice-streaming service delivery; an SAS incorporated in France but managed from abroad for tax avoidance is exposed to recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and recapture of incentives.
