Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered office (e.g., Greffe de Paris); centralized filing through the INPI Guichet unique (guichet-unique.inpi.fr).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title II, Chapter VII, Articles L. 227-1 to L. 227-32 (Société par Actions Simplifiée), together with Decree No. 2020-1541 on formalities via the INPI Guichet unique.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de constitution de la société (unified incorporation dossier) filed electronically through the INPI Guichet unique, culminating in the RCS registration extract (Extrait Kbis).

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 13 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS; the Articles freely fix the amount, usually €1 or more as determined by the shareholders. At incorporation, at least 50% of cash subscriptions must be paid up and deposited with an authorized French bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations by way of a blocked temporary account; the balance may be paid within 5 years. In-kind contributions are fully paid upon issuance and require a statutory contributions auditor (commissaire aux apports) unless exempt. The depositary issues an Attestation de dépôt des fonds, which is included in the filing dossier and released on Kbis delivery.

Funding & unlock path: No dedicated clearance certificate is mandatory, but office premises must comply with the Code du travail and the Code de la construction et de l'habitation: emergency exits and fire-safety equipment, electrical installation verification, accessibility requirements, structural load safety, and the occupational health registration with the CARSAT; if the office is open to the public, the local fire-safety commission (Commission de sécurité) issues the ERP classification for 5th-category premises.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for an SAS; the Articles freely fix the amount, usually €1 or more as determined by the shareholders. At incorporation, at least 50% of cash subscriptions must be paid up and deposited with an authorized French bank, a notary, or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations by way of a blocked temporary account; the balance may be paid within 5 years. In-kind contributions are fully paid upon issuance and require a statutory contributions auditor (commissaire aux apports) unless exempt. The depositary issues an Attestation de dépôt des fonds, which is included in the filing dossier and released on Kbis delivery.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
RESOLVED: (1) That the shareholders, having subscribed to the shares listed in the subscription schedule, ratify all share subscriptions and confirm that the share capital, fixed in the Articles, has been lawfully subscribed and paid up in accordance with Article L.227-2 of the French Commercial Code; (2) That the registered office shall be adopted at [Registered Office Address], France; (3) That the undersigned shall be appointed as President and Director-General, with full authority to bind the Company, open bank accounts and represent the Company before all authorities; (4) That the Chairman shall execute all incorporation filings, publish the legal notice and complete the Guichet unique registration. The resolution was unanimously approved.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sectoral industrial license is required for ISIC 6201 digital services; DRIEETS (Direction régionale de l'économie, de l'emploi, du travail et des solidarités) holds general competence for workplace oversight of service enterprises.

Category: Not required — the LegalTech SaaS activity operates under ordinary commercial registration (Kbis) without a pre-licensing regime, manufacturing permit, or regulated-sector concession.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France uses the euro and has full current-account and capital-account convertibility within the European Union and the euro area. There is no general French exchange-control approval for receiving capital, paying suppliers, repatriating dividends, servicing shareholder loans, or converting euros into foreign currencies. Transfers remain subject to bank anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, sanctions, beneficial-ownership, tax-compliance, and transaction-monitoring controls. Dividend distributions require distributable profits and applicable corporate approvals; withholding tax, treaty relief, beneficial-owner evidence, and French tax reporting may affect net remittance. Interest, royalties, management fees, and other related-party payments require transfer-pricing support, corporate-benefit evidence, and applicable withholding-tax analysis. Transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions, restricted counterparties, protected strategic sectors, or unusual payment structures may require enhanced review, blocking, reporting, or governmental authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to repatriate export proceeds to France and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may generally be held in euros or foreign currencies and may be collected through French, European, or foreign accounts, subject to accounting, tax, customs, sanctions, AML, and statistical-reporting requirements. For intra-EU supplies and exports outside the European Union, the company must retain commercial invoices, contracts, transport evidence, customs records, VAT documentation, and proof of payment. Banks may request transaction-purpose information and supporting documents, and Banque de France or other authorities may require statistical information under applicable reporting frameworks. No universal statutory collection deadline or compulsory currency conversion deadline applies.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France; foreign-exchange policy is principally governed by European Union law, the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, French monetary and financial legislation, and the French Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty through the Directorate-General of the Treasury.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and operating flows; low as the sole French corporate banking platform for a newly incorporated SAS. A regulated European Union electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide accounts, payment services, card acquiring, SEPA transfers, and foreign-exchange services through passporting or local authorization. It does not automatically provide a French bank account, bank deposits protected by the French deposit-guarantee scheme, capital-deposit escrow accepted for incorporation, lending, cash-management depth, or unrestricted access to all clearing products. Stripe and other payment facilitators may be used for customer card payments subject to merchant underwriting, prohibited-business rules, reserve requirements, chargeback controls, PSD2-related allocation of responsibilities, AML screening, and settlement-account verification. Offshore unregulated digital wallets should not be used for capital escrow, tax remittances, payroll, or material treasury balances.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident shareholder, director, or ultimate beneficial owner with an opaque ownership chain or nominee arrangement

Root cause: Non-resident shareholder, director, or ultimate beneficial owner with an opaque ownership chain or nominee arrangement

Defense evidence: Current articles of association; extrait Kbis or equivalent registration evidence; complete ownership chart to natural-person ultimate beneficial owners; shareholder register; passports and proof of address; corporate registries for upstream entities; board or shareholder resolutions; beneficial-owner declaration; tax-residency self-certifications; and notarized, apostilled, certified, or translated documents where requested.
No demonstrated French operating substance for a newly formed LegalTech SaaS company

Root cause: No demonstrated French operating substance for a newly formed LegalTech SaaS company

Defense evidence: Business plan; product and service description; website and customer terms; software-development and hosting contracts; office or registered-address evidence; employment or contractor documentation; projected monthly inflows and outflows; target-market analysis; customer and supplier geographies; invoices or signed contracts; and a written explanation of why France is the operating or holding location.
Expected high-volume card receipts, payment-facilitator settlements, or rapid movement of funds through multiple jurisdictions

Root cause: Expected high-volume card receipts, payment-facilitator settlements, or rapid movement of funds through multiple jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Payment-flow diagram; acquiring and processor agreements; expected transaction volumes and average ticket size; chargeback and refund policy; customer onboarding and fraud controls; sanctions and screening procedures; reconciliation process; settlement-account ownership evidence; source-of-funds memorandum; and sample invoices and customer contracts.
Capital injection or shareholder loan from a foreign group entity with unclear economic purpose

Root cause: Capital injection or shareholder loan from a foreign group entity with unclear economic purpose

Defense evidence: Executed subscription agreement or loan agreement; shareholder or board approval; funds-flow schedule; source-of-funds and source-of-wealth evidence; audited or management accounts of the remitter; ownership proof; tax and transfer-pricing analysis; payment instruction showing capital or loan purpose; and evidence of capitalization or registration in the SAS accounting records.
Payments involving sanctioned, high-risk, or poorly documented countries, counterparties, or intermediaries

Root cause: Payments involving sanctioned, high-risk, or poorly documented countries, counterparties, or intermediaries

Defense evidence: Counterparty due-diligence file; beneficial-owner verification; sanctions-screening results; contract and invoice; shipping or service-delivery evidence; end-use and end-user statement; license or authorization where applicable; bank routing details; and documented escalation and approval under the company's sanctions policy.
Virtual assets, cash-intensive activity, unexplained third-party payments, or personal-account use

Root cause: Virtual assets, cash-intensive activity, unexplained third-party payments, or personal-account use

Defense evidence: Written policy prohibiting personal accounts and unauthorized third-party settlement; source-of-funds records; wallet and exchange compliance evidence if legally relevant; transaction-level reconciliation; independent accounting review; director certification; and a commitment to use regulated financial institutions and segregated corporate accounts.
Inconsistent revenue model, legal-contract automation claims, or possible provision of regulated legal services

Root cause: Inconsistent revenue model, legal-contract automation claims, or possible provision of regulated legal services

Defense evidence: Detailed product architecture; customer use cases; terms identifying the service as software rather than legal advice where applicable; professional-liability and cybersecurity insurance; data-processing agreements; licensing analysis; customer contracts; pricing schedule; and legal memorandum confirming that the activities do not constitute unauthorized regulated professional services.
Tax-residency, CRS, FATCA, VAT, or beneficial-owner information inconsistent with account records

Root cause: Tax-residency, CRS, FATCA, VAT, or beneficial-owner information inconsistent with account records

Defense evidence: French tax identification and registration records; VAT registration where applicable; CRS self-certification; FATCA status documentation; tax-residency certificates; corporate income-tax details; organizational chart; financial statements; and prompt written correction of any discrepancy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity through a regulated French or European bank into a dedicated capital-deposit account before SAS incorporation or capital increase, or into the SAS operational account after incorporation and registration of the capital increase. The remittance should identify the legal nature of the payment as share capital, share premium, shareholder loan, or other documented funding; use the bank's applicable SEPA or international-payment purpose code; name the SAS as beneficiary; and include the shareholder resolution, subscription agreement, loan agreement where relevant, and invoice or corporate-purpose documentation. The bank should verify sender identity, ultimate beneficial ownership, source of funds, sanctions status, and consistency with the corporate documents. For non-euro transfers, use a regulated correspondent-bank chain and retain the SWIFT payment confirmation, exchange-rate record, fee record, and beneficiary-credit evidence. Foreign investment in a protected French strategic activity may require prior notification or authorization; ordinary cloud document automation and e-signature software is not ordinarily subject to sectoral foreign-investment approval, but the activity and technology should be screened against the current French protected-sector rules. Record the transaction in the SAS ledger and financial accounts and complete applicable Banque de France, tax, accounting, and statistical reports.Use SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eligible euro payments within the SEPA area, subject to bank limits and sanctions screening. Use SWIFT cross-border payments, commonly evidenced by an MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for non-SEPA or non-euro suppliers; retain the payment confirmation, invoice, contract, beneficiary verification, and purpose information. Use commercial documentary letters of credit, standby letters of credit, documentary collections, or open-account payments according to counterparty risk and trade terms. A commercial LC should specify issuing and confirming banks, governing rules such as UCP 600 where agreed, required documents, expiry, shipment or service conditions, discrepancy procedures, charges, and sanctions clauses. For SaaS services, payment is generally supported by contracts, invoices, service-acceptance evidence, and subscription records rather than shipping documents. Maintain segregation of duties, dual approval, beneficiary-change call-back controls, invoice matching, sanctions screening, and reconciliation. Payments of dividends, interest, royalties, and related-party service fees require separate corporate, tax, withholding, transfer-pricing, and distributable-profit analysis.Deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with a regulated bank or eligible investment firm, used to hedge documented forecast or committed foreign-currency receivables, payables, or funding exposures and subject to suitability, confirmations, collateral, accounting, and counterparty limits.Non-deliverable forwards where the relevant currency or jurisdiction uses restricted convertibility or where settlement is contractually made in a reference currency; the instrument must be offered by an appropriately regulated counterparty and supported by a genuine underlying exposure and sanctions-compliant settlement.Currency swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps for documented funding or balance-sheet exposures, subject to counterparty credit approval, ISDA or equivalent documentation, collateral terms, valuation, and applicable European Market Infrastructure Regulation requirements.Foreign-exchange options, collars, and participating forwards may be used where the company understands premium, contingent-obligation, leverage, and accounting risks; execution should be limited to hedging rather than speculative trading.For a French SAS, derivatives with financial counterparties may trigger EMIR clearing, reporting, margin, risk-mitigation, legal-entity-identifier, and recordkeeping obligations depending on instrument, counterparty classification, and transaction status. MiFID II conduct and appropriateness or suitability requirements may also apply. Treasury policy should prohibit uncovered speculation, establish board-approved limits, document hedge relationships under applicable accounting standards, and retain confirmations and valuation records.
