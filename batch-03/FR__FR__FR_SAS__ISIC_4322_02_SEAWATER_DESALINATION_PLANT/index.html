Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital applies; capital may be EUR 1 or more. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Funds must be deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts, and the deposit certificate must support incorporation. Contributions in kind must be fully contributed and generally require a commissaire aux apports unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied. The stated USD amount is an approximate conversion of the standard EUR 37.45 registration charge and excludes legal-publication, beneficial-owner, translation, and professional-service costs.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain the applicable building permit and safety approvals from the mairie, consult the Service départemental d'incendie et de secours (SDIS) for fire strategy, provide fire detection, suppression, emergency access, hazardous-material storage, and evacuation measures, and complete structural and equipment conformity inspections. Industrial electrical, pressure-equipment, lifting-equipment, and machinery inspections must be performed by competent bodies where required. Occupational compliance requires a risk assessment document (document unique d'évaluation des risques), occupational-health arrangements, worker training, and labor-law compliance; a separate generic workplace operating clearance is not generally required.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital applies; capital may be EUR 1 or more. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid at incorporation, with the balance payable within five years. Funds must be deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts, and the deposit certificate must support incorporation. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a euro-area Member State and applies full currency convertibility under the EU free-movement-of-capital framework (Articles 63-65 TFEU and Articles L.151-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code). There are no exchange-control permits, no forced conversion requirements, and no restrictions on holding EUR or foreign currency balances in licensed bank accounts. Current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted, subject only to general AML/KYC, sanctions, tax declaration and statistical reporting obligations. Profit remittance from the French SAS is lawful once corporate formalities are completed; dividends may be paid in EUR or foreign currency without FX approval, but are subject to French withholding tax unless reduced by treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive, and to beneficial-ownership, substance and anti-abuse verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion rules exist in French or EU law. There is no statutory repatriation timeline, no minimum conversion ratio and no obligation to surrender foreign currency earned from desalination EPC contracts. Export receipts may be retained in USD/EUR operating accounts or applied directly to foreign-currency trade payables. The only obligations are (i) correct recording of each transaction in the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistics through the resident bank using the applicable purpose code, and (ii) full compliance with EU sanctions, customs and foreign-asset reporting rules. No central-bank licence is required for holding export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), with the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (ACPR) as the integrated banking licensing, AML/CFT and conduct supervisor; the Ministry of Economy and Finance retains sovereignty over financial sanctions, strategic foreign-investment screening and balance-of-payments statistical rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-Low as a primary treasury rail but acceptable as a secondary payment-companion. ACPR-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Qonto, Shine, Treezor or other BaaS providers can offer French IBANs, SEPA/SEPA Instant clearing, multicurrency virtual accounts and corporate cards; however, they cannot grant credit, issue commercial letters of credit, hold project-finance escrow as a licensed credit institution, or provide MiFID II FX derivative execution without a separate investment-services licence. Offshore digital EMIs with no French establishment or EU passport have low viability because they cannot issue French IBANs, create ACPR-authorisation risk if used as a main account, and are frequently debanked under enhanced KYC. Recommended structure: use an ACPR-regulated fintech for operational expenses and supplier convenience payments, while placing project capital escrow, trade disbursements and hedging with tier-1 credit institutions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque beneficial-ownership chain or non-resident holding company incorporated in a low-transparency jurisdiction

Root cause: Opaque beneficial-ownership chain or non-resident holding company incorporated in a low-transparency jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Complete French UBO declaration registered at the Greffe; certificate of incorporation and articles of association in authenticated translation; apostilled ownership chart naming every natural-person shareholder with 25% or more; tax-residence certificates of all intermediate entities; audited financial statements and a written narrative explaining the commercial purpose of each holding layer.
Desalination project counterparties located in high-risk or sanctions-sensitive regions, including advance payments from those jurisdictions

Root cause: Desalination project counterparties located in high-risk or sanctions-sensitive regions, including advance payments from those jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Original signed EPC contract and full commercial invoices; corporate registers and KYC documentation of project owners and end-buyers; host-country government approvals or central-bank permits; end-user and non-proliferation certificates; UN/EU sanctions screening results with same-name explanations; funds-origin evidence and a description of the full payment chain.
Large and rapid capital injections inconsistent with the company's stated share capital and project stage

Root cause: Large and rapid capital injections inconsistent with the company's stated share capital and project stage

Defense evidence: Shareholder resolution approving the capital increase; capital deposit attestation issued by the receiving bank; source-of-wealth and source-of-funds statements; shareholder bank statements evidencing provenance of funds; project-finance facility agreement or term sheet; board-approved treasury forecast showing allocation of injected funds to EPC costs and escrow requirements.
Nominee directors, absence of local substance, or reliance on a managed-service provider for the French SAS

Root cause: Nominee directors, absence of local substance, or reliance on a managed-service provider for the French SAS

Defense evidence: Directors' declaration confirming real executive responsibilities; CV and business track record; minutes of board and shareholder meetings; French registered-office lease and utility bills; substance documentation for France such as payroll records, project-management location, and decision-making minutes; tax-residence certificates of all directors and beneficial owners.
Disbursement instructions to intermediary payees not named in the EPC contract or to unrelated shell companies

Root cause: Disbursement instructions to intermediary payees not named in the EPC contract or to unrelated shell companies

Defense evidence: Escrow/segregation agreement and approved payee schedule; executed supplier and subcontractor agreements; bank confirmation of payment purpose codes; invoices, delivery and acceptance certificates; withholding and tax clearance documentation; a written commercial rationale for each intermediary beneficiary and its connection to the desalination project.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions must be wired from the shareholder's own bank account to the French SAS account in the exact corporate name. Use a SWIFT MT103 and include in field 70 the remittance text 'APPORT EN CAPITAL / [SAS name] / [shareholder name] / [date of capital increase resolution]'. The receiving bank will require the capital-increase attestation or 'attestation de dépôt des fonds' before permitting operational use. For non-EU shareholders, no prior FX approval is required, but the resident bank will report the transaction to Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics using the applicable direct-investment purpose code. Where the capital increase or share-transfer gives a non-EU shareholder voting rights of 25% or more in an entity owning sensitive water infrastructure, prior French strategic-sector FDI screening approval may be required under Articles L.151-1 et seq.; in such cases the subscription cannot close until approval or the legal waiting period has elapsed. Project-financing escrow inflows from lenders or clients should be tagged with the underlying project name, facility agreement and disbursement conditions.EU/EEA supplier and subcontractor payments should be cleared through SEPA Credit Transfer, including SEPA Instant where required for urgent milestone payments. Non-SEPA and foreign-currency payments should be routed by SWIFT MT103 for customer credits, with MT202 used only for interbank treasury repositioning between the SAS's own accounts. Large equipment purchases for the desalination plant should be supported by irrevocable at-sight or usance commercial letters of credit opened by the French bank in favour of manufacturers, with full transport, insurance and acceptance documentation. Escrowed client funds must be released only against signed milestone certificates, approved drawdown requests and completion reports. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal year ending 31 December, the deadline is the second business day after 1 May of the following year; for other fiscal year-ends, the deadline is within 3 months of the year-end.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 (Résultat fiscal) Taxable income (or loss) after adjustments for non-deductible expenses and tax credits. Non-resident entities must report French-source income only; allocate expenses proportionally if applicable. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due, increased by 0.5% per month of delay, capped at 10% of the tax due; additional 10% penalty for failure to file after a formal notice; interest on late payment at 0.20% per month.
Box 2 (Déficits reportables) Tax losses carried forward from prior years, subject to the €1,000,000 plus 50% of excess limitation. Non-resident entities may carry forward losses only if they have a permanent establishment in France. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due, increased by 0.5% per month of delay, capped at 10% of the tax due; additional 10% penalty for failure to file after a formal notice; interest on late payment at 0.20% per month.
Box 3 (Crédits d'impôt) Tax credits (e.g., CIR, CII, foreign tax credits) applied against the tax liability. Non-residents must attach supporting documentation for any foreign tax credit claimed. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due, increased by 0.5% per month of delay, capped at 10% of the tax due; additional 10% penalty for failure to file after a formal notice; interest on late payment at 0.20% per month.
Box 4 (Résultat fiscal) Final taxable base after adjustments; used to compute the corporate income tax at 25% (or 15% for eligible small profits). Ensure all French-source income is correctly reported; non-residents without a PE are subject to withholding tax instead of filing Form 2065. Late filing penalty: 5% of the tax due, increased by 0.5% per month of delay, capped at 10% of the tax due; additional 10% penalty for failure to file after a formal notice; interest on late payment at 0.20% per month.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400,000,000 (turnover or balance sheet total) for the entity or the group to which it belongs; below this threshold, simplified documentation may suffice.

Economic substance scope: France applies substance-over-form principles and anti-abuse rules (e.g., Article 155-A of the French Tax Code) to prevent artificial arrangements. While no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) exist, the entity must demonstrate real economic activity (e.g., premises, employees, decision-making) to maintain tax residency and treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years (Article 1741 of the French Tax Code); civil penalties up to 80% of the evaded tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes if they participated in or knowingly allowed the evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed; criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, and imposition of 40% penalty for abuse of law (Article 64 of the French Tax Procedure Code); potential criminal charges for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for the company's tax debts if they are found to have created or used the shell structure; personal fines up to €75,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 5% of turnover (Monetary and Financial Code); criminal penalties up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine for money laundering (Article 324-1 of the Criminal Code).

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable for AML breaches if they knowingly participated or failed to prevent; personal fines, imprisonment, and prohibition from managing a company for up to 10 years.
