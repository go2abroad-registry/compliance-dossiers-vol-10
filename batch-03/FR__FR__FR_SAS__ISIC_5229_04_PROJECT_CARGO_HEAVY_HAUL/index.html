Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), acting through the competent Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE). Corporate income tax and VAT administration is conducted through the impots.gouv.fr professional portal. VAT numbers for French-established entities are allocated by the DGFiP. Non-established EU and non-EU businesses generally register through the competent French VAT service for foreign businesses or appoint a French tax representative where legally required.

E-government filing portal

Obtain the SIREN and SIRET identifiers and the applicable activity classification from the national business register. 3. Open the professional tax account on impots.gouv.fr using the legal representative's or authorized agent's credentials. 4. Submit the required tax registration information, including legal name, registered office, business activities, accounting period, bank details, establishment information, and expected VAT transactions. 5. Request VAT registration from the competent SIE where the SAS conducts taxable activities. 6. Receive the French VAT identification number in the format FR plus a two-digit key and the nine-digit SIREN. 7. For intra-Community transactions, validate counterparties' VAT numbers through the EU VIES system and maintain transaction evidence. A foreign entity without a French establishment must register before undertaking French VAT-taxable transactions when no applicable simplification or reverse-charge rule removes its registration obligation. A non-EU entity generally requires a French fiscal representative unless a specific legal or treaty-based exemption applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by default. An SAS meeting statutory conditions may elect temporarily for income-tax treatment (impôt sur le revenu), generally for a maximum of five financial years, subject to conditions concerning ownership, employee headcount, turnover or balance-sheet size, unlisted status, business activity, and individual ownership of capital and voting rights. The election is not a general United States-style check-the-box election. No general French hybrid-mismatch election changes the entity's legal classification. Anti-hybrid mismatch provisions may deny deductions or impose inclusion where arrangements produce deduction/non-inclusion or duplicate deduction outcomes. French tax residence generally follows the place of effective management or the location of the registered office, subject to applicable tax treaties. A non-resident entity is taxed on French-source profits or profits attributable to a French permanent establishment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

France has no universal VAT registration threshold for a business that is required to charge French VAT. Small-business franchise en base thresholds may apply instead. For services, including many logistics services, the principal annual turnover threshold is EUR 37,500, with a tolerance threshold of EUR 41,250 under the applicable rules. For deliveries of goods and comparable activities, the principal threshold is EUR 85,000, with a tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500. A business exceeding the relevant threshold or otherwise losing the franchise must register and charge VAT from the legally prescribed date. Foreign businesses may have a French VAT registration obligation from their first taxable French transaction, irrespective of the domestic small-business threshold, unless reverse charge, an exemption, or another simplification applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from France to a destination outside the European Union may be invoiced without French VAT when the supplier establishes that the goods physically leave the EU and satisfies the French customs and documentary requirements. The supplier must retain the export declaration and customs evidence of exit, normally including the Movement Reference Number (MRN) and an electronic exit result or equivalent proof. Intra-Community supplies of goods may be exempt from French VAT where the customer is identified for VAT in another EU Member State, the goods are dispatched or transported to that Member State, and the supplier retains the required transport and VAT-number evidence. For services, France generally applies place-of-supply rules rather than a technically defined zero-rate. B2B services supplied to a business established outside France are generally located at the customer's establishment and are invoiced without French VAT where the conditions are met. B2B services supplied to an EU business are generally subject to the customer's Member State reverse charge. Logistics, freight-forwarding, and heavy-haul services require transaction-specific analysis, particularly where services are connected with imports, exports, immovable property, passenger transport, or intermediary services. Input VAT refunds require valid invoices, business purpose, non-exempt taxable activity, and compliance with applicable deduction restrictions.

  • Commercial invoice identifying the supplier, customer, goods, value, delivery terms, and VAT treatment
  • French or EU customs export declaration
  • Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Electronic customs exit confirmation or other legally accepted proof that the goods exited the European Union
  • Packing list and transport documentation, including bill of lading, CMR consignment note, or equivalent multimodal transport record
  • Evidence linking the commercial invoice, export declaration, goods, and transport movement
  • Import or destination-country customs documentation where available
  • Customer VAT identification evidence for intra-Community supplies, where applicable
  • Proof of payment or bank records where relevant to evidencing the transaction; France does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a statutory condition for export VAT exemption
  • Contracts, purchase orders, freight instructions, delivery records, and subcontractor records supporting the actual supply and export chain

France is implementing mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic transaction reporting through approved private platforms (plateformes agréées), with the French public invoicing portal serving as the state platform framework. All French-established businesses must be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026. Issuing obligations are scheduled to apply from 1 September 2026 to large and medium-sized businesses and from 1 September 2027 to small and micro-enterprises, subject to statutory implementation changes. Domestic B2B invoices will generally be transmitted through an approved platform, and specified B2C and cross-border transactions will be reported through e-reporting. France does not operate a universal real-time invoice-clearance requirement for all transactions. Public-sector invoices are submitted through Chorus Pro. There is no universal statutory digital stamp requirement for ordinary commercial invoices, but invoices must contain mandatory VAT particulars, be issued and retained in an auditable form, and satisfy authenticity, integrity, and legibility requirements.

France has no general statutory 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export VAT treatment. Export proceeds may remain abroad subject to banking, anti-money-laundering, accounting, sanctions, and transfer-pricing requirements. If export exemption or an input VAT refund is unsupported, the DGFiP may reassess output VAT, deny or claw back input VAT, charge late-payment interest, and impose tax penalties. Common penalties include a 10% surcharge for specified late or non-compliant filings, a 40% surcharge for deliberate failure, and an 80% surcharge for fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights, subject to statutory conditions and procedural safeguards. Late-payment interest is generally charged at the statutory rate applicable for the relevant period. Customs authorities may apply Union Customs Code recovery, administrative penalties, seizure, or other customs measures for false declarations or unsupported export status. Serious intentional tax fraud may also trigger criminal proceedings, including imprisonment and substantial fines. Export documentation must therefore be retained for the applicable French accounting and tax limitation periods, commonly at least six years for tax audit purposes, with longer retention where another legal obligation applies.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions within the applicable European Union framework. A French SAS may generally receive, hold, convert, and remit euros and foreign currencies without a general licensing requirement, mandatory use of a domestic bank, capital controls, or profit-repatriation approval. Dividends, interest, royalties, management fees, shareholder-loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted subject to distributable-profit rules, corporate approvals, withholding-tax analysis, transfer-pricing requirements, treaty entitlement, beneficial-owner verification, anti-money-laundering controls, sanctions screening, and proof of the underlying transaction. Banks may require invoices, contracts, board resolutions, tax forms, source-of-funds evidence, and ultimate-beneficial-owner information before execution. Foreign direct investment screening may apply to investments in protected strategic sectors; ordinary project-cargo logistics is generally outside the protected-sector perimeter unless the transaction involves a protected asset, technology, infrastructure, or activity. Cash payments and certain cross-border cash movements remain subject to anti-money-laundering and customs restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement for an exporter to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no general statutory deadline requiring conversion of foreign-currency export receipts into euros. Export proceeds may generally remain in foreign-currency accounts or be converted at the company's discretion, subject to bank controls, accounting in euros, tax compliance, sanctions compliance, and contractual financing covenants. Trade, customs, VAT, statistical, and balance-of-payments information may be collected through customs declarations, banking records, Intrastat or successor EU reporting systems, and Banque de France statistical reporting where applicable. Any cash carried across an external European Union border at or above the applicable declaration threshold must be declared to customs. The company should document invoice currency, receipt date, settlement currency, hedge designation, exchange rates, and reconciliation to customs and accounting records.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, operating within the Eurosystem under the monetary authority of the European Central Bank. France has no separate general foreign-exchange administration imposing routine exchange controls. Applicable oversight also includes the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution), the French Financial Markets Authority (Autorité des marchés financiers), the French Customs Directorate for applicable reporting, and the French anti-money-laundering authority Tracfin.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for routine EUR collections, SEPA payments, expense management, and selected foreign-currency services; Medium or Low as the sole treasury bank for capital injections, high-value project-cargo settlements, documentary credits, guarantees, cash deposits, and complex trade finance. A licensed electronic-money institution or payment institution in France or another European Economic Area state may provide services under its authorization and passporting framework, but it does not provide bank deposits unless separately licensed as a credit institution. Customer funds must be safeguarded rather than treated as ordinary insured bank deposits, and services remain subject to payment-services law, safeguarding rules, transaction limits, sanctions screening, AML/KYC, source-of-funds controls, and provider risk appetite. Offshore digital EMIs without an applicable European authorization or lawful passport should not be used for regulated payment services directed at France. Maintain a regulated French or EEA bank as the primary account for shareholder funding, tax payments, payroll, trade finance, and liquidity concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). A reduced 15% rate may apply to qualifying small and medium-sized companies on the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit, subject to statutory ownership, turnover and paid-in-capital conditions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The standard French withholding tax on dividends and similar distributions paid by a French company to a non-resident beneficiary is generally 25%, subject to treaty relief, the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive as implemented in France, domestic exemptions and anti-abuse conditions. The French SAS must generally file and pay the withholding using the applicable withholding tax forms and procedures.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and tax package are generally due within three months after the fiscal year-end. For a financial year ending on 31 December, the filing deadline is generally the second business day following 1 May, subject to the deadline displayed by the French tax administration. Corporate tax instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December. The final balance is generally due by the statutory balance-payment deadline following filing.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December. The fourth instalment and final balance may be subject to special percentage and deadline rules depending on the company's prior-year corporate tax liability and applicable large-company provisions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory full transfer-pricing documentation generally applies where the French entity or French tax group has annual pre-tax turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 400 million, or where it is owned, directly or indirectly, by an entity meeting that threshold, or where it belongs to a qualifying group meeting the statutory conditions. A French entity below the threshold must still apply the arm's-length principle and be able to substantiate its pricing. Country-by-country reporting generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Annual simplified transfer-pricing declarations may apply to entities meeting separate statutory thresholds, commonly including EUR 50 million turnover or gross assets or a qualifying group relationship.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a separate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime applicable generally to ordinary French SAS companies. Nevertheless, French tax law applies substance-over-form principles, the abuse-of-law doctrine, beneficial-ownership requirements, anti-hybrid rules, controlled foreign company rules, interest limitation rules and general anti-abuse provisions. The SAS should maintain French operational substance proportionate to its logistics activity, including genuine management, personnel or outsourced operational control, premises or facilities where appropriate, customer and supplier contracts, decision-making records, bank activity, transport documentation and commercially justified related-party arrangements. A passive conduit or contractual intermediary without corresponding functions, assets and risks is high risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent tax reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, 40% or 80% tax penalties depending on the conduct, and criminal tax-fraud sanctions that may include up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 500,000 fine, increased in aggravated cases to up to seven years' imprisonment and a EUR 3 million fine, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face criminal prosecution, personal fines, confiscation, director disqualification and civil tax-liability proceedings where they organized or materially contributed to fraudulent non-payment. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from judicial control, criminal proceedings or enforcement measures.
VAT fraud and carousel transactions

Statutory sanction: VAT reassessment, recovery of improperly deducted or refunded VAT, late-payment interest, 40% or 80% penalties, potential joint and several liability in specified fraud circumstances, and criminal fraud sanctions.

Director personal liability: A director may incur criminal liability for knowingly participating in VAT fraud and may face personal asset exposure where serious and repeated tax misconduct supports a judicial tax-liability claim.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and profit diversion

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to arm's-length profit, interest, tax penalties, documentation penalties and possible withholding-tax reassessment. Failure to provide required transfer-pricing documentation can result in a specific penalty, potentially up to 0.5% of the amount of transactions lacking documentation or 5% of the reassessed profits, subject to statutory limits and the applicable calculation.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil and, where intentional fraud is proven, criminal consequences for directing artificial profit shifting. Normal commercial losses do not create personal liability if pricing is commercially supported and properly documented.
Shell company, conduit or abuse-of-law arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty or domestic tax benefits, recharacterization of transactions, corporate tax and withholding-tax reassessment, abuse-of-law penalties that may reach 80% in aggravated cases, and possible criminal investigation where false documents or fraud are involved.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally exposed for knowingly implementing an artificial arrangement, including civil restitution, management bans, confiscation and criminal penalties. A travel ban is possible only if ordered under applicable judicial or enforcement procedures.
Illegal dividend or disguised distribution

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unlawful distributions, corporate and shareholder tax reassessment, withholding tax, penalties, director liability and potential insolvency-related claims. Certain unlawful distributions may constitute criminal corporate-officer misconduct.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable to restore improperly distributed amounts and may face civil damages, management disqualification and criminal sanctions where the distribution was knowingly unlawful.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing failure

Statutory sanction: Criminal money laundering may carry up to five years' imprisonment and a EUR 375,000 fine, with higher sanctions for aggravated or organized conduct. Corporate sanctions, confiscation and regulatory consequences may also apply. Reporting failures by regulated professionals can trigger supervisory sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate laundering may face imprisonment, personal fines, confiscation and management restrictions. Travel restrictions may be imposed by a criminal court or investigative judge but are not an automatic consequence of an AML deficiency.
Beneficial ownership and corporate transparency

Statutory sanction: Court orders, fines, registration restrictions and criminal penalties may apply for failure to disclose or inaccurate disclosure. The company may face enhanced scrutiny by banks, tax authorities and customs authorities.

Director personal liability: The legal representative may be personally prosecuted for knowingly false or incomplete filings and may face fines, management disqualification and, where linked to fraud, confiscation or imprisonment.
Customs, sanctions and export-control breach

Statutory sanction: Seizure, forfeiture, customs recovery, administrative fines, suspension or loss of customs authorizations, and criminal sanctions for serious customs or sanctions offenses. Penalties depend on the goods, conduct, jurisdiction and applicable EU and French measures.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible managers may be prosecuted where they ordered, approved or knowingly tolerated the breach. Courts may impose fines, confiscation, professional bans and, in serious cases, imprisonment or travel restrictions.
False accounting, forged documents and obstruction

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, accounting and corporate-law penalties, evidentiary presumptions against the company, criminal sanctions for forgery or obstruction, and possible confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors, accounting officers and de facto managers may incur personal criminal liability, civil damages, disqualification, confiscation and judicial travel restrictions where ordered.
Undeclared employment and subcontractor compliance

Statutory sanction: Social-security reassessment, surcharges, administrative fines, exclusion from public contracts, joint liability in specified cases and criminal sanctions for concealed work.

Director personal liability: Directors may face criminal liability and personal civil exposure where they knowingly organized concealed work or ignored obvious subcontractor irregularities. Management bans and confiscation may apply in serious cases.
