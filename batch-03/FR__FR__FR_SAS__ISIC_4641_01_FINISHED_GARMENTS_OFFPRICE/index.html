Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises operated by the Institut National de la Propriété Industrielle (INPI), with registration recorded in the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) by the competent commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 and R.227-1 to R.227-2 governing the Société par Actions Simplifiée; Articles L.123-1 et seq. and R.123-1 et seq. governing commercial registration; Articles L.561-46 et seq. governing beneficial ownership declarations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic declaration of creation of a legal entity (formalité de création) submitted through the INPI Guichet unique, together with the signed SAS articles of association and the declaration of beneficial owners.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 32 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The amount is freely determined in the articles of association. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid on incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must generally be fully contributed at incorporation. Funds may be deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the deposit certificate is required for registration. The stated amount excludes the separate beneficial-owner filing fee, legal-notice publication cost, and professional fees.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain premises fire-safety documentation and maintain accessible emergency exits, extinguishers, alarm systems, emergency lighting, evacuation plans, and periodic inspections. If the premises are open to the public, comply with établissement recevant du public rules and obtain the required safety opinion or authorization. Verify structural floor-load capacity for garment storage and racking, secure racking against collapse, perform electrical and equipment inspections, prepare the required workplace risk assessment document (document unique d’évaluation des risques professionnels), and provide occupational safety training. No separate general occupational operating clearance is normally required for an ordinary wholesale warehouse.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. The amount is freely determined in the articles of association. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid on incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Contributions in kind must generally be fully contributed at incorporation. Funds may be deposited with a bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations; the deposit certificate is required for registration. The stated amount excludes the separate beneficial-owner filing fee, legal-notice publication cost, and professional fees.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable for ordinary off-price wholesale trading of finished garments. For premises or storage activities potentially falling under an environmental classification, the competent authority is the relevant Préfecture and its regional environmental directorate, Direction Régionale de l’Environnement, de l’Aménagement et du Logement (DREAL).

Category: No general manufacturing or industrial operating licence is required for ordinary wholesale trading of finished garments. A premises-specific authorization, declaration, or registration may be required if the warehouse falls within a classified installation regime under the French nomenclature des installations classées pour la protection de l’environnement (ICPE).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - National tax authority; official portals: impots.gouv.fr and Douane.gouv.fr (customs/VAT on imports/exports)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities obtain a French SIREN/SIRET and Tax ID (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) by filing Form 331A (Cerfa 11984) with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) of the French tax representative or permanent establishment. For remote sellers, simplified electronic VAT registration via impots.gouv.fr is available; an EORI number must also be obtained through pro.douane.gov.fr for customs operations. The registration is effective immediately and a French VAT ID is issued.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Foreign entities obtain a French SIREN/SIRET and Tax ID (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire) by filing Form 331A (Cerfa 11984) with the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) of the French tax representative or permanent establishment. For remote sellers, simplified electronic VAT registration via impots.gouv.fr is available; an EORI number must also be obtained through pro.douane.gov.fr for customs operations. The registration is effective immediately and a French VAT ID is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. A newly created SAS may elect for up to 5 fiscal years to be treated as pass-through (assujettie à l'impôt sur le revenu) under CGI Art. 239 bis AA, provided it files the option before the end of the third month after incorporation and satisfies the SME and ownership tests. Alternatively, certain qualifying SASs may irrevocably elect corporate income tax treatment under CGI Art. 206. Group VAT and corporate income tax elections are available under French organic tax law for established groups. Anti-hybrid rules under EU ATAD and CGI Art. 212 bis / 214 ter restrict mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for non-established foreign entities making taxable supplies in France; a mandatory French VAT registration is triggered from the first taxable transaction. For domestic small businesses the annual turnover exemption is EUR 85,000 for goods and EUR 37,500 for services. The EU distance-selling threshold of EUR 35,000 applies to intra-Community remote sales to French consumers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from France to a destination outside the EU qualify for 0% VAT where the exporter retains proof of physical dispatch and the export entry information, and the goods leave the EU by way of a customs formality (EX-E electronic declaration or paper DAU). Intra-Community supplies of goods to another EU member state are zero-rated if the customer holds a valid EU VAT ID and the EC-sales list and INTRASTAT are filed. Services directly related to exported goods, or rendered to a non-EU customer, where the place of supply is outside the EU, are also zero-rated; otherwise an exemption may require evidence that the customer is established outside the EU and the service is actually used outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic DAU / EX-E)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / Airway Bill as the proof of transport
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Attestation de domiciliation bancaire) or bank proof of foreign-exchange repatriation
  • Commercial Invoice with full Incoterms

B2G e-invoices must be submitted through the Chorus Pro portal using Factur-X formats. Mandatory B2B e-invoicing is phased-in from 1 September 2026 for large taxpayers and 1 September 2027 for all taxable persons; e-reporting of transaction data (B2B cross-border and B2C sales) is required via certified API infrastructure. France, however, does not operate a general real-time fiscal clearance/stamping regime for wholesale trading; retail cash receipts require approved fiscal memory software or a certified fiscal till system.

Tax authorities may retroactively reclaim zero-rated VAT where export evidence is deficient, supported by a penalty of 40% for lack of good faith (défaut de bonne foi) or 80% for abusive/manifestly false declarations (CGI Art. 1729). Monthly late-payment interest accrues at 0.55% per month. Customs false export declarations carry a separate penalty under the Code des Douanes (Art. 413-14) of up to EUR 750,000 and a potential jail term; repeat or organised fraud reduces all VAT and customs reliefs and triggers full clawback plus interest and sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is a eurozone member; the euro is fully convertible and the current account is free from exchange control restrictions. Capital account transactions are likewise open, with no prior authorization required for ordinary commercial wholesale activities such as finished-garments liquidation trading. Profit remittances to non-residents are permitted free of any foreign exchange control barrier, subject only to French withholding tax on dividends or interest unless reduced under an applicable tax treaty or the EU Parent-Subsidiary/Interest and Royalties Directives. Non-resident shareholders must however observe French fiscal and administrative declarations, including Banque de France outward payment reporting for direct investment transactions. No restrictions apply to invoicing or settling off-price apparel imports and exports in EUR, USD, or other freely convertible currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory mandatory surrender or conversion ratio for export proceeds for French-resident entities. Export receipts in foreign currency may be retained in domestic or foreign-currency accounts. Nevertheless, all export proceeds must be promptly repatriated to a French/EU bank account for corporate treasury control, and the company must maintain supporting invoices, customs declarations, and bank records for six years for French tax and customs audits. For balance-of-payments statistical purposes, Banque de France may require monthly reporting of cross-border settlements above the applicable threshold, but no conversion obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BdF), acting within the Eurosystem and under the legal framework of the French Monetary and Financial Code (Code monétaire et financier) and relevant EU regulations; foreign exchange and cross-border financial relations are jointly administered by the Banque de France and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) for balance-of-payments statistics and inward investment declarations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material constraints. Licensed Electronic Money Institutions in France (e.g., Qonto, Shine, N26) can open a French EUR IBAN for a SAS quickly through fully remote onboarding, which is operationally agile for day-to-day EUR receipts and supplier payments. However, EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot provide documentary letters of credit, trade finance limits, or committed cross-border foreign-exchange hedging facilities. They also may reject complex liquidation wholesaler flows where many beneficiary countries and USD wires are involved. EMIs should therefore be treated as a supplementary collection/disbursement rail only; the primary treasury account and capital escrow should be held with a tier-1 French banking institution.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-EU ultimate beneficial owner located in a tax-haven or FATF grey-listed jurisdiction

Root cause: Non-EU ultimate beneficial owner located in a tax-haven or FATF grey-listed jurisdiction

Defense evidence: Certified copies of passports, apostilled/legalized corporate registry abstracts, bank references from the UBO's primary bank, source-of-wealth documentation including tax returns and audited financial statements, notarized shareholder register, UBO declaration filed in France, FSMA/FATF-compliant risk assessment, and a commercial rationale letter describing the SAS's actual activities in France and the EU.
High-volume, multi-country disbursements to numerous apparel suppliers and liquidation intermediaries inconsistent with the entity's short history

Root cause: High-volume, multi-country disbursements to numerous apparel suppliers and liquidation intermediaries inconsistent with the entity's short history

Defense evidence: Executed wholesale and consignment contracts, warehouse leases, inventory reconciliation reports, original supplier invoices with HS/ISIC codes, bill of lading and customs entry evidence, pro-forma transaction-flow diagram, the company's AML risk self-assessment, board resolution approving treasury limits, and an external auditor's letter confirming negative pledge and beneficial ownership of the goods.
Transactions with counterparties in countries flagged for sanctions or high-money-laundering risk, especially in secondary apparel export territories

Root cause: Transactions with counterparties in countries flagged for sanctions or high-money-laundering risk, especially in secondary apparel export territories

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident shareholders must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through the receiving French bank's clearing channel. The payment must clearly indicate the French SAS SIREN number and purpose using Banque de France's international transaction nomenclature (direct investment purpose code for capital increases, or loan purpose code for shareholder advances). Non-EU investors must file the statutory direct foreign investment declaration with Banque de France after the operation, unless the sector is strategic and requires prior authorization; finished-garments wholesale is not a regulated sensitive sector. A compliance-ready package should include the board/shareholder resolution approving the capital contribution, the notarized or certified copy of the share issuance deed, source-of-funds documentation, and the updated RNE extract. If the capital is injected as a shareholder loan, a formal loan agreement with commercial terms, ISIN/purpose label, and French tax entity reporting is required. The receiving bank will verify OBO (ordering bank) and BIC validations and may request six months of source-account statements for AML purposes.For trade settlements to suppliers and third-party liquidators, the preferred clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 for high-value cross-border USD/EUR wires; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR payments to EU counterparties; (3) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for urgent disbursements; (4) Commercial letters of credit issued by the tier-1 French bank under UCP600, because off-price apparel liquidation frequently requires documentary protection against title disputes; and (5) trade collection (documents against payment) for smaller recurring purchases. The SAS should maintain a dedicated USD correspondent account at a French clearing bank with access to the U.S. Fedwire system if paying U.S. vendors, while flows to Southeast Asian and South Asian suppliers may be routed through EUR-USD cover payments. All outbound wires must include precise item descriptions, HS codes, customs invoice numbers, and the French exporter reference to satisfy both the bank's transaction monitoring and the French customs authorities.Outright EUR/USD FX forwards with maturity up to 12 months under ISDA or French FBF master agreementsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) booked by a French licensed credit institution, permitted for hedging restricted-currency receivables when settlement in non-convertible currency is impossibleCurrency swaps (FX swaps) combining spot and forward legs for managing short-term liquidity in USD, GBP, and CNY if the bank approves a trading linePlain-vanilla EUR/USD and EUR/GBP options purchased for downside protection on liquidation receivablesCross-currency interest-rate swaps solely when the SAS has an underlying cross-border debt facility, not for speculative purposes
