Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS) held by the Greffe du Tribunal de commerce having territorial jurisdiction over the registered office; filings are made through the INPI 'Guichet unique électronique'.

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), Book II, Title VII, Chapter VII — Articles L.227-1 through L.227-32 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); Article L.210-6 (legal personality upon registration); Articles R.210-1 to R.210-8 and R.123-1 et seq. (RCS filing formalities); Décret n°67-236 and related regulatory acts as codified.

Formation / constitutional filings

Déclaration de création d'entreprise / formulaire M0 (Cerfa n°13627*06) — transmitted as part of the electronic 'Dossier unique d'immatriculation au RCS' through the INPI portail.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 26 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No legally prescribed minimum share capital; a zero-EUR capital is permitted. Where a positive capital is adopted, cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations account and may be withdrawn only after RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a report by a statutory appraiser (commissaire aux apports) unless an exemption applies.

Funding & unlock path: Commercial premises must comply with Code du travail fire-safety rules (access, extinguishers, means of egress, periodic fire checks) and electrical installation safety verifications by a competent service. If a physical retail/showroom is open to the public, it may be classified as ERP and require fire-safety approval from the commission de sécurité. For warehouses/offices not open to the public, fire-safety compliance is evidenced by internal checks and insurance inspections. Structural load of existing buildings is attested by the landlord's or structural engineer's certificate, and workplaces must maintain a risk assessment (DUERP) and meet occupational health clearance requirements.

Paid-in capital rule: No legally prescribed minimum share capital; a zero-EUR capital is permitted. Where a positive capital is adopted, cash contributions must be deposited in a blocked bank, notary, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations account and may be withdrawn only after RCS registration. In-kind contributions require a report by a statutory appraiser (commissaire aux apports) unless an exemption applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

French Directorate General of Public Finances (Direction générale des finances publiques, DGFiP), through the relevant Service des impôts des entreprises (SIE); foreign businesses are generally administered by the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE). Official portal: impots.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS is registered through the French business formalities one-stop shop (Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises), operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). The registration process results in allocation of a SIREN and SIRET number and tax registration with the DGFiP. The French VAT identification number is generally in the format FR followed by a two-digit control key and the entity's SIREN. A non-established foreign entity making taxable supplies in France must apply for French VAT registration with the DGFiP, generally through the SIEE or the competent tax service. It must provide incorporation documents, proof of business activity, identification of directors and beneficial owners, evidence of taxable transactions, bank and contact details, and, where applicable, a French fiscal representative. Entities established outside the European Union generally require a French fiscal representative unless an applicable mutual assistance arrangement or specific exemption applies. OSS registration may be used for qualifying intra-EU distance sales and certain electronic commerce supplies, but it does not replace a French VAT registration where domestic taxable transactions or other registration obligations arise.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is generally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés). An eligible SAS may elect temporary taxation under the personal income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu) under Article 239 bis AB of the French Tax Code, subject to statutory ownership, age, activity, size and duration conditions; the election is generally limited to five financial years and is not a general check-the-box election. A SASU may also have access to specific income-tax options subject to statutory conditions. France has no United States-style entity classification election. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules implement the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive and do not constitute an elective pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general French VAT registration threshold applies to a non-established business carrying out taxable transactions in France. For French-established businesses eligible for the franchise en base de TVA, the principal annual turnover threshold for sales of goods and equivalent retail activities is EUR 85,000, with a higher tolerance threshold of EUR 93,500, subject to the applicable tax year and statutory rules. For qualifying intra-EU distance sales and certain electronically supplied services, the Union-wide EUR 10,000 annual threshold applies; above that threshold, VAT is generally due in the customer's Member State, with OSS available as a compliance mechanism. The French franchise thresholds do not eliminate VAT registration where the business voluntarily opts into VAT, makes intra-EU acquisitions, imports goods, or performs transactions for which registration is otherwise required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to a destination outside the European Union may be exempt from French VAT with the right to deduct input VAT if the goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU by the seller or by a qualifying customer and the exporter can substantiate the physical exit from the EU. The exemption normally requires a valid customs export procedure, consistency between commercial, transport and customs records, and retention of proof of exit. Intra-EU supplies of goods are generally exempt as intra-Community supplies rather than exports, subject to customer VAT identification, transport evidence, recapitulative statement and other conditions. Services are not automatically zero-rated: the place-of-supply rules determine whether French VAT applies. Certain services supplied to non-established business customers may be outside the scope of French VAT, while services directly connected with exported goods or qualifying international transport may benefit from specific exemptions. Export VAT refunds require valid purchase invoices, deductible business use, compliance with filing requirements and evidence that the underlying transactions satisfy the applicable exemption or place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs export declaration containing the correct exporter, consignee, commodity classification, customs procedure and statistical data
  • MRN and electronic proof of exit from the European Union, including the customs exit result or equivalent certified exit evidence
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and transport documentation, such as bill of lading, air waybill, CMR consignment note or courier manifest
  • Purchase orders, sales contracts, delivery records and accounting entries linking the goods to the export transaction
  • Evidence of export-related freight, insurance and logistics arrangements where relevant
  • Customer and destination records sufficient to demonstrate dispatch to a non-EU destination
  • Inward remittance evidence only where required by a financing, banking, contractual or audit policy; France does not generally require an inward remittance certificate as a statutory condition for export VAT exemption

France is introducing mandatory electronic invoicing and transaction reporting on a staged basis. From 1 September 2026, all businesses must be able to receive electronic invoices; issuance obligations apply from that date to large and intermediate-sized businesses and from 1 September 2027 to small and micro-enterprises, subject to statutory implementation rules. Domestic B2B electronic invoices will generally be transmitted through an approved private platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire, PDP), with the public portal serving the state reporting infrastructure. The system is based on structured invoice exchange and transmission of transaction and payment data, rather than universal real-time invoice clearance at the time of issuance. Cross-border B2B transactions and B2C transactions may require e-reporting rather than domestic B2B e-invoice transmission. Current VAT invoices and records must remain compliant with French authenticity, integrity, legibility, retention and audit-trail requirements. Retail B2C sales generally do not require an invoice unless requested or otherwise required, but VAT receipts and cash-register controls may apply.

The French tax administration may deny or claw back export VAT exemption where export evidence is missing, contradictory, late, falsified or insufficient to prove exit from the EU. The resulting VAT, interest for late payment and applicable penalties may be assessed retrospectively, generally within the statutory limitation period, subject to fraud and other extended-period rules. Deliberate or serious inaccuracies may trigger tax penalties based on the nature and gravity of the conduct, including penalties for bad faith or fraudulent manoeuvres; fraudulent conduct may also create criminal exposure. Customs infringements can result in additional customs debt, administrative penalties, seizure or forfeiture measures. Input VAT refunds may be reduced or refused where invoices, business purpose, payment records, transport evidence or export substantiation are deficient. France has no general statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline of 180 days for ordinary export proceeds; any 180-day requirement would arise only from a specific financing, banking, grant or contractual arrangement rather than the general French VAT regime.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully and freely convertible in both the current and capital accounts. France applies complete current-account liberalisation under Articles 63-66 TFEU and the EU Code of Liberalisation; capital movements within the EU/EEA are unrestricted, and movements vis-à-vis third countries are covered by the EU grandfathering regime (Regulation (EC) No 1869/2005). No exchange-control licence, prior central-bank authorisation or documentary approval is required to open foreign-currency accounts, to hold USD/CNY/GBP receipts, or to remit distributable profits to foreign shareholders. The only operative control is indirect: the SAS's banks file balance-of-payments declarations (opérations de change) with the Banque de France for each transaction at or above EUR 12,500. Profit remittance from an SAS is unrestricted, but is subject to a shareholder dividend resolution, standard AML documentation, and French withholding tax (25% for distributions decided in 2024, 26.5% in 2025, 25% from 2026) unless reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive for a qualifying EU corporate shareholder or under an applicable double-tax treaty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export proceeds in France. A French SAS may retain export receipts indefinitely in authorised foreign-currency settlement accounts (comptes en devises) and may convert them at its commercial discretion and at market-competitive pricing. The sole compliance duties are: (i) the customs export declaration (SAD/DAU) at the point of shipment; and (ii) the periodic balance-of-payments statistical declaration filed by the instructing credit institution to the Banque de France, using the OECD purpose code S2000 (trade in goods) and the transaction reference. No percentage of export receipts must be converted into EUR and no conversion timeline is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), exercising delegated foreign-exchange, balance-of-payments and financial-stability functions under the Code Monétaire et Financier (CMF), within the European System of Central Banks (ESCB) and the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM) of the European Central Bank (ECB).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital contributions and shareholder loans (comptes courants d'associés) must arrive via SWIFT MT103 (ISO 20022 pacs.008) from the investor's regulated bank. Required data: OECD purpose code S1800 (direct investment - equity capital) or S1810 (shareholder loan), EUR or named foreign currency, and remittance narrative 'Apport en capital - [SAS legal name] - [deed/reference]'. The receiving bank files the Banque de France foreign-direct-investment (IDE) statistical declaration; because e-commerce retail of camping equipment is not in the Art. L151-3 CMF sensitive-sector list, a non-EU/EEA investor needs no prior authorisation, only the post-hoc IDE/balance-of-payments declaration. Supporting documents: shareholder resolution (procès-verbal d'assemblée générale), capital-subscription deed (acte de souscription) for contributions in kind, the bank's capital-deposit certificate (attestation de dépôt de fonds), and, for injections above EUR 1 million, the corporate-group structure chart and ultimate-investor identity file.Disbursements will clear through four rails: (i) SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer via the French STET/ECB-TIPS platforms for EUR supplier payments across the EU, with same-day or T+1 execution and low per-transaction cost; (ii) SWIFT MT103 / ISO 20022 pacs.008 single-customer credit transfers for USD, CNY and GBP invoice payments, carrying the correct OECD purpose code (S2100 for goods-related payments such as freight, or the specific merchandise code) and full structured remittance data; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) governed by UCP 600 (SWIFT MT700/MT707) for high-value bulk imports of camping hardware and textiles from Asia, issued by the French anchor bank and advised through the beneficiary's confirming bank; and (iv) documentary collections (Cash against Documents, D/P) for mid-size supplier balances. All outbound wires are screened against EU restrictive measures, UN sanctions and OFAC lists; the bank requires the pro-forma invoice, purchase contract and HS tariff classification code for every transfer above EUR 5,000, with the SAS retaining the full evidential trail (commande, facture, certificat de conformité CE/UKCA) per disbursement.Deliverable FX spot and outright forward contracts (achat/vente à terme de devises) on EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/CHF and EUR/JPY, documented under the FBF (Fédération Bancaire Française) master agreement and permitted under Articles L211-1 et seq. CMF when consistent with forecast commercial exposure; no underlying-lot certificate is demanded for amounts within the bank's market-risk envelope.FX swaps (swaps de change) and cross-currency swaps for treasury rebalancing of foreign-currency settlement accounts and for hedging a foreign-currency shareholder loan or the EUR revolving credit facility, executed under the ISDA 2002/FBF framework with the anchor bank's market-risk desk.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) - admissible only for thin-market or restricted-convertibility currencies where no onshore deliverable forward is available to a French corporate counterparty; expressly not permitted for EUR/USD or other G10 pairs with a liquid deliverable market, as such use would constitute speculative trading outside the SAS's corporate-object capacity (ultra vires) and would be rejected by compliance.Vanilla FX options (achat d'options de change - calls and puts) for asymmetric protection of import margins on seasonal promotional inventory purchases; premiums are paid in full up-front, no naked short-option positions are permitted for a non-financial SAS, and execution is limited to regulated European counterparties.

(ACPR-licensed, PSAN-registered) and Shine (Société Générale group) provide fully remote onboarding, French IBANs and SEPA Instant clearing suitable for Stripe and PayPal collections. Offshore EU EMIs and neobanks (Wise Europe and Revolut Bank UAB, passporting into France under PSD2/branch registration) are viable as secondary collection or disbursement accounts but are rated Medium as sole treasury counterparties because safeguarding arrangements, contingency funds and trade-finance capabilities do not fully support L/Cs, FX forwards or high-limit documentary disbursements. Legal constraint: the SAS must verify ACPR/EBA register listing, segregated safeguarding of client funds under Articles L526-1 et seq. CMF, and the EMI's French reimbursement plan (fonds de garantie) before concentrating treasury balances.
