Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A French SAS must maintain a declared registered office in France under the French Commercial Code. Domiciliation with a licensed domiciliation company or at the legal representative's premises may be permissible if statutory conditions, written agreements, identification checks, mail access, and document-retention requirements are satisfied. A virtual office alone is not adequate for a parcel-consolidation hub if the company actually sorts, stores, labels, handles, dispatches, or consolidates parcels. The operational site should be separately documented, with appropriate lease or occupancy rights, insurance, health and safety controls, customs records where relevant, and evidence of personnel and equipment. Actual activities may trigger additional rules concerning transport, postal services, customs representation, warehousing, labor, consumer protection, data protection, or site safety despite the stated assumption that no incorporation-stage statutory license is required.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum share capital for SAS. The capital is freely fixed in the articles. Cash contributions must be deposited with a bank or notary in a blocked account pending registration and are released only upon production of the RCS certificate (extrait Kbis); no separate escrow requirement applies beyond this mandatory deposit protocol.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), Ministry for the Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty; tax administration portal: impots.gouv.fr, including the professional tax account and the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères (SIEE) where applicable.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS obtains its SIREN and SIRET numbers through registration with the competent company-formalities authority via the Guichet unique operated by the Institut national de la propriété industrielle (INPI). The DGFiP then assigns the corporate tax account and, where the entity is liable to VAT, a French VAT identification number in the format FR plus two check digits and the nine-digit SIREN. The entity must identify its French establishment, activities, legal representatives, bank details, expected turnover, VAT liability, and applicable filing frequency. A foreign entity without a French establishment applies to the SIEE or the competent tax office before commencing taxable French activities. The application normally includes constitutional documents, certificate of incorporation, foreign tax registration evidence, proof of business activity, representative details, French customer or supplier information where relevant, and a power of attorney if a tax representative is appointed. EU-established businesses generally do not require a French fiscal representative; non-EU businesses may require one for specified VAT refund or compliance procedures. VAT registration is required from the first taxable transaction where no exemption or reverse-charge mechanism applies. The French OSS system may be used for qualifying intra-EU distance sales and certain B2C services but does not replace all French domestic registrations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A SAS is subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) by default. A qualifying SAS may elect temporarily for taxation under the individual income tax regime (impôt sur le revenu), generally for a maximum of five fiscal years, if statutory conditions are satisfied, including qualifying ownership, non-listing, size limits, and commencement of activity within the prescribed period. The election must be made within the statutory deadline and notified to the DGFiP. France has no general US-style check-the-box election for a SAS and no elective hybrid-mismatch classification mechanism. French and EU anti-hybrid rules can deny deductions or impose inclusion where arrangements produce deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exempts a foreign business from French VAT where it makes taxable transactions in France and no reverse charge applies. The domestic franchise en base thresholds are relevant only where their conditions are met: principally EUR 85,000 prior-year turnover and EUR 93,500 current-year tolerance for goods and certain accommodation activities, and EUR 37,500 prior-year turnover and EUR 41,250 current-year tolerance for services and non-commercial activities, subject to statutory sectoral rules and annual legislative adjustments. A parcel-consolidation and logistics business must separately assess French establishment, place-of-supply, import VAT, domestic services, and customer reverse-charge treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from France to destinations outside the European Union may qualify for VAT exemption at the French level where the goods are dispatched or transported outside the EU by the seller, by a carrier acting for the seller, or by the buyer subject to the applicable statutory conditions. The exporter must retain reliable evidence that the goods physically exited the EU customs territory and must reconcile the export transaction with accounting and customs records. Services require separate place-of-supply analysis. B2B services supplied to a business customer established outside France are generally taxed where the customer is established and may be outside the scope of French VAT, subject to customer-status and anti-abuse evidence. Certain services directly connected with exports or international transport may qualify for a specific exemption only if their statutory conditions are met. Freight, warehousing, consolidation, handling, brokerage, and linehaul services must be classified individually; contractual description alone does not establish zero-rating. VAT refunds may be claimed through periodic French VAT returns or, for eligible non-established businesses, under the applicable EU Directive 2008/9/EC or reciprocal non-EU refund procedure.

  • Customs exit results or other customs-generated evidence confirming that the goods left the EU customs territory.

France does not currently operate a universal real-time VAT invoice-clearance system for all private-sector transactions. Domestic B2B e-invoicing reform is scheduled to require all French businesses to be able to receive compliant electronic invoices from 1 September 2026; issuance obligations are scheduled from 1 September 2026 for large and mid-sized businesses and from 1 September 2027 for small and micro businesses, subject to statutory implementation changes. Invoices will generally be transmitted through an approved private electronic invoicing platform (plateforme de dématérialisation partenaire) or the public architecture designated by law. Structured data must include mandatory invoice fields, including supplier and customer identifiers, VAT numbers where applicable, taxable amounts, VAT rates, VAT amounts, payment terms, and transaction category. E-reporting will cover specified B2C and cross-border transaction data. Public-sector invoicing uses the Chorus Pro system. There is no general requirement for digital tax stamping or prior DGFiP clearance of each invoice, although customs electronic declarations and certified electronic records may be required for exports and imports.

France has no general statutory requirement that export proceeds be repatriated within 180 days. Unsupported or incorrectly claimed export VAT exemption may be reassessed as French VAT, together with statutory late-payment interest currently calculated under the applicable French tax rules and tax penalties. A 40% penalty may apply for deliberate non-compliance, and an 80% penalty may apply in cases of fraudulent manoeuvres or abuse of rights; additional penalties can apply for serious or repeated failures, false invoices, concealed transactions, or obstruction of audit. Improperly refunded input VAT may be recovered with interest and penalties. Customs violations may trigger customs debt, administrative fines, seizure or forfeiture measures, and liability under the Union Customs Code and French customs law. Criminal prosecution may arise for aggravated tax fraud, forged documentation, or organized fraudulent export schemes. The DGFiP may retrospectively deny zero-rating where exit evidence is incomplete, inconsistent, untimely, or contradicted by customs, transport, accounting, or payment records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EUR is fully convertible with zero capital controls; France applies EU free movement of capital under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Current account and capital account transactions involving EUR or other freely convertible currencies are not subject to prior central bank approval. Profit remittances from a French SAS are permitted without exchange control licence, subject only to corporate tax distribution formalities, validation by the general meeting for dividends, and anti-money laundering source-of-funds checks by the paying bank. There is no domestic surrender, prior declaration, or repatriation requirement for ordinary commercial and capital flows, except EU balance-of-payments statistical reporting thresholds in certain large cross-border transactions, which is an informational rather than authorising mechanism.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts applicable to a French SAS. Export proceeds may be retained in EUR or in any convertible foreign currency, and may be used for operational payments or repatriated without restriction. A French exporter must nonetheless comply with customs invoicing documentation, transfer pricing rules for intra-group goods or services, and the EU Regulation 2018/1672 cash-control declarations if physically transporting funds. For the e-commerce parcel consolidation model, export proceeds generated through payment aggregators must be credited to the French entity's bank account and should be supported by VAT invoices, customs declarations, and transport manifests.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France and Direction Générale du Trésor (France)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for operational collection and disbursement accounts through ACPR-licensed or EU/EEA passporting electronic money institutions such as Airwallex, Qonto, or Revolut Business, but medium-to-low viability for capital escrow or secured treasury functions because an EMI cannot grant credit, create legal pledge accounts, or provide a full cash management mandate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: €400 million (annual turnover or gross assets) triggers full Master File and Local File; below threshold, simplified documentation required

Economic substance scope: France applies substance-over-form and anti-abuse rules (e.g., Article 155-A, 238-A) to deny treaty benefits or tax advantages if the entity lacks real economic substance (personnel, premises, decision-making). No separate ESR regime, but substance is critical for transfer pricing and treaty eligibility.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: 40% penalty on understated tax (80% if fraudulent), plus interest; criminal prosecution possible with fines up to €500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly liable for corporate tax debts if they participated in fraudulent acts; travel bans and asset freezes possible
Shell Company / Lack of Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of transactions, 40% penalty for abuse of law, and potential criminal charges for tax fraud

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for unpaid taxes and penalties; possible criminal sanctions including imprisonment
AML/CTF Violations

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to €5 million or 5% of turnover; criminal penalties for money laundering up to 5 years imprisonment and €375,000 fine

Director personal liability: Directors can be held criminally liable if they knowingly facilitate money laundering; travel bans and confiscation of assets
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable income, 40% penalty on recharacterized amounts, and interest; documentation failure penalty up to €10,000 per document

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approved non-compliant pricing; potential criminal charges for tax evasion
