Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual offices are permissible for SAS registration in France provided the address is a real commercial premises with a domiciliation agreement (contrat de domiciliation) from a registered domiciliataire approved by the Préfecture per Code de commerce Articles L.123-11-1 to L.123-11-3 and Décret n° 2017-1094. For the silicone fidget manufacturing industry (ISIC 3240), a virtual office is not acceptable for the manufacturing activity itself; a physical production site is required for regulatory and customs compliance. However, the registered office may be domiciled at a virtual office if the manufacturing is outsourced to a third-party contractor with a separate physical facility. The domiciliation agreement must be filed with the RCS and includes the domiciliataire's liability for compliance with AML/CFT obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

EUR is fully convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. France maintains no capital account restrictions for EU-resident entities. For non-resident shareholders, profit remittance is unrestricted after corporate tax declaration and payment. However, outbound transfers exceeding EUR 100,000 to non-EU jurisdictions may trigger AML/CFT reporting under Article L.561-1 of the French Monetary and Financial Code. Routine dividend distributions require documentation of shareholder resolution and tax clearance (if applicable).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender requirement. French exporters may retain foreign currency proceeds in domestic or offshore accounts. Repatriation is not compulsory. However, for statistical reporting, cross-border payments exceeding EUR 125,000 must be reported to Banque de France via the 'Déclaration de Mouvements de Capitaux' (DMC) system within 20 working days. Failure to report may incur administrative fines.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (BDF) and Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) for banking supervision; Direction Générale du Trésor for exchange control policy; European Central Bank (ECB) for monetary policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Qonto are viable for operational accounts but generally restricted for capital escrow or large-volume trade settlements due to limited deposit insurance and prudential oversight. Cross-border EMI accounts for a French SAS are feasible for low-value inbound/outbound payments, but Tier 1 banks remain mandatory for capital injection and LC issuance. Legal constraints: EMIs must be licensed as 'Établissement de Paiement' by ACPR. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Payoneer) are accepted only for platform payouts, subject to FATF-compliant AML policies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection to a French SAS must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'INVEST' or 'CAPITAL'. The receiving bank requires a copy of the incorporation documents (Kbis), shareholder resolution, and a certificate of capital deposit from a notary or lawyer. For amounts exceeding EUR 1 million, the bank may request a source of funds declaration. Verification: the bank will verify the sender's identity against the shareholder register. Funds must be credited to a Capital Escrow Account before registration with the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés).Outbound payments for trade settlements can be executed via SWIFT MT103 for clean payments or SWIFT MT700 for commercial letters of credit (LC). For routine supplier payments, SEPA transfers are preferred for EU-based suppliers. For non-EU suppliers, SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'TRADE' is standard. The bank may require commercial invoice, bill of lading, and customs declaration (DAU) for amounts above EUR 50,000.
