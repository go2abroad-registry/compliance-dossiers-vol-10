Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum capital for an SAS (Art. L.227-2 alinéa 1 C. com.); cash contributions must be paid up to at least 50% upon incorporation (alinéa 2), with the balance payable within 5 years; in-kind contributions must be fully paid upon subscription; paid-up cash must be blocked in escrow with a French bank, a notary or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the Kbis registration certificate is delivered.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: compliance with Code du travail Articles R.4227-1 et seq. (fire extinguishers, emergency exits, alarms and evacuation drills) for employee-only logistics or office premises; any customer-accessible showroom or pickup point triggers ERP (type M) classification and inspection by the departmental fire safety commission (commission de sécurité). Occupational clearance: mandatory Document Unique d'Évaluation des Risques Professionnels (DUERP), affiliation with an occupational health service under Article L.4624-1 C. trav., structural and load inspection certificate issued by an approved bureau de contrôle (e.g., SOCOTEC or Apave), and the lessor's conformity certificate for the leased facility.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum capital for an SAS (Art. L.227-2 alinéa 1 C. com.); cash contributions must be paid up to at least 50% upon incorporation (alinéa 2), with the balance payable within 5 years; in-kind contributions must be fully paid upon subscription; paid-up cash must be blocked in escrow with a French bank, a notary or the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the Kbis registration certificate is delivered.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
By unanimous decision of the shareholders and executive officers of the proposed SAS, made subject to registration, the following is resolved: (1) the draft Articles of Association are adopted exactly as signed; (2) the share subscriptions, capital blocking certificate and the non-cash contribution report are approved; (3) the registered office is fixed at 15 Rue de la Paix, 75002 Paris, France; (4) [NAME] is appointed Président with full authority to represent the Company toward third parties; (5) [NAME] is appointed Directeur Général; (6) signatory authorities and indemnification are established; (7) all formalities for public-notice publication and RCS registration through the Guichet Unique are authorized, with power to attorn to counsel.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sector-specific operating license is required for ISIC 4791 (online retail of maternity and infant supplies). Oversight is exercised by the DGCCRF for consumer-product safety under Regulation (EU) 2023/988 and by the DREAL and the Préfecture for any warehouse facility subject to the ICPE environmental regime.

Category: Exempt from statutory license for the core e-commerce activity; an ICPE declaration or registration authorization (Rubrique 1510 — Entrepôts couverts) is required only if a covered logistics warehouse exceeding 500 m³ of storage volume is operated.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) - Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for direct taxes and VAT; professional portal: impots.gouv.fr and GEST; corporate registration platform: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (Guichet unique). Export and customs supervision: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), portal: pro.douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned French SAS: non-resident shareholders appoint a French resident legal representative and registered office; submit the incorporation dossier electronically through the Guichet unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Upon completion, the company receives Kbis/SIREN, INSEE generates SIRET and NAF/APE code, and the tax administration issues a French VAT identification number (TVA intracommunautaire) in the format 'FR' + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN. If the foreign entity operates without forming a French company but is carrying out taxable transactions in France, it must register directly for VAT at the Centre des Impôts des Non-Résidents (CINR); non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a tax representative under Article 289A CGI where no EU establishment exists.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign-owned French SAS: non-resident shareholders appoint a French resident legal representative and registered office; submit the incorporation dossier electronically through the Guichet unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr. Upon completion, the company receives Kbis/SIREN, INSEE generates SIRET and NAF/APE code, and the tax administration issues a French VAT identification number (TVA intracommunautaire) in the format 'FR' + 2-digit key + 9-digit SIREN. If the foreign entity operates without forming a French company but is carrying out taxable transactions in France, it must register directly for VAT at the Centre des Impôts des Non-Résidents (CINR); non-EU businesses may be required to appoint a tax representative under Article 289A CGI where no EU establishment exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

French law has no US-style check-the-box election; a SAS is irrevocably a corporate entity taxable at entity level under Article 206(1) CGI. Available elections include: (i) fiscal integration / consolidation under Article 223A CGI when a 95% parent controls the SAS; (ii) VAT grouping election (assujetti unique) under Article 256C CGI for closely connected French entities; (iii) waiver of the small-business VAT exemption (renonciation à la franchise en base) under Article 293B CGI. Hybrid mismatch treatment is non-elective and governed by mandatory EU ATAD implementation (Articles 209, 212 bis B, 209 B bis CGI); no check-the-box hybrid section 988 or branch mismatch election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

€85,000 annual turnover exclusive of VAT for sales of goods (Article 293B CGI) as the small-business VAT exemption threshold; €37,500 for services. A French SAS is subject to VAT registration from the first taxable transaction unless it qualifies for and remains within the franchise en base de TVA. The threshold applies to French-established enterprises; foreign distance sellers may be required to register from the first euro unless using the EU OSS/IOSS regimes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Sales of goods physically dispatched or transported by or on behalf of the supplier to a destination outside the European Union qualify for VAT zero-rating under Article 262(I) CGI. The supplier must retain documentary proof that the goods left the EU customs territory before claiming 0% VAT; title and risk must pass to the non-EU customer. Intra-EU B2B supplies are exempt under Article 262 ter CGI only where the customer provides a valid EU VAT identification number and acquisition VAT is accounted for. For cross-border B2B services connected to export supplies, the place of supply rules under Article 259-1 CGI may render the service outside French VAT where the recipient is outside France.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'exportation / DEX submitted via DGDDI DELTA system) with MRN (Movement Reference Number)
  • International transport document: Bill of Lading, CMR, CIM or Air Waybill evidencing departure from EU territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment confirmation evidencing the foreign buyer's settlement of the export invoice

France has implemented a mandatory structured electronic invoicing/e-reporting reform under Article 289(VI) CGI. B2B invoices must be exchanged in structured electronic form through an accredited dematerialization platform (PDP) or the national Public Portal (Portail Public de Facturation - PPF); B2C transaction data must be transmitted through e-reporting to the DGFiP in near-real-time, using the European EN16931 / Factur-X syntax and standard API interfaces. This is a data-clearance model rather than a pre-approval stamping model: fiscal clearance is performed centrally on transaction data after issuance, without digital stamping. Mandatory phases: 1 September 2026 for large and mid-cap enterprises; 1 September 2027 for SMEs and micro-enterprises; non-compliance is sanctionable by graduated administrative fines.

The DGFiP and DGDDI scrutinize export substantiation files; any missing, contradictory or unverifiable customs or transport documentation will trigger immediate re-taxation of the sale at standard French VAT (20%) plus late-payment interest and penalties. Intentional misclassification or fabrication of export documents attracts the Article 1729 CGI 80% penalty for bad-faith concealment, or up to 100% in cases of abuse of law under Article 1729-0 CGI. Fraudulent export zero-rating may also give rise to criminal tax fraud prosecution under Article 1741 CGI with significant fines and imprisonment. Late or non-repatriation of export proceeds beyond the 180-day financial benchmark may invalidate the invoice-payment evidence required in a VAT refund/export audit, and can expose the exporter to a denial of the zero-rating benefit and recovery of input VAT previously deducted.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: France has no exchange controls since 1990 and applies the TFEU Articles 63-65 free-movement-of-capital regime; the euro is freely convertible on current and capital account. No prior administrative clearance is required for equity injection, trade settlement, or profit repatriation for a non-sensitive e-commerce business (ISIC 4791). The residual constraints are fiscal and statistical: outbound dividends may attract treaty-based or domestic withholding tax; every cross-border payment above EUR 10,000 is captured in the bank's FICOBA reporting to the French state; direct investment operations of EUR 15,000,000 or more per transaction require a Banque de France balance-of-payments declaration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. France imposes no statutory surrender, repatriation deadline, or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds; receipts may be retained in any currency in any bank, in France or abroad. The only obligations are informational: cross-border transactions exceeding EUR 10,000 are reported by the bank through FICOBA, and direct-investment flows above EUR 15,000,000 per operation are declared to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) within the Eurosystem, with foreign-exchange and balance-of-payments statistics administered by the Banque de France and the Direction Generale du Tresor; prudential banking supervision and AML/CFT controls are enforced by the ACPR (Autorite de Controle Prudentiel et de Resolution).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for EU/EEA-licensed EMIs passported into France under PSD2 (e.g., Qonto, licensed by the ACPR; Wise Europe, Belgian passport) because they permit fully remote onboarding, euro and multi-currency IBANs, and direct card/PayPal/Stripe settlement. Rating: LOW-MEDIUM for offshore digital EMIs without an EU passport: they are not ACPR-supervised, cannot provide a French euro IBAN, and fail the bank-reference expectation of Tier-1 French banks; they are acceptable only as auxiliary collection/wallet gateways and never as the sole operating account of the French SAS.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Non-resident director or UBO located outside the EEA, especially in a FATF-grey-listed or high-risk third country, without an existing French banking relationship.

Root cause: Non-resident director or UBO located outside the EEA, especially in a FATF-grey-listed or high-risk third country, without an existing French banking relationship.

Defense evidence: Apostilled and sworn-translated passports; recent proof of address; a formal bank-reference letter (attestation de bancarisation) from the originating bank; 6-12 months of clean bank statements; a source-of-funds file for the capital contribution (boards resolutions, shareholder loan agreement, salary/tax records); and the completed French UBO declaration.
Newly incorporated SAS without French credit history, audited financials or demonstrated commercial substance at onboarding.

Root cause: Newly incorporated SAS without French credit history, audited financials or demonstrated commercial substance at onboarding.

Defense evidence: RCS/Kbis extract, notarised statuts, proof of registered office or domiciliation agreement; a credible 3-year business plan; parent-entity financial statements and board resolution; marketplace substance evidence (Stripe/PayPal merchant IDs and processing screenshots); and the French VAT registration number.
E-commerce flows flagged as card-not-present risk, with high platform concentration or abnormal refund/dispute ratios.

Root cause: E-commerce flows flagged as card-not-present risk, with high platform concentration or abnormal refund/dispute ratios.

Defense evidence: 12-month processing statements from Stripe/PayPal; monthly turnover and dispute metrics; VAT returns (CA3 or E-TVA) and customs import records for the baby-products category; returns/refund policy; supplier contracts showing Incoterms and order flows.
Third-country supplier payments (mainly China/Hong Kong) triggering sanctions screening or opaque beneficial-ownership review.

Root cause: Third-country supplier payments (mainly China/Hong Kong) triggering sanctions screening or opaque beneficial-ownership review.

Defense evidence: Commercial invoices and supply contracts with Incoterms; the supplier's business licence and export credentials; customs/import documentation (entry summary declarations) proving physical trade; a full beneficial-ownership declaration of the supplier; confirmation that no sanctioned party appears on any watchlist.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

30% (25% domestic rate plus 12.8% dividend-withholding levy, subject to exemptions and treaty reduction); standard dividend withholding tax at 30% for non-residents, with a 10% administrative surcharge in certain non-cooperative cases.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed by the second business day following May 3 of the year following the fiscal year-end; for fiscal years ending December 31, deadline is generally May 3 (or next business day) unless extended; must be filed within 60 days following the close of the fiscal year when the fiscal year does not coincide with the calendar year (under certain conditions).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million consolidated group revenue (where the group includes the French entity and meets CbCR threshold); Local File must be maintained when the French entity's related-party transactions exceed EUR 400 million group revenue, or when the French entity's own annual related-party turnover exceeds EUR 50 million (Article L.13AA of the French Tax Procedures Code); Master File and Local File thresholds are aligned to CbCR and EU requirements; a simplified documentation obligation applies to intermediate entities with turnover below EUR 50 million.

Economic substance scope: France follows the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and general anti-abuse provisions (abus de droit) under L.64 of the Tax Procedures Code; although not adopting the EU ESR Artificiality Test, the French doctrine requires sufficient economic substance (employees, offices, management decisions, and operational control in France) for an SAS to be recognized as a taxable resident; treaty benefits are denied where a shell parent in a zero-tax jurisdiction lacks substance and the French SAS is controlled from abroad (test of de facto management, Art. 209 B IE and SAAR rules).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: 40% or 80% moral intentionality penalty on understated tax (Articles 1728 and 1729 CGI), plus 100% penalty for concealment, and interest at 0.20% per month; criminal prosecution possible for tax fraud (fraude fiscale) under Article 1741 of the General Tax Code, carrying a penalty of up to EUR 1,000,000 and imprisonment of 5 years (up to EUR 2,000,000 and 7 years for aggravating circumstances).

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally liable for tax fraud or complicity; civil joint and several liability for unpaid corporate tax when fraudulent manoeuvre occurred; travel bans are available in the framework of criminal proceedings and banking restrictions; in serious cases, a director can be personally disqualified and subject to asset seizure.
Shell Company / Substance Abusive Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the SAS as transparent, denial of treaty benefits, application of the French anti-abuse doctrine, imposition of a 100% solidary penalty for abusive transaction, and assessment of hidden income at EUR 0 (reverse burden); potential application of the EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate artificial setups are jointly and severally liable for the full tax debt; can face a personal sanction equal to 5% to 10% of the hidden tax base; imprisonment up to 5 years where criminal evasion is established.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of EUR 10,000 per file not provided, or EUR 100 per page up to the same cap; each missing document is assessed separately; potential recharacterization of profit and double taxation; 40% penalty on adjusted corporate tax base.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign transfer pricing documentation declarations are personally liable for false documentation; can be criminally prosecuted for false statements of value or wilful misrepresentation; the tax administration may hold the director jointly liable when acting maliciously.
AML / Complex Cross-Border Payment Violation

Statutory sanction: French Monetary and Financial Code penalties up to EUR 375,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering; fines up to 5% of turnover for corporations; payment institutions may be blacklisted; temporary or permanent prohibition of commercial activity.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners of the SAS may be personally prosecuted for AML violations under the Criminal Code; assets seized, travel bans issued, and the corporate veil pierced where the SAS is merely an instrumentality of fraud.
Failure to Appoint Statutory Auditor / Accounting Irregularities

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine of EUR 9,000 and imprisonment of 6 months for directors for non-appointment; administrative penalty of EUR 1,500 for failure to file accounts with the registry; nullity of certain corporate decisions possible; additional tax penalties for untruthful tax returns.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the consequences of missing statutory controls; civil liability for losses caused by inaccurate financial statements; criminal consequences for knowingly signing filings containing false information.
