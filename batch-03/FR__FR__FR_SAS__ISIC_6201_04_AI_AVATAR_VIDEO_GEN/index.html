Formation / constitutional filings

Dossier de formalité unique de création d'entreprise : Statuts d'une Société par Actions Simplifiée (SAS) and Formulaire d'immatriculation M0 (Déclaration de création d'une personne morale) submitted electronically through the Guichet Unique.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an SAS; shareholders may set a freely determined amount in the Articles. If any capital is subscribed, full payment for shares represented by in-kind contributions or at least 50% of nominal cash shares must be made at subscription and deposited with a regulated French financial institution or a notary as a sequestration act until the RCS registration. Release of capital occurs after registration and issuance of the Kbis extract.

Funding & unlock path: For offices staffed with employees, the premises must comply with the French Labor Code (Code du travail – articles R.4222-1 onwards): indoor air volume, natural lighting, temperature control, electrical and fire safety, accessible emergency exits, and first aid equipment. A building permit (permis de construire) or prior administrative authorization (déclaration préalable) is required if any structural modifications are made to the rented space. Fire safety certification (attestation de sécurité) is required when premises are open to the public or where the local fire brigade (Service Départemental d'Incendie et de Secours – SDIS) undertakes a technical inspection. The employer must also file a ‘accident du travail’ registration with URSSAF/CARSAT and obtain an occupational health and safety statement; for a SaaS or AI video-generation activity the workplace risk assessment (document unique) must list technical and cyber-risks, and the company must maintain a compliance checklist for electrical equipment and workstations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP) — Ministry of Economy and Finance; professional online portal: impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel); single business formalities portal: formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; VAT administration governed by the Code Général des Impôts (CGI); customs administration (exports, EORI) is supervised by the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), portal: douane.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned SAS obtains statutory tax identifiers as follows: (1) file incorporation and initial disclosures electronically on formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (single national portal effective 1 January 2023); (2) INSEE issues the SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) identifiers and notifies DGFiP, creating the enterprise's tax file; (3) DGFiP assigns the French intra-community VAT number in the format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN under art. 286 ter CGI, together with the corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) account reference; (4) for immediate VAT invoicing, complete electronic registration through the professional portal (demande d'immatriculation, equivalent to form 3527-SD); (5) for export activity, separately obtain an EORI number (FR + SIREN) from DGDDI; (6) where the taxable person is a non-established foreign entity supplying B2C digital services into France, a fiscal representative may be mandatory (art. 286-1 al. 4 CGI) and registration is effected through the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) with DGFiP.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign-owned SAS obtains statutory tax identifiers as follows: (1) file incorporation and initial disclosures electronically on formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr (single national portal effective 1 January 2023); (2) INSEE issues the SIREN (9-digit) and SIRET (14-digit) identifiers and notifies DGFiP, creating the enterprise's tax file; (3) DGFiP assigns the French intra-community VAT number in the format FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN under art. 286 ter CGI, together with the corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) account reference; (4) for immediate VAT invoicing, complete electronic registration through the professional portal (demande d'immatriculation, equivalent to form 3527-SD); (5) for export activity, separately obtain an EORI number (FR + SIREN) from DGDDI; (6) where the taxable person is a non-established foreign entity supplying B2C digital services into France, a fiscal representative may be mandatory (art. 286-1 al. 4 CGI) and registration is effected through the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) with DGFiP.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France has no check-the-box regime: an SAS has a fixed legal status and is mandatorily subject to entity-level corporate income tax (art. 206-1 CGI). Available statutory elections: (i) art. 239 bis AB CGI — newly created small companies (turnover ≤ €10m in the first fiscal year and qualifying shareholders) may elect, for the first five fiscal years, taxation at the shareholder level (pass-through treatment); (ii) art. 223 A CGI — group tax consolidation (intégration fiscale) for a French parent holding at least 95% of French subsidiaries, elected at the opening of a fiscal year; (iii) art. 145 CGI — parent-subsidiary régime mère-fille 95% exemption on qualifying dividends with 5% recapture; (iv) the French anti-hybrid rules (transposing ATAD 1 and 2) under arts. 212 bis and 205 ter CGI neutralize hybrid mismatch outcomes and provide no elective reclassification for an SAS.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registrational threshold for persons established in France: any person performing an independent economic activity with a French nexus must register for VAT from the first euro of turnover (déclaration dès le premier euro). The only statutory exemption is the franchise en base de TVA (art. 293 B CGI) for qualifying established small enterprises whose annual turnover is ≤ €91,900 for supplies of goods and ≤ €47,500 for services (2025 revalorised scale); during the franchise the business is exempt from charging VAT and from filing VAT returns, though optional registration remains permitted. Non-established foreign suppliers of electronically supplied services (including AI avatar video generation SaaS) must register via the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) or French VAT number to account for B2C French VAT from the first euro, with no threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the EU are exempt from French VAT with full credit for input VAT (effective 0%) under art. 262-I CGI, provided the goods physically leave EU territory as evidenced by the electronic Customs Export Declaration (DAU) processed in the DGDDI DELTA system. Intra-EU supplies (livraisons intracommunautaires) qualify for the corresponding exemption under art. 262 ter CGI where the customer holds a valid EU VAT number and the goods are dispatched to another Member State (EC Sales List filing). For AI avatar video generation software supplied as an electronically supplied service: B2B supplies are outside French VAT when the recipient is established outside France (place-of-supply rule, art. 259 CGI); B2C supplies to French consumers are subject to French VAT at 20%; B2C supplies to consumers in other EU Member States are declared via the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) at the destination state's rate; B2C supplies to non-EU customers are outside the scope of French VAT and follow local consumption rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU / Document Administratif Unique submitted electronically via DELTA through the National Single Window) cleared and discharged by DGDDI showing the exporting SAS as declarant/exporter
  • Transport document evidencing physical exit from the EU: Bill of Lading (Connaissement) for sea carriage, CMR consignment note for road freight, or Air Waybill for air carriage
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of delivery/arrival in the non-EU destination (or the destination country's customs entry document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing payment of the export proceeds into the French exporter's bank account
  • For intra-EU zero-rated supplies: customer's valid intra-community VAT number, the EC Sales List (état récapitulatif des clients) and, where thresholds apply, the monthly/quarterly Intrastat DEB (Déclaration d'Échanges de Biens)

Mandatory e-invoicing and e-reporting reform enacted by art. 921 of the Loi de finances pour 2024 and implemented by decree (notably décret n° 2024-1523 of 27 December 2024): from 1 September 2026, all French VAT-registered businesses must receive structured electronic invoices, and large enterprises plus mid-tier companies (ETI) must issue them through a certified Partner Dematerialization Platform (PDP — Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire) or the State-run Public Invoicing Portal (PPF — Portail Public de Facturation); from 1 September 2027, SMEs and micro-enterprises must also issue e-invoices. Transmission is API-based using standard formats (Factur-X, UBL, EDI) with mandatory transaction and payment data reporting to DGFiP on the same timetable. France does not apply fiscal digital stamping or real-time clearance; penalties apply under art. 1734 CGI (€15 per non-compliant invoice, capped at €15,000 per calendar year) together with administrative fines for failure to transmit e-reporting data.

Under art. 283 CGI and DGFiP doctrine (BOI-TVA-CHAMP-40-10), the exporter bears the burden of proving the physical export; missing or inconsistent documentation triggers reassessment of French VAT at 20% plus monthly late interest (0.20% per month), within the standard three-year statute of limitation (art. L. 176 LPF), extended where fraud is alleged. Penalties follow art. 1729 CGI: 40% for bad faith (mauvaise foi) and 80% for fraudulent manoeuvres (manœuvres frauduleuses) or abuse of right. Fraudulent zero-rating or fictitious exports further expose the SAS and its directors to criminal tax fraud under art. 1741 CGI (fine up to €500,000 and up to five years' imprisonment, with aggravated amounts doubled), to clawback of deducted input VAT (art. 272 CGI), and to solidary liability where the issuer knew the transaction was irregular; e-invoicing and e-reporting failures attract the specific fines of art. 1734 CGI (€15 per invoice, capped at €15,000 per year).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France operates under a fully liberalised convertibility regime for both current and capital account items, consistent with Articles 63-66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (free movement of capital), EU Capital Markets Union rules, and the OECD Codes of Liberalisation. There are no exchange-control licensing, prior dividend-remittance authorisation, foreign-currency quota, or profit-blocking mechanisms applicable to a French SAS. Profits may be remitted freely to non-resident shareholders, subject to (i) corporate-law tests under Articles L. 232-11 to L. 232-18 of the Code de commerce (no distribution of net assets below share capital), (ii) French withholding tax under Article 119 bis of the Code général des impôts (CGI) at the domestic 25% rate, reduced to 0% for qualifying EEA corporate shareholders under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (Directive 2011/96/EU) where at least 10% ownership and the applicable holding period are satisfied, and reduced further under bilateral treaties (e.g., 15% standard on dividends under most OECD treaties, with lower 5% portfolio thresholds where available), and (iii) CRS/FATCA reporting obligations which do not constrain the remittance itself. Convertibility is real and immediate: the Banque de France publishes daily euro reference rates, and repurchase of EUR is unlimited. The only functional constraints arise from EU/UN/OFAC sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering origin-of-funds diligence, and the general prohibition on financing sanctioned persons — none of which amounts to a currency-convertibility hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is expressly no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion of foreign currency export receipts into EUR, and no compulsory repatriation timeline in France. The regime is governed by the Code monétaire et financier (Articles L. 151-1 to L. 165-1), which abolished exchange controls and replaced them with statistical and AML reporting obligations. A French AI/software exporter may invoice in USD, EUR, GBP, JPY, or any agreed currency, hold export receipts in foreign-currency accounts with authorised credit institutions, and choose commercially rational conversion timing. The mandatory framework consists of (i) bank-filed balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque de France for every cross-border payment equal to or above EUR 15,000, with bank-assigned transaction and purpose codes; (ii) continued retention of underlying contracts, invoice files, commercial documentation, and, for exports outside the EU, customs export declarations (exit summaries via the DELTA declaration system), while intra-EU sales require Intrastat/Recapitulative statement reporting where thresholds apply; (iii) compliance with EU Regulation 2015/847 (information accompanying funds transfers) for payable-through and correspondent chains; and (iv) arm's-length transfer pricing documentation for any intercompany settlement in accordance with Article 57 CGI and the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. No conversion ratio or exchange-surrender timetable is imposed by Banque de France.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) — member of the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks (ESCB); banking and market-infrastructure supervision delegated to the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR); foreign-exchange statistical reporting, balance-of-payments surveillance, and external financial relations policy coordinated with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) under the Ministry of Economy, Finance and Industrial and Digital Sovereignty.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Domestic ACPR-licensed French fintech banks and electronic-money institutions (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Allicance? Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for operational liquidity, LOW-MEDIUM for escrow and hedging. Such providers offer rapid remote onboarding, multi-currency IBANs, virtual cards, and automated accounting feeds, making them viable as an ancillary operating account for collection of Stripe/Paddle payouts, but they are not licensed credit institutions and therefore cannot hold client funds as true deposits, issue commercial letters of credit, provide authorised lending, furnish derivative documentation, or ensure protected custody of statutory share capital. Cross-border offshore digital EMIs: a UK FCA-authorised EMI cannot passport into France post-Brexit; it requires either a separate French ACPR licence under the PSD2 regime, a French branch, or reliance on the limited reverse-solicitation exemption, and any UK-handled collection will be regarded by French banks as a non-regulated payment intermediary — an AML concentration risk. EEA-licensed EMIs (e.g., Lithuanian, Dutch, Irish, German) may passport into France through freedom to provide services or a branch, but in practice Tier-1 French banks will require that the final settlement of material outbound flows (royalties, dividends, intercompany invoices, supplier payments above EUR 50,000) pass through a fully supervised French credit institution rather than an EMI cascading structure. Overall viability: use a regulated French fintech (e.g., Qonto) for day-to-day operational collection and expense management in parallel with a Tier-1 universal bank for statutory capital, escrow, clearing, and hedging.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax (Impôt sur les Sociétés, IS) under article 219-I-CGI; reduced rate of 15% applies to the first EUR 38,120 of annual taxable profit for qualifying SMEs with turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up share capital, and at least 75% of capital held directly or indirectly by natural persons.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory withholding of 25% on dividends distributed by a French SAS to non-resident corporate shareholders (article 119 bis (2) CGI); 12.8% final withholding (PFU) for non-resident natural persons. Distributions to a qualifying EU/EEA parent under the Parent-Subsidiary Directive are exempt from French withholding where the parent holds at least 10% of the capital, has held it for at least 2 years, meets the anti-abuse and substance requirements, and the parties are eligible corporate forms within the directive. French-resident corporate shareholders receive dividends without withholding and may apply the 95% participation exemption.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual IS return must be filed within three months of the close of the fiscal year. For a calendar-year entity (financial year ending 31 December), the deadline is the third business day of May; e.g., for FY 2023 the deadline was 3 May 2024. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory preparation of the French Master File and Local File arises where either (i) the French entity's annual turnover exceeds EUR 50 million, or (ii) the worldwide group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750 million (article L. 13 AA-LPF). Entities below these thresholds must nevertheless keep and, upon formal demand, produce documentation demonstrating that intercompany prices for AI video-generation services, hosting, GPU capacity, software licences, SaaS subscriptions, and group services are consistent with the arm's length principle. Failure to furnish complete documentation on request triggers a penalty of 5% of the gross amount of the undocumented cross-border intra-group transactions (article 1735 ter-CGI), in addition to any adjustment and late-payment interest.

Economic substance scope: France does not apply a dedicated Economic Substance Regulation statute akin to the UAE/offshore ESR regime; however, treaty benefits and the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive require genuine economic substance in the State of residence (real decision-making, personnel, premises, and business activity). Artificial arrangements lacking substance are recharacterised under the French abuse-of-law doctrine (article L. 64-LPF) and the OECD BEPS GAAR, causing denial of treaty relief, reclassification of the French SAS as fiscally transparent, and an 80% penalty. The AI avatar video SaaS entity must therefore demonstrate real operational substance in France (engineering team, servers or cloud contracts negotiated from France, strategic meetings, IP ownership and payroll) to protect its DTA position.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (fraude fiscale)

Statutory sanction: Article 1741-CGI: 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 500,000, increased to twice the illicit proceeds when the offence is committed as an organised group or using an overseas conduit; complementary penalties may include publication of the conviction, closure of the business, and forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: The legal representative (Président / Directeur Général) is prosecuted as perpetrator or accomplice; courts may impose a travel ban, suspension of civic rights, a prohibition on managing a company for up to 15 years, and confiscation orders. Under article L.267-LPF, directors who deliberately caused the tax deficiency are jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalties, and interest.
Abuse of Law (abus de droit)

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation under article L.64-LPF with a penalty of 80% of the avoided tax (or 40% where the abuse was not concealed); the case may be submitted to the Comité de l'abus de droit fiscal; loss of treaty protection and full reassessment of profits, plus interest at 0.20% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or ordered the sham arrangements are personally pursued for the 80% penalty under the 'manoeuvres frauduleuses' doctrine; in insolvency proceedings, creditors and administrators may seek 'comblement de passif' against directors for management acts contrary to the company interest.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Article 1735 ter-CGI: 5% penalty on the gross amount of undocumented cross-border intra-group transactions, added to the reassessed tax, the 40% surcharge for lack of good faith, and late-payment interest; adjustment may extend to the entire foreign group's French taxable base.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed the pricing policy are subject to personal assessment for the penalties; tax inspection reports identifying 'manoeuvres frauduleuses' allow the administration to seize assets and initiate criminal proceedings for fiscal fraud.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the OECD MLI / Principal Purpose Test (PPT); reclassification of the SAS under article 165 bis-CGI; application of domestic withholding on deemed distributions at 25% and on royalties at 33.33%; 80% penalty for abuse of law; potential forced liquidation of the entity by the registrar for absence of genuine activity.

Director personal liability: Directors face a personal obligation to prove the reality of the corporate seat; misrepresentation of registered office or statutory seat is a criminal offence under article L.236-3 of the French Commercial Code carrying 2 years' imprisonment and EUR 30,000 fine; directors may also be banned from managing and be ordered to restore the corporate tax paid by third parties.
AML/CTF and Financial Fines

Statutory sanction: Article 324-1 of the French Criminal Code: money laundering is punishable by 5 years' imprisonment and a fine of EUR 375,000; aggravated laundering (habitual, or using one's professional activity) carries 10 years' imprisonment and EUR 750,000 fine; the entity itself may be sentenced to a corporate fine of up to EUR 375,000 and dissolution.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives are presumed authors of laundering committed for the benefit of the entity; senior managers may be subjected to a crypto-asset and bank freeze, a professional interdiction, a travel ban, and confiscation of corporate property; compliance failures by the entity do not shield directors who personally approved the suspicious transactions.
