Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Generale des Finances Publiques (DGFIP), French Ministry of Economy and Finance, acting with the Direction Generale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI) for export controls; primary portal is impots.gouv.fr (Professional Space); company registration is administered via the INPI Guichet Unique at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

The French SAS is constituted through the INPI Guichet Unique (08 February 2023 single-window regime). The president/legal representative files the digital registration package comprising the articles of association (statuts), proof of registered office, identity and residential address of the president, and, where relevant, the foreign parent company's certificate of incorporation and power of attorney. INSEE automatically allocates the 9-digit SIREN and the subsidiary SIRET code; DGFIP enters the company in the French VAT register (assujetti a la TVA) from the first taxable transaction and, where intra-Community transactions or distance sales are made, assigns the statutory French VAT identification (Intra-Community VAT ID, e.g., FR47XXXXX). A definitive NIF/SIREN confirmation is issued as an extrait d'immatriculation au registre national des entreprises through the Guichet Unique dashboard. Non-EU foreign sellers without a French fixed establishment use the specific non-established procedure (form 1536-SDF) through the same portal or the nearest DGFIP non-resident section; processing time is generally 5 to 15 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in France. The default regime is corporate income tax (impot sur les societes, IS) levied at 25%, with the reduced IS rate of 15% on the first EUR 42,500 of annual profit for SMEs with turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million. Available statutory elections: (i) option for personal income tax (option IR) under Article 239 bis AA CGI, available to qualifying SMEs for five fiscal years and tacitly renewable (with mandatory dividend treatment adjustments and restrictions on depreciation of the initial franchise value); (ii) VAT fiscal unity election (groupe TVA) under Article 256 C bis CGI for closely-linked French entities; (iii) French tax consolidation (integration fiscale) under Article 223 A CGI, applicable to a 95%-owned group of French subsidiaries, on a five-year elective basis; and (iv) automatic hybrid-mismatch qualification under EU ATAD 2 implemented by Article 212 bis A CGI, which is not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Domestic: no net turnover threshold — a French SAS is subject to VAT from the first taxable supply; nevertheless, the small-enterprise exemption (franchise en base de TVA) under Article 293 B CGI applies to goods/activities suppliers with turnover below EUR 85,000 (2025), with an option for taxation. Intra-EU distance sales of goods by a French SAS are within the EUR 10,000 EU-wide distance-selling threshold (Article 293 B CGI); above this threshold French VAT must be declared and paid via the OSS (One-Stop-Shop) regime. Non-EU suppliers selling into France have no minimum threshold: VAT registration is mandatory from the first sale.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods, including hand-cast epoxy-resin polyhedral dice and tabletop gaming accessories, are taxable at 0% under Article 262-1-1° CGI provided: (i) the goods are physically exported outside the customs territory of the European Union; (ii) transport commences within 90 days of the transfer of power over the goods; (iii) the goods demonstrably arrive outside the EU; and (iv) the complete documentary package proving movement is retained by the SAS. Services directly related to exported goods follow the B2B destination principle (Article 259 A CGI) and are zero-rated where the business customer is established outside France. Sales funded via Kickstarter/Stripe/PayPal qualify as exports when the goods are dispatched from France to a non-EU address and the transport receipt is reproducible; volume discounts, promotion packaging and freight packing do not disqualify zero-rating provided sale and export are sequential and evidenced.

France applies the EU ViDA-aligned e-invoicing reform (Ordonnance 2021-1190, Decret 2022-1299 as amended). B2B domestic transactions must be issued and received as structured electronic invoices (Factur-X, UBL or CII) transmitted through the national Portail Public de Facturation (PPF) or a registered Platforme de Dematerialisation Partenaire (PDP). Deadlines: from 1 September 2026 all VAT-registered entities must be able to receive e-invoices, while grandes entreprises and ETIs must issue them; from 1 September 2027 SMEs and micro-enterprises must issue them. E-reporting to DGFIP covers B2C transactions and cross-border B2B data. Communication is exclusively by API between the issuer's software/partner platform and the PPF/PDP; transmission must occur within one business day. France does not apply real-time fiscal pre-clearance or digital stamping; a qualified electronic signature or seal is optional. Non-compliance triggers Article 1737 CGI invoicing fines (EUR 15 per offence, capped at EUR 15,000 per entity, increased to proportional penalties up to 50% of concealed amounts in bad faith), together with ordinary documentary controls and potential tax audit adjustments.

DGFIP applies strict documentary audit to zero-rated export transactions. If the export evidence is incomplete, shipment is disproved, or goods return to the EU, the 20% VAT is clawed back on the gross invoice value, together with late interest (interet de retard, approximately 0.4% per month) and penalties under Article 1729 CGI: 40% for deliberate misstatement and 80% for abuse or manoeuvres frauduleuses; additional fines apply under Article 1737 CGI; intentional fraud triggers Article 1741 CGI with fines up to EUR 75,000 and imprisonment of five years for the SAS president. Foreign-currency export proceeds must be received and repatriated to the SAS's French bank account within 180 days of the invoice due date; unjustified deferral risks recharacterisation under the French foreign-exchange documentation regime, additional assessments, and denial of the zero-rating refund. A request for refund of the VAT credit balance (remboursement de credit de TVA) is filed via form 3517-SIE with the local Service des Impots des Entreprises; for annual refund claims below EUR 600 a simplified declaration suffices, while higher claims require full e-audit validation and the maintained export evidence bunch is at all times the primary safeguard against clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France is part of the eurozone and applies the euro as its legal tender. Capital account and current account operations are fully liberalized under EU Treaty provisions, particularly Articles 63–66 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Inbound and outbound foreign exchange transactions in any freely convertible currency are not subject to exchange controls or licensing. Profit remittance by an SAS to non-resident shareholders is generally unrestricted if the entity is in good standing, corporate tax returns are filed, and any applicable treaty documentation is maintained. For non-EU investors, the only practical hurdles are anti-money-laundering verification by the receiving bank, French balance-of-payments statistical reporting for specific cross-border transactions, and, where the investor is resident in a high-risk jurisdiction, enhanced due-diligence reviews. No withholding tax is applied on dividends to residents of France or the EEA under French domestic law, and non-EEA residents may benefit from tax-treaty rates provided they submit the required certificate of residence. There are no currency convertibility restrictions affecting the distribution of attributable profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement under French law. Exporters are permitted to receive and retain proceeds in foreign currency in French-licensed bank accounts, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio or deadline for converting export receipts into euros. However, export proceeds must be declared to the Banque de France in the monthly balance-of-payments reporting if the individual transaction or aggregated flows exceed the stated thresholds, and the underlying commercial invoice and customs export declaration (including the EX1 form or the relevant Single Administrative Document) must be retained as audit evidence. For all euro-area trade settlements, SEPA Credit Transfers are used; for non-euro-area counterparties, the exporter may freely receive SWIFT MT103 payments in EUR or any convertible currency.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), acting as the National Central Bank and as the operator of the French balance-of-payments and external financial accounts reporting framework, in coordination with the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) and the French Treasury (Direction Générale du Trésor) within the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is Medium under the French PSD2 framework. Electronic Money Institutions and Payment Institutions authorized in their European Economic Area home state may passport into France and accept a French SAS as a payment-service user, but they cannot provide an equivalent of a full licensed French commercial bank account. TransferWise/Borderless and similar EEA-authorized EMIs can be used for fast cross-border currency conversion and isolation of payment flows, yet they do not satisfy all statutory escrow or share capital blocking requirements and are generally treated as supplemental payment rails rather than as a substitute for a tier-1 French bank. Non-EEA offshore digital EMIs without a valid EEA license or a French ACPR passport are legally unusable for the French SAS treasury operation and create a severe AML/regulatory risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital contributions from a non-resident shareholder are executed by a SWIFT MT103 or an international wire transfer from the contributor’s bank to the French SAS’s euro-denominated IBAN (FR76 prefix for a bank domiciled in France). The message must clearly state the purpose as 'Share Capital Contribution – Société par Actions Simplifiée [legal name]', and the contributor must include the French banking statistical classification code for 'apports en capital / capital social' used in Banque de France balance-of-payments reporting. If the capital is paid into a lawyer or notary escrow account before the SAS is registered, the payment reference must indicate the future legal name and the statutory capital table (répartition du capital). The bank will verify the purpose against the corporate articles and the influx must be reconciled with the incorporation capital certificate and the French commercial registry KBIS. For investments exceeding EUR 50 million or in sectors subject to sector-specific regulation, additional pre-transaction authorization files may be required, although a typical tabletop gaming accessories SAS does not trigger French foreign-investment screening.Outbound disbursements to EU/EEA suppliers use SEPA Credit Transfer to the beneficiary’s IBAN, with structured remittance information quoting the purchase order and invoice number. For non-EEA trade counterparties (e.g., epoxy resin chemical exporters in Asia, custom mold makers, or polyhedral dice moulding subcontractors), the preferred clearing rails are SWIFT MT103, MT202/MT202COV if a cover payment is required, and documentary letters of credit for high-value raw material consignments. Commercial Letters of Credit are opened at the French bank through the issuance of an irrevocable LC advised to the foreign beneficiary’s bank; the underlying invoice, packing list, bill of lading/airway bill, and certificate of origin become the conformity documents. To avoid FX exposure on the payable side, the SAS may request the money transfer be executed in euros even for non-EEA beneficiaries, or it may purchase forward cover as further described in the hedging section.Deliverable forward foreign exchange contracts with French/EEA banks for hedging commercial receivables and payables exceeding 12 months of forecast cash flows; available freely for EUR/USD, EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY, and any freely convertible currency.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permitted only in very limited cases and generally unavailable because the EUR is fully convertible and an NDF would have no lawful purpose; French banks do not offer NDF contracts for euro conversion against liquid currencies.Plain-vanilla cross-currency swaps are available for hedging multi-currency loans, trade-finance facilities, or substantial supplier prepayment structures and must be documented under the ISDA Master Agreement with French ACPR-compliant collateral and EMIR margin rules.Currency options (OTC and exchange-traded) are permissible to hedge foreign-currency auction settlement risk from Kickstarter/direct international sales, but they require a board resolution authorizing the hedging mandate and a quarterly mark-to-market reporting process to the French bank.
