Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

SAS has no legal minimum share capital; capital is fixed by the Articles of Association. For cash contributions, at least 50% of the subscribed capital must be paid up upon incorporation, with the remainder payable within five years. Capital funds must be deposited in a blocked special account with a notary, bank, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations until the RCS registration certificate is issued. No mandatory escrow account beyond the depositary requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification (attestation de sécurité incendie) issued by a competent commission based on the building's resistance classification; fire containment system and clear emergency evacuation routes are compulsory. A structural load inspection certificate confirms the floor can support the maximum bottle pallet load. Occupational clearance is required from the DREETS to ensure compliance with the French Labour Code (eight-hour ventilation, face-mask provisions, alcohol exposure signage, and emergency response drills). The workplace environment must hold an up-to-date document unique d'évaluation des risques and a valid health and safety register.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the SAS incorporation through the Guichet Unique (formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr, mandatory since 1 January 2023), which automatically delivers the SIREN/SIRET business identifiers. Step 2: DGFiP then allocates the French intra-Community VAT number in the format FR + 2 control digits + SIREN; this VAT number is the statutory TIN for corporate income tax, VAT, payroll and local taxes, and must be verified in the VIES database before use in intra-EU transactions. Step 3: Obtain an EORI number (FR + SIREN) from DGDDI for customs declarations and, because the activity is wholesale distribution of high-proof sorghum baijiu (ISIC 4632 / HS 2208.90), obtain DGDDI approval as an excise operator (entrepositaire agréé or registered consignor) to export alcohol under duty suspension with the DAE excise document. Step 4: A foreign entity without a French business presence cannot obtain a French VAT number directly; it must appoint an accredited fiscal representative (représentant fiscal) under CGI Article 289 A and file form 1544-SD with the Service des impôts des entreprises étrangères, or use the EU OSS/IOSS special schemes; the French SAS vehicle is established and therefore files directly with its local service des impôts des entreprises (SIE).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France, as a eurozone member, maintains full convertibility of the euro for current account and capital account transactions under Article 63 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union and Articles L.411-1 et seq. of the French Monetary and Financial Code. There is no general exchange-control authorization required for an SAS to receive capital injections, pay foreign suppliers, or remit dividends to a Chinese parent. Profit distribution hurdles are tax-driven rather than FX-driven: dividends sourced in France to a Chinese resident beneficial owner are subject to withholding tax of 10%, reduced to 5% where the beneficial owner is a company directly holding at least 25% of the SAS capital and meets the treaty anti-abuse and substance conditions in Article 10 of the France-China double tax treaty. The CNY is not freely convertible onshore; the SAS must use EUR, CNH offshore, or convertible USD settlement lanes for all France-side flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, mandated conversion ratio, or required conversion timeline applies in France; exporters may retain foreign currency balances in licensed banks. The relevant mandatory obligation is statistical reporting: each cross-border payment equal to or exceeding EUR 12,500 must be reported to the Banque de France by the servicing bank under EU Regulation ECB/2018/2317 and the French arrêté of 13 January 2020. For goods exporting, invoices must be matched to customs declarations (DX7) with the Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), and the complete remittance evidence file must be retained for five years for AML/CFT and customs audit.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France) operating jointly with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) and the Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution (ACPR) as the banking resolution and licensing authority. The Banque de France holds exclusive power over balance-of-payments statistics, cross-border payment declarations, and euro wholesale settlement (TARGET2).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict limitations. A French-licensed or EU-passported EMI (e.g., Qonto, Revolut Business, Payoneer) can support day-to-day operational euro flows, invoice payments, and low-value cross-border transfers below EUR 250,000 monthly, and may open accounts remotely. However, EMIs generally cannot originate documentary Letters of Credit, execute EUR/CNH delivery-versus-payment trade settlement, or interface with the DGDDI excise (accises) payment module required for high-proof Baijiu importation. Legal constraints require the EMI to hold a valid ACPR payment-services licence or EU passport, subject the entity to full RBE and UBO KYC, and apply sanctions screening to every beneficiary; alcohol excise duty payments must always be routed through a full-service credit institution. The EMI rail is therefore viable only as an auxiliary operating account, not as the principal import settlement and capital custody rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from the Chinese shareholder must first clear China's SAFE ODI registration and outward remittance filing, then be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 in EUR (converted from CNY/CNH) or directly in CNH to the French SAS account. The wire must carry the Chinese remittance purpose code for outbound direct investment and a Bank-to-Beneficiary narrative reading 'INVESTMENT CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - APPORTS EN CAPITAL SAS' referencing the notarial deed and RBE record. On the French side, the servicing bank receives the transfer through TARGET2, files the Banque de France cross-border declaration where the value exceeds EUR 12,500, and temporarily places the funds in the blocked Capital Escrow Account until the SAS incorporation and Commercial Registry registration (greffe du tribunal de commerce) are formally evidenced. After release, the treasury is moved onto the operational account with documented source-of-funds chain. No intermediary shell bank should be used; the settlement should remain within SEPA/EUR T2 rails for full compliance traceability.Settlements to Chinese Baijiu distilleries and freight forwarders are cleared through three permitted rails: (i) direct SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the SAS EUR or CNH account to the supplier's account at a Chinese bank or its EU correspondent; (ii) documentary commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) opened on behalf of the SAS, payable against presentation of the commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, customs export/import declarations, certificate of origin, insurance policy, and EU conformity statement, with documents examined by the advising bank under UCP 600; and (iii) optional CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) settlement for CNY-denominated payments where both counterparties maintain CIPS member accounts. Every outbound disbursement must be screened against EU sanctions (EU Consolidated Financial Sanctions List, OFAC, UN) and, where above EUR 12,500, is reported by the bank to the Banque de France for balance-of-payments statistics. Excise duty payments to the DGDDI are executed through the secured customs electronic payment module (SIGAL/Comp@ct) linked to the same liquidity account, with automatic debit authority granted to the customs administration.Outright forward foreign-exchange contracts (change à terme or deliverable forwards) on EUR/CNH and EUR/USD up to 12 months tenor, permitted only against documented import/export commitments and executed under the EMIR non-financial-counterparty hedging exemption.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on onshore CNY (USD/CNY reference rate via CFETS fixing) when physical CNH settlement is commercially unavailable, conditional on an underlying China-import commitment and the bank's market-risk approval through the ACPR framework.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps / EUR-CNH cross-currency swaps) for maturities beyond 12 months used to hedge the foreign-currency exposure on shareholder loans, purchase payments, and future dividend remittances, settled through TARGET2 with full collateralisation.Zero-premium FX option collars on EUR/CNH packaged by the bank's markets desk; permissible where the SAS qualifies as a professional client under MIFID II and the board has approved a written treasury hedging policy with utilisation limits.Spot EUR/CNH and EUR/USD transactions (T+2) executed against identified underlying commercial flows, limited to not more than 10% of the annual cleared trade value and subject to the bank's markets-conduct and suitability controls.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS). Qualifying small and medium-sized companies may apply 15% to the first EUR 42,500 of taxable profit for a 12-month period; the company must generally have turnover not exceeding EUR 10 million, fully paid-up share capital, and at least 75% of its capital held by individuals or qualifying companies meeting the ownership condition.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For dividends paid by a French SAS to a non-resident corporate shareholder, the domestic withholding rate is generally 25%, subject to exemptions and treaty relief. The French domestic exemption for qualifying EU/EEA parent companies may apply if statutory ownership, holding-period, beneficial-ownership, and anti-abuse conditions are satisfied. For an eligible Chinese corporate beneficial owner, the France-China income tax treaty generally limits withholding to 5% where the beneficial owner is a company directly holding at least 25% of the payer's capital, and 10% in other qualifying cases. Different domestic rules apply to individual recipients.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: French full transfer-pricing documentation under Article L.13 AA of the Tax Procedures Code generally applies where the French entity or relevant group meets a statutory threshold, including annual turnover or gross assets of at least EUR 150 million, or specified group-ownership or control conditions involving an entity meeting that threshold. The annual simplified transfer-pricing declaration (Form 2257-SD) generally applies at a lower EUR 50 million turnover or gross-assets threshold, or where a qualifying group relationship brings the company within scope. Separate documentation obligations can apply below these thresholds under general audit rules; confirm current statutory scope and related-party transaction requirements.

Economic substance scope: France does not operate a general UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime. French tax outcomes remain subject to transfer-pricing requirements, the general anti-abuse rule, abuse-of-law doctrine, beneficial-ownership analysis, substance-over-form principles, and applicable anti-hybrid and controlled-foreign-company rules. The SAS should maintain actual decision-making, personnel, premises, functions, assets, and risk-control evidence proportionate to its claimed commercial role. A baijiu import and wholesale business should document inventory ownership, pricing, customs valuation, credit and foreign-exchange risks, and the allocation of marketing, logistics, and regulatory functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate tax evasion or fraudulent tax return

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, surcharges generally up to 80% in aggravated cases, and criminal prosecution where statutory fraud elements are met. Tax fraud can carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of EUR 500,000, increased to seven years and EUR 3 million in specified aggravated circumstances; additional penalties may apply under the Criminal Code.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal criminal liability for their own participation, civil liability where statutory conditions are met, and tax-responsibility proceedings for fraudulent or serious and repeated tax non-compliance. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax penalty; any restriction requires a separate lawful judicial or enforcement basis.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and undeclared related-party transactions

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, interest, applicable tax surcharges, and a specific documentation penalty generally equal to the greater of EUR 10,000 or 5% of the reassessed profits connected with undocumented transactions, subject to statutory conditions and the applicable version of the law. Failure to file a required Form 2257-SD can incur a separate penalty, generally EUR 150 per omitted or inaccurate item, subject to statutory caps and rules.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for deliberate participation in fraud or for statutory tax-responsibility grounds. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustments do not automatically pierce the corporate veil or create personal liability.
Concealed permanent establishment or artificial non-resident arrangement

Statutory sanction: Assessment of French tax on attributable profits, interest, surcharges, and possible penalties for deliberate or fraudulent conduct; treaty relief may be denied under beneficial-ownership, principal-purpose, or domestic anti-abuse rules.

Director personal liability: Personal liability depends on individual participation, statutory tax-responsibility grounds, or separate civil or criminal findings; it is not automatic merely because an entity is found to have a French taxable presence.
Unlawful dividend distribution or withholding-tax non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid withholding tax, late-payment interest, applicable surcharges, and potential penalties for inaccurate declarations or deliberate concealment. Invalid or unlawful distributions may also be subject to corporate-law remedies and repayment claims.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur civil or criminal liability for knowingly authorizing unlawful distributions, fraud, or failure to comply with statutory duties. Personal liability is fact-specific and is not an automatic consequence of a withholding-tax reassessment.
Alcohol excise, customs, and market-access non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Customs and excise assessments, seizure or forfeiture, interest, administrative fines, and potential criminal penalties under applicable customs, excise, and alcohol-trade legislation. The exact consequence depends on the breached obligation, goods, intent, and applicable procedure.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers may face personal administrative or criminal exposure for their own unlawful acts or statutory responsibilities. Corporate limited liability does not shield an individual from personal criminal liability.
Money laundering, sanctions, or suspicious payment activity

Statutory sanction: Potential account restrictions, transaction blocking, confiscation, regulatory or customs penalties, and criminal prosecution. Money laundering can carry imprisonment of up to five years and a fine of EUR 375,000, with higher penalties for aggravated conduct; sanctions breaches may attract separate severe penalties.

Director personal liability: Individuals may be criminally liable for knowing participation, facilitation, or specified failures in their own duties. Travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment require the relevant statutory or judicial basis and are not automatic consequences of a compliance breach.
