Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon incorporation, with the balance payable within five years of registration. Cash contributions are deposited into a bank or qualifying deposit account, and the deposit certificate is used for registration. Contributions in kind require valuation by a commissaire aux apports unless the statutory exemption conditions are satisfied.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupation, obtain required fire-safety compliance evidence under the building and workplace rules, including emergency exits, fire detection or suppression systems, electrical conformity, and safe storage of chemicals. Secure structural-load and equipment-installation verification where relevant. Complete the employer's occupational-risk assessment document (DUERP), worker training, machinery conformity checks, and, if ionizing radiation sources or X-ray-generating equipment are operated, radiation-protection arrangements and regulatory declarations or authorizations through the applicable ASN framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France fully liberalises current-account and capital-account payments for both EU and non-EU transactions, with no remaining exchange-control licensing, surrender, or quantitative conversion limits. The euro is freely convertible at market rates. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is possible without foreign-exchange authorisation: a French SAS distributes dividends through the paying agent, while the non-resident parent may be exempt from French withholding tax under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive (for EU/EEA parents with at least 5% shareholding), or receive treaty relief under the applicable double-tax treaty; otherwise French statutory withholding (captive at 25% or 30% as applicable) is collected. Inward direct investment is subject only to ex-post balance-of-payments reporting and, if the investor is non-EU and the sector is not among the regulated list (defence, dual-use, energy safety etc.), no prior screening is required for ISIC 3250 digital X-ray flat-panel manufacturing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, mandatory conversion ratio, or fixed repatriation deadline applicable to France. Export invoices in foreign currency may be collected in fully retained foreign-currency accounts opened with a French credit institution or may be converted on the interbank market at any time. Banks are nonetheless required to observe the EU Payment Services Regulation and the French Monetary and Financial Code by reporting every individual transaction with non-residents when the amount exceeds EUR 12,500, through the bank's automated balance-of-payments declaration; cash and bearer instruments crossing the French border must be declared to customs from EUR 10,000. Thus, in a practical treasury protocol, export proceeds should be credited to the company's EUR or FX account within the contractual payment date (usually 30–120 days), but the delay is civil and trade-credit driven, not an exchange-control deadline.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), operating within the Eurosystem and the European System of Central Banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for EUR collections and remote treasury onboarding; Low for large trade-finance commitments. An EEA-licensed electronic-money institution (EMI) or payment institution holding an ACPR passport (e.g., Qonto, Shine, Wise Business, Bunq) may legally maintain a French business payment account and provide an IBAN, SEPA transfers, and MT103 wires through its sponsor bank. However, these platforms generally cannot issue commercial letters of credit, act as negotiating bank, or provide settlement insurance; transfer caps and tier-1 bank sponsor risk may become a treasury bottleneck for an OEM with EUR/USD payables above EUR 100,000 per transaction. Offshore EMIs without an EEA/ACPR licence are not a legal substitute for a French resident SAS account; using such institutions creates a contravention of the Electronic Money Directive and triggers enhanced AML scrutiny by counterparties. Therefore, an ACPR-licensed fintech is an operational complement but not the primary clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocol: SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank high-value) transmitted to the French beneficiary account, with the 'purpose of payment' field populated using the appropriate ISO 20022 code — 'EQUI' for equity/share-premium injection, 'LOAN' for shareholder debt or convertible loan, and, if the platform cannot represent the code, the freetext 'Direct investment in France' in field 70. The receiving bank reconciles the flow to Banque de France's balance-of-payments reporting. Verification requires: (i) an executed shareholder/capital-increase resolution; (ii) updated statuts showing the new shareholding; (iii) registration of the UBO in the RBE; (iv) a bank-issued attestation de dépôt for the escrowed funds if this is an incorporation; (v) a French authorised controller's certificate for a capital increase, if applicable. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For calendar-year fiscal years ended 31 December, the return must be filed no later than the second business day following 1 May; for non-calendar fiscal years, within three months of the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File required when the French entity's annual revenue or total assets exceed EUR 400 million, or when it belongs to a group whose consolidated turnover or total assets exceed EUR 400 million; Country-by-Country Reporting applies when group consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: France has no separate Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to ESR jurisdictions; however, substance-over-form, Article 155A controlled foreign company rules, the general abuse-of-law doctrine, and OECD BEPS Action 5 principles require real French substance (physical presence, strategic decision-making, personnel, assets) to avoid recharacterization and penalty.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to EUR 500,000 (or doubled to the amount evaded) and imprisonment up to five years; administrative tax increase of 80% for bad faith and intentional concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held civilly joint and severally liable for the tax debt under CGI Article L.267 if they participated in culpable management causing non-payment; courts may impose travel bans and, in aggravated cases, imprisonment.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as a sham: the tax administration re-attributes income to the actual beneficial owner, applies transfer pricing corrections, and imposes a 80% penalty for artificial arrangements; loss of loss carryforward and denial of VAT recovery.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced for fraud or gross negligence; directors face civil restitution of diverted profits, personal bankruptcy proceedings, and criminal conviction with travel restrictions and imprisonment.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to EUR 150,000 (or double for repeated offense); under the French Monetary and Financial Code, money-laundering sanctions reach five years' imprisonment and fines up to EUR 375,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to criminal liability for money laundering if they intentionally ignore suspicious red flags; authorities may impose travel bans, asset freezing, and sequestration, with civil damages payable to the state.
