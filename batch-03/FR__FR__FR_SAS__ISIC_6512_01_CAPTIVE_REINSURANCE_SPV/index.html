Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum under the Commercial Code for an SAS; capital must be fully subscribed and paid at registration. Under the French Insurance Code and Solvency II, a captive reinsurance undertaking must maintain minimum guarantee own funds of at least EUR 2,500,000; funds must be placed in a bank or notarial blocked account until the company obtains its RCS registration and necessary ACPR approval.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance for the registered office under ERP (Etablissement Recevant du Public) classification, including inspection of emergency exits, smoke detectors, electrical load assessments; occupational health and safety registration with CARSAT; display of internal fire-safety and evacuation instructions; implementation of an employer declaration with the Pôle Emploi / URSSAF for employment and civil liability coverage.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie et des Finances; online services via impots.gouv.fr and the formalités portal at formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr.

E-government filing portal

For a French SAS, registration starts with electronic submission of incorporation documents to the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés) through the formalities portal. DGFiP assigns the SIREN/SIRET numbers immediately upon dossier validation. The SAS then files a tax registration form and a 'Mise en activité' statement where relevant. For intra-community transactions, DGFiP issues the TVA intracommunautaire number, normally within two to four weeks, after the entity's first VAT return is filed. Non-resident shareholders appoint a fiscal representative registered in France for VAT obligations if the SAS is treated as not fixed in France for VAT purposes, though the domestic SAS itself is a French tax resident entity.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a French SAS, registration starts with electronic submission of incorporation documents to the RCS (Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés) through the formalities portal. DGFiP assigns the SIREN/SIRET numbers immediately upon dossier validation. The SAS then files a tax registration form and a 'Mise en activité' statement where relevant. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

France operates under a unitary corporate income tax system without a check-the-box partnership election. Relevant elections for the captive reinsurance SAS include: creation of a fiscal group (intégration fiscale) under CGI Article 223A with wholly-owned French subsidiaries; election for VAT grouping under CGI Article 1693-ter and EU VAT Directive provisions; and the statutory election under CGI Article 239 (for limited duration, a newly incorporated SAS may, under strict statutory conditions, elect to be treated as a partnership for income tax purposes). The captive reinsurance activity, however, normally defaults to entity-level corporate income tax at the standard IS rate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

EUR 85,800 taxable turnover for supplies of goods; EUR 34,400 taxable turnover for supplies of services; lower reduced thresholds apply under the French CGI and the DGFiP aménagements. For financial and insurance/reinsurance-related supplies, a taxable person may also hold a voluntary registration if no threshold is met but input VAT deduction is required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported from France to non-EU destinations under CGI Article 262-I and EU VAT Directive 2006/112/EC Article 146. Reinsurance and captive insurance services may be either outside the scope of French VAT or exempt without credit, depending on whether the counterparty is established in the EU and the actual risk location test is satisfied. Offshore reinsurance for risks located outside the EU can qualify for zero-rate treatment where the services are expressly and directly attributable to non-EU exports and the beneficiary is established outside the EU. The captive reinsurance SAS must maintain contractual risk-certification documentation demonstrating connection to non-French, non-EU underwriting portfolios. For goods-related reinsurance export support, a direct proof of physical export movement is mandatory. The zero-rated supply must be correctly coded on the French e-invoicing platform and reconciled with the customs export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent multimodal transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or proof of payment settlement via SWIFT/BIC wire transfer
  • Shipping and freight documents (packing list, export invoice)
  • Risk-location certificates for the reinsured underlying risks
  • Carnet de passage or other transport/insurance supporting documents where applicable

France is deploying mandatory e-invoicing for most VAT-taxable persons. Mandates are phased from 1 September 2026 for all entities subject to VAT for B2B invoices, complemented by a mandatory e-reporting regime. Invoices must pass through a certified 'Plateforme de Dématérialisation Partenaire (PDP)' or the public portal 'Portail Public de Facturation (PPF)' using the EN 16931 format, with structured data elements (SIREN, VAT ID, transaction codes) and full OCR/XML lodgement. The régime is not a real-time clearance model; data is transmitted in near-real-time business-to-tax-authority reporting without physical digital stamping. The DGFiP archives and reconciles the electronic dataset with VAT returns and customs declarations. For export zero-rating evidence, the e-reporting dataset must include the customs export declaration number, shipping evidence, and counterparty third-party country VAT/GST registration where applicable.

DGFiP and the Direction Nationale des Enquêtes Fiscales (DNEF) scrutinise export zero-rating claims. Absence of valid customs and financial evidence triggers reassessment of VAT at 20%, addition of late payment penalties at 0.2% per month, a 40% or 80% bad-faith penalty under CGI Article 1729, and civil interest. For fraudulent or fictitious export schemes, criminal tax fraud sanctions apply under CGI Article 1741, including fines up to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment for five years, increased when the scheme involves organized fraud or misuse of corporate vehicles. Retrospective clawback extends for the standard three-year tax assessment period, extended to six or ten years for hidden activity or unregistered establishment. Reinsurers may also be held jointly and severally liable with contractual counterparties when the participation in a circular self-billing or export-washing arrangement is established. The captive reinsurance SPV should maintain documentary custody of all export records for at least six years to withstand audit challenge.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The euro is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. France has no general exchange-control approval, mandatory domestic-currency settlement, or general restriction on repatriation of dividends, interest, insurance premiums, claims proceeds, or liquidation proceeds. Cross-border transfers remain subject to bank KYC, beneficial-ownership verification, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, tax documentation, transfer-pricing support, and prudential asset-allocation rules applicable to an authorized reinsurance undertaking. Distributions by a captive reinsurance company must also comply with French corporate-law distributable-reserve rules, solvency-capital requirements, the approved business plan, Solvency II requirements, and any ACPR supervisory expectation. Payments to related non-residents require arm's-length support, contractual evidence, withholding-tax analysis, and, where relevant, treaty-benefit documentation. A French SAS intended to conduct regulated reinsurance business requires prior legal-form validation because an SAS is not automatically an eligible form for an insurance or reinsurance undertaking under the French Insurance Code. Banking access does not replace the required ACPR authorization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

France has no general statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the Banque de France, convert export receipts into euros, or repatriate them within a prescribed period. Export proceeds may generally be received and retained in euro or foreign-currency accounts, subject to contractual, tax, accounting, sanctions, and AML obligations. There is no general mandatory conversion ratio. Foreign-currency balances and transactions must be accurately recorded under French accounting rules, with appropriate valuation of monetary items and disclosure where applicable. Cash receipts or payments involving France are subject to applicable cash-payment limits and reporting rules; ordinary banking transfers should therefore be used for material commercial, insurance, capital, and intra-group payments. The entity must separately comply with Intrastat, customs, VAT, statistical, prudential, and Banque de France reporting requirements where applicable. For a reinsurance undertaking, premium, reserve, collateral, and claims accounts must also be consistent with the authorized technical-business model and Solvency II asset-liability governance.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France, the French Treasury Directorate General (Direction générale du Trésor), and the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR) for regulated reinsurance undertakings. France applies the European Union free-movement-of-capital framework, including Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union Articles 63 to 66, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, prudential, tax, and reporting controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary payment operations and Low for the primary treasury bank of an authorized captive reinsurer. A licensed European electronic-money institution or payment institution may provide euro payment accounts, SEPA transfers, foreign-exchange execution, and certain collection services under its passported authorization. It cannot accept deposits as a bank, provide unrestricted deposit-taking, hold regulated insurance technical provisions as a substitute for a prudential custodian, or remove the reinsurance undertaking's ACPR, Solvency II, AML, safeguarding, and accounting obligations. Use of an offshore or non-EEA digital EMI requires verification of its local license, EU authorization or passporting status, safeguarding model, correspondent-bank chain, sanctions controls, data-processing arrangements, and ability to support regulated insurance flows. The primary reserve, collateral, and operational accounts should generally be maintained with an EU-authorized credit institution acceptable under the undertaking's investment and risk-management policies.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Provide the legal-form memorandum, French Insurance Code eligibility analysis, ACPR authorization or formal application evidence, approved or submitted business plan, governance chart, fit-and-proper files, actuarial and risk documentation, Solvency II capital assessment, and a written statement that no regulated underwriting will begin before authorization.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route capital through an EU-authorized credit institution to an account opened in the exact legal name of the French SAS. For incorporation, use a French capital-deposit or capital-escrow account and instruct the remitter to state the legal basis and purpose, such as paid-in share capital, share premium, shareholder loan, or subordinated capital, without combining distinct instruments. The payment message should identify the shareholder, recipient legal name, French registration number where available, ultimate beneficial owner, and purpose; use the bank's applicable SEPA or SWIFT purpose and regulatory coding rather than inventing a code. Obtain the bank's source-of-funds approval before remittance, retain MT103 or equivalent payment evidence, and reconcile the receipt to the shareholder resolution, subscription agreement, capital table, and notarial or incorporation file. Release of escrow funds should follow registration and production of the updated Kbis extract. Shareholder loans, hybrid instruments, collateral funding, and funds supporting Solvency II own funds require separate documentation, arm's-length terms, accounting classification, regulatory eligibility analysis, and possible ACPR notification or approval. Do not route capital through personal accounts, unrelated group entities, anonymous virtual-asset channels, or an offshore EMI lacking verified EU authorization.For ordinary cross-border commercial payments, use SEPA Credit Transfer for euro payments within the SEPA area and SWIFT customer credit transfers, normally MT103 or its ISO 20022 equivalent, for non-SEPA or foreign-currency payments. Each payment should contain accurate originator, beneficiary, bank, invoice or treaty reference, purpose, currency, and underlying-contract information. For reinsurance premiums and claims, retain the treaty, bordereau, invoice, claims file, reserve approval, sanctions clearance, and beneficiary verification. Commercial letters of credit may be used for genuine goods or service transactions but are not a substitute for evidence supporting insurance or intra-group transfers; documentary credits should be issued or confirmed by an acceptable bank under applicable ICC rules, with independent trade-document review. Use separate payment workflows for premiums received, claims paid, collateral movements, operating expenses, taxes, dividends, and intercompany loans. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% corporate income tax. A 3.3% social contribution on corporate income may apply to companies with annual turnover of at least EUR 7.63 million and corporate income tax exceeding EUR 763,000, producing an effective rate of approximately 25.825% before temporary or sector-specific surcharges.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The standard French withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders is generally 25%, subject to treaty relief, EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive relief and statutory exemptions. Dividends paid by a French SAS to a qualifying EU parent may be exempt where the ownership, holding-period, subject-to-tax, anti-abuse and beneficial-ownership conditions are satisfied. French resident individual shareholder distributions are subject to separate personal-income withholding rules.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a financial year ending on 31 December, the return is generally due on the second business day following 1 May of the following year. For a year-end other than 31 December, the return is generally due within three months after the financial year-end. The final corporate income tax balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following the financial year-end; for a 31 December year-end, the balance is generally due by 15 May. Electronic-filing administrative extensions may apply under current filing-calendar rules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Corporate income tax instalments are generally due on 15 March, 15 June, 15 September and 15 December, subject to fiscal-year and tax-calendar adjustments. The final balance is generally due by the 15th day of the fourth month following year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 400 million threshold for the principal French full transfer-pricing documentation obligation, generally based on turnover or gross assets, or where the French entity is associated with an entity meeting the statutory threshold. A country-by-country report generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Entities below the full-documentation threshold must still apply the arm's-length principle and produce supporting information upon request.

Economic substance scope: France has no separate Cayman-style general Economic Substance Regulations regime. Nevertheless, French anti-abuse doctrine, the abuse-of-law rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, permanent-establishment principles, controlled foreign company rules, anti-hybrid rules, interest limitation rules and general arm's-length requirements require genuine commercial substance. A captive reinsurance SAS should maintain French governance, appropriately qualified personnel or demonstrable outsourced capabilities, risk-management systems, underwriting authority, actuarial support, claims oversight, capital and solvency resources, board minutes, decision-making evidence and a commercially coherent risk-retention and retrocession policy. Reinsurance activity also requires authorization and continuing prudential compliance with the French Insurance Code, ACPR supervision and applicable Solvency II requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unauthorized insurance or reinsurance activity

Statutory sanction: Administrative measures may include injunctions, business restrictions, authorization withdrawal, fines and liquidation proceedings. Unauthorized insurance activity may also constitute a criminal offence under the French Insurance Code, with imprisonment and corporate and individual fines potentially reaching statutory maximums of approximately EUR 75,000 for an individual and EUR 375,000 for a legal entity, subject to the applicable provision and facts.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto managers may face personal criminal prosecution, civil damages, regulatory disqualification and asset claims. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may be ordered in criminal proceedings; imprisonment is possible where the statutory offence and culpability are established.
Tax evasion and fraudulent tax returns

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment, late-payment interest, 40% or 80% tax increases, confiscatory measures and criminal tax-fraud penalties. Aggravated tax fraud can result in imprisonment of up to seven years and fines of up to EUR 3 million, subject to statutory conditions.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur criminal liability, civil tax liability for fraudulent conduct, management bans and asset seizure. A travel ban or pre-trial detention may be ordered by a court where legally justified; neither is automatic.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax reassessment under the arm's-length principle, withholding tax exposure, interest, penalties, documentation-related fines and possible 40% or 80% increases where deliberate abuse or fraud is established.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for intentional fraud, serious mismanagement or participation in sham transactions. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of transactions, denial of treaty benefits, permanent-establishment findings, transfer-pricing adjustments, abuse-of-law penalties, withholding tax assessments and possible authorization or prudential sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be liable where they knowingly orchestrate sham arrangements, conceal beneficial ownership or misuse company assets. Civil restitution and asset seizure may follow; imprisonment requires a separately proven criminal offence.
Withholding tax non-compliance

Statutory sanction: The French payer may be assessed for the unremitted withholding tax, late-payment interest and applicable surcharges. Treaty relief may be denied where residence, beneficial ownership, anti-abuse or procedural requirements are not satisfied.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur liability for deliberate withholding-tax evasion or misuse of withheld funds. Criminal exposure is fact-dependent; automatic personal liability does not arise from an innocent administrative error.
AML, sanctions and beneficial-ownership violations

Statutory sanction: ACPR or other competent-authority enforcement may include reprimands, injunctions, business restrictions, substantial administrative fines, authorization withdrawal and publication of sanctions. Money-laundering and terrorist-financing conduct may trigger criminal prosecution, confiscation and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal criminal liability for knowing participation, facilitation or serious compliance failures. Asset freezing, confiscation, judicial control, travel restrictions and imprisonment may apply when ordered under the relevant criminal or sanctions regime.
False claims, accounting fraud and reserve manipulation

Statutory sanction: Restatement, prudential capital measures, regulatory sanctions, tax consequences, damages to counterparties and criminal penalties for fraud, forgery or false accounting.

Director personal liability: Directors, senior officers, actuaries and other responsible persons may face personal civil and criminal liability, professional bans, confiscation and imprisonment where the elements of the relevant offence are proven.
Improper profit distribution and capital impairment

Statutory sanction: Repayment of unlawful dividends, shareholder and director liability, corporate-law penalties, insolvency claims and potential criminal sanctions for unlawful distribution or misuse of corporate assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may owe restitution and damages, face management disqualification and be exposed to insolvency contribution claims. Imprisonment or travel restrictions require a qualifying criminal offence and judicial order.
