Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

The National Institute of Industrial Property (Institut national de la propriété industrielle, INPI) operates the mandatory online One-Stop Shop (Guichet unique des formalités des entreprises). The competent commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de commerce) records the company in the Registre du commerce et des sociétés (RCS).

Governing statute

French Commercial Code (Code de commerce), including Articles L.210-1 et seq. (commercial companies), L.227-1 to L.227-20 (SAS), L.123-1 et seq. (commercial and companies registers), and R.123-1 et seq. (registration formalities). Civil Code provisions on contributions and company formation also apply.

Formation / constitutional filings

Electronic company-formation declaration submitted through the Guichet unique, with the signed SAS articles of association and supporting documents; the filing results in RCS registration and issuance of a SIREN number and Kbis extract.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

A SAS has no statutory minimum share capital. At least 50% of cash contributions must be paid upon subscription; the balance must be paid within five years after registration. Cash contributions must be deposited with an authorized depositary, such as a bank or notary, and a deposit certificate obtained before filing. Contributions in kind are generally fully contributed at formation and may require a contribution auditor unless a statutory exemption applies. The USD fee figure is an estimate; official fees are charged in euros and vary with applicable filing charges and exchange rates.

Funding & unlock path: No sector-specific fire, structural-load, or workplace operating clearance is ordinarily required for a conventional office. The company must comply with applicable workplace health and safety duties. If employees work in premises open to the public, applicable ERP fire-safety and accessibility requirements apply; premises, equipment, or activities subject to specific building or installation rules require the relevant inspections and approvals.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No industrial licensing board is ordinarily competent for a marine cargo or hull damage survey consultancy in France. The activity does not, by itself, require an industrial operating licence. The competent formalities authority remains the INPI Guichet unique; sector-specific matters may involve the French Prudential Supervision and Resolution Authority (Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution, ACPR) if the company conducts regulated insurance distribution or other regulated insurance activity.

Category: No general operating or manufacturing permit is required solely to perform marine insurance surveys. The company must not conduct insurance distribution, brokerage, or another regulated activity without satisfying the applicable authorization or registration requirements.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), operating through the Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) for corporate income tax and TVA; statutory business filing and creation formalities are channelled through the Guichet Unique on formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr; taxpayer portal: impots.gouv.fr

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of a French SAS, the statutory representative submits the single incorporation file (M0) through the Guichet Unique, which automatically generates the SIREN/SIRET identifiers and requests from the DGFiP the intra-Community TVA number, the EU forwarding data and the customs EORI number; a non-established foreign entity that carries out or intends to carry out taxable operations in France must file Form 3555 (CA-SD) with the competent SIE before its first transaction, attaching a power of attorney, identity documents and, where applicable, proof of foreign VAT registration; the DGFiP then attributes a French TVA number within approximately 4 to 6 weeks, and an EORI number must separately be obtained via the French Customs portal if the entity undertakes customs formalities.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — Upon incorporation of a French SAS, the statutory representative submits the single incorporation file (M0) through the Guichet Unique, which automatically generates the SIREN/SIRET identifiers and requests from the DGFiP the intra-Community TVA number, the EU forwarding data and the customs EORI number; a non-established foreign entity that carries out or intends to carry out taxable operations in France must file Form 3555 (CA-SD) with the competent SIE before its first transaction, attaching a power of attorney, identity documents and, where applicable, proof of foreign VAT registration; the DGFiP then attributes a French TVA number within approximately 4 to 6 weeks, and an EORI number must separately be obtained via the French Customs portal if the entity undertakes customs formalities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

A French SAS is by default subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on its worldwide profits and benefits (art. 206-1 CGI); there is no general check-the-box election. A qualifying small SAS incorporated within the last five years may elect, under restrictive control and shareholding conditions, taxation in the name of the partners under personal income tax (art. 239 bis AA CGI) by filing Form 2065-IR. A parent SAS may elect the French fiscal integration regime (art. 223 A CGI) with its 95%-owned French subsidiaries. EU ATAD hybrid mismatch rules are enacted in art. 212 bis and art. 209-0 CGI, requiring the profit-attribution adjustment in the payer entity and disallowance of deductions in hybrid mismatch situations; no US-style check-the-box mechanism exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic registration threshold applies to foreign or non-established taxable persons: any supplier making taxable supplies in France must obtain a French TVA number from the first transaction. For French-established small businesses only, the domestic franchise en base de TVA exemption applies where annual turnover does not exceed EUR 85,000 (principal activity: sale of goods, accommodation or food) or EUR 37,500 (principal activity: services, including survey activities); this exemption is not available to foreign-established persons and does not create a registration threshold for export-oriented operators.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported outside the European Union benefit from an exemption with the right to deduct input VAT (art. 262-I-1° CGI) where the existence of the export is proved by documents confirming that the goods were actually transferred out of the territory of the EU and that the exporter holds the ownership and transport records; intra-EU B2B supplies are exempt under art. 262-ter CGI subject to the customer's valid intra-Community TVA number and recapitulative statement requirements. For the marine insurance survey service rendered to an underwriter established outside France, the B2B place-of-supply rule (art. 259-1 CGI) situates the service in the customer's country, so the service is outside the scope of French VAT; the French SAS must retain the service contract, assignment letter, survey report, inward remittance voucher and, where applicable, the foreign customer's official tax registration evidence to support non-taxation in France.

  • Customs Export Declaration (DAU/ECS, filed through the DELTA system)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical exit from the EU
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT statement evidencing credit of export proceeds to the French VAT-registered account
  • Insurance policy and survey assignment letter linking the service to the marine claim
  • Cargo damage survey report with photographic and log evidence
  • Foreign underwriter's official tax identification / EORI or legalised VAT certificate confirming non-French establishment

France mandates structured electronic invoicing and e-reporting under the 'Y' model, in which approved dematerialisation platforms (PDP) exchange e-invoice flows with taxpayers and transmit the required transaction data to the DGFiP, alongside the public portal (Portail Public de Facturation). All companies must be able to receive structured e-invoices from 1 September 2026; large and mid-size enterprises must issue e-invoices and file e-reporting from 1 September 2026, and small enterprises from 1 September 2027. Cross-border e-reporting obligations (B2B supplies to non-French parties, intra-Community supplies, B2C online sales) operate through the same e-reporting channel; there is no real-time transactional clearance or physical digital stamp; import and export movements are transmitted through the French Customs DELTA declaration system.

The DGFiP rigorously audits export exemption proof; the export or service documentation must be complete by the due date of the VAT return concerned. Where proof is missing, late, incomplete or false, the exemption is retrospectively withdrawn and the French SAS must pay the VAT omitted, plus automatic late-payment interest of 0.40% per month and penalty interest of 5% (ordinary failure) or 40% (bad-faith intentional omission) under art. 1728 and 1729 CGI; in cases of fabricated customs, transport or banking documents, the 80% penalty for fraudulent schemes (manoeuvres frauduleuses) applies, and criminal prosecution under art. 1741 CGI may result in up to EUR 500,000 fine and five years' imprisonment. The tax authorities may recover for three years from the event giving rise to the tax (art. L. 186 LPF), and the survey company may additionally be exposed to professional liability and insurance-regulatory sanctions where survey documentation has been falsified.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate (post-2022) plus 15% reduced rate on the first EUR 42,000 of taxable profit for SME-eligible taxpayers

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

25% withholding tax under French domestic law (Article 119 bis du CGI) on dividends distributed to non-resident corporate shareholders; 12.8% for non-resident individuals. Withholding reduced to 0% under the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive where the parent holds at least 5% of the SAS for a minimum 2-year period and meets anti-abuse conditions. Tax treaties may also reduce the rate to 0%, 5%, or 10% depending on the treaty and shareholding threshold.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For fiscal years ending on 31 December, deadline is the second business day following May 1 of the following year. For off-balance fiscal years, deadline is the third month after the accounting year-end. Any balance of corporate income tax must be paid on the same deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Transfer Pricing Documentation (Local File and Master File) for a French SAS where the entity's annual turnover or balance sheet total exceeds EUR 50 million (Article L. 13 AA of the French Tax Procedure Code). Country-by-Country Reporting is required for a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million (with the French entity as the reporting constituent entity or a designated surrogate).

Economic substance scope: France does not apply Gulf-Cooperation-Council-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the substance doctrine is enforced through the corporate-law shell-company jurisprudence, the general anti-abuse rule (abus de droit, Article L. 64 LPF) and the OECD principal-purpose test. A marine insurance surveyor SAS must maintain genuine staff (surveyors), functional premises, client contracts, business records, and decision-making in France. Absence of such substance will trigger recharacterization, denial of deductions, 80% penalty, and potential criminal liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraude Fiscale

Statutory sanction: Article 1741 of the General Tax Code: criminal fine up to EUR 75,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; aggravated fraud committed in an organized band raises the fine to EUR 500,000 and imprisonment to 7 years. Civil sanctions include an 80% fraud penalty (Article 1729 CGI) plus late payment interest at 0.2% per month.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally committed or concealed the fraud may be jointly liable for the corporate tax debt under the action en comblement de passif (Commercial Code L.651-2). Courts may order travel bans, asset freezes, or imprisonment in flagrant cases; persistent director involvement may lead to a director ban or imprisonment equal to the criminal sentence.
Shell Company / Abus de Biens Sociaux

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the French SAS's tax base under the abuse of law procedure (Article L. 64 LPF) leading to additional tax of 80%; denial of deductions for non-business charges; the French Commercial Court may order corporate dissolution if the company has no real business activity.

Director personal liability: Directors and the president of the SAS may be held personally liable for the entire fiscal reorganization, including a treble damage penalty under abuse of law. The president may face additional civil action for misuse of corporate assets (abus de biens sociaux) with criminal sanctions of up to 5 years of imprisonment and EUR 375,000 fine (or 7 years / EUR 500,000 aggravating circumstances).
Money Laundering / AML

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 324-1 and following of the French Penal Code: for legal persons, a fine up to EUR 1 million (five times the mandatory for individuals?) and imprisonment up to 5 years for individuals; additional sanctions include confiscation of the amount laundered and temporary prohibition of the professional activity. Fines may rise to 75% of the value of the laundered funds.

Director personal liability: Corporate criminal liability does not exclude directors or managers individually. Directors found complicit in money-laundering operations face imprisonment up to 5 years and individual fines up to EUR 750,000, plus travel bans and freezing of personal assets. Their professional insurance license may be revoked by the relevant survey registrar.
