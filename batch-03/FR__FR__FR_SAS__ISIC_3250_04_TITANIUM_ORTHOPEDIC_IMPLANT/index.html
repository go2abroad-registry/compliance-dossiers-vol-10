Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; operating portal: impots.gouv.fr (Espace Professionnel); corporate registration: guichet-entreprises.gouv.fr and INSEE SIRENE; customs and export clearance: Direction Générale des Douanes et Droits Indirects (DGDDI), douane.gouv.fr, via the DELTA electronic filing platform.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1: Incorporate the SAS by executing articles of association; no minimum capital applies (Art. L. 227-2 Code de commerce); the president may be a non-resident individual or legal entity. Step 2: File the incorporation package through the single digital window 'Guichet Entreprises' (successor to the CFE) to obtain RCS registration (Kbis certificate) and the INSEE SIREN/SIRET identifiers. Step 3: Submit the declaration of existence (formulaire n° 1487-SD) to the competent SIE; DGFiP then assigns the French intra-Community VAT identification number (format: FR + 2-digit control key + 9-digit SIREN), with VIES activation normally within five working days. Step 4: Open a French bank account for share capital deposit, FX repatriation, and settlement of export transactions. Step 5: A foreign enterprise without a French permanent establishment may register directly for VAT (standard forms 3559/3517 via impots.gouv.fr) or apply the OSS for distance sales; however, the French-established SAS manufacturing entity is mandatorily registered with DGFiP from its first taxable transaction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
No US-style check-the-box election exists under French law. Default: the SAS is subject to corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés) at entity level (CGI Art. 206-1; standard 25%; reduced 15% on the first EUR 42,500 of profit for qualifying SMEs). Elective mechanisms available: (i) option for pass-through taxation for small/young SAS meeting CGI Art. 239 bis AA (transparency at the level of individual shareholders for a maximum of five fiscal years); (ii) VAT grouping option (assujettissement unique à la TVA, CGI Art. 256 C-III bis) for closely related French and EEA entities; (iii) tax consolidation (régime d'intégration fiscale, CGI Art. 223 A) where the SAS or its French parent holds at least 95% of eligible French subsidiaries; (iv) worldwide/consolidated income regimes (bénéfice mondial/consolidation) requiring specific ministerial authorization and rarely granted. France's ATAD 2 anti-hybrid rules (CGI Art. 209-IX and Art. 212 bis) neutralize hybrid mismatches automatically; a French SAS cannot be treated as a disregarded or hybrid entity for French tax purposes.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The euro is fully convertible for current and capital account transactions within France and the EU. No exchange control authorization is required for inward or outward payments related to trade, capital contributions, or profit repatriation. Profit remittance hurdles are corporate-law based (distributable reserves, solvency tests) and, where applicable, dividend withholding tax under domestic law and tax treaties; there are no FX convertibility or liquidity restrictions on the transfer of euro-denominated earnings. Inward direct investment in the manufacture of orthopedic implants is not a national-security-sensitive sector requiring prior MNE approval, although standard EU/Moneyval AML reporting and French balance-of-payments statistical declarations apply to all cross-border flows.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. French and EU law impose no surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for repatriating export proceeds. Exporters may retain foreign currency in authorized bank accounts in France or elsewhere in the EU and may convert freely into euro. Settlement of export transactions is monitored by resident banks for balance-of-payments reporting to the Banque de France, but no conversion obligation attaches to the exporter.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Banque de France (France's Central Bank; within the Eurosystem, the Banque de France acts as monetary authority and conducts balance-of-payments statistical collection, while foreign exchange regulatory powers are largely superseded by EU capital-movement liberalization under the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Medium feasibility as supplementary payment rail, but Low viability as a primary treasury account for the French SAS. ACPR-licensed or EU passported electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions can provide lighter-touch payment accounts and facilitate cross-border payouts, but they are not permitted to offer full current-account credit facilities, foreign exchange custody, or letters of credit. For offshore digital EMIs operating without an ACPR licence, legal constraint is absolute: they cannot provide payment services to French establishments without a passport or authorization under PSD2, and any such arrangement is unenforceable and exposes the company to unlicensed-activity risk. Accordingly, fintech EMIs should be used only for small-value disbursements and not for capital escrow, commercial LC issuance, or major export collections.

Root cause: Manufacturing entity with limited operating history but high initial turnover projections and exports to non-EU medical distributors

        
Defense evidence: Fully executed commercial contracts and pro-forma invoices with the first ten target distributors; ISO 13485 certificate; notified-body CE technical file attestation; corporate group structure; extraction RCS (registre du commerce et des sociétés); directors' passports; evidence of lease and physical manufacturing site; detailed 3-year cash-flow forecast.

      

      
      

        Large inbound shareholder loan or capital injection from a non-EU parent without clear commercial purpose
        
Root cause: Large inbound shareholder loan or capital injection from a non-EU parent without clear commercial purpose

        
Defense evidence: Shareholder resolutions and board minutes; share subscription agreement or formal loan deed with interest rate and maturity; source-of-funds evidence from the parent's bank; parent's audited financial statements; tax residency certificate; group organisational chart; statement of intended use for the funds (CAPEX, titanium powder purchases, payroll).

      

      
      

        Beneficial ownership held through a multi-tiered holding structure or entities resident in jurisdictions with high AML opacity
        
Root cause: Beneficial ownership held through a multi-tiered holding structure or entities resident in jurisdictions with high AML opacity

        
Defense evidence: Complete UBO chain with identity documents for each natural person; register extracts of all intermediate entities; declaration of non-PEP/non-sanctions status; notarised ownership chart; trust declaration if applicable; explanation of the commercial rationale for the holding structure; legal opinion from a French avocat validating the ownership chain.

      

      
      

        Request for rapid operational disbursements to third-party suppliers in jurisdictions designated as higher-risk for money laundering or sanctions exposure
        
Root cause: Request for rapid operational disbursements to third-party suppliers in jurisdictions designated as higher-risk for money laundering or sanctions exposure

        
Defense evidence: Approved supplier due diligence packets including trade licence, VAT/TPIN number, bank details, beneficial owner list; original purchase order and logistics documentation; bill of lading/air waybill for the specific titanium powder or ancillary materials; risk-based sanctions screening memo; legal export control clearance for the final implant product if crossing non-EU territory.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Capital injection should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident provider to the French SAS capital-escrow account at the chosen Tier-1 bank. The payment message must carry the explicit corporate purpose in field 70 (remittance information), e.g. 'subcription to increase share capital of FR SAS' or 'shareholder loan for equipment financing', and the French bank will upload the transaction into the Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical framework. The company must deliver, before execution, the board/shareholder resolutions, signed subscription documents, and proof that the incoming amount matches the corporate records. No prior FX licence is needed because the euro is fully convertible, but the bank may delay release until the KYC - including source of funds and ultimate beneficial owner - is fully cleared. If the injection qualifies as non-resident direct investment, the French Treasury's 'sensitive sector' screening does not apply to orthopedic implant manufacturing, yet the receiving bank must still capture the economic ownership details for INSEE/balance-of-payments classification.Raw-material purchases (titanium powders, additive manufacturing consumables) and supplier invoicing should settle via SWIFT MT103 for low-value recurring payments, using ISO 20022 purpose codes and exact vendor reference numbers. For large supply contracts, use commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 from the French bank, with the company as applicant and the supplier as beneficiary. For export sales of orthopedic implants, collections from foreign distributors should be routed through SWIFT MT103 cash payments or, for larger orders, documentary collections (DP/DA) or an export L/C advised by the foreign buyer's bank. The common clearing rails therefore are instant SEPA CRT for euro-denominated EU counterparties, SWIFT MT103 for intra-EU and cross-border non-EU wires, and SWIFT MT700/MT760 for trade-finance instruments; no alternative digital asset or virtual account clearing rail should be used while France applies standard EU payment security rules.Deliverable FX Forward (EUR/USD, EUR/CNY) for firm trade commitmentsNon-Deliverable Forward (NDF) restricted to non-convertible or limited-convertibility counterpart currencies where a deliverable offshore market is unavailableCross-Currency Swap (EUR/USD) for long-term balance-sheet offsetting or matched cash-flow hedgingEuropean-style currency option or swaption tailored to budget-rate protection on large titanium powder purchases

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to 500% of evaded tax plus imprisonment up to 5 years

        
Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability, disqualification from office, and potential civil restitution claims

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Retroactive tax adjustments, penalties up to 500% of additional tax, and interest charges

        
Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability if they knowingly approve or fail to supervise non-compliant arrangements

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Recharacterization as non-resident, loss of tax residency benefits, and fines up to €1 million

        
Director personal liability: Personal liability for misrepresentation, possible criminal sanctions, and director disqualification
