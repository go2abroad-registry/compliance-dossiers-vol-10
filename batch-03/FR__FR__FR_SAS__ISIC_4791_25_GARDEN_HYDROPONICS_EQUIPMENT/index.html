Stage 02

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) kept by the Greffe du Tribunal de Commerce of the registered office, operating through the INPI single electronic portal (Guichet unique — formalites.entreprises.gouv.fr); INSEE attributes the SIREN and SIRET identifiers upon registration.

Governing statute

Code de commerce (French Commercial Code), Book II, Title II, Chapter VII, Articles L.227-1 to L.227-20 (Société par Actions Simplifiée); general incorporation provisions of Articles L.210-1 to L.210-10; RCS registration rules of Articles L.123-1 to L.123-12; Decree No. 2019-1061 and the INPI Guichet unique implementing decree of 9 June 2022; PACTE Act No. 2019-486 abolishing the minimum capital threshold.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statuts de constitution signed by all shareholders, filed together with the standard M0 declaration of incorporation, submitted electronically to the Guichet unique and inscribed in the RCS; the official company extract issued is the extrait Kbis.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 2 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; the amount set in the Statuts must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be deposited into an escrow account (licensed bank, notaire, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations) before filing and may be released only after the RCS registration of the company; in-kind contributions require a valuation report of a commissaire aux apports unless waived unanimously by the shareholders.

Funding & unlock path:

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum share capital applies to an SAS; the amount set in the Statuts must be fully subscribed. Cash contributions must be deposited into an escrow account (licensed bank, notaire, or Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations) before filing and may be released only after the RCS registration of the company; in-kind contributions require a valuation report of a commissaire aux apports unless waived unanimously by the shareholders.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (DGFiP), under the Ministère de l'Économie, des Finances et de la Souveraineté industrielle et numérique; online services accessible via https://www.impots.gouv.fr. Business registration and VAT number procurement coordinated through the Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés (RCS) via Infogreffe and the CFE/Guichet unique managed by the INPI.

E-government filing portal

A French SAS, including one wholly owned by non-residents, obtains its tax identity by filing the standard incorporation dossier (M0 plus supporting documents, e.g., statutory auditors, shareholding identification, principal shareholder statements) through the electronic one-stop shop (Guichet unique) administered by INPI. The DGFiP automatically assigns the SIREN (national company number) and SIRET (establishment number), and the VAT number (numéro de TVA intracommunautaire FRXXXX) is issued once the company is registered at RCS. Non-established foreign entities making taxable supplies in France without an EU establishment must request direct VAT registration from the DGFiP's Service des Impôts des Entreprises (SIE) and, in certain circumstances, appoint a fiscal representative established in France who is jointly and severally liable for VAT. For intra-EU e-commerce distance sales and imports, the non-resident may instead use the Union One-Stop Shop (OSS) or Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) regimes rather than full French VAT registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation. A SAS is a capital company (société de capitaux) and is unconditionally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on its worldwide profits, subject to the regime of territoriality (régime territorial) which exempts profits of permanently-established foreign branches and does not allow deduction of related foreign losses. There is no pass-through or fiscal transparency treatment available for a SAS under French domestic law; the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance-Directive and hybrid mismatch rules do not alter this default. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A SAS is a capital company (société de capitaux) and is unconditionally subject to French corporate income tax (impôt sur les sociétés, IS) on its worldwide profits, subject to the regime of territoriality (régime territorial) which exempts profits of permanently-established foreign branches and does not allow deduction of related foreign losses. There is no pass-through or fiscal transparency treatment available for a SAS under French domestic law; the EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance-Directive and hybrid mismatch rules do not alter this default. Tax_Exempt_Zone is not applicable to the standard commercial profit center; however, specific territorial tax reliefs (e.g., zones franches d'activités, régime fiscal de Corse, or IFER exemptions) are available only as narrowly-scoped incentives and do not change the overall entity-level IS status.

Available election forms

France operates no general check-the-box election akin to the U.S. 'eligible entity' classification. The principal elections relevant to a French SAS are: (1) election under CGI Article 223 A to form an intégration fiscale (tax group) with French subsidiaries if the SAS holds at least 95% of the shares directly or indirectly; (2) election for the 'régime fiscal des sociétés mères' (mother-daughter regime) allowing 95% exemption of dividend income, coupled with the optional EBIT/dividend quota mechanism; (3) election under Article 239 quater C for a SARL to be treated as transparent, not available for a SAS; (4) annual option, if eligible, for the VAT réel simplifié regime; and (5) application of the French hybrid mismatch neutralization rules under CGI Article 212 bis and the ATAD 1 rules (interest limitation, CFC, exit taxation), which are mandatory rather than elective. There is no check-the-box election to treat a French SAS as a partnership for French tax or for U.S. check-the-box mapping; U.S. or other foreign owners may inadvertently create a CFC or PFIC from the foreign side but the SAS remains an entity-level taxpayer in France.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a French-established SAS: mandatory VAT registration from the first taxable transaction (no statutory de minimis threshold). For intra-EU cross-border distance sales of goods to non-taxable persons (e-commerce), the European-wide annual distance-sales threshold applicable via the One-Stop Shop (OSS) is €10,000; below that threshold, a French vendor may apply French VAT throughout the EU, and once the €10,000 aggregate threshold is exceeded, the vendor must account for VAT in each destination Member State via the OSS. There is no separate French domestic turnover threshold for online sales of gardening equipment by an already-established SAS; the company is already registered. A foreign e-commerce seller without a French establishment becomes subject to French VAT from its first sale into France unless it uses the IOSS for consignment stocks or low-value imports, in which case the IOSS threshold per consignment is €150.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A French SAS may apply the 0% VAT rate (exonération à l'exportation) under CGI Article 262-I-1 for goods physically removed from France to a destination outside the European Union, provided the sale is evidenced by the transport of goods out of the EU territory. The exporter must hold documentary proof of export (preuve d'exportation) in the form prescribed by Revenue (BOI-TVA-CHAMP-30-10-10), normally an electronic or paper Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Échanges de Marchandises) via DELTA, corroborated by a transport document (CMR, bill of lading, airway bill, or postal receipt) and, for certain transport modalities, the customs exit confirmation. Intra-EU dispatches to a VAT-registered customer are not 'export zero-rating' but rather exempt non-collection under Article 262 ter CGI, qualifying on the basis of the customer's valid EU VAT number, Intrastat or recapitulative statement reporting, and proof of physical movement into the other Member State within the statutory deadlines (generally not later than the 15th day of the month following the quarter). Services directly connected to exported goods (FOB/transport services) may also be zero-rated under Article 259 D CGI when the recipient's effective establishment is outside the EU; digital/e-commerce services to consumers are subject to OSS rather than export zero-rating. Because the underlying platform sale of hydroponics equipment is treated as a distance sale of goods, the zero-rating applies only to the physical export movements, not to the mere sale of hardware or software downloads unless outside the EU.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Déclaration d'Échanges de Marchandises / DAE) submitted via the DELTA customs system and bearing the enforceable exit date
  • Export commercial invoice (facture commerciale d'exportation) with complete seller and buyer identification, breakdown of VAT treatment, and Incoterms clause
  • Transport evidence: Bill of Lading (B/L) or International Consignment Note (CMR) or Air Waybill or Post Office receipt for postal exports, showing the destination outside the EU
  • Proof of exit from EU customs territory (exit confirmation from the customs office of export or, for non-EU transit, the final customs office) and any dual-use or agricultural documentation if applicable
  • Bank documentation supporting receipt of payment from the foreign buyer, such as the SWIFT MT103/MT202 receipt or an Inward Remittance Certificate if the foreign exchange/immigration regime so requires
  • Where an intra-EU dispatch is involved instead of an export outside the EU: valid EU VAT number of the customer as separately verified through the VIES electronic database, the recapitulative statement (état récapitulatif / EC Sales List), and dispatch proof

France is phasing in a mandatory B2B electronic invoicing and e-reporting reform. Beginning 1 September 2024, all businesses must be able to receive structured electronic invoices via the Chorus Pro platform or through accredited Private Digital Platforms (PDPs); from 1 September 2026, all B2B e-invoicing becomes compulsory, and B2C transaction data (e-reporting) must be transmitted in near-real-time to the DGFiP. In the interim, electronic cash-register software used for B2C sales must be certified or self-declared as compliant with Articles 286-2 and 290 CGI and the NF 525 / NF 550 technical certification. There is no current general real-time fiscal stamping/clearance mechanism for ordinary e-commerce like the Italian 'lotteria degli scontrini' or a continuous controls system; however, the DGFiP requires the periodic transmission of VAT return data via the professional portal, and exports are separately monitored through the customs export declaration system (DELTA/GUN). Invoices issued to French public-sector entities must be transmitted through Chorus Pro under Decree No. 92-466. The e-invoice must use a compliant format (Factur-X, UBL, CII) and contain mandatory French VAT fields, including the intra-Community VAT numbers of both parties, when supplies are intra-EU.

The DGFiP applies increased audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims, requiring full documentary proof of physical exit. If the documentary bundle is incomplete, false, or fabricated, the initial zero-rating is reversed and the full 20% French VAT is assessed on the ex-VAT sale price, triggering: late payment interest (intérêt de retard at 0.20% per month, i.e., 2.4% annually), plus an automatic 5% penalty (majoration) solely for late tax filing, and a 40% systematic penalty (insuffisance de déclaration) under CGI Article 1731; in cases of bad faith or dissimulation of facts, the penalty rises to 80% (Article 1729); if the fraudulent substance is established, criminal prosecution under Article 1741 CGI may lead to fines up to €500,000 (increased to €1,000,000 in aggravated cases), imprisonment up to five years, and posting/banishing sanctions. The clawback period for assessed VAT is generally the current calendar year plus three prior calendar years (six years in certain cases of non-filed or late-filed returns, or derived from undisclosed beneficial-owner facts), and Belgium and France trigger joint liability for falsified intra-EU claims where a self-supply has not been accounted for; the fraudster and any accomplices are solidarily liable under Article 1741. Furthermore, an entity relying on a deliberately false Inward Remittance Certificate or on a nominal 'commissionnaire' arrangement to simulate exports is exposed to tax fraud prosecution and an even higher 100% penalty in cases of deliberate non-invoicing or pure withholding on groundless exports; the French customs administration may also institute import- and export-control offenses. To mitigate audit risk, the SAS should retain a full export file (DAE, transport contract, insurance certificate, delivery notes) for at least six years after the tax period and reconcile export data quarterly with the EC Sales List and Intrastat filings. A failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days also triggers French foreign-exchange regulation sanctions, includes the application of the Malta or similar indexation rules when applicable, and can be used by the tax authority as evidence of simulating an export transaction in order to deny both the zero-rating and the corresponding exemption from VAT on services.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

France maintains full current and capital account convertibility under Articles 63-66 TFEU and the French Monetary and Financial Code. No general exchange-control authorization is required for a French SAS to receive foreign equity contributions, repatriate funds, or convert EUR to foreign currency. Profit remittance hurdles are primarily tax-related: dividends paid by a French SAS to non-resident shareholders are subject to French withholding tax at the statutory 25% rate unless reduced under an applicable double-tax treaty, and the distribution must be approved by the shareholders in accordance with SAS corporate formalities. Additional operational constraints include EU financial sanctions screening, FATCA/CRS reporting, and Banque de France balance-of-payments statistical filings; these are not currency-convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements and no mandatory conversion ratios in France or the Eurozone. Export receipts may be retained in EUR or foreign currency in accounts held with authorized credit institutions. Banks require underlying commercial documentation for credit of export proceeds, and Banque de France may require balance-of-payments reporting for transactions above regulatory thresholds. All payments remain subject to AML/KYC controls and EU/OFAC-style sanctions screening.

Central bank supervision: Banque de France (Bank of France), together with the Direction Générale du Trésor (French Treasury) for international capital movement oversight and balance-of-payments reporting; ACPR (Autorité de Contrôle Prudentiel et de Résolution) supervises banking and payment-institution conduct.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as a supplementary payment channel but Low as the sole banking solution. EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions passporting into France may provide IBAN accounts, local collecting, and SEPA/CBPR+ payment rails, but they are not credit institutions and are generally unsuitable for capital escrow, FX hedging, overdrafts, or documentary trade finance. Offshore digital EMIs without an EU/EEA license may not lawfully hold client funds or provide payment services to a French entity; using such providers creates regulatory, tax, and treasury-control risks. Any fintech/EMI solution must verify ACPR authorization or valid EU passport notification, maintain independent client accounts, and execute full French-standard KYC/UBO identity verification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pain.001) from the shareholder's corporate bank account directly to the French SAS's dedicated capital contribution account with a BIC/IBAN of the French receiving bank. The payment must include clear remittance information identifying the purpose as 'augmentation de capital / apport en capital' and reference the shareholder resolution and notarial/corporate filing. No third-party payers are acceptable.
