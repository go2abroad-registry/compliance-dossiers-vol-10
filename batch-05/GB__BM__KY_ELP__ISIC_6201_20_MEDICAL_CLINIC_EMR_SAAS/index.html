Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar (USD), thereby eliminating convertibility risk between BMD and USD. For a non-resident-owned Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP), current account remittances such as software subscription fees, service fees, and royalties are freely convertible and may be repatriated without central bank approval. Capital account transactions, including equity contributions and distributions to foreign partners, are likewise unrestricted provided that the ELP is designated as non-resident for Bermuda Exchange Control purposes, no BMA-regulated activity is conducted without licensing, and all AML/KYC documentation is maintained. Profit remittances are not subject to Bermuda withholding tax or dividend tax; however, the authorised bank may require a board resolution, updated beneficial ownership declarations, and a completed exchange control form where the entity conducts any local Bermuda-source activity or maintains a resident status.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export-proceeds surrender requirement applies to this structure. The Bermuda ELP is treated as non-resident for exchange control purposes and is therefore permitted to retain export receipts from EMR software licensing and related services in USD or any other foreign currency without converting them into BMD. Statutory surrender obligations under Bermuda's Exchange Control Regulations apply to resident entities, which generally must offer foreign currency received from exports to an authorised dealer within a prescribed period; the subject ELP, being non-resident, falls outside that rule. The only compliance requirement is to record the receipt accurately in the ELP's books and preserve source-of-receipt evidence for the bank's periodic review.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility is Low-to-Medium. An offshore digital EMI or electronic money institution may be used only for ancillary incoming payment collection (for example via Stripe Billing), but the principal treasury liquidity must be held at a BMA-licensed bank. Legal constraints arise because Bermuda treats deposit-taking, electronic money issuance, and digital asset activities as regulated activities under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 and the Digital Asset Business Act 2018. An unlicensed offshore EMI cannot lawfully maintain a local Bermuda clearing account or offer settlement services in BMD/USD without a BMA branch or licence. Therefore, the ELP should treat an offshore EMI as a payment collection gateway only, with full custody and clearing at a Bermuda tier-1 bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no corporate income tax on an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP). The ELP is tax-transparent in Bermuda and is not subject to any tax on profits, income, capital gains, or distributions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda does not impose withholding tax on distributions by an exempted limited partnership. Profit distributions are not dividends under Bermuda tax law.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 0 — Bermuda imposes no local transfer pricing filing threshold and no statutory Local File or Master File requirement. However, if the ELP is part of an international group, OECD Master File/Local File obligations arise at the ultimate parent's level. UK-resident partners must also satisfy the arm's length standard under Part 4 TIOPA 2010 for UK-related SaaS transactions, irrespective of Bermuda's nil-tax regime.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018 applies to every Bermuda entity carrying on a 'relevant activity'. For a medical EMR SaaS vehicle under ISIC 6201, the relevant activities likely engaged are (a) Intellectual Property Business if the ELP owns or exploits the EMR software IP, or (b) Distribution and Service Centre Business if it provides services to related parties. The ELP must demonstrate Bermuda governance, qualified local employees, proportionate local operating expenditure, and physical presence. Pure IP holding with royalty-only income is a high-scrutiny category and requires enhanced substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic Substance Failure / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Failure to satisfy the Bermuda economic substance test exposes the entity to financial penalties up to BMD 50,000 per breach, or up to BMD 250,000 for an intellectual-property business, plus continuing daily penalties. The Bermuda Registrar may also exchange information with HMRC under international tax transparency arrangements, and may strike off the ELP, causing forfeiture of Bermudian legal capacity.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the corporate general partner who caused or permitted the substance failure may be personally liable if the offence was committed with their consent or connivance. UK-resident controlling directors may also face HMRC CFC assessments and penalties for deliberate non-disclosure.
Tax Evasion / False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Bermuda prosecutions for payroll tax evasion or false declarations may result in substantial fines, daily surcharges, back tax plus interest, and custodial sentences. Under UK law, HMRC may recharacterise the ELP as UK resident if central management and control is exercised in the UK, triggering UK corporation tax at 19% or 25% on global profits, plus penalties up to 100% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: General partners carry unlimited liability; a fraudulent or negligent general partner can be pursued for unpaid taxes, penalties, and civil restitution. Directors of a corporate general partner may be disqualified or criminally charged, and signatories to false declarations can face imprisonment, travel bans, and asset freezing pending trial.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda's Beneficial Ownership and AML regimes impose monetary penalties, imprisonment, and adverse regulatory findings. The Proceeds of Crime Act provides criminal enforcement for money laundering offences; sustained non-compliance can jeopardise the ELP's Bermuda banking and registered agent relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers of the general partner can be held personally liable where the offence is attributable to their neglect. Limited partners who take part in management may also lose their limited liability shield and become personally responsible for partnership obligations.
Transfer Pricing / Cross-Border Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Although Bermuda levies no tax, the taxing right is reallocated in the counterparty jurisdiction. HMRC may make transfer pricing adjustments under TIOPA 2010, disallow deductions, charge late payment interest, and impose penalties up to 100% of the understated tax. BEPS disclosure and country-by-country reporting may further expose the structure to multilateral review.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length arrangements may be sued for breach of fiduciary duty. In insolvency, the general partner may be required to restore mispriced amounts, and individual signatories may face HMRC tribunal rulings, personal penalty assessments, and adverse cost orders.
