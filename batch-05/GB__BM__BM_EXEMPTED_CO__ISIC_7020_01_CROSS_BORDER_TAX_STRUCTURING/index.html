Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Government of Bermuda, Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC), Ministry of Finance; online portal via the Bermuda Tax eServices (https://www.gov.bm/tax) and the Companies eRegistry portal

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Bermuda Exempted Company, the statutory procurement procedure is: (1) incorporate under the Companies Act 1981 with the Registrar of Companies and obtain a Certificate of Incorporation; (2) if the company is a constituent entity of a multinational enterprise group with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more, register with the OTC under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CITA) within 30 days of becoming a registered person; (3) obtain a Corporate Income Tax (CIT) registration number and a Bermuda Tax Account Reference (TAR); (4) for groups outside the scope, no CIT registration is mandatory, but the Exempted Company must still apply for an Employer Payroll Tax registration number if it employs Bermuda resident staff and obtain a Customs Identification Number for importing goods; (5) registration is completed through the Bermuda Tax eServices online portal (gov.bm) with submission of the certificate of incorporation, local address, directors' details, and ownership chart.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, an in-scope Bermuda Exempted Company may elect to be a qualified domestic minimum top-up tax (QDMTT) payer, or elect to apply transitional economic substance safe-harbour calculations. There is no US Check-the-Box election because a Bermuda corporation is a per se corporation. For UK hybrid mismatch purposes, the entity may file an election to disapply certain hybrid regulations in cross-border arrangements, but no general pass-through election is available under Bermuda law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Bermuda Customs Duty and Employer Payroll Tax (no VAT/GST or sales & use tax)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - no VAT/GST; no general economic threshold applies. For customs duty, every importer must register regardless of value; for payroll tax, an employer with annual Bermuda remuneration above the applicable exemption (currently BMD 3,000 per employee per quarter) must register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No VAT/GST is imposed in Bermuda; therefore there is no statutory zero-rating or refund mechanism for exported goods or offshore services. For customs duty, exported goods are outside the charge to duty if properly declared. For the purposes of corporate income tax, zero-rating has no application; the Bermuda Exempted Company will only be subject to CITA if it is in-scope of the EUR 750M threshold, in which case exports of tax structuring advisory services remain taxable under ordinary source and attribution rules.

  • Bermuda Customs Export Declaration (Form CUS-EXP1)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Certificate of Origin
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank debit/credit statement evidencing FX repatriation

Bermuda does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or API-based digital stamping. The Bermuda Customs Service uses the Automated Commercial Environment (ACE) for import clearance, but there is no continuous transaction control or e-invoice clearance obligation for services. Paper or PDF invoices are accepted.

False or fraudulent export declarations are punishable under the Bermuda Customs Management Act 2010; goods may be seized and forfeited, and a fine of up to BMD 10,000 and/or imprisonment of up to two years may be imposed. For CITA purposes, fraudulent misrepresentations or failure to register attract penalty units up to BMD 1,500 per day of default, plus interest and retrospective clawback of any wrongly claimed exemption or reduced rate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged 1:1 to the US Dollar. Bermuda maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions, with no exchange control restrictions. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriations are freely made subject only to standard AML/ATF transaction reporting thresholds. The BMA does not require exchange control approval for cross-border movements of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirements exist. Bermudian law permits the free retention, allocation, and repatriation of export receipts in any currency. Businesses are only obliged to maintain accurate records and report certain large transactions to the BMA under the Proceeds of Crime Regulations 2008.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Bermuda's Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 requires any entity taking deposits to hold a full banking licence. Foreign fintech EMI platforms (e.g., UK-based electronic money institutions) are generally not permitted to act as primary treasury banks for a Bermuda Exempted Company, though they may be used as ancillary payment gateways for outbound low-value transactions. The BMA requires all deposit-taking activity to be conducted through licensed institutions.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Bermuda's Companies Act 1981 does not impose a general statutory audit on an ordinary exempted company. An audit becomes mandatory only when the company (i) is a regulated entity (insurance, banking, fund administration, trust company, money service business), (ii) holds a permit to carry on business in Bermuda, (iii) is listed or registered on an approved stock exchange, or (iv) is required by shareholder resolution, lender covenants, or a Bermuda Corporate Income Tax / Pillar Two compliance need. A cross-border tax-structuring advisory exempted company therefore commonly operates without a statutory audit unless its bank, group governance, or the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax regime requires audited financial statements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for Bermuda Exempted Companies not within the scope of the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CIT Act); 15% applies only to constituent entities of MNE groups with consolidated group revenue equal to or exceeding EUR 750m for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2024.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by an Exempted Company to its shareholders, whether resident or non-resident. Distribution of profits is also free of capital gains or income tax in Bermuda.

Persistent non-filing may result in an estimated assessment by the Tax Administration Office and criminal prosecution with fines up to BMD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No domestic currency-specific transfer pricing filing threshold exists under Bermuda law. For in-scope MNE groups (EUR 750m consolidated revenue threshold, approximately BMD 1.25bn), the TAO may request the preparation of OECD-mandated Local File, Master File, and Country-by-Country Report under the Bermuda CIT Act, mirroring OECD Pillar Two rules. The statutory record-keeping threshold is set at BMD 750,000 of related-party transactions or BMD 7.5m of intercompany financing balances, whichever is lower, for entities choosing to rely on the transactional safe harbours.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2018 apply to relevant activities such as banking, insurance, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, shipping, distribution and service centres, intellectual property holding, and pure equity holding. 'Tax structuring consultancy' (ISIC 7020) is not currently a statutorily classified relevant activity; therefore, the Exempted Company is not required to file an ESR Economic Substance Return solely on account of this business line. However, the company must maintain substantive presence, including a licensed registered office, at least two directors of appropriate standing, board meetings held in Bermuda, and core income-generating activities undertaken in Bermuda, to avoid recharacterisation as a shell or as tax-resident in an onshore jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Concealment

Statutory sanction: Assaulted tax assessments up to 300% of the evaded CIT, financial penalties up to BMD 250,000, dissolution of the entity, and blacklisting by the TAO. Criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and shadow directors may be jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt if they signed false declarations. The Supreme Court of Bermuda may issue a civil restraint order, a travel ban, and confiscation of personal assets. Individual fines of up to BMD 100,000 and custodial sentences are available on conviction.
Lack of Economic Substance / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may strike the company off the register, reject the annual declaration, and impose a financial penalty of BMD 10,000 plus a daily penalty of BMD 100. The entity loses all legal capacity to enforce contracts. In cross-border cases, foreign tax authorities may disregard the entity entirely.

Director personal liability: Directors face breach of fiduciary duty liability for allowing the company to continue without substance. In insolvency, a court may pierce the corporate veil and order directors to return distributed profits. The Bermuda Financial Sanctions Tribunal also has power to impose travel restrictions and asset freezes where the shell is used as a conduit for illicit flows.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Failures

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalties of up to BMD 500,000 per breach, revocation of the corporate services provider licence, and disqualification of the company from providing consultancy services. The entity may also be subject to a Suspicious Activity Report referral to the Financial Intelligence Agency and the Bermuda Monetary Authority.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers can be held personally criminally liable for AML offences, with imprisonment up to 5 years. Civil liability extends to compensation for loss suffered by clients or third parties. The Proceeds of Crime Act allows the court to order a director's personal bank accounts to be frozen and to require a personal financial declaration.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The TAO may adjust the taxable income to reflect arm's length pricing, deny deductions for non-compliant intercompany payments, and impose a penalty of 100% of the additional tax assessed. Repeated manipulation may lead to a Guernsey-style 'default surcharge' of 20% of the reassessed amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve transfer pricing the board knew to be artificial may be liable for tortious interference with tax administration. They may be required to personally pay the avoided tax if the company is insolvent, and the court can grant worldwide freezing orders over their assets. Criminal referral for tax evasion is possible if they sought to mislead the TAO.
Unauthorised Cross-Border Tax Structuring Activity

Statutory sanction: Where the activity triggers a licensing requirement under the Corporate Service Provider Business (CSPB) Act, the company faces fines up to BMD 250,000 and an order to cease business. If the UK HMRC later telephones the arrangement as tax avoidance under the Disclosure of Tax Avoidance Schemes (DOTAS) rules, the Bermuda entity may be subject to UK sanctions through mutual assistance channels.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable for unauthorised business losses and any resulting tax restitutions. A director who knowingly participated in marketing a tax avoidance scheme that is later counteracted by HMRC may be subject to a UK investigation and an equivalent civil penalty, requiring the director to attend a UK tax tribunal hearing and potentially face a UK travel ban.
