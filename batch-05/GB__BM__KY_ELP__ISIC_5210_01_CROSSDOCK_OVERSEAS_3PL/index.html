Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Office of the Tax Commissioner (Ministry of Finance) with electronic filing portal 'Bermuda Tax Administration'; corporate registry filings with the Registrar of Companies; customs administration via Her Majesty's Customs Bermuda (Asycuda World).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate or register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) under the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992 with the Registrar of Companies to obtain the Bermuda partnership registration number. Step 2: If the ELP is a constituent entity of a large multinational group (consolidated group revenue at or above the EUR 750 million OECD GloBE threshold), register for Corporate Income Tax with the Office of the Tax Commissioner within 30 days of becoming subject to the CIT Act 2023, obtaining a CIT registration reference. Step 3: Register as an employer with the Office of the Tax Commissioner for Payroll Tax and Social Insurance (PRSI) if the ELP has Bermuda-based employees, obtaining a Bermuda Tax Number (BTN). Step 4: Obtain an Importer of Record customs bond with Her Majesty's Customs Bermuda if the ELP imports goods into Bermuda. Step 5: No general VAT/GST TIN exists in Bermuda because Bermuda does not levy a value-added tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Bermuda law treats an ELP as a fiscally transparent flow-through entity; profits allocated to non-resident partners are not subject to Bermuda income tax unless the ELP is within the scope of the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 applicable to large multinational groups, in which case the GloBE rules may attribute income to constituent entities; otherwise no entity-level Bermuda taxation of partnership profits).

Available election forms

Available under the Bermuda CIT Act 2023: (i) Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (QDMTT) election to satisfy Pillar Two safe harbour; (ii) flow-through entity election for a partnership to be treated as a separate constituent entity where required by the GloBE model rules; (iii) partnership income allocation elections among constituent entities; (iv) US-style check-the-box treatment may be achieved indirectly for US federal purposes by a US partner filing IRS Form 8832, but no direct Bermuda check-the-box election exists; hybrid mismatch elections are not part of Bermuda domestic law, and UK hybrid mismatch rules apply only where UK-resident investors are present.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No value-added tax or goods and services tax in Bermuda; indirect taxation is limited to import customs duties under the Customs Tariff Act 1995, stamp duties, and payroll tax; the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 is a direct tax, not an indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST and therefore no turnover registration threshold. Customs duty registration as Importer of Record is required for all commercial importers regardless of turnover; applicable duty rates range from 0% to 25% ad valorem depending on HS classification, with specific sin taxes on alcohol, tobacco, and fuel.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

N/A within Bermuda because Bermuda levies no VAT on supplies or exports. For UK VAT purposes, if the Bermuda ELP registers for UK VAT as a non-established taxable person and acts as exporter of record for goods dispatched from Great Britain to its overseas fulfilment hubs, zero-rating is available under VATA 1994 s.30 and the VAT (Exports) Order 2000, provided the goods are physically exported to a destination outside the UK within 3 months (extendable to 6 months on prior application to HMRC) and the ELP holds valid commercial and customs evidence of export. Cross-dock warehousing and order fulfilment services supplied to overseas e-commerce sellers are generally outside the scope of UK VAT at 0% under the B2B place-of-supply rule (Article 44 of the implementing VAT Directive as retained in UK law) because the recipient is established outside the UK.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK CDS electronic entry with Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from the UK and arrival at the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer records evidencing foreign currency settlement of the export sale
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Certificate of the overseas customer's VAT or business registration evidencing establishment outside the UK

Bermuda has no electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate; no digital stamping or continuous transaction controls exist. Customs declarations are processed electronically through the Asycuda World single-window system via licensed customs brokers, and there is no SAF-T filing obligation in Bermuda.

HMRC aggressively audits export zero-rating claims by non-established fulfilment operators; insufficient, contradictory, or false export evidence triggers repayment of the zero-rated VAT, default interest, and penalties of up to 100% of the tax under FA 2007 Schedule 24; fraudulent evasion can result in criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s.72, asset freezing orders, and liability for directors and partners. In Bermuda, false customs declarations or duty evasion attract forfeiture of goods, penalties of up to 250% of the duty evaded, and prosecution under the Customs Act 1941.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is pegged to the US dollar at 1:1 and is freely convertible for residents and non-residents alike. Bermuda operates no modern exchange-control regime under the Exchange Control Act 1972, and there is no prior-authorisation distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility for genuine commercial transactions. Profit distributions, capital account repatriations, management fees and liquidation proceeds payable to the UK parent or non-resident limited partners may be remitted freely without BMA approval, provided the receiving Bermudian bank has completed source-of-funds verification and satisfied AML/CFT record-keeping obligations. The BMA may require data on large or unusual cross-border flows for statistical and financial-crime monitoring, but this is not a profit-remittance restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Bermuda imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio and no requirement to deposit export receipts in a designated account. A non-resident-owned exempted limited partnership engaged in cross-border 3PL and overseas cross-dock fulfilment may hold cash in BMD, USD, GBP, EUR or other settlement currencies at its Bermudian account bank. The only documentary expectations are contemporaneous commercial contracts, export invoices, shipping/transport records and platform payout statements to evidence the underlying trade flows for AML purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969. The BMA acts as the central bank, monetary authority and sole regulator of banking, currency and clearing/investment institutions in Bermuda. HM Treasury retains ultimate sovereignty over the British Overseas Territory, but Bermuda has delegated statutory monetary supervision and exchange-control functions to the BMA.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for pure UK-registered electronic money institutions (EMIs). A UK EMI may lawfully serve the Bermuda-domiciled ELP as an upstream collection and payout-optimisation channel for UK/EU marketplace receipts, but it cannot hold or clear the Bermudian settlement account without a Bermuda deposit-taking licence or a licensed local agent relationship. Airwallex and comparable cross-border payment providers may therefore be used as secondary collection wallets and conversion engines, while the final clearing treasury account must remain at a BMA-licensed tier-1 Bermudian bank. This structure complies with Bermuda banking law and avoids unlicensed deposit-taking risk.

Route the UK parent capital contribution via SWIFT MT103, CHAPS or SEPA to a UK clearing bank and onward by wire to the Bermudian account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BD$0 — Bermuda prescribes no transfer-pricing documentation threshold because no corporate income tax exists; the Economic Substance Regulations require every resident ELP performing a relevant activity to file an annual ESR return irrespective of turnover. UK-consolidated groups with annual turnover above GBP 200 million and cross-border related-party transactions remain subject to HMRC transfer-pricing documentation requirements under TIOPA 2010 Part 4

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018 and Economic Substance Regulations 2018 apply. The ELP's cross-dock overseas-warehousing and e-commerce order-fulfillment activity constitutes a 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity. The ELP must satisfy all four substance elements: (i) direction and management exercised in Bermuda; (ii) adequate qualified employees on the Island; (iii) adequate physical premises including the warehouse; and (iv) core income-generating activities performed in Bermuda, reflected in adequate annual operating expenditure. A compliant ELP must file the ESR return annually; failure triggers penalties, mandatory exchange of information with HMRC, and potential strike-off

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Misleading ESR Statements

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Act 2018: fixed penalty BD$10,000 plus BD$2,000 per day; grave offences attract an additional penalty up to BD$250,000, automatic exchange of information with HMRC, and strike-off of the ELP

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of a corporate general partner who knowingly cause default are personally liable for penalties up to BD$10,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; civil restitution orders, UK director disqualification, and travel bans may apply
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Act sanctions: fine up to BD$250,000 for grave non-compliance, mandatory communication of information to HMRC, applicability of UK CFC and partnership anti-avoidance rules, and possible compulsory dissolution of the ELP

Director personal liability: Civil restitution and disgorgement of profits, joint and several liability for any UK tax attributable to UK-resident partners, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 2 years for directors and general partners
Anti-Money Laundering Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fine up to BD$50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years for the senior officer or MLRO; regulatory enforcement by the Bermuda Monetary Authority including licence revocation and public censure

Director personal liability: Direct criminal liability of the MLRO and compliance officers, confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, travel bans, and extended civil asset freeze
Failure to File / Late Filings

Statutory sanction: Annual Declaration late fee BD$500 per day; ESR late filing BD$10,000 fixed plus BD$2,000 per day; persistent default results in removal from the Bermuda register and forfeiture of tax protection

Director personal liability: General partner is jointly and severally liable for all registry penalties; directors of a corporate general partner face personal fines and disqualification from acting as Bermuda company directors
UK Tax Evasion / Partnership Transparency Abuse

Statutory sanction: UK penalties up to 100% of the tax charge plus daily penalties under TMA 1970; criminal prosecution for deliberate concealment; exchange of information between HMRC and Bermuda under SI 2019/817; UK CFC attribution under Part 9A CTA 2010

Director personal liability: HMRC civil fraud penalties, joint and several liability for UK tax, disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent evasion of UK tax
