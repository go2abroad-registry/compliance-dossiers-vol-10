Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda: Registrar of Companies (roc.gov.bm) for Exempted Partnership registration and the Bermuda Office of the Tax Commissioner (taxcommissioner.gov.bm) for the limited payroll-tax/land-tax perimeter. United Kingdom: HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC, gov.uk/hmrc) as the competent authority for UK VAT, customs duty (Customs Declaration Service - CDS) and any UK direct tax obligations of the Bermuda ELP where a UK permanent establishment or taxable UK supply nexus arises.

E-government filing portal

Bermuda layer: the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is created by filing the partnership agreement and a registration statement under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2015 through a Bermuda-resident sponsor; the Registrar issues an entity registration number and, upon application, a Certificate of Exemption confirming exemption from all Bermudian taxes (Bermuda levies no income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax or VAT; only the annual government fee and, if local employees exist, payroll tax apply). No Bermuda tax TIN is issued because no income tax return regime applies to the exempted ELP. UK layer: if the ELP makes taxable supplies in the UK, it must register for UK VAT through the HMRC digital registration service using Form VAT1 (VAT1GB for Great Britain; VAT1NI for Northern Ireland), submitting evidence of business activity, principal place of establishment and expected UK turnover; HMRC issues a 9-digit GB or NI VAT registration number. For customs declarations, the ELP must also obtain a GB EORI number (granted automatically on VAT registration or by standalone EORI application). HMRC may require the appointment of a UK VAT representative under VATA 1994 s.48 where the ELP is not UK-established. If the ELP operates a UK branch or permanent establishment (e.g. a UK 3PL warehouse), it must register for UK corporation tax on Form CT41G and register the branch at Companies House under the Overseas Companies Regulations 2009.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. The Bermuda ELP is tax-transparent and domiciled in a tax-neutral zone: Bermuda imposes no entity-level income, corporate, capital gains or withholding taxation, and the ELP holds statutory exemption via the Certificate of Exemption; the territorial scope is therefore offshore-exempt in Bermuda. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Bermuda ELP is tax-transparent and domiciled in a tax-neutral zone: Bermuda imposes no entity-level income, corporate, capital gains or withholding taxation, and the ELP holds statutory exemption via the Certificate of Exemption; the territorial scope is therefore offshore-exempt in Bermuda. For UK tax purposes the ELP is transparent on normal partnership principles (ITTOIA 2005 Part 9; CTA 2009 Part 17), so no UK entity-level corporation tax arises absent a UK permanent establishment; however, UK VAT and import VAT may apply to the e-commerce supply chain depending on the location of goods and the customer.

Available election forms

Neither Bermuda nor the UK operates a US-style check-the-box election. Bermuda: tax-exempt status is statutory and automatic; no fiscal election is required or available for the ELP. UK: partnership transparency is mandatory on the legal-form test and no transparency election exists. The ELP may, however, elect for voluntary UK VAT registration (VATA 1994 Sch.1) in order to recover input VAT, and may register for the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) or UK/EU OSS regimes for eligible distance sales of goods in consignments not exceeding GBP 150. Hybrid mismatch: UK TIOPA 2010 Part 6A applies automatically where a partner is taxed on a non-transparent basis; no elective carve-out is available, although the ELP can structure partner jurisdiction to avoid hybrid payee and imported-mismatch outcomes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the United States Dollar (USD) and enjoys full, unrestricted convertibility. Both current account and capital account transactions are freely convertible with no residual exchange control approval requirements. Profit remittances, capital repatriation, and dividend distributions by an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) are subject to no statutory currency hurdles; however, the BMA and regulated banks apply standard anti-money laundering (AML) scrutiny to cross-border wire transfers. The ELP's tax-transparent, non-resident ownership profile does not trigger additional convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation exists under Bermuda law. Bermuda has maintained an open exchange control system since decolonisation; there is no mandatory conversion timeline and no prescribed percentage of export receipts must be converted into BMD or USD. The e-commerce receipts of the ELP may be retained in any foreign currency, provided the associated Bermuda bank account is maintained in accordance with the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008, as amended.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) – the autonomous monetary authority and financial services regulator for the Bermuda jurisdiction; while Bermuda is a British Overseas Territory, the Bank of England exercises no direct monetary or regulatory control over Bermuda's banking system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium with material legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI services licensed in the UK or EU (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise, or specialised B2B e-money institutions) generally decline to onboard Bermuda-domiciled Exempted Limited Partnerships because such entities are not EEA/UK resident and present enhanced corporate transparency challenges. Bermuda-licensed fintechs and money service businesses under the BMA's digital asset or money service regulatory framework offer partial treasury capability, but they rarely provide full commercial banking services, dedicated IBAN/SWIFT settlement, or multi-currency accounts. A two-rail approach is recommended: a tier-1 Bermuda bank for core liquidity and a BMA-licensed EMI or payment service provider for card-acquiring and low-value e-commerce settlement.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity in the Exempted Limited Partnership structure

Defense evidence: A completed and notarised beneficial ownership register under the Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act 2020 and the BMA Beneficial Ownership (Limited Partnerships) Regulations 2022, together with legal opinions confirming the general partner and each limited partner's identity, source of wealth, and the absence of bearer-share or nominee complexities.
Non-resident general partner and non-resident limited partners lacking Bermuda nexus

Root cause: Non-resident general partner and non-resident limited partners lacking Bermuda nexus

Defense evidence: A comprehensive corporate residency and tax file: certified copies of passports, audited financial statements for the past two years, a board resolution authorising the account, and an up-to-date KYC questionnaire detailing the global business operations, the ultimate beneficial owner's country of residence, and the anticipated transaction flows through Bermuda.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions into the Bermuda ELP's ESR (Enhanced Status) capital escrow account must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the investor's bank using the BIC of the receiving Bermuda bank and the ELP's SWIFT address. The wire should state purpose code 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – PARTNERSHIP EQUITY' in the remittance field; for USD wires touching the US clearing system, the sender must include a valid Federal Reserve reference and comply with OFAC sanctions checks. Alternative protocols: (i) transfer via an internal omnibus account at HSBC or Butterfield from the investor's same-bank corporate account, (ii) for EU investors, SEPA/IBAN to a Bermuda bank's correspondent in London/Frankfurt, followed by a SWIFT onward transfer in USD or GBP, and (iii) for non-resident limited partners investing more than USD 5 million, use of a London or New York branch of the Bermuda bank to reduce correspondent friction. All inbound wires must be matched against the capital account ledger under the Limited Partnerships Act 1883 (as amended), and no cryptocurrency or personal account may be used.Trade payments for automotive parts and tuning accessories will clear through two principal rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for intercompany and direct supplier settlements in USD, EUR, GBP, and CNY, with transaction charges split by the sending bank's correspondent network and the beneficiary bank's standard fees; (ii) commercial Letters of Credit (section 7 UCP 600) for bulk orders from East Asian manufacturing hubs, opened through Butterfield's trade finance desk with co-ordinated shipping documentation. For recurring low-value disbursements to vetted distributors, the ELP may use HSBC's Global Collections and Payments platform via ISO 20022 cleartext, but the legacy MT103 remains the default rail to ensure full traceability. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% for constituent entities of in-scope MNE groups (Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025); 0% for all other exempted partnerships, which remain subject to no Bermuda income, capital gains, or gross receipts tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends, partnership distributions, or capital profits repatriated to non-resident partners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No standalone Bermuda TP documentation threshold for out-of-scope ELPs (BMD 0). In-scope MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750M (approximately BMD 835M at the 1:1 USD peg) in either of the two preceding fiscal years must maintain OECD-aligned Master File and Local File documentation under the CIT Act 2023 and the Tax Information Exchange (International Tax Compliance) Regulations, and must file related-party transaction schedules with the CIT return.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Requirements) Act 2018 and Regulations 2019 apply to all Bermuda legal entities, including ELPs. Online retail of automotive replacement parts (ISIC 4791) would constitute a 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity only if core income-generating activities are performed in Bermuda; a passive e-commerce ELP with no Bermuda premises, employees, or local management (managed from a foreign parent) is either a 'No Relevant Activity' entity or a 'Pure Equity Holding Entity' and must file the annual ESR declaration confirming exemption. The Tax Commissioner and the Registrar exchange filing data with EU and OECD jurisdictions, and failure to maintain substance when a relevant activity is declared is a serious offence attracting escalating penalties and possible strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent CIT Filing

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: fine up to BMD 10,000 and daily default surcharges of BMD 50 per day; on indictment: fine up to BMD 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years, plus civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged and interest at the statutory rate.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who consent to, connive in, or negligently permit the offence are jointly and severally liable for the civil penalty, subject to personal fines up to BMD 10,000, disqualification from acting as a director in Bermuda, and imprisonment up to 5 years. Restraint orders can include asset freezes and travel restrictions.
Economic Substance Non-Compliance / Shell Company Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of BMD 500 plus BMD 100 per week; failure of the economic substance test: first contravention BMD 10,000, second within 3 years BMD 30,000, third or subsequent BMD 70,000; the Registrar may strike off the ELP and notify the UK/EU competent authorities under the exchange-of-information framework, leading to the entity being treated as a tax resident of the foreign jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be personally fined up to BMD 10,000 for knowingly causing the ELP to make a false ESR declaration; repeated defaults trigger disqualification and a potential Court order for restitution of Government costs; travel bans may be imposed pending investigation under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997.
AML/CFT Breach (Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 and Regulations 2008)

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: fine up to BMD 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; for corporate bodies, a fine of up to BMD 250,000; the court may also order forfeiture of the funds received through the payment rails and cancellation of the ELP's registration.

Director personal liability: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, a director of the general partner who consents to or connives in the offence is personally liable to the same penalties as the primary offender; senior management may face disqualification orders and travel bans pending the conclusion of criminal proceedings.
Cross-Border Permanent Establishment / Diverted Profits Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The UK HMRC may recharacterise the Bermuda ELP as a UK permanent establishment, assess UK corporation tax on the attributable profits, apply the Diverted Profits Tax at 25% (from April 2025, 31% as announced), and impose penalties of up to 100% of the unpaid tax plus interest; similar recharacterisation risks arise under EU ATAD and DAC6 disclosure rules.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner who knowingly enable the artificial arrangement may be personally liable under the UK Senior Accounting Officer regime (penalties up to GBP 5,000 per period), and under the UK Criminal Finances Act 2017 the corporate general partner commits a strict-liability criminal offence of failing to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion, exposing directors to unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 2 years.
