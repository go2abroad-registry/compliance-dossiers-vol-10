Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC), Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda (portal: www.tco.gov.bm) for Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CITA) administration; Registrar of Companies (www.roc.gov.bm) for corporate registration, Economic Substance Declarations, and license-adjacent filings; Bermuda Revenue Authority for employer Payroll Tax registration (www.bra.bm). The Company Registration Number is the primary statutory identifier absent a general TIN.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Bermuda exempted company via the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 1981 to obtain the Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum of Association, and Company Registration Number. Step 2: Asses CITA scope: if the entity belongs to an in-scope MNE group (consolidated revenue equal to or exceeding EUR 750 million in at least two of the four fiscal years immediately preceding the tested year), register for Bermuda Corporate Income Tax with the OTC using the prescribed CIT registration form and obtain a CIT registration number. Step 3: Register as an employer for Bermuda Payroll Tax (if Bermuda-resident staff are engaged) via the Bermuda Revenue Authority. Step 4: File the annual Economic Substance Declaration with the Registrar of Companies, irrespective of CITA scope. No general income tax identification number or VAT number is issued in Bermuda.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: Bermuda imposes no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, or tax on distributions, and the exempted company's income (whether Bermuda-sourced or foreign-sourced) is outside the Bermuda tax net unless the entity falls within the new CITA 2023 15% corporate income tax applicable only to in-scope MNE groups (revenue threshold EUR 750m). The entity is not a pass-through vehicle; it is a separate legal person and, solely for CITA in-scope groups, a separate taxable person. Crowdfunding portal services fall within the 'investment entity' definition under the GloBE Model Rules for CITA purposes.

Available election forms

Bermuda has no check-the-box or US-style entity classification election. Under CITA 2023, available elections mirroring the OECD GloBE Model Rules include: (i) election to treat the Bermuda domestic top-up tax as a Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (QDMTT); (ii) election to apply the taxable distribution method for investment entities (GloBE Article 7.4); (iii) election to exclude an entity from the GloBE IIR on a year-by-year basis for ownership interests in other group entities; (iv) deemed tangible asset basis election; (v) transitional substance carve-out election; and (vi) allocation elections for the IIR/UTPR across constituent entities. Elections are filed electronically with the Tax Commissioner in the form and manner prescribed under the CITA regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales tax regime exists in Bermuda; customs duty (import tariff) is the only indirect tax, levied under the Customs Act 1946 and Customs Tariff Act 1972. Digital services supplied by an equity crowdfunding portal, including platform fees, escrow facilitation services, and intermediation fees, are outside the scope of any Bermuda indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: Bermuda levies no VAT/GST and prescribes no indirect tax registration threshold. Importers must appoint a licensed customs broker and obtain a Becon (Bermuda Customs) importer code; customs duty on physical imports is assessed ad valorem at rates generally ranging from 0% to 33.25% depending on tariff classification, with specific rates for certain goods. No registration threshold applies to the digital crowdfunding services themselves.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable: Bermuda has no VAT/GST mechanism, and therefore no 0% zero-rating regime and no input VAT refund mechanism exists for goods or offshore services. Services rendered by the exempted company crowdfunding portal to non-Bermudian investors and issuers are simply outside the scope of Bermuda indirect taxation. If CITA 2023 applies to the group, export or offshore service income is not preferentially exempted; corporate income tax is computed on the group's GloBE book profit rather than on territorial sourcing, so no export zero-rating election is available or required.

All import and export declarations are cleared through the ASYCUDA World customs administration platform, and underlying invoices must be retained as evidentiary documentation for customs audits and Economic Substance substantiation; however, no tax authority digital clearance or fiscal stamp is affixed to supplier invoices.

Bermuda has no VAT zero-rating or clawback regime; penalties relevant to this entity include: (i) under CITA 2023, failure to register, file, or pay the top-up tax attracts default interest (benchmark rate plus 5%) and civil penalties up to USD 10,000, with wilful evasion carrying imprisonment; (ii) under the Customs Act 1946, false or misleading export declarations may trigger forfeiture of goods and penalties up to treble the value of the goods; (iii) under the Economic Substance (Companies) Act 2018, non-compliance with substance filings attracts fines up to USD 10,000 and 'default' designation with potential corporate restoration consequences; (iv) under the Investment Business Act 2003 and the Digital Assets Business Act 2018, misrepresentation in licensed crowdfunding activity exposes the entity to the Bermuda Monetary Authority's disciplinary action, including suspension or revocation of the license. Retrospective audit review spans six years for CITA purposes and carries full interest and penalty restoration for under-reported tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is pegged at par to the United States dollar (USD), and USD is universally accepted at par and freely usable for payments; tier-1 banks maintain dual- and multi-currency accounts. Current-account transactions, including subscription flows, trade payments, fees, royalties and dividends, are fully convertible without BMA pre-approval, subject to the statutory certificate of solvency for dividend distributions. Capital-account convertibility remains legally reserved to the BMA, but broad general permissions for non-resident-designated exempted companies render equity injections, loan repayments and capital repatriation documentary rather than discretionary. Remittance hurdles are therefore limited to completeness of the filing package: the remitting bank must state the appropriate purpose/payment code, and the Bermuda authorised dealer must map the MT103 reference to an allotment resolution or solvency certificate. No withholding or transfer tax is imposed on profit remittances by a Bermuda exempted company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, mandatory conversion ratio or conversion timeline applies in Bermuda. Because BMD is pegged 1:1 to USD, foreign-currency export receipts are local-currency-equivalent and may be retained or swept to global concentration accounts at the entity's discretion. The residual statutory power of the BMA to direct surrender of foreign currency is limited to scheduled-territory residents and is not applied to non-resident-designated exempted companies. Aggregate external-trade statistical reporting (Form E) may be required for balance-of-payments monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), exercising central-banking powers of currency issuance, reserves management and exchange-control administration under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969 and the Exchange Control Act 1972.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with conditions. FCA-authorised and EEA-authorised electronic money institutions generally accept a Bermuda exempted company when the beneficial-ownership register, economic substance and source of funds are fully transparent and the portal operates segregated-client-money controls. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs classify equity-crowdfunding portals as high-risk and require mandatory transaction-monitoring integration; (ii) the Bermuda entity cannot rely on the EMI alone for investor payouts unless a safeguarding arrangement is recognised by the relevant regulator; (iii) EU/UK payment corridors require beneficiary-name screening and purpose-code mapping; (iv) acceptance of cryptocurrency subscriptions may trigger heightened EBA/ESMA due diligence or outright denial. The recommended architecture is a tier-1 Bermuda bank for escrow plus one UK EMI for operational spread and small-value clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity subscriptions are routed by SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire for US-origin wires) to the escrow account at a Bermuda authorised dealer, with the payment purpose coded as 'equity capital subscription under Companies Act 1981'. The Bermuda registered agent files the prescribed exchange-control capital notification (share-allotment return) with the BMA; the authorised dealer verifies originator regulatory standing and matches the MT103 field-70 reference against the board allotment resolution before releasing credit. Subscriptions are quarantined in escrow pending milestone verification, and release requires joint approval of the portal compliance officer and the independent escrow agent. Turnover: Under the Companies Act 1981, every Bermuda exempted company must appoint an auditor at its annual general meeting. There is no general exemption based on size for Bermuda exempted companies incorporated in the International Business sector; audit exemption is available only for a wholly owned subsidiary of a company incorporated in Bermuda if the ultimate parent provides a guarantee and the shareholders unanimously consent, and for certain specified private companies meeting solvency and shareholder consent conditions. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

A 15% Corporate Income Tax applies under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CITA) only to Bermuda business units that are members of MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least EUR 750 million, effective for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.
