Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Ministry of Finance – Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC), 1 Finance Department, Hamilton; corporate registration via the Registrar of Companies, Government of Bermuda; official portal: www.gov.bm and www.taxcommissioner.gov.bm

E-government filing portal

A foreign parent/shareholder incorporates the Bermuda Exempted Company under the Companies Act 1981 with the Registrar of Companies and obtains a Certificate of Incorporation bearing the Bermuda Company Number. The company then files an application with the Registrar for a Tax Exemption Certificate (TEC) under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, which guarantees no Bermuda taxes on profits, income, distributions or capital gains until 31 March 2035. If the exempted company employs staff in Bermuda, it must separately register with the OTC as an employer to obtain a Payroll Tax Account Number (employer reference), file quarterly payroll tax returns, and withhold employee payroll tax. Registration for Bermuda customs duty is required only if physically importing goods; the Customs Department issues a Customs Broker/Importer Code via the ASYCUDA World electronic single window. No further VAT or income-tax registration is required because Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST and no general corporate income tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. The operative statutory election is the application for a Tax Exemption Certificate (TEC) under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (guaranteed exemption until 31 March 2035), made by the Exempted Company through its registered agent. A separate election may be made under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 (CIT Act) to register with the OTC as a domestic member of a large multinational group (consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more) for the 15% Bermuda corporate income tax, applicable to fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025; this is a mandatory filing obligation, not a discretionary election, for qualifying in-scope groups. For payroll, the employer elects between quarterly or annual filing periods and may elect to apply the BMD 1,000,000 annual remuneration exemption when computing payroll tax.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the United States dollar (USD); the BMA maintains full convertibility for current account transactions. A Bermuda Exempted Company is ordinarily designated as a non-resident entity for exchange control purposes, permitting unrestricted retention and remittance of foreign currency including USD operating accounts. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and repayment of share capital are freely permitted without BMA approval for non-resident designated entities, provided the underlying funds are not derived from locally resident prohibited activities and the entity remains compliant with BMA anti-money laundering (AML/CFT) obligations. Capital account convertibility is uniform in practice; inward and outward FDI-related flows are processed by authorised banks with standard purpose declaration, while only transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions or unlicensed financial activity attract restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda does not impose a mandatory percentage surrender rule or statutory conversion deadline for export proceeds on Bermuda Exempted Companies holding non-resident designation. Export receipts in USD or other convertible currencies may be retained in foreign currency accounts, credited directly to the operational account, without obligation to sell to an authorised dealer. For completeness, resident persons and entities not exempted remain subject to Exchange Control Act 1972 requirements to offer foreign currency to an authorised bank within a transitional period unless a general or specific permission exempts them; the Exempted Company's annual tax/exchange control filing will evidence its non-resident classification and avoid such surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) acting as the Central Bank and exchange control administrator; HM Treasury retains sovereign jurisdiction over Bermuda as a British Overseas Territory but delegates financial regulation to the BMA under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969 and the Exchange Control Act 1972.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with strict safeguards and scope limitation. UK Financial Conduct Authority-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise can open cross-border multi-currency accounts for a Bermuda Exempted Company because no Bermuda banking licence is required when the EMI does not take deposits in Bermuda or provide local BMD settlement. The EMI route is viable for small-value cross-border contractor payroll, fee concentration, and low-latency disbursements in GBP/EUR/USD, but it is not a substitute for a licensed BMA bank for capital escrow, trade financing, or corporate deposits. Legal constraints include prohibition on holding BMD accounts outside the authorised banking framework, inability to access Bermuda's local payroll direct debit rail from a non-BMA entity, and the need to evidence the underlying customer contracts to counter BMA AML/CFT expectations. The entity should maintain at least one BMA-licensed transactional account to receive equity injections and provide the required banking counterparty for substance and corporate registers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan funding must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's bank to the Exempted Company's BMA-licensed USD account, using the beneficiary bank's correct Swift/BIC plus ABA routing or Fedwire details where applicable. The remittance file should contain the Beneficiary Legal Name exactly matching the bank account title, the corporate registration number, and a transparent payment purpose description. Recommended ISO 20022 purpose codes are 'COST' for corporate equity contribution, 'LOAN' or 'CFDI' for shareholder advances, and 'CLIR' for treasury clearing. Bermuda exchange control verification is performed by the receiving authorised bank; the entity must supply a Board resolution authorising the capital receipt, UBO declaration, copy of the register of members, and, where the transfer exceeds USD 100,000, a BMA-compliant currency declaration or statistical return triggered by the bank's reporting thresholds. Funds should first land in the Capital Escrow Account if held in escrow pending conditions, then be transferred to the Operational Account after compliance sign-off.Outbound contractor payroll and service supplier payments are cleared through the BMA-licensed bank's SWIFT MT103 correspondent network for USD, GBP, and EUR, with settlement via Fedwire, CHAPS, SEPA Instant, and Target2 as applicable. For local Bermuda payroll, the entity must use the local payroll direct debit rail via the BMA-licensed bank, using the Bankers Automated Clearing Services (BACS-style) entry or the bank's proprietary payroll module. For scale-up cross-border payroll in lower-value jurisdictions, Wise Business settlements via SWIFT / local fast rails are permissible on a segregated 'hot wallet' / e-money ledger basis, provided the underlying funds originate from the operational account and are not used for deposit taking. Commercial letters of credit are not a core clearing requirement for the EOR / staffing model but can be supported for any future back-office outsourcing or procurement of equipment; the bank will issue an irrevocable LC at the entity's request subject to a credit line. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% general corporate income tax; 15% domestic top-up tax applies exclusively to multinational enterprise (MNE) groups with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more in at least two of the preceding four fiscal years under the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by a Bermuda Exempted Company to any resident or non-resident shareholder, and no Bermuda capital duty applies on dividend distributions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the annual general meeting; if no AGM is held, within six months after the anniversary of incorporation. The exempted company annual government fee (based on authorized share capital) is separately due on 31 January of each calendar year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within six months after the end of the relevant financial year (e.g., 30 June for a 31 December fiscal year-end).

Deadline: · Frequency:

The CIT return must be filed within 15 months after the end of the fiscal year of each constituent entity. For a 31 December 2025 fiscal year-end, the first return is due by 31 March 2027. Quarterly estimated payments commence in subsequent fiscal years once the top-up tax liability is determinable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 0 statutory threshold — Bermuda domestic law imposes no general transfer pricing documentation requirement and no local file or master file filing obligation for Bermuda Exempted Companies. Mandatory documentation is triggered by (i) Economic Substance Regulations applicability where the EOR entity performs a 'Distribution and Service Centre' or 'Headquarters' relevant activity, and (ii) CIT Act 2023 registration for in-scope MNE groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more. In practice, the BTA expects contemporaneous Local File, Master File, and CbC Report evidence for any intra-group EOR payroll, management-fee, or royalty flow exceeding BMD 1,000,000 in annual transaction value once the group falls within Pillar Two scope.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2018 (as amended) and the International Business Companies (Amendment) Act require a Bermuda exempted company undertaking a relevant activity to maintain substantial economic presence proportionate to its income. For the Global EOR Platform, the principal relevant activities are 'headquarters', 'distribution and service centre', and, where the proprietary payroll software or employer-of-record IP is held, 'intellectual property business'. The entity must demonstrate that it is directed and managed in Bermuda (board meetings and strategic decisions occurring in Bermuda), maintains adequate physical premises, employs full-time staff at an appropriate seniority level, and performs core income-generating activities locally. Outsourcing to offshore affiliates is permissible only where the rationale is commercial necessity and the Bermuda board retains monitoring, control, and full access to outsourced records. The annual ESD filing is mandatory; failure to satisfy the substantive test exposes the entity to penalties up to BMD 250,000, automatic exchange of information with the ultimate parent's jurisdiction, and potential strike-off by the Registrar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Wilful Misstatement of Pillar Two Top-Up Tax

Statutory sanction: Penalty of BMD 25,000 per erroneous item; daily surcharge BMD 1,000 per day of continued failure; on conviction, fines up to BMD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly participate in the misstatement attract criminal liability, civil recovery orders, and confiscation of the tax benefit. Bermuda courts may impose travel bans pending trial and disqualification from acting as a director of any Bermuda company for up to 10 years.
Shell Company — Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance penalties escalate from BMD 10,000 to BMD 250,000, plus BMD 1,000 per month for late filing; referral to the OECD and foreign tax authorities for automatic exchange of tax information; the Registrar may strike off the entity.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced on proof of personal direction or participation: directors face civil restitution to the Registrar, personal fines up to BMD 10,000, travel bans of 30 days or more pending inquiry, and imprisonment up to 5 years for knowingly making a false declaration.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Violation

Statutory sanction: Corporate fine up to BMD 100,000; individual fine up to BMD 25,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years; regulatory suspension of the EOR license, freezing orders over bank and Wise payment rails, and potential civil forfeiture of the processed funds.

Director personal liability: Directors face disqualification, confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, travel bans during investigation, and personal civil suits for restitution to defrauded contractors and tax authorities.
Money Laundering Through Fraudulent Payroll Conduit

Statutory sanction: Money laundering conviction carries imprisonment up to 14 years, ancillary forfeiture orders over assets derived from the payroll flows, and disqualification from holding an employment agency license in Bermuda.

Director personal liability: Directors who knew or suspected that payroll funds represented criminal proceeds incur individual criminal liability; the court may issue interim receiving orders, worldwide asset freezes, travel restrictions, and confiscation on the English model.
Unlicensed Employment Agency and Employment Act 2001 Violation

Statutory sanction: Licensing fines up to BMD 10,000 per violation plus BMD 250 per day of continuing operation; the licensing authority may prohibit the entity from using local Payroll Direct Debit rails and compel immediate cessation of on-island operations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate without a license are personally liable for restitution of all fees collected while unlicensed; persistent breach leads to travel bans, director-ban orders, and imprisonment up to 2 years where employee statutory wages were unpaid.
