Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Ministry of Finance: Office of the Tax Commissioner (payroll tax, stamp duty, customs duty) and the Registry General: Registrar of Companies (incorporation, economic substance, beneficial ownership). Primary portal: www.gov.bm; corporate filings portal: www.registry.gov.bm. No income-tax or VAT authority exists because Bermuda levies no such taxes.

E-government filing portal

Bermuda does not issue a general corporate income-tax TIN or VAT ID. A Bermuda Exempted Company obtains its statutory identity by: (i) incorporating through a licensed Bermuda law firm or corporate services provider under the Companies Act 1981; (ii) receiving the Certificate of Incorporation, which bears the unique Company Registration Number used as the operative TIN for CRS/AEOI, banking and contractual purposes; (iii) filing the Declaration of Status and completing the Beneficial Ownership Register filings with the Registrar of Companies; and (iv) registering for an Employer Payroll Tax Account with the Office of the Tax Commissioner only if the company has Bermuda-resident employees. If the entity is in scope of the Economic Substance Act 2018, an annual Economic Substance Return is filed via the Registry General portal. No other tax registration is required for a purely offshore CAD/BIM service exporter.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda has no US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch election because it imposes no corporate income tax. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) the non-resident entity election under the Economic Substance Act 2018, whereby a company that is tax resident in a qualifying foreign jurisdiction can be treated as non-resident in Bermuda, replacing the Bermuda economic substance filing with a supported foreign tax residence representation; (ii) an application for a Certificate of Tax Residence or tax exemption confirmation under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, which protects the company from future Bermuda taxes on income, profits, dividends and capital gains until 2035 (or 2057 for grandfathered undertakings); and (iii) CRS/FATCA self-classification using the Bermuda Company Registration Number as the tax identification number.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No comprehensive VAT, GST or sales-and-use tax. Bermuda imposes customs import duties under the Customs Tariff Act, employer payroll tax under the Payroll Tax Act 1995, and stamp duties on specified instruments and land transactions. Cross-border services supplies are outside any Bermuda indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: there is no VAT/GST registration threshold for services. Customs registration is triggered only for physical imports into Bermuda. Payroll tax registration is compulsory for any employer of Bermuda-resident staff with no de minimis turnover threshold; the employer payroll tax rate ranges from 0% to 11.7% of total remuneration depending on payroll size, levied in Bermudian dollars (BMD, pegged 1:1 to USD). For the ISIC 7110 BIM/CAD export model with no Bermuda-resident employees and no physical imports, no indirect-tax or payroll registration is triggered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable: Bermuda has no VAT or sales tax, so there is no 0% export VAT rate, no export VAT refund mechanism and no zero-rating claim procedure. Cross-border CAD/BIM services supplied by a Bermuda Exempted Company are outside the Bermuda tax net ab initio: no corporate income tax, no profit or remittance tax, and no withholding tax on royalties, interest or service fees. To preserve the exempt-zone posture and support international invoicing, the company should maintain a current Certificate of Incorporation, file any applicable Economic Substance Return (demonstrating that it is not a passive shell), and obtain, where needed, a Certificate of Tax Residence from the Bermuda Ministry of Finance. The 180-day FX repatriation benchmark in this dossier is an internal treasury policy only, because Bermuda imposes no exchange controls and no statutory profit repatriation deadline.

Where the Bermuda company invoices EU or UK clients, it must separately satisfy the counterparty jurisdiction's e-invoicing or Making Tax Digital obligations, but no Bermuda fiscal clearance applies.

No VAT clawback or export zero-rating fraud penalties can apply because Bermuda has no VAT. The relevant compliance risks for the exempted company are: (i) Economic Substance Act 2018: failure to file or materially false filing of the Economic Substance Return attracts civil penalties up to BMD 10,000 plus BMD 1,000 per day of continued default and, on conviction, imprisonment up to 2 years; (ii) Companies Act 1981 and Beneficial Ownership Act: false incorporation declarations or deficient beneficial-ownership records carry penalties up to BMD 10,000 and possible strike-off with loss of limited liability; (iii) CRS/FATCA self-certification misstatements can attract penalties up to BMD 10,000 per instance and enhanced AML reviews; (iv) payroll tax misdeclarations, if Bermuda-resident employees are later discovered, carry interest of 10-20%, penalties up to 100% of the tax due, and potential director liability. Any clawback is therefore civil-administrative in nature rather than a VAT refund recapture.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates a liberalised exchange control regime under the Exchange Control Act 1972. The Bermudian dollar is pegged at par to the US dollar. For Bermuda exempted companies, the BMA grants blanket or transaction-specific exchange control permissions enabling free convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. Profit remittances, dividend distributions and liquidation proceeds can be remitted to non-resident shareholders without material restriction, subject only to compliance with corporate law formalities and standard know-your-customer obligations. Practical friction may arise in the form of documentary burdens and bank-level compliance reviews rather than statutory convertibility hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no repatriation timelines for export proceeds. A Bermudian exempted company carrying on international BIM and CAD services may retain all export receipts in any foreign currency indefinitely. The only relevant obligations are exchange control reporting obligations to the BMA under the Exchange Control Act, which are typically satisfied through banking channels and do not require conversion into Bermudian dollars.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) — integrated financial regulator, central monetary authority and sole issuer of the Bermudian dollar

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer and Mercury can be used for low-value ancillary disbursements and to receive certain overseas client payments. However, they are not a substitute for a licensed Bermuda bank account for capital escrow, trade settlement, or regulatory evidentiary purposes. Legal constraints include the absence of a Bermuda EMI licence for such platforms, the lack of Bermudian dollar clearing, reliance on foreign e-money licences, the lack of deposit insurance, and potential restrictions on holding funds for corporate treasury purposes. For a Bermuda exempted company, the primary cross-border clearing rail should be a domestic commercial bank account with a US correspondent banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward capital injections to a Bermuda exempted company should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's bank to the company's Bermuda commercial bank account. The remittance instruction must include the company's full name, account number, and a purpose code clearly identifying the transaction as either 'Equity Injection' or 'Shareholder Subordinated Loan'. For US-dollar funding through the Bermudian bank's US correspondent, settlement occurs via CHIPS or Fedwire; for UK or European funding, the MT103 may be cleared through the local correspondent network in US dollars or converted into Bermudian dollars at the bank's prevailing FX desk. Pre-advice and a copy of the board resolution or share allotment notice may be requested for amounts above USD 100,000 to satisfy Bermuda's exchange control reporting and AML verification obligations.For operational outflows related to civil infrastructure BIM outsourcing, the primary clearing rail is the SWIFT MT103 wire transfer for invoices under commercial thresholds, with immediate funds settlement via the bank's international correspondent network. Larger project milestone payments may be effected through a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued by the Bermuda bank, advised through a confirming bank in the beneficiary's jurisdiction, particularly for procurement of construction software or cloud services. For recurring subscription disbursements (e.g., CAD software licences, cloud hosting) a standing order via SWIFT or an open-banking API-enabled virtual account is permissible. All outbound trade disbursements require the recipient's bank details, invoice substantiation, and a valid purpose code; transactions exceeding USD 50,000 may trigger enhanced due diligence and a written description of the commercial services.Currency forwards up to 12 months with BMA-licensed or permitted banks, hedging Bermudian-dollar/US-dollar export billingsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) through international dealers where physical settlement is unnecessary or prohibited by currency delivery constraintsCross-currency swaps with BMA-regulated domestic banks or international counterparties under an ISDA Master Agreement and approved by the treasury risk committee

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for all Bermuda Exempted Companies under the territorial exemption; a 15% corporate income tax applies exclusively to in-scope multinational enterprise groups (consolidated revenue of €750 million or more in at least two of the four preceding fiscal years) under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, including the 15% Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax for Bermuda-resident constituent entities.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends, distributions or liquidation surpluses paid by an Exempted Company to its UK or any foreign shareholder.

Late annual fee: penalty of 10% per month or part-month (capped at 50% of the annual fee); persistent non-filing leads to strike-off, dissolution and restoration costs.
Part 1 — Group and constituent entity identification Ultimate parent entity (UPE) jurisdiction, GloBE identifier, constituent entity registration, and confirmation of the €750 million revenue threshold. For a UK-parented group, identify the United Kingdom as the UPE jurisdiction and confirm whether the UK will file the GloBE Information Return. Failure to file: fixed penalty plus monthly accrual of 5% of unpaid tax (capped at 25%); late payment interest at the statutory rate; inaccurate returns attract up to 150% of understated tax; criminal liability for deliberate evasion.

Align the disclosure with the UK group's TP policy; a cost-plus or TNMM method is usual for BIM/CAD outsourcing services. Failure to file: fixed penalty plus monthly accrual of 5% of unpaid tax (capped at 25%); late payment interest at the statutory rate; inaccurate returns attract up to 150% of understated tax; criminal liability for deliberate evasion.

Required only if the exempted company employs staff in Bermuda; remote UK employees are not subject to Bermuda payroll tax. Late filing and payment: 10% penalty on the unpaid tax plus statutory interest; continued default triggers enforcement, director personal liability and disqualification.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No mandatory Bermuda Local File or Master File applies to a standalone exempted company outside OECD Pillar Two scope. Mandatory OECD-conformant transfer pricing documentation and CIT disclosure attach only where the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds €750 million (approximately BMD 750 million) in at least two of the four preceding fiscal years, triggering the CIT Act 2023 TP rules.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance (Requirements and Conformity) Act 2018 requires every Bermuda tax-resident entity carrying on a relevant activity to maintain substantial economic presence in Bermuda: direction and management in Bermuda, adequate full-time employees, adequate operating expenditure, and physical premises. Cross-border BIM/CAD engineering outsourcing for UK/EU clients normally constitutes a 'service business' (relevant activity) when performed for related parties; pure equity holding entities benefit from reduced substance requirements. Entities tax resident outside Bermuda may claim exclusion only with foreign tax residency evidence. Non-compliance exposes the entity to penalties up to BMD 250,000 per relevant activity year, mandatory exchange of information with HMRC, and loss of tax-exempt standing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic substance failure (Shell Company profile)

Statutory sanction: Under the ESR Act: penalty up to BMD 250,000 per relevant activity year plus monthly default penalties; mandatory automatic exchange of information with UK HMRC; denial of the foreign-resident exclusion; eventual strike-off and dissolution of the exempted company by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who approve false ESR declarations or cause the company to trade without substance are jointly and severally liable for the penalty debt; may be disqualified as directors; subject to injunctions and asset-freeze orders; in aggravated cases, criminal prosecution for fraud on the Register.
Tax evasion under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (in-scope groups)

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 150% of the understated tax, fixed penalties for failure to file or keep records (up to BMD 50,000–250,000), monthly 5% surcharge on unpaid tax (capped at 25%), statutory interest, and criminal fines on conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and controlling minds are personally exposed to civil recovery, director disqualification orders, travel restrictions and imprisonment for deliberate evasion, and may be pursued by the BRA for the unpaid penalty without the benefit of corporate indemnity.
Abuse of Exempted Undertaking Tax Protection certificate

Statutory sanction: Revocation or variation of the tax protection certificate; imposition of Bermudian taxes on the unprotected business retroactively; financial penalties and costs; administrative strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false declarations in support of the certificate face personal penalty assessments, restitution orders for taxes avoided, and potential criminal liability for furnishing false information to a public authority.
Anti-money laundering and terrorist-financing breaches

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997: fines and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; regulatory sanctions by the Bermuda Monetary Authority and the Financial Intelligence Agency; confiscation and asset forfeiture orders; closure of the Bermuda bank accounts and correspondent banking loss.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers and beneficial owners bear personal criminal liability, are subject to worldwide travel bans, asset confiscation and extradition exposure, and may be barred from acting as directors of any Bermudian company.
Persistent filing default and trading while struck off

Statutory sanction: Automatic late-filing penalties (10% per month on annual fees; BMD 500 and monthly surcharges on ESR filings); strike-off and dissolution; restoration only by court order with penal costs; the struck-off company's contracts and assets vest in the Registrar as bona vacantia.

Director personal liability: Directors who trade through a struck-off company are jointly and severally liable for all debts incurred after dissolution, may be disqualified by the court, fined, and in egregious cases prosecuted for fraudulent trading under the Companies Act 1981 and the Criminal Code.
