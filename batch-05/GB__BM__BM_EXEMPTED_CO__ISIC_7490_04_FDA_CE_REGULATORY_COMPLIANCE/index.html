Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC) – operates via the Bermuda Tax Administration Portal; corporate registry oversight by the Registrar of Companies under the Bermuda Companies Act 1981

E-government filing portal

Foreign-owned Bermuda exempted companies are not automatically issued a domestic tax identification number for income tax purposes because Bermuda levies no income tax. The entity must, however, obtain a Bermuda Business Number (BBN) through the Registrar of Companies (ROC) online portal (www.roc.gov.bm) as part of incorporation and annual compliance. For customs and trade purposes, the Bermuda Customs Department assigns a Customs/Trade Number upon registration. An exempted company that applies for a tax exemption certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 receives a certificate referencing the Minister of Finance’s guarantee of no future direct taxes, but this is not a TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda exempted companies may elect to obtain a tax exemption certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, guaranteeing an exemption from all taxes (including income, profits, capital gains, and dividends) until 31 March 2035, with extensions to 2050 available for qualifying undertakings. Additionally, under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CIT), exempted companies that are part of large multinational groups must register for Bermuda CIT (a 15% tax on adjusted profits, effective for fiscal years starting on/after 1 January 2025); an election to apply the UK Pillar Two qualified domestic minimum top-up tax (QDMTT) safe harbour may be made. No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are available in the traditional US IRS sense; Bermuda tax law does not offer pass-through classification for exempted companies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar is freely convertible into United States Dollars at a fixed 1:1 peg. Bermuda operates a selective exchange control regime: current account transactions for resident persons are generally unrestricted, while capital account transactions require exchange control permission unless an exemption or non-resident designation is granted. A Bermuda Exempted Company designated as non-resident is treated as outside the exchange control perimeter for most purposes and may hold, borrow, and remit foreign currency without prior BMA approval. Profit remittance and dividend payments to non-resident shareholders are permitted with no withholding taxes in Bermuda; the only practical hurdles are documentary and AML/KYC related, specifically the need to evidence source of funds and maintain records with an authorised dealer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda-resident persons receiving foreign currency export proceeds must offer 100% of those proceeds for sale to an authorised dealer within three months of receipt, unless an exemption is granted by the Bermuda Monetary Authority. An Exempted Company designated as non-resident is exempt from the surrender obligation and may retain export proceeds in any currency. Since the underlying scope is ISIC 7490 professional consultancy services for FDA 510(k) and CE marking regulatory compliance, no physical merchandise is exported and the statutory export surrender rules do not operate in practice; only the general record-keeping duties of the authorised dealer bank apply.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the central bank and the exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. An offshore digital EMI that is not licensed by the BMA cannot accept deposits or provide payment accounts to persons with a Bermuda nexus. However, a Bermuda Exempted Company designated as non-resident may, subject to BMA and bank consultation, maintain payment functionality through a UK- or EU-regulated EMI if the EMI does not solicit Bermuda-domiciled clients and no Bermuda resident signatory or control nexus exists. The safer legal route is to use a BMA-authorised bank for primary escrow and operational liquidity, with an EMI used only as a supplementary collection rail for non-Bermuda client payments.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (nil) for a Bermuda Exempted Company outside the scope of the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023; a 15% corporate income tax applies only to in-scope multinational enterprise (MNE) groups whose consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750 million, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends, whether paid to UK residents, other non-residents, or sister companies; no advance corporation tax, no dividend distribution tax, and no branch remittance tax applies to a Bermuda Exempted Company.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For in-scope MNE groups only: within 15 months after the fiscal year-end (first filing window opens for fiscal years beginning 1 January 2025); provisional domestic top-up tax instalments are due at fiscal year-end with final top-up payable within 4 months after the year-end. A Bermuda Exempted Company below the EUR 750 million group-revenue threshold has no CIT filing obligation and must instead retain a signed declaration of non-in-scope status at its registered office.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly return due on or before the 15th day of the month following the payroll month; annual reconciliation due by 31 January following the tax year-end (31 December).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 810,000,000 (approximate equivalent of EUR 750 million) consolidated annual group revenue — only MNE groups above this threshold are required to prepare and file OECD Pillar Two-aligned transfer pricing documentation, including a Master File and a Local File, under the CIT Act 2023. A standalone Bermuda Exempted Company engaged in FDA/CE regulatory consulting is below the threshold and has no statutory obligation to prepare Local/Master Files, but all transactions with the UK parent, affiliates, or foreign clients must nonetheless be priced on an arm's length basis and evidenced by contemporaneous documentation (e.g., fee schedules, hourly rate benchmarks, margin studies).

Economic substance scope: Applicable — Bermuda Economic Substance (Requirements) Regulations 2018, as amended, and the Economic Substance Guidance apply to all exempted companies. The FDA/CE regulatory consulting activity is assessed as a 'service centre business' (or 'headquarters' business where intra-group services are provided). The company must satisfy the adequate substance test by demonstrating: (i) its core income-generating activities are directed and managed in Bermuda; (ii) it maintains an adequate physical presence and premises in Bermuda; (iii) it employs an adequate number of full-time employees in Bermuda; and (iv) it incurs adequate annual expenditure in Bermuda, in each case proportionate to the level of consulting income. Annual ESR filing is mandatory even where no relevant activity is declared, and the economic substance doctrine is independently enforced by the Registrar alongside the separate CIT Act 2023 requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Returns

Statutory sanction: Under the Payroll Tax Act 1995 and CIT Act 2023, evasion penalties of up to 100% of the tax undercharged (CIT Act: fine up to BMD 1,000,000); late payment interest at 8.5% per annum; criminal prosecution of the company and responsible individuals with imprisonment up to 5 years; automatic revocation or suspension of the tax-exemption certificate.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the exempted company is a mere instrumentality of the UK parent: directors and officers who signed false returns are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of all evaded taxes and penalties; courts may impose travel bans and asset freezes pending mutual legal assistance, and offender directors face disqualification from acting as directors for up to 10 years and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance (Requirements) Regulations 2018: fine of up to BMD 100,000 plus BMD 10,000 per day for continuing default; the Registrar may impose an adequate-substance assessment, strike the company off the register, and report the non-compliance to HMRC under the UK-Bermuda Tax Information Exchange Agreement, triggering a UK corporate investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed for trading while the company has no substance; Bermuda courts may disregard the separate legal personality and hold the UK parent and its directors liable for the Bermudian entity's debts; directors face disqualification, travel bans pending extradition, and criminal fines for knowingly permitting the shell arrangement.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Beneficial Ownership Act and Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering) Regulations 2005: fines of up to BMD 25,000 plus daily default penalties of BMD 2,500 for BO non-compliance and fines up to BMD 500,000 with imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; the Bermuda Monetary Authority may order the freeze of bank accounts and revocation of any licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally liable for BO filing failures and AML breaches; the BMA and Bermuda courts can issue travel bans and asset-freezing orders, and directors of the UK parent remain subject to extradition from the UK under the Bermuda-UK mutual assistance treaties for intentional money laundering facilitation.
Fraudulent Trading and Misrepresentation to Regulators

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent trading under common law and Companies Act 1981: unlimited civil liability for all company debts; the Bermuda court may impose a winding-up order, strike-off, or restitution order; criminal conviction carries fines of up to BMD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent trading: directors, including UK-resident shadow directors, are personally liable for civil restitution to creditors and clients; the court may issue travel bans preventing departure from Bermuda, disqualification orders of up to 15 years, and, for egregious cases, custodial sentences with UK extradition enforcement.
