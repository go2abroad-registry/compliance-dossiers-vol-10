Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda is a fiscally autonomous British Overseas Territory under the Bermuda Constitution Order 1968; the United Kingdom does not extend VAT or UK income tax to Bermuda. The competent authorities for stage 3 are: (i) the Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda, which administers the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CIT Act) and issues Bermuda Tax Reference Numbers, with filing conducted through the Bermuda Government eCIT portal at https://www.gov.bm/; and (ii) the Bermuda Customs Department, which administers customs duties, transshipment relief, and electronic bills of entry through the ASYCUDA World single-window platform at https://www.customs.gov.bm/. Entity formation is administered by the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Formation: file the certificate of exempted limited partnership with the Registrar of Companies via the Bermuda e-Registrar; obtain the Certificate of Exempted Partnership. No minimum capital requirement applies and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted. Step 2 - Default status: a fiscally transparent ELP is not automatically registered for income tax or CIT; the Registrar's partnership number is the sole statutory identifier, and no separate tax ID is issued because Bermuda imposes no general corporate income tax on transparent partnerships. Step 3 - CIT registration: if the ELP is, or is a member of, an MNE group with annual consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000 (GloBE/Pillar Two scope), the ELP must apply to the Tax Commissioner for CIT registration within the prescribed period (generally 90 days after the end of the fiscal year in which it first becomes in scope) and will receive a Bermuda Tax Reference Number required for all CIT filings. Step 4 - Customs registration: any ELP importing, exporting, or transshipping goods through Bermuda must obtain a customs client code in ASYCUDA World before the first entry; no turnover threshold applies. Step 5 - FX: no Bermuda Monetary Authority approval or exchange-control permit is required for trade-related remittances.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (default). The Exempted Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent: each partner is subject to tax in its own jurisdiction of residence on its allocable share of the partnership's trading profit, and the ELP itself is not a Bermuda CIT taxpayer unless it is non-transparent by election or constitutes a Bermuda permanent establishment of an in-scope MNE group. Territorial_Scope: Bermuda applies territorial taxation; foreign-source wholesale arbitrage income from LME-delivered copper cathodes is outside the Bermuda CIT net where the contract formation, delivery, and settlement occur outside Bermuda, and no Bermuda capital gains, inheritance, withholding, or turnover taxes apply. Tax_Exempt_Zone: customs duty and payroll tax are the only general impositions; there is no VAT/GST. Legacy exempted-undertaking tax protection assurances are curtailed for MNE groups in scope of the CIT Act with effect from 1 January 2025.

Available election forms

Bermuda does not operate a US-style check-the-box system, but the CIT Act and Bermuda tax practice provide statutory elections aligned with OECD/GloBE: (i) Transparency/Opacity Election - an ELP may elect to be treated as a separate CIT taxable entity (opaque), or, where it would otherwise be within CIT, may maintain flow-through treatment with partners taxed on their allocable shares; (ii) Collective Investment Vehicle (CIV) Election - aligning the equity-inclusion rules for eligible investment entities; (iii) QDMTT Safe Harbour Election - allowing an in-scope MNE group to elect the Bermuda Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax computation for GloBE purposes; and (iv) Hybrid/Elections for UK-resident partners under CTA 2010/CFI rules, including hybrid mismatch and interest restriction elections, which must be documented and filed with the first CIT return. No partnership-level election is required for customs or payroll purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax exists in Bermuda. Indirect taxation is limited to customs duty on importation (Customs Act 1941 and Customs Tariff Act 1957), stamp duty (Stamp Duties Act 1976), and payroll tax (Payroll Tax Act 1995). For the copper cathode wholesale arbitrage business, the operative indirect regime is customs duty, including duty-free transit/transshipment of goods that neither enter Bermuda consumption nor undergo alteration within Bermudian customs territory.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration threshold: not applicable (no VAT regime). Customs registration threshold: none - any person importing, exporting, or transshipping goods must obtain an ASYCUDA World customs client code from the first consignment, irrespective of turnover. CIT registration threshold (analogue): an MNE group with annual consolidated revenue of at least BMD 750,000,000 (EUR 750,000,000; BMD pegged 1:1 to USD) is required to register the Bermuda constituent entities, including any non-transparent ELP, with the Tax Commissioner. For context, Bermuda customs duty on goods entering local consumption averages approximately 20-25%, with maximum rates up to 185%, while copper cathodes in transit are subject to 0% duty.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT concept: Bermuda levies no VAT, so no 0% VAT export rate and no input-VAT refund mechanism can arise. The functional equivalent is duty-free transshipment/export under the Customs Tariff Act 1957 transit rules: Grade A refined copper cathodes may be entered for transshipment or export without duty if the goods remain under customs control, are not consumed, mixed, or altered in Bermuda, and are exported within the prescribed period (generally 30 days, extendable by the Comptroller of Customs). Export of goods processed or manufactured in Bermuda is likewise duty-exempt, provided a valid export/transit bill of entry is filed in ASYCUDA World. For the pass-through ELP, no Bermuda CIT applies to the arbitrage profits where all contracting, delivery, and settlement occur outside Bermuda.

Since Bermuda has no VAT, the classic export zero-rating fraud and VAT clawback regime does not apply. Scrutiny instead attaches to customs and CIT misconduct: (i) Customs fraud - false declarations, false invoices, or misdescription of goods to obtain duty-free transit/export treatment attract forfeiture of the goods, a monetary penalty of up to three times the dutiable value or BMD 5,000 (whichever is greater), recovery of the unassessed duty plus interest, and imprisonment of up to 2 years under the Customs Act 1941; failure to export transit goods within the prescribed bond period triggers immediate assessment of full duty plus penalties. (ii) CIT Act penalties (for in-scope MNE groups) - late registration, late filing, or understatement attract interest and penalties up to 50% of the understated tax, and the General Anti-Abuse Rule may recharacterise a transparent ELP arrangement where the principal purpose is to obtain a Bermuda tax advantage. (iii) Partner-level exposure - UK-resident partners remain subject to HMRC hybrid mismatch and transfer pricing adjustments under UK/Bermuda information-exchange arrangements. No Bermuda penalty applies to the 180-day FX repatriation benchmark; that deadline is a contractual trade-finance standard tied to LME documentary credit settlement and enhances banking AML due diligence at the Medium_Risk_Enhanced tier.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged 1:1 to the US dollar and is fully backed by USD reserves. The ELP must apply to the BMA for non-resident designation under the Exchange Control Act 1972. Once designated, current account flows (trade proceeds, fees, interest) and capital account flows (partner capital injections, distributions, loan repayments, liquidation proceeds) are freely convertible into USD and freely transferable to approved offshore accounts. Profit remittance hurdles are documentary rather than fiscal: the ELP must file BMA exchange control declarations, maintain the non-resident designation, and evidence that all distributions derive from bona fide arm's-length copper cathode transactions; no Bermuda tax withholding applies to a non-resident designated ELP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Exchange Control Act 1972, a Bermuda-resident exporter must offer export proceeds to an authorised dealer within 6 months of receipt and may be required to convert to BMD. A wholly non-Bermudian-owned ELP with BMA non-resident designation is exempt from the surrender and mandatory conversion regime; proceeds in USD, EUR, or GBP may be retained in offshore or Bermuda external accounts without conversion. The ELP must preserve this exemption by ensuring no Bermuda-resident partner or beneficial owner exists and by renewing its non-resident designation annually with the BMA.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) administering the Exchange Control Act 1972 (Bermuda); for UK bank access, the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) and Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) govern the banks serving the Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for primary treasury, Medium as a secondary collection wallet. UK FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Verto, and similar multi-currency platforms) can support sub-threshold GBP/EUR working capital and fast FX conversion, but present critical legal constraints: (i) e-money is safeguarded, not a bank deposit, and is not accepted by LME clearing houses or reputable copper cathode counterparties; (ii) EMIs cannot issue or advise documentary Letters of Credit, provide trade loans, or operate as a settlement bank in the LME warrant chain; (iii) high-value copper parcels (typically USD 2-10 million per transaction) will trigger enhanced due diligence and source-of-wealth review that most EMI compliance teams cannot clear; (iv) Bermuda Exchange Control requires capital account flows to route through a Bermuda authorised dealer unless a specific BMA non-resident account exemption is granted. Therefore, EMIs are legally viable only for minor operating expenses, never for title-transfer settlements or capital flows.

Capital contributions from the Cayman parent or limited partners route via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the source bank to the Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda Capital Escrow Account using ISO 20022 purpose code CAPC (Capital Contribution) and citing the BMA exchange control permission number. The BMA non-resident designation permits onward transfer to the London operating account (Standard Chartered or Barclays) without BMD conversion. The UK inbound leg uses UK BOPS code E002 (Investment by non-resident) or ISO purpose code CAPC, supported by the Limited Partnership Agreement, capital call notices, and the Bermuda non-resident designation certificate, to prevent misclassification as trading revenue. The escrow account is opened in USD; no conversion to BMD is required.Primary clearing rail: irrevocable confirmed documentary Letter of Credit (UCP600) issued by Standard Chartered or HSBC on behalf of the ELP, transmitted via SWIFT MT700/MT707, and settled against presentation of the LME warrant, commercial invoice, insurance certificate, full-set bills of lading, and inspection certificates. Secondary rails: (i) MT103/MT202 telegraphic transfers for pre-approved counterparties on a Documents Against Payment (DP) basis; (ii) LMEclear ring settlement for LME warrant transfers; (iii) ICC Uniform Rules for Bank Payment Obligations (URBPO) for digitised trade matching. All outbound USD payments clear over SWIFT/CHIPS/Fedwire; GBP and EUR clear over CHAPS, SEPA, and TARGET2 respectively.Vanilla deliverable FX forwards (USD/BMD at offshore parity, USD/GBP, USD/EUR) for matched trade settlement datesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in restricted currencies (CNH, INR, BRL, IDR) for copper cathode buyers or sellers requiring non-convertible settlementCross-currency swaps (USD/GBP and USD/EUR) for term-matching trade finance and working capital liabilitiesEuropean-style FX options for contingent bid or offer trade flowsLME copper futures, options, and commodity swaps as ancillary price-hedge instruments integrated with the FX programme

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for primary treasury, Medium as a secondary collection wallet. UK FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Verto, and similar multi-currency platforms) can support sub-threshold GBP/EUR working capital and fast FX conversion, but present critical legal constraints: (i) e-money is safeguarded, not a bank deposit, and is not accepted by LME clearing houses or reputable copper cathode counterparties; (ii) EMIs cannot issue or advise documentary Letters of Credit, provide trade loans, or operate as a settlement bank in the LME warrant chain; (iii) high-value copper parcels (typically USD 2-10 million per transaction) will trigger enhanced due diligence and source-of-wealth review that most EMI compliance teams cannot clear; (iv) Bermuda Exchange Control requires capital account flows to route through a Bermuda authorised dealer unless a specific BMA non-resident account exemption is granted. Therefore, EMIs are legally viable only for minor operating expenses, never for title-transfer settlements or capital flows.
