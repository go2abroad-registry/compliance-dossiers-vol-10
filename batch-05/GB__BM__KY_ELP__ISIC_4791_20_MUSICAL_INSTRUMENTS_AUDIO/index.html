Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), 100 Parliament Street, London SW1A 2BQ, United Kingdom; official portal: https://www.gov.uk (VAT registration: https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat; Making Tax Digital API: https://developer.service.gov.uk/api-catalogue/making-tax-digital). For Bermuda situs matters: Registrar of Companies and the Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC), Hamilton, Bermuda; portal: https://www.gov.bm/tax-information.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Jurisdictional formation: register the Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership with the Bermuda Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992 and obtain the Certificate of Registration; the ELP has no minimum capital requirement, may be wholly non-resident owned, and must file an annual return. Step 2 — UK VAT registration: as a non-established taxable person, the ELP submits online form VAT1A (or VAT1) to HMRC, providing (i) a certified copy of the ELP deed and Bermuda registration certificate, (ii) identity of the general partner and ultimate beneficial owners for anti-money-laundering clearance, (iii) evidence of intended UK economic activity (supply contracts, marketplace account details, warehouse or third-party fulfilment agreements), and (iv) bank account details; HMRC issues the VAT registration number (GB 000 000 000 format) and certificate within 3–4 weeks. Registration is mandatory once UK taxable turnover reaches or is expected to reach the £90,000 rolling-12-month threshold, and is required from the first UK taxable supply for goods situated in the UK at sale or for online sales of goods dispatched from outside the UK in consignments exceeding £135 without an online marketplace deemed supplier. Step 3 — EORI/customs: apply for a UK EORI number (GB + 9/12 digits) at https://www.gov.uk/eori before moving goods across the UK border. Step 4 — Bermuda payroll (if applicable): register as an employer with the OTC within 15 days of the first payroll payment (payroll tax band 4.75%–10.75%); Bermuda levies no income tax, capital gains tax, VAT, or sales tax on the ELP.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: under UK tax law the Bermuda ELP is statutorily transparent — each partner is taxed directly on his/its share of the ELP's profits (ITTOIA 2005, Pt. 9; CTA 2009, Pt. 17), and the ELP itself is not chargeable to UK income or corporation tax except as a representative of its partners. For UK VAT, however, the ELP is an independent taxable person in its own right. Bermuda imposes no entity-level income, capital, or profits tax, and the ELP may obtain a statutory certificate of exemption from Bermudian taxation.

Available election forms

UK: no general check-the-box election is available; partnership transparency is mandatory. The ELP may, however, make targeted elections under the UK hybrid mismatch rules in CTA 2010, Pt. 21A (ss. 259A–259DB), in particular an election to align the UK treatment of the ELP with the treatment adopted in the jurisdictions of its partners (so that the ELP may be treated as non-transparent for corporation tax purposes where necessary), thereby preventing double deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes. Bermuda: the ELP may apply to the Minister of Finance for a certificate of exemption from all present and future Bermuda taxes under the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992, with the exemption period stated in the certificate and renewable. United States (if US owners exist): a check-the-box election on Form 8832 (Treas. Reg. § 301.7701-3) is available to change the default partnership classification to association taxable as a corporation; the consequent UK hybrid mismatch effects must then be assessed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 (general registration threshold for taxable turnover supplied in the UK in a rolling 12-month period; VATA 1994, Sch. 1). A non-established Bermuda ELP is registrable from its first UK taxable supply where the goods are situated in the UK at the point of sale; where goods are dispatched from outside the UK in consignments exceeding £135 and no online marketplace is involved, the ELP is liable to register without threshold relief and import VAT is payable at the border. Online marketplaces such as eBay/Amazon are the deemed suppliers for consignments not exceeding £135, removing the ELP's registration obligation for those specific sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The ELP may zero-rate (0% VAT) supplies of musical instruments (HS 9205, 9206, 9207; audio equipment HS 8518) that are physically exported from the UK to customers outside the UK (excluding Northern Ireland, which retains EU VAT rules), provided: (i) the ELP is registered for UK VAT and the supply qualifies as an export under VATA 1994 s. 30(6) and HMRC VAT Notice 703; (ii) the goods leave the UK within three months of the time of supply; (iii) the ELP obtains, within that period, commercial evidence of export such as shipping manifests, customs clearance messages (MRN), courier tracking, and delivery confirmation at a foreign address; (iv) consideration is received in foreign or UK currency and properly recorded; and (v) for postal exports, Royal Mail/Parcelforce or courier tracking logs are retained. Closely connected services (e.g., warranty, setup, or audio-engineering support supplied by the ELP in relation to exported goods) may be zero-rated where the conditions in VAT Notice 703 are met and the recipient belongs outside the UK; otherwise, the place-of-supply rules in VATA 1994 s. 7A determine UK VAT liability.

  • UK Customs Export Declaration (Customs Declaration Service — CDS) with the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice (HS 9205/9206/9207/8518 codes, value in GBP or foreign currency, Incoterms, ultimate consignee)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier consignment note (FedEx/UPS/DHL) showing dispatch from the UK and delivery overseas
  • Proof of departure and delivery (port/airport departure records, destination customs entry, signature and tracking log)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the ELP's Bermuda bank account
  • Packing list and export packing declaration
  • Royal Mail/Parcelforce certificate of posting or comprehensive tracking record where postal dispatch is used
  • Freight forwarder's certificate confirming physical removal of the goods from the UK where third-party export evidence is used

The UK does not operate a continuous transaction control or real-time fiscal clearance regime; there is no digital stamping or pre-clearance of e-invoices. The mandatory digital regime is Making Tax Digital for VAT (MTD): VAT-registered businesses above the £90,000 threshold must keep digital records and submit each VAT return through HMRC-recognised software using the live MTD Application Programming Interface (API), with filing within 1 calendar month plus 7 days after each accounting period and a points-based penalty system under FA 2021. E-invoicing in PEPPOL/UBL format is not compulsory for domestic B2B/B2C but is increasingly used; customs import/export declarations must be filed electronically through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS), which has replaced CHIEF.

HMRC rigorously scrutinises zero-rating claims. If the ELP cannot substantiate export with the required evidence, the supply is reclassified as standard-rated UK VAT at 20%, and HMRC may: (i) raise a best-judgment assessment under VATA 1994 s. 73 to recover the VAT together with default interest under VATA 1994 s. 74 from the due date; (ii) impose penalties under Finance Act 2007, Sch. 24 of up to 30% (careless), 70% (deliberate but not concealed), or 100% (deliberate and concealed) of the potential lost VAT, reducible for unprompted disclosure; (iii) open a criminal investigation under VATA 1994 s. 72 for fraudulent evasion where false export documents, phantom shipments, or circular export–import schemes are detected, with unlimited fines, imprisonment, and confiscation under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002; (iv) issue joint-and-several liability notices to online marketplaces knowingly facilitating non-collected VAT; and (v) apply extended assessment time limits of up to 20 years for deliberate non-disclosure, with retrospective clawback of input VAT previously recovered on the exported goods. The ELP should retain all export evidence for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged 1:1 to the US Dollar and is fully convertible; Bermuda law does not distinguish between capital and current account convertibility, and there are no exchange controls on profit remittance or capital repatriation for a Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership. The UK abolished exchange controls in 1979, and the Bank of England imposes no approval requirement on capital injections, trade payments, or profit distributions. Practical friction is limited to bank-level source-of-funds and economic-substance documentation, not statutory convertibility limits; the ELP must maintain a valid Economic Substance Declaration or a non-resident tax attestation to support unimpeded distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither Bermuda nor the UK imposes any statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Receipts from online sales of musical instruments and studio audio gear may be retained freely in USD, GBP, EUR, or other permitted currencies. Bermuda-licensed banks must include external receipts in aggregated transaction reports filed with the BMA, but no exporter is obligated to convert export proceeds into BMD or GBP within any prescribed period.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as the exchange-control, banking, and AML/CFT supervisory authority for Bermuda; Bank of England (BoE) retains regulatory oversight of the UK's GBP clearing infrastructure (CHAPS/RTGS) and the UK banking sector relevant to cross-border settlement; both authorities coordinate through standard correspondent-banking reporting channels.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires a SWIFT MT103 wire from the UK remitter to the ELP's Bermuda USD or GBP account, carrying a Purpose of Payment code classified as direct investment equity capital (IMF BPM6 code 8015 or the UK Foreign Exchange Joint Standing Committee purpose code 1305). Settlement is verified under the BMA's aggregated reporting framework; the receiving Bermuda bank requires the certified ELP certificate, executed partnership agreement, capital account ledger, and a partner resolution authorizing receipt. No central-bank approval is required in either jurisdiction, but the remitting UK bank may apply enhanced due diligence where the beneficiary is an offshore entity.Trade payments to instrument and audio-gear suppliers are executed through (i) SWIFT MT103 international wires for standard supplier invoices; (ii) documentary or standby Commercial Letters of Credit (ICC Uniform Customs and Practice, UCP 600) for bulk manufacturer orders in Asia and the EU; and (iii) SEPA credit transfers where the ELP maintains a EUR-denominated account with a UK EMI. USD-denominated outflows clear via UK or US correspondent networks; every disbursement must be matched to a purchase order, commercial invoice, and beneficiary-entitlement declaration to satisfy Bermuda AML obligations.Deliverable FX forwards (USD/GBP, USD/EUR, BMD/USD) with Bermuda-licensed banks or HSBC/Butterfield dealing desks; tenors up to 12 months; no BMA pre-approval required when used to hedge verified trade exposures.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted currencies (e.g., CNH, KRW, INR) where suppliers invoice in non-convertible currencies; notional settled in USD; permitted under Bermuda Exchange Control general consent for trade hedging.Cross-currency swaps (USD/GBP/EUR) for structural balance-sheet hedging of the ELP's capital base and supplier credit facilities; requires ISDA documentation, a credit-support annex, and bank-limit approval; permissible as a non-financial-business treasury activity under BMA practice.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium-High. UK FCA-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) may on-board the Bermuda ELP for multi-currency GBP/EUR/USD settlement; most EMIs classify offshore Bermudian entities as 'exotic non-resident corporate' risk, requiring enhanced UBO documentation and a demonstrable e-commerce revenue trail. Legal Constraints: Bermuda's Digital Asset Business Act 2018 does not extend to fiat-currency EMI services, so no Bermuda EMI license is available; balances held with UK EMIs are not protected by the UK Financial Services Compensation Scheme; and the ELP must maintain at least one Bermuda-licensed bank account to satisfy Exchange Control Act reporting and demonstrate a substantive local banking nexus.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable under Bermuda domestic law; no Local File or Master File regime exists. If the ELP carries on a relevant activity in Bermuda, the Economic Substance Regulations require arm's-length remuneration and documentation commensurate with local substance, but no monetary threshold is prescribed.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda's Economic Substance Regulations apply to entities undertaking relevant activities, including distribution and service centres. An ELP conducting purely foreign-facing online retail with no Bermuda premises, employees, or local management falls outside the scope. If any relevant activity is conducted in Bermuda, the ELP must demonstrate adequate people, assets, and expenditure proportionate to income-generating activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Artificial Profit Diversion

Statutory sanction: HMRC may recharacterise the arrangement as a sham, impose UK corporation tax and national insurance charges, apply penalties up to 100% of the tax unpaid, and pursue criminal prosecution for tax evasion with unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil if the corporate general partner is a bare nominee; the UK directing minds face civil restitution, confiscation orders, travel bans, and disqualification orders.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Breach

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations empower the Registrar to impose civil penalties, which can reach BDA 100,000 per notice, and to strike the ELP off the register. The regulator may also share information with HMRC under the UK-Bermuda information-sharing arrangement.

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally liable for partnership liabilities arising from non-compliance; directors of a corporate general partner face disqualification, financial penalties, and civil restitution for wilful neglect.
AML / Payment-Rails Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Violations trigger Bermuda AML/ATF criminal offenses and UK financial sanctions enforcement, with fines, asset freezes, and custodial penalties up to 14 years for serious breaches.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner face direct criminal liability for knowingly permitting AML breaches; corporate indemnity does not cover such personal culpability, and courts may impose confiscation orders and travel restrictions.
Beneficial Ownership / Register Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Breach of Bermuda's Beneficial Ownership Act attracts criminal fines, continuing default penalties, possible strike-off, and restrictions on distributions until the register is correctly completed.

Director personal liability: Officers of the corporate general partner are treated as personally responsible for defaults; liability may include fines, prohibition orders, and in cases of fraud, imprisonment and personal confiscation orders.
