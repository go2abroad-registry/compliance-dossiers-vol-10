Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Ministry of Finance — Office of the Tax Commissioner (employment/payroll tax, Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 administration, Economic Substance Act 2018 filings) and Bermuda Customs Department (customs duties); entity registration is effected through the Registrar of Companies. Official portal: www.gov.bm. HM Revenue & Customs (www.gov.uk/hmrc) is not a Bermuda tax authority but may apply if the ELP establishes a UK taxable presence.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Exempted Limited Partnership with the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2015; the Certificate of Registration (ELP registration number) constitutes the de facto tax identifier. Step 2: If in scope of the Economic Substance (Bermuda) Act 2018, file the annual ES Return and demonstrate that core income-generating activities occur outside Bermuda. Step 3: Register for payroll tax only if the ELP employs Bermuda-resident staff, because exempted partnerships that do not carry on business in Bermuda are not subject to Bermuda income tax. Step 4: Only in-scope MNE groups (consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million) must register with the Bermuda corporate income tax administration (CITA 2023, in force for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025) and file the 15% global minimum top-up tax return; the SME ELP remains outside CIT scope. No VAT number is obtainable because Bermuda has no VAT/GST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Bermuda statutory tax election exists: an Exempted Limited Partnership is treated as fiscally transparent in Bermuda provided it does not carry on business in the Territory. For US federal tax purposes, a Bermuda ELP is an eligible entity: the default classification is partnership (if more than one member), adjustable or confirmed via IRS Form 8832 'check-the-box' election within 75 days of a classification change. For UK tax purposes, the ELP is normally transparent but may become opaque if central management and control is exercised in the UK; UK hybrid mismatch rules (CTA 2010 s 259) must be reviewed if a UK-resident member obtains a deduction without a corresponding foreign inclusion.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Bermuda has no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax; indirect levies are limited to customs and excise duties under the Customs Act 1968 and the Tariff Act 2020.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Bermuda; any person importing dutiable goods into Bermuda must be approved as an importer and obtain customs clearance (bonded/warehouse arrangements are available for transshipment).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Bermuda: because no VAT/GST exists, exports are outside the scope of Bermuda indirect taxation and there is no zero-rating or refund mechanism. The ELP must nevertheless evidence physical export from Bermuda (through customs declarations and shipping documents) to support: (i) its non-taxation status under the Exempted Partnerships Act; (ii) Economic Substance Act book-keeping; and (iii) customers' entitlement to zero-rating or VAT reduction in destination markets (e.g., UK, EU, US, GCC) by proving delivery outside the importing jurisdiction's customs territory.

  • Bermuda Customs export declaration (outward manifest/customs entry record with ELP as consignor)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing removal of the dried goji berries and herbal botanicals from Bermuda
  • Commercial invoice and ISIC 4631 wholesale purchase contract
  • Packing list and, where the destination regime requires, phytosanitary certificate and certificate of origin
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/Wire credit advice evidencing the foreign buyer's payment
  • Warehousing and transport records supporting the customs declaration (Economic Substance records)

No statutory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, Continuous Transaction Control, or digital stamping mandate applies in Bermuda. Export entries are processed electronically by the Bermuda Customs Department via licensed customs brokers; the ELP should retain electronic records (customs declarations, invoices, SWIFT confirmations) for at least 5 years per Bermuda record-keeping obligations.

Fraudulent export declarations or undervaluation are prosecuted under the Customs Act 1968: goods are liable to forfeiture and offenders face fines up to three times the duty evaded and/or imprisonment. Misstated export documents also expose the ELP to Bermuda Economic Substance penalties (from BD$4,800, with default penalties of up to BD$10,000 and additional daily fines, plus striking-off). Internationally, any customer refund or zero-rating granted abroad on the strength of false 'export evidence' may be clawed back with interest; in the UK HMRC can raise assessments for up to 20 years in deliberate-error cases and impose penalties of up to 100% of the VAT (Finance Act 2007, Schedule 24).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at a fixed 1:1 rate to the US Dollar (USD), and the two currencies circulate interchangeably within Bermuda. The Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP), being a non-resident entity for exchange control purposes, enjoys unrestricted capital and current account convertibility. There are no prior BMA consent requirements for non-resident capital movements, no profit remittance ceilings, no restrictions on dividend or distribution withdrawals, and no limitation on holding foreign currency accounts, making Bermuda effectively a fully convertible, no-exchange-control jurisdiction for this structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Bermuda imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender regime, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no repatriation windows for non-resident ELPs. Export receipts from dried goji berry and herbal botanical sales may be retained in USD, BMD, GBP, or other freely convertible currencies in licensed Bermuda bank accounts. Only prudent internal record-keeping is required to evidence the trade flows and to support any Bermuda economic substance and tax compliance filings.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the delegated Exchange Control Authority for Bermuda under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs; Medium for BMA-licensed or fully regulated digital payment providers. Legal constraints: offshore EMIs (e.g., UK or EU authorised electronic money institutions) are not automatically passportable into Bermuda; the provision of payment services to Bermuda domestic entities requires BMA authorization under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 and the applicable payment systems regulatory framework. Accordingly, the ELP should maintain core liquidity and settlement accounts at a licensed Bermuda bank, using recognized EMIs only for ancillary, low-value disbursements such as e-commerce collections and expense payments, never as primary treasury rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 750,000,000 (the BMD equivalent of the EUR 750m OECD GloBE revenue threshold) triggers full Master File / Local File duties exclusively for in-scope MNE groups. An out-of-scope ELP is not required to file transfer pricing statutes or documentation under Bermuda law; however, the UK parent must still apply UK CTA 2010 Part 4 transfer pricing rules and OECD TP principles to intercompany purchases of goji berries from related Chinese/HK suppliers, including a functional analysis benchmark study in BMD/USD if the ELP's cross-border transactions exceed the UK exemption de minimis.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Requirements) Act 2018 and related ESR Regulations apply to the ELP if it is tax resident in Bermuda and carries on a relevant activity. Bulk wholesale of dried lycium/herbal botanicals falls within the 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity. The ELP must demonstrate in Bermuda: outbound and inbound decision-making (board/general partner meetings with minutes and quorum), qualified resident staff (minimum 3 directors/managers physically present), physical office and warehouse lease, management of customer contracts and all risks, and revenue-generating accounting records. For taxable years beginning 2025, the OECD-aligned CITA 2023 imposes a similar economic substance and income inclusion test; a 'pure equity holding entity' exemption is not available to this trading ELP because it carries on active distribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and facilitation (UK Criminal Finances Act 2017, s.45/46; Bermuda CITA 2023 fraud penalties)

Statutory sanction: UK: corporate offence of failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion - unlimited fine plus debarment; ancillary HM Revenue guidance demands robust prevention procedures. Bermuda: under s.64 CITA 2023, fraudulent avoidance of tax attracts a fine up to BMD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; under Bermuda Criminal Code, theft/corruption prosecutions may add forfeiture of double the benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner can be prosecuted personally as joint tortfeasors; HMRC can apply for civil restitution orders, worldwide freezing injunctions and travel bans (up to 2 years' restraint); conviction may trigger director disqualification under UK Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986 for up to 15 years.
Shell company / false economic substance declaration (Bermuda ESR and UK GAAR anti-abuse rule)

Statutory sanction: Bermuda ESR: initial penalty up to BMD 150,000 and further daily penalties of BMD 10,000 for continued non-compliance; the Commissioner may require a forensic substance audit; persistent failure leads to exchange of information with HMRC under the TIEA. UK: GAAR counteraction arrangement - tax advantage counteracted plus penalty of 60% of the counteracted tax; DPT (Diverted Profits Tax) at 25% of diverted profits.

Director personal liability: A director/general partner who knowingly signs a false ESR declaration commits an offence under Bermuda ESR s.13(2) carrying up to 2 years' imprisonment and a fine; UK GAAR 'designer' provisions allow HMRC to issue a personal liability notice against advisers and officers who knowingly facilitated a tax avoidance arrangement; travel restrictions and cross-border arrest warrants are obtainable in serious cases.
AML/beneficial ownership compliance (Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 (Bermuda); Beneficial Ownership (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Regulations 2017; UK Money Laundering Regulations 2017)

Statutory sanction: Bermuda: penalties up to BMD 500,000 and 14 years' imprisonment for money laundering (POCA 1997); BO register offences are punishable by a fine of up to BMD 100,000 per violation and possible strike-off. UK: failure to have adequate AML policies is punishable by fines and public censure by the FCA; unpaid tax + interest of 100% of the obligation under the Money Laundering Regulations.

Director personal liability: The general partner, being the key control person, bears personal criminal liability for AML failures; civil liability extends to restitution of the laundered payments; HMRC and the Bermuda Financial Intelligence Agency may issue travel restraint orders and seize the passports of non-compliant beneficial owners.
Profit repatriation mismatch and unlawful distribution (Exempted Partnerships Act 1992 s.24; UK hybrid mismatch rules TIOPA 2010 Part 6A)

Statutory sanction: Bermuda: unlawful distribution is void; the general partner is jointly and severally liable to restore the sum to the ELP for the benefit of creditors; company-law-style fraudulent trading charges can be brought. UK: hybrid mismatch rules deny the exemption and subject the distribution to corporation tax at 25%, and in a double-deduction structure, a UK PAYE/NIC penalty plus National Insurance arrears may be assessed.

Director personal liability: The general partners who authorised an unlawful distribution must personally indemnify creditors (EPA s.24(3)); the UK recipient partner's directors are personally liable for the corporation tax not deducted at source under HMRC's 'tax as a debt' provisions, and they may be disqualified; a deliberate mismatch is a 'failed avoidance' offence leading to 5 years' imprisonment under UK CTA 2010 s.1119 (tax evasion).
