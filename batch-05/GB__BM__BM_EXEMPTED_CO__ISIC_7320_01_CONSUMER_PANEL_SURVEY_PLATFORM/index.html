Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is fully convertible and pegged at 1:1 to the USD. Bermuda imposes no exchange-control restrictions on exempted companies designated as non-resident under the Exchange Control Act 1972. Both capital-account and current-account transactions (share subscriptions, dividends, intercompany loans, royalties, service fees) are freely remittable in BMD or any foreign currency without BMA pre-approval. Profit repatriation is unimpeded because Bermuda levies no income, withholding, capital gains, or corporation tax on exempted companies. The only operative compliance overlays are BMA AML/ATF supervision over resident banks and the annual economic substance declaration, which is a nil-relevant-activity filing for an ISIC 7320 market-research platform.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Bermuda has no export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no required holding period for foreign-currency receipts. Survey-platform service revenues may be retained in full in USD, EUR, or GBP accounts at Bermuda banks or overseas EMIs and may be applied directly to cross-border panel payouts without repatriation to Bermuda.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the integrated financial regulator and central bank under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, Currency Act 1972, and Exchange Control Act 1972.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

High viability as a disbursement utility, subject to a two-tier treasury architecture. Wise (UK FCA-authorised e-money institution) and PayPal Global Mass Payout are permissible rails for consumer-panel incentives under Bermuda's framework because the BMA does not licence non-resident EMIs as deposit-takers when used solely as payment-service vendors. Legal constraint: the EMI must not solicit deposits from Bermuda residents or maintain a Bermuda-resident current account without a licence under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999. Accordingly, core balance-sheet positions and intercompany treasury must sit at Butterfield or HSBC, with Wise and PayPal holding only a prefunded disbursement float not exceeding one month of expected survey payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: the UK or group shareholder remits from a regulated UK/EU bank into the Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda USD account via SWIFT MT103 with the purpose-code narrative 'Equity capital injection - subscription of ordinary shares under the Companies Act 1981'; the bank reference field must mirror the company's exact name and Bermuda non-resident certificate number. No BMA exchange-control approval is required because the BMA-authorised dealer bank relies on the entity's non-resident endorsement. For injections above USD 1 million, the remitter must provide a source-of-funds pack, and the receiving bank may impose a 24- to 72-hour hold pending AML file completion. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Not applicable to tax-exempted companies outside the CIT scope. If the company becomes subject to the Bermuda CIT as a large multinational group constituent, mandatory Local File documentation is required where in-scope cross-border related-party transactions exceed BMD 1,000,000 in a fiscal year; Master File is required at group revenue of €750m or above.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 requires 'relevant entities' engaged in 'relevant activities' (including intellectual property holding, distribution and service business, financing and leasing, insurance, shipping, and banking) to maintain substantial economic presence in Bermuda. A consumer panel survey and market research SaaS platform is generally not a relevant activity unless it provides such services intra-group or holds intangibles and derives income from intellectual property. If caught, the company must file an annual economic substance declaration and demonstrate adequate management, employees, and expenditure; failure attracts penalties up to BD$250,000 and potential mandatory winding up.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Dual Residence and UK Corporation Tax Exposure

Statutory sanction: HMRC may assess UK corporation tax at 25% (main rate from April 2023) on worldwide profits; penalties for failure to notify and register under FA 2008 Sch 41; interest on underpaid tax; CFC charge under Part 9A TIOPA 2010 may apply if profits are artificially diverted from the UK.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for under-assessed tax where they are knowingly concerned; disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986; deliberate non-disclosure carries up to 7 years' imprisonment under UK fraud legislation and travel bans may be imposed.
Bermuda Economic Substance Act Violations

Statutory sanction: Imposition of civil penalties up to BD$250,000, plus BD$50,000 for continuing default; the Registrar may apply to the court to strike off the company; the Minister may require the company to pay taxes that would have been due had the exemption not applied.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's penalties where they knowingly participated in the failure; a Bermuda court may issue a compliance order and the director may be barred from acting as a director of any Bermuda company for up to 5 years.
Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 (Bermuda) and applicable UK Money Laundering Regulations, fines of up to BD$500,000 and 14 years' imprisonment for the company; licence revocation; freezing of funds under court order.

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally prosecuted for consenting or conniving at the laundering; unlimited fines, civil forfeiture, travel bans, and imprisonment terms of up to 14 years in Bermuda; UK financial sanctions breaches may trigger additional penalties.
Fraudulent Tax Avoidance / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under Bermuda's Companies Act and criminal code provisions, conviction may result in a fine at the discretion of the court, forfeiture of property, and voiding of the tax exemption undertaking with retrospective tax application.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false statutory declarations commit perjury and are personally liable for fines and imprisonment up to 5 years; the court may order restitution of avoided taxes and disqualify the director from managing corporations in Bermuda and other common law jurisdictions.
