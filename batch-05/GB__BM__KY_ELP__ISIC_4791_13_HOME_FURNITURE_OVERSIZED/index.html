Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar and is generally freely convertible for non-resident designated businesses. An Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) that obtains non-resident status under Bermuda's Exchange Control regime may hold foreign currency accounts and make/receive payments in USD, GBP, EUR and other major currencies without surrender or conversion obstacles. Current account transactions, including supplier payments, freight disbursements, platform fees and commissions, are freely permitted once the ELP's non-resident designation is endorsed by the BMA. Capital account injections from foreign limited partners require a routine exchange control permission, but no material convertibility hurdle exists for an ELP that is properly designated. Profit distributions to foreign partners are not subject to Bermuda withholding tax or capital controls, provided the entity remains non-resident and outside the scope of Bermuda domestic tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident Bermuda businesses, Bermuda's Exchange Control Act 1972 requires foreign currency export proceeds to be brought into the jurisdiction within three months of the export shipment and surrendered to an authorised dealer within 30 days of receipt, generally at a mandatory conversion ratio of 100% to BMD unless an exemption or general permission is granted. For the non-resident designated ELP, these surrender and conversion obligations are disapplied: the ELP may retain 100% of export proceeds in the original invoicing currency in its authorised operational account, provided that the BMA designation and the bank's exchange-control permissions are maintained and that all export invoices, freight documentation and platform settlement records are available for inspection.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as the Bermuda Exchange Control Authority, operating under the Exchange Control Act 1972, with supervisory coordination by the Bank of England and H.M. Treasury for UK sovereign financial crime and sanctions obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is medium-to-high for supplementary GBP/EUR collection accounts but low as a primary treasury banking solution. Authorised UK/EU EMIs such as Wise Platform, Airwallex or Revolut Business can provide streamlined onboarding and lower FX costs for digital commerce receipts; however, they are not commercial banks and cannot hold BMD, issue letters of credit, or extend treasury credit lines. Legal constraints include the need for UK Electronic Money Regulations 2011 or EU PSD2 authorisation, client-fund segregation, and the requirement that the Bermuda ELP provide full UBO and source-of-funds evidence; several EMIs reject Bermuda-registered partnerships, so a regulated UK bank account is required as the underlying settlement spine.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner and shareholder capital injections should be routed through SWIFT MT103 wires from regulated partner bank accounts to the ELP's Bermuda operating account. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'Capital contribution to Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership' and quote the applicable BMA exchange control permission number. The sending bank should use a direct-investment purpose code or equivalent national statistical/BOP classification for capital account transfers; the receiving Bermuda bank must obtain the SWIFT reference, a certified copy of the partnership deed, source-of-funds evidence, partner identification, and FATCA/CRS self-certification before crediting. No cash or digital currency injections should be used, and capital contributions passing through unlicensed EMIs should be rejected by the receiving bank.Supplier and freight settlements should be executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 for direct invoice payments and through irrevocable commercial letters of credit (usually sight L/C) for large rolled-goods purchases. MT103 remittances must carry full invoice numbers, delivery terms, product HS codes, and beneficiary bank identifiers. For commercial L/Cs, use MT700 issuance and, if appropriate, MT730 confirmation or MT710 advice from the Bermuda bank’s correspondent network.
