E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Exempted Limited Partnership with the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2009 and the Limited Partnership Act 1883; obtain the registration certificate and Bermuda entity registration number. Step 2: Obtain a Bermuda tax reference / CIT TIN from the OTC only upon a triggering event: (i) CIT registration where the ELP is a constituent entity of an MNE group with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million in at least one of the two preceding fiscal years; (ii) payroll tax registration where the ELP has Bermuda-resident employees; or (iii) customs registration where the ELP imports goods into Bermuda. Step 4: Non-resident partners deriving no Bermuda-source income and lacking a Bermuda permanent establishment are not required to register or file. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

(i) Reverse-hybrid election under the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023: a fiscally transparent ELP may elect to be treated as a taxable entity where it is not tax-transparent in another relevant jurisdiction; (ii) GloBE-related administrative elections for CIT-registered groups, including transitional country-by-country reporting safe harbour, substance-based income exclusion, and qualified domestic minimum top-up tax elections; (iii) Government Exemption under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2009: a statutory ministerial undertaking exempting the ELP from future Bermuda income, capital gain, dividend, or profits taxation until 31 March 2035.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Bermuda imposes no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax; customs import duties apply under the Customs Tariff Act 1975. UK VAT at 20% may separately apply if the ELP makes taxable supplies in the United Kingdom through a UK taxable presence or exceeds the UK VAT registration threshold.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for Bermuda VAT/GST because no such regime exists; Bermuda customs duty registration and entry filing via the Customs Automated Clearance System are required on first importation into Bermuda without any turnover threshold. For UK VAT, registration is mandatory once UK taxable turnover exceeds GBP 90,000 per rolling 12-month period, or from the first supply where the ELP is a non-established taxable person.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No export zero-rating or refund mechanism exists under Bermuda law because Bermuda levies no VAT/GST; all exports from Bermuda fall outside the scope of Bermuda indirect taxation. Goods imported into Bermuda solely for transshipment, warehousing, or subsequent re-export may qualify for customs duty relief under the Customs Tariff Act where re-export occurs within an approved period and Customs approval is evidenced. UK zero-rating is relevant only where the ELP is a UK VAT-registered person exporting goods from the UK: direct exports qualify for 0% VAT where the goods are exported within 3 months (or a longer HMRC-approved period) and the exporter retains the statutory evidence package.

  • Bermuda Customs export/re-export entry (electronic declaration filed via the Customs Automated Clearance System)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing goods, values, and HS codes
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Bermuda has no real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing API, pre-clearance approval, or digital stamping for commercial invoices. Import entries must be electronically lodged with Bermuda Customs through the Customs Automated Clearance System. If the ELP registers for UK VAT, VAT returns must be submitted digitally under HMRC's Making Tax Digital mandate using MTD-compatible software; no pre-clearance invoice approval or continuous transaction control regime applies to B2B supplies.

Bermuda imposes no exchange controls and no statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter is the documented internal compliance benchmark for proceeds repatriation and supporting customs duty relief audits. Customs duty relief on re-exports is clawed back if goods remain in Bermuda beyond the approved re-export window or are diverted to local consumption; penalties under the Bermuda Customs Act include forfeiture of goods, fines, and recovery of unrelieved duty plus interest. For any UK VAT zero-rating claim, HMRC may deny the 0% rate and assess VAT at 20% where export evidence is deficient, with penalties up to 100% of the tax for deliberate default and potential criminal proceedings for fraudulent claims; all export evidence must be retained for at least 180 days and, in practice, for six years plus the relevant statutory period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda maintains a fully convertible currency regime anchored to the Bermudian Dollar (BMD) at par with the US Dollar (USD). No exchange controls restrict capital account transactions, and both current and capital account convertibility are effectively unrestricted. Profit remittance for non-resident partners is lawful without prior central bank approval, subject only to routine anti-money laundering and sanctions screening imposed by banks under Bermuda’s Proceeds of Crime Amendment Acts and Sanctions Regulations. For an exempted limited partnership holding passive electronic inventory and conducting wholesale distribution of electronic components, invoicing and settlement may freely occur in USD, EUR, or other convertible currencies. No conversion hurdles apply to dividend or capital distributions to the UK-based partnership owners. Compliance focuses on accurate record-keeping, filing of the partnership’s economic substance declaration (if applicable), and maintaining adequate documentation of the commercial rationale for the Bermudian treasury hub.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no repatriation-time mandates. A Bermudian exempted limited partnership may retain export receipts in any foreign currency indefinitely, with no obligation to convert into BMD or deposit into a local-currency account. All proceeds from the cross-border disposition of excess multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors, including receipts from EU, US, and Asian customers, may remain in USD or other settlement currencies. No central bank acknowledgment or waiver must be procured. The only requirements are general tax record-keeping and compliance with accounting standards as evidenced in audited or examined financial records.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is moderate with legal caution. Bermuda regulates digital asset businesses under the Digital Asset Business Act 2018, but that regime is not a fast-track EMI licence equivalent to a UK FCA electronic money institution or EU e-money licence. A UK or EU EMI may be used to hold a virtual IBAN and receive settlement funds from electronics distributors, but Bermuda banks and the Bermuda Monetary Authority will flag this as higher risk if the EMI is used as the primary operating account. Recommended architecture: keep the primary financial ledger and tax-relevant clearing in a fully licensed Bermudian bank account (HSBC or Butterfield); use an offshore EMI only as a secondary receivable-concentration rail for high-frequency customer receipts, strictly ring-fenced and swept daily back to Bermuda. Legal constraints require that the EMI agreement expressly prohibit lending, granting of credit, or deposit-taking activities on behalf of the partnership and that the EMI not be used to evade Bermuda anti-money laundering obligations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% headline — Bermuda levies no corporate income, capital gains, or branch profits tax on Exempted Limited Partnerships (ELPs); the ELP is tax-transparent so all income flows to partners. In-scope MNE groups (consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million) are, however, subject to the Bermuda Domestic Minimum Tax Act 2024 (effective 1 January 2025), which imposes a 15% top-up tax on Bermuda profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions, profit shares, or partnership capital/income distributions to non-resident partners.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before 31 January in each year, covering the preceding calendar year (31 December pull-date); newly registered ELPs file their first return on the next 31 January following formation.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 6 months after the end of the ELP's financial year (e.g., 30 June for a 31 December year-end), in accordance with Economic Substance Regulations 2018 (as amended) and the Tax Commissioner's Notices.

Deadline: · Frequency:

BO particulars must be filed/confirmed annually within 30 days after the ELP's registration anniversary, and any change in beneficial ownership must be filed within 30 days of the change.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Initial group registration by 30 June 2025 (or such later date directed for a non-calendar-year group); annual top-up tax return due within 15 months after the end of the first reporting fiscal year, and within 12 months after each subsequent fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: None — Bermuda imposes no local transfer-pricing (Local File / Master File) legislation and no transfer-pricing adjustment obligation; documentation thresholds do not exist in Bermuda law (no corporate income tax). De facto threshold for ancillary compliance: (i) MNE groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million must file Bermuda DMT returns and provide OECD GloBE/Country-by-Country notification data; (ii) if the ELP transacts with affiliated distributors/manufacturers, OECD-aligned TP documentation (Local File/Master File) is prudent in partner jurisdictions for UK CFC / PE exposure and in the EU for BEPS 2.0; (iii) for Bermuda ESR purposes, the GP must document the ELP's CIGA, premises and employees, not the arm's-length margin.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda ESR (Economic Substance Act 2018 and Regulations, as amended; Tax Commissioner's Guidance Notes v4.0+) requires every entity 'resident' in Bermuda for tax purposes and carrying on a Relevant Activity to maintain economic substance in Bermuda proportionate to its activity: (i) manage and direct the relevant activity in Bermuda, (ii) have an adequate number of full-time employees and qualified employees in Bermuda, (iii) incur adequate operating expenditure in Bermuda, and (iv) have physical premises in Bermuda. For an ELP trading passive electronic components: a third-party wholesale operation is generally 'not a relevant activity' because 'distribution and service centre' under Bermuda law covers distribution of goods to affiliated/related parties, not arm's-length third-party resale; a pure passive inventory holder that only holds title and employs no trading staff is not a 'pure holding entity' (because the activity is holding trading inventory, not equity participations) and must correctly categorize itself; if the ELP is treated as tax resident outside Bermuda (e.g., UK-managed), it may elect ESR non-resident treatment by filing ESR-0 with evidence of foreign tax residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / UK Permanent Establishment & Sham Office

Statutory sanction: UK: assessment of the ELP's UK-source trading profits against non-resident partners (per FA 2019 s.3 and CTA 2010 s.5(3)); penalties under Sch. 24 FA 2007 up to 100% of the tax for deliberate errors, increased by offshore amounts up to 200% under Sheet FA 2019; HMRC discovery assessments extend 20 years for offshore non-disclosure (TMA 1970 s.36); the ELP can be struck off in Bermuda for carrying on foreign business from a non-Bermuda fixed place of business without consent.

Director personal liability: The general partner is jointly and severally liable for all ELP debts and taxes to the extent of the partnership's liabilities (Exempted Partnerships Act s.27); a limited partner who takes part in management loses limited liability and becomes personally liable for all obligations; UK shadow directors can be personally assessed for penalties; fraudulent evasion is a criminal offence carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines; HMRC can freeze bank accounts (FA 2022) and pursue confiscation orders against the GP's personal assets (Proceeds of Crime Act 2002).
Economic Substance Failure / Shell Distribution Entity

Statutory sanction: Initial substance penalty BMD 125,000 plus BMD 25,000 per month (or part) of continued failure; information-filing penalty BMD 10,000 plus BMD 500/day; cancellation of 'good standing'; referral to the Registrar for compulsory striking-off; automatic exchange of the ESR failure with the UK, US and EU via TIEA, triggering tax audits in partner jurisdictions.

Director personal liability: The GP (and directors of a corporate GP) are personally liable for the ELP's statutory penalties where the ELP is insolvent or struck off (Companies Act s.296 / EPA s.27 overlapping liability); the Tax Commissioner may disqualify a Bermuda-resident manager or registered agent; for a non-resident GP, Bermuda may issue a travel ban under the Extradition (International Co-operation) Act and notify foreign authorities; criminal prosecution for knowingly providing an untrue ESR declaration.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Bermuda BO regime: default fine up to BMD 10,000 plus continuing daily fines and, on indictment, BMD 50,000 and/or 5 years' imprisonment for false statements; AML breach under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008: civil penalties and public censure by the Bermuda Monetary Authority (up to BMD 10,000 per breach and daily penalties), suspension of the financial licence or correspondent banking relationships; loss of SWIFT access and trade-credit facilities; FATF/EU listing risk for the group.

Director personal liability: The GP is the statutorily designated 'nominated officer' and is criminally liable (imprisonment up to 5 years in Bermuda) for failure to report suspicious proceeds of crime; limited partner veil is pierced (EPA s.11) if limited partners are involved in management; UK POCA 2002 s.327 liability applies extraterritorially to persons controlling the ELP in the UK; confiscation and restraint orders against personal bank accounts; directors' disqualification (Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986) and travel restrictions.
Transfer Mispricing / UK CFC & GAAR Attack

Statutory sanction: UK CFC charge: apportionment of the ELP's non-exempt profits to UK companies at the UK corporation tax rate (currently 25% for FY2025); transfer-pricing adjustments re-attributing profits to UK sellers under TIOPA 2010 Part 4; Balanced-Profile penalties 35-100% of the underpaid tax; GAAR counteractions under Finance Act 2013 with a 60% GAAR penalty; denial of treaty benefits and double taxation.

Director personal liability: Individual partners arranging the structure are within the DOTAS/POTAS rules — the promoter/GP faces penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided; personal liability for CFC apportionment if the UK shareholder is an individual (via s.725 ITTOIA 2005 attribution); criminal investigation for offshore evasion (TMA 1970 s.106A) — extradition between Bermuda and the UK is available; Advisory opinions may allow a deduction for the GP's personal guarantee fees, but fraudulent artificiality exposes the GP to civil fraud claims by lenders.
Statutory Registrar and DMT Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Annual return default: fine of BMD 500 plus BMD 100/day and loss of good standing; DMT registration failure: BMD 10,000 penalty + BMD 1,000/day; DMT top-up tax at 15% plus interest at the default rate; the Registrar's strike-off procedure removes the ELP's legal capacity — assets vest automatically in the Crown/title issues for the inventory; the corporate veil is removed because a struck-off ELP cannot be sued or litigated, and its creditors can recover directly from the GP.

Director personal liability: The GP remains personally liable for the ELP's debts and tax after striking-off (EPA s.27); UK/EU directors who sign returns containing false declarations face personal criminal penalties; DMT false declarations attract fines up to BMD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; automatic exchange under the GloBE framework exposes the ELP to top-up tax payable by the ultimate parent in the UK — a person directing the evasion may be disqualified as a director in Bermuda for up to 5 years.
