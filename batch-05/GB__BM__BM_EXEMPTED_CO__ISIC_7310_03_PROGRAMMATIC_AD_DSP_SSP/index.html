Available election forms

Bermuda law does not provide a domestic check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election. For foreign tax purposes, particularly U.S. federal tax, the exempted company may file IRS Form 8832 to elect classification as a disregarded entity, partnership, or corporation. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax; Bermuda imposes customs duties on imported goods and payroll taxes on remuneration

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. An importer of goods into Bermuda must comply with Bermuda Customs Department registration and duty declaration requirements under the Customs Tariff Act; no minimum turnover threshold is prescribed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda does not impose VAT or GST; therefore no export zero-rating mechanism exists. Goods exported from Bermuda are not subject to Bermuda indirect tax, and offshore services supplied by a Bermuda exempted company are generally outside the scope of Bermuda taxation. The exempted company's tax-exempt status is evidenced by its Certificate of Exemption issued under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bermuda Customs Department)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (where available)

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime is in force. The Bermuda Tax Commissioner and Registrar of Companies accept standard digital and paper filings; records must be retained under the Companies Act 1981 and the Payroll Tax Act, but no government pre-clearance of invoices is mandated.

As no VAT zero-rating exists, there are no VAT refund clawbacks or associated penalties. Bermuda Customs may impose penalties for false export declarations, including monetary fines up to the value of the goods, confiscation and forfeiture of goods, and criminal prosecution. Misrepresentation in corporate filings with the Registrar of Companies may expose the company to fines and strike-off action under the Companies Act 1981.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to USD and is fully convertible on current account for service-export receipts (ISIC 7310 programmatic advertising fees). Capital-account flows are governed by the Exchange Control Act 1972; however, a non-resident-owned Bermuda Exempted Company automatically receives non-resident exchange-control designation, which grants blanket BMA permission for equity injections, intercompany loans, dividend repatriation and capital repatriation without prior Exchange Control approval. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal: no Bermudian withholding tax is imposed on dividends, interest, royalties or service fees paid to the UK parent, and no capital gains tax applies; the only formal prerequisite is the filing of first-year management accounts or audited financial statements plus a directors' solvency statement. Temporary quasi-equity sweeps from the UK parent are unrestricted but must be booked through an Authorised Dealer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or service-export proceeds. The BMA's exchange-control framework requires only that foreign-currency receipts be recorded with the company's Authorised Dealer (the Bermuda bank) for statistical balance-of-payments reporting; the non-resident exempted company may retain 100% of USD, GBP and EUR proceeds offshore without any surrender obligation. No partial conversion into BMD is mandatory at any point, and no holding-period limit applies before repatriation or disbursement to publishers.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), operating under the Bermuda Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999; the BMA acts as Central Bank, Exchange Control Authority and AML/CFT supervisor for all permitted business activity in Bermuda.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM as a payment-overlay supplement; LOW as a substitute for the BMA-licensed primary bank. Offshore EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Revolut Business) are legally available to a Bermuda Exempted Company for holding segregated client funds in the UK, EU or US through those EMIs' licensed banking partners, and they provide efficient mass-payout rails for small publisher settlements (US ACH, SEPA CT, UK FPS). However, no offshore EMI may accept BMD deposits, join Bermuda's local clearing house, or hold a BMA banking licence; therefore a primary account at a licensed Tier-1 Bermudian bank remains mandatory for substance, treasury, and FX-hedging counterparty credibility. Recommended target architecture: Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda core treasury account plus a Wise Business multi-currency overlay (funded by a daily USD sweep) executing approximately 80% of SSP publisher micro-payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial equity injection of US$100,000 is routed from the UK parent's HSBC UK account to the Bermuda treasury account via SWIFT MT103. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must state 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION DIRECT INVESTMENT', and the UK remitting bank will assign its internal balance-of-payments purpose classification for direct-investment equity capital (the bank's standard 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' purpose code must be confirmed with the HSBC UK relationship manager during remittance setup). The Bermuda receiving bank (as Authorised Dealer) credits the funds without prior BMA approval by virtue of the company's non-resident exchange-control designation, then files a routine exchange-control statistical declaration to the BMA within 30 days. Supporting verification comprises the certified board resolution allotting shares, updated UK Companies House and Bermuda Register of Members records, bank credit advice, and where aggregate injections exceed USD 1,000,000, a BMA capital-movements statistical declaration endorsed by the Bermuda Registered Agent.Publisher payouts and vendor disbursements use four clearing layers: (1) SWIFT MT103 direct transfers from the Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda treasury account to publisher banks in Europe/APAC/UK; (2) US-dollar correspondent clearing via the Bermuda bank's New York agent, using Fedwire for amounts above USD 100,000 and ACH Direct Credit for sub-USD-100,000 payouts to US publisher accounts; (3) EMI overlay batch-rail (Wise Business / Airwallex) funded by a daily USD sweep from the Bermuda account to the EMI's US or UK sponsor account, executing SEPA CT, UK FPS and mass ACH for thousands of micro-payouts; and (4) Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) issued by HSBC Bermuda for premium publisher or SSP supply contracts above GBP 100,000 when bank-assured payment is contractually required, although MT103 is the market-norm rail for programmatic net-30/60 settlement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 0 / no local turnover threshold — a Bermuda exempted company outside the Pillar Two scope has no mandatory transfer-pricing documentation duty. For an in-scope MNE group with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million, the CIT Act requires contemporaneous Local File and Master File in line with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, including substance-based income exclusion documentation; records must be produced within 45 days of a formal request.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Act 2018: every exempted company must file an annual economic substance declaration with the Registrar. If it performs a 'relevant activity' (including IP holding, headquarters, financing or leasing, distribution, or service centre), it must demonstrate direction and management in Bermuda, adequate physical presence, staff, and expenditure in Bermuda, and substantial activity proportionate to the income. A programmatic ad exchange company that is not providing intra-group services may be classified as a non-relevant entity; if it serves group affiliates as a service centre, it must satisfy the full economic substance test for that category. Failure triggers financial penalties and automatic exchange of information with foreign tax authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax-Exempt Status

Statutory sanction: Tax evasion and fraudulent false statement charges under Bermuda's tax and criminal law: administrative tax penalties and interest, criminal fines up to BMD 250,000, imprisonment up to five years, seizure of proceeds, and automatic exchange of information with OECD competent authorities.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors may be personally ordered to pay restitution and reasonable costs; they may face travel bans in Bermuda and the UK, disqualification from acting as directors, and custodial sentences where fraud is proved.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Act 2018: initial and daily default penalties (from BMD 10,000 per default), deregistration or strike-off, cancellation of tax-protection assurances, and mandatory exchange of information with foreign tax authorities; the entity may be dissolved and its Bermuda assets forfeited.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors can be disqualified, fined, ordered to repay diverted profits, and prohibited from re-entering Bermuda; international travel restrictions may follow if criminal proceedings or enforcement proceedings are active.
AML / CTF Red Flag

Statutory sanction: Violation of Bermuda's Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Framework (Proceeds of Crime Act, AML/ATF Regulations and the FIA Guidance): civil fines, confiscation, suspension or revocation of the exempted company status, and referral for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors and compliance officers are personally liable for attributable AML lapses; sanctions include personal fines, imprisonment up to five years, and permanent director disqualification.
Transfer Pricing / Profit-Shifting Abuse

Statutory sanction: If the group is within Pillar Two, Bermuda may assess additional top-up tax under the CIT Act, with interest and penalties; in the counterpart high-tax jurisdiction, recharacterisation of the pricing exposes the group to secondary adjustments, penalty taxes, and CFC/GloBE charges.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: approving directors can be sued for breach of fiduciary duty, negligent misstatement, and fraudulent conveyance; asset-recovery actions may attach personal bank accounts and travel bans may be imposed pending resolution.
