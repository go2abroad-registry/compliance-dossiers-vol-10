Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC), Government of Bermuda; administered via the Bermuda Tax Administration Portal (gov.bm). Customs duties are administered separately by the Bermuda Customs Department, Ministry of Finance.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1: Register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Bermuda Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 1981 (Exempted Partnerships Act 2015 for ELPs) to obtain the Certificate of Limited Partnership. Step 2: Apply for a Bermuda Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the OTC Tax Administration Portal; this is mandatory only if the ELP is a constituent entity of an in-scope multinational group (consolidated annual revenue ≥ EUR 750 million) under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CITA), with registration due within 30 days of the first day of its first CITA-relevant fiscal year. Step 3: For out-of-scope ELPs, obtain an employer payroll-tax registration (first employee triggers liability) and a Customs Importer/Exporter Code from the Bermuda Customs Department for physical movement of dental zirconia blocks and milling discs; no general income-tax registration applies to out-of-scope ELPs. Step 4: Register for any applicable sectoral licensing via the Ministry of Health dental materials notification procedure (ISIC 3250 medical-device-class consumables).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible

        
Available election forms
Under CITA, an ELP is fiscally transparent (flow-through) by default. The ELP may, with OTC consent, elect to be taxed as a separate taxable entity. For cross-border purposes, a reverse-hybrid election may be made under the OECD GloBE implementation rules to reclassify the ELP as a non-transparent constituent entity; domestic top-up tax elections under CITA sections 18-20 are also available. No check-the-box regime exists outside CITA; hybrid mismatch consequences follow OECD Pillar Two commentary.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
No VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use-Tax; Bermuda levies customs import duties (Customs Tariff Act 1968) and employer payroll tax (Payroll Tax Act 1995) as indirect-type levies

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
N/A for VAT/GST (no such tax). Customs registration is mandatory for all importers/exporters without a turnover threshold. Payroll-tax registration is triggered upon engaging the first employee. CIT registration applies only to multinational groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750,000,000 (approximately BMD 750,000,000); no de minimis threshold exists for ELPs falling within that group test.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For CIT-eligible ELPs, qualifying export income from substantive local manufacturing activities may benefit from the foreign-derived income exclusion and substance-based carve-out under CITA, subject to OECD GloBE rules.

No mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime. Import/export declarations are processed electronically through the Bermuda Customs single-administrative-document (SAD) portal; CIT filings and payments are made through the OTC Tax Administration Portal. Paper invoices remain legally valid, but customs records must be retained for 7 years for duty-audit purposes.

      
The OTC may assess and audit within 6 years of the return filing date (7 years for fraud, wilful neglect, or failure to file). Customs duty-drawback fraud triggers seizure/forfeiture of goods, immediate repayment of the drawback, and fines up to 200% of the duty evaded under the Customs Act 1941. CITA misstatements attract interest at 8.25% per annum and penalties of up to 50% of the understated CIT, with general partners of the ELP jointly and severally liable. Absent the mandatory documentation bundle, an export is rebuttably presumed to be a domestic supply, resulting in full duty clawback, loss of the foreign-derived income exclusion, and potential referral to the Bermuda Financial Intelligence Agency for suspected invoice fraud.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is legally pegged to the US Dollar at a fixed rate of 1:1, and the two currencies circulate interchangeably within Bermuda. For a designated non-resident Exempted Limited Partnership, capital and current account transactions are generally freely convertible, and profits, dividends, partner capital returns, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted without prior BMA exchange-control approval, provided the entity has secured and maintained its non-resident designation and is not carrying on business with Bermudian residents except as expressly permitted. The primary practical hurdles to profit remittance are not exchange-control rationing but rather robust AML/KYC documentation, demonstrated economic substance, clear source-of-funds records, and a stable banking relationship. If the ELP were inadvertently classified as resident or engaged in substantive local business activity with residents, the full exchange-control consent regime would apply, exposing remittances to prior BMA authorisation and conversion requirements. The treasury blueprint must therefore preserve the ELP's non-resident classification and document every cross-border capital flow to prevent inadvertent BMA resident status.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Bermuda does not impose mandatory surrender or conversion ratios on export proceeds for non-resident designated businesses. An Exempted Limited Partnership classified as non-resident may retain export receipts in the original currency, including USD, GBP, EUR, or other trade currencies, in Bermuda bank accounts without any statutory timeline for repatriation or forced conversion into BMD. For a resident-classified entity, the BMA retains exchange-control powers and may impose conversion obligations or transaction-specific consents in respect of Bermuda-source receipts. Since the target ELP manufactures and distributes biocompatible dental ceramic blocks and milling discs through a UK/EU digital trade gateway, all trade receivables should be held in operational USD/GBP/EUR accounts and converted only when commercially necessary. The compliance file should include the ELP's non-resident designation and a clear statement of the applicable no-surrender treatment for the bank's relationship review.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), exercising central bank, monetary, exchange control, and financial supervisory functions under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, the Exchange Control Act 1972, the Banks and Deposit Companies Act, and the AML/ATF regulatory framework.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary disbursement-only services, but Low as a primary treasury rail. Bermuda law does not establish a domestic electronic money institution (EMI) licensing category; an offshore digital EMI, such as a UK FCA-authorised electronic money institution, may lawfully hold fiat balances and provide GBP/EUR/USD IBANs, but it is not licensed or supervised by the BMA as a Bermuda deposit-taking entity, cannot issue BMD clearing accounts, cannot provide documentary letters of credit, and does not participate in the Bermuda banking system or deposit protection framework. The Exempted Limited Partnership should not enter into any arrangement under which an offshore EMI is used as the principal operational bank, because the BMA's AML/ATF expectations, the need for stable cross-border trade settlement, and the medical-device distribution profile require a fully regulated Bermudian bank. An offshore EMI may, however, be used as a limited parallel rail for low-value supplier payments in the UK/EU, subject to a written outsourcing risk assessment, balance caps, and full audit trail integration. The Digital Asset Business Act 2018 is not engaged by a fiat-based EMI current account, but unlicensed processor risk should be disclosed to the Bermuda relationship manager during onboarding.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Exempted Limited Partnership classified as non-resident, with the General Partner located outside Bermuda and the manufacturing operations executed through a UK or EU related entity, giving the appearance of offshore substance arbitrage rather than a genuine commercial enterprise.
        
Root cause: Exempted Limited Partnership classified as non-resident, with the General Partner located outside Bermuda and the manufacturing operations executed through a UK or EU related entity, giving the appearance of offshore substance arbitrage rather than a genuine commercial enterprise.

Root cause: High-frequency outbound payments to related-party dental CAD/CAM equipment suppliers and zirconia powder or block manufacturers, with the payment profile resembling trade-based money laundering or invoice fraud rather than genuine cross-border commercial trade.

Root cause: Use of a low-risk medical-sector classification while the final product is a biocompatible dental ceramic block, which may be treated as a medical device in certain jurisdictions and therefore trigger additional licensing, customs, and product-liability scrutiny during the AML review.

Include a narrative explaining the distribution route from manufacturing site to dental laboratory or milling centre, and demonstrate that all regulated medical-device obligations are actively managed by the UK gateway entity, thereby satisfying the Bermudian bank's sector-risk assessment and confirming the ELP as a commercially substantial, low-risk trade enterprise.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        All limited partner capital contributions and equity bridge funding must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or international TT to the ELP's dedicated Capital Escrow Account at the chosen Bermudian bank. Each inbound wire must include the ELP's legal name, the partner name, and a structured ISO 20022 purpose code of CAPI for capital payment or GDSV for goods-or-services related injection; the bank will not accept personal family remittance codes. The remitter must be a named, sanctioned-screened limited partner, and the ELP's relationship manager must be given advance written notice through a capital call letter, the updated partnership contribution schedule, and a source-of-wealth and source-of-funds questionnaire. For amounts above USD 50,000, the receiving bank will apply enhanced verification of the partner's bank account and expected transaction narrative. The BMA does not require a separate foreign investment approval for a non-resident ELP, but the ELP must maintain its non-resident designation and ensure that no resident Bermudian person carries on business through the partnership. Inbound funds remain segregated in the escrow or capital account until the compliance clearance notice is issued, after which they may be transferred to the operational account for trade settlement.Outbound disbursements to dental ceramic block and milling disc suppliers should be executed using SWIFT MT103 for individual confirmed wire payments, MT102 for batched low-value supplier remittances, and MT101 for customer-initiated corporate payment instructions. For high-value and repeatable supply contracts, the ELP should use an irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued under UCP600, payable against complying documents including commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, certificate of origin, and inspection certificate. The Bermudian bank will act as issuing bank and route the LC to a confirming bank in the supplier's jurisdiction; the ELP must pre-fund or provide a bank-approved credit line for LC cover. Because BMD is pegged 1:1 to USD, USD-denominated clearing can be executed domestically and through the bank's US correspondent network without any conversion loss, while GBP and EUR clearing should be conducted through dedicated sub-accounts to reduce FX conversion friction. All outbound payments must be permit-compliant, include the appropriate purpose code such as TRAD, SUPP, or GDSV, and be supported by the matched-invoice policy described in the AML/KYC defence dossier. Profit distributions to partners should be paid only from cleared operational funds and coded DIVI or CAPI in accordance with the bank's exchange-control classification.Deliverable outright FX forwards between BMD and USD at the fixed 1:1 peg, and between USD and GBP or EUR for tenors up to 12 months, documented under an ISDA 2002 master agreement or the bank's standard FX agreement.Cross-currency swaps between USD and GBP or EUR to match long-term dental supply contracts and distributor revenue streams, subject to the bank's credit approval and the ELP's underlying trade exposure.Plain-vanilla FX swaps combining a spot transaction with a forward reversal, used for short-term operational liquidity management in USD, GBP, and EUR.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in currencies for which no deliverable BMD or local bank market exists, provided the counterparty is a licensed bank and the BMA-regulated bank confirms that the NDF is not being used to circumvent exchange controls or to create speculative leverage unrelated to the ELP's commercial trade flows.

Bermuda law does not establish a domestic electronic money institution (EMI) licensing category; an offshore digital EMI, such as a UK FCA-authorised electronic money institution, may lawfully hold fiat balances and provide GBP/EUR/USD IBANs, but it is not licensed or supervised by the BMA as a Bermuda deposit-taking entity, cannot issue BMD clearing accounts, cannot provide documentary letters of credit, and does not participate in the Bermuda banking system or deposit protection framework. The Exempted Limited Partnership should not enter into any arrangement under which an offshore EMI is used as the principal operational bank, because the BMA's AML/ATF expectations, the need for stable cross-border trade settlement, and the medical-device distribution profile require a fully regulated Bermudian bank. An offshore EMI may, however, be used as a limited parallel rail for low-value supplier payments in the UK/EU, subject to a written outsourcing risk assessment, balance caps, and full audit trail integration. The Digital Asset Business Act 2018 is not engaged by a fiat-based EMI current account, but unlicensed processor risk should be disclosed to the Bermuda relationship manager during onboarding.
