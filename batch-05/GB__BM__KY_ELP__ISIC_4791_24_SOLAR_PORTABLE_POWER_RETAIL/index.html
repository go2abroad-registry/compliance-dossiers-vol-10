Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Revenue Authority (BRA), Government of Bermuda — statutory administration for payroll tax, land tax, stamp duty, and customs duty; entity registration is conducted by the Registrar of Companies, Ministry of Finance. Official portals: https://www.bra.bm (BRA) and https://www.gov.bm (Registrar of Companies).

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) obtains its statutory identity in Bermuda through registration with the Registrar of Companies, which issues a Certificate of Exempted Partnership and a unique Bermuda Company Number; no general-purpose tax identification number is issued because Bermuda imposes no income tax or VAT. If the ELP employs Bermuda-resident staff, it must register as an employer with the BRA and obtain a Payroll Tax Account Number. If the ELP imports or exports goods through Bermuda, it must obtain a customs client/account identification and file electronic declarations with BRA Customs. No VAT ID is obtainable or required in Bermuda. For US tax purposes, the ELP may obtain a US Employer Identification Number by filing IRS Form SS-4; for UK group reporting, the ELP is referenced by its legal entity identifier and Bermuda Company Number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the ELP is transparent for Bermudian tax purposes and Bermuda does not impose entity-level income, capital gains, or withholding taxes; profits are allocated to partners and taxed, if at all, in their respective residence jurisdictions. Bermuda operates a territorial revenue model: payroll tax, land tax, stamp duty, and customs duty apply only to persons, property, or transactions within Bermuda; offshore business income of an exempted partnership is outside the scope of Bermudian taxation.

Available election forms

Bermuda has no domestic check-the-box election, no entity classification election, and no hybrid mismatch election under Bermuda law. For foreign tax classification, the ELP may be treated as an eligible entity under US check-the-box rules (IRS Form 8832) to elect partnership, disregarded, or association status; under UK tax law an overseas partnership is generally transparent unless managed and controlled in the UK; hybrid mismatch rules under UK CTA 2010 Part 6A and OECD BEPS Action 2 may apply to neutralize inconsistent treatment of the ELP between the UK, Bermuda, and other jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Bermuda does not impose VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax; Bermuda levies customs duties on imported goods, payroll tax, land tax, and stamp duty as its principal fiscal exactions.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Bermuda dollars (BMD; pegged 1:1 to USD). There is no VAT/GST registration threshold because no such tax exists. Customs duty, assessed ad valorem by tariff heading generally up to 35% on the CIF value of imports, applies without turnover threshold; payroll tax registration applies only if the ELP employs Bermuda-resident staff.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda has no VAT/GST regime, so no VAT zero-rating claim is available or required. Exports of goods from Bermuda are outside the scope of Bermudian customs duty, which is chargeable only on importation. A customs duty drawback may be claimed where duty has been paid on imported inputs or finished goods subsequently exported; the ELP must prove that the dutiable goods were exported within the statutory period and are in the same or substantially transformed state. Offshore services supplied by a Bermuda ELP to non-residents are not subject to any Bermudian indirect tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed electronically with Bermuda Customs (export entry showing exporter, consignee, goods description, quantity, value, and tariff classification)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation of the goods from Bermuda
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bermuda Customs import entry and duty receipt evidencing duty paid at importation, required for any drawback claim
  • Inward Remittance / SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds (customary banking, AML, and export credit evidence; not a Bermudian statutory filing requirement)
  • Supplier declaration or Certificate of Origin where preferential tariff origin is claimed

Bermuda does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, tax authority API connectivity, or digital stamping. Customs declarations are filed electronically through the Bermuda Customs electronic single-window system; payroll and land tax returns are filed through the BRA online portal. No continuous transaction controls, SAF-T reporting, or government e-invoice clearance is required.

Bermuda Customs and the BRA may audit export entries, drawback claims, and customs documentation. Under the Customs Act 1952 (as amended), false or fraudulent declarations may result in forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties of up to treble the value of the goods or treble the duty evaded, and criminal prosecution for revenue officers conducting the proceedings. No VAT clawback can arise in Bermuda because no input VAT is levied. Where the ELP is consolidated into a UK group, UK anti-avoidance rules remain relevant, including the CFC regime and CTA 2010 Part 6A hybrid mismatch rules, and any artificial arrangement intended to secure unintended UK or EU VAT recovery on retail sales will be scrutinized by HMRC. Bermuda statute does not prescribe an FX repatriation deadline; 180 days is the conventional treasury, banking, and AML benchmark applied by counterparties and referenced in the pipeline schema.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates a fully convertible currency regime with the Bermudian dollar (BMD) pegged 1:1 to the US dollar (USD). Both current account and capital account transactions are unrestricted; no exchange control approvals are required for profit remittance, capital repatriation, or trade payments. Non-resident ELPs may freely convert BMD to USD and other major currencies. The only practical obstacles to dividend or profit distribution are partnership governance approvals, tax residency compliance, and anti-money laundering (AML) verification of beneficial owners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement under Bermuda law to surrender export proceeds to the BMA or any domestic bank. Export receipts may be held in foreign currency accounts, used directly for trade settlements, or converted at market rates. The United Kingdom similarly imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion ratios on export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as the competent foreign exchange and banking regulator for Bermuda; Bank of England (BoE) exercises sovereign monetary oversight within the United Kingdom's Overseas Territories framework.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda transfer-pricing or documentation requirement applies to a standalone ELP below the OECD Pillar Two threshold. A Master File and Local File are mandatory only when the Bermuda entity is part of an MNE group with consolidated revenue above approximately BMD 850 million (EUR 750 million). Even below the threshold, contemporaneous records of intercompany pricing for online retail sales and IP royalties are recommended for UK CFC, customs, and global minimum tax review.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda’s Economic Substance Act 2018 applies. A pure e-commerce retailer using a Bermuda ELP as a pass-through invoice platform must file a nil ESR declaration and demonstrate that no core income-generating activities, employees, offices, or local management exist in Bermuda. If the ELP is centrally managed and controlled in Bermuda, or operates a Bermuda warehouse and distribution team, it becomes subject to full economic substance requirements with annual board meetings and locally performed CIGAs.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Zero-Return

Statutory sanction: Bermuda tax and customs authorities may impose penalties up to BMD 10,000 per filing, add two times the unpaid duty or tax, and bring criminal prosecution; repeat offenders are subject to automatic information exchange.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are personally liable for civil restitution, disqualification, a court-issued travel ban, and imprisonment for up to five years for knowingly making false declarations.
Shell Company Abuse / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Registrar strike-off, revocation of the exempted partnership certificate, punitive annual return penalties, and reclassification of the entity as a general partnership with unlimited liability.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced; directors and the general partner face joint and several liability for the partnership’s unpaid debts, asset freeze orders, and a travel ban pending restoration of corporate records.
Anti-Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties imposed by the Bermuda Monetary Authority, confiscation of transaction-linked assets, deregistration, and criminal prosecution under the Proceeds of Crime legislation.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any officer with knowledge or recklessness may be personally fined, banned from holding financial service roles, sentenced to imprisonment for up to 14 years, and subject to full civil asset forfeiture.
Transfer Pricing Adjustment / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: UK HMRC may recharacterize the arrangement, deny deductions, and apply CFC attribution; under Pillar Two, a 15% top-up tax plus interest and penalties up to 20%–40% of the adjusted profit may be assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as officers in default, exposing them to joint and several liability for the exact amount of the underpaid tax and penalties, together with travel restrictions pending resolution of the assessment.
