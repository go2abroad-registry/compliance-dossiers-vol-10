Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Government Ministry of Finance / Office of the Tax Commissioner (for payroll tax, social insurance, and corporate income tax under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024); Bermuda Customs and Excise Department (for customs duties and import/export controls).

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Bermuda Exempted Company are first incorporated via the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 1981, obtaining a unique company registration number. For ongoing compliance, the entity must register for payroll tax with the Office of the Tax Commissioner (if it becomes an employer) and, where within scope, for Bermuda's 15% corporate income tax regime under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024. There is no general VAT/GST registration because Bermuda imposes no such tax. A Bermuda Tax Identification Number is issued only upon payroll tax, social insurance, or corporate income tax registration; otherwise the company number serves as the statutory identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda does not operate a U.S.-style 'check-the-box' pass-through election. A Bermuda Exempted Company is, by default, subject to the territory's tax-exempt status with respect to ordinary offshore income, unless it becomes liable under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 for a qualifying multinational group (annual consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more). No hybrid mismatch elections are available locally; the regime follows OECD BEPS principles. Limited transitional asset and election notices exist under the Economic Substance (Substance Requirements) Act 2018, and foreign tax credit pooling elections are permitted under the Corporate Income Tax Rules for entities within the CIT net.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax system; indirect taxation is limited to Bermuda Customs Duties, stamp duties, and specific excise taxes.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not Applicable — no VAT/GST accounting registration threshold exists. Any person or entity importing goods into Bermuda must obtain a Business Licence and register with the Bermuda Customs and Excise Department, with no minimum turnover threshold for customs duty purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not Applicable — Bermuda has no VAT/GST, hence there is no 0% export VAT category. Goods exported from Bermuda are outside the scope of Bermuda customs duty, subject to submission of a valid export declaration and physical departure evidence. Services supplied from Bermuda to foreign parties (e.g., patent prosecution, trademark registration) are substantially offshore and fall outside Bermuda's domestic customs duty or payroll tax base; no indirect tax refund mechanism is required.

  • Bermuda Customs Export Declaration (electronic CAS submission)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Bermuda
  • Commercial Invoice describing goods/services and value
  • Packing List (for goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice evidencing repatriation of exports proceeds to Bermuda within exchange-control deadlines

Bermuda imposes no real-time VAT e-invoicing or digital stamping regime. Customs clearance is administered through the Customs Automated Services (CAS) electronic system, requiring electronic import/export declarations, electronic cargo manifests, and duty payment via approved clearing banks. A customs-issued digital receipt and clearance notice are generated upon each transaction; no government-owned continuous fiscal clearance API is mandated.

Bermuda does not operate an export zero-rating refund system, therefore fraud-specific VAT clawbacks are inapplicable. However, misdeclaration, false representation, or attempted customs duty evasion in connection with exported goods are subject to investigation by the Bermuda Customs and Excise Department, with penalties under the Customs Act 1968: maximum fine of BD$10,000 plus three times the value of evaded duty, forfeiture of goods and vehicles, suspension or revocation of business licence, and imprisonment for up to two years. Retrospective duty assessments and clawback of granted duty relief or export entitlements may be made for up to six years after the date of offence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates a managed exchange control regime; the Bermudian dollar is pegged at 1:1 to the United States dollar. Exempted companies designated as non-resident for exchange control purposes may maintain foreign currency accounts freely, transfer capital and profits abroad without individual BMA consent, and are effectively fully convertible within the non-resident designation. Current account transactions for resident-designated entities are generally unrestricted, while capital account transactions require prior BMA approval. Profit remittance is structurally unimpeded for an exempted company with non-resident status, but any change in resident designation or direct or indirect ownership by Bermuda-resident persons may trigger consent requirements. The patent prosecution agency should maintain its non-resident designation and hold operating accounts in USD, GBP, and EUR to minimize conversion friction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda does not impose a mandatory statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for surrender of export proceeds on non-resident designated entities. The BMA may require resident persons to offer specified foreign currency proceeds for purchase within a prescribed period under Exchange Control regulations, but exempted companies designated as non-resident are excluded from such surrender obligations. Proceeds from patent prosecution agency fees and official fee disbursement reimbursements may be retained in the original currency in which received, and no conversion to Bermudian dollars is required as long as the non-resident designation remains valid.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) acting under the Bermuda Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High for ancillary payment routing but limited as the primary banking backbone. BMA-licensed fintech and electronic money institutions operating under the Digital Asset Business Act 2018 or the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 can support cross-border fee collections via Wise, Currencycloud, and similar regulated payment facilitators. However, Bermuda Exempted Companies seeking tier-1 correspondent banking credibility for large patent office wires and treasury settlement are advised to combine a domestic commercial bank account with an EMI for low-value disbursements. Unregulated offshore digital EMIs without a BMA license or equivalent EEA/FCA authorization are not viable for holding client trust funds or official fee escrows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inward transfers from the UK parent shall be executed through SWIFT MT103 with the remitter being the UK parent entity, not a personal account. For equity injections, the payment reference must state 'Share capital contribution to Bermuda Exempted Company' and the beneficiary account shall be designated as the Capital Escrow Account or a non-resident operational account. For shareholder loans, the purpose code should reference 'Intercompany loan' with the loan agreement available for BMA scrutiny if requested. All inbound wires must be supported by a board resolution of the Bermuda exempted company, and the exchange control designation 'non-resident' must be evidenced to the receiving bank. Where the UK parent uses a UK clearing bank, the routing should be via CHAPS to the UK correspondent, then through the SWIFT network to the Bermuda beneficiary bank. The practice of routing through Wise Business for capital injections should be avoided for sums exceeding USD 25,000; use direct SWIFT to preserve a transparent audit trail.Official patent office fees and international trademark registration fees shall be disbursed primarily via SWIFT MT103 from the Bermuda operational account to designated receivers such as the World Intellectual Property Organization, the UK Intellectual Property Office, the European Patent Office, or the United States Patent and Trademark Office. For low-value and recurring official fees, a regulated EMI such as Wise may be used, provided the underlying transaction is recorded with the invoice and WIPO/payment reference. Commercial letters of credit are not applicable to patent prosecution agency disbursements and should not be used, as the underlying transaction is a fee transfer rather than goods trade. For intra-group settlements, the Bermuda entity may use MT103 or internal book transfers if the counterparty bank participates in the same network; for non-participating banks, use correspondent clearing via a US-dollar settlement bank because the Bermudian dollar is USD-pegged and most clearing is effectively dollar-denominated.FX forwards in USD/BMD, USD/GBP, and USD/EUR executed with a BMA-licensed bank or an authorized non-resident bank counterparty, limited to covering known official fee liability timelines.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in Bermudian dollars are generally unnecessary because the BMD/USD peg is fixed at 1:1 and freely convertible for non-resident entities; NDFs may be used only if a counterparty requires linear exposure to isolated BMD liquidity stress.Cross-currency swap agreement between GBP- and USD-denominated intercompany balances with the UK parent to hedge multi-currency cash flow mismatch arising from UK operating costs and Bermuda patent fee payments.Plain-vanilla currency options to cap GBP/USD conversion cost when anticipating surges in PCT filing volumes invoiced in GBP or EUR, subject to board-approved treasury policy.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 0 — Bermuda has no domestic transfer pricing legislation and imposes no statutory Local File or Master File documentation threshold; nevertheless, the Economic Substance Rules 2019 require that any relevant activity be supported by real substance, and any intercompany arrangement must be commercially rational to survive foreign tax-authority attack.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018 and Economic Substance Rules 2019 transpose the OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices. A patent and trademark prosecution agency carrying on the 'Provision of Services' or 'Intellectual Property Business' relevant activity must satisfy two tests: (i) direction and management must occur in Bermuda with board meetings held at appropriate intervals in Bermuda; and (ii) core income generating activities (CIGA) must be performed in Bermuda using adequate premises, full-time employees, and operating expenditure. For pure IP holding, the CIGA are specifically the acquisition, exploitation, management, administration, protection, and enforcement of the IP, and these cannot be outsourced to a non-Bermuda service provider without destroying substance. The company must file the annual ESR, maintain records for at least 6 years, and, for in-scope IP entities, demonstrate that product development, up-keep costs, and business decisions occur in Bermuda. Failure is treated as evasion of the substance doctrine and triggers penalties and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Economic Substance Failure / Shell Company Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to BMD 50,000 per violation under the Economic Substance Act 2018, additional daily penalty of BMD 500 per day of continuing default, and a direction that the Registrar strike off or wind up the company. False declarations attract further fines of up to BMD 10,000 per false statement.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated compliance officer who knowingly sign false ESR declarations face personal fines up to BMD 50,000 and imprisonment of up to 2 years; directors also face disqualification orders under the Companies Act 1981, restriction orders, and civil restitution in a winding-up for assets transferred in breach of substance obligations. Travel bans and asset freezes can be imposed under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997.
Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Statements

Statutory sanction: Under Bermuda criminal law, evasion via false documentation attracts fines on indictment without statutory ceiling and imprisonment up to 5 years; the Tax Administration Act 2023 adds civil penalties of BMD 25,000 for inaccurate returns plus daily default penalties of BMD 25,000 per month. Confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 may be made equivalent to the alleged tax benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors are individually liable where fraud was committed with their knowledge or connivance; sentencing courts impose personal fines equal to the evaded amount, asset forfeiture, disqualification from managing companies for up to 15 years, prohibition on leaving Bermuda, and custodial sentences. The corporate veil is pierced under section 180 of the Companies Act 1981.
Anti-Money Laundering Breach / Criminal Property

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 and the AML/CFT Regulations 2008, a body corporate convicted of money laundering faces an unlimited fine; designated non-financial businesses failing to maintain AML controls face penalties up to BMD 250,000 per breach. Knowingly laundering criminal property is punishable by imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who expressly or tacitly approve the conversion of criminal funds are personally liable as principals or secondary parties; sanctions include imprisonment of up to 14 years, disgorgement of all fees received, aggravated financial penalties, travel bans, and seizure of personal assets. The global nature of patent payments increases the extraterritorial reach of UK and US prosecutions.
Beneficial Ownership Register Default

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to BMD 10,000 and a further daily penalty of BMD 1,000 for continued non-compliance; the Registrar may suspend or strike off the exempted company.

Director personal liability: Each director who fails to take reasonable steps to maintain the beneficial ownership register commits an offence and is liable to a fine of up to BMD 10,000, imprisonment up to 6 months, and disqualification; deliberate concealment for tax-avoidance purposes elevates the offence to fraud and engages the same confiscation and travel-ban sanctions as tax evasion.
Transfer Mispricing / Sham Intercompany Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Bermuda itself has no TP penalty, but the Bermuda Tax Commissioner and Registrar treat the absence of arm's length rationale as evidence of a sham and will impose ESR penalties and strike-off; the foreign parent faces primary adjustments, secondary adjustments, denying deductions, and penalty taxes under UK CFC rules, US GILTI/FDII, and OECD pillar two where the profit sits in a 0% jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Foreign tax authorities and Bermuda liquidators may name directors for receiving distributions sourced from sham arrangements; directors are liable for fraudulent trading under the Companies Act 1981 if trades are carried on with intent to defraud foreign revenue authorities, with personal liability to contribute to assets, disqualification, and extradition exposure under bilateral assistance treaties.
Sanctions and International Conduct Red Flag

Statutory sanction: Under UK Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 applied to Bermuda and the OFSI monetary framework, a corporate breaching financial sanctions faces a fine of up to 50% of the value of the transaction or GBP 1 million per breach, whichever is higher; breach of trade sanctions is a strict-liability criminal offence.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or negligently execute sanctioned patent work face imprisonment up to 7 years for deliberate evasion; personal monetary penalties up to GBP 1 million; travel bans; asset freezing orders; and debarment from professional patent law bodies. The Bermuda courts cooperate with international freezing orders and anti-evasion warrants.
