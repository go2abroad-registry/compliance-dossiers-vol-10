Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Ministry of Finance – Office of the Tax Commissioner (tax.gov.bm); Registrar of Companies (roc.gov.bm) for entity registration; Bermuda Customs Department for customs duties.

E-government filing portal

A foreign general partner forms the ELP through a licensed Bermuda registered agent by filing the Exempted Limited Partnership Act 1883 application with the Registrar of Companies. The approved ELP receives a Certificate of Registration and a registration number. As Bermuda imposes no VAT, no general corporate income tax, and no sales tax, no TIN or VAT ID is issued automatically for ordinary ELPs. If the ELP has Bermuda-resident employees, it must register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner for payroll tax and obtain an employer account reference. If the ELP is a constituent entity of an in-scope multinational group with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750 million, it must register under Bermuda's corporate income tax regime and obtain a CIT/TIN account before the relevant filing deadline.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Bermuda has no check-the-box election and no domestic hybrid-mismatch election. An ELP is tax-transparent by default in Bermuda; no entity-level tax filing is required for ordinary partnerships. Foreign classification, for example as a corporation or partnership under U.S. or UK tax law, is determined by the partner's domestic entity-classification rules. Where mismatched transparency gives rise to hybrid mismatch outcomes, UK anti-hybrid rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A may apply; Bermuda does not provide a corrective election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax (Bermuda levies only customs duties, stamp duties and certain transaction-specific fees)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – BMD 0; no Bermudian indirect tax turnover threshold exists. UK VAT registration threshold of GBP 90,000 applies only if the ELP creates a UK taxable establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No local export zero-rating claim exists because Bermuda has no VAT/GST. The ELP's LegalTech SaaS and e-signature services are intangible digital services; when exported from Bermuda to non-UK customers, no Bermudian indirect tax is chargeable. If the ELP is within the UK VAT net, supplies of cloud document automation services to non-UK customers are outside the scope of UK VAT by place-of-supply rules rather than zero-rated; supplies to UK customers attract 20% VAT unless a statutory exception applies.

  • No customs export declaration, bill of lading, or inward remittance certificate required for purely digital SaaS/e-signature services.
  • Commercial invoice and subscription pricing statement describing the platform services.
  • Signed legal contract automation agreement identifying customer jurisdiction and contracting counterparty.
  • Electronic delivery evidence, including IP geolocation and user access logs.
  • Stripe and credit-card payment settlement reports evidencing consideration received.

Bermuda has no electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API reporting mandate. If the ELP later becomes UK VAT-registered, HMRC's Making Tax Digital regime requires digital record-keeping and digital submission of VAT returns, but does not require real-time invoice clearance.

Bermuda has no VAT/export refund regime, so no local clawback risk arises for zero-rating. If UK VAT treatment is misapplied, HMRC may assess understated VAT plus interest and penalties, including penalties up to 100% of the VAT for deliberately concealed non-compliance; the general partner may be jointly and severally liable. Retrospective clawback of input VAT may also occur if digital services are used for exempt or non-business purposes.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates a fixed exchange-rate regime, with the Bermudian Dollar (BMD) pegged 1:1 to the US Dollar. For an exempted partnership designated as 'non-resident', both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible; there are no exchange-control approval steps for subscription of foreign capital, payment of trade invoices, or distribution of profits. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to documentary compliance: the ELP must file or retain audited financial statements if required, a capital account ledger, and any tax authority declarations (e.g., UK CFC or other annual tax filings) to demonstrate that no Bermuda-source income arises. Because the ELP is pass-through eligible, distributions are not subject to Bermudian withholding tax; the only practical hurdle is bank-level source-of-funds verification and beneficial ownership disclosure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For resident exporters, the Exchange Control Act 1972 and subordinate regulations require that export proceeds be repatriated to an authorised dealer and offered for conversion into BMD within six months of receipt, with the mandatory conversion ratio generally set at 100% unless an exemption is granted by the BMA. A non-resident ELP is not a resident exporter: receipts from legal-tech SaaS licensing and contract-automation services are treated as invisible earnings and may be retained in any convertible currency, typically USD, without any compulsory surrender or conversion. In practice, Bermuda-licensed banks may request that USD-denominated receipts be credited to a separate USD account and kept distinct from BMD operating accounts for clear regulatory reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) — central bank and exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for UK-based EMIs licensed by the FCA and Medium for EU/EEA EMIs passporting under PSD2; Low for offshore digital EMIs without a substantive banking or e-money licence. Bermuda's Digital Asset Business Act 2018 does not provide an EMI licensing regime for non-resident partnerships; therefore, an offshore digital EMI cannot legally hold custodial escrow in BMD/USD on behalf of an ELP. Material legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not deposit-taking institutions; (ii) capital contributions intended for the ELP must not be parked with an EMI beyond the immediate settlement cycle; (iii) EMIs may be used only for low-value operational collections such as SaaS subscription fees, not for capital injections or outbound trade settlements above USD 100,000.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use direct SWIFT MT103 from a UK-regulated bank to a licensed Bermudian authorised dealer, with the ELP's legal name, bank reference, and purpose code clearly stated: for capital subscription use 'Equity Capital Contribution' (IMF BOP code 52B or ISO 20022 category „EQUI'); for intercompany shareholder loans use 'Intercompany Loan' (IMF BOP code 231). Before the first drawdown, the ELP must submit a Capital Injection Letter to the receiving bank and comply with BMA's exchange-control transaction threshold of USD 100,000. For amounts equal to or above USD 1,000,000, the bank may require pre-advice, a board resolution, and a legal opinion confirming the availability of funds. The ELP must maintain a capital call ledger and coordinate with its registered agent to ensure the subscription is recorded as partner capital under Bermuda's Exempted Partnerships Act 2009.For outbound service payments relating to legal contract automation, the primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'Operating Invoice Payment' and supporting contract references. For high-value settlement of hardware or embedded trade-related components, a Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) may be issued through HSBC Bank Bermuda or Butterfield, although an irrevocable LC is rarely necessary for a pure LegalTech SaaS model. Local Bermuda disbursements should use the domestic ACH rails; cross-border USD clearing should leverage the BMD/USD parity through correspondent relationships with US Federal Reserve member banks.
