Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Under the Companies Act 1981, no minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for incorporation of an exempted company. However, to obtain an Electronic Money Institution licence from the Bermuda Monetary Authority, the company must maintain minimum paid-up share capital of at least USD 50,000, of which at least USD 12,500 must be deposited into an escrow account before the licence is granted.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner (Bermuda Ministry of Finance); official portal: Bermuda Tax Administration 'TAM Online' eServices at https://www.taxation.gov.bm. Customs duties are administered by the Bermuda Customs Department under the Ministry of Finance; the Registrar of Companies administers corporate registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Bermuda Exempted Company is first incorporated under the Companies Act 1981 and receives a company registration number; no general income tax TIN is issued to out-of-scope entities. The statutory procurement procedure is: (1) incorporate the Exempted Company via a Bermuda licensed corporate service provider and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation; (2) if the company will have employees, register as an employer for payroll tax and social insurance (pension/health) and obtain a payroll tax account number through TAM Online; (3) if importing goods, register with the Bermuda Customs Department for an importer code; (4) if the entity is part of an MNE group with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million and is within scope of the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 (effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025), the entity must obtain a corporate income tax identification number from the Tax Commissioner; (5) annually file the exempted company return and pay the business fee to remain in good standing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda law does not provide a 'check-the-box' or generic pass-through election. Statutory elections for in-scope multinational groups include: (a) election to adopt a fiscal year aligned with the ultimate parent's reporting period for Corporate Income Tax purposes under the CIT Act 2024; (b) international tax relief and transitional safe harbour elections available under Bermuda's GloBE/CIT implementation. Entities not forming part of an in-scope MNE group require no election because they remain outside Bermuda corporate income tax. There is no hybrid mismatch election under Bermuda domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/sales and use tax in Bermuda. The relevant indirect tax is the Bermuda customs import duty; financial services, including virtual IBAN issuance and digital wallet reserve management, are outside the scope of indirect taxation. Bermuda has not implemented an equivalent of EU VAT.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST/sales tax because Bermuda levies none. For customs purposes, any person or entity importing goods into Bermuda must be registered with Bermuda Customs and submit import entries; there is no de minimis turnover threshold, although low-value consignments may be exempt administratively.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating as a VAT concept is not applicable because Bermuda has no VAT/GST. Exported services provided by a Bermuda Exempted Company to non-Bermudian counterparties (e.g., virtual IBAN issuance, SEPA credit payment execution, and digital wallet reserve management) are not subject to any Bermuda indirect tax and any non-Bermuda income from qualifying offshore business is generally outside Bermuda corporate income tax. Eligibility for this tax-exempt treatment is conditioned on the entity maintaining valid exempted company status, satisfying economic substance requirements (which for a pure equity holding or financing activity require minimum substance in Bermuda), paying annual government fees, and, if in-scope for CIT, complying with the CIT Act 2024. For goods exported out of Bermuda, no Bermuda export duty is imposed.

  • Bermuda Customs Export Declaration / electronic export entry (where goods are exported; not normally applicable to pure financial services)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from Bermuda
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign sales consideration
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Exempted Company Certificate of Incorporation and validity certificate for tax-status proof

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control is imposed by Bermuda law. Tax compliance filings (payroll tax, corporate income tax where applicable, social insurance) are made via the Bermuda Tax Administration's digital portal, and customs declarations are filed electronically through the Bermuda Customs cargo system.

Bermuda does not operate an export VAT zero-rating or refund regime; therefore the export fraud penalties that exist in VAT jurisdictions are not directly mirrored. The applicable enforcement framework includes: (a) under the Customs Act 1948, customs offences can lead to penalties of up to three times the duty value, forfeiture of goods, and criminal sanctions; (b) under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024, in-scope entities face default assessments, interest at the statutory rate, penalties up to 10% of unpaid corporate income tax and, for fraud or neglect, further criminal fines; (c) under the Economic Substance Act 2018, failure to satisfy substance requirements may incur initial and escalated civil penalties and possible provision of information to the UK tax authorities; (d) misrepresentations to the Tax Commissioner may trigger retrospective recharacterisation and loss of the exempted status assurance. The 180-day FX repatriation benchmark in this dossier is retained as a conservative compliance control; Bermuda imposes no statutory inward remittance deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar (USD) and is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account purposes for non-resident entities such as a Bermuda Exempted Company. Following the 1997 exchange-control liberalisation administered by the BMA, no prior approval is required for foreign-currency financing, share subscriptions, loan repayments, dividend distributions, or profit repatriation to non-resident shareholders. The only residual hurdles are: (i) all Bermuda-resident-to-resident transactions must be settled in BMD through a BMA-authorised dealer; (ii) an entity classified as 'resident' for exchange-control purposes must channel outward remittances through an authorised dealer; and (iii) the BMA retains statistical reporting powers for balance-of-payments monitoring. For the EMI's digital-wallet reserve float, foreign currency (USD, GBP, EUR) may be held offshore without mandatory conversion, provided the segregated client-money ledger is reconciled at least daily and audited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for Bermuda Exempted Companies. The Exchange Control Regulations ceased imposing compulsory surrender of export receipts in 1997. A non-resident Exempted Company is entirely free of any obligation to convert USD or other foreign-currency export receipts into BMD. A Bermuda-resident commercial entity (including an operating EMI subsidiary that is deemed resident for exchange-control purposes) is required to settle export invoices through a licensed authorised dealer and may be subject to BMA statistical return obligations, but retains 100% of the foreign-currency proceeds, and may use them directly for trade settlement without a BMA conversion order. All interbank BMD-denominated settlements must clear through the local Bermuda clearing infrastructure.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) — the statutory central bank, currency board, and exchange-control administrator of the British Overseas Territory of Bermuda; Bank of England (BE) is the sovereign-level systemic authority for sterling clearing in the UK context, while HM Treasury retains ultimate oversight of the offshore territory's financial stability and sanctions framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-CONDITIONAL. The Bermuda banking market does not recognise a UK FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institution (under the E-Money Regulations 2011) as a licensed deposit-taker; therefore an offshore digital EMI cannot directly access BMA central-bank clearing or Bermuda's settlement-account system. A Bermuda Exempted Company may achieve cross-border viability through one of two compliant routes: (a) establish a segregated settlement account with a Bermuda-licensed bank (e.g., Butterfield) under a 'bank-as-a-service' white-label arrangement, whereby the bank holds the pooled client-money reserve and the EMI issues virtual IBANs; or (b) passport into the UK/EEA through the FCA Temporary Permissions Regime or obtain full FCA EMI authorisation, enabling direct SEPA Credit and Faster Payments access. Legal constraints: the BMA's Digital Asset Business Act 2018 covers any incidental digital-asset holdings and imposes a separate licence; local banks impose enhanced due diligence on cross-border virtual-IBAN aggregators and may deny direct nostro access; Bermuda's Fintech Innovation Framework requires a Regulatory Sandbox or a restricted-activity 'e-money issuer' permission before any principal-to-principal clearing relationship is approved. The commercially most viable path is a hybrid: a Bermudian Exempted Company holding client funds in a Butterfield segregated trust account, with a UK FCA-authorised EMI subsidiary as the SEPA access point.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder-loan injections from the foreign parent are wired via SWIFT MT103 to the Bermuda bank's BIC (e.g., NTBLBMBM for Butterfield; CLBNBMBM for Clarien), with the narrative referencing 'Share Subscription / Capital Contribution' and the Bermuda Exempted Company registration number. GBP injections can clear through CHAPS or Faster Payments from a London nostro of the Bermuda bank and are then translated into BMD/USD at the pegged rate. EUR injections use SEPA Credit Transfer (pain.001 ISO 20022) via the Bermuda bank's European correspondent, referencing the capital-escrow account. The wire must be preceded by a board resolution authorising the issuance, a Certificate of Incumbency, a source-of-funds questionnaire, and the BMA exchange-control notification (where the company is exchange-control resident). The funds are parked in the Capital Escrow Account and released to the Operational Account only after the bank's compliance department and the BMA's investment/remittance approval (where applicable) have been obtained.Outbound disbursements are executed over the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for standard third-party beneficiary payments in USD/GBP/EUR; (ii) SWIFT MT202 COV for interbank reimbursement when correspondent cover is required; (iii) documentary commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP600 for trade settlements; (iv) SEPA Credit Transfers and SEPA Instant for EUR-denominated wallet disbursements within the EEA; (v) the Bermuda Automated Clearing House (ACH) for BMD-denominated local payments; and (vi) New York CHIPS/Fedwire via a US correspondent for high-value USD wires. The EMI's virtual-IBAN clients are served through a white-label clearing arrangement: inbound funds are collected into a pooled segregated client account at the Bermuda bank, and outbound instructions are transmitted via the bank's host-to-host ISO 20022 API. The BMA's settlement risk policy requires all interbank BMD payments to be pre-funded or settled on a real-time gross basis within the local clearing window; EUR and GBP settlement risk is mitigated through correspondent banks with SWIFT gpi tracking.Vanilla deliverable FX forwards in USD/BMD/GBP/EUR with tenors up to 12 months, executed with the Bermuda host bank or a London prime FX counterparty under an ISDA master agreement.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) used solely for emerging-market currency exposures arising in the EMI's foreign payment corridors (e.g., INR, NGN, BRL) where local deliverability is restricted; settled on a net basis in USD or BMD.Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS) covering the notional and interest-rate mismatch between USD/BMD-denominated reserve assets and EUR/GBP-denominated electronic-money liabilities.FX options and zero-cost collars purchased in the interbank market to protect the GBP/EUR payment corridors from adverse rate movement without margin-call exposure.Central-counterparty-cleared FX swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps through a UK-licensed bank (e.g., via London Clearing House) permitted only where the underlying exposure is a verified client-money float or a trade receivable; BMA does not operate a domestic derivatives clearing house.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no corporate income tax on an Exempted Company holding a valid Tax Exemption Certificate; an in-scope multinational group (consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million) becomes subject to the 15% Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 rate for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda levies no withholding tax on dividends paid by an Exempted Company to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual Return due no later than 31 January in each year, reflecting information as at 1 January; Economic Substance Declaration due within 6 months after the financial year-end (30 June for a 31 December year-end); nil payroll tax returns due quarterly where no Bermuda staff are engaged.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 15 months after the end of the group's fiscal year (e.g., 31 March 2026 for a 31 December 2025 year-end), with the 15% preliminary CIT payment obligation on the same timetable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory Local File or Master File threshold applies in Bermuda's zero-tax regime; prudent arm's-length documentation under OECD TP guidelines is recommended once related-party financing, royalty, or treasury flows exceed BD$5,000,000 annually; in-scope MNE groups subject to the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million) must maintain full GloBE booking records and OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 (as amended 2019) applies: an Exempted Company carrying on a relevant activity — including financing and leasing (any credit facility or financing arrangement, which covers EMI treasury and credit functions) or holding business — must satisfy management-and-direction tests in Bermuda, adequate premises and employees, and CIGA performance in Bermuda. A pure exempted holding vehicle faces reduced substance requirements, but an EMI financing and digital-wallet treasury operation must register annually, file the Economic Substance Declaration, and evidence local board control and headcount; UK/EEA-outsourced operations must be carefully ring-fenced to avoid a failed ESR declaration.
