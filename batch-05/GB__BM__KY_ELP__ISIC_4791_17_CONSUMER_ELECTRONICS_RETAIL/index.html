Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda: Ministry of Finance - Office of the Tax Commissioner and Corporate Income Tax (CIT) Unit (portal: https://www.gov.bm/corporate-income-tax; filing portal: Bermuda Tax Administration online services) for CIT, payroll tax and social insurance registration; Registrar of Companies, Government of Bermuda (portal: https://www.roc.gov.bm) for all Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) formation and annual compliance under the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992. United Kingdom overlay: HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) (portal: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc; VAT registration service via Government Gateway at https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration) for UK VAT, corporation tax, and Making Tax Digital (MTD) affairs whenever a UK taxable nexus is triggered by the pipeline's online retail of consumer electronics and smart gadgets.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Bermuda ELP formation: file with the Registrar of Companies the Declaration of General Partner, the partnership particulars (name, principal place of business, nature of business, general partner details), and the consent/undertaking of the general partner; obtain the Certificate of Registration. No minimum capital, no statutory license for ISIC 4791 online retail, and no requirement to file the limited partner register publicly. Step 2 - Tax identification: Bermuda issues no general income-tax TIN because the territory imposes no income, capital gains, withholding, or corporation tax on ordinary or exempted undertakings. A Bermuda CIT Tax Registration Number (TRN) must be obtained from the Tax Commissioner only where the ELP is an 'included person' within the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 - i.e., part of a multinational enterprise group with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more, with registration due within 30 days of the entity falling within scope; small and mid-market online retail structures are outside this regime. Step 3 - Employment taxes: if the ELP has Bermuda-resident employees (only relevant to a Bermuda staffed back-office), register for Bermuda payroll tax and social insurance; a pure online reseller with no Bermuda employees and no Bermuda premises will have no such obligation. Step 4 - UK VAT registration: a non-established (Bermuda-resident) taxable person selling goods to UK consumers must register with HMRC immediately upon the first supply of goods consigned directly from abroad to UK consumers when the consignment value does not exceed GBP 135 (zero-threshold compulsory registration for direct sellers), or when the rolling 12-month UK taxable turnover reaches GBP 90,000 for other UK supplies; the application is made online using the non-established registration path (legacy form VAT1C, now digitised under MTD), yielding a GB VAT number. Step 5 - Payment rail alignment: embed the Bermuda ELP registration number, VAT number (once obtained), and corporate KYC pack with Stripe, PayPal, and the chosen EMI banking provider; settlement currencies must be repatriated to the ELP's operating account and the inward remittance evidence retained for UK export zero-rating audits and for any Bermuda CIT/GloBE file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent for Bermuda income tax, CIT and, by extension, UK tax (ITTOIA 2005 part 9 applies to UK-resident partners). Bermuda taxes no entity-level income, no capital gains, no distributions, and no non-resident partner income; the ELP is therefore a Tax_Exempt_Zone resident vehicle with Territorial_Scope limited to Bermuda-source activities (which, for an offshore e-commerce reseller, are nil). Under the UK overlay, the ELP is not a company and is outside UK corporation tax unless it carries on a trade through a UK permanent establishment (e.g., a UK fulfilment warehouse), in which case UK corporation tax applies at 19% (profits up to GBP 50,000, small profits rate band) or 25% (main rate, profits above GBP 250,000) with marginal relief between bands. Bermuda's new 15% CIT applies only to large multinational groups and is structurally inapplicable to the instant consumer-electronics retail ELP, but remains relevant to the compliance monitoring file.

Available election forms

(a) Bermuda CIT Act 2023, section 6 partnership election: an ELP may elect to be treated as a separate taxable person rather than as a look-through pass-through for Bermuda CIT purposes - relevant only if the ELP is nonetheless inside the EUR 750m group threshold; the default and recommended position for the instant structure is no election, preserving full transparency. (b) Exempted Partnerships Act 1992, section 5 election for overseas partnerships: permits a partnership formed abroad to register as an exempted partnership in Bermuda and obtain a certificate of exemption, thereby securing the statutory 0% tax guarantee and exemption from exchange control. (c) Economic Substance (Amendment) Act 2019 status election: if the ELP is centrally managed and controlled in Bermuda it is tax-resident and must satisfy economic substance for any 'relevant activity' (a distribution and service centre business would be caught only if such activity is actually carried on in Bermuda); if managed and controlled abroad, the ELP should file a non-resident declaration and is outside the substance regime. (d) US check-the-box election under Treas. Reg. 301.7701-3 (Form 8832): any US-resident partner may elect the ELP to be taxed as a partnership (default), as a corporation, or as a disregarded entity; no such election is available or needed for UK or Bermuda purposes. (e) UK hybrid mismatch rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6A): because the ELP is transparent in both Bermuda and the UK, no hybrid mismatch arises, but the entity must maintain a partnership-transparency status memo to refute any assertion of double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes under the 2024 OECD hybrid framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

UK VAT: GBP 90,000 taxable turnover on a rolling 12-month basis (threshold from 1 April 2024); however, a non-established Bermuda seller of imported goods in consignments not exceeding GBP 135 sold at a distance directly to UK consumers has a zero-threshold compulsory registration duty (Finance Act 2020, Schedule 3 changes abolishing Low Value Consignment Relief and shifting the deemed supplier obligation to the overseas seller when selling through its own website; where sales are intermediated by an online marketplace such as Amazon or eBay, the marketplace, not the ELP, is the deemed supplier for those consignments). For services, the UK has no general registration threshold for non-established businesses supplying B2C digital or electronic services to UK consumers. Bermuda: no VAT threshold exists because no VAT is imposed; customs duty registration is required only for importers of goods into Bermuda under the Customs Act 1941, and the CIT registration threshold (group revenue EUR 750 million) is irrelevant to the instant retail structure.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

UK direct-export zero-rating under VATA 1994 s.30(6) and VAT Notice 703 applies where the Bermuda ELP supplies goods that are physically removed from the UK to Bermuda or any destination outside the UK, provided (i) the supply is for consideration, (ii) the goods are exported within three months of the supply date, and (iii) the ELP holds full export evidence comprising both 'commercial evidence' (contract, commercial invoice, packing list) and 'transport evidence' (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or the shipping company's receipt with the port of departure and foreign destination). For services: B2B services supplied to a Bermuda business customer are outside the scope of UK VAT because the place of supply is where the business customer belongs (VAT Act 1994, Sch 4A and VAT Notice 741A), subject to the Bermuda customer applying any local reverse charge; B2C e-supplies (in-app purchases, downloadable content) made from the UK or from a non-UK establishment to a Bermuda consumer are outside UK VAT under the place-of-supply rules for electronically supplied services. Bermuda imposes no export duty and no VAT, so there is no Bermuda 'export VAT' to zero-rate; however, where the ELP previously imported goods into Bermuda and later re-exports them, a customs drawback or exemption may be claimed under the Customs Tariff Act 1971 within the prescribed re-export window. Stripe and PayPal settlement statements are accepted by HMRC only as payment evidence - they do not prove the physical export of goods and must never be relied on as sole evidence.

  • UK Customs Export Declaration (CDS submission - Single Administrative Document / C88 equivalent) showing the Export MRN and the destination country as Bermuda or another non-UK customs territory
  • Master and house Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) naming the ELP as exporter/consignor and a non-UK consignee
  • Commercial invoice (fully itemised with HS codes, unit values, Incoterms 2020, and the zero-rating statement 'Exported under VATA 1994 s.30(6)')
  • Packing list and weight/measurement certificate
  • Export Cargo Shipping Instruction (SI) and the carrier's signed receipt confirming collection of the goods for export
  • Proof of payment and foreign-currency receipt (Stripe/PayPal payout summaries, EMI and bank account statements)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing repatriation of the export sale proceeds into the ELP's operating account within 180 days of the supply
  • Certificate of shipment or port authority/customs endorsement confirming physical departure from the UK territory
  • Intercompany agreement and transfer-pricing documentation if the export is made through a related distributor or a UK fulfilment subsidiary

UK: no real-time fiscal clearance regime, no pre-clearance digital stamping, and no mandatory government-authorised e-invoicing platform. The binding digital obligation is Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT - all VAT-registered businesses, including non-established taxpayers, must maintain digital VAT records and submit each VAT return to HMRC exclusively through MTD-compatible software using HMRC Application Programming Interfaces (API); manual portal uploads are not permitted. Peppol-network e-invoicing is permitted voluntarily and is standard practice in consumer-electronics cross-border supply chains, but carries no fiscal clearance effect. Bermuda: no e-invoicing or clearance mandate whatsoever; CIT registration and return filing are handled through the Bermuda Tax Administration online portal, and all record-keeping remains document-based with a six-year retention obligation for CIT entrants.

HMRC audit scrutiny: the ELP must produce concurrent commercial and transport evidence on request under VAT Notice 703 paragraph 7; an inability to produce both categories within the HMRC time limit causes the zero-rating to collapse and the supply to be re-characterised as a UK taxable supply at 20% output VAT. HMRC may raise assessments within 4 years of the end of the accounting period (extended to 20 years where fraud, dishonest conduct, or failure to notify is involved, FA 1998 Sch 18 para 46). Clawback mechanics: HMRC will issue a VAT assessment for the undeclared output tax, add statutory interest under VATA 1994 s.74, and impose penalties under FA 2007 Sch 24 - up to 30% for careless inaccuracy, 70% for deliberate inaccuracy, and 100% for deliberate and concealed inaccuracy, all reducible only through unprompted disclosure and cooperative correction. Criminal exposure: deliberately falsifying export evidence, furnishing materially false statements in a VAT return, or engaging in fraudulent evasion of UK VAT is an indictable offence under VATA 1994 s.72(1)-(3) and the Customs and Excise Management Act 1979 s.167-168, punishable on conviction by up to 7 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine; customs seizure and confiscation proceedings under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 apply to the traced proceeds. Bermuda layer: the Tax Commissioner may disallow any CIT-related drawbacks, re-determine an included entity's Bermuda taxable income, and impose penalties of up to 100% of the tax shortfall plus interest for failure to register, failure to file, or fraudulent CIT returns; false customs declarations attract additional sanctions under the Customs Act 1941. In practice, HMRC will also challenge 'paper-only' export schemes where goods are sold ex-works to a freight forwarder and the claimed export is not independently evidenced by transport documentation naming the ELP or its agent as the party who removes the goods from the UK; such arrangements are treated as a UK supply and clawed back with full penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the United States dollar. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible without a requirement to surrender foreign exchange or to obtain prior BMA approval for profit remittance. Non-resident limited partners may freely repatriate distributions from the Exempted Limited Partnership, subject only to standard bank KYC screening and BMA reporting for transactions that meet the Exchange Control Act 1972 notification thresholds. No distinction is made between the personal current account and the business capital account for convertibility purposes, and the BMA actively supports cross-border e-commerce treasury structures using BMD/USD as the functional currency.
