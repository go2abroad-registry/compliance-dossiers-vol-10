No statutory income-tax TIN is issued because Bermuda levies no income tax, corporate tax, capital gains tax, or VAT. A Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) obtains an Exempted Partnership Certificate from the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992 and is placed on the register under the Companies and Partnerships (Fees) Act for the annual government fee. The ELP is not required to procure a tax identification number for Bermuda income tax purposes. If the ELP employs Bermuda-resident individuals, it must register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner as an employer for Payroll Tax (Payroll Tax Act 1995) and Social/Unemployment Insurance. A foreign-owned ELP with no Bermuda employees, no premises, and no Bermuda-source supplies files no Bermuda tax return beyond the annual statutory fee; no TIN, VAT ID or Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number is required absent a UK or EU customs nexus.

Bermuda has no check-the-box or fiscal transparency election because an ELP is per se fiscally transparent and no Bermuda entity-level income tax exists. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Bermuda imposes no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax; limited Customs import duties apply only to physical imports into Bermuda

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Bermuda has no VAT/GST registration threshold. Customs duty registration applies only to physical importers of goods into Bermuda; this ELP's offshore bulk-grain brokerage and trading (soybeans and yellow corn) do not touch a Bermuda customs threshold. By contrast, if a UK establishment of the same group exists and UK taxable turnover exceeds GBP 90,000 (2024/25 threshold), mandatory UK VAT registration arises under VATA 1994 Sch 1 para 1.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable at Bermuda level because Bermuda has no VAT on exports and no refund mechanism. The Bermuda ELP is not a UK VAT registrable person solely by reason of Bermuda residence or non-resident ownership.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged to the US dollar at a fixed rate of 1:1, and convertibility for exempted non-resident undertakings is effectively full on both current and capital account. Trade-related current-account payments, operating expenses, trustee and brokerage fees, and profit remittances can be settled in USD without BMA conversion licence or quantitative ceiling. Capital-account transactions comprising initial LP capital contributions, additional capital injections, partnership distributions and redemption of limited partnership interests are permitted without exchange-control levy, provided the exempted partnership maintains non-resident exchange-control status and all transaction documentation is available for BMA-sanctioned AML/ATF review. Practical hurdles are documentary rather than regulatory: Bermudian banks will require full beneficial-ownership disclosure, source-of-wealth evidence for contributed capital, and clear commercial contracts evidencing the grain/supply-chain purpose before allowing large USD inflows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation and no mandatory conversion timeline. An authorised dealer may credit USD-denominated export, re-export, brokerage or trading proceeds directly to a foreign-currency account of an exempted non-resident ELP. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; there is no BMA requirement to convert USD receipts into BMD. The 1:1 BMD/USD peg is maintained through the BMA's monetary arrangements, but the bank account architecture should be maintained in USD to avoid any immaterial conversion friction.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), constituted under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969 and entrusted with exchange-control administration under the Exchange Control Act 1972. The Bank of England retains no direct domestic FX supervisory mandate in Bermuda, although the UK Parliament has residual sovereignty over Bermuda as a British Overseas Territory.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility rating. UK FCA-regulated e-money institutions may accept a Bermuda exempted limited partnership as a client where the EMI maintains a dedicated corporate desk and can satisfy its own overseas-vehicle UBO policy. Legal constraints are material: BMA does not issue a standalone e-money institution licence under Bermudian law, so any Bermuda-incorporated payment institution collecting deposits would require authorisation under the Banks and Trust Companies Act 1991. A UK EMI therefore acts only as an unlicensed foreign payment service provider vis-a-vis Bermuda, usually through a multi-currency partner-bank account. That nested correspondent structure is often rejected by Bermudian commercial banks for trade settlement because it obscures the underlying wire chain and complicates LC reimbursement. Consequently, global EMIs remain viable only for small operational payments, not for bulk-grain LC disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions should be routed exclusively by SWIFT MT103 from a regulated upstream bank account directly to the ELP's HMS-style segregated USD account at the selected Bermuda clearing bank. The receiving bank requires the sending institution to include the ELP name exactly as registered, a unique contribution reference tied to the subscription agreement, and purpose details reading 'LP Capital Contribution - Commercial Bulk Grain Venture'. If the upstream bank asks for a treasury purpose code, use 'EQUITY' or 'CAPCONT' as its local translation; otherwise the remittance information should state 'Capital contribution under LPA dated [ ], exempted limited partnership'. For USD wires into Bermuda, the sender must use the Bermuda bank's US correspondent and include the Bermuda beneficiary's full account number. Funds will be placed in an exchange-control pre-approval suspense ledger if the KYC pack has not been pre-cleared. To eliminate friction, the complete account-opening file, including the LPA, ELP certificate, BMA letter, UBO register and source-of-funds declaration, must be transmitted to the bank before the first wire is initiated. All capital contributions should be denominated in USD to avoid non-USD conversion risk.Settlement of bulk soybean and yellow corn purchase transactions should be effected through irrevocable, transferable documentary letters of credit issued by the Bermuda bank and advised through the beneficiary's confirming bank. The standard clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for settlement of trade invoices and SWIFT MT202/MT202COV for cover payments between banks. The LC must be expressed in USD and require presentation of a commercial invoice, full set of clean-on-board marine bills of lading, certificate of origin, phytosanitary certificate, weight and quality certificates and an LC-compliant insurance certificate. Where the underlying trade is a back-to-back broker arrangement, an irrevocable transferable LC allows the intermediate ELP to transfer the credit to the ultimate seller. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax (CIT) under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, effective for financial years beginning on or after 1 January 2025; applies ONLY to Bermuda constituent entities of in-scope MNE groups (consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750M). The typical Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) below this threshold bears 0% Bermuda income tax under the traditional territorial nil-tax regime.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends, profit distributions, redemptions, or liquidating distributions paid by an ELP to its partners, whether resident or non-resident.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual Form CIT-1 is due within 15 months after the financial year-end (CIT Act 2023 s.79); quarterly instalments are due 30 days after each quarter-end (final instalment aligns with the annual return).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 6 months after the ELP's financial year-end; the Registrar may grant one extension of up to 3 months.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No standalone Bermuda TP documentation threshold. CIT obligations attach only where the MNE group has consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750M (approximately BMD 825M at 1.10 USD/EUR). CIT filers must contemporaneously document all cross-border related-party transactions in line with OECD TPG Chapter V (Master File, Local File, CbCR). Below-threshold ELPs still require written commission-rate benchmarks (CUP or TNMM) to substantiate arm's length brokerage fees and to defend against UK HMRC transfer-pricing and diverted-profits adjustments.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations 2018 (Bermuda ESR) apply. Bulk-grain brokerage ELPs, where they charter or operate vessels for transporting soybeans/corn, conduct 'Shipping Business' – a relevant activity requiring: (i) direction and management in Bermuda; (ii) CIGAs (crew management, vessel maintenance, cargo monitoring, chartering-in, insurance, claims handling) in Bermuda; (iii) adequate employees/office; and (iv) proportionate local expenditure. A pure third-party commission broker (no vessel operation) falls outside the enumerated activities and faces only standard ESR enrolment. Authorities cross-check ESR filings against HMRC data under the UK–Bermuda TIEA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: UK HMRC transfer-pricing adjustment (CTA 2010 Part 4) restoring arm's length profit; diverted profits tax at 25% of the avoided PE/transfer pricing amount (FA 2015 s.86/87); penalties up to 100% of the unpaid tax for deliberate offshore non-disclosure; CFC charge under TIOPA 2010 Part 9A if profits are parked offshore.

Director personal liability: Senior accounting officer personal penalty of GBP 5,000 (FA 2009 s.93); joint and several liability for dishonest officers under Finance Act 2009; publication on HMRC's deliberate defaulters list; disqualification as UK director (up to 15 years); on fraud conviction, imprisonment up to 7 years and travel imposition/bail conditions.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure (Bermuda ESR)

Statutory sanction: Level 1 penalty of BMD 25,000 per contravention; Level 2 penalty of BMD 500,000 for repeated failure; compulsory strike-off of the ELP registration and cancellation of the permit to carry on business; notice sent to HMRC under the TIEA enabling a coordinated UK investigation.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner and each director knowlingly in default are liable for the penalties; court-ordered restitution of fees generated; director disqualification up to 5 years in Bermuda; travel bans and surrender of passports where enforcement proceedings issue; criminal prosecution for false declarations.
UK Tax Residency / Permanent Establishment Exposure

Statutory sanction: Bermuda ELP becomes UK-resident (CTA 2010 s.66(2) via central management and control), exposing worldwide profits to UK corporation tax at 25%; alternatively, UK permanent establishment attribution of trading profits under CTA 2010 s.19; diverted profits tax at 25%; double tax charges on audit, plus interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of the UK corporate general partner incur personal liability for breach of duty in failing to file UK returns; HMRC may prosecute for 'fraud by failing to disclose information' (Fraud Act 2006 s.4) – unlimited fine and/or 7 years' imprisonment; management-control anti-avoidance provisions impose joint liability; travel restrictions under the Extradition Act 2003 for cross-border tax evasion.
Sanctions Evasion in Agricultural Commodity Trading

Statutory sanction: UK Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018: strict liability corporate offence – unlimited fine and forfeiture of cargo/proceeds; HM Treasury monetary penalties up to 50% of transaction value or GBP 1,000,000 (aggregate); OFAC civil penalties on any USD-denominated clearing; EU parallel sanctions where grain is in EU jurisdiction; all banks freeze correspondent relationships.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners with knowledge are individually criminally liable (up to 7 years); travel bans, asset freezing, and seizure of passports; exclusion of the entity from UK Government contracts; extradition risk via Interpol Red Notices; successor liability under US OFAC guidelines for the entire corporate group.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Evasion

Statutory sanction: Bermuda AML Act 2018 and Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2018: fines up to BMD 1,000,000 per contravention for the ELP and corporate GP; imprisonment up to 10 years for the beneficial owner; FSC regulatory enforcement and revocation of the ELP's exempted status; UK Regulation of Investigatory Powers? Not applicable - use Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 s.328: authorising arrangements – unlimited fine and/or 14 years

Director personal liability: Fines up to BMD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years for each knowing officer; personal disqualification from acting as a licensed/corporate service provider; international travel bans; confiscation orders under POCA 2002; liability extends to UK-based parent directors for failure to prevent money laundering (Criminal Finances Act 2017 s.45 corporate offence – unlimited fine).
