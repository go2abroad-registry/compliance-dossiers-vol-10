Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

The Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is constituted by filing a Declaration of Exempted Limited Partnership with the Registrar of Companies; the Registrar issues the Registration Certificate and a Bermuda Business Number (BBN), and annual government fees and declarations are required thereafter. Bermuda levies no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or VAT; the ELP should apply for a Section 5 Tax Assurance Certificate confirming exemption until 31 March 2035. For the UK trade gateway, the non-UK-established ELP must apply to HMRC for UK VAT registration (no £90,000 threshold applies to non-established persons making positive-rated UK supplies) and for a GB Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number to lodge Customs Declaration Service (CDS) export declarations. If the ELP maintains a UK fixed establishment or employs staff in the UK, PAYE and Corporation Tax registration (CT41G) are required. Multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding €750 million are separately subject to Bermuda corporate income tax at 15% under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, requiring distinct registration.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

UK: £90,000 (effective 1 April 2024) for UK-established taxable persons; a non-UK-established Bermuda ELP must register for UK VAT whenever it makes or intends to make any positive-rated taxable supply in the UK, regardless of turnover, and may register voluntarily to recover input VAT and obtain export zero-rating. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of used construction excavators qualify for 0% UK VAT as exports of goods under VATA 1994, Sch. 8, Group 7 and HMRC VAT Notice 703 where: (i) the goods are exported from the UK within three months of the time of supply; (ii) the ELP is the exporter, holds the goods at the time of supply, and consigns them to a non-UK customer outside the UK's indirect-tax territory; (iii) the ELP obtains and preserves commercial and customs evidence of export within the prescribed periods; and (iv) export proceeds are received within 180 days of export or commensurate commercial credit terms are documented. The second-hand margin scheme remains available for qualifying used machinery, but exports are zero-rated in priority.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic CDS declaration / SAD C88 equivalent, showing the MRN and export goods classification)
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic 'origin or dispatched' evidence) or CMR waybill / airway bill for non-sea carriage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing repatriation of export proceeds within the 180-day deadline
  • Commercial invoice itemising machine serial/chassis numbers, unit values, FOB value, currency, and Incoterms
  • Packing list and container/seal numbers reconciling the dispatched units
  • Export valuation statement and, where applicable, pre-shipment inspection certificates for used heavy plant

UK: no real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or pre-clearance API acceptance; however, HMRC mandates Making Tax Digital (MTD) electronic submission of VAT returns through API-compatible software, and customs export declarations must be filed electronically via the Customs Declaration Service (CDS). E-invoicing (PEPPOL/EDI or structured PDF) is commercially acceptable and not state-cleared. Bermuda: no e-invoicing or fiscal-clearance mandate.

HMRC may withdraw zero-rating, reinstate 20% output VAT plus default interest, and reclaim any input VAT credited where export evidence is missing, goods do not depart the UK within three months, or proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days. Civil penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 escalate to 100% of the VAT lost (30% careless, 70% deliberate, 100% deliberate-and-concealed); false documentation invokes criminal liability under VATA 1994 s.72 with unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 7 years; customs duty and C18 post-clearance demand notices may also apply, and retrospective clawbacks extend up to 4 years (6 years for deliberate inaccuracies). Bermuda imposes no VAT and therefore contains no clawback regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates a currency board system with the Bermuda Dollar (BMD) pegged at par to the US Dollar (USD). Current account convertibility is unrestricted, and profit remittance is freely available to non-resident entities. The Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP), once granted non-resident status by the BMA, faces no material capital account restrictions provided transactions do not involve Bermuda-resident persons or Bermuda-sourced assets. In practice the only remittance hurdle is documentary: banks require the BMA non-resident status certificate, current consent letters, partner resolutions, and a Bermuda counsel tax opinion confirming the absence of Bermuda exchange control or tax objections.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation. There is no mandatory timeline, no minimum conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate or convert export receipts into BMD. A non-resident ELP may retain, hold and disburse export proceeds in USD, BMD or any freely usable foreign currency; the only binding disciplines are the record-keeping, verification and reporting duties imposed by the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2015 and related BMA AML/ATF guidance.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), operating under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969 and administering exchange control powers vested by the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, with the Bank of England as the sovereign parent central bank for GBP clearing corridors.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary Bermuda clearing, Medium for ancillary corridors. Bermuda has no digital EMI licensing regime equivalent to the UK Electronic Money Regulations 2011 or EU EMD2, and UK/EU EMI passports do not extend to Bermuda. An FCA-authorised e-money institution may serve as a secondary treasury rail for GBP/EUR trade collections and donor-country payments, but it cannot hold a Bermuda-domiciled account, clear BMD/USD, or replace a licensed Bermuda bank for escrowed capital. The neutral legal wrapper is a client-funds account held by the UK EMI with the FCA safeguarding rules applying; Bermuda SLB compliance is unaffected.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital contributions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or equivalent FIT credit transfer from each contributing partner's home bank into the Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda USD/BMD escrow account. The remittance must bear the ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI (equity investment) or, for partner loan funding, CFDI; the beneficiary account name must match the ELP legal name precisely; the remitter must be the named contributing partner. The receiving bank requires prior notice, an executed capital contribution certificate, and a source-of-funds breakdown. Structuring, such as splitting contributions into multiple transfers under the USD 10,000 reporting threshold, is prohibited. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No general Bermuda threshold. TP documentation obligations arise only if the ELP is a constituent entity of an MNE group with consolidated revenue equal to or above EUR 750 million (approximately BMD 750 million); out-of-scope ELPs have no Local File or Master File requirement.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Bermuda) Act 2018 applies to ELPs carrying on relevant activities. 'Distribution and service centre' is relevant only where goods or services are supplied to related parties. Pure third-party remarketing of used construction excavators is not a relevant activity; hence the economic substance test and related reporting obligations do not apply to a third-party trading ELP.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraud

Statutory sanction: On conviction on indictment, imprisonment for up to 5 years and/or an unlimited fine; civil penalties in partner jurisdictions can reach 200% of any tax understated.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any managing partner may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; courts may impose travel bans and restraint orders on personal assets.
Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty of BMD 10,000 for the first offence, BMD 500 per day for continuing default, up to BMD 50,000 for repeat offences; possible strike-off of the ELP.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners can be held personally liable for daily penalties and disqualified from serving as directors for up to 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda's Beneficial Ownership regime for exempted partnerships requires accurate beneficial ownership registers; non-compliance carries fines up to BMD 25,000 and removal from the register.

Director personal liability: The general partner may be liable for fines and barred from acting as a regulated professional; courts may lift the limited liability veil, making partners personally liable for ELP debts.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Bermuda AML/ATF Act 2004 and related money laundering provisions, penalties include fines up to BMD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years; assets can be seized.

Director personal liability: The general partner and senior officers are personally exposed to criminal conviction, travel bans, and confiscation of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable profit, impose interest, and levy penalties up to 100% of the understated tax; intentional disregard attracts heightened penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in transfer pricing mispricing can be personally liable for penalties and may be disqualified from acting as directors.
