Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC) under the Ministry of Finance; corporate legal status administered by the Registrar of Companies. Taxpayer portal: Government of Bermuda Tax Portal (tax.gov.bm).

E-government filing portal

Bermuda does not issue a general taxpayer identification number (TIN) or VAT registration number. A foreign-owned Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) obtains a Register of Companies number at incorporation. If the partnership has Bermudian employees, it must apply with the OTC for an employer payroll tax account number. For import/export activity, the ELP should register with the Bermuda Customs Department for a customs broker/declarant account, using the company's Bermuda entity number. No separate tax ID is required for corporate income tax as none exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Bermuda ELP is tax-transparent and not subject to Bermuda corporate income tax; income and gains are generally outside Bermuda's tax net for non-resident partners. No entity-level Bermuda tax on capital gains or profit; not a Tax_Exempt_Zone but operates in a zero-income-tax jurisdiction.

Available election forms

No statutory check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under Bermuda domestic tax law. The ELP may, however, make foreign classification elections outside Bermuda (e.g., U.S. Form 8832 for federal tax classification) to be treated as a pass-through or association taxable as a corporation; such elections have no Bermuda filing effect and do not alter the Bermuda tax position.

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (no Bermuda VAT/GST/sales tax; customs duties and stamp duties are the only indirect taxes)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT or sales tax system is in force in Bermuda; therefore no economic turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Bermuda does not impose VAT or GST on exports or any other supply. For subscriptions or goods exported from Bermuda, no Bermuda VAT is charged and no zero-rating or refund mechanism is required. If the ELP were to carry on taxable business through a UK permanent establishment, UK VAT zero-rating might apply under the VAT Act 1994, Schedule 8, Group 7 to goods exported outside the UK, subject to HMRC substantiation rules.

  • Bermuda Customs Export Entry (electronic declaration) for goods leaving Bermuda
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for air freight)
  • Evidence of dispatch/delivery to foreign destination (e.g., carrier or postal records)
  • Foreign exchange/remittance advice where proceeds are repatriated (if any)

Not applicable — Bermuda has no mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime for invoices. Customs electronic filing is available through the Bermuda Customs Department but does not constitute tax clearance for invoicing purposes.

No Bermuda VAT or export zero-rating regime exists to claw back. False or fraudulent customs declarations are subject to sanctions under the Bermuda Customs Act 1946, including forfeiture of goods, penalties not exceeding BMD 10,000, and/or imprisonment. If UK VAT zero-rating were ever claimed improperly for UK-established operations, HMRC could issue assessments, recover under-declared VAT, apply interest, and charge civil penalties of up to 100% under the Finance Act 2007, in addition to criminal sanctions for fraudulent evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar. The capital account remains subject to exchange control for resident-controlled transactions, but non-resident exempted entities, and specifically exempted limited partnerships with non-resident partners and no local business, benefit from general permission permitting unrestricted conversion and repatriation of profits. Current account payments for goods and services are freely permitted. Profit remittances, capital distributions, and liquidating distributions are routinely approved upon submission of audited financial statements, tax clearance, and partner resolution. The withholding tax on dividends is nil under Bermuda law, and no currency conversion restrictions are imposed on non-resident partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Bermuda's Exchange Control Regulations 1973, a resident exporter must offer foreign currency export proceeds to an authorized dealer (a BMA-licensed bank) within one month of receipt unless the BMA has granted permission to retain, hold, or credit such currency. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to BMD because the free market rate is at par with USD and foreign currency accounts may be maintained with BMA permission; in practice, export receipts can be held in a USD-denominated account at a Bermuda authorized bank without mandatory conversion, provided the non-resident beneficial owners are recorded and the account is designated as an External Account.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs are not permitted to accept deposits or provide payment accounts in Bermuda unless separately licensed under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 or granted a special fintech banking license. Non-bank EMI entities may operate only as payment service providers to the extent they route transactions through a BMA-licensed bank. A foreign EMI without physical Bermuda presence cannot hold customer money or clear through the local clearing system, and reliance on such EMI significantly raises correspondent banking and AML risk. The viable structure is to use a Bermuda-licensed bank as the primary settlement bank and process card/subscription collections via regulated payment aggregators (e.g., Stripe) in partnership with that bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions should be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the partners' bank accounts into the ELP's Bermuda External Account. Purpose code must be selected as 'Capital Contribution/Payment for Equity' (or SWIFT text 'CAPITAL-CONTRIBUTION' in field 70) and reference the ELP's unique registration number and the partnership agreement section. The receiving Bermuda bank will require: (1) partner resolution, (2) bank reference, (3) certified copies of passports and proof of address, and (4) the original or certified copy of the capital contribution schedule. Section C Level of physical presence in Bermuda: number of employees, premises, and management presence. Enter '0' for offshore-managed ELP; do not overstate presence. Non-filing of ESR-01 attracts a penalty of BMD 10,000 plus BMD 1,000 for each day of continued default; the Registrar may strike off the ELP and impose director disqualification orders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold applies to entities outside the CIT regime. For in-scope CIT taxpayers under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, a Local File is required where related-party cross-border transactions exceed BMD 1,000,000 in a fiscal year; a Master File is required where the group's total related-party revenue exceeds BMD 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to 'relevant activities' such as banking, insurance, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, IP holding, and distribution and service centers. A subscription box e-commerce merchandising business is not a relevant activity; therefore a pure offshore ELP conducting such business via third-party suppliers generally has no affirmative substance requirement. However, the Registrar may require evidence of commercial substance if the entity seeks to demonstrate Bermuda tax residence or if the activity falls within the definition of a 'pure holding' or distribution entity, and a shell company with no operations or substance despite claiming tax benefits will be scrutinized under the CIT Act anti-avoidance rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Failure to File In-Scope CIT Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, s.37–40: a fixed penalty of BMD 10,000, a daily default penalty of BMD 500, recovery of the understated tax as a debt due to the Government of Bermuda, and criminal fines up to BMD 250,000 plus imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner may be personally liable for civil restitution of the avoided tax; the Supreme Court may issue travel bans and asset freezing orders; officers who consciously participate in evasion face criminal conviction and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Amendment Act 2019, penalties include a fine of BMD 10,000 plus BMD 1,000 per day for continuing non-compliance; the Registrar can strike the ELP off the register, nullify its legal existence, and impose a liability on the entity for unpaid corporate income tax (if it is in-scope).

Director personal liability: General partners and directors can be jointly and severally liable for penalties, ordered to pay compensation to creditors, disqualified from acting as company directors in Bermuda, and banned from travelling pending regulatory proceedings.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Cross-Border Arrangement Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income, assess unpaid top-up tax with interest (Bermuda base rate plus 2%), and impose a misstatement penalty of up to 50% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers who approved the transaction may be required to reimburse the ELP for any penalty imposed, and may face criminal liability for fraudulent financial statements.
AML / Criminal Finance

Statutory sanction: Money laundering offences under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 carry penalties of up to BMD 5 million in fines and imprisonment for up to 14 years; the ELP's assets and bank accounts are subject to confiscation; continued non-compliance leads to revocation of the ELP's exemption permit.

Director personal liability: Directors, the general partner, and compliance officers may face extradition to Bermuda for criminal trial, personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, and exclusion from financial services; corporate veil-piercing is available where the entity was used as a device for fraud.
