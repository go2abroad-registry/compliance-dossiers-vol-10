Governing statute

Exempted Limited Partnerships Act 2015 (Bermuda), ss. 4-8 (formation and registration) and s. 31 (registered office and resident representative); the Companies Act 1981 applies to exempted companies and is not the governing statute for an ELP.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account rule. Contributions are defined in the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be in cash or in kind; no capital verification or escrow deposit is required for formation.

Funding & unlock path: Prior to occupation, premises must obtain a Fire Safety Certificate from the Bermuda Fire and Rescue Service and a completion or occupancy certificate from the Department of Planning where applicable. If five or more workers are employed, the employer must register under the Occupational Safety and Health Act 1982 and maintain a written safety policy; any racked storage system requires a structural load inspection certificate.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account rule. Contributions are defined in the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be in cash or in kind; no capital verification or escrow deposit is required for formation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda; entity filing with the Registrar of Companies, Department of the Registry General; digital portal: tax.gov.bm and roc.gov.bm

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must file a certificate of limited partnership under the Exempted Limited Partnership Act 2014 with the Registrar of Companies and receive a partnership registration number. For employment activities in Bermuda, the ELP must register as an employer with the Office of the Tax Commissioner and obtain a payroll tax account number. If the ELP forms part of a large multinational group within the scope of the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax (CIT), registration with the Tax Commissioner for CIT and a CIT reference number is mandatory. No VAT/GST registration is required because Bermuda imposes no VAT or general sales tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Bermuda levies no general corporate income tax on an ELP; the private fund or trading partnership itself is not subject to entity-level Bermudian income tax; income is attributed to partners for foreign tax purposes unless the entity becomes subject to the OECD-aligned Bermudian CIT applicable to in-scope multinational groups).

Available election forms

Under Bermuda law, an exempted limited partnership is inherently a legal partnership and has no Bermuda statutory election to change its tax transparency for direct Bermudian tax purposes. For foreign tax classification, the ELP may make a check-the-box election on IRS Form 8832 to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity under U.S. tax rules. Where the ELP is part of an in-scope multinational enterprise, elections under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024, including filing group designations, qualified refundable tax credit treatment, and IIR/UTPR elections, are available to the extent permitted by the Tax Commissioner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax; Bermuda applies Customs Duty on imported goods under the Customs Tariff Act and Customs Act, plus separately administered payroll tax on Bermuda source employment income.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A (Bermuda has no VAT/GST; customs duty applies from first import with no economic turnover threshold triggering indirect tax registration).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Bermuda has no VAT/GST. Export sales of garments are outside the scope of any Bermuda indirect tax. In the customs context, duty relief may be available through temporary importation, in-bond warehouse procedures, duty drawback on re-exports, or transhipment relief, provided goods are identifiable, the export is bona fide, and statutory time limits are met.

  • Customs Import Entry (Bermuda Customs Declaration through ASYCUDA World) covering temporary importation or warehousing
  • Re-Export Declaration / Customs Export Entry
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export to the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the finished garments and off-price wholesale value
  • Proof of import duty payment where drawback is claimed
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank Swift/MT103 documentation evidencing repatriation of export proceeds

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists for domestic transactions. Imported goods must be electronically declared through the Bermuda Customs Department using ASYCUDA World; duties are assessed and paid before goods are released.

Bermuda has no VAT clawback mechanism because no VAT exists. However, false declarations, evasion of customs duty, fraudulent drawback/re-export claims, and defective documentation are subject to customs seizure, forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties up to treble the duty evaded, and criminal liability for partners and responsible officers. Tax Commissioner and Customs audit authorities may retrospectively deny duty relief and recover unpaid duty with interest and penalties if export evidence is insufficient or goods are not actually exported.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at a statutory exchange rate of one BMD to one United States Dollar (USD) and is fully and unconditionally convertible. Bermuda operates an open, fully liberalised capital account: there are no BMA approvals, no surrender requirements, no capital controls, and no repatriation licences for non-resident exempted partnerships. Current-account transactions (trading of finished garments, freight, warehousing, insurance, and logistics) and capital-account transactions (capital contributions, limited partner withdrawals, profit distributions, and redemption of partnership interests) may be freely settled in any convertible currency. The ELP, formed under the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992 and classified as non-resident for exchange-control purposes, suffers no statutory withholding on profit distribution and no exchange-control charge on conversion of USD profits to GBP, EUR, or other currencies. The only practical friction is correspondent-bank de-risking and documentary KYC, not a statutory impediment to convertibility. The UK parent perspective is equally unrestricted: the United Kingdom imposes no exchange controls on GBP outflows or inbound remittances, subject only to financial sanctions screening and HMRC balance-of-payments reporting codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for the surrender of export proceeds. Under the Exchange Control Act 1972 (Bermuda), non-resident exempted partnerships are not required to repatriate, convert, or surrender any percentage of export earnings generated from the wholesale liquidation of finished garments; the ELP may lawfully retain USD and foreign currency in offshore and onshore multi-currency accounts indefinitely. For the United Kingdom, there is likewise no export-proceeds surrender rule: UK exporters may hold foreign currency balances freely, although the remitting bank is obliged to record a balance-of-payments transaction code (typically the HMRC International Transactions Reporting System code for goods trade and direct investment) and retain underlying export documents, bills of lading, and commercial invoices for at least six years. The absence of mandatory surrender supports the treasury strategy of maintaining USD inventory-replenishment liquidity in Bermuda and converting only the net trading margin to GBP for partner distributions.

Central bank supervision: The Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, is the central banking, monetary, supervisory, and foreign-exchange control authority for Bermuda, a self-governing United Kingdom Overseas Territory. The BMA administers the remaining provisions of the Exchange Control Act 1972 (Bermuda), the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, and the AML/ATF regulatory codes applicable to licensed banks and trust companies. In the United Kingdom parent jurisdiction, the Bank of England (BoE) and the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) govern sterling and banking stability, while HM Treasury exercises ultimate sovereign control over sanctions and monetary policy. In practice, treasury and FX governance for the Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is split: BMA authorisation and prudential supervision for the Bermuda banking relationship, and FCA/PRA rules for any UK-resident payment institution or clearing counterparty.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM - permissible strictly as a secondary payment accelerator, never as the sole treasury custodian. Legal and regulatory constraints: (i) a UK FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institution (EMI), such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, or Airwallex, is not a bank and does not hold client funds as deposits; its safeguarding arrangements do not qualify as a regulated Bermuda bank account under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, and therefore cannot satisfy the ELP's requirement for a licensed domestic account in its own name; (ii) the BMA treats unlicensed EMI processing corridors as higher-risk third-party payment processors, and Bermuda-licensed correspondent banks may reject or de-risk wire traffic originating from EMIs unless the EMI is FCA-regulated, holds client funds segregated in a major UK clearing bank, and provides a full source and sink audit trail; (iii) an EU-licensed EMI operating under the Second Electronic Money Directive has passporting rights only within the EEA and cannot lawfully provide payment services into Bermuda without a separate BMA payment services authorisation or a licensed Bermuda banking partner; (iv) permitted treasury use is confined to low-value B2B trade payments to verified factory suppliers, capped at pre-agreed daily and monthly limits, with automatic rebalancing to the primary Bermuda operating account and no use for overdraft, interest-bearing liquidity, or settlement of partnership capital. Recommended configuration: primary operating account at Butterfield; secondary UK EMI current account for GBP-denominated factory deposits; and a domestic ACH Direct rail for US-based warehouse and logistics counterparties.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM - permissible strictly as a secondary payment accelerator, never as the sole treasury custodian. Legal and regulatory constraints: (i) a UK FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institution (EMI), such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, or Airwallex, is not a bank and does not hold client funds as deposits; its safeguarding arrangements do not qualify as a regulated Bermuda bank account under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, and therefore cannot satisfy the ELP's requirement for a licensed domestic account in its own name; (ii) the BMA treats unlicensed EMI processing corridors as higher-risk third-party payment processors, and Bermuda-licensed correspondent banks may reject or de-risk wire traffic originating from EMIs unless the EMI is FCA-regulated, holds client funds segregated in a major UK clearing bank, and provides a full source and sink audit trail; (iii) an EU-licensed EMI operating under the Second Electronic Money Directive has passporting rights only within the EEA and cannot lawfully provide payment services into Bermuda without a separate BMA payment services authorisation or a licensed Bermuda banking partner; (iv) permitted treasury use is confined to low-value B2B trade payments to verified factory suppliers, capped at pre-agreed daily and monthly limits, with automatic rebalancing to the primary Bermuda operating account and no use for overdraft, interest-bearing liquidity, or settlement of partnership capital. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% Bermuda tax at the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) level; a 15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax (CIT) applies under the CIT Act 2023 only to Bermuda constituent entities of MNE groups with consolidated annual group revenue of EUR 750 million or more (approximately BMD 812 million, Bermuda Dollar pegged 1:1 to USD) in at least one of the two preceding accounting periods. A typical mid-market off-price garments wholesale ELP remains outside the CIT charge unless the group-wide revenue threshold is crossed.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no withholding tax, dividend tax, or distribution tax. Distributions and profit allocations from the ELP to the UK/Cayman/Gulf limited partners are paid gross with zero Bermuda deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory OECD-aligned Master File and Local File preparation is triggered in Bermuda when the MNE group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EUR 750 million (approximately BMD 812 million at the 1:1 BMD/USD peg). Below this threshold, Bermuda imposes no statutory TP documentation requirement on an ELP; nonetheless, contemporaneous evidence of the arm's-length off-price liquidation margin (typically a TNMM operating margin of 3%–8% on net passed cost) must be maintained to defend the pricing against HMRC and partner-jurisdiction audits.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Amendment) Acts 2018–2023 (ESR) apply to Bermuda entities carrying on 'relevant activities'. Wholesale distribution of finished garments to connected persons is classified as a 'distribution and service centre' only to the extent the ELP derives income from connected parties and conducts core income-generating activities in or from Bermuda. An ELP purchasing off-price overstock from independent brands and selling to unrelated third-party buyers outside Bermuda falls outside the ESR net. If in scope, the ELP must annually demonstrate: (i) directions and management exercised in Bermuda; (ii) full-time employees and exclusive-use premises in Bermuda; (iii) adequate operational expenditure; and (iv) board/GP meeting minutes physically held in Bermuda. Failure triggers automatic exchange of information with HMRC and partner-state authorities.
