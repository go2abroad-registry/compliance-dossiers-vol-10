Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, Registry General, Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda (with exempted-status approval coordinated with the Minister of Finance).

Governing statute

Exempted Limited Partnerships Act 2015 (Bermuda) ('ELP Act 2015'), ss. 4-8 (registration duty, name approval, registered office, resident representative) and s. 9 (limited liability protection); Exempted Limited Partnerships (General) Regulations 2016; Partnership Act 1902 (supplementary general partnership law); Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Amendment Act 2019 for substance-reporting assessment.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1 — Declaration for Registration as an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP Act 2015, s. 5(1)), filed together with the executed Partnership Agreement.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up or subscribed capital applies to an ELP; partnership capital is fixed solely by the executed Partnership Agreement. No escrow capital account, capital-duty deposit, or bank confirmation is required at registration; the sole statutory payment is the registration/first-year government fee of BD$1,780 (Bermuda dollar pegged 1:1 to USD) payable to the Registrar.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety — Bermuda Fire and Rescue Service must issue Fire Safety Certificates for the registered office (means of escape, emergency lighting, extinguishers, and annual inspection). Structural/Occupancy — the Department of Planning issues a Development and Use of Premises Permit confirming structural load and maximum occupancy. Occupational — the ELP must register as an employer with the Department of Labour, obtain employer's-liability and workers' compensation cover, and maintain a written health-and-safety policy under the Occupational Safety and Health Act 1982.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up or subscribed capital applies to an ELP; partnership capital is fixed solely by the executed Partnership Agreement. No escrow capital account, capital-duty deposit, or bank confirmation is required at registration; the sole statutory payment is the registration/first-year government fee of BD$1,780 (Bermuda dollar pegged 1:1 to USD) payable to the Registrar.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — no industrial licensing board is triggered for ISIC 6201 (payroll SaaS). For physical premises, the Bermuda Planning Board (Department of Planning) grants use-of-premises and development permits; the Business Licensing Section at the Treasury administers the local trade-licence regime, from which this exempted, non-local-trading ELP is exempt.

Category: Not applicable — no operating or manufacturing permit is required for automated payroll automation software; an approval or notification is only required for PIPA data-protection compliance governed by the Office of the Privacy Commissioner and, where relevant, the economic-substance filing with the Ministry of Finance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner, Government of Bermuda; Registrar of Companies, Corporate Registry — portal: https://www.gov.bm

E-government filing portal

A foreign sponsor forms an Exempted Limited Partnership by filing a declaration with the Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnership Act 1883 (as amended). Bermuda imposes no income tax and generally does not issue corporate tax TINs. Any ELP with Bermuda-resident partners or employees that incurs payroll tax liability must separately register with the Office of the Tax Commissioner and obtain a Bermuda employer/payroll tax account reference.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No Bermuda check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch classification election exists. The ELP is tax-transparent by statute; all income, gains, deductions, and credits flow through to partners. Partnership agreement allocations may determine Bermuda payroll tax exposure for resident partners, subject to anti-avoidance review by the Office of the Tax Commissioner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax; customs duties apply at import

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; no indirect tax registration threshold exists because Bermuda does not levy VAT/GST. Importers declare through Bermuda Customs Service and may require import permits depending on tariff classification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Bermuda does not impose VAT/GST on exports of goods or offshore services; therefore no claim for 0% export VAT is needed. Cross-border payroll SaaS services supplied from Bermuda fall outside any Bermuda indirect tax.

No statutory e-invoicing, fiscal API clearance, or digital stamping regime. Bermuda Customs Service uses ASYCUDA World for electronic customs declarations; commercial invoices and supporting shipping documents must be retained, but no real-time tax-authority clearance is mandated.

No export VAT refund or clawback mechanism exists. Misdeclarations to Bermuda Customs (e.g., commodity code, valuation, or duty relief misuse) may be penalized under the Customs Act 1947 and Customs Tariff Act 1973, including fines, seizure, and revocation of licenses. Payroll-tax non-compliance may be assessed retrospectively with penalties and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar (USD). The KY_ELP (Exempted Limited Partnership) is designated 'non-resident' for exchange-control purposes, which grants unrestricted convertibility on current-account transactions (SaaS licensing fees, payroll service fees) and full liberalization on capital-account transactions (capital injections, loan repayments, and in-kind distributions). Profit remittance: Bermuda imposes no income tax, no withholding tax, and no capital gains tax; distributions and capital repatriation from the non-resident ELP to the UK parent are freely remitted in USD, GBP, or EUR without BMA prior approval, provided the receiving bank holds the funds in a foreign-currency non-resident account and maintains the entity's non-resident designation on the Exchange Control register.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender applies. Regulation 3 of the Exchange Control Regulations 1973 obliges Bermuda-resident persons to offer foreign currency to an authorized dealer within one month, but the non-resident ELP is statutorily exempt. All export proceeds (payroll SaaS subscription revenue, implementation fees, and support retainers) may be retained offshore in foreign-currency accounts without any conversion ratio. Only BMD-denominated domestic transactions require BMA approval where concluded with residents; this structure avoids BMD exposure entirely by operating solely in USD and GBP.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting through its Department of Exchange Control under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM - acceptable as a secondary payment rail only. Bermuda has no dedicated electronic-money-institution (EMI) licensing regime under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, so a Bermuda-regulated EMI is unavailable. UK-FCA-authorized EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud/Visa) and EU e-money institutions may open multi-currency accounts for the ELP without a Bermuda banking license, enabling low-cost GBP/EUR payroll disbursement, virtual IBANs, and API-driven reconciliation. Legal constraints: (i) balances held at an EMI are not covered by the Bermuda Deposit Insurance Corporation regime and are not ring-fenced client money unless the EMI's home rules require otherwise; (ii) the EMI cannot provide Bermuda domestic USD clearing; (iii) the BMA's AML/ATF Guidance Notes expect the ELP's registered agent and general partner to maintain a complete audit trail of all EMI flows; (iv) the ELP must retain a licensed Bermuda bank as primary treasury custodian to preserve the non-resident exchange-control designation and banking pedigree.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: UK parent GBP account at a UK authorized bank (HSBC UK or Barclays) sends a SWIFT MT103 to Butterfield or Clarien for credit to the ELP's USD non-resident operational account (GBP converted to USD at the receiving bank's dealing desk). Purpose code in SWIFT field 72 or 77B: 'EQUI' (equity) or 'TRDP' cross-referenced to the capital contribution agreement; UK-side reporting is captured on the parent's Bank of England ITRS return as 'Direct Investment Abroad - Equity Capital'. For amounts above USD 250,000 equivalent, the UK bank applies enhanced fraud verification and requires the beneficiary to be pre-verified by the Bermuda bank prior to release. The Bermuda bank credits only after matching the contribution agreement number, the non-resident designation, and the registered agent's confirmation. If the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, the MT103 must carry purpose code 'LEND' (loans) with an executed intercompany loan agreement to avoid misclassification.Dual-rail clearing structure: (1) SWIFT MT103 for high-value payroll and vendor settlements (above USD 10,000) from the Butterfield or Clarien operational account, using full beneficiary name, IBAN or account number, and SWIFT BIC, with purpose codes '9100' (goods trade) or '3900' (other services); (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP 600, irrevocable, confirmed) issued by Butterfield or Clarien for data-center infrastructure, software licensing, and cross-border SaaS procurement contracts exceeding USD 100,000; (3) for sub-USD 10,000 recurring payroll runs, Direct Debit ACH via a UK-FCA-authorized EMI (Wise or Currencycloud) as a secondary rail, or the bank's batch payment portal with automated reconciliation. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda does not levy dividend withholding tax or any distribution tax on payments from an Exempted Limited Partnership to partners, including non-resident partners.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

AR-ELP: due on or before the date stated in the Registrar's renewal notice, generally within 60 days of each registration anniversary. Economic Substance Return: due no later than six months after the end of the ELP's financial year.

Part 1 — Entity Identification Registered ELP name, registration number, status, formation date, registered office address and principal business activity. Use the exact name as registered with the Registrar of Companies. Non-resident partners must appoint and disclose a Bermuda-located registered office; a post-office box alone is not acceptable. Late filing or defective AR-ELP submission can attract civil monetary penalties, the withholding of the Certificate of Compliance, and strike-off and dissolution by the Registrar. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Nil (BMD 0) — Bermuda has no statutory Local File or Master File obligation because there is no corporate income tax base. However, a UK-parented group containing this ELP must still apply UK transfer-pricing rules and, where group revenue exceeds EUR 750 million, OECD Pillar Two GloBE documentation requirements. Intercompany transactions should be supported by contemporaneous arm's-length benchmarking to avoid UK CFC and transfer-pricing adjustments.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations apply to ELPs carrying on a Bermuda-defined relevant activity. A payroll automation software business is generally not in scope when it serves unrelated third-party customers from outside Bermuda. The activity is in scope only if the ELP operates as a related-party headquarters, distribution and service centre or intellectual property business in Bermuda. An in-scope ELP must demonstrate management and control in Bermuda, adequate local employees and premises, proportionate local expenditure and, for IP-derived revenue, the OECD 'full nexus' approach. Failure to satisfy these substance indicators results in financial penalties, strike-off and possible exchange of information with the UK or other foreign tax authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Offshore Avoidance

Statutory sanction: HMRC may re-characterise the ELP as UK tax resident, attribute profits to a UK permanent establishment or CFC, and assess corporation tax at up to 25%; civil penalties can reach up to 200% of the tax evaded under the deliberate offshore non-disclosure rules, with criminal prosecution available for cheating the public revenue.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the ELP is a mere facade; general partners and directors can be held jointly and severally liable, subjected to civil restitution and worldwide freezing orders, banned from travel, disqualified as directors and imprisoned for up to seven years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance Act authorises penalties up to BMD 10,000, daily surcharges for continuing violation, loss of the ELP's exempted status and strike-off from the Bermuda register; HMRC may subsequently deny treaty-like benefits and assess the controlled foreign company charge on the UK shareholder.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who knowingly submit an inaccurate substance declaration are personally exposed to civil restitution, disqualification from acting as Bermuda fiduciary or company director, travel bans and imprisonment for knowing false statements.
Anti-Money Laundering / Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Under Bermuda's Proceeds of Crime Act 1997, Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Regulations 2018 and United Nations financial sanctions orders, penalties include unlimited fines, confiscation of proceeds, suspension of banking relationships and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; because the business is classified as medium-risk enhanced, mandatory enhanced due diligence applies.

Director personal liability: General partners, directors and compliance officers can be prosecuted personally for failure to prevent economic crime, with personal asset forfeiture, travel bans, disqualification from regulated activities, and custodial sentences of up to 14 years.
Dishonest Filing / Misrepresentation to Registrar

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may impose administrative financial penalties, strike the ELP off and refer the matter for criminal prosecution; a person who knowingly makes a false statement in a Bermuda regulatory filing commits an indictable offence punishable by fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The general partner and each signatory officer are personally responsible for the accuracy of every return; sanctions include personal restitution orders, travel bans, permanent disqualification from directorships in the UK or Bermuda, and incarceration.
Cross-Border Permanent Establishment / CFC Ignorance

Statutory sanction: HMRC can assess UK corporation tax on the profits attributable to the permanent establishment, add interest and late-filing penalties, and apply CFC rules to apportion the ELP's passive or non-genuine profits to the UK parent at up to 25% corporation tax, together with information-gathering penalties.

Director personal liability: Senior officers who caused the omission may face personal liability notices, civil penalties, director disqualification and, in serious facilitation cases, personal liability under the corporate criminal offence of failing to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion, including travel bans and imprisonment.
