Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda (official electronic filing portal: Bermuda Tax Administration accessed via gov.bm); corporate registry: Registrar of Companies, Government of Bermuda; indirect tax and customs authority: Bermuda Customs Service, Department of Customs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the ELP is treated as a transparent partnership under Bermuda law and has no Bermuda income tax for entities outside CITA scope; from fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025, CITA imposes a 15% corporate income tax on constituent entities of in-scope MNE groups, with the ELP itself generally remaining flow-through and the tax falling on its partners where applicable; Bermuda otherwise maintains a territorial fiscal regime with employer payroll tax and customs duties rather than a general income tax.

Available election forms

Bermuda does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; however CITA 2023 and Tax Commissioner guidance recognise partnership transparency and provide: (i) election for a qualifying ELP to be treated as a transparent flow-through entity for CIT purposes; (ii) election designating a partner or partners as the CIT taxpayer where the ELP is used as a pure holding or finance vehicle; (iii) group relief and consolidated CIT return elections for constituent entities of the same MNE group; (iv) transitional elections under the CIT Transitional Rules regarding deemed fiscal years, basis periods and loss carry-forward. For cross-border investors, a US Form 8832 entity-classification election remains available for federal tax purposes where US partners are present, and hybrid mismatch analysis is required under the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework, the UK hybrid mismatch rules for UK-connected partners, and the anti-hybrid provisions embedded in CITA 2023.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST or general sales and use tax; Bermuda levies Customs Duties on imported goods under the Customs Tariff Act 1968, a 15% Hotel Occupancy Tax under the Hotel Occupancy Tax Act 2012, a payroll tax on employers under the Payroll Tax Act 1995, and various stamp duties; franchise licensing fees and royalty services supplied from Bermuda are not subject to any Bermuda VAT or sales tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST registration threshold. All commercial importers must register with the Bermuda Customs Service irrespective of transaction value; employers must register for payroll tax upon the first Bermuda-relevant employee; CITA in-scope MNE groups register on the Bermuda Tax Administration portal without a domestic turnover threshold where group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750 million.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Bermuda imposes no VAT or GST, no export zero-rating mechanism exists and no VAT is chargeable on goods physically exported from Bermuda or on offshore services such as franchise royalties, licensing fees, and central management services supplied to non-resident franchisees. Goods imported into Bermuda and subsequently re-exported may qualify for customs duty relief under the temporary importation for re-export regime, bonded warehouse procedures, or the licensed manufacturer export promotion scheme. Offshore service income of CITA in-scope ELPs is subject to normal CITA 2023 quantification rules and must be supported by arm's-length transfer pricing documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bermuda Customs SAD export entry with unique declaration reference)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing export from Bermuda
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / FX settlement evidence evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin where required by the destination market and any export permit applicable to the goods
  • Carrier's Export Manifest / shipping instruction from the Bermuda freight forwarder
  • Duty-paid import entry (for re-export duty-relief claims) and warehouse/goods-movement records

Bermuda has no real-time fiscal e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or VAT API mandate. Import and export declarations and duty settlement are processed electronically through the Bermuda e-Customs single-administrative-document platform. CITA in-scope entities must file returns and records electronically via the Bermuda Tax Administration portal and retain underlying transactional documentation for the statutory retention period of six years.

The Bermuda Customs Service and the Office of the Tax Commissioner may audit export and duty-relief claims within their respective statutory limitation windows. False or fraudulent export declarations, misdeclaration of export proceeds, or unsupported duty-relief claims attract penalties under the Customs Tariff Act 1968, including seizure of goods, fines, criminal sanctions and mandatory retrospective clawback of any duty or tax erroneously refunded, together with statutory interest. For CITA in-scope entities, deliberate understatement of export or offshore income is subject to the CITA 2023 penalty regime, including civil penalties of up to 150% of the understated tax, statutory interest, and potential criminal liability for evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged to the US Dollar at 1:1 and is freely usable alongside USD in the domestic banking system. Current account payments are fully convertible; capital account transfers require exchange control permission unless the entity holds non-resident designated status, which the BMA routinely grants to exempted partnerships such as the ELP. With non-resident status, the ELP may maintain foreign currency accounts, receive franchise royalties, remit distributions, and repatriate capital without BMA case-by-case approval. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to standard bank-level AML/KYC and source-of-funds verification; no withholding tax or exchange control levy applies in Bermuda.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda does not impose mandatory export proceeds surrender or conversion ratios on entities with non-resident exchange control designation; the ELP may retain franchise royalties and trade receipts in foreign currency accounts at an authorised dealer. For resident-designated entities, the Exchange Control Act 1972 requires that export proceeds be offered for sale to an authorised dealer within one month of receipt unless a general or specific retention permission is granted. The ELP, being non-resident designated, falls outside the surrender obligation and may freely deploy USD, GBP, and EUR proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and as supervisor under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium supplementary viability. UK-authorised electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business) can provide fast multi-currency operational accounts and cost-effective clearing for franchised royalty inflows collected across Europe and Asia. However, they cannot satisfy the ELP's Bermuda substance requirements, cannot act as authorised dealers, and are not supervised by the BMA under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999. Funds held with EMIs are safeguarded rather than deposited, and franchisee payments routed through EMIs may attract enhanced source-of-funds scrutiny from the primary Bermuda bank. EMIs are therefore recommended only as secondary clearing rails, never as the sole banking platform.

Funds held with EMIs are safeguarded rather than deposited, and franchisee payments routed through EMIs may attract enhanced source-of-funds scrutiny from the primary Bermuda bank. EMIs are therefore recommended only as secondary clearing rails, never as the sole banking platform.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for a standard Bermuda exempted limited partnership (ELP); 15% only for a Bermuda constituent entity of an MNE group whose consolidated annual revenue is EUR 750,000,000 or more, pursuant to the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends, partnership distributions, or profit shares paid by an exempted limited partnership to its non-resident partners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File obligations arise for Bermuda constituent entities of an MNE group with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more, equivalent to approximately BMD 810,000,000 at the fixed 1:1 US dollar peg. A standard out-of-scope franchise ELP is not legally obliged to prepare OECD Local File or Master File documentation, but the corporate general partner should maintain contemporaneous intercompany agreements covering the franchise royalty rate, syrup transfer prices, and group cost allocations for banking due diligence, economic substance review, and UK HMRC enquiries.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Economic Substance Amendment Act 2019 and the Economic Substance (Information Return) Regulations 2019 apply to every entity or partnership, including this ELP, carrying on a relevant activity. The franchise licensing of boba-tea intellectual property and the syrup supply-chain functions constitute 'IP business' and 'distribution and service centre' activities respectively. The ELP must demonstrate adequate and demonstrable economic presence in Bermuda: physical premises, full-time qualified employees, local management decision-making, and material recurrent expenditure in Bermuda. Core income-generating activities must be directed and performed in Bermuda; outsourcing to the UK or other group companies without local oversight does not satisfy the test and will trigger enforcement action.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Declarations

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Bermuda Criminal Code and the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023; on conviction, fines up to BMD 50,000 or twice the tax evaded, imprisonment up to five years, confiscation of the proceeds of crime, and civil restitution orders covering the tax, interest, and penalties.

Director personal liability: The general partner, and the directors of a corporate general partner, are jointly and severally liable for taxes and penalties arising from fraud; courts will pierce the ELP's liability shield, impose travel bans, freeze personal assets, and order personal restitution of underpaid taxes.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic substance penalties between BMD 5,000 and BMD 10,000 per offence plus daily penalties of BMD 1,000; the Registrar may strike the ELP off the register; ministerial direction may compel liquidation and cancellation of exempted status; IP-business substance failures may attract financial penalties up to BMD 250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be disqualified and held personally accountable for restitution; the general partner loses the benefit of limitation of liability and must indemnify the partnership for all regulatory sanctions and third-party claims.
Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Offences under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008 and the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997: fines up to BMD 250,000 or twice the value of the laundered funds, imprisonment up to two years, revocation of the ELP's banking licence relationship, and freezing orders against the UK EMI account.

Director personal liability: The money laundering reporting officer and the directors of the corporate general partner face personal fines and up to two years' imprisonment; the Bermuda Supreme Court may issue travel bans and worldwide freezing orders against the individuals directing the ELP.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation in CIT-In-Scope Groups

Statutory sanction: Bermuda CIT transfer-pricing adjustment with interest; administrative penalties up to 100% of the understated CIT; the UK HMRC may simultaneously apply diverted profits tax and controlled-foreign-company rules to the same royalty and supply payments, causing duplicative aggregate assessments of 155% or more of the shifted profit.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are jointly and severally liable for adjusted CIT where they knowingly participated; HMRC may issue personal liability notices for diverted profits tax and director disqualification proceedings in the UK, and Bermuda courts may impose travel bans pending resolution.
Sanctions Evasion and Unlicensed Franchise Operation

Statutory sanction: Offences under the UK Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 as extended to Bermuda and under Bermuda's Economic Sanctions Act: fines up to BMD 1,000,000, imprisonment up to 14 years, asset forfeiture, and suspension of the franchise operations; violation of licensing requirements leads to immediate cease-and-desist orders and revocation of the ELP's registration.

Director personal liability: Each officer, director, and partner is separately prosecutable; courts may issue travel bans, refusals of entry to the UK and Bermuda, extradition cooperation with the UK, and personal restitution orders over the franchise royalty account.
