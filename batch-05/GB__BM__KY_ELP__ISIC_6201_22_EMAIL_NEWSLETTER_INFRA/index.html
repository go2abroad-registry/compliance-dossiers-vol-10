Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Revenue Agency (BRA), Ministry of Finance, Bermuda — statutory tax administration, with official e-filing portal at https://etax.gov.bm (government portal: https://www.gov.bm). Registrar of Companies, Ministry of Economic Development, Bermuda handles exempted partnership registration; Department of Customs administers import/export declarations via the Bermuda Online Services (BOS) single-window system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The Bermuda-resident registered agent files the Certificate of Formation with the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2015, obtaining the ELP registration number; no minimum capital is required and non-resident ownership is permitted. Step 2: The BRA does not issue a statutory TIN automatically to exempted partnerships; a Bermuda payroll-tax registration and associated BRA employer TIN is required only if the ELP employs Bermuda-resident staff. Step 3: If goods will physically enter Bermuda, the ELP or its customs agent must register as an importer with the Department of Customs (no monetary threshold). Step 4: For foreign tax administration, the ELP uses its Bermuda registration number; if it files a US partnership return or elects corporate classification, it obtains a US EIN via IRS Form SS-4; for UK reverse-charge/OSS purposes, a UK VAT registration number may be obtained through HMRC online services only where a UK fixed establishment is created.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2015; Bermuda imposes no entity-level corporate income tax, capital gains tax, or wealth tax. Profits and losses flow through to partners for taxation exclusively in their residence jurisdictions. As an offshore special territory, territorial scope is exclusionary: no CFC or exit-tax regime applies to non-resident partners, and the ELP is eligible for default pass-through treatment under the US check-the-box rules.

Available election forms

Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (EUTPA) declaration — grants statutorily guaranteed exemption from all future Bermuda taxes until 31 March 2035; US federal check-the-box election via IRS Form 8832 to classify the ELP as either a partnership or an association taxable as a corporation; annual Economic Substance Act 2018 declaration and substance filing through the BRA (relevant only if the ELP carries on an in-scope activity in Bermuda); partnership-agreement profit/loss allocation elections are available to coordinate hybrid-mismatch outcomes under OECD BEPS Action 2 in investor jurisdictions, although no standalone hybrid-election mechanism exists in Bermuda domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax in Bermuda. The jurisdiction levies only customs import duties under the Customs Tariff Act and payroll tax on Bermuda-source remuneration. Cross-border digital services (recurring email API and newsletter hosting subscriptions) supplied by the non-resident ELP from Bermuda to overseas customers are entirely outside the Bermuda indirect tax net; supplies to UK business customers fall within the UK reverse-charge mechanism (VATA 1994 Sch. 4A) rather than any Bermudian tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — Bermuda has no VAT threshold because no VAT exists. Customs importer registration is required solely upon physical importation of goods into Bermuda, with no monetary threshold. The UK VAT registration threshold of GBP 90,000 (VATA 1994 Sch. 1) would apply only if the ELP creates a UK fixed establishment and makes UK-taxable supplies; otherwise no UK VAT registration is triggered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Bermuda — with no VAT/GST system, there is no zero-rating mechanism for exported goods or offshore services; the ELP's email-infrastructure exports are simply untaxed by default. For the UK context (if a UK VAT registration is ever added), zero-rating is available solely for physical exports of goods under VATA 1994 s.30(6)/(7) with prescribed export evidence; cross-border digital services to UK business customers are reverse-charged, and supplies to UK consumers are taxed at 20% via the non-UK OSS scheme — neither qualifies for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration / single administrative document — required only for physical goods exported via Bermuda or the UK; not required for pure digital services.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export (goods only).
  • Commercial Invoice, packing list, and service contract / API subscription agreement (for digital services).
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign-currency proceeds.
  • Proof of customer destination and, where applicable, the customer's VAT registration number (for UK reverse-charge substantiation).

No electronic-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital-stamping mandate exists in Bermuda. Customs entries for physical goods are lodged electronically through the Bermuda Online Services (BOS) single-window system (ASYCUDA-based); digital service exports require no fiscal clearance or digital stamping. UK Making Tax Digital (MTD) accounting obligations would attach only upon any future UK VAT registration.

No Bermudian clawback exists because Bermuda imposes no VAT. If the ELP becomes UK VAT-registered and improperly zero-rates exports or incorrectly applies the reverse charge, HMRC may: (i) assess under-declared VAT plus default interest under VATA 1994 ss.73–74; (ii) impose an accuracy penalty of 30% up to 100% of potential lost revenue under FA 2007 Sch. 24, increased for deliberate and concealed conduct; (iii) extend the assessment window to 20 years where fraud or deliberate non-compliance caused the loss (VATA 1994 s.77); and (iv) institute criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s.72 and the Fraud Act 2006, carrying fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years for fraudulent export zero-rating or digital-service misclassification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda's official currency is the Bermudian dollar (BMD), pegged at 1:1 to the United States dollar (USD). Capital account transactions are broadly convertible, but the Exchange Control Act 1972 and BMA Notices require prior written approval for certain inward and outward capital movements, including foreign currency borrowing, the issue of shares or partnership interests to non-residents, and substantial outward direct investments. Current account items, including trade related payments and profit remittances, are generally freely permitted upon submission of statutory declarations and underlying supporting documents; however, the ELP must submit Form EC/2 (or its electronic equivalent) and obtain a 'no objection' certificate for profit distributions to non-resident partners if the remittance exceeds the BMA's de minimis threshold. There are no exchange controls on USD denominated current account activity because the BMD is fully interchangeable with USD in the clearing system, but any conversion between BMD and foreign currency must be executed through an authorised bank and reported to the BMA under the exchange control statistical return. Practical profit remittance hurdles are limited to anti-money laundering (AML) verification of the final beneficial partners and documentary proof of economic substance, if applicable, as the BMA and the Revenue Authority periodically review cross-border payment flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda does not impose a statutory surrender requirement for export proceeds. Under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and BMA Notices, authorised banks in Bermuda may credit export receipts in foreign currency to an ELP's designated foreign currency account without requiring conversion into BMD. The ELP must, however, retain complete invoices, contracts of sale, and shipping or service delivery evidence for at least five years to satisfy the BMA's record-keeping obligations and the Customer Due Diligence (CDD) requirements under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008. For transactions denominated in BMD, the bank will credit the account in BMD at the official peg rate. There is no mandatory repatriation period; the ELP may keep export proceeds offshore, but if the proceeds are routed into a BMD account, the bank must apply standard exchange control formality, which is administrative rather than restrictive.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the island's central monetary authority and exchange control administrator under the Exchange Control Act 1972; the series of delegated regulations and notices are issued by the Financial Policy, Resolution and Risk Directorate.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for offshore fully-digital FinTech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) that lack a physical Bermuda presence. The BMA does not license standalone offshore EMIs; a foreign EMI cannot operate a bank account solely through an e-money license from another jurisdiction. The Bermuda Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 restricts deposit-taking to licensed banks or exempt companies. An offshore EMI (e.g., UK-authorised or EEA-authorised) may open a settlement account with a Bermuda licensed bank only if it passes the same AML/CDD and 'sub-account' scrutiny as any non-resident corporate client; conversely, the ELP may hold funds at an offshore EMI only for ancillary working capital, not as its principal treasury vehicle, because the BMA imposes effective control over all Bermuda-headquartered partnership assets. The ELP may use a UK FCA-authorised EMI (such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Checkout.com) to collect card payments and convert GBP to USD, but the primary treasury and clearing account must be at a full Bermuda bank or a major correspondent bank outside Bermuda to satisfy the BMA's exchange control and substance expectations. Legal constraints: (i) no EMI passporting exists in Bermuda; (ii) EMIs cannot offer Bermudian current accounts denominated in BMD without a banking licence; (iii) the ELP's general partner must ensure that using an offshore EMI does not inadvertently create a permanent establishment in the EMI's jurisdiction or violate the BMA's books-and-records location requirement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection to the Bermuda ELP may be effected by wire transfer from the UK parent or a designated group funding vehicle. The routing protocol is set out as follows: (1) The sending bank in London initiates a SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent) to the receiving bank in Bermuda (HSBC or Butterfield); the Beneficiary Account number must be the ELP's segregated capital account; the Beneficiary Name must exactly match the registered legal name of the Exempted Limited Partnership; the Remittance Information field must contain the purpose code 'CD' (Capital Development) or, where the BMA online portal requires a separate code, 'CAP-ELP' followed by the BMA reference number if a prior approval has been obtained; (2) the sending bank must debit the UK entity's GBP or USD account and pay either in USD or convert to USD at the London interbank rate; the receiving bank in Bermuda will credit BMD at the 1:1 peg unless the beneficiary has a distinct USD omnibus account; (3) the following verification documents must be pre-paid to the receiving bank via secure electronic upload: the executed capital contribution certificate; a copy of the BMA Form EC/2 for inward remittance, if applicable; a board resolution from the contributing UK entity; the ELP's certificate of registration; and UBO declaration; (4) in case the contribution exceeds USD 100,000, the receiving bank may request a 'Declaration of Source of Funds' signed by the contributing entity's CFO; (5) for multi-currency contributions, use a foreign currency account to avoid conversion slippage; convert to BMD only at the exact moment of payment disbursement; (6) the BMA must be notified within fourteen days of any capital injection via the authorised bank's electronic exchange control return; the bank shall provide a stamped acknowledgements letter as proof of compliance. Purpose codes that are acceptable for inbound digital service and technology entities include 'UK321' (investment in a service enterprise) and 'UK351' (royalty or technology service payment) when processing through UK bank clearers; but the ELP's own account must only rely on 'capital injection' rather than 'trade payment', to avoid an incorrect classification that may complicate repatriation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for offshore fully-digital FinTech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) that lack a physical Bermuda presence. The BMA does not license standalone offshore EMIs; a foreign EMI cannot operate a bank account solely through an e-money license from another jurisdiction. The Bermuda Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 restricts deposit-taking to licensed banks or exempt companies. An offshore EMI (e.g., UK-authorised or EEA-authorised) may open a settlement account with a Bermuda licensed bank only if it passes the same AML/CDD and 'sub-account' scrutiny as any non-resident corporate client; conversely, the ELP may hold funds at an offshore EMI only for ancillary working capital, not as its principal treasury vehicle, because the BMA imposes effective control over all Bermuda-headquartered partnership assets. The ELP may use a UK FCA-authorised EMI (such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Checkout.com) to collect card payments and convert GBP to USD, but the primary treasury and clearing account must be at a full Bermuda bank or a major correspondent bank outside Bermuda to satisfy the BMA's exchange control and substance expectations. Legal constraints: (i) no EMI passporting exists in Bermuda; (ii) EMIs cannot offer Bermudian current accounts denominated in BMD without a banking licence; (iii) the ELP's general partner must ensure that using an offshore EMI does not inadvertently create a permanent establishment in the EMI's jurisdiction or violate the BMA's books-and-records location requirement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: N/A - Bermuda does not maintain mandatory transfer pricing documentation thresholds for local tax purposes. However, a Bermuda ELP performing relevant activities in an international group must maintain contemporaneous economic substance records, which may include transfer pricing analysis to demonstrate alignment of profits with people and risks in Bermuda.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018 (as amended) applies in Bermuda. ELPs carrying on a relevant activity must satisfy the economic substance test by having (i) management and control conducted in Bermuda, (ii) core income-generating activities performed in Bermuda, (iii) adequate number of qualified employees, (iv) adequate physical premises in Bermuda, and (v) adequate annual expenditure in Bermuda. For ISIC 6201 email infrastructure SaaS, the activity must be assessed as either 'technology service' or 'provision of services to related parties'; failure to comply results in penalties and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to File ESR Return / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty of up to BD$100,000; cancellation of exemption or strike-off from the Register; fixed penalties and daily default penalties for continued noncompliance.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors who knowingly permit false filings are liable to personal penalties; the Court may issue a disqualification order and, in egregious cases, imprisonment up to one year for willful failure to provide information or obstruction.
Offshore Shell Company / Artificial Tax Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Under Bermuda law, penalties for failure to comply with economic substance rules reach BD$100,000; under the UK's anti-avoidance rules (e.g., CFC and transfer-pricing assessments), the UK tax authority can recharacterize profits and impose UK corporation tax at up to 25% plus interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: UK directors may be liable for UK CFC apportionment penalties; in Bermuda, general partners who sign false declarations commit an offence. Civil recovery and travel bans may be imposed in cases of cross-border sham arrangements.
Missing AML Compliance for Email Payments Infrastructure

Statutory sanction: Fines up to BD$100,000 per offence; regulatory license suspension; the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) may issue a prohibition order against the ELP's beneficial owner from acting in regulated financial services.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners face personal criminal liability if they collude or are knowingly concerned in money laundering; imprisoned up to 5 years. Travel bans and asset freezes can be issued pending investigation.
False Tax Exemption Certificate / Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Misrepresentation to the Bermuda Registrar can result in revocation of the exempted partnership certificate; a criminal offence under the Exempted Partnerships Act carries a fine and imprisonment up to one year.

Director personal liability: General partners who knowingly benefit from misrepresentation are personally liable for restitution of wrongfully received transaction amounts, and may be subject to international extradition where fraud is alleged.
Failure to Maintain Register of Beneficial Owners

Statutory sanction: Nightly penalty of BD$500 per day for non-compliance; criminal fines and imprisonment for up to two years for refusal to provide information to the authorities.

Director personal liability: Each general partner or designated partner is personally liable for the daily penalty; court-ordered compensation may be imposed on individual partners in the event of loss suffered by third parties.
