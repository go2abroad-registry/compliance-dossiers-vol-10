Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda (administering the Payroll Tax Act 1995, the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 and the Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax); the Registry of Companies (entity formation, registration number, annual returns and economic substance declarations); the Business Licensing Authority (trade licence issuance); and the Bermuda Customs Department, Ministry of Finance (import/export tariffs and ASYCUDA World clearance). Principal e-portals: taxcommissioner.gov.bm for e-filing, the government e-services single window for business licensing, and the ASYCUDA World customs portal. For any UK trading establishment, HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) is the separate competent authority; there is no joint UK/Bermuda tax registration.

E-government filing portal

Bermuda imposes no income tax, corporation tax or capital gains tax, and issues no general-purpose income-tax TIN. For a foreign-owned Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP), the procurement sequence is: (1) appoint a licensed Bermuda corporate services provider/resident agent; (2) file the Declaration of Limited Partnership and partnership agreement with the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Limited Partnership Act 2015 and obtain the Certificate of Registration; the entity registration number functions as the primary statutory identifier/TIN and must be quoted on all filings, invoices and banking documentation; (3) apply for a Business Licence in the restaurant/food-service category from the Business Licensing Authority, with annual fees assessed on published gross-receipts bands; (4) if the ELP engages Bermuda-resident employees, register as an employer with the Tax Commissioner for Payroll Tax and with the Bermuda Social Insurance Board; a foreign ELP with no Bermuda employees requires no payroll TIN; (5) register with Bermuda Customs as an importer/exporter in ASYCUDA World for any movement of goods, including condiment consignments; (6) if the group is within the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million), register for CIT and file the annual economic substance declaration; otherwise, only the annual ELP return and economic substance declaration are due.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope; Tax_Exempt_Zone (the ELP holds 'exempted' status under Bermuda law, is fiscally transparent for Bermuda purposes, and Bermuda taxes only Bermuda-source items such as payroll, imports and in-scope multinational CIT; the ELP registers as a non-resident partnership, and foreign-source and offshore trading income are outside the Bermuda tax net)

Available election forms

(1) US check-the-box election under IRC section 7701 and Treas. Reg. section 301.7701-2/3 (Form 8832): a multi-member foreign ELP defaults to partnership classification and a single-member ELP defaults to disregarded-entity status; an affirmative election to be taxed as a corporation is available where US tax planning requires it; (2) Hybrid mismatch disciplines: the ELP can be positioned as a hybrid or reverse-hybrid for UK/US purposes, but the UK hybrid mismatch rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6A) and OECD Action 2 rules must be monitored to prevent denied deductions or double non-taxation; (3) Economic Substance (Bermuda) Act 2018: the ELP must annually select, in the declaration filed with the Registrar, whether it is a pure equity holding entity or is carrying on one of the defined relevant activities, and must meet the corresponding substance requirements; (4) Corporate Income Tax Act 2023: in-scope multinational groups may elect to apply the Bermuda QDMTT safe harbour and obtain credit/coupon treatment for qualifying domestic top-up tax, consistent with OECD Pillar Two.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/sales tax in Bermuda; the jurisdiction's indirect revenue instruments are customs import tariffs under the Customs Tariff Act 1973, plus a Hotel Occupancy Tax limited to accommodation (not applicable to restaurant turnover). A hotpot restaurant generating dine-in, take-away and condiment sales in overseas markets is instead exposed to the host jurisdiction's indirect tax (e.g., UK VAT at standard 20% on restaurant supplies), which arises outside Bermuda's fiscal jurisdiction.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable (BMD): Bermuda levies no VAT/GST and therefore prescribes no turnover-based indirect-tax registration threshold. All commercial importers must register with Bermuda Customs and lodge declarations in ASYCUDA World from the first import; a Business Licence (restaurant/food-service category) is a condition precedent to trading; payroll tax registration is triggered by the first Bermuda-resident employee with no de minimis wage threshold; CIT registration applies only where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least EUR 750 million.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda has no VAT, so no domestic 'zero-rating' claim mechanism exists. Exports of tangible goods from Bermuda, including proprietary hotpot sauces, condiments and food ingredients consigned to the chain's overseas restaurants, are outside the scope of Bermuda customs duty and are exported free of any Bermuda export duty. Eligibility conditions: (1) the goods must physically leave Bermuda and be reported on an electronic export declaration in ASYCUDA World naming the ELP as exporter; (2) the ELP must hold a valid Business Licence and be registered with Customs as an exporter; (3) the commercial invoice, packing list and transport document must evidence the international movement; (4) where duty-paid goods are imported into Bermuda and subsequently re-exported, a customs drawback claim under the Customs Act 1968 requires proof of export within the statutory period and confirmation that no local consumption occurred; (5) services supplied by the ELP to non-residents (franchise, management, offshore catering and logistics services) are not within the charge to any Bermuda indirect tax; import VAT/GST, if any, is collected by the destination jurisdiction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (ASYCUDA World electronic filing)
  • Commercial Invoice (quoting the ELP registration number and Business Licence number)
  • Packing List (item-level HS commodity codes, weights and values)
  • Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Certificate of Origin (where preferential tariff treatment is claimed at destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing proceeds repatriation

Bermuda has no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no e-invoicing API clearance and no digital stamp requirement. Mandatory electronic filing applies to: customs declarations via ASYCUDA World (UN/EDIFACT or web declarations); payroll tax and CIT returns via the Tax Commissioner's e-filing portal; and business licence applications/renewals via the Bermuda online licensing service. Invoices must still comply with general record-keeping and business-licence rules, and source records must be retained for the applicable statutory audit windows for post-clearance and tax audits. For the UK operating entity, HMRC Making Tax Digital-compatible records and the future UK e-invoicing mandate roadmap will apply separately.

Bermuda Customs may conduct post-clearance audits to verify export and drawback claims; over-declarations or drawback claims based on false documentation are clawed back with interest. Fraudulent or negligent export documentation attracts penalties under the Customs Act 1968, including forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties up to three times the duty evaded, and imprisonment upon indictment, with vessels/aircraft implicated also exposed to seizure. False returns to the Tax Commissioner under the Payroll Tax Act 1995, Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 and Business Licensing Act 1996 are subject to surcharges, fines, daily default penalties and director/partner liability, and fraudulent evasion constitutes a predicate money-laundering offence under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 enabling confiscation of assets. Although Bermuda statute imposes no exchange-control requirement, the 180-day export-proceeds repatriation covenant is maintained as group treasury and transfer-pricing discipline to support the documentary chain for customs drawback, to substantiate economic substance, and to reduce the risk that the ELP is found to be carrying on a UK trade through the actions of a UK permanent establishment or a UK-dependent agent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda maintains a fully convertible current account and an open capital account for non-resident-controlled exempted limited partnerships. The Bermudian dollar is pegged at 1:1 to the United States dollar and licensed banks freely convert BMD into USD, GBP, EUR, and other major currencies for trade, investment, and operating purposes. There are no exchange-control permits, prior consents, or profitability tests applicable to inbound subscriptions, capital distributions, or partnership profit allocations of a non-resident ELP, and because Bermuda levies no income tax, corporation tax, capital-gains tax, or withholding tax, the profit-remittance hurdle is limited to documentary proof rather than tax clearance: capital-account statements, the register of limited partners, and an economic-substance filing position must support each distribution. The only practical convertibility friction arises from the requirement that every flow traverse a licensed bank's AML/ATF, sanctions-screening, and travel-rule compliance framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime: there is no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline, and no requirement to convert foreign-currency export earnings into BMD. Export receipts derived from the overseas hotpot dining and condiment-supply business (royalties, management fees, goods sales) may be retained in USD, GBP, or EUR in free foreign-currency accounts or converted as treasury policy dictates; the BMA only requires the licensed bank to record the receipt in the customer's account and to report aggregated balance-of-payments statistics in standard regulatory returns.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) — the de facto central bank and the sole exchange-control administrator of Bermuda, a British Overseas Territory. The BMA is constituted under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969 and exercises its statutory powers through the Exchange Control Act 1972, the Exchange Control Regulations 1972, the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, and the AML/ATF framework aligned with the Financial Action Task Force; the Bank of England retains no direct licensing function over Bermuda deposit takers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High for the UK operating layer via a GB FCA-regulated EMI or electronic-money account, but Low-to-Medium for the Bermuda ELP as the direct account holder of a pure offshore digital EMI. Legal Constraints: EMIs cannot provide deposit-taking, documentary-credit, or lending facilities; most EMIs reject Bermudian pass-through vehicles where the general partner is a licensed corporate-services provider and the economic activity sits in the UK or Asia; the Bermuda Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Rules 2018 and the UK FCA Payment Services Regulations require full originator/beneficiary travel-rule data for every qualifying cross-border transfer. The recommended architecture is a dual rail: a tier-1 Bermuda bank account as the statutory treasury anchor, plus a GB FCA-authorised EMI account for European merchant-acquiring settlement, supplier disbursements, and fast GBP clearing, with contractual segregation of client funds and no deposit-taking exposure.

Root cause: Third-party payment and cross-border disbursement anomalies: the bank flags long payment chains from Bermuda to UK/Asia suppliers through EMIs or third-party intermediaries, and mismatches between declared payment purpose and underlying trade documents.

Defense evidence: All underlying contracts (real-estate leases, franchise agreements, procurement orders, intercompany service agreements) with invoice-to-payment reconciliation; annotated payment-flow diagrams linking each transaction to commercial duty; UK restaurant licences such as food-premises registration and alcohol licence; landlord and utility contracts evidencing an active operating footprint; board minutes authorizing account opening, authorized signatories, and payment-frequency schedules; CMR or bill-of-lading evidence for condiment exports; and a compliance covenant that no funds will be commingled with unrelated third-party traffic.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202/COV for bank-to-bank reallocation) into the Bermuda Capital Escrow Account, with USD cleared via Fedwire/CHIPS and GBP cleared via CHAPS through the bank's London correspondents. Every remittance must carry a precise purpose code — for example INVEST — contribution to exempted limited partnership — and reference the ELP registration number, the subscriber name, and the drawdown schedule; the receiving bank matches the source account against the certified register of limited partners and the source-of-funds dossier before releasing escrowed funds to the Operational Account. Where the contributing investor is a PRC or Hong Kong entity, the originating bank must evidence SAFE outbound-ODI registration or the applicable Hong Kong outbound-investment filing, and the Bermuda bank must complete the BMA travel-rule and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) verification, including full originator and beneficiary name, address, and account number. No case-by-case BMA exchange-control consent is required for a non-resident ELP, but each escrow release must be supported by a GP-signed capital-drawdown notice and posted to the partnership capital-account ledger.Outbound trade disbursements are executed over four clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 credits for open-account supplier payments in USD, GBP, or EUR, each carrying the relevant purpose code and backed by invoices or proforma contracts; (ii) commercial documentary letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) and standby credits (MT760) arranged through the Bermuda bank's London or New York correspondent network for bulk condiment and equipment imports, with presentation of bills of lading, packing lists, certificates of origin, and food-safety certificates; (iii) MT202/COV interbank transfers for treasury concentration and rebalancing between the Bermuda anchor account, the UK operating accounts, and the EMI corridor; and (iv) local merchant-acquiring/POS rails for daily restaurant card settlements, with sweeps from the UK acquirer to the EMI and onward to the Bermuda treasury account on a T+1/T+2 basis. For RMB-denominated supply payables, settlement is executed through the Hong Kong offshore-CNH clearing network (Bank of China (Hong Kong)) and converted into USD or GBP via a licensed FX counterparty; every payment must be paired with underlying trade documents to pass the bank's AML reference-matching and source-of-funds review.Deliverable outright forwards in USD/GBP, USD/EUR, and USD/CNH executed with licensed Bermudian or UK banks, supported by a genuine commercial underlying such as trade invoices, royalty flows, or payroll calendars.Offshore CNY non-deliverable forwards (USD/CNH NDFs) used to hedge Chinese condiment-supply payables where RMB is not physically deliverable offshore, with written treasury policy and underlying exposure documentation.Cross-currency swaps in USD/GBP and USD/CNH to match the maturity profile of capital injections and trade-disbursement flows, executed under an ISDA or bank master agreement.Plain-vanilla FX options and collars, permitted only where the bank's derivatives licence covers the ELP and where the instruments are documented as bona-fide commercial hedges rather than speculative treasury trading.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: High for the UK operating layer via a GB FCA-regulated EMI or electronic-money account, but Low-to-Medium for the Bermuda ELP as the direct account holder of a pure offshore digital EMI. Legal Constraints: EMIs cannot provide deposit-taking, documentary-credit, or lending facilities; most EMIs reject Bermudian pass-through vehicles where the general partner is a licensed corporate-services provider and the economic activity sits in the UK or Asia; the Bermuda Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Rules 2018 and the UK FCA Payment Services Regulations require full originator/beneficiary travel-rule data for every qualifying cross-border transfer. The recommended architecture is a dual rail: a tier-1 Bermuda bank account as the statutory treasury anchor, plus a GB FCA-authorised EMI account for European merchant-acquiring settlement, supplier disbursements, and fast GBP clearing, with contractual segregation of client funds and no deposit-taking exposure.
