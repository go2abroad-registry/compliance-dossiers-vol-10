Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Ministry of Finance — Registrar of Companies (entity registration and Bermuda Business Number/BBN issuance; portal: gov.bm); Office of the Tax Commissioner (payroll tax); Bermuda Customs Department (import/export customs duty); Bermuda Monetary Authority (exchange-control classification). Bermuda operates a distinct tax system from the United Kingdom and has no single income-tax administration because no income tax is levied on exempted partnerships.

E-government filing portal

No statutory TIN or VAT ID exists in Bermuda. For a Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP), the general partner files a registration application with the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2014; the Registrar issues a Certificate of Registration and a unique partnership registration number, which is accepted as the Bermuda TIN for CRS/FATCA reporting purposes. The entity then applies for a Bermuda Business Number (BBN) through the gov.bm portal, which is mandatory for customs clearance and all government transactions. If the ELP engages Bermuda employees, it must register for payroll tax with the Office of the Tax Commissioner; if it physically imports goods into Bermuda, it must obtain a customs importer registration code from the Bermuda Customs Department. VAT registration is neither available nor required because Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. Bermuda imposes no income, corporation, capital gains, estate, or withholding tax on offshore exempted partnerships: the ELP is fiscally transparent and its non-resident partners are taxable only in their own jurisdictions of residence. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Bermuda imposes no income, corporation, capital gains, estate, or withholding tax on offshore exempted partnerships: the ELP is fiscally transparent and its non-resident partners are taxable only in their own jurisdictions of residence. Scope exception: the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 introduces a 15% entity-level tax on constituent entities of multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more in at least two of the prior four fiscal periods, effective for periods commencing on or after 1 January 2025; a boutique vintage-luxury resale vehicle does not prima facie meet this threshold.

Available election forms

Bermuda has no domestic check-the-box, deemed-election, or hybrid-mismatch election because no Bermuda income tax exists. Available mechanisms: (1) governance and economic-allocation elections specified in the ELP partnership agreement under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2014; (2) non-resident status designation under the Exchange Control Act 1972, approved by the Bermuda Monetary Authority, permitting unrestricted foreign-currency remittance and offshore bank accounts; (3) for U.S. federal tax purposes, an entity-classification election on IRS Form 8832 under Treas. Reg. 301.7701-3(c) (a Bermuda ELP is an eligible entity and may elect corporate, partnership, or disregarded-person treatment); (4) CRS/FATCA self-certifications identifying each partner-reported TIN and status. No election can create a Bermuda taxable presence absent the Pillar Two threshold above.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales tax is levied in Bermuda. The only indirect border charge is customs import duty under the Customs Act 1974 and Customs Tariff Act 1974 (general ad valorem tariff of approximately 22.25% on many categories, with specific rates for motor vehicles, alcohol, tobacco, fuel, and selected luxury classifications). Minor stamp duty applies to certain instruments; payroll tax and land tax are direct, not indirect, taxes.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Bermuda has no VAT/GST regime and therefore no economic-turnover threshold for indirect-tax registration. Customs registration is mandatory for every commercial importer regardless of value (nil threshold) and is enabled by the BBN. A modest annual exempted partnership government fee, as gazetted by the Ministry of Finance, is payable regardless of turnover. Payroll tax registration arises only upon first engagement of Bermuda employees. UK/EU distance sales of vintage goods do not trigger Bermuda indirect-tax registration; any threshold-based registration (e.g., UK VAT OSS or EU OSS/IOSS) would arise in the destination states, not in Bermuda.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Bermuda export zero-rating claim does not exist because Bermuda levies no VAT on goods or offshore services. The functional equivalent is customs duty relief: goods imported into Bermuda and later re-exported (e.g., vintage luxury items temporarily entered for consignment, auction, or restoration) may qualify for a drawback or refund of duty paid under the Customs Act 1974, conditional on Customs verifying export within the permitted statutory window. In a drop-ship or cross-dock model, goods are never imported into Bermuda and consequently bear no Bermuda duty; VAT zero-rating analysis then belongs exclusively to the destination regimes — because the ELP has no fixed establishment in the UK/EU, consignment exports to UK/EU consumers are generally not zero-rated at Bermuda level, and the seller should instead rely on the EU IOSS for consignments below EUR 150 or on standard import-VAT collection; UK VAT arises at import unless the seller distance-registers via the UK OSS.

  • Bermuda Business Number (BBN) certificate and customs importer registration code
  • Electronic customs import entry (Bermuda Customs automation declaration) evidencing duty paid
  • Commercial invoice, consignment contract, and authenticity/provenance certificates for the vintage luxury goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill covering the physical movement into and out of Bermuda
  • Customs-endorsed re-export / outward entry or temporary-importation bond discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit evidence retained for audit, AML, and CRS documentation purposes

No e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-reporting mandate applies in Bermuda. Import/export entries are filed electronically with the Bermuda Customs Department through its customs automation platform using the BBN; this is a border-control filing for goods physically crossing the Bermuda frontier, not a transactional fiscal clearance obligation. Onward sales into the UK/EU remain outside Bermuda's mandate, but may be separately captured by destination-state e-invoicing, OSS, or IOSS reporting rules.

Misuse of Bermuda customs duty relief (including false import or re-export declarations, overvalued duty repayments, or missed re-export deadlines) triggers retrospective clawback of duty relieved, statutory interest, and penalties under the Customs Act 1974: forfeiture of the goods, monetary fines generally up to BMD 10,000 (rising to treble the value of the goods or duty evaded for fraudulent entry), and criminal liability of the general partner and signing officers. Failure to re-export within the permitted temporary-importation period converts relief into an immediate duty liability with penalty surcharge. Where UK/EU VAT zero-rating, OSS, or IOSS mechanisms are used, HMRC and EU tax administrations may impose extended destination-market verification, VAT assessments, interest, penalty surcharges of up to 100% for deliberate inaccuracy, and criminal sanctions for export-based VAT fraud; contemporaneous dispatch, courier tracking, provenance, and FX-credit evidence must therefore be archived.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates a tied currency regime in which the Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at par to the United States dollar (USD), and USD is legal tender and circulated interchangeably. There are no separate capital account vs current account conversion restrictions for an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) that has been granted 'non-resident' exchange control status by the BMA under section 3 of the Exchange Control Act 1972. Such a designation authorises the ELP to maintain, hold, receive, and disburse balances exclusively in non-Bermudian currencies (typically USD, GBP, EUR) without requiring case-by-case consent for current account trade-related remittances, profit distributions, repayments of partner loans, or capital account injections. Full convertibility is effectively available: capital account transactions are freely convertible for non-resident designated entities because the BMA does not impose convertibility quotas, surrender surcharges, repatriation taxes, or delayed availability rules. The only practical hurdle for profit remittance is the need to substantiate the beneficial ownership of the remitted funds and the commercial substance of the underlying transaction to the correspondent bank and the receiving bank; Bermuda imposes no dividend withholding tax or remittance tax, and there is no requirement to convert profits into BMD before distribution. Partnership profits may be distributed in the currency of the capital contributions or in any agreed contract currency without BMA conversion consent, provided the non-resident status is maintained and the partnership does not transact in BMD for domestic Bermuda-facing activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement for a non-resident designated Exempted Limited Partnership under Bermuda law. The Exchange Control Act 1972 and its regulations do not impose any mandatory timeline or conversion ratio for export receipts earned by such an entity. The BMA has granted a general permission as a matter of administrative practice to non-resident entities under section 2 of the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, allowing them to retain export sales proceeds in foreign currency indefinitely without any obligation to sell, deposit, or convert them into BMD. For the avoidance of doubt, the entity should not surrender export proceeds to BMA or to any licensed Bermuda bank unless the entity has been inadvertently classified as 'resident' for exchange control purposes, in which case a specific application for exemption would be made to the BMA with the support of the partnership's resident representative and the bank. Therefore, the applicable surrender rule is 'nil / not applicable' for this ELP, and no internal treasury policy relating to compulsory conversion of export receipts is required.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), constituted under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, acting as the central financial services regulator and exchange control authority for Bermuda. BMA sets and administers the provisions of the Exchange Control Act 1972, the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, and related directions. Although the United Kingdom's HM Treasury retains residual sovereignty oversight over Bermuda as a British Overseas Territory, no HM Treasury permission is required for the day-to-day exchange control matters of an exempted partnership designated as non-resident.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MODERATE. There is no dedicated electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime in Bermuda comparable to the UK's FCA EMI framework, and the BMA does not charter offshore digital EMIs under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999. A non-Bermuda-licensed EMI housed in the United Kingdom (e.g., an FCA-authorised EMI with passporting rights) can theoretically provide payment card issuance, virtual IBANs, and payment collection services to a Bermuda ELP; however, onshore Bermuda banks generally refuse to clear or settle through unregulated third-party EMI accounts because of correspondent banking de-risking policies. Additionally, the ELP's operating rails (Stripe, Escrow_Pay) are themselves payment facilitators that aggregate merchant funds; in practice, the ELP will not be able to open a treasury account with an offshore digital EMI for the core capital escrow or operational treasury function. The BMA's view is that any entity performing money transmission, payment services, or stored-value issuance in or from Bermuda must fall under the Banking Act or be expressly licensed; an offshore EMI cannot rely on a UK licence to conduct business with a Bermuda resident. Accordingly, full treasury functionality should be built on tier-1 Bermudian and UK clearing banks rather than offshore EMIs. If the ELP wishes to use an EMI solely for acquiring card payments in the European market, it must do so through a downstream merchant-of-record arrangement and must not hold partner capital or escrow balances in the EMI. Recommended defensive approach: restrict the EMI to settlement-only pass-through accounts, maintain sweep governance to a tier-1 bank within 24 hours, and disclose the arrangement in the AML/KYC file.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MODERATE. There is no dedicated electronic money institution (EMI) licensing regime in Bermuda comparable to the UK's FCA EMI framework, and the BMA does not charter offshore digital EMIs under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999. A non-Bermuda-licensed EMI housed in the United Kingdom (e.g., an FCA-authorised EMI with passporting rights) can theoretically provide payment card issuance, virtual IBANs, and payment collection services to a Bermuda ELP; however, onshore Bermuda banks generally refuse to clear or settle through unregulated third-party EMI accounts because of correspondent banking de-risking policies. Additionally, the ELP's operating rails (Stripe, Escrow_Pay) are themselves payment facilitators that aggregate merchant funds; in practice, the ELP will not be able to open a treasury account with an offshore digital EMI for the core capital escrow or operational treasury function. The BMA's view is that any entity performing money transmission, payment services, or stored-value issuance in or from Bermuda must fall under the Banking Act or be expressly licensed; an offshore EMI cannot rely on a UK licence to conduct business with a Bermuda resident. Accordingly, full treasury functionality should be built on tier-1 Bermudian and UK clearing banks rather than offshore EMIs. If the ELP wishes to use an EMI solely for acquiring card payments in the European market, it must do so through a downstream merchant-of-record arrangement and must not hold partner capital or escrow balances in the EMI. Recommended defensive approach: restrict the EMI to settlement-only pass-through accounts, maintain sweep governance to a tier-1 bank within 24 hours, and disclose the arrangement in the AML/KYC file.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold applies to KY_ELP itself as a pass-through, out-of-scope partnership. Bermuda's OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation rules (Local File and Master File) apply only to entities of in-scope MNE groups under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024, i.e., groups with consolidated global revenue of EUR 750 million or more. Nevertheless, KY_ELP should maintain contemporaneous arm's-length documentation for any related-party transactions, such as consignment inventory sourced from an affiliated seller, to protect UK-resident partners' tax positions.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulation applies through the Economic Substance Act 2021. KY_ELP must file the annual ESR declaration. If it carries on a 'distribution and service business' - the category that covers online consignment and secondhand luxury goods retail - with core income-generating activities performed in Bermuda, it must demonstrate full substance: a physical office in Bermuda, a local bank account, sufficient local employees and annual expenditure, and board meetings held in Bermuda. If all core income-generating activities are performed outside Bermuda, reduced substance is acceptable, but the declaration must truthfully state that fact; a false declaration attracts escalating penalties under the Act.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / False Economic Substance Declaration

Statutory sanction: Escalating BMA penalties under the Economic Substance Act 2021: BD$10,000 for late or non-filing; BD$50,000 for a first substance failure; BD$100,000 for a second; BD$250,000 for a third; and up to 3x the financial benefit for intellectual-property-related abuse. Repeat offences lead to compulsory deregistration, dissolution, and referral to the Financial Sanctions Committee.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who knowingly submit false declarations face criminal liability: fines up to BD$250,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 2 years, disqualification from holding office, travel restrictions on entry to Bermuda, and civil restitution; the corporate veil is pierced so that personal assets of the general partner's principals are attachable.
Tax Evasion Through Misreported Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Fines up to BD$50,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 2 years for knowing concealment, with daily default fines of BD$5,000 for continuing non-compliance; the Registrar may strike the ELP from the register and forfeit the benefit of the exemption certificate.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability attaches to directors of the corporate general partner; civil restitution of concealed value can be enforced through the UK-Bermuda Tax Information Exchange Agreement, with freezing orders available under Bermuda law and a Bermuda territorial entry ban of up to 2 years.
AML / Unlicensed Money Transmission

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008 and the Monetary Authority Act, civil penalties of up to BD$250,000 per breach; upon conviction, fines of up to BD$1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering offences.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for authorising unlicensed transmission; personal assets may be seized, travel bans imposed, the exemption certificate revoked, and the ELP excluded from Stripe and Escrow payment rails.
Loss of Limited Partnership Shield (Prohibited Management Participation)

Statutory sanction: Under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2014, a participating limited partner is treated as a general partner with unlimited personal liability for all debts and obligations of the ELP; the ELP's registration may be voided ab initio by court order, and the senior management may face disqualification proceedings.

Director personal liability: The limited partner's personal assets become directly attachable by creditors; directors of the corporate general partner face breach-of-fiduciary claims and civil restitution; Bermuda courts may issue charging orders and bankruptcy orders, and in aggravated fraud cases, imprisonment of up to 7 years with cross-border asset tracing.
