Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope (UK trade through a permanent establishment creates direct partner-level taxation); Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda historically imposes no income, capital gains, or withholding taxes, but the 15% Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 applies effective 1 January 2025 to multinational groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million, subject to Pillar Two carve-outs). The Bermuda ELP is statutorily transparent under Bermuda law and is treated as a qualifying partnership for UK tax purposes, so income is allocated to partners rather than taxed at entity level.

Available election forms

UK VAT and tax elections: (a) VAT grouping election under VATA 1994 s.43 treating the ELP and UK affiliates as one taxable person; (b) option to tax (waiver of exemption) on land and buildings; (c) self-billing and cash accounting/annual accounting scheme elections for aviation supply chains; (d) partial exemption annual adjustment elections. Bermuda: no local check-the-box election; however, the partnership agreement may elect continuity or dissolution provisions affecting US Form 8832 classification, and the ELP may elect to align with Bermuda CIT group reporting under the Economic Substance Regulations. Hybrid mismatch rules under UK TIOPA 2010 Part 6A and OECD hybrid model rules must be assessed if another jurisdiction treats the ELP as opaque.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 taxable turnover per rolling 12-month period for mandatory registration; deregistration threshold is GBP 88,000. Voluntary registration is permitted for a business making exclusively zero-rated supplies (including international inflight catering) to recover input VAT; there is no registration threshold where the supply of aircraft stores is zero-rated and the customer is an overseas airline.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under VATA 1994 Sch. 8 in two limbs: (1) Group 7 export of goods: direct export by the ELP to a non-UK destination with removal evidence retained within 3 months of supply, applicable where catering is shipped to an overseas airline outstation; (2) Group 15 aircraft stores: supply of inflight meal packages, beverages, and bonded catering supplies loaded onto an aircraft operated by a relevant airline on an international route departing from a UK airport, where the catering is delivered directly to the ramp and taken on board; the flight must be to a destination outside the UK. Services integral to the international flight (airport handling, tray assembly under bond, and ramp delivery) are treated as ancillary and follow the zero-rating of the aircraft stores. Input VAT on raw food, kitchen equipment, and handling services is fully recoverable if outputs are 100% zero-rated; partial exemption de minimis applies if UK domestic catering is also supplied. A CVD (Customs and VAT) authorisation for bonded input procurement may further reduce cash-flow cost.

  • Customs Export Declaration: Single Administrative Document (SAD)/Customs Declaration completed via HMRC Customs Declaration Service (CDS), or an Entry in Declarant's Records (EIDR) for high-volume catering exports, quoting the Movement Reference Number (MRN).
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (AWB): original or electronic transport document evidencing shipment on the specific international flight; the airline cargo manifest and general declaration may serve as supplementary proof.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: authenticated bank credit advice or SWIFT MT103 confirming foreign-currency settlement of the catering invoice within the 180-day repatriation window, cross-referenced to the relevant AWB and contract.
  • Signed Ramp Delivery Receipt (airline stores certificate): equivalent to Form C130/C134 aircraft stores certificate or airline accepting signature confirming goods were loaded on the international aircraft.
  • Executed Airline Catering Agreement and Purchase Order stating the route, flight number, and non-UK destination for the relevant sector.

No real-time fiscal clearance, government pre-approval, or digital stamping mandate exists in the UK. All VAT-registered businesses must comply with Making Tax Digital (MTD): maintain digital VAT records in functional compatible software and submit VAT Returns exclusively through the HMRC MTD API (no manual filing). E-invoices must contain the Directive 2010/45/EU data set; PEPPOL BIS, EDIFACT D96A, or airline-specific XML/API exchange is commercially customary for aviation catering. HMRC requires retained digital records for 6 years, and the invoice must include a clear reference to zero-rating (Group 15, Sch. 8 VATA 1994) where applicable.

HMRC may issue a supplementary VAT assessment under VATA 1994 s.73 clawing back the 20% VAT previously zero-rated where export or aircraft-stores evidence is deficient; the standard assessment window is 4 years, extended to 6 years for careless conduct and 20 years for deliberate fraud. Civil inaccuracy penalties under FA 2007 Sch. 24 apply: up to 30% for careless errors, 70% for deliberate errors, and 100% for deliberate and concealed errors. The C18 Civil Evasion Penalty of 100% of the tax evaded applies for dishonest conduct, and criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s.72(1) for fraudulent evasion carries imprisonment up to 7 years and unlimited fines. False customs declarations attract penalties under CEMA 1979 s.167 and s.170. Directors and controlling partners of the ELP are jointly and severally liable; HMRC may cancel the VAT registration, deny input VAT recovery for the entire audit period, and hold the airline customer jointly liable for unpaid VAT if it knowingly participated in unjustified zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the USD and circulates interchangeably with US currency. Bermuda maintains separate exchange-control treatment for 'resident' and 'non-resident' persons. The KY_ELP, structured as a non-resident Exempted Limited Partnership with non-Bermudian Limited Partners and a corporate General Partner located outside Bermuda, is classified as non-resident and therefore enjoys full current-account and capital-account convertibility: capital contributions, receipts from airline catering contracts, and profit distributions may be held and transferred in USD, GBP, EUR, or other freely convertible currencies without BMA authorization. Profit remittance hurdles are not FX-derived but documentary: (i) the annual BMA exempted partnership registration fee must be current; (ii) the ELP must maintain an accurate UBO register and file capital-movement updates; (iii) if profit remittance exceeds USD 100,000 per transaction, the paying bank applies enhanced due diligence and requires a distribution resolution and audited or management accounts; (iv) the ELP must ensure its non-resident election is recorded in the partnership agreement and reflected on the BMA certificate, otherwise a resident characterization would require BMA permission for any BMD-denominated outflow to a non-resident.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to non-resident exempted partnerships under Bermuda law. The Exchange Control Act 1972 imposes the obligation to offer and convert export proceeds to an authorized dealer only on 'resident' persons, who must typically convert foreign currency to BMD within three months of receipt unless the BMA grants a general or specific exemption. The KY_ELP, as a non-resident person, may retain 100% of export proceeds (including settlement proceeds from aviation inflight catering contracts) in foreign-currency external accounts indefinitely and may freely disburse them for working capital, distributions, or intercompany settlements. The treasury blueprint should preserve this exemption by: (i) obtaining a BMA non-resident designation confirmation at formation; (ii) ensuring all onboarding documents identify the ELP as non-resident; and (iii) maintaining a written exchange-control compliance memo so that any Bermuda-resident signatory does not accidentally trigger resident status through management-and-control activity within Bermuda.

Central bank supervision: The Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973, is the central bank and foreign exchange administration for Bermuda. The BMA administers the issue and redemption of the Bermudian dollar, supervises licensed banks under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, and enforces the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008. For an Exempted Limited Partnership designated as non-resident, the BMA exercises no prior approval over external-account capital flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary operating liquidity; Low for primary capital-escrow custody. UK-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and 'new-breed' digital banking platforms domiciled in the UK sovereign gateway offer rapid onboarding, GBP Faster Payments, SEPA EUR credit transfers, and virtual USD account numbers, which are suitable for the target rails 'Monthly_ACH' and medium-value aviation contract wires. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not licensed banks and cannot hold capital escrow as deposit money; funds are safeguarded e-money and carry no Bermuda Deposit Insurance or FSCS protection at the ELP's external-account tier; (ii) an offshore exempted partnership is frequently treated by EMI risk teams as a 'non-resident corporate vehicle' requiring UBO disclosure, a BMA non-resident certificate, and a UK economic nexus (UK branch, UK invoice flows, or UK-regulated introducer) to pass onboarding; (iii) EMIs cannot issue Commercial Letters of Credit, arrange deliverable FX forwards, or provide BMA-format exchange-control reporting; (iv) the BMA does not regulate EMIs, so any EMI balance creates an unlicensed counterparty risk and must be swept daily to the Butterfield or HSBC master account; (v) EMI account agreements frequently prohibit use as a sinking-fund or escrow account. Recommended architecture: deploy the EMI as a narrow collections and disbursement gateway capped below the GBP 85,000 FSCS-equivalent safeguarding threshold per counterparty, with the Bermuda external account designated as the treasury master.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary operating liquidity; Low for primary capital-escrow custody. UK-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and 'new-breed' digital banking platforms domiciled in the UK sovereign gateway offer rapid onboarding, GBP Faster Payments, SEPA EUR credit transfers, and virtual USD account numbers, which are suitable for the target rails 'Monthly_ACH' and medium-value aviation contract wires. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not licensed banks and cannot hold capital escrow as deposit money; funds are safeguarded e-money and carry no Bermuda Deposit Insurance or FSCS protection at the ELP's external-account tier; (ii) an offshore exempted partnership is frequently treated by EMI risk teams as a 'non-resident corporate vehicle' requiring UBO disclosure, a BMA non-resident certificate, and a UK economic nexus (UK branch, UK invoice flows, or UK-regulated introducer) to pass onboarding; (iii) EMIs cannot issue Commercial Letters of Credit, arrange deliverable FX forwards, or provide BMA-format exchange-control reporting; (iv) the BMA does not regulate EMIs, so any EMI balance creates an unlicensed counterparty risk and must be swept daily to the Butterfield or HSBC master account; (v) EMI account agreements frequently prohibit use as a sinking-fund or escrow account. Recommended architecture: deploy the EMI as a narrow collections and disbursement gateway capped below the GBP 85,000 FSCS-equivalent safeguarding threshold per counterparty, with the Bermuda external account designated as the treasury master.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda statutory threshold; Bermuda does not impose transfer pricing documentation requirements. UK CFC and transfer pricing rules may apply indirectly if the ELP is controlled by a UK-resident entity; a UK Local File becomes mandatory when the aggregate value of relevant transactions exceeds £5 million intra-group and the UK entity is within the UK transfer pricing regime.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2018 require an exempted partnership carrying on a relevant activity to demonstrate economic substance in Bermuda by having its core income-generating activities, physical presence, employees, and management in Bermuda. Inflight catering operations constitute a service/distribution centre relevant activity. The ELP must file an annual ESR return and maintain adequate records of income, expenses, and operational activity; failure causes automatic exchange of information with the UK and potential penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Misrepresentation of Presence or Income)

Statutory sanction: A fine not exceeding BMD 50,000, imprisonment for up to 2 years, and daily default penalties of BMD 100. The Registrar may strike the ELP from the register and confiscate the tax exemption certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors, partners, or managers who authorised or knowingly permitted the false return may be personally liable for fines and imprisonment; the Supreme Court may also disqualify them from acting as directors or managers of any Bermudian entity for up to 5 years and grant civil restitution orders for unpaid taxes or penalties.
Economic Substance Non-Compliance (Shell Company)

Statutory sanction: An administrative penalty equal to BMD 10,000 for the first offence; subsequent offences attract up to BMD 10,000 plus daily penalties of BMD 100. The Registrar may exchange all information with the UK tax authority, leading to reclassification and potential UK tax charges. A strike-off from the register is mandatory for prolonged non-compliance.

Director personal liability: The general partner and each limited partner participating in management may have the corporate veil pierced; they are jointly and severally liable for penalties, and the Bermuda Court may issue travel bans preventing departure from Bermuda and asset-freeze orders pending restitution.
AML and Sanctions (Proceeds of Crime Act 2007)

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Proceeds of Crime Act 2007 and the Anti-Money Laundering Regulations 2008 exposes the partnership to an unlimited fine; money laundering offences carry imprisonment up to 14 years. The Financial Sanctions Act 2023 imposes fines up to BMD 250,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years for sanctions violations.

Director personal liability: A designated compliance officer, general partner, or director who fails to discharge AML obligations may be personally fined and imprisoned. The Supreme Court may order freezes, forfeiture of the catering kitchen assets, and prohibition orders from holding any financial services licence or acting as an accountable person in Bermuda.
Failure to Maintain Statutory Records and Audit Trail

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may impose a maximum fine of BMD 10,000 and daily default fines of BMD 100, and may direct that the partnership be wound up or struck off for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: The manager or general partner is personally liable to the extent of the partnership's unsettled liabilities if records are destroyed or concealed, and may be subject to a disqualification order and an order to make good the deficiency from personal assets.
