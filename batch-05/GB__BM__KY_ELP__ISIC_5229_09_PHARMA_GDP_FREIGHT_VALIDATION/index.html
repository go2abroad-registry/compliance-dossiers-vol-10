Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC) and the Registrar of Companies, Government of Bermuda; official portals: www.gov.bm/department/office-tax-commissioner and www.bermudabusinessregistry.bm. Customs matters are administered by the Bermuda Customs and Revenue Department (www.gov.bm/customs). Social Insurance and Payroll Tax are administered by the Department of Social Insurance (www.gov.bm/social-insurance).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2012 and obtain the Certificate of Registration and ELP number. Step 2: Apply to the OTC for a Corporate Income Tax (CIT) registration only if the ELP is a constituent entity of a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more, per the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023; absent such scope, no income tax registration is required as Bermuda levies no income tax on ELPs. Step 3: For customs purposes, obtain an importer/exporter code from Bermuda Customs and register the ELP as a declarant if it will move goods through Bermuda. Step 4: If the ELP employs staff in Bermuda, register for payroll tax (Bermuda Payroll Tax Act 1995), social insurance, and pension health contributions. Step 5: Obtain a Bermuda business register ('licence') number from the Bermuda Business Registry if carrying on business in Bermuda from a Bermuda place of business; offshore non-resident ELPs with no Bermuda operations may obtain an exemption.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Bermuda ELP is statutorily fiscally transparent: partnership income, gains, and losses flow through to partners, and Bermuda imposes no income, corporation, capital gains, estate, or withholding tax on ELP distributions or on non-resident partners' Bermuda-source income. The ELP is also outside Bermuda's payroll tax unless it has Bermuda employees. The only material entity-level exposure is the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (effective 1 January 2025) for ELPs that are part of a multinational group exceeding the EUR 750 million revenue threshold, applying a 15% top-up tax on Bermuda profits in line with OECD Pillar Two. Since the underlying activity is offshore GDP pharma freight logistics without local Bermuda nexus, the ELP is expected to maintain a pure offshore tax classification.

Available election forms

Bermuda has no check-the-box or entity classification election because ELP pass-through status is fixed by statute under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2012. Where the ELP is a constituent entity inside a Pillar Two multinational group, the Bermuda CIT regime permits transitional safe harbour elections under the GloBE rules (Transitional Country-by-Country Reporting Safe Harbour and Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax election). For hybrid mismatch purposes, Bermuda law contains no formal hybrid election, but the ELP may rely on its transparency treatment under Bermuda law and on the foreign parent jurisdiction's CFC/anti-hybrid rules; all intercompany arrangements must be documented to evidence that no hybrid deduction/non-inclusion outcome arises. Any election to apply the QDMTT must be filed with the OTC through the prescribed CIT return portal within the statutory filing window.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None for general sales or value added tax: Bermuda imposes no VAT, GST, or national sales tax. Indirect taxation is limited to (i) customs import duties under the Customs Tariff Act 1971, (ii) stamp duties under the Stamp Duty Act 1976, (iii) fuel and airport departure taxes, and (iv) employer payroll tax. Digital services, freight forwarding, and professional fees invoiced by a Bermuda ELP are outside any Bermuda VAT/GST, and no invoice-level tax is chargeable. For the UK parent/PPH context, UK VAT may apply to cross-border pharmaceutical logistics services depending on place-of-supply rules (B2B reverse charge), but that is outside Bermuda's indirect tax code.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A for any turnover-based VAT/GST threshold because Bermuda has none. For customs import duty: any person bringing goods into Bermuda (including the ELP acting as importer of record) must obtain an importer registration code from Bermuda Customs and post a customs surety/bond where applicable; import duty tariffs run from 0% (e.g., certain medical/pharmaceutical supplies) to a maximum of 27.25% (passenger vehicles and luxury goods), assessed on the customs valuation (CIF or transaction value). There is no statutory economic turnover threshold for customs registration. No Bermuda indirect tax registration threshold applies to the ELP's export services because services are outside scope.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable: Bermuda has no VAT/GST against which an export zero-rating can be claimed. Consequently, the ELP's export pharmaceutical logistics services are 'out of scope' of any Bermuda indirect tax and require no zero-rating substantiation. For customs purposes, goods transshipped, in transit, bonded, or re-exported from Bermuda are relieved from import duty under Sections 25 and 27 of the Customs Tariff Act 1971, provided the goods are exported within the statutory time limits (normally 30 days for transshipment with extension), under the supervision of Bermuda Customs. Completing the customs import/export declaration with the correct procedure code (e.g., 70-00 for re-export) and preserving the outward manifest is essential to substantiate the duty relief; services are not subject to customs duty at all.

  • Customs Export Declaration / electronic transit-transshipment declaration filed through ASYCUDA World (Bermuda Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical cross-border movement of the pharmaceutical goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice evidencing the freight fee paid by the offshore customer
  • Original import declaration (when goods were landed in Bermuda) with matching re-export entry references for duty relief substantiation
  • Pharmaceutical GDP compliance records (temperature-logger reports, stability documentation, GDP apprenticeship certificate or GDP-qualified-person declarations) evidencing the regulated nature of the logistics service
  • Commercial invoice and packing list in the ELP's name, with Incoterms and delivery terms stated

No real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, QR code, or digital stamping mandate exists in Bermuda for VAT or income tax. Customs clearance is processed electronically via the Bermuda Customs Automated System (CECAS/ASYCUDA World) using electronic Single Administrative Document declarations; no continuous transaction control (CTC) API requirement applies. Issuance of commercial invoices is governed by ordinary commercial practice, and the ELP's freight invoices should contain the partner's VAT ID where relevant to EU/UK reverse-charge rules, but there is no Bermuda government clearance stamping requirement.

Because no VAT/GST export mechanism exists, there is no VAT export fraud clawback regime. However, Bermuda Customs may audit transshipment and re-export entries; any fraudulent or erroneous claim of duty relief (e.g., goods diverted to domestic use without payment of import duty) triggers penalties under the Customs Tariff Act 1971 and the Customs Act 1949, including forfeiture of the goods, fines of up to three times the unpaid duty, and imprisonment up to two years on conviction. For Pillar Two purposes, a Bermuda ELP that artificially routes freight income to avoid UK or other group taxation may be scrutinized under the Bermuda CIT anti-avoidance provisions, UK CFC rules, and the OECD anti-BEPS rules; penalties for misdeclared CIT top-up tax can reach 100% of the unpaid top-up tax plus interest, with retrospective adjustment up to 6 years under the Bermuda Limitation Act 1984. All FX repatriation receipts and customs documentation should be retained for a minimum of 7 years to support audit defense.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates a currency-board regime with the Bermuda dollar pegged 1:1 to the US dollar. Current account payments are freely convertible and require no BMA approval; capital account flows (contributions, distributions, intercompany loans) are also fully convertible for exempted partnerships, with no profit repatriation tax or withholding. The practical hurdles are not exchange-control limits but: (i) beneficial ownership and source-of-funds verification by the Bermuda licensed bank; (ii) BMA economic substance and compliance filings if the ELP is considered carrying on geographically mobile activity; (iii) payment-reference/purpose-code documentation necessary for both the remitting and beneficiary banks to classify the flow under BOP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Bermuda does not impose any statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio; exporters and exempted partnerships may maintain proceeds in foreign currency accounts at BMA-licensed banks. For exempted partnerships not resident for exchange-control purposes, all foreign currency receipts may be retained. Banks must still report suspicious transactions and sanction matters, and include foreign assets in annual BMA returns under the Exchange Control Act 1972. There is no enforced statutory retention period; beneficial use is immediate upon entry into the Bermuda banking system.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the foreign exchange administration and currency-board-style custodian of the Bermuda dollar; ultimate monetary sovereignty resides with the United Kingdom but operational FX control is delegated to the BMA.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for a Bermuda-regulated primary bank account. Bermuda has no fintech EMI licensing regime analogous to the UK electronic money institution authorization; a digital wallet provider would need a BMA banking or digital asset business licence under the Digital Asset Business Act 2018. UK/EEA EMIs can be used as upstream GBP clearing nodes for SEPA/Faster Payments, but they cannot open the ELP's principal treasury account or clear USD/BMD domestic transactions. The viable structure is: Bermuda licensed bank as settlement account plus UK EMI as collection-only rail for low-value payments, with daily sweeping to the licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing uses SWIFT MT103 and MT202 COVER through a BMA-licensed correspondent. The remitting bank must be a recognised financial institution and the beneficiary line must show the ELP name and account with the Bermuda bank. Payment narrative must state 'capital contribution', 'subscription', or 'loan to exempted partnership' and include the partnership's registration number and the capital-call notice number. For UK-origin remittances, the outward payment purpose code should reflect direct investment and the remitter must complete the bank's Source of Funds and Anti-Money Laundering questionnaire. BMA uses the authorised-dealer bank to validate conversion into BMD only if requested; otherwise settlement remains in USD/GBP. The funds are held in a capital escrow account until the bank clears beneficial ownership, source-of-wealth and economic sanction checks; after verification they are released to the operational account.Trade disbursements for GDP pharma logistics should clear over a two-rail structure: (i) low-value payments and invoice settlements in GBP/SEPA via Direct ACH/Faster Payments through the UK EMI or clearing partner; (ii) high-value invoice payments, freight settlements and LC settlements via SWIFT MT103/MT700 against USD. For Bermuda-dollar payables, conversion at 1:1 with same-day local clearing through the Bermuda Automated Clearing House is used. Commercial letters of credit should be issued under UCP600 via the licensed bank, with the beneficiary presenting full GDP/cold-chain compliance documentation; for ACH, use standard corporate credit transfer with remittance data including invoice number, GDP certificate references, Incoterms and good-arrival confirmation. Bermuda has no fintech EMI licensing regime analogous to the UK electronic money institution authorization; a digital wallet provider would need a BMA banking or digital asset business licence under the Digital Asset Business Act 2018. UK/EEA EMIs can be used as upstream GBP clearing nodes for SEPA/Faster Payments, but they cannot open the ELP's principal treasury account or clear USD/BMD domestic transactions. The viable structure is: Bermuda licensed bank as settlement account plus UK EMI as collection-only rail for low-value payments, with daily sweeping to the licensed bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No BMD statutory threshold. Bermuda has no domestic transfer pricing documentation rules and does not require Local File or Master File submission. OECD BEPS-aligned documentation may nevertheless be required in the United Kingdom or other active jurisdictions if the ELP enters into controlled transactions with UK affiliates and exceeds UK transfer pricing thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 applies to companies and partnerships carrying on relevant activities including banking, insurance, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, shipping, intellectual property, distribution, and service centres. A pharmaceutical GDP freight logistics entity is not automatically a relevant activity unless it functions as a distribution centre or service centre with substantial operations in Bermuda. Where the ELP is centrally managed and controlled from Bermuda and derives pure freight forwarding income, it must maintain real physical presence, local employees, and active direction to avoid substance-based penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Artificial Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Bermudian authorities may impose economic substance penalties of up to BMD 250,000, dissolve the partnership, and apply for a winding-up order. HM Revenue & Customs in the UK may also apply diverted profits tax of 25% on taxable diverted profits and raise civil penalties of up to 100% of unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and the General Partner may be held personally liable for civil restitution, subjected to travel bans, and, in the case of intentional concealment or fraud, prosecuted for criminal tax evasion carrying imprisonment in the UK or Bermuda.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from the Registrar, monetary penalties, restriction of voting rights, and potential court-ordered de-registration or dissolution under the Economic Substance Act.

Director personal liability: Partners of the ELP lose the corporate veil and may be jointly and severally liable for partnership debts; directors face disqualification, travel restrictions, and personal orders to make good any diverted sums.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Violation

Statutory sanction: Bermuda's beneficial ownership legislation imposes severe financial penalties, default fines, and, in the case of false filings, criminal sanctions including custodial sentences. The BMA may also revoke or suspend the ELP's approval and freeze bank accounts held with Bermudian licensee banks.

Director personal liability: General Partners, directorship figures, and compliance officers may be personally fined, imprisoned, banned from acting as directors or partners in Bermuda, and detained pending travel bans where an AML offence is disclosed.
Unlawful GDP Pharmaceutical Freight Operations

Statutory sanction: The UK MHRA may suspend or revoke any wholesale distribution authorisation, seize controlled goods, and refer the matter to HMRC. The ELP may face cancellation of its Bermudian registry status if the unlawful activity is disclosed.

Director personal liability: Directors and the General Partner may face unlimited personal liability for regulatory penalties and third-party claims for unsafe or non-compliant pharmaceutical handling, including travel bans and criminal prosecution if gross negligence or wilful misconduct is proven.
Failure to Maintain Economic Substance in Distribution Centre

Statutory sanction: The Registrar may impose substance penalties, report the entity to the Tax Commissioner, and, in cases of gross default, apply to the court for forced surrender of the partnership's certificate.

Director personal liability: General partners and any local directors may be issued with personal compliance orders, assessed for financial restitution, prohibited from managing a Bermudian entity in the future, and subjected to travel bans pending resolution of the enforcement action.
