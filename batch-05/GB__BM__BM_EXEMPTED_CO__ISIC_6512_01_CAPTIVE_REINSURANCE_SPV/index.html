E-government filing portal

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A customs duty drawback may be claimed under the Customs Act 1961 for tangible goods re-exported from Bermuda within statutory time limits, but this is not a VAT mechanism and is outside the scope of this captive reinsurance SPV's services.

  • No VAT export zero-rating exists in Bermuda; a mandatory VAT export document bundle is therefore not applicable.
  • If a customs duty drawback were claimed (not applicable to a captive reinsurance SPV), Bermuda Customs would require the original import entry, evidence of re-export (bill of lading or airway bill), and duty payment records.

None. Bermuda does not impose any real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance API, or digital stamping mandate. Customs import declarations are processed electronically through BRA's customs cargo declaration system, but this is not a VAT/GST e-invoicing or fiscal clearance regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged at par to USD (BMD 1.00 = USD 1.00) and both currencies circulate interchangeably in Bermuda. Under the Exchange Control Act 1972, a Bermuda exempted company is designated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes and is therefore unrestricted in opening and operating multi-currency accounts, holding USD/GBP/EUR balances, and making outward remittances without prior BMA approval. Both current-account flows (intra-group reinsurance premiums and claim recoveries) and capital-account flows (paid-up share capital injection, subordinated loan advances, dividend and surplus remittances) are fully convertible with no repatriation moratorium. Profit and capital remittance to the UK parent faces no Bermuda withholding tax and no exchange-control impediment; the UK similarly maintains full capital-account convertibility and imposes no requirement for Treasury consent on outbound equity funding to a Bermuda captive, only bank-side AML, sanctions screening, and balance-of-payments purpose classification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Bermuda imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no central-bank surrender ledger for foreign-currency receipts, including reinsurance premium collections, investment income, or recoveries. The Class 3A captive reinsurer may retain all proceeds in the currency of denomination or convert freely at arm's length through its Bermuda bank. Neither the UK nor Bermuda applies surrender, forced conversion, or ear-marking rules to cross-border reinsurance proceeds; only record-keeping obligations under the Bermuda Anti-Money Laundering / Anti-Terrorist Financing Code of Practice 2015 (and its 2018 amendments) apply.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) - the central bank and prudential regulator administering the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, Exchange Control Act 1972, and Insurance Act 1978; UK competency is shared between HM Treasury (sanctions and financial-crime policy), the Bank of England (Sterling clearing and macro-prudential oversight), and the Financial Conduct Authority (e-money institution licensing).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low as a core treasury venue but High as an auxiliary payment wallet. An FCA-regulated UK electronic money institution (EMI) cannot hold the BMA's statutory minimum solvency margin, maintain Bermuda-admissible assets, or act as custodian for policyholder trust collateral required under the Insurance Act 1978; those functions must sit with a licensed Bermuda bank or an approved foreign banking institution. The EMI is viable only for low-value, high-frequency UK/EEA claims disbursements and administrative expense payments, with a daily zero-balance sweep back to the Bermuda master account; the captive's board-approved treasury mandate and the BMA-reviewed investment policy must expressly permit such wallets.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The UK parent injects the Class 3A statutory minimum paid-up share capital of BMD 250,000 (plus any additional capital and surplus approved by the board) via SWIFT MT103 to the captive's account at HSBC Bank Bermuda Limited or Butterfield Bank. Payment narrative and bank-level balance-of-payments purpose code must read 'investment in equity - wholly-owned subsidiary' with reference to the entity name; the transfer is routed via the bank's London/New York correspondents over CHAPS/Fedwire and posted in USD/BMD at par. Before funds are released the bank collects the capital-authorisation board minute, issued share certificates, and an officer's certificate; the BMA requires the minimum and any additional share capital to be fully paid in cash in advance of licence issuance, evidenced in the statutory solvency certificate filed under the Insurance Act 1978.Claims, return premiums, and retrocession settlements are cleared via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for correspondent transfers), using Fedwire/CHIPS for USD, CHAPS/Faster Payments for GBP payments to the UK cedant, and SEPA through correspondents for EUR. 0% statutory headline rate for Bermuda exempted companies; 15% Corporate Income Tax (CIT) under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 for in-scope multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more (effective for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda levies no withholding tax on dividends paid by exempted companies to non-resident shareholders.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Part A — Entity Identification Constituent entity name, Bermuda company registration number, registered office address, ultimate parent entity identifier, and jurisdiction of tax residence. Non-resident-owned exempted companies must enter the UK ultimate parent company name and country of tax residence, and appoint a BRA-approved filing agent to submit the return. Failure to file or pay within the prescribed time attracts fixed monetary penalties under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, daily default surcharges for continued non-compliance, and interest on unpaid tax. Negligent or fraudulent understatement may trigger civil penalties of up to 100% of the understated tax, with enforcement against directors where the company defaults.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Filing Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to 100% of the understated tax, interest on overdue tax, fixed penalties and daily default surcharges under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023; criminal prosecution for fraud with fines at the court's discretion.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who cause or participate in defaults may be jointly and severally liable for restitution of taxes and penalties; Bermuda courts may pierce the corporate veil for fraud; travel bans and imprisonment of up to 5 years may be imposed.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties: BMD 10,000 initial fine, daily penalty up to BMD 5,000, and fines up to BMD 250,000 for false declarations; automatic exchange of information with HMRC; mandatory corrective measures; potential striking-off from the Register.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for failure to ensure substance; court-imposed disqualification, travel restrictions, restitution of economic benefits, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for knowingly false declarations.
Insurance Regulatory and AML/ATF Violation

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed insurance business: fine up to BMD 20,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; each day of continued operation constitutes a separate offence. AML non-compliance: fines up to BMD 250,000 and regulatory sanctions; the Bermuda Monetary Authority may revoke licenses and freeze assets.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability, civil restitution for losses, disqualification orders, asset freezes, and imprisonment; senior management responsible for AML breaches may be personally fined and banned from the financial services sector.
Fraudulent Misrepresentation / Concealment of Ownership

Statutory sanction: Fine up to BMD 250,000, imprisonment up to 5 years, daily penalties under the Companies Act and ESR framework; the Registrar may strike off the company and exchange information with HMRC.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are personally liable; penalties include disqualification, restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment; the corporate veil will be pierced where the company is used as a sham or facade.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: BRA and GloBE adjustments increasing top-up tax; penalties up to 100% of the additional tax assessed; interest accrual; automatic exchange of information with HMRC under bilateral arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for failure to maintain records and for approving artificial pricing arrangements; civil restitution and officer-level fines apply, with potential criminal sanctions for fraud.
