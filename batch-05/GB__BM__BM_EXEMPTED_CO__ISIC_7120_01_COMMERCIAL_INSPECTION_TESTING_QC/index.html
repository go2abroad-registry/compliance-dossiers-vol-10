Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda: Registrar of Companies, Ministry of Finance, 4th Floor, Government Administration Building, 30 Parliament Street, Hamilton (portal: www.roc.gov.bm); direct tax matters fall under the Office of the Tax Commissioner (no income tax applies to exempted companies). United Kingdom: HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), 100 Parliament Street, London SW1A 2BQ (portal: www.gov.uk/hmrc), competent for any UK VAT or Corporation Tax nexus of the Bermuda entity.

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company has no statutory tax TIN because Bermuda levies no corporate income tax or VAT; the entity's identifier is the Registrar company number appearing on the Certificate of Incorporation, obtained by filing the Memorandum of Association and eConsent through a licensed registered agent via the Bermuda Online Gateway. For UK VAT, if the entity makes taxable supplies in the UK or its UK taxable turnover exceeds GBP 90,000 in a rolling 12-month period, it must register on the HMRC Government Gateway (Form VAT1) and obtain a 9-digit UK VAT number; HMRC may require an anti-fraud registration security deposit for non-UK applicants. If UK Corporation Tax applies because central management and control is exercised in the UK, the company must register via HMRC Form CT41G within 3 months of commencing UK residence and will obtain a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda income, capital gains, withholding, and dividend taxes under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, with the statutory no-tax guarantee extended to 31 March 2035). Territorial Scope: Bermuda imposes no corporate tax on worldwide or Bermuda-sourced income; however, the entity is exposed to UK Corporation Tax at 19% (small profits rate) / 25% (main rate) if centrally managed and controlled in the UK, and to UK VAT on UK taxable supplies.

Available election forms

(i) The Bermuda company may apply to the Minister of Finance under s 2(3) of the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 for a Certificate of Tax Protection confirming that no income, withholding, capital gains, dividend, or estate tax will be imposed until 31 March 2035. (ii) Under the UK hybrid mismatch regime (CTA 2010 Part 6A), the company may file elections pursuant to s 657 and s 695 to neutralise or reduce hybrid mismatches in cross-border arrangements involving the Bermuda entity. (iii) No check-the-box election exists in UK or Bermuda law; for any US federal tax exposure, a Form 8832 entity classification election (US Treasury Reg. §301.7701-3) may be filed to determine flow-through or corporation status for US purposes. (iv) UK tax residence is not elective; it is determined by factual central management and control, and the entity may avoid UK residence by retaining all board meetings, statutory records, and strategic decision-making in Bermuda.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 of UK taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period (mandatory UK VAT registration threshold from 1 April 2024); voluntary UK registration is available below the threshold. Bermuda has no VAT/GST, so no equivalent registration threshold exists in Bermuda.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The Bermuda Exempted Company may rely on UK VAT zero-rating or outside-the-scope treatment for on-site factory inspection, pre-shipment quality control, and technical testing services in two scenarios: (a) services directly related to goods physically exported from the UK (VATA 1994 Sch 8 Group 14) with goods removed outside Great Britain and Northern Ireland; and (b) B2B supplies of testing and analysis services to an overseas business customer where the place of supply falls outside the UK under VATA 1994 Sch 4A and Article 44 of the Principal VAT Directive. Where the inspection is performed at a non-UK factory for a non-UK business customer, the supply is outside the scope of UK VAT. Bermuda levies no VAT, so no domestic export zero-rating or refund mechanism arises in Bermuda.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export itinerary, exporter, consignee, and port/airport of departure
  • Signed Commercial Invoice describing the inspection/QC services and the goods tested
  • Duly executed Inspection Report / Certificate of Conformity / Factory Audit Report

United Kingdom: no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), or digital stamping mandate. Mandatory Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT requires digital bookkeeping and quarterly VAT Return submission via HMRC-approved API software (Xero, Sage, QuickBooks, etc.) by the 9th working day after each VAT quarter; e-invoices are permissible only with customer consent and must preserve integrity of content. Bermuda: no e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or stamping regime exists.

UK HMRC may open a compliance check and issue a VAT assessment under VATA 1994 s 73 for under-declared VAT, with interest under s 74. Deliberate or concealed inaccuracies in export zero-rating claims attract a penalty of up to 100% of the tax at risk under FA 2007 Sch 24, and a pattern of failed export evidence can trigger retrospective deregistration and removal of zero-rating. Civil evasion penalties under FA 2003 s 35 and criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s 72 apply where false documents or fraudulent intent are evidenced in export substantiation. A finding that the Bermuda entity has a UK fixed establishment can retroactively bring all UK-related supplies within the UK VAT net, with retrospective VAT, penalties, and interest. The EUTPA 1966 guarantee operates only for Bermuda taxes and does not shield the entity from UK VAT assessments or penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

BMD is pegged at 1:1 to the US Dollar and USD circulates as parallel legal tender. The Exchange Control Act 1972 (as amended) imposes no capital or current account exchange controls; BMA consent is not required for equity subscriptions, loan injections, dividend distributions, trade settlement, or profit remittance. Convertibility is full for both current and capital account transactions. The only practical hurdles are bank-level Source-of-Funds and Economic Substance Committee reviews: a resident Bermuda bank will require a valid Tax Compliance Certificate (TCC), a filed Economic Substance Declaration (ESD), and audited or compiled financial statements before executing high-value remittances. For a Bermuda Exempted Company holding an active trade (factory inspection/testing receivables), an ESD filing as a non-pure-equity-holding entity is mandatory if the company has income from that trade; a pure equity holding exemption is available only where the company does not carry on the inspection business itself.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Bermuda imposes no statutory surrender ratio, no mandatory conversion timeline, and no repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Inspection and testing fee receipts in USD, BMD, or other convertible currencies may be retained offshore at the company's discretion. Resident banks file statistical balance-of-payments reports under the Exchange Control Act 1972 for monitoring purposes only; there is no monetary conversion obligation. The operative restriction is evidentiary: the bank will ask for the underlying inspection invoices, ISO/IEC 17020 accreditation, and a reconciliation ledger to demonstrate that retained proceeds correspond to bona fide commercial service income, not undisclosed intra-group financing or crypto-adjacent flows.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), Hamilton, Bermuda. The BMA is the integrated financial services regulator, currency authority, and de facto central bank under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969 (as amended). Bermuda operates a quasi-currency-board regime for the Bermudian Dollar (BMD) rather than a conventional discretionary central bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Rating: Low as the primary treasury or capital account rail; Medium as a supplementary low-value disbursement rail. Legal constraints: the BMA does not license EMIs and only banks licensed under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 may take deposits in or from Bermuda. An FCA-authorised EMI (e.g., Wise) is therefore neither recognised as a Bermudian credit institution nor permitted to clear BMD or to service the statutory capital account of a Bermuda Exempted Company. Wise Business may be used operationally for sub-USD 50,000 per-transaction outbound payments (per-diem QC staff, travel, software subscriptions, marketing) routed through its UK/EEA partner banking stack, but it cannot receive capital injection, hold TCC-related escrow, or perform settlement of commercial letters of credit. The compliant architecture is nested: UK parent maintains an FCA-authorised GBP/USD account and Wise Business for corridor efficiency; the Bermuda Exempted Company holds its primary operational and escrow accounts at HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield to preserve the BMA-regulated audit trail.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Low as the primary treasury or capital account rail; Medium as a supplementary low-value disbursement rail. Legal constraints: the BMA does not license EMIs and only banks licensed under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 may take deposits in or from Bermuda. An FCA-authorised EMI (e.g., Wise) is therefore neither recognised as a Bermudian credit institution nor permitted to clear BMD or to service the statutory capital account of a Bermuda Exempted Company. Wise Business may be used operationally for sub-USD 50,000 per-transaction outbound payments (per-diem QC staff, travel, software subscriptions, marketing) routed through its UK/EEA partner banking stack, but it cannot receive capital injection, hold TCC-related escrow, or perform settlement of commercial letters of credit. The compliant architecture is nested: UK parent maintains an FCA-authorised GBP/USD account and Wise Business for corridor efficiency; the Bermuda Exempted Company holds its primary operational and escrow accounts at HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield to preserve the BMA-regulated audit trail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No transfer pricing documentation is required for a Bermuda exempted company outside CITA. For a multinational group with consolidated revenue equal to or exceeding EUR 750 million, CITA requires a Master File and Local File in accordance with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. The local currency equivalent threshold is BMD 750 million (BMD is pegged to USD and EUR threshold is applied by reference to the parent company's consolidated revenue).

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2018 apply only to Bermuda exempted companies carrying on a relevant activity (banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, holding company, intellectual property, distribution, or service center). Commercial inspection and testing is not a listed relevant activity, and therefore no ESR substance test is triggered by the core business. However, if the company also provides centralised administrative services to affiliates, it may fall into the 'service center' category and must demonstrate sufficient physical presence, employees, and expenditure in Bermuda. Non-compliance attracts fines of BMD 10,000 to BMD 200,000 and possible strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years, fine up to BMD 250,000, plus a civil penalty of 150% of the tax underpaid with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate are personally liable for the company's unpaid tax, face fines up to BMD 100,000, travel bans, and 10-year disqualification from acting as director.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Strike-off and dissolution by the Registrar, penalty under ESR of BMD 10,000 to BMD 200,000, potential referral to UK HMRC, and freezing of bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for any fine, may be banned from acting as a company director, and may be subject to a travel prohibition order. UK tax authorities may treat the company as a tax resident in the UK, exposing the individual directors to assessment on worldwide income.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violation

Statutory sanction: Corporate fine up to BMD 1,000,000, confiscation of proceeds, and suspension or revocation of trade licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers may be imprisoned for up to 14 years, fined up to BMD 500,000, and subjected to civil asset recovery. Personal assets may be seized to the extent of unjust enrichment.
