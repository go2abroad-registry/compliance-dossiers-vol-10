Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Bermuda Ministry of Finance — Office of the Tax Commissioner (OTC), the Registrar of Companies (Bermuda Business Register), and the Bermuda Customs Department (UNCTAD ASYCUDA World platform); UK HMRC retains authority only for any UK permanent establishment, UK source income or UK-seated partners, while Bermuda itself is outside the UK VAT and UK corporation tax territory.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP): (1) appoint a Bermuda-licensed legal/corporate services provider; (2) file the declaration of limited partnership with the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992 with the statutory application fee; (3) obtain the Certificate of Registration carrying the permanent ELP registration number, which functions as the TIN-equivalent for banking, customs and tax filings; (4) apply to the Minister of Finance for non-resident partnership status where business will not be carried on in Bermuda; (5) register for payroll tax and social insurance with the OTC only if the ELP has Bermuda employees; (6) register with Bermuda Customs as an exporter/shipyard declarant and enrol for ASYCUDA World electronic filing; no general income-tax TIN is issued because exempted partnerships are outside Bermuda income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — a Bermuda ELP is fiscally transparent by default: no entity-level Bermuda income/corporation tax; profits, losses and credits flow through to partners under the limited partnership agreement; pass-through is automatic unless (a) the partnership affirmatively elects corporate classification under U.S. check-the-box rules, or (b) the partnership is a flow-through constituent entity of an MNE group with consolidated revenue above the Bermuda CIT Act 2023 threshold (EUR 750m), in which case the 15% qualified domestic minimum top-up tax may apply to its Bermuda profits. — For a foreign-owned Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP): (1) appoint a Bermuda-licensed legal/corporate services provider; (2) file the declaration of limited partnership with the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992 with the statutory application fee; (3) obtain the Certificate of Registration carrying the permanent ELP registration number, which functions as the TIN-equivalent for banking, customs and tax filings; (4) apply to the Minister of Finance for non-resident partnership status where business will not be carried on in Bermuda; (5) register for payroll tax and social insurance with the OTC only if the ELP has Bermuda employees; (6) register with Bermuda Customs as an exporter/shipyard declarant and enrol for ASYCUDA World electronic filing; no general income-tax TIN is issued because exempted partnerships are outside Bermuda income tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a Bermuda ELP is fiscally transparent by default: no entity-level Bermuda income/corporation tax; profits, losses and credits flow through to partners under the limited partnership agreement; pass-through is automatic unless (a) the partnership affirmatively elects corporate classification under U.S. check-the-box rules, or (b) the partnership is a flow-through constituent entity of an MNE group with consolidated revenue above the Bermuda CIT Act 2023 threshold (EUR 750m), in which case the 15% qualified domestic minimum top-up tax may apply to its Bermuda profits.

Available election forms

(a) Non-resident partnership election under the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992 (tax and exchange-control neutrality for offshore activities); (b) U.S. Form 8832 check-the-box election to treat the ELP as a corporation or disregarded entity for U.S. federal tax purposes; (c) UK hybrid-mismatch neutralisation positions under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A where UK partners are involved; (d) Bermuda CIT Act 2023 elections, including QDMTT safe-harbour calculations, IIR/UTPR entity designations and flow-through allocation statements for in-scope multinational groups.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Bermuda imposes no VAT, GST or sales and use tax; indirect taxation is limited to customs import duty (Customs Act 1960 and tariff), stamp duty on specific instruments and payroll tax; UK VAT at 20% does not apply to supplies made in or from Bermuda and arises only when a UK customer imports the finished vessel into the UK.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Bermuda; customs duty is charged per importation on consumption and may be zero-rated for qualifying industrial inputs used by a licensed commercial shipyard; the economic turnover trigger for Bermuda CIT registration is EUR 750m annual consolidated group revenue in at least one of the two preceding fiscal years.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as VAT zero-rating because Bermuda has no VAT. Exports of newly built ocean container vessels, components and construction services from Bermuda are outside the scope of Bermuda indirect taxation, so no output tax is charged. A customs duty drawback and 0% industrial input rate are available: import duty paid on components incorporated into a vessel exported to a non-Bermuda customer is refunded under the drawback provisions of the Customs Act 1960, provided the ELP holds a shipyard licence, identifies the duty-paid inputs in the vessel cost build-up, and files the complete export bundle within 12 months of the vessel's departure. UK-side VAT arises only in the hands of the UK purchaser on importation at 20% (with possible reliefs), never as a Bermuda export charge.

  • ASYCUDA World electronic Customs Export Declaration (pre-departure filing with customs reference number)
  • Original or certified negotiable Bill of Lading (or sea waybill where short-sea/highway feeder)
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment evidence from LC/escrow settlement
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list identifying shipyard inputs
  • Import entry and duty payment records supporting the drawback computation
  • Certificate of deletion or export from the Bermuda Registry of Ships for the delivered vessel
  • Shipyard licence reference and owner's attestation of final destination outside Bermuda

Electronic customs clearance is mandatory through the Bermuda Customs automated system built on UNCTAD ASYCUDA World; all exports, including ocean container vessels cleared from the shipyard, require an approved electronic export declaration filed before departure; there is no real-time fiscal invoice clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping of invoices in Bermuda, and B2B invoicing remains free-format under the common law.

Bermuda Customs and the Ministry of Finance may audit export and drawback claims for a statutory limitation period, extended indefinitely where fraud or false statements are evidenced. Consequences include: retrospective clawback of every duty drawback received, payment of three times the duty evaded, penalties of up to BMD 1,000 per false declaration plus forfeiture of goods/vessel value, and suspension or revocation of the licensed shipyard status; for in-scope MNE groups, misstatements in CIT Act 2023 QDMTT filings carry up to 100% of the tax underpaid as penalty and are reportable to UK HMRC under exchange-of-information arrangements; corporate-service-provider and AML enforcement under the Bermuda Proceeds of Crime Act can add substantial fines and disqualification of the general partner. The 180-day inward remittance window applies to the consideration for the exported vessel and must be evidenced by the inward remittance certificate retained in the customs file.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates a currency-board-style peg of BMD 1:1 to USD. Current account transactions are freely convertible and capital account transactions by non-resident entities are generally unrestricted. Profit remittance and dividend distributions by a non-resident Exempted Limited Partnership may be made without prior exchange control approval provided that the underlying capital was introduced from outside Bermuda and the entity maintains current beneficial ownership declarations and audited accounts. Resident entities may require specific or general permission from the BMA for certain capital transfers, but a non-resident ELP is treated as outside the scope of most exchange control restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Bermuda does not impose a statutory export proceeds surrender regime. The Exchange Control Act 1972 and subsidiary regulations contain no mandatory conversion timeline and no compulsory conversion ratio for export receipts. A non-resident ELP may retain 100% of export proceeds in any permitted currency without obligation to convert into BMD or USD. For resident entities, the BMA may attach conditions to exchange control approvals, but no automatic surrender obligation exists for shipbuilding export earnings.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting as the monetary authority and exchange control administrator for Bermuda under the Exchange Control Act 1972

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Legal Constraints: Bermuda does not maintain a distinct Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence. Fintech payment providers must either obtain a banking licence under the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 or register as a money service business under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2018. UK or EU EMI passports do not extend to Bermuda. A BMA-licensed entity is required for regulated deposit-taking or money transmission. For pre-funded escrow payments, a third-party licensed payment provider may be used as a pass-through conduit, but the ELP must maintain its primary treasury accounts at a fully licensed Bermuda bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers or MT202 cover payments to the ELP's USD account held at a Bermuda bank. USD clearing is effected through Fedwire or CHIPS using the bank's correspondent network. The payment narrative must identify the transfer as 'Capital Contribution – ELP Equity' and include the partnership registration number. Pre-verification requires SWIFT sanctions screening, BMA reporting checks, and reconciliation against the executed capital commitment schedule. A non-resident ELP does not require prior BMA approval for an inbound capital contribution; the bank will, however, hold a BMA-completed declaration form that identifies all ultimate beneficial owners.Outbound trade settlements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for supplier payments and through commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 or MT710 for documentary trade finance. Maritime installment payments should use a Commercial LC with a vessel identification reference and invoice number. A standby letter of credit or demand guarantee may be issued under MT760 in favour of the shipyard to secure progress payments. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% on Bermuda-sourced and worldwide income for qualifying Exempted Limited Partnerships (ELPs); 15% domestic Corporate Income Tax (CIT) applies only to constituent entities of in-scope multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more, effective for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2025 (Income Tax Act 2024).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda levies no statutory withholding tax on distributions, dividends, or partnership profit remittances to non-resident partners (including the shipyard ELP's limited partners).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before 30 June following the end of the ELP's financial reporting period (or 6 months from fiscal year-end, whichever date falls later).

Deadline: · Frequency:

Non-resident ELPs must attach a statement of foreign tax residence (e.g., UK or Hong Kong tax residency certificate) to be outside ESR scope. Failure to file the ESD-1 attracts an immediate civil penalty of BMD 6,000; a false or misleading declaration attracts a fine of BMD 84,000 and confirmation of the entity as a 'shell company' by the Registrar, exposing the ELP to dissolution.
Line 2 Core Income Generating Activities (CIGA) — narrative disclosure of where shipyard contracts, slipway operations, and vessel construction activities are performed. Confirm all CIGA for shipbuilding are performed outside Bermuda; if conducted in Bermuda, describe local employees, premises, and annual expenditure. Failure to file the ESD-1 attracts an immediate civil penalty of BMD 6,000; a false or misleading declaration attracts a fine of BMD 84,000 and confirmation of the entity as a 'shell company' by the Registrar, exposing the ELP to dissolution.
Line 3 Qualified expenditure and headcount in Bermuda (BMD). Enter '0' for both fields if the non-resident ELP has no Bermudian staff or operating premises; gross negligence in this field attracts false-declaration penalties. Failure to file the ESD-1 attracts an immediate civil penalty of BMD 6,000; a false or misleading declaration attracts a fine of BMD 84,000 and confirmation of the entity as a 'shell company' by the Registrar, exposing the ELP to dissolution.
Line A — Revenues Total group revenues attributable to the Bermuda ELP, including maritime construction milestone payments. Report on a CIT country-by-country basis; if the shipyard and vessel sales are booked offshore, the ELP only includes Bermuda-sourced revenues. Late CIT return: fixed penalty of BMD 10,000 plus BMD 1,000 per month; inaccurate return: penalty equal to 100% of the understated tax for deliberate error; interest accrues at BMD base rate plus 2% per annum.
Line B — Related Party Transactions Value of cross-border transactions with affiliates (vessel components, IP royalties, management fees, financing). Apply arm's length standard and OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; attach a Schedule B-1 listing each related counterparty jurisdiction. Late CIT return: fixed penalty of BMD 10,000 plus BMD 1,000 per month; inaccurate return: penalty equal to 100% of the understated tax for deliberate error; interest accrues at BMD base rate plus 2% per annum.
Line C — Substance-Based Income Exclusion (SBIE) Computation of payroll and tangible asset carve-outs (5% for tangible assets, 5% payroll) that reduce GloBE/Elligible Income. Shipyard physical assets located abroad are generally not eligible assets for Bermuda SBIE; exclude them. Late CIT return: fixed penalty of BMD 10,000 plus BMD 1,000 per month; inaccurate return: penalty equal to 100% of the understated tax for deliberate error; interest accrues at BMD base rate plus 2% per annum.
Line D — Tax Liability 15% top-up tax computation after QDMTT / GloBE credits. Bermuda's Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (DMTT) may apply to in-scope ELPs; file the DMTT return module simultaneously. Late CIT return: fixed penalty of BMD 10,000 plus BMD 1,000 per month; inaccurate return: penalty equal to 100% of the understated tax for deliberate error; interest accrues at BMD base rate plus 2% per annum.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 0 (no general threshold for local filing); however, for in-scope MNE groups (EUR 750M+), contemporaneous Master File and Local File documentation is mandatory for all intercompany transactions with Bermudian constituent entities regardless of transaction size. Unless the shipyard ELP exceeds BMD 10 million in related-party purchases or BMD 5 million in related-party sales, no simplified TP documentation is locally accepted; the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines apply directly under the Income Tax Act 2024.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance (Bermuda) Regulations 2018 apply; 'Relevant Activities' include banking, insurance, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, shipping (international vessel operation), distribution and service centres, intellectual property holding, and pure equity holding. Commercial container-vessel shipbuilding (ISIC 3011) is NOT a listed Relevant Activity; the ELP must nonetheless maintain sufficient real presence in Bermuda for its registered-office and administrative functions. If the ELP also holds ship-owning subsidiaries (chartering), the 'pure equity holding' and 'shipping' activities become Relevant, requiring full economic substance (board meetings, senior management, CIGA, and adequate expenditure in Bermuda).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent CIT Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil fraud penalty of up to 150% of the tax liability; criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act 2024 with fines up to BMD 1,000,000; in the case of false sworn declarations to the Bermuda Registrar, a fine of BMD 84,000 and imprisonment not exceeding 5 years under the Criminal Code Act 1907.

Director personal liability: Partners and directors of the ELP's management entity are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid CIT and penalties if they authorized the false return; a Director Disqualification Order and a travel ban may be issued pending restitution of tax and penalties.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Registrar's declaration of the ELP as a shell company; mandatory strike-off and dissolution; monetary penalty of BMD 250,000 plus BMD 10,000 per day of continuing non-compliance; referral to the UK Secretary of State for sanctions under the Overseas Sanctions Regulations.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the filing of false substance declarations are personally liable for penalties up to BMD 250,000; the Supreme Court of Bermuda may order restitution of all profits earned through the sham, seizure of the ELP's bank account, and a 10-year travel ban.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML/CTF) Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2008 — civil penalty up to BMD 200,000, criminal fine up to BMD 5,000,000, and imprisonment up to 10 years; the ELP's license as a shipbuilder and its operating bank account may be suspended.

Director personal liability: A general partner who signs off a suspicious maritime transaction report late is personally liable for a daily penalty of BMD 500; on indictment, directors face forfeiture of personal assets obtained from the transaction and a 7-year custodial sentence.
Statutory Non-Compliance and Untrue Declarations to the Registrar

Statutory sanction: Default fine BMD 500 plus BMD 100 monthly; automatic administrative dissolution after 13 months of non-filing (Exempted Partnerships Act 2021); re-registration requires a Supreme Court order with costs against the ELP.

Director personal liability: Any director or general partner knowingly submitting a false annual return is liable to BMD 84,000 fine and 4 years imprisonment; the court may order the individual to compensate all third-party creditors for debts incurred while the ELP was in default.
