E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company is formed under the Companies Act 1981 through the Registrar of Companies, obtaining a unique company registration number. No income tax TIN is issued because Bermuda does not impose general income tax. Employers must obtain a payroll tax account number from the Office of the Tax Commissioner upon becoming an employer. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

There is no Bermuda equivalent to the U.S. check-the-box election; hybrid mismatch elections are not applicable locally. Exempted companies may obtain a tax exemption certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, providing statutory protection against future Bermuda income, dividend, and capital-gains taxes for the duration stated in the certificate (commonly to 2035 or 2050 depending on incorporation date); the certificate is subject to exceptions under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023. Under the CIT Act 2023, in-scope groups may elect group taxation treatment and may apply OECD Pillar Two transitional safe harbour elections where available.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar is pegged at 1 BMD = 1 USD, and the USD is legal tender in Bermuda. Current-account convertibility: fully unrestricted for service income, vendor payments, wages, software subscriptions, and intercompany charges; no BMA authorization is required for current payments. Capital-account convertibility: liberalized for Exempted Companies; the issuance and transfer of shares to non-residents and the subscription of share capital in USD fall under the BMA's general permissions under the Exchange Control Act 1972, eliminating case-by-case approval. Profit-remittance hurdles: none in Bermuda law; no withholding tax, capital gains tax, or profits tax applies to dividends, interest, royalties, or liquidation proceeds for this small/medium Exempted Company (the 15% Corporate Income Tax for multinational groups under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 only applies above the EUR 750 million group-revenue threshold). Residual constraints: (i) Bermuda-domiciled client funds intended for regulated purposes must be held with a BMA-licensed bank; (ii) the entity must file FATCA/CRS returns and an annual economic substance declaration; (iii) sanctions screening of counterparties applies under the Bermuda Sanctions Regulations and UK/HMT sanctions as a matter of policy.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Bermuda has no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime and no mandatory conversion ratio. Under the BMA's exchange-control general permissions, an Exempted Company may retain 100% of its foreign-currency service receipts indefinitely, in any currency, whether held in Bermuda or abroad. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. Statutory retention timeline: unlimited. No filing with the BMA is required for the receipt or retention of export/service proceeds, although the local bank will classify the account under standard exchange-control reporting in the ordinary course.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), established under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969; the BMA is the sole currency-issuing authority and exchange-control regulator for the Bermuda dollar (BMD), administering the Exchange Control Act 1972 and related delegated general permissions. The United Kingdom (HM Treasury) is the sovereign parent state, but Bermuda's FX regime is administered locally by the BMA and not by the Bank of England.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

UK Tax Evasion / Offshore Mismanagement

Statutory sanction: UK corporation tax on attributable profits, penalties up to 100% of the tax, and unlimited criminal fines under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 for corporate failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion. HMRC may also issue assessments, information notices, and asset freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors risk personal assessment, penalty notices, director disqualification, travel bans, and imprisonment for tax fraud or false declarations. Judicial veil-piercing is available where the company is a façade for personal offshore tax abuse.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties imposed by the Bermuda Registrar, daily default penalties, public strike-off, compulsory winding-up, and information exchange to HMRC and other competent authorities. The company's bank and payment processors may terminate Stripe Billing and Wise accounts.

AML / KYC Deficiencies and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Bermuda AML/ATF Act 2018 and Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 impose criminal fines and imprisonment for money laundering and financing arrangements. Payment processors and correspondent banks will close accounts, and HMRC/FIU exchange information.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or recklessly enable unregulated money movement face personal criminal exposure, asset freezes, disqualification, and imprisonment; the corporate veil will be pierced where the company is used as a vehicle for fraud or money laundering.
Fraudulent Trading / False Filing

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Companies Act 1981 permits criminal penalties for fraudulent trading, civil restitution, disqualification orders, and winding-up. False declarations to the Registrar are independently sanctionable with fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for company debts where fraud is proved. Courts may order contribution to the company’s assets, impose travel bans, and sentence directors to imprisonment where deliberate deceit is established.
