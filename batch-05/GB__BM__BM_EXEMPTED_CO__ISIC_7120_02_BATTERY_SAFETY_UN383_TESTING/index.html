Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Bermuda exempted company; the incorporator may issue a single no-par-value share fully paid at subscription. Where issued capital is less than BMD 12,000, a section 8(1) Companies Act 1981 declaration must accompany Form 1A. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda (administering payroll tax, social insurance contributions, Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 and Economic Substance Act 2018); Bermuda Customs and Border Control for import/export duty; Registrar of Companies for company registration. Official digital portal: Government of Bermuda e-Tax system and Registrar of Companies e-Services (gov.bm).

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the exempted company is corporately tax-exempt in Bermuda for non-CIT-caught groups (no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, dividends tax or withholding tax); it is an 'eligible entity' for US check-the-box purposes and may be classified as a pass-through (disregarded entity or partnership) or as an entity-level corporation. Territorial scope: foreign-source income is outside Bermuda taxation and the exchange-control 'non-resident' designation allows unrestricted capital repatriation. Exception: from 1 January 2025, the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 imposes a 15% domestic CIT on Bermuda constituent entities of in-scope international MNE groups (Pillar Two).

Available election forms

Bermuda law provides no local check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. Available elections: (i) US federal — IRS Form 8832 election to classify the exempted company as a disregarded entity, partnership or corporation; (ii) CIT Act 2023 — election to apply the transitional Country-by-Country Reporting safe harbour for the first taxable period, elections governing the recognition of Qualified Refundable Tax Credits, and formulaic application of the Substance-Based Income Exclusion without election; (iii) Payroll Tax Act 1995 — election to compute payroll tax on remuneration paid versus payable and to accelerate quarterly prepayments to annual rate bands; (iv) Economic Substance Act 2018 — annual filing election declaring performance of core income-generating activities in Bermuda.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST system — Bermuda levies zero indirect tax on local supplies, services and digital transactions. The only border indirect levy is Customs Import Duty under the Customs Act 1964 and the Customs Tariff Act 1970, applied to goods entering Bermuda. Where the wider group maintains a UK VAT registration, UK VAT under the VAT Act 1994 may apply to a UK-established branch, with cross-border B2B testing services falling under the place-of-supply rules.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Bermudian dollar (BMD) — not applicable: no VAT/GST registration threshold, fiscal representative regime or registration cycle exists. Importers must obtain a government Importer registration and may require a customs broker licence; customs duty is chargeable per consignment from the first dollar without a de minimis threshold for commercial goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Bermuda because no VAT is chargeable; export supplies are simply outside the scope of any Bermuda indirect tax and no VAT refund mechanism exists. Export-protective treatment requires: (i) testing services to offshore clients are not subject to any Bermuda indirect tax; (ii) test battery samples temporarily imported for UN38.3 testing are relieved from customs duty on re-export under temporary-importation relief provisions; (iii) if a UK group member is the supplier, the supply of B2B laboratory testing services to a foreign business customer is treated as made where the customer belongs, therefore outside the scope of UK VAT, provided the supplier holds valid commercial evidence of the customer's non-UK business status, a signed test contract, and the outward transfer of test data.

  • Validated Bermuda Customs electronic export entry (CUSEX/e-Export filing under CEDARS) covering re-exported battery samples, fixtures and test equipment
  • Commercial invoice and certified packing list identifying UN38.3 classification, cell chemistry and net weight
  • Bill of Lading or Master/Air Waybill evidencing outward shipment from Bermuda
  • Accredited Dangerous Goods Declaration (IATA DGR Shipper's Declaration) for UN38.3 lithium cells and batteries
  • UN38.3 test summary sheet and ISO/IEC 17025 laboratory accreditation certificate
  • Original testing services contract evidencing the offshore customer and scope of testing
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT/MT103 or Wise/Wire transfer confirmation) evidencing receipt of export proceeds into Bermuda

No fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, digital stamping or real-time API mandate applies. Bermuda Customs operates an electronic single-window clearance environment (CEDARS and CEBIS) for air and sea manifests and for import/export entries; carriers file electronic manifests and Customs may require digital attachments such as certificates of analysis, dangerous-goods declarations and test reports.

Under the Customs Act 1964, false export declarations expose goods to seizure and forfeiture, monetary penalties of up to treble the duty at stake, and criminal prosecution; misuse of temporary-importation relief for battery samples triggers retrospective customs duty clawback for the full relief period. Under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, in-scope MNE groups face correction assessments, late-payment interest at prescribed rates, daily default penalties for late returns, and penalties of up to 50% of understated tax for deliberate inaccuracy. In the UK nexus, HMRC may deny VAT zero-rating, apply a 100% dishonest-evasion penalty under VATA 1994 s.60/61, and raise retrospective assessments for up to four years, with an additional FA 2007 Schedule 24 accuracy penalty where the related corporation tax return is incorrect.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar (USD). Capital and current account convertibility is unrestricted for non-residents and exempted companies; there is no prior approval requirement for repatriation of dividends, capital, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance is effected through any BMA-licensed bank with no statutory withholding at the territorial level; UK-initiated sanctions screening applies. The practical hurdle is not exchange control but bank-level AML/KYC documentary burden.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion is imposed in Bermuda. Exporters may retain 100% of export receipts in any permitted currency. The Exchange Control Act 1972 (as liberalised) requires only record-keeping and banks may request supporting invoices for BMD-denominated credits; the effective surrender ratio is 0% and there is no timeline for conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), operating under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969; together with HM Treasury of the United Kingdom as the residual sovereign authority for financial sanctions and international obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Moderate. Bermuda does not permit unlicensed foreign digital EMI operations within its territory; any entity providing payment services to Bermuda corporates must be licensed under the Payment Services Act 2009 or act as an agent of a BMA-licensed bank. Wise Business, through its UK entity, can provide secondary USD/EUR/GBP payment rails and virtual account numbers, but the primary treasury account must remain with a BMA-licensed SWIFT-member bank. Legal constraint: the EMI cannot clear BMD payments and cannot hold escrow accounts in Bermuda.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% Bermuda corporate income tax on profits of an Exempted Company outside the scope of the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023. A 15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax (CIT) and a Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax apply exclusively to Bermuda business units of multinational enterprise groups with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more, effective for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an Exempted Company to non-resident shareholders, whether corporate or individual, and no branch remittance tax on repatriated profits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD 0 (not applicable) for an Exempted Company outside the CIT Act scope; no Bermuda statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold exists for standalone entities. For MNE groups within the CIT Act 2023, Master File and Local File documentation is mandatory where the ultimate parent group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750 million, and the Bermuda constituent entity must retain contemporaneous records demonstrating arm's length pricing for intragroup testing fees, shared facilities, and management charges; failure to produce the documentation on notice attracts a BMD 25,000 record-keeping penalty and permits the Tax Commissioner to make an adjustment based on the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018, the Economic Substance Amendment Act 2020, and the Economic Substance Regulations require every Bermuda Exempted Company to file an annual economic substance return and to demonstrate, where it carries on a 'relevant activity,' adequate physical presence, a sufficient number of senior executives and full-time employees in Bermuda, adequate operating expenditure, and the performance of Core Income Generating Activities (CIGA) in Bermuda. Relevant activities are: banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centre, intellectual property business, and holding entity. A third-party UN38.3 battery safety and CE-RED electromagnetic testing laboratory is not itself a relevant activity; if the lab provides testing exclusively to affiliated companies within the same group, it must self-classify as a distribution and service centre and retain contemporaneous CIGA records. In every case, a nil or positive ESR-1 return must be filed annually; an omission or false 'no relevant activity' declaration where intra-group services are in fact rendered constitutes a criminal offense rather than a mere administrative defect.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023

Statutory sanction: On conviction, a fine of up to BMD 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years; civil interest at 10% per annum on the avoided tax; penalties of BMD 10,000 for each false statement or return; the Tax Commissioner may strike off the entity and revoke its tax protection certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who directed, consented to, or connived in the false filing are individually liable to the same criminal fines and imprisonment; the court may order restitution of avoided taxes and costs; Bermuda courts may issue travel bans pending trial and disqualification orders prohibiting directorship of any Bermuda company for up to 5 years.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties for economic substance failure: not less than BMD 10,000 and up to BMD 250,000 for a first offense, increasing to BMD 500,000 for a continuing default; daily penalties of BMD 5,000 for non-compliance with information notices; the Registrar may strike off the company, causing all assets to vest in the Crown.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined for the corporate default where the failure is attributable to their willful neglect; concealment of information from the Tax Commissioner exposes directors to imprisonment of up to 7 years; the company's insurance liability cover is void upon dissolution, and directors become personally exposed to third-party claims arising from pre-dissolution testing certificates.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Registry Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Beneficial Ownership Act 2017: a fine of up to BMD 2,000 plus a default penalty of BMD 250 per day; on conviction for false information, imprisonment of up to 6 months; under the Proceeds of Crime Regulations 2018 (AML/ATF), regulated entities face civil penalties up to BMD 1 million and criminal fines up to BMD 250,000 for systemic AML failures.

Director personal liability: Directors are individually liable for the daily default penalties where the failure to file BO particulars was caused by their oversight; senior management of a regulated entity may be personally fined up to BMD 50,000 and disqualified; non-compliant structures are reported to UK HMRC and the Bermuda Financial Intelligence Agency, triggering cross-border investigations.
Late Annual Return, Annual Fee Default, and Strike-Off

Statutory sanction: Strike-off and dissolution by the Registrar; all real and intangible assets of the company vest in the Crown; the dissolved company cannot enforce contracts, defend litigation, or hold bank accounts; restoration requires a court petition, payment of all arrears and penalties, and costs of BMD 2,000 to BMD 7,000; contracts entered after dissolution are void.

Director personal liability: Directors who trade after strike-off are personally liable for all debts incurred subsequent to dissolution; directors may also be personally liable for the restoration costs and for unpaid government fees where the company has no assets; a court may impose an order for civil restitution in favour of the Crown.
Payroll Tax Evasion and Social Insurance Default

Statutory sanction: A fine of up to BMD 5,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years for wilful evasion; the Tax Commissioner may make a jeopardy assessment of deemed remuneration with a 10% late-payment surcharge and interest; employers face mandatory contribution claims from the Contributory Pension Fund.

Director personal liability: Directors and 'responsible officers' are jointly and severally liable for unpaid employer payroll tax, employee payroll tax, and social insurance contributions where the company's default resulted from their directions; repeated default may lead to a court-imposed travel ban and disqualification.
UK-Connected Offshore Tax Non-Compliance for Beneficial Owners

Statutory sanction: UK penalties up to 200% of the tax liability for deliberate offshore non-disclosure; the corporate offense of failure to prevent facilitation of UK tax evasion carries an unlimited fine; HM Revenue and Customs may also petition Bermuda under the Tax Information Exchange Agreement for bank and accounting records; UK Proceeds of Crime Act asset freezing orders may attach to the Bermuda shares.

Director personal liability: UK-resident directors and senior officers face personal prosecution for the failure-to-prevent offense where the company did not maintain proportionate prevention procedures; UK civil recovery orders and travel bans under the Proceeds of Crime Act apply; offshore tax evasion which results in a mistaken exemption certificate exposes directors to Bermuda regulatory sanctions plus UK criminal liability.
