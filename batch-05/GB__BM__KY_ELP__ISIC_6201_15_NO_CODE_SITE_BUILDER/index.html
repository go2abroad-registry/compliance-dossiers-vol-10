Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — The Bermuda ELP is fiscally transparent by statute; all income, gains, and deductions flow through to partners, and no Bermuda entity-level income tax applies. Non-resident partners are not subject to Bermuda income tax, payroll tax (on their share), or withholding. Bermuda's Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (15% CIT) applies only to constituent entities of large MNE groups (≥ EUR 750 million consolidated revenue) and is currently deferred/under public referendum; a standalone digital-services ELP is outside its scope. The ELP also falls outside Bermuda's Economic Substance Act 'relevant activities' unless it holds IP for exploitation as an IP business or operates as a headquarters/ancillary service center.

Available election forms

Bermuda has no general domestic 'check-the-box' election; pass-through status is automatic. Available elections and options: (i) US federal income tax classification election under IRS Form 8832 (default partnership classification, may elect corporate treatment if commercially rational); (ii) Bermuda CITA deferred-tax transition election, loss carry-forward nomination, and pre-2024 asset transition recognition where the group crosses the EUR 750m threshold; (iii) UK hybrid mismatch elections under Finance Act 2016 Part 6A for partnerships within UK tax net, and UK Pillar Two/G loBE jurisdictional safe-harbour elections; (iv) partnership-agreement allocation election designating income/receipts of non-resident partners to maintain Bermuda territorial exclusion and foreign-only tax nexus; (v) Bermuda payroll-tax optional annual scale election for qualifying employer remuneration. No VAT registration election exists because Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Bermuda levies no VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax. The only transaction-adjacent levies are customs duties (Customs Tariff Act 1980) imposed exclusively on importation of physical goods, payroll tax on Bermuda-sourced emoluments, and stamp duties on certain documents/land transactions; none of these apply to the digital no-code website builder service.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — no VAT/GST registration regime or economic turnover threshold exists in Bermuda. Customs duty registration applies to importers as physical goods cross the border (no monetary threshold); payroll tax registration applies upon first payment of Bermuda emoluments (no turnover threshold); CIT registration under CITA applies only to constituent entities of MNE groups meeting the EUR 750m consolidated revenue test.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Bermuda. As a non-VAT jurisdiction, Bermuda has no export zero-rating mechanism, VAT refund regime, or exporters' evidence scheme. Income from the ELP's no-code web application subscriptions (ISIC 6201, NAICS 511210) is entirely outside the Bermuda tax base irrespective of export nature. For customers in VAT territories, place-of-supply rules (UK B2B: VATA 1994 s.7A and Sch 4A; EU B2B: art. 44 EU VAT Directive) treat the software licence as supplied to the customer's jurisdiction, where the customer self-assesses reverse-charge VAT or the ELP registers for borderless B2C OSS/IOSS; any 'zero-rating' or 'refund' right arises solely under those foreign laws, and export documentation to substantiate such foreign claims is mandatory in the recipient jurisdiction.

  • None required by Bermuda for digital/software exports (no physical goods, no customs declaration).
  • Where incidental physical media (e.g., branded USB/firmware) exported: Bermuda Customs Export Declaration per Customs Act 1980 and supporting airway bill / bill of lading.
  • Commercial substantiation bundle for foreign zero-rating/place-of-supply claims: executed SaaS/no-code subscription agreement, itemised tax invoice, payment settlement evidence (Stripe/Paddle transaction logs), server/usage logs showing recipient location, and IP licence grant.
  • Banking/treasury evidence of offshore receivable repatriation to Bermuda bank account (inward remittance advice) for OECD/GloBE and UK CFC documentation.
  • Intercompany transfer-pricing file (OECD TP documentation) where the ELP invoices affiliates in the UK or EU.

None — Bermuda has no e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate. No API-fiscalisation exists for Bermudian invoices. Where the ELP invoices customers located in VAT jurisdictions (e.g., UK under VAT Act 1994; EU under Council Directive 2006/112/EC; US states with economic nexus), the Bermuda entity may trigger foreign e-invoicing/reporting obligations (e.g., UK Making Tax Digital VAT, Italy SDI/FatturaPA, France Factur-X rollout) but such obligations are extraterritorial consequences of the customer jurisdiction, not Bermuda-law requirements.

Bermuda has no VAT zero-rating regime, hence no VAT clawback. However, mischaracterisation of the ELP's status is penalised as follows: (i) Bermuda CITA — failure to register/file or negligent understatement: penalty up to USD 10,000 plus up to 150% of tax avoided for fraud, per CITA 2023 s.91/92; (ii) Payroll Tax Act — unregistered employer/suppressed emoluments: assessment plus penalty up to 200% of tax; (iii) Beneficial Ownership Act — late/incorrect filings: penalties up to BMD 120,000 and potential imprisonment for officers; (iv) HMRC anti-avoidance exposure on any UK-related attribution: UK Diverted Profits Tax 100% of diverted profits, Part 4 Finance Act 2016, disclosure under DOTAS, and CFC apportionment; (v) potential retrospective clawback of foreign VAT credits if HMRC/EU authorities determine the Bermuda ELP was the effective supplier failing applicable local invoicing rules, with joint-and-several liability in the UK under VAT group/pay-and-display provisions; (vi) general anti-abuse rules in Bermuda's International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act 2018 impose financial penalties and amendment/cancellation of licence for artificial substance arrangements.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates a currency board regime with the Bermuda Dollar (BMD) pegged at parity to the United States Dollar (1 BMD = 1 USD). No exchange control legislation exists under the Exchange Control Act 1972, which was effectively liberalized; both current account and capital account transactions are fully convertible and freely transferable. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, partnership distributions, and capital repatriation by non-resident partners are unrestricted and require no central bank approval. Settlement may be executed in BMD, USD, or other freely usable currencies; the absence of differential current/capital account treatment eliminates conversion hurdles. However, the BMA retains regulatory oversight over licensed financial institutions and may review cross-border flows only for AML/CFT compliance, not for convertibility or balance-of-payments reasons.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio applies to Bermuda-resident entities; all export receipts and trade settlement proceeds may be retained, freely converted, or held in any currency. The BMA imposes no time limit for repatriation and no requirement to deposit export earnings domestically. Bermuda's status as an offshore financial center is distinct from IMF Article VIII commitments in the mainland United Kingdom; the territory adheres to FATF standards but maintains a permissive capital account. Enterprises should still document invoicing and settlement records to evidence economic substance and to satisfy bank account opening and audit requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) acting as the central bank, financial regulator, and currency board authority under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for BMA-licensed digital asset and payments licenses, but Low for unlicensed offshore EMIs. Bermuda permits 'Digital Asset Business' under the Digital Asset Business Act 2018 and offers a Fast-Track payments license for e-money/FinTech operations. However, an open corporate account with a non-resident EMI based outside Bermuda without a BMA license is operationally impractical for a Bermuda-domiciled ELP because the BMA-regulated banking system may not recognize it as a 'bank' for SWIFT settlement purposes. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA e-money institutions, EU E-Money/PSD2 institutions) generally will not accept a Bermuda-regulated partnership without a demonstrable business nexus and may impose stricter KYC and payment restrictions due to cross-border correspondent banking withdrawal. The prudent structure is to hold a primary account at a Bermuda-licensed bank and use regulated EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud partner banks) as secondary settlement rails with prior bank consent. Legal constraints include: prohibition on unlicensed deposit-taking, BMA AML/CFT code expectations, FATCA/CRS reporting burdens, and the Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 which requires the ELP to book economic substance in Bermuda.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wiring protocol: remitter will initiate a SWIFT MT103 via their corporate bank using the following details: beneficiary bank (e.g., Butterfield Bank, Valley Place, 65 Front Street, Hamilton HM 11, Bermuda; SWIFT BNTBBMHM), beneficiary account in BMD or USD; the ELP's legal name and Bermuda registered address. The purpose code should be 'Capital Contribution' or 'Partnership Capital Injection' with memo: 'Capital contribution to KY_ELP per LPA section X'. Supporting documentation: partnership agreement extract, capital call notice, pro-forma invoice where applicable, and proof of funding source. The receiving bank will apply beneficiary verification under BMA circulars and CRS/FATCA classification; prior notice to the relationship manager is mandatory for wires exceeding USD 100,000 or if originating from a non-equivalent jurisdiction. Non-compliant use of personal accounts or money transmitters may result in reversal or account freeze.Clearing rails for trade settlements: primary rail is SWIFT MT103/202 for bank-to-bank wire transfers, with MT400/MT410 for documentary collections and MT700 series for standby letters of credit / commercial LCs. For SaaS/low-code website builder subscription payments, the main outbound treasury flows are VAT/GST remittances to HMRC and other tax authorities, vendor payments for cloud hosting and software licenses, and intercompany management fees. Settlement should be executed via the bank's online treasury portal in USD or EUR using SWIFT; simultaneously, the corporation should establish a dedicated segregated USD account at an intermediate tier-1 bank (e.g., JP Morgan, Citi, or Barclays) when BMD/USD parity minimization is desired. Bermudian clearing is primarily through the local Automated Clearing House (ACH) for local currency items, but cross-border trade must use SWIFT or correspondent banking. The bank will require payment authorization matrix per LPA and may impose daily transfer limits; prior notification is required for high-value outbound disbursements (> USD 250,000).Forwards (Vanilla FX) - Fully permitted via Bermuda-licensed banks and best used to fix USD/BMD/EUR rates for contractual obligations up to 12 monthsNon-Delivery Forwards (NDFs) - Permitted for currencies where delivery is impractical, primarily via London or US counterparty desks, usable for EUR/USD and GBP/USD hedgingFX Swaps (Swap USANCE) - Custom interest-optimized cross-currency swaps between BMD, USD, EUR, and GBP, require BMA-approved ISDA master agreement and bank collateralCurrency Options - Excluded from the required list but permissible under license; the schema constraints limit the enumerated list to the above three categories

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High for BMA-licensed digital asset and payments licenses, but Low for unlicensed offshore EMIs. Bermuda permits 'Digital Asset Business' under the Digital Asset Business Act 2018 and offers a Fast-Track payments license for e-money/FinTech operations. However, an open corporate account with a non-resident EMI based outside Bermuda without a BMA license is operationally impractical for a Bermuda-domiciled ELP because the BMA-regulated banking system may not recognize it as a 'bank' for SWIFT settlement purposes. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA e-money institutions, EU E-Money/PSD2 institutions) generally will not accept a Bermuda-regulated partnership without a demonstrable business nexus and may impose stricter KYC and payment restrictions due to cross-border correspondent banking withdrawal. The prudent structure is to hold a primary account at a Bermuda-licensed bank and use regulated EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud partner banks) as secondary settlement rails with prior bank consent. Legal constraints include: prohibition on unlicensed deposit-taking, BMA AML/CFT code expectations, FATCA/CRS reporting burdens, and the Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 which requires the ELP to book economic substance in Bermuda.
