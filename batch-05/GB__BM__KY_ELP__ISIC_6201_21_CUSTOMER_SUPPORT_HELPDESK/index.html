Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Tax Commissioner, Ministry of Finance, Government of Bermuda (primary Gibraltar-Bermuda home authority); administrative operations are conducted through the Government of Bermuda tax and customs portals (gov.bm tax pages and ASYCUDA World customs platform), while entity incorporation and the exempted partnership certificate are administered by the Registrar of Companies, Ministry of Finance. Separately, where the ELP makes UK-facing taxable supplies of digital helpdesk services, a parallel HMRC registration obligation arises under the UK VAT and Making Tax Digital framework, filed through the HMRC Government Gateway. Bermuda is a British Overseas Territory with autonomous tax sovereignty and is not within the UK VAT territory.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - incorporate under the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992 by filing a declaration of registration with the Registrar of Companies through the Registry's electronic filing system, appointing a Bermuda registered office and a resident representative, and obtaining the Certificate of Exemption / Exempted Partnership certificate, which functions as the statutory enterprise identifier. Step 2 - register for a Bermuda PAYE and Payroll Tax account (Business Number) with the Office of the Tax Commissioner only when the ELP engages Bermuda-resident employees; no general income tax TIN is issued and no VAT registration exists for Bermuda domestic purposes. Step 3 - if the ELP is part of a multinational enterprise group with consolidated global revenue of at least EUR 750 million, apply for Corporate Income Tax registration under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 (effective for financial years commencing on or after 1 January 2026) and obtain a Bermuda CIT filing identifier; the ELP remains transparent and each corporate partner is separately identified. Step 4 - for destination-market VAT compliance, obtain a HMRC UK VAT registration number where the ELP supplies digital services to UK consumers (nil registration threshold for non-established suppliers) or rely on the UK customer's reverse charge for B2B supplies; obtain a Certificate of Tax Residence or Non-Residence Status from the Office of the Tax Commissioner where treaty or foreign zero-rating substantiation is requested.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent in its home jurisdiction: it bears no Bermuda corporate income tax on profits, no withholding tax on distributions or remittances, no capital gains tax, and no estate or gift type taxes on partner transfers; partners are separately assessed in their respective jurisdictions of tax residence. Bermuda's Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 confirms that an eligible partnership is transparent, attributing each corporate partner's proportionate share of ELP income or loss to that partner for the 15% Bermuda CIT where the group is in scope. The ELP is likewise transparent for UK tax purposes; UK-resident partners are taxed on their shares under the UK partnership rules, and the UK CFC rules (Part 9A TIOPA 2010) address any non-transparent deferred income only if the ELP's structure is engineered to avoid UK tax.

Available election forms

Bermuda domestic law provides no check-the-box election: an ELP cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation domestically, although parties may file an 'eligible entity' confirmation under the CIT Act 2024 in respect of transparent treatment. The meaningful elections are cross-border: (a) US federal check-the-box - the ELP is an eligible entity under Treasury Regulation Section 301.7701-3 that defaults to partnership (or disregarded-entity status if single-member) franchise; a Form 8832 election to be taxed as an association is legally available but strategically disfavoured because it would nullify the offshore pass-through posture and create US entity-level exposure; (b) UK hybrid and CFC elections - the ELP's transparency eliminates UK hybrid mismatch outcomes under Part 7 TIOPA 2010, and partners may file protective CFC clearance applications to evidence that no income has been shifted to a non-UK straddling intermediate; (c) OECD Pillar Two safe harbour designations and GloBE information returns should be lodged by in-scope partners where the group has EUR 750 million or greater revenue; (d) the partnership agreement, annual capital statements, and allocation schedules must be archived to substantiate that partner interests are equity rather than debt.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Bermuda imposes no VAT, GST, sales and use tax, or any general consumption tax. The indirect levy universe is limited to Customs import duties under the Customs Tariff Act, Stamp Duty on real property instruments, payroll tax, and land tax. The Helpdesk SaaS cloud supply is therefore entirely outside Bermuda's indirect tax net. In destination markets (UK, EU, US states, Canada), the supply qualifies as an export of services subject to the customer's reverse charge, self-assessment, or deferred use tax, producing no Bermuda filing obligation and no Bermuda output tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Bermuda and no indirect tax registration arises from turnover, invoicing volume, or the volume of digital services supplied. Non-tax-related thresholds: (a) a Bermuda Customs Importers Licence is advisable for regular physical imports of hardware or embedded supports, with no minimum monetary threshold; (b) payroll tax registration applies from the first Bermuda-resident employee, with no de minimis; (c) for UK consumption of the digital helpdesk service, HMRC applies a nil registration threshold to non-established suppliers of e-services to UK consumers, whereas a UK-established person would pass GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover (2025/26); B2B supplies are commonly subject to the UK customer's reverse charge at 20%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda does not operate a domestic export zero-rating mechanism because the territory simply has no VAT: all cross-border digital helpdesk services are outside the scope of Bermuda's indirect taxation, requiring no claim, no form, and no prior approval. Internationally, the ELP may assert a 0% or reverse-charge outcome in a destination jurisdiction upon demonstrating: (a) for B2B supplies, that the customer is a business established outside the taxing jurisdiction, so the place of supply is the customer's location and the local tax is discharged by reverse charge; and (b) for B2C supplies, that the customer is resident outside the destination market and that the effective use and enjoyment of the multi-channel omnichannel helpdesk and conversational bot cloud occurs outside that market. Supporting records must evidence the non-Bermudian establishment of the ELP, the contractual flow, the payment rails, and the underlying hosting topology.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Goods Declaration filed through ASYCUDA World - mandatory only for tangible exports (e.g., on-premise helpdesk appliances or embedded hardware); for pure cloud SaaS the functional equivalent is the executed SaaS order form, the hosting and data-residency topology, and a Place-of-Supply Certification letter.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - required exclusively for physical export movements from Bermuda; for digital services, the substitute evidence is the customer contract, the dated service invoice, and the platform access logs evidencing supply to the non-Bermudian counterparty.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate obtainable from the ELP's Bermuda-licensed bank on request - confirms receipt of export proceeds in the original invoiced currency; it is not a statutory requirement under Bermuda law (the territory has no exchange control regulations), but it is the customary bank-level substantiation supporting overseas VAT zero-rating claims, cash repatriation records, and economic substance filings.

Bermuda operates no continuous transaction control (CTC) regime and no real-time fiscal clearance system: there is no tax authority e-invoicing API, no digital stamping, and no invoice clearance gateway. Customs import declarations are cleared through ASYCUDA World with duty assessment at the point of entry, which is separate from commercial invoicing. The ELP may therefore issue standard PDF or platform-native invoices without fiscal watermarking. To support zero-rating claims in destination jurisdictions, invoices should carry an ISO 20022-compliant remittance reference, the customer's VAT registration number where applicable, and a place-of-supply statement aligned with EU VAT Directive Article 44 (B2B) or Article 58 (B2C e-services), or the equivalent UK VAT place-of-supply rules.

Bermuda-side scrutiny of a pure digital services exporter is limited: the Office of the Tax Commissioner and the Registrar's financial and tax administration review the ELP's Economic Substance filings (where a specified activity exists) and, for in-scope groups, CIT Act 2024 returns. Evasion or knowing misstatement attracts civil penalties of up to 100% of the under-assessed tax, daily late-filing penalties capped at BMD 500 per day, and criminal fines of up to BMD 25,000 with imprisonment for up to 3 years in aggravated cases. If the ELP or its advisers manufacture false export documentation to support a 0% VAT outcome in the UK or an EU member state, the destination authority may retrospectively issue assessments for uncollected VAT with statutory default interest (UK: Bank of England base rate plus 2.5%), impose careless or deliberate error penalties of up to 100% of the VAT understated, and commence criminal proceedings under the UK Value Added Tax Act 1994 section 72. All export-substantiation records should be retained for a minimum of 7 years and linked to the ELP's underlying billing and ledger platform to allow a full audit trail.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the USD. Current-account transactions for an exempted partnership are free of exchange control restrictions and can be settled in BMD or USD. Capital-account inflows and outflows require Exchange Control approval or general permission via an authorized bank in Bermuda. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is treated as partnership distributions, and no Bermuda withholding tax is imposed provided the ELP does not engage in local business. However, large capital distributions must be cleared through an authorized bank with BMA notification, and documentation must evidence the underlying transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory export-proceeds surrender or conversion ratio applies to a Bermuda exempted partnership. Export receipts in USD may be retained in the ELP's operational account. The authorized bank must report inward and outward remittance movement through BMA-balance-of-payments coding, and underlying invoices should be retained for at least five years to support AML/KYC and exchange control compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) acting as the Exchange Control Authority under the Bermuda Exchange Control Act 1972 (as amended)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs. A non-Bermudian EMI cannot provide account services to the Bermuda ELP without holding a licence under the Bermuda Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 or the Digital Asset Business Act 2018, where applicable. A UK FCA-authorised EMI may be used for capturing customer subscriptions from SaaS clients, but treasury custody and capital injections must be channelled through a BMA-regulated bank. An EMI cannot be used to disguise beneficial ownership or circumvent Bermuda exchange control.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital arrives via SWIFT MT103 from the contributing partner's bank to the BMA-authorised Bermuda clearing bank, with instruction to credit the ELP capital escrow account. The purpose code must be 'CAPT' (Capital Transfer) or the applicable BMA remittance code, and the credit narrative must include the partnership name and investment round together with the executed subscription document and Exchange Control confirmation from the presenting bank. For UK-source capital, the remitting bank applies its own FCA AML checks; field 70 must state 'Contribution to Bermuda exempted limited partnership - no local business - non-resident ELP.' A single remittance exceeding USD 1,000,000 triggers balance-of-payments reporting to BMA.Routine helpdesk SaaS expenditures (hosting, telephony, API subscriptions) settle via the Bermuda operational bank's SWIFT MT103 payment rail in USD, with the relevant invoice and contract referenced; no commercial letter of credit is necessary. For high-value or lesser-known counterparties, a standby LC or documentary collection through a confirming bank may be requested, but ordinary low-risk SaaS disbursements should be completed by SWIFT transfer. Payments to UK vendors may be aggregrated through the bank's CHAPS/Faster Payments correspondent or SEPA where an IBAN exists, but the primary account remains with the Bermuda bank.USD/BMD foreign-exchange forwards are permissible but rarely required due to the fixed 1:1 peg; where a non-USD settlement currency is used, outright FX forwards up to 12 months can fix vendor costs.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on GBP/USD or EUR/USD are permissible for hedge accounting when the ELP books revenue in GBP/EUR but pays vendors in USD; the NDF must reflect real underlying exposure and be executed through an authorised bank with BMA oversight.Currency swaps are permissible only against a documented commercial exposure and with a licensed swap counterparty; for tenors beyond 12 months, the bank will require an ISDA agreement and BMA exchange-control approval.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs. A non-Bermudian EMI cannot provide account services to the Bermuda ELP without holding a licence under the Bermuda Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999 or the Digital Asset Business Act 2018, where applicable. A UK FCA-authorised EMI may be used for capturing customer subscriptions from SaaS clients, but treasury custody and capital injections must be channelled through a BMA-regulated bank. An EMI cannot be used to disguise beneficial ownership or circumvent Bermuda exchange control.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) entity level (pass-through; partners are taxed on their distributive share). 15% under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (CIT Act) only if the ELP is a member of an in-scope multinational group with consolidated annual revenue of at least €750M.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on distributions by an Exempted Limited Partnership.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No domestic transfer-pricing law applies to ELPs outside CIT scope. For CIT Act in-scope groups (consolidated revenue ≥ €750M, approximately BMD 750M): mandatory Country-by-Country Report, Master File, and Local File. Local File preparation is prudent where related-party SaaS support revenue exceeds BMD 2,000,000 per annum.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda's Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Amendment Act 2018 applies to ELPs carrying on 'relevant activities', including intellectual property business and 'providing any other services'. The ELP must demonstrate: (i) core income-generating activities performed in Bermuda; (ii) adequately qualified full-time employees; (iii) physical premises; and (iv) direction and management in Bermuda. A helpdesk SaaS ELP that merely holds IP licenses while employees operate in the UK or India will fail ESR and be recharacterised as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent CIT Filing

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 100% of the tax evaded; daily failure penalties of BMD 500 during fraud or neglect; interest on underpaid tax; the Tax Commissioner may issue an information notice compelling production of Stripe/PayPal merchant records.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors are jointly and severally liable where the ELP is insolvent or acted fraudulently; conviction carries imprisonment up to 5 years.
Shell Company / ESR Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Initial ESR penalty BMD 10,000; continued non-compliance BMD 100,000 to BMD 250,000; mandatory strike-off and loss of exempted status.

Director personal liability: The Registrar may disqualify the general partner's directors for up to 5 years and impose travel bans; the court may order restitution of fees earned while the shell operated.
AML/CFT Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fine up to BMD 250,000 per breach; revocation of the registered agent's licence; mandatory independent audit.

Director personal liability: Senior management, including the non-resident general partner, can be fined and imprisoned up to 2 years; the Financial Intelligence Agency can freeze the ELP's merchant bank accounts.
False Declaration to Registrar

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction fine up to BMD 10,000; additional late fee of BMD 250 per month; risk of strike-off from the registry.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are subject to director-disqualification orders and civil restitution for reliance losses suffered by third-party creditors.
Thin Capitalisation / Sham Intercompany Loans

Statutory sanction: If in CIT Act scope: reclassification of interest as distributions, denial of deductions, and penalty up to 100% for transfer-pricing adjustments; outside CIT scope, HMRC may recharacterise under OECD DPTA rules.

Director personal liability: The Bermuda court may pierce the corporate veil where the general partner operates the loan structure as an instrument of fraud, imposing joint liability for the borrowed principal.
