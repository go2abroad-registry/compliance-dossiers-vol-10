Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

UK: HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), portal https://www.gov.uk/hmrc. Bermuda: No national income or corporate tax authority; tax-exemption administration is handled by the Bermuda Registrar of Companies and the Bermuda Ministry of Finance, with e-services via the Bermuda Government Registry portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate/register the Exempted Limited Partnership with the Bermuda Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2015; obtain Certificate of Registration and ELP number. Step 2: Apply to the Bermuda Minister of Finance for an Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection certificate (EUTP Act 1966), which provides a 50-year statutory assurance from Bermuda income, capital gains, and stamp duty taxes, subject to payroll tax only for locally performed duties. Step 3: For UK VAT, register with HMRC as a non-established taxable person using Form VAT1 via a Government Gateway account; where the ELP supplies digital services to UK consumers, use the Non-UK VAT Mini One Stop Shop (MOSS) application to obtain a UK VAT number and submit quarterly VAT returns. Step 4: If a UK corporation tax Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) is necessary (e.g., if a UK permanent establishment exists or a partner is a UK-resident corporate), apply using Form CT600 guidance and HMRC online services. Step 5: Maintain certified copies of the Bermuda ELP registration, tax-exemption certificate, partner identification, and UK VAT registration in the corporate records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Bermuda law, an Exempted Limited Partnership is a contractual partnership and is not a separate taxable person; it is fiscally transparent by default and there is no Bermuda check-the-box or opaque election. For UK tax purposes, the ELP is treated as transparent for income tax and corporation tax; partners are assessed on their share of income, so no election is required to achieve pass-through treatment. For hybrid mismatch cases, the UK Part 6A aversion rules (TIOPA 2010) impose a primary response on UK group members using an ELP where another jurisdiction treats the ELP as opaque; disclosure may be required under DOTAS or the HMRC risk notification framework. The ELP may also make a US federal Form 8832 check-the-box election to be treated as an association taxable as a corporation, but this is generally not advisable for the intended royalty-free stock music licensing model and is not available under Bermuda or UK domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a Bermuda-established non-UK supplier of electronically supplied digital music subscriptions to UK consumers: no turnover threshold; registration is mandatory from the first sale to a UK private consumer unless using the Non-UK VAT MOSS. For a UK-established taxable person, the annual VAT registration threshold is GBP 90,000; however, once the ELP is treated as supplying digital services from a UK VAT establishment, all UK B2C supplies become taxable and the 90,000 threshold is not applied to each digital supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical exports of goods, zero-rating applies where the goods are exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK and the export is evidenced within 3 months by a customs export declaration and shipment documentation. For royalty-free stock music licensing, the supply is an electronically supplied service, not a physical export; UK VAT is not charged where the place of supply is outside the UK. For B2B customers outside the UK, the ELP must obtain and retain the customer's valid VAT registration number and country code to treat the supply as outside the scope of UK VAT. For B2C customers who are non-UK residents, the ELP must collect and retain two non-conflicting pieces of evidence of the customer's usual country of residence (e.g., billing address, IP address, bank country, mobile country code) to avoid UK VAT. If the customer is a UK consumer, the supply is subject to 20% UK VAT via the MOSS scheme and cannot be zero-rated; no customs export declaration applies to digital licences.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX form or CDS electronic declaration) for physical goods exports
  • Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or equivalent transport document evidencing export from the UK
  • Commercial Invoice, Licence Agreement, and Payment Evidence (e.g., Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT confirmation)
  • For digital music supplies: two independent electronic indicators of customer location (billing address, IP address, bank country, mobile country code) and, where necessary, a signed customer status declaration
  • B2B customer's valid overseas VAT registration number and country code where reverse charge relief is claimed

The UK does not operate a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-approval e-invoicing regime. Instead, HMRC requires digital record keeping and API-based submission of VAT returns under Making Tax Digital (MTD) using HMRC-recognised software. VAT invoices must contain the statutory elements under the Value Added Tax Regulations 1995, but there is no central invoice clearance requirement. Bermuda has no e-invoicing mandate.

HMRC may open a VAT audit and require production of export declarations, payment receipts, customer location evidence, and contractual documentation. Where zero-rating or outside-scope treatment is not substantiated, HMRC will claw back the VAT relief by assessing output VAT at 20%, plus late payment interest. Penalties are assessed under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007: up to 30% for careless inaccuracies, 70% for deliberate inaccuracies with voluntary disclosure, and 100% for deliberate inaccuracies with concealment. Criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s. 72 can lead to imprisonment for up to 7 years and unlimited fines for fraudulent evasion. VAT assessments may extend 4 years from the accounting period, or 20 years where there was deliberate non-registration or misconduct. The 180-day FX repatriation period is a commercial efficacy and transfer-pricing benchmark; failure to repatriate consideration within the deadline may be scrutinised by HMRC as an indicator of sham transactions or as non-commercial behaviour and can trigger accelerated criminal or civil fraud procedures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermuda Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the United States Dollar (USD); USD is accepted as legal tender. Bermuda operates a liberal exchange control framework under the Exchange Control Act 1972. An Exempted Limited Partnership designated as a non-resident ('external') entity is granted deemed general permission for external accounts, enabling unrestricted conversion of BMD to USD, free repatriation of profits, capital, and distributions, and no withholding taxes on outbound royalties or partner remittances. Capital and current account transactions are effectively fully convertible for non-resident ELPs; there are no quotas, licensing, or prior approval requirements for capital injections, trade settlements, or dividend/royalty remittances. The sole procedural hurdle is obtaining and maintaining non-resident designation to avoid domestic exchange-control restrictions applicable to resident accounts. For this UK-facing royalty licensing business, all funding and profit flows may be routed through the ELP's Bermuda external account in USD or GBP without BMA approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applicable in Bermuda for export receipts, royalty proceeds, or subscription income. Under the BMA's General Permission framework, non-resident entities may retain all foreign currency receipts indefinitely in external accounts without converting to BMD. For the ISIC 5920 royalty-free stock music licensing model, all Stripe and PayPal settlements in USD, GBP, or EUR may be held, swept, or converted at will. Documentary obligations require that the ELP maintains clear segregation between external (non-resident) operational accounts and any potential resident account, and that all export/royalty contracts and payout records be available to the bank's compliance unit for audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH for rapid treasury execution, but with binding legal constraints. UK-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise or Currencycloud may onboard a Bermuda ELP for GBP/EUR/SEPA payment processing and virtual IBAN issuance, but they (i) require a demonstrated economic nexus to UK/EU flows, typically evidenced by an active Stripe/PayPal settlement pattern and UK distribution agreements; (ii) are not covered by deposit insurance and are not authorised to lend, so balances above GBP 85,000 (or EMI safeguarding limits) must be swept to a Tier-1 bank; (iii) may impose onboarding declines where the ELP's limited partner register is not fully transparent; and (iv) will trigger additional AML review because the entity is domiciled in an offshore special territory. The BMA does not license resident EMIs, so a Bermuda ELP cannot obtain a local EMI licence; a UK EMI account is cross-border and must be treated as a treasury rail, not as a primary banking relationship. Optimal structure: operate a Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda operational account as the master account and use a UK EMI for payment-processing settlement only.

Compliant protocol: instruct each contributing Limited Partner to remit by SWIFT MT103 from their verified external USD or GBP account to the ELP's external account at HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield, using reference 'Capital Contribution – KY_ELP – [Partner Name]' and purpose code consistent with 'equity capital contribution / partnership capital investment' (e.g., BOFA reportable as 'Capital contribution to partnership'). For amounts above USD 1,000,000, the receiving bank's enhanced due diligence unit may request the commercial rationale plus evidence of the contributor's source of wealth. UK-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise or Currencycloud may onboard a Bermuda ELP for GBP/EUR/SEPA payment processing and virtual IBAN issuance, but they (i) require a demonstrated economic nexus to UK/EU flows, typically evidenced by an active Stripe/PayPal settlement pattern and UK distribution agreements; (ii) are not covered by deposit insurance and are not authorised to lend, so balances above GBP 85,000 (or EMI safeguarding limits) must be swept to a Tier-1 bank; (iii) may impose onboarding declines where the ELP's limited partner register is not fully transparent; and (iv) will trigger additional AML review because the entity is domiciled in an offshore special territory. The BMA does not license resident EMIs, so a Bermuda ELP cannot obtain a local EMI licence; a UK EMI account is cross-border and must be treated as a treasury rail, not as a primary banking relationship. Optimal structure: operate a Butterfield or HSBC Bermuda operational account as the master account and use a UK EMI for payment-processing settlement only.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory transfer pricing documentation requirement for an ELP outside the CIT net. Under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, in-scope entities with related-party transactions exceeding BMD 2,000,000 aggregate in the year must maintain contemporaneous local and master files aligned with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda's Economic Substance Regulations apply to entities engaging in 'relevant activities'. The licensing and distribution of royalty-free stock music constitutes 'intellectual property business' for substance purposes. The ELP must demonstrate: (i) management and control exercised in Bermuda through regular board or partner meetings; (ii) adequate number of full-time employees in Bermuda; (iii) sufficient operating expenditure for the activity; and (iv) physical office presence. A pure holding or passive royalty collection vehicle without any of these features is subject to substance assessment and may incur financial penalties. Since the ELP is not subject to CIT merely for being a partnership, economic substance requirements remain the primary enforcement hazard for UK-linked owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Bermuda's Criminal Code and Tax Administration Compliance Act, wilful failure to file an annual certificate or providing false information in a CIT return carries fines up to BMD 250,000 and imprisonment up to five years. In the UK, HMRC can apply the transfer of assets abroad provisions to assess the UK resident individual directly on 100% of the ELP's undistributed income; penalties can reach 200% of the unpaid UK tax plus daily penalties of £1,000.

Director personal liability: The general partner of an ELP bears unlimited personal liability for partnership debts and penalties. A UK court may lift the ELP's veil if the partnership is shown to be a sham, allowing HMRC to recover unpaid UK tax from the beneficial owner personally. Issuing charging orders or bankruptcy proceedings and seeking a travel ban under the UK Proceeds of Crime Act may also occur.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Evasion

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance Amendment Act imposes a BMD 5,000 annual substance assessment fee; failure to demonstrate initiated in-scope economic substance results in a BMD 75,000 financial penalty on the partnership. The Registrar may strike the entity if no appeal cures the defect. The UK may classify the ELP as UK resident under the central management and control rule, causing UK corporation tax at 25% plus National Insurance and interest surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors of the incorporated general partner are personally liable up to BMD 25,000 for penalties caused or authorised by them. HMRC can issue a section 453 Finance Act 1989 personal liability notice against those directors for unpaid UK corporation tax of the ELP, allowing direct recovery from private assets and a five-year prohibition on acting as directors in the UK.
AML

Statutory sanction: Under the Bermuda Proceeds of Crime Regulations 2020, the ELP or its general partner faces fines up to BMD 500,000 per offence, forfeiture of assets, and possible revocation of any necessary money service business licence. Payment processors will suspend the merchant account and may report the entity to the Bermuda AMLCU and the UK FCA. The ELP can also be listed by the Eurasian AML body, crippling future banking.

Director personal liability: The general partner acts as reporting entity for AML duties. Persistent violations allow disqualification of directors of the corporate general partner for up to ten years, and imprisonment up to seven years. Directors who participate in or facilitate suspicious payment throughput become personally subject to confiscation orders under the POCA.
