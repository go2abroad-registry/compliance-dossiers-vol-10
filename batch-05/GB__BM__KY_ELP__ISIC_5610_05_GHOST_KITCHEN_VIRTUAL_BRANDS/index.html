E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Bermudian law and CITA 2023 treat an Exempted Limited Partnership as fiscally transparent: income, gains, losses, and credits flow through to partners in proportion to their partnership interests, and no Bermuda entity-level income, capital gains, dividend, or withholding tax applies to a standard-scale ELP. Partners are taxed only in their residence jurisdictions, subject to CFC/offshore attribution rules. CITA 2023 imposes a 15% corporate income tax only on in-scope MNE groups (consolidated revenue at least EUR 750m) and respects transparency for ELPs.

Available election forms

Bermuda does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available elections and designations: (i) Certificate of Exemption election under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2015 to be classified as an exempted (non-resident) partnership; (ii) for in-scope MNE groups, the QDMTT election and transitional GloBE safe-harbour elections under CITA 2023; (iii) partner-level election of Bermuda-resident status for economic substance purposes; and (iv) alignment of hybrid or reverse-hybrid entity treatment under OECD GloBE IIR/UTPR rules, including any prescribed hybrid-mismatch elections, so that the ELP's transparent status is respected in Bermuda and the partner jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax is imposed in Bermuda; Bermuda levies import customs duties and payroll taxes only. The relevant sovereign-country indirect tax is UK VAT at 20%, applicable where the ghost-kitchen/virtual-brand operations are supplied through a UK fixed establishment or UK electronic marketplace.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST regime, no registration threshold, and no VAT registration procedure (BMD 0.00). For UK nexus purposes, the UK VAT registration threshold is £90,000 of taxable supplies; however, food sold for delivery in the UK through a UK fixed establishment is standard-rated at 20% and the ELP may have a mandatory day-one registration obligation if it makes any taxable supply in the UK through that establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda: exports of goods from Bermuda and cross-border services supplied to persons outside Bermuda fall outside the scope of Bermuda indirect taxation, so no zero-rating claim or refund mechanism is needed. For UK VAT zero-rating (0%) where the ELP exports physical goods from the UK: the goods must be removed from the UK within three months of the time of supply (six months where the customer arranges removal), and the ELP must hold specified export evidence under HMRC VAT Notice 703. Offshore services supplied to non-UK business customers are outside the scope of UK VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule. Food delivered to UK consumers is standard-rated at 20% and is never zero-rated; platform payouts from UberEats/DoorDash for UK delivery sales are UK taxable supplies.

Bermuda Customs requires electronic import/export declarations through the Bermuda Customs Management System (BCMS). If the ELP is separately UK VAT-registered, it must issue VAT invoices within 14 days of supply, maintain records, and file VAT returns electronically under Making Tax Digital (MTD); no pre-clearance tax-authority approval or stamping is required for private invoices.

Bermuda: under the Customs Tariff Act 1973 and Customs Act 1941, false export declarations render goods liable to forfeiture and the declarant to fines of up to three times the value of the goods and/or imprisonment; CITA 2023 imposes fixed and daily late-filing penalties, up to 100% shortfall penalties for deliberate or reckless misstatements, and interest at prescribed rates. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the United States Dollar under a currency-board-style arrangement administered by the BMA. Current-account transactions (platform settlements, supplier payments, partner distributions) are fully convertible without restriction. Capital-account transactions (capital contributions, subscription proceeds, liquidation distributions) are freely convertible for a non-resident-designated exempted partnership, subject only to prior BMA exchange-control consent or an authorized-dealer declaration. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal: Bermuda levies no income tax, withholding tax, or capital gains tax on distributions under the Income Tax Act 1986; a non-resident ELP may remit distributable profits to UK limited partners with no conversion ratio or surrender obligation once 'non-resident' exchange-control status is obtained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Bermuda Exchange Control Regulations 1973, a 'resident' person receiving foreign currency (including UberEats/DoorDash USD receipts) must offer such currency for sale to an authorized dealer within one month of receipt. However, the BMA grants blanket general permission allowing non-resident-designated exempted partnerships to hold USD and other foreign-currency accounts without surrender; BMD is pegged to USD at parity, so any mandatory conversion would be value-neutral. No statutory minimum conversion ratio applies to non-resident ELPs.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) — the sole central bank and financial services regulator for Bermuda, operating under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969 and the Exchange Control Act 1972. The Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) retains supervisory jurisdiction over UK clearing counterparties through which GBP settlement is executed.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% domestic corporate income tax for a Bermuda Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) that is not a constituent entity of a multinational enterprise group with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more; a 15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax (CIT) applies under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024 for such group entities with accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Bermuda does not impose withholding tax on distributions, dividends, or allocations paid by an ELP to its partners. The UK-resident partners are taxed on their share of the ELP's profits under UK law (transparent treatment where the ELP is a true partnership for UK tax purposes), but Bermuda levies no withholding at source on repatriation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Monthly payroll tax remittances are due on or before the 15th day following the month of payment; the quarterly Form PT-R is due within one month after the end of each calendar quarter. For the year ended 31 December, the final quarterly return is due no later than 31 January of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

CIT registration must be completed no later than 31 March of the year following the first accounting period; annual CIT return is due by the 15th day of the ninth month after the fiscal year-end (e.g., year ended 31 December 2025 is due 15 September 2026), with residual top-up tax payable by the same date.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ESR Declaration must be filed within six months of the financial year-end (e.g., a 31 December year-end is due by 30 June of the following year).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda statutory Local File or Master File threshold is enacted in BMD. The operative trigger is the OECD GloBE / CIT Act 2024 threshold: consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more, which mandates the filing of the GloBE Information Return and the maintenance of contemporaneous arm's length documentation for all related-party transactions. In practice, the TAD may request transfer pricing evidence (intercompany kitchen management fees, virtual-brand royalty streams, platform settlement charges) for any related-party arrangement where the annual transaction value exceeds BMD 1,000,000, even outside the large-group threshold.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2018 (as amended) apply to all Bermuda resident entities, including ELPs. A ghost kitchen facility operating purely for third-party food delivery platforms (UberEats, DoorDash) is a 'non-relevant activity' where no services are provided to related parties; however, if the ELP provides central kitchen management, menu licensing, or logistics services to the UK group's virtual brands, it falls within 'distribution and service business' as a relevant activity. The ELP must then demonstrate, in Bermuda, adequate premises, full-time employees, CIGA performance, and direction and management. A large-group ELP subject to the CIT Act 2024 is additionally subject to the OECD Pillar Two substance-aligned rules: the QDMTT computation must reflect real economic presence, and no 'paper substance' arrangements such as nominee directors or mailbox offices will pass the TAD review.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Payroll Tax Suppression

Statutory sanction: Under the Payroll Tax Act 1995 (as amended): a penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded, a fine of up to BMD 25,000, and imprisonment not exceeding 2 years; separate penalties of BMD 3,000–25,000 for falsification of records; the TAD may also revoke the ELP's compliance certificate and require full disclosure to HMRC under the UK–Bermuda arrangement.

Director personal liability: The general partner (including the UK holding director exercising management) is jointly and severally liable for the ELP's payroll tax debts where the ELP's assets are insufficient; knowing participation in evasion exposes directors to criminal prosecution, civil restitution orders for unpaid tax plus interest, a travel ban issued by the Bermuda courts, and disqualification as a director in Bermuda for up to 10 years.
Shell Company — Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Act 2018 (as amended): first-instance penalty of BMD 10,000 for failure to meet the substance test, plus BMD 1,000 for each month of continued default; a subsequent offence attracts BMD 50,000; knowingly filing a false ESR Declaration attracts a fine of up to BMD 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years; the Registrar may strike the ELP from the Register of Partnerships and trigger mandatory exchange of information with UK HMRC.

Director personal liability: UK parent directors who cause the ELP to file false economic substance declarations are liable as secondary participants under the Bermuda Criminal Code; personal liability includes disgorgement of the profit attributable to the non-substantive entity, travel bans pending investigation, disqualification, and custodial sentences of up to 5 years; private equity sponsors who imposed the shell structure may face restitution on a joint-tortfeasor basis.
AML and Beneficial Ownership — Platform Payout Round-Tripping

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime (Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing) Regulations 2020 and the Beneficial Ownership Act 2017: failure to register attracts daily default penalties of up to BMD 800 and fines up to BMD 250,000; failure to report suspicious transactions concerning settlement payouts attracts fines and forfeiture orders; conviction for money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 carries imprisonment of up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner who knowingly concern themselves in laundering platform settlement proceeds face personal criminal liability, confiscation orders covering body-shop nominee fees, travel bans during FIA investigations, permanent exclusion from regulated financial services, and imprisonment up to 14 years; nominee directors who lend their signatures without exercising independent judgment are treated as active participants.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing — CIT Top-up Tax and Round-Trip Royalties

Statutory sanction: Where the large-group test is met, the TAD may adjust GloBE income to the arm's length amount under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2024, assess top-up tax at 15% through the QDMTT, and impose interest plus penalties of up to 100% of the underpaid top-up tax; on the UK side, HMRC can issue a transfer pricing adjustment under TIOPA 2010 Part 4 with penalties of 20%–40% of the additional UK corporation tax for careless or deliberate error, and may apply the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) with a 60% penalty.

Director personal liability: UK parent directors who knowingly approved the mispriced fees face personal assessments for GAAR counteraction and CFC attribution, civil restitution orders for the tax lost, travel bans issued under the UK Proceeds of Crime Act as part of confiscation proceedings, disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act, and imprisonment of up to 7 years for fraudulent income shifting; Bermuda directors are concurrently liable for knowingly failing to file accurate CIT returns.
