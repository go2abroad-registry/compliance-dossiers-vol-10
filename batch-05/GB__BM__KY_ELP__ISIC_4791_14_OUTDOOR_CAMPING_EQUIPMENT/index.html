Standard VAT / GST rate

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – Bermuda imposes no VAT/GST; no economic turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Bermuda levies no VAT/GST; therefore there is no 0% export VAT rate to claim and no VAT refund mechanism. Goods exported from Bermuda may be subject to Bermuda Customs duties at import prior to re-export; duty relief may be available under Temporary Importation or Inward Processing and Re-Export arrangements when goods are re-exported within defined time frames.

  • Bermuda Customs Declaration (Bill of Entry/SAD)
  • Commercial Invoice with complete cargo description
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (house/master as applicable)
  • Packing List
  • Evidence of prior customs duty payment or duty relief authorization for re-export

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping regime exists in Bermuda. Invoice retention for record-keeping and AML/CDD under the Proceeds of Crime Regulations applies; exports are cleared through the Bermuda Customs Department using the Bermuda Single Administrative Document (SAD) and the Air Cargo Automation (ACA) or Sea Cargo systems.

Bermuda has no VAT clawback legislation, no export zero-rating fraud regime, and no statutory FX repatriation deadline; however, breaches of Customs Act duty-relief conditions may trigger retrospective duty assessment, seizure, fines up to three times the duty value, and vessel/aircraft penalties. False declarations attract forfeiture and imprisonment under the Customs Tariff Act 1995 and Customs Act 1949. Partners remain taxable in their own jurisdictions on Bermuda-source pass-through income under applicable CFC/PTEP rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) operates under a currency board system with a fixed 1:1 peg to the US Dollar (USD). Current account transactions are fully convertible for non-resident persons and exempted entities; no BMA permission is required for trade, service, or retail e-commerce payments routed through Bermuda. Capital account transactions are also freely convertible for non-resident exempted partnerships (ELPs) where the partners and assets are non-Bermudian, provided any designated 'resident' account activity is separately authorised. Profit remittance and partnership distributions from an ELP to non-resident limited partners are unrestricted and free of Bermuda withholding tax; the only requirement is a declaration to the BMA for balance-of-payments statistics. There are no currency queues, no licensing of FX dealers for spot trades in BMD/USD, and no restrictions on holding USD. The principal compliance burden is anti-money-laundering (AML) record keeping rather than exchange control approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or compulsory conversion ratio applies to exported proceeds. A Bermuda ELP engaged in internet retail of camping gear may retain export receipts in USD or any freely convertible currency. The BMA does not impose a statutory wind-down timeline for reconverting export proceeds. However, the ELP must file a periodic International Transaction Reporting System (ITRS) return via its licensed bank when cumulative receipts exceed the reporting threshold (typically USD 50,000 per transaction or aggregated month-end reporting), and every receipt must carry the correct economic purpose code. The relevant requirement is retention of underlying trade documents (invoice, airway bill, payment processor settlement report) for 5 years for AML/tax transparency review, not surrender of the currency.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA), acting under the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, the Banks and Deposit Companies Act 1999, and the Exchange Control Act 1972 (Bermuda, as modified) together with the Exchange Control Regulations 1973 and related directions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Bermuda does not operate a distinct E-Money Institution (EMI) licence; digital asset firms are regulated under the Digital Asset Business Act 2018, and money service businesses under the Money Service Business Act 2016. Consequently, a Bermuda-incorporated ELP cannot obtain a local EMI licence for itself. As a practical treasury rail, the ELP may open a merchant settlement account with a UK FCA-regulated EMI (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, or Revolut Business) using the Bermuda entity as a customer rather than a licensed EMI. Legal constraints: (i) UK EMIs generally accept Bermuda-exempted partnerships only with a clean UBO disclosure and a copy of the BMA certificate of exemption; (ii) EMIs must apply their own risk appetite, and Bermuda is treated as an offshore financial centre requiring enhanced due diligence (EDD) even for a Low_Risk_Standard retail business; (iii) EMIs cannot provide Bermudian dollar clearing, so the ELP would hold USD/GBP/EUR multi-currency pockets and settle via SWIFT; (iv) the ELP's underlying customer card transactions processed via Stripe/PayPal are unaffected because those PSPs are separate, but the EMI account must have an international payment purpose code for onward supplier remittances. Use of such EMI rails is acceptable for treasury optimisation (speed of disbursement) but cannot replace a licensed Bermuda deposit account for statutory capital holding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident limited partners are remitted by SWIFT MT103 (or Fedwire/CHIPS for USD correspondents) to the Bermuda ELP's Capital Escrow Account at HSBC or Butterfield. The wire must carry the beneficiary account IBAN/BIC, the reference 'CAP-2025-LP-XXX', and the ISO 20022/SPFS purpose code of 'Securities and equity transfers – equity capital for investment in commercial activity' (purpose code INVC-Equity or, if the bank uses the UK EBA framework, 'CUST' for custody? No—use the OECD purpose classification 'Varies by bank: HSBC uses 'CAPITAL' for inward remittances). A statutory declaration from the GP plus a certified schedule of the limited partners' contributions must accompany the first injection to satisfy the BMA's Exchange Control statistics. There is no ceiling on inward capital for a non-resident ELP. Each contribution must be verified against the signed capital commitment schedule in the limited partnership agreement; if the LP calls capital from a UK-resident partner, the transfer should be routed via a UK clearing bank (e.g., Barclays or HSBC UK) using a covering MT103 with reason 'Business investment' (DAK/CAP) and HMRC notification is not required. The BMA only requires the receiving Bermuda bank to file an ITRS report for any inward remittance above USD 50,000. The escrow account may be funded initially with a minimum of USD 1,000 to demonstrate activation; practical banking thresholds for the Business account often require USD 10,000 minimum opening balance.Trade settlements flow through two primary clearing vectors. (1) Direct supplier disbursements: execute SWIFT MT103 messages from the Bermuda operational account via the bank's New York or London correspondent, using the appropriate purpose code 'Trade in goods – settlement of imported merchandise' (e.g., OECD/ISO 4217 code 'GOODS' or the bank-specific 'IMP+'). Each MT103 must contain the supplier's IBAN/BIC, invoice number, and a description of the camping equipment goods (sleeping bags, tents, portable stoves). For US-dollar clearing use CHIPS/Fedwire with the Bermuda bank's USD correspondent; for UK sterling use the CHAPS rail (or Faster Payments if < GBP 1 million) through a UK settlement account; for Euro use TARGET2/SEPA. (2) Commercial Letters of Credit: for large one-off purchase orders with a Chinese or Vietnamese manufacturer, the Bermuda bank establishes an irrevocable LC (UCP600) on behalf of the ELP, where the LC is issued in USD and confirmed by a UK or Singapore confirming bank. The ELP must hold an available LC collateral of 50–100% of the LC value in the Trade Finance Account to avoid a pre-funding requirement. Stripe/PayPal payouts are received into the operational account from the PSP's partner bank via regular SWIFT daily sweeps; the settlement descriptor must include the PSP reference ID. For PayPal, funds arrive in USD from PayPal's Wells Fargo account; for Stripe, funds arrive from Stripe's Citibank or Barclays account depending on the merchant country. All outbound payments to suppliers must align with the approved beneficiary list and the bank's sanctions screening. The ELP must maintain no more than 15% of monthly receipts in the PSP float; excess funds are swept to the capital escrow account or an interest-bearing money market account to avoid negative-yield charges.Outright foreign exchange forward contracts (deliverable, tenor up to 12 months) executed with HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield under an ISDA/IFEMA master agreement, primarily for hedging USD/GBP and USD/EUR outflows when the ELP pays UK/EU suppliers while holding USD proceeds.Rolling or window forwards with a flexible fixing date, allowing the e-commerce treasury to hedge forecast monthly supplier invoices without naming exact settlement dates.Cross-currency swaps (ISDA 2002 with Bermuda law governing the master agreement) for notional values above USD 5 million, used to convert a fixed-rate USD liability into GBP exposure where the ELP issues a promissory note to a UK parent.NDFs (non-deliverable forwards) on less-convertible Asian currencies (e.g., Chinese renminbi offshore (CNH) via CNH deliverable, or Vietnamese dong NDF) referencing a Reuters/Bloomberg fixing; permitted only if the counterparty is an international bank branch (HSBC London or Standard Chartered Singapore), and only for hedging of a committed import invoice where currency convertibility in the local market is restricted. The BMD/USD fixed peg makes any BMD/USD hedge unnecessary.Bermuda-dollar buy/sell agreements are not required, but the ELP may use a depository sweep agreement with Butterfield to automatically convert USD receipts to BMD at par for local expense purposes, which is a zero-cost treasury instrument equivalent to an FX swap and requires no BMA approval.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium. Bermuda does not operate a distinct E-Money Institution (EMI) licence; digital asset firms are regulated under the Digital Asset Business Act 2018, and money service businesses under the Money Service Business Act 2016. Consequently, a Bermuda-incorporated ELP cannot obtain a local EMI licence for itself. As a practical treasury rail, the ELP may open a merchant settlement account with a UK FCA-regulated EMI (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, or Revolut Business) using the Bermuda entity as a customer rather than a licensed EMI. Legal constraints: (i) UK EMIs generally accept Bermuda-exempted partnerships only with a clean UBO disclosure and a copy of the BMA certificate of exemption; (ii) EMIs must apply their own risk appetite, and Bermuda is treated as an offshore financial centre requiring enhanced due diligence (EDD) even for a Low_Risk_Standard retail business; (iii) EMIs cannot provide Bermudian dollar clearing, so the ELP would hold USD/GBP/EUR multi-currency pockets and settle via SWIFT; (iv) the ELP's underlying customer card transactions processed via Stripe/PayPal are unaffected because those PSPs are separate, but the EMI account must have an international payment purpose code for onward supplier remittances. Use of such EMI rails is acceptable for treasury optimisation (speed of disbursement) but cannot replace a licensed Bermuda deposit account for statutory capital holding.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% Bermuda corporate income tax on Exempted Limited Partnerships; no capital gains tax, VAT, or GST. UK main rate 25% (19% small profits rate) applies only if central management and control is actually exercised in the United Kingdom.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on distributions, profit shares, or dividends paid by an Exempted Limited Partnership to resident or non-resident partners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Bermuda imposes no statutory Local File or Master File threshold given the zero CIT regime. Best practice is to maintain OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation for all related-party e-commerce transactions aggregating above USD 500,000 (BMD equivalent) annually. For UK exposure, HMRC requires adequate transfer pricing documentation for large businesses (250+ employees or turnover above GBP 50 million); Country-by-Country reporting is triggered at consolidated group revenue above EUR 750 million, and HMRC may impose penalties of up to 100% of the understated tax where documentation is not maintained for transactions above GBP 200,000.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 applies only to 'relevant activities' (banking, insurance, fund management, financing, leasing, headquarters, shipping, IP holding, and distribution and service centres). Internet retailing of outdoor camping equipment under ISIC 4791 is not a relevant activity; however, the ELP must file an annual economic substance declaration confirming this. The ELP must nevertheless maintain commercial substance proportionate to its actual activity (physical presence, employees, expenditure, and governance) to resist UK CFC, POEM, and diverted profits tax challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and UK Controlled Foreign Corporation (CFC) Attribution

Statutory sanction: UK corporation tax at 25% on apportioned profits plus interest; penalties up to 100% of the tax for deliberate and concealed behaviour; criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of income tax under TMA 1970 s.106A carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner may be pursued for fraudulent or wrongful trading; HMRC personal liability notices up to 100% of outstanding tax where assets have been stripped; director disqualification under CDDA 1986 and travel bans in serious cases.
Diverted Profits Tax (DPT) and Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: DPT charged at 31% (increased from 25% on 1 April 2025) on diverted profits, plus interest, and penalties up to 100% for failure to notify; HMRC may alternatively open a transfer-pricing adjustment under TIOPA 2010 Part 4 taxing the ELP at 25%.

Director personal liability: Senior accounting officer penalties of GBP 5,000 per individual for failure to establish adequate tax governance; the corporate offence of failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion under FA 2016 s.45 carries unlimited fines; civil restitution orders and international travel alerts are available.
Bermuda Economic Substance and Filing Defaults

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018: fixed penalty of BMD 10,000; continuing default of BMD 10,000 per month; on conviction, fines up to BMD 100,000 and imprisonment of officers up to 2 years; strike-off of the ELP and forfeiture of limited liability protection.

Director personal liability: The general partner and its directors are personally liable for the same penalties during continued default; the Registrar may prohibit the individual from acting as a general partner or director of any Bermuda exempted entity.
Anti-Money Laundering and Opaque Shell Company Misuse

Statutory sanction: Bermuda AML/ATF Regulations: fines up to BMD 1,000,000, asset freezing orders and confiscation; Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 money laundering offences carry up to 14 years' imprisonment; the ELP may be dissolved and the partners' limited liability lost.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner face personal criminal fines, confiscation, and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil liability for restitution and clawback of distributions; Unexplained Wealth Orders and mutual legal assistance requests from the UK NCA or FBI may impose travel bans and passport surrender.
Payment-Rails Misconduct and Consumer Restitution

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under Bermuda Consumer Protection Act 1999 up to BMD 25,000 per violation; termination of merchant accounts; winding-up proceedings and fraudulent conveyance actions to reverse distributions made while insolvent.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner remain personally liable for unpaid consumer refunds if assets are stripped; disqualification as a director for up to 15 years; travel bans under proceeds-of-crime freezing orders.
Wrongful Trading and General Partner Unlimited Liability

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Companies Act 1981 wrongful and fraudulent trading provisions applied by analogy to ELPs: the court may order the general partner to contribute to the ELP's assets; fraudulent trading carries criminal penalties and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Piercing of the ELP veil renders the general partner unlimitedly liable; directors of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the ELP's debts; civil restitution, disqualification, and travel bans may be imposed.
