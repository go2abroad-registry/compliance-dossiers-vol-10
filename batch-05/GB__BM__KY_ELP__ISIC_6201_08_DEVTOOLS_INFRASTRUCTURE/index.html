Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: The ELP is fiscally transparent under Bermuda law; the partnership is not itself subject to Bermuda income tax or corporate tax. Profits, gains, and losses pass through to partners, who are taxable only in their respective jurisdictions of residence. Bermuda imposes no direct taxation on capital, income, profits, or distributions of an exempted partnership, whether the income is of Bermuda or offshore source.

Available election forms

Bermuda has no domestic check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election. For U.S. federal income tax purposes, the Bermuda ELP is an eligible entity and may file Form 8832 to elect classification as a partnership or as an association taxable as a corporation. Such election must be exercised at the partnership level and effective prospectively in accordance with IRC § 301.7701-3. Any mismatch arising from inconsistent classification between Bermuda (transparent) and a partner jurisdiction must be assessed under OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No value-added tax or goods and services tax; Bermuda levies customs duties on imported tangible goods and a payroll tax on Bermuda employment income, but digital and cloud services supplied by an ELP are outside the scope of any Bermuda indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Bermuda does not operate a VAT/GST regime and therefore imposes no turnover-based registration threshold.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Bermuda operates a fully liberalised exchange control regime under the Exchange Control Act 1972, with the BMA administering the framework. The Bermudian dollar (BMD) is pegged one-for-one to the US dollar, and both currencies circulate freely. no restriction is imposed on current account convertibility; the majority of business undertaken by an Exempted Limited Partnership designated as 'non-resident' is treated as offshore and freely convertible. Capital account transactions likewise require no exchange control approval provided the entity is resident outside Bermuda for exchange control purposes; therefore profit remittances to unitholders in the United Kingdom are permitted without prior BMA consent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Bermuda's Exchange Control Regulations, proceeds from the export of goods must normally be surrendered to an authorised dealer within six months of receipt, unless the BMA grants a specific retention authority for a non-resident-designated ELP. For a service-based SaaS exporter, no mandatory conversion ratio applies; retained foreign currency receipts may be held in a 'foreign currency account' for operational purposes, provided the eventual disposal is reported through standard bank reconciliation. Trading operations are typically structured to fall outside the surrender requirement by obtaining 'non-resident status' from the BMA.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High. A Bermuda-resident ELP may open an operational account with an offshore digital EMI (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, or Mercury) to support USD/GBP collections and disbursements. Legal constraints: the EMI must hold an appropriate electronic money licence in its own jurisdiction (e.g., FCA in the UK, FINTRAC in Canada, State licensing in the US); Bermuda's BMA imposes no additional restriction on using a foreign EMI, provided the ELP discloses the account in its compliance file and the EMI meets the bank's beneficial ownership reporting obligations. However, for capital escrow deposits above USD 250,000, a full commercial bank is recommended, as EMIs are not licensed deposit-takers and cannot accept client escrow balances.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For capital injection into the KY-ELP from the UK parent or external investors, the compliant protocol is to remit either US dollars or GBP cross-border via SWIFT MT103, using an explicit purpose code (e.g., 'capital contribution to partnership' or 'equity injection for business operations') in field 70; no personal or 'gift' purpose codes should be used. The remittance must be accompanied by a board resolution or partners' capital call notice, a signed capital contribution schedule, and a beneficial ownership declaration. The receiving Bermuda bank will validate source of funds and credit the segregated capital escrow account; funds are available for utilisation only after the bank completes its final verification.Clearing rails are dominated by international SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for USD and GBP vendor payments (e.g., AWS, Stripe, Datadog payroll), and for intercompany settlement with the UK parent. For cloud infrastructure hardware procurement, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued through the Bermuda bank's corresponding relationship is available but not standard practice for SaaS. Domestic ACH (Bermuda Automated Settlement System) is used only for recurring Bermuda-local obligations; cross-currency settlements outside Bermuda are cleared via the bank's correspondent network.Deliverable FX forward contracts in GBP/USD and EUR/USD, with a maximum tenor of 12 months, hedged against forecast subscription receipts and vendor invoices.European-style FX options (put and call) on GBP/USD contracted with the bank or a broker-dealer under a two-way ISDA/CSA agreement, subject to conservative option premium limits.Cross-currency swaps (including back-to-back structures) for long-term intercompany loan repayments in GBP/USD.Non-deliverable forward contracts (NDFs) only in restricted-currency counterparties, e.g., Chinese Central Bank-rate-accessed CNH, limited to the amount supported by actual trade invoices.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda domestic transfer pricing statute imposes Local File or Master File obligations on an out-of-scope ELP. For in-scope MNE groups, the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 incorporates the OECD GloBE rules which require contemporaneous documentation on intercompany transactions, cap deductions, and mandate a GloBE Information Return; a qualifying transitional CbCR safe harbour applies where the MNE group's consolidated revenue is below EUR 750 million and the jurisdiction applies a nominal rate of 20% or more. Bermuda's zero-rate regime therefore creates a presumed jurisdiction-level ETR of 0%, and an ELP that is not excluded must document the arm's length nature of any UK-managed functions.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Act equivalent to the Cayman Islands or BVI regimes. Economic substance is instead enforced indirectly: (i) the Exempted Partnerships Act 1992 requires a Bermuda registered office, a resident general partner or licensed agent, and a local partnership register; (ii) the CIT Act 2023's substance-based income exclusion and ETA reward entities with substantial local activity; and (iii) the UK's 'central management and control' doctrine recharacterizes the ELP as UK-resident if strategic decisions are taken in the UK. A pure shell ELP with no Bermudian substance, no local books, and no UK-CFC planning is an immediate audit target.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Willful Evasion of Corporate Income Tax

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid CIT at 15% plus statutory interest; late filing penalty of BMD 500 per day up to BMD 50,000; civil penalty of up to 200% of the avoided tax; criminal prosecution for willful evasion with fines up to BMD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally liable for all assessed penalties; a corporate general partner's directors can be named personally in follow-on proceedings, disgorgement orders are available, and travel restrictions may be imposed pending criminal trial.
UK CFC / Central Management and Control Misconduct

Statutory sanction: UK corporation tax at 25% on the ELP's worldwide profits under CTA 2009 and TIOPA 2010 Part 9A; UK transfer-pricing adjustments and GAAR counteraction under Finance Act 2013; penalties of up to 100% of the tax for deliberate inaccuracies plus interest and late payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: UK-resident partners and the corporate general partner's directors face joint and several liability for CFC charges; HMRC may impose personal liability for penalties under FA 2007 Schedule 24 and pursue director disqualification proceedings.
Shell Company Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from the Bermuda Registrar of Companies, revocation of exempted status, refusal to issue certificates of compliance, inability to sustain bank relationships, and re-registration penalties; daily default fines of BMD 10 per day and monetary penalties up to BMD 10,000.

Director personal liability: The general partner loses its statutory limited-liability shield and becomes jointly and unlimitedly liable for all ELP obligations; directors of a corporate general partner are subject to a 5-year directorship ban and personal restitution claims by creditors.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Regime Breach

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty up to BMD 25,000 for the ELP; daily default penalty up to BMD 1,000 for continuing non-compliance; criminal conviction for individuals with imprisonment up to 2 years; CRS/FATCA non-reporting additionally triggers a separate penalty regime of BMD 5,000 per unreported account.

Director personal liability: The general partner and each director of the corporate general partner who knowingly permits the breach is personally liable for the daily penalties, may face criminal conviction, and can be prohibited from acting as a director in Bermuda for up to 5 years.
Artificial Profit Stripping and Sham Intercompany Invoicing

Statutory sanction: UK transfer-pricing adjustments under TIOPA 2010 Part 4 disallowing the excess deduction; GAAR (Finance Act 2013) counteraction with penalties of up to 60% of the counteracted tax; HMRC may recharacterize the arrangement as a dividend, triggering UK corporate tax and potential CFC charges; interest accrues on all underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of the UK payer and of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the penalties; HMRC may apply a 'responsible member' assessment against UK partners individually, and the UK court may issue a seven-year directorship disqualification order.
