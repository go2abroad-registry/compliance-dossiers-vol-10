Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Bermuda imposes zero corporate income tax, zero capital gains tax, and zero withholding tax. The ELP is fiscally transparent by statute; online retail profit flows through to partners under the Exempted Partnerships Act 2014. Offshoredistance-sold baby/maternity e-commerce income has no Bermuda territorial nexus and no economic-substance reporting obligation for a pure non-emitting partnership engaged in offshore sales.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity-election regime exists in Bermuda because there is no income tax; transparency is statutory, not elective. UK-resident partners may file elections under the UK hybrid mismatch rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6A) to align the UK tax treatment of the foreign transparent vehicle and to prevent double deduction/non-inclusion; Bermuda requires no ruling or election for pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Bermuda Customs Duty (import-based tariff); no VAT, no GST, no sales/use tax, no excise on digital services. UK VAT (20%) and destination-country consumption taxes apply to B2C sales of imported goods at the point of foreign delivery, not in Bermuda.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: no VAT registration threshold exists because no VAT is levied. Bermuda customs duty is exigible from the first import entry on CIF value under tariff bands generally ranging from 0% to 33.25% (the standard consumer-goods band is 22.25%; essential infant and maternity items typically fall in duty-reduced 0%-7.25% bands). Recurring fiscal costs are the annual ROC exempted-entity fee (low four figures in BMD) and, if resident staff are engaged, quarterly payroll tax at graduated employer rates. There is no turnover-based tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Bermuda: there is no Bermuda export VAT to zero-rate or reclaim. Digital/online export of baby and maternity supplies from Bermuda to foreign consumers is entirely outside Bermuda's indirect tax net. The ELP, however, as a non-established seller supplying goods from overseas consignment to UK consumers, must register for UK VAT at the nil threshold under the £135 import consignment regime (VAT Act 1994, Sch 9ZB) and charge UK VAT at point of sale; if it holds UK inventory, it becomes UK-established and is subject to the £90,000 standard registration threshold. EU consumer sales trigger OSS/IOSS registration. No Bermuda zero-rating certificate or exporter status is required,

  • Bermuda electronic export declaration filed via the Bermuda Customs single-window portal
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Bermuda
  • Commercial invoice with HS classification for ISIC 4791 maternity/baby commodities
  • Packing list with value, quantity, and destination per consignment
  • Stripe/PayPal settlement reports evidencing inbound foreign-currency remittances

No real-time fiscal clearance, no B2B/B2C e-invoicing mandate, no Peppol/CTCs, and no digital stamping regime in Bermuda. Bermuda Customs operates an electronic single-window for import and export declarations; exporters file digital entries with commercial invoices, packing lists, and transport documents. Stripe/PayPal settlement records must be retained as primary evidence for partner-level UK GAAP reporting and for any HMRC or overseas VAT audit of the e-commerce supply chain.

Bermuda has no export-VAT clawback because no export VAT or refund exists. Enforcement exposure arises elsewhere: (1) UK HMRC - failure to register/charge VAT on UK consumer sales triggers assessment of unpaid VAT plus default interest, civil penalties up to 100% of potential lost revenue (Finance Act 2007, Sch 24), and default surcharge under VATA 1994; (2) Bermuda Customs Act 1948 - false export declarations render goods liable to forfeiture and monetary penalties up to treble the duty payable; (3) OTC - undeclared Bermuda-resident staff incur monthly late-filing payroll tax penalties, assessments, and interest; (4) partner level - mispriced profit attribution to the transparent ELP invites UK CFC/transfer-pricing adjustments with penalties up to 100% of additional tax. Note: Bermuda imposes no exchange controls or statutory FX repatriation deadline; the 180-day indicator is a commercial best-practice benchmark for working-capital substantiation, not a Bermudian legal term.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Bermudian Dollar (BMD) is pegged to the United States Dollar (USD) at a fixed 1:1 ratio, with USD accepted as legal tender in Bermuda. The capital account and current account are fully convertible; there are no exchange control restrictions on inbound or outbound payments, dividends, profit remittances, or liquidation proceeds for exempted partnerships. The BMA maintains a free-market approach, requiring no prior approval for foreign exchange transactions, and there are no surrender requirements for export proceeds. Profit repatriation for an ELP is unrestricted, subject only to standard anti-money laundering (AML) verification and statutory record-keeping obligations under the Bermuda Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Act 2018.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or central bank surrender obligations for export receipts in Bermuda. An Exempted Limited Partnership engaged in cross-border e-commerce may hold export proceeds in any freely convertible currency, including USD, EUR, GBP, or BMD, without requirement to convert to domestic currency. All receipts are eligible for direct deposit into an operational account and may be repatriated or used for commitments abroad without BMA licence or approval.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for offshore digital EMI accounts (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer) as treasury sub-ledgers or collection rails, provided the EMI is licensed in a FATF-equivalent jurisdiction (e.g., UK FCA or European EBA). Legal constraint: Bermuda does not recognise EMIs as deposit-taking institutions, and an ELP may not hold client funds in an EMI as its primary bank account. However, an EMI can be used as a disbursement/prepaid instrument for supplier payments and marketplace settlement (Stripe/PayPal) if the account is in the ELP's own name, the EMI provider performs full KYC/beneficial ownership checks, and the BMA's Travel Rule and record-keeping obligations are satisfied. Use of an EMI for material corporate treasury is discouraged due to lack of Bermudian deposit insurance and unregulated status; it should be supplementary to a regulated bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity or loan capital must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 or, for large amounts, MT202/MT205 through a corresponding bank relationship. The ELP should instruct the remitter to use a purpose-of-payment code aligned with the market convention: for capital contribution use 'INVCAP' (Investment/Capital) or the beneficiary bank's alternate 'CRED' code; for intercompany loan use 'LOAN' or 'CLRC' (Corporate Lending). The beneficiary reference line should contain the ELP legal name and the General Partner's reference, along with the invoice or subscription agreement number. For online sales receipts, payment gateways (Stripe/PayPal) settle via payout batches to a USD or BMD account using standard bancassurance codes (e.g., 'TRDPAY' or 'SUPP' for supplier payment). BMA-regulated banks require the remitter to be an authorised signatory of the ELP or a disclosed beneficial owner. A copy of the capital-introduction resolution and signed subscription document must be held on file. Onboarding banks may levy a 0.25% deposit fee but no FX conversion charge when USD is settled, given the fixed BMD peg.Outbound trade settlements from the Bermuda operational account are executed via (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct account-to-account payments to suppliers, with a concise narrative referencing the underlying purchase order; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) issued by a Bermudian bank (e.g., Butterfield or HSBC) through their trade finance desks for larger, recurring supplier purchases, particularly from Asia-Pacific distributors; and (iii) SWIFT MT202 for high-value interbank transfers between the ELP's treasury accounts at different banks. Payment gateways (Stripe/PayPal) also support mass payouts to linked bank accounts or cards, but only after funds are settled to the primary operational account. Utility bills, merchant fees, and refunds to customers are cleared by direct debit / ACH via the local Cheque & Direct Clearing System of the BMA, or via Fedwire/CHIPS for USD payments in the United States. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium for offshore digital EMI accounts (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Payoneer) as treasury sub-ledgers or collection rails, provided the EMI is licensed in a FATF-equivalent jurisdiction (e.g., UK FCA or European EBA). Legal constraint: Bermuda does not recognise EMIs as deposit-taking institutions, and an ELP may not hold client funds in an EMI as its primary bank account. However, an EMI can be used as a disbursement/prepaid instrument for supplier payments and marketplace settlement (Stripe/PayPal) if the account is in the ELP's own name, the EMI provider performs full KYC/beneficial ownership checks, and the BMA's Travel Rule and record-keeping obligations are satisfied. Use of an EMI for material corporate treasury is discouraged due to lack of Bermudian deposit insurance and unregulated status; it should be supplementary to a regulated bank account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% Bermuda headline rate for the Exempted Limited Partnership; 15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 rate applies only to in-scope constituent entities of multinational groups with consolidated revenue equal to or above EUR 750 million. UK corporation tax on profits is 19%-25% but is applicable only through UK-resident partners or a UK permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% Bermuda dividend withholding tax; no Bermuda income tax is withheld on distributions or allocations to ELP partners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Bermuda local-file or master-file transfer-pricing threshold has been prescribed. CIT Act 2023 in-scope threshold is EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue, expressed in BMD-equivalent only for administrative filing; UK transfer-pricing documentation applies if a UK-resident partner carries on a trade through a Bermuda ELP and participates in controlled transactions.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Act 2018 applies to Bermuda exempted entities conducting relevant activities. E-commerce distribution of baby and maternity supplies is not automatically relevant if purchases from and sales to customers are with unrelated parties. If the ELP acts as an intragroup distributor or provides services to related parties, it must maintain physical presence, staff, management and expenditure levels in Bermuda proportionate to the relevant income. Failure to satisfy economic substance can subject the ELP to penalties and forced deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false declaration

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty up to BMD 10,000 plus daily default penalty for non-filing; false or misleading statements may be prosecuted summarily and attract imprisonment and superior fines up to BMD 50,000, plus assessment of unpaid tax and interest. UK HMRC may apply up to 100% tax-geared penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007.

Director personal liability: A general partner of the ELP is personally liable for penalties caused by a deliberate false return. Fraudulent trading or tax evasion may pierce the partnership veil, leading to joint and several restitution, director disqualification, travel restraint and imprisonment.
Shell company without economic substance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance assessment can impose a penalty of up to BMD 10,000, a further BMD 10,000 per continuing non-compliance period, and strike-off or deregistration. UK HMRC may re-characterize the ELP as a sham and tax the UK resident partners directly.

Director personal liability: General partners and shadow partners who knowingly permit the sham arrangement are jointly and severally liable for unpaid UK tax and National Insurance contributions; the court may order asset freezes and UK travel bans.
Money laundering and AML/CTF breaches

Statutory sanction: Bermuda's Proceeds of Crime Act and AML/ATF Regulations permit asset forfeiture, account closure, regulatory fines and imprisonment; senior officers face personal criminal liability. Payment intermediaries may terminate accounts and blacklist the entity.

Director personal liability: The general partner and appointed compliance officer may be criminally prosecuted for failing to file suspicious activity reports; individuals can be fined and sentenced to imprisonment, and partnership assets are recoverable.
Transfer pricing manipulation in multinational group

Statutory sanction: UK HMRC will adjust profits to the arm's-length standard, apply interest, and issue penalties for deliberate errors; the Bermuda in-scope CIT Act 2023 may assess top-up tax to raise the group tax to 15%. Civil penalties can reach 100% of the understated UK tax.

Director personal liability: The senior accounting officer or general partner responsible for transfer-pricing policies may be personally liable for failure to maintain accurate accounting records; in serious cases, criminal tax evasion proceedings and extradition arrangements apply.
